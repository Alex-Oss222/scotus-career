#### Love v. Pepersack

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 94-1582; no separately established Supreme Court docket; judgment below, 47 F.3d 120.
**Decided:** December 11, 1995.

##### Substantive-due-process dismissal and its independent common propositions

**Authority of the judgment:** Affirm dismissal of substantive due process, including contractual liberty, 5–4: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate. O'Connor's Part II-B has four votes for its constitutional-insufficiency application. Stone-Zsela's separate state-law/federal-right distinction and specific Second Amendment source ground supplies the fifth judgment vote but depends on incorporation, while the plurality's application does not. Neither sufficient rationale is established as a logical subset of the other; no controlling application rationale exists under Marks.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** A state-created entitlement, even with knowing violation of state law, does not by itself establish the distinct substantive federal deprivation required by §1983.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-A, joined by all eight other Justices; an actual unanimous proposition independent of the fractured ultimate application.

**Current force:** This state-law/federal-wrong distinction controls as stated; it does not supply a common sufficient rationale for the ultimate due-process application.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** Ordinary economic contracting supplies no independent heightened entitlement to complete every otherwise lawful transaction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-A, joined by all eight other Justices; an actual unanimous proposition independent of the fractured ultimate application.

**Current force:** This contractual-liberty limit controls as stated; the ultimate due-process dismissal binds the parties without a common sufficient application rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general executive-licensing test, automatic cure through later state review, general specific-source displacement rule, tangible-loss prerequisite, or adoption of Gardner's circuit entitlement rule follows. The alleged deliberate arrest-only policy is accepted for dismissal analysis, not proved; the claimed Maryland entitlement is not definitively construed. Procedural due process and abandoned Contracts Clause or state constitutional claims are not revived.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Roth separates the claimed state interest from constitutional protection; Collins distinguishes state duty from federal wrong; Hafer separates capacity, personal wrong and defenses. Albright remains fractured. Marks supplies no synthesized application rule; legislative rational-basis burdens are not transferred to executive-misconduct claims.

##### The arms guarantee belongs to individuals without formal militia enrollment

**Holding:** The Second Amendment protects an individual's keeping and bearing of arms within its constitutional scope. Formal enrollment or current organized-militia service is not a prerequisite to invoking it; the Court does not decide every protected weapon, defensive purpose or manner of carrying.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six direct votes, as in Parts III-B and III-C below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The right's personal holder does not eliminate its scope limits, establish an unrestricted right to every weapon or use, or convert Miller into a complete self-defense code.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Miller's weapon-and-record inquiry and militia-related reasoning survive without a formal-enrollment prerequisite. Nunn, Andrews and Chandler supply historical evidence of personal protection with regulatory limits, not binding federal incorporation holdings; Andrews retains its state-law and military-suitability scope. The lower-court Johnson collective-only premise cannot support dismissal. Lewis's extant-felony disability does not decide this nonfelon allegation or state incorporation and remains unchanged.

##### Fourteenth Amendment application of the personal arms guarantee

**Holding:** The Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause applies the personal Second Amendment guarantee to States under the same constitutional standards governing federal action. Cruikshank and Presser are overruled only insofar as they foreclose that application; no total incorporation or new Privileges or Immunities holding follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-B; Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas join, six direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No entire state firearms regime is invalidated. The personal-keeping and acquisition evidence and Reconstruction protection support this right-specific fundamental-character determination without prescribing every regulatory boundary. The temporary 1866 Freedmen's Bureau Act and Howard's speech are historical evidence, not a perpetual nationwide licensing regime or a conclusive statement of every ratifier's understanding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Duncan supplies the right-specific fundamental-to-American-justice inquiry, Malloy equal standards for incorporated guarantees, and Benton historical examination and express displacement of contrary nonincorporation precedent. Cruikshank and Presser lose only their barrier to Fourteenth Amendment arms application. Their account of the 1791 Amendment's original federal operation survives, and Presser's unauthorized armed military-organization disposition is undisturbed.

##### Necessary acquisition as an incident of protected keeping

**Holding:** Acquisition necessary to constitutionally protected keeping falls within that keeping's protection; a State cannot categorically exclude the claim merely because it obstructed purchase rather than seized an existing arm. The weapon and conduct must fall within the guarantee and the actual restriction must be assessed under it; violation of state authorization alone is not a constitutional infringement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-C; Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas join, six direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The principle neither protects every handgun nor guarantees purchase from every seller, prohibits investigation or licensing, or supplies a scrutiny tier, comprehensive weapons classification or closed regulatory list. Stone-Zsela's affirmative infringement application is not adopted.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Wright, decided 1998-10-05, Part II-B, supplies Core-Preservation Review for justification of restrictions on the personal arms right; constitutional coverage remains a separate inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Miller's scope inquiry survives. Andrews's purchase, ammunition, repair and related-transport discussion supplies qualified historical evidence without adoption of its whole state-law analysis or broader separate opinion.

##### Return of the individual-capacity arms claim for constitutional application

**Holding:** Neither nonapplication to States nor lack of formal militia affiliation sustains the arms dismissal. The actual weapon and conduct, constitutional restriction, lawful regulation, each officer's responsibility, properly presented defenses and available relief remain for determination under the personal-right, incorporation and acquisition holdings.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five direct votes for formal vacatur and reserved application. Stone-Zsela would reverse with affirmative infringement; Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm. Those four do not join this remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the judgment only insofar as it affirms the individual-capacity Second Amendment dismissal and remand. Past damages are not mooted by subsequent state approval, but no continuing denial or injunction entitlement is established. No infringement, damages amount, fee or punitive entitlement, immunity ruling, automatic discovery or trial is ordered. State, official-capacity, unappealed and abandoned dispositions remain undisturbed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Each officer's own acts require assessment; actual-knowledge allegations differ from should-have-known allegations, and office alone supplies no liability. The first-instance immunity nonreach rule is under Federal Courts, Love, December 11, 1995, Fourth Circuit No. 94-1582.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hafer requires personal federal wrongdoing and preserves personal defenses; recognition of the arms guarantee does not itself establish liability or retrospective notice.

#### United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** February 12, 1996.

##### The right is personally held, and military usefulness supplies no automatic immunity

**Holding:** A person may invoke the Second Amendment without proving membership in, current service to, or preparation for an organized militia; constitutional protection still depends on the actual weapon, conduct and restriction. Military usefulness alone neither excludes an arm from the guarantee nor creates an unrestricted exemption from manufacture, transfer, possession, taxation or registration controls.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-A, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Love's personal-holder rule governs; its state-incorporation holding is unnecessary in this federal prosecution. Miller preserves actual weapon-and-record inquiry, not categorical immunity for military-capable arms or automatic conviction affirmance.

##### The thirteen-count appellate affirmance returns for the preserved arms inquiry

**Holding:** Because the court below materially relied on a personal-affiliation prerequisite that Love rejects and supplied no adequate independent application of the surviving weapon-and-regulation inquiry, its judgment is vacated insofar as it affirms the thirteen §922(o) convictions. On remand, the lower courts must decide the preserved arms challenge under Love and Miller using the actual objects, statutory bases and record, with constitutional coverage and permissible regulation kept distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-B, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela dissents from the remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exhaustive weapons classification, scrutiny tier or categorical public-safety rule is adopted. Through §921(a)(23), §5845(b) includes a weapon shooting, designed to shoot or readily restorable to shoot more than one shot automatically without manual reloading by a single trigger function; its frame or receiver; a part designed and intended solely and exclusively, or combination of parts designed and intended, for conversion; and an assembly combination possessed or controlled by one person. Each branch keeps its distinct predicates. The lower courts must use actual count-specific objects and statutory branches as necessary, without creating a new offense element or reopening every established finding.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Wright, decided 1998-10-05, Part II-B, supplies Core-Preservation Review for justification of restrictions on the personal arms right; constitutional coverage remains a separate inquiry.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the appellate affirmance of the thirteen §922(o) counts solely for the preserved arms application under Love and Miller. Existing-record decision or ordinary further proceedings remain available as necessary; no automatic hearing, acquittal, release, new trial or resentencing follows. Consequential sentencing treatment depends on lawful disposition of those counts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Love removes the personal-affiliation prerequisite; Miller supplies the surviving weapon-and-regulation inquiry. The remand rests on identified legal error, not missing published trial detail.

##### Military usefulness supplies no exemption from the separate registration duty

**Holding:** The personal character of protected keeping and a weapon's military usefulness do not themselves exempt a possessor from the NFA's identification and registration requirements administering lawful making and transfer. The three §5861(d) convictions are affirmed against the presented arms objection; the Court does not decide a materially different claim that a particular firearm could not lawfully be registered.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight rationale joins. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No count-specific premise shows legally unavailable registration. The mere presence of machineguns among seized objects does not assign them, or a post-1986 prohibition, to a particular registration count.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Miller, Sonzinsky and Freed preserve registration's separate tax-administration role; Love distinguishes constitutional coverage from lawful regulation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The three registration convictions are affirmed against the presented arms objection independently of the unresolved §922(o) application.

#### Pinder v. Johnson

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-2125; no Supreme Court docket stated in the Record
**Decided:** May 21, 1996

##### Do protective assurances or custody of a private assailant establish a custodial duty to protect the threatened family?

**Holding:** Official knowledge of danger, promises of protection, and custody of the private assailant do not establish a custodial special relationship with a mother or her children. An alleged affirmative deprivation of otherwise available private protection remains analytically distinct from an omission of public protection; absence of custody of the threatened persons does not itself resolve that different claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale. Part I's separate appellate holding appears under Federal Courts, Qualified-immunity appeals and presentation.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a protection duty founded merely on official knowledge and assurances, but its discussion of increased vulnerability does not fully resolve this alleged affirmative course of conduct. The substantive-due-process inquiry concerns the official's own deprivation and the culpability attached to that deprivation, not simply the making of a statement. Daniels excludes mere negligence; the Court does not decide its reserved questions about recklessness or gross negligence. Pinder alleged that Johnson's assurance induced her to return to work rather than provide or arrange private protection. That favorable account must remain distinct from a general right to police protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court establishes no general constitutional duty to detain an assailant, warn a victim, or fulfill every protective promise. It does not hold that custody is indispensable to every affirmative-danger claim, or decide the constitutional merits of the assumed affirmative-deprivation theory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- DeShaney rejects knowledge-and-promise protection duties without deciding this entire affirmative-increased-vulnerability theory.
- Daniels excludes merely negligent deprivation while leaving its reserved culpability questions unresolved here.

##### Was Johnson's alleged conduct sufficiently established as unconstitutional by March 10, 1989 to defeat qualified immunity?

**Holding:** Qualified immunity turns on whether all relevant law existing when the officer acted made the particular conduct's unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable officer, without demanding a factual twin. On the favorable allegations here, law existing March 10, 1989 did not sufficiently establish that this noncoercive undertaking, release of the assailant, and uncorrected exposure constituted Johnson's own substantive-due-process deprivation; he therefore receives individual immunity on the reviewed claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II's notice standard joined by all nine Justices; Part III's application and affirmance joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, a five-Justice majority. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from the immunity disposition; their substantive theory is not controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not identical prior facts, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant law. DeShaney had rejected an omission-based duty while leaving the distinct affirmative theory incomplete. Bowers discussed active official wrongdoing but rejected release-based liability; Archie discussed obstruction of private aid in a materially different culpability setting and reached an adverse outcome; Jensen's discussion of a distinct danger did not preserve a knowledge-and-promise special relationship after DeShaney. Those sources did not sufficiently identify the assumed noncoercive interference here as the officer's own deprivation. A judge's disagreement, the number of judges on each side, or an officer's subjective belief in legality does not independently establish immunity. Later cases cannot retroactively supply the missing conduct-time notice.

**Material application:** The Court assumes that the assurance caused the mother to return to work and forgo alternative protection and that the remaining favorable allegations are true. It does not award immunity because actual reliance was absent, choose disputed facts against Pinder, or reject constitutional liability on the merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is notice-specific and claim-specific. Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets do not supply retroactive notice; Love establishes no mandatory merits-first sequence. Neither broader affirmative-danger liability nor the ultimate culpability and causation merits is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's reviewed individual-immunity judgment for Johnson is affirmed, 5-4. No new constitutional merits judgment or damages award is entered. Official-capacity and municipal claims, equal protection, section 1985, state wrongful-death and survival claims, and other excluded matters remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Anderson supplies the objective, particularized notice inquiry without an exact-facts requirement.
- Elder requires review of all relevant contemporaneous authority rather than an isolated citation or subjective belief.
- DeShaney supplies the rejection of a general protective duty but does not itself resolve the assumed affirmative-deprivation merits.
- Bowers, Archie and Jensen are considered as conduct-time circuit-law notice sources; their distinct facts, outcomes and culpability limits are preserved, and they are not converted into Supreme Court holdings.
- Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets cannot establish notice for conduct preceding those decisions; Love does not impose mandatory sequencing.

#### Fraternal Order of Police v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 97-5304; judgment below, 152 F.3d 998.
**Decided:** 1998-10-07.

##### Equal protection does not require extending the older exemption

**Holding:** Congress may withhold section 925(a)(1)'s governmental-use exception from the domestic-violence-misdemeanor supply and possession provisions while retaining that exception for the felony provision when the distinction rationally closes a practical gap in restrictions on access to weapons. The Fifth Amendment does not require Congress simultaneously to perfect every older exemption, and the challenged exclusion satisfies that rule on the developed official-use claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court.

##### Core-Preservation Review permits this conduct-based disability and official-use exclusion

**Holding:** The qualifying domestic-violence disability is a substantial regulation of particular persons under Wright's Core-Preservation Review and satisfies intermediate scrutiny on the developed official-use challenge. Keeping and bearing remain covered at Stage 1; at Stage 2, preventing armed recurrence is an important objective, the adjudicated violent-conduct predicate and conviction safeguards substantially connect the restriction to that objective, and the defined eligibility and relief limits constrain its burden; the personal right does not compel an official-use exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** *Love* recognizes personal protection, *Hale* distinguishes coverage from regulation, and *Rybar* rejects a setting-based denial of the right. Keeping or bearing does not lose protection merely because it occurs at work. There is, however, no independent fundamental entitlement to an armed public job. *Wright* measures justification by practical burden. This serious disability, confined by its adjudicated conduct predicate and statutory safeguards, is a substantial regulation of particular persons rather than incidental paperwork or a general abolition of ordinary defensive arms. The developed claim concerns access through the governmental channel for armed service; no individual's ordinary home-possession application is presented.

Intermediate scrutiny requires an important objective, a substantial relationship to it, and a restriction that does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate protected activity. Preventing renewed armed domestic violence is important. The force, attempted-force or threatened-deadly-weapon predicate within defined domestic relationships, counsel and jury-where-entitled protections, and statutory conviction-relief qualifications supply the substantial connection and constrain this conduct-based restriction. The classification rests on those limits, not merely on the availability of arms to other people. No particular claimant's access to relief is found.

The contrary argument confuses an individual right with immunity from a justified conduct-based restriction. A service weapon remains accessible to its possessor; official employment does not neutralize the risk established by qualifying conduct. Intermediate scrutiny is satisfied on the presented record, independently of ordinary equality review. This does not authorize disarmament for any misdemeanor, a mere allegation or an unrelated offense. An improperly classified conviction, an unavailable statutory safeguard or a genuinely different individual private-possession record remains open. Wright's prohibition of a general abolition of meaningful home possession remains intact; no public-carry licensing, training or recertification question is presented.

##### Remedy and questions not reached

**Holding:** Because the developed constitutional objections do not invalidate this exclusion, section 928 supplies no basis to extend the governmental-use exception. Reversal returns the case for a consistent judgment and any grounds that are both properly preserved and still undecided; it does not decide their preservation, revive an undeveloped theory, or automatically reinstate every District Court rationale.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the developed official-use controversy, not every privately held weapon of a public employee. No individual conviction-classification or restoration ruling, private-possession application, or unresolved commerce or commandeering ground is decided. Section 928 does not authorize extending an exemption the developed objections do not invalidate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns firearms and ammunition in the governmental channels defined by section 925(a)(1): transportation, shipment, receipt, possession or importation of items imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for the use of the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. That provision separately withholds its exception for sections 922(d)(9), 922(g)(9), and provisions concerning firearms prohibited by section 922(p). It is not an exemption for every privately held weapon of a public employee and is distinct from the machinegun exceptions in section 922(o)(2).

Section 922(d)(9)'s supply prohibition requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction. Section 922(g)(9)'s separate language addresses interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of an item previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Neither provision receives the other's elements by implication. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with a timely renewal application when required, during indictment and until any conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded administrative process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement for an individual in this case.

The qualifying misdemeanor must be under federal or state law and have an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. Counsel or a knowing and intelligent waiver is required. If the person was entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, there must have been a jury trial or a knowing and intelligent waiver by plea or otherwise; no new jury entitlement is created.

A conviction does not count when expunged or set aside, or when the person was pardoned or had civil rights restored if the jurisdiction provides for loss of civil rights for that offense, subject to the statute's express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court does not enlarge that wording or resolve its application to an individual. These are statutory limits on the rule, not newly imposed evidentiary procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Hunt* is applied to this uniform prospective associational claim; its three requirements remain distinct.
- *United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group* is applied for concrete member injury and the distinction between constitutional and prudential requirements; its statutory participation exception is not generalized.
- *Craig v. Boren* and *Barrows v. Jackson* support the directly regulated relationship for the officers' equality claim; no universal third-party standing rule follows.
- *FCC v. Beach Communications* and *Williamson v. Lee Optical* permit fitted incremental regulation; they do not make a generic public-safety assertion conclusive.
- *Heller v. Doe* retains both its actual proof-burden and family-participation holdings; its requirement of a rational relation to the challenged disadvantage is applied here.
- *Romer v. Evans* retains its protection against the unrelated blanket exclusion it addressed; this conduct-defined access restriction is distinguished by its actual regulatory connection.
- *Love* is applied for personal invocation and necessary acquisition; recognition of the holder supplies no unconditional official-use exemption.
- *Hale* is applied for the distinction between coverage and permissible regulation; its machinegun and registration holdings do not decide this different statute's commerce question.
- *Rybar* is applied for the rule that the setting alone does not extinguish personal invocation; its machinegun holding neither decides this disability nor orders automatic remand.
- *Wright* is applied through both stages of Core-Preservation Review and extended to restrictions on particular persons. The qualifying domestic-violence disability is a substantial regulation satisfying intermediate scrutiny on the developed official-use claim. Its prohibition of a general abolition of meaningful home possession and reservations concerning public-carry licensing, training and recertification retain their scope; no automatic remand or universal misdemeanor exception follows.

For the other questions in Fraternal Order of Police v. United States, decided 1998-10-07, D.C. Circuit No. 97-5304; judgment below, 152 F.3d 998., see Federal Courts.

#### United States v. Wright

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 95-8397; judgment below, 117 F.3d 1265. No separately established Supreme Court docket.
**Decided:** 1998-10-05.

##### Personal invocation does not depend on state-militia affiliation

**Holding:** Wright may invoke the personal arms guarantee without proving membership in, service to, or preparation for a militia actively maintained and trained by a State. Military usefulness neither excludes an arm from the guarantee nor gives an unrestricted exemption from regulation; the actual weapon, conduct and restriction must be considered.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this holding.

##### Core-Preservation Review governs restrictions on the personal arms right

**Holding:** Stage 1 asks whether the law interferes with protected keeping, bearing, acquisition, necessary ammunition or transportation of arms, with constitutional coverage determined from text, history and the Court's precedents. If protected activity is burdened, Stage 2 requires justification corresponding to the severity of the restriction's practical operation, subject to the independent categorical rule that government may not abolish meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home.

**Operative qualifications:** A core deprivation receives strict scrutiny: the government must establish a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and the absence of a comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative. A substantial regulation receives intermediate scrutiny: an important governmental objective, a substantial relationship between the regulation and that objective, and a restriction that does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate the protected activity. An incidental regulation imposing no meaningful interference receives deferential reasonableness review; arbitrary administration, discrimination and cumulative destruction of the right remain unconstitutional. Routine equipment specifications, administrative procedures and minor requirements qualify for that last category only when they do not materially obstruct the right. Actual operation controls: calling an effective prohibition a safety measure or administrative rule cannot reduce its level of review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg as well as its author, Stone-Zsela. These five adopt the entire framework, including each tier, the practical-operation rule and the categorical home boundary. The rule is direct majority law, not a synthesis of separate opinions.

**Material application:** Love identifies the personal holder, incorporates the guarantee and protects acquisition necessary to exercise it. Hale requires attention to the actual weapon and keeps coverage distinct from lawful regulation; military usefulness decides neither immunity nor exclusion. Rybar requires a genuine application instead of disposing of the claim through militia affiliation. Those decisions leave the measure of justification open. The Court now supplies that measure because a remand must identify how protected activity and permissible regulation relate. Text and history inform coverage and regulatory limits without creating a closed catalogue. A real individual guarantee requires protection against practical destruction, while regulation that leaves its exercise meaningfully available calls for a justification fitted to its actual burden. Public safety is important, but its invocation alone does not establish that fit. Conversely, recognizing a personal right does not subject every equipment rule or administrative step to strict scrutiny. Registration and a possession prohibition must be examined separately. The home boundary protects a meaningful core; it does not grant immunity to every weapon, person or use. Necessary ammunition and transportation receive protection as incidents of protected keeping or bearing, not through coverage of every item or transport method. That boundary, the graduated standard and the application to particular facts perform different functions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Prohibiting ordinary functional handguns in the home and effectively eliminating all lawful public carry illustrate core burdens; no particular public-carry law is before the Court. Public-carry licensing, safety instruction, examinations, demonstrated proficiency, refresher training and recertification, including their classification and justification, are expressly reserved. No universal numerical rule, militia exclusion, military-weapon immunity or comprehensive prohibited-arms list is adopted.

##### The machinegun-count affirmance requires both stages in the first instance

**Holding:** When the appellate affirmance of this machinegun conviction materially rests on the invalid affiliation requirement and supplies no adequate independent application of the personal arms guarantee to the actual weapons and restriction, that affirmance must be vacated for the preserved constitutional application. On remand, the lower courts must apply both stages of Core-Preservation Review in the first instance: determine constitutional coverage of the completed automatic weapon under Hale, address the disassembled weapon and its actual statutory branch where material, and then classify and justify any burden on protected activity by its practical operation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II-C, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, controls the direction to apply both stages of Core-Preservation Review. Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer also join the vacatur and bounded remand apart from that framework direction; the judgment is unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Only the appellate affirmance of the one section 922(o) count is vacated. The existing record may suffice; ordinary additional proceedings are permitted only as needed. The Court creates no new statutory element, second machinegun count or severance of the conditional plea, and does not reopen admitted possession. It orders no empirical study, hearing, new trial, acquittal, release or return of arms. The alternative jury-instruction request returns only insofar as it remains procedurally material under the preserved plea after lawful disposition of the arms claim; no particular instruction or trial is ordered. This is not an automatic-remand rule for every arms case.

##### The Ninth Amendment supplies no established independent exemption here

**Holding:** Invoking privacy and natural self-defense under the Ninth Amendment does not itself establish an independent constitutional immunity for this machinegun possession and destructive-device nonregistration. The asserted exemption fails without resolving the separately preserved Second Amendment application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Core-Preservation Review governs restrictions on protected activity. The tier and ultimate validity of this machinegun restriction remain undecided; no closed weapons catalogue or categorical exclusion of dangerous arms is adopted. The applicable statutory exceptions and registration limits remain intact. The Court orders no acquittal, release, return of arms, new trial, automatic hearing, acceptance adjustment or particular sentence; the machinegun count and sentence return on their distinct grounds. A reserved legal challenge does not immunize false factual denials or other conduct inconsistent with actual acceptance.

For the other questions in United States v. Wright, decided 1998-10-05, Eleventh Circuit No. 95-8397; judgment below, 117 F.3d 1265. No separately established Supreme Court docket., see Constitutional Structure, Criminal Procedure.

#### Village of Willowbrook v. Olech, 528 U.S. 562

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1288.

**Decided:** February 23, 2000.

##### Intentional irrational treatment of an individual may violate equal protection

**Holding:** An individual states an equal-protection claim by alleging intentional differential treatment from materially similarly situated persons without a rational basis for the distinction; membership in a separately identified group is unnecessary. Subjective ill will may be evidence of arbitrariness or pretext, but is not an independent element of this objectively irrational intentional-treatment claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight directly adopt this rule and the pleading application. Breyer concurs in the judgment on the additional allegation of vindictive action and does not join the objective rule's independent sufficiency.

**Material application:** The complaint's intentional demand for a 33-foot easement, compared with 15 feet for materially similar owners, together with alleged irrationality and arbitrariness, states the claim independently of spite. Alternative reckless-disregard allegations do not replace intent; later acceptance of 15 feet proves neither original irrationality nor liability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Negligence, ordinary error, discretionary disagreement, material differences and objectively rational distinctions do not suffice. The challenger must negate reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds; no contemporaneous official rationale, heightened scrutiny, reversed burden or evidence-pleading requirement is imposed. The alternative subjective-ill-will theory remains undecided. Intent, comparability, irrationality, liability and damages remain unproved; no general administrative tort or substantive-due-process right is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The pleading judgment is affirmed unanimously and the claim may proceed through ordinary adjudication. Breyer's judgment vote does not add a ninth join to the objective rule. No damages or injunction is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sioux City Bridge Co. v. Dakota County: applied for equal protection of an individual against intentional unequal administration, without requiring a separately defined group.
- Allegheny Pittsburgh Coal Co. v. County Commission: applied for deliberate disparate treatment of similarly situated persons; its principle is not limited to a showing of subjective spite.
- FCC v. Beach Communications: its reasonably conceivable legitimate-ground rule, challenger burden and rejection of compulsory formal findings remain.
- Heller v. Doe: applied for the rational relationship to the actual disadvantage; relevant material differences and ordinary government latitude remain distinct from irrational selection.
- Romer v. Evans: applied for objective evaluation of the imposed disadvantage without a required finding about every actor's subjective hostility; no heightened tier is added.

#### United States v. Emerson, judgment below 270 F.3d 203

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 99-10331; no Supreme Court docket is established.
**Decided:** 2002-06-17.

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-B and III, resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. Part II-A preserves the anti-evasion/material-impairment route; Part III requires material support for either mechanism.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the live-controversy holding in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

##### Whether officials may organize clergy-led worship at public secondary-school graduation

**Holding:** Public secondary-school officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request invocations and benedictions, guide the prayers' religious content and incorporate that worship into the official graduation program. **Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient sponsorship ground. Kennedy does not join Part II.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified school-organized graduation worship, independently of a separate coercion showing.

A desire for solemnity does not authorize the State to undertake religious presentation; the record does not establish that officials wrote or edited a complete prayer script.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not prohibit genuinely private prayer or private forum speech, replace the general Establishment Clause framework, or resolve adult legislative prayer, funding, denominational aid or universal accommodation questions. Lemon's purpose and entanglement prongs are not separately decided; broader views in Souter's concurrence are not additional Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit and preserve the prospective injunction against officials' authorizing or encouraging the challenged clergy-led graduation prayer practice. The injunction reaches the official program, not private prayer or genuinely private forum speech.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, are applied: nonsectarian language, voluntary attendance and permission to abstain do not cure official school worship. Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, is applied through its prohibition on a primary effect advancing religion, without a replacement test. Marsh v. Chambers, 463 U.S. 783, is distinguished as legislative prayer in a different setting; Board of Education of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, protects private religious equal-access activity rather than official sponsorship.

Formally optional attendance and the opportunity to stand or remain silent do not remove that pressure here; mere offense alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's concurrence in part and in the judgment, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient ground. It controls through direct majority support, not Marks. Stone does not join this writing.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains independently controlling for the stated school-directed secondary-school graduation practice and substantial participation pressure.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The absence of a disciplinary penalty does not eliminate the injury; a student need not protest publicly or miss the singular educational ceremony to avoid apparent participation. The ground does not equate disagreement or ordinary courtesy with coercion and leaves mature adult audiences and other accommodations open. It independently sustains the same limited prospective injunction. Engel and Schempp apply to this indirect religious pressure; Marsh is distinguished by the adult legislative setting and different freedom to leave. Lynch v. Donnelly, 465 U.S. 668, supplies the minimum prohibition on coerced religious support or participation without extending its display holding. County of Allegheny v. ACLU, 492 U.S. 573, is not reopened as to displays or every accommodation dispute.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

#### Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-948.
**Decided:** June 11, 1993.

##### Objective religious targeting defeats neutrality

**Holding:** A law burdening religious practice is not neutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive restriction, even if it avoids naming the religion. Hialeah’s interlocking scheme targets Santeria sacrifice on that objective showing; adverse impact alone and the words “ritual” or “sacrifice” alone do not establish the rule’s trigger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Parts II-A-1 and II-A-3; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fowler v. Rhode Island, 345 U.S. 67: discrimination in the treatment of religious exercise prohibited.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872: targeting falls outside the neutral-law rule; that rule is not reconsidered here.
- McDaniel v. Paty, 435 U.S. 618: special religious disabilities distinguished from neutral regulation.
- Bowen v. Roy, 476 U.S. 693: the individualized-exemption discussion supports requiring a compelling reason for denying religious hardship treatment where comparable secular reasons are assessed.

##### Substantial exemptions for comparable secular harm defeat general applicability

**Holding:** A law is not generally applicable where it substantially leaves unregulated comparable secular conduct threatening the interests invoked to burden religious exercise. Each challenged ordinance fails that minimum requirement here, including 87-72’s express state-law exemption for small-number hog or cattle operations; the Court does not define every boundary of general applicability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Smith, 494 U.S. 872: general-applicability requirement applied, without reopening its neutral-law holding.
- Fowler, 345 U.S. 67: unequal treatment of religious exercise applied.
- Hobbie v. Unemployment Appeals Commission, 480 U.S. 136: protection against unequal treatment of religious observers preserved.

##### Narrow tailoring independently fails for every ordinance

**Holding:** A restriction of religious practice that is nonneutral or not generally applicable must satisfy compelling-interest scrutiny and narrow tailoring. These ordinances fail narrow tailoring independently of whether the asserted ends could be compelling because comparable harms are left unregulated and neutral, narrower controls on disposal, care, killing methods and location could address the city’s concerns.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, narrow-tailoring ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wisconsin v. Yoder, 406 U.S. 205: rigorous justification of restrictions on religious exercise applied.
- McDaniel, 435 U.S. 618: demanding scrutiny of religious disabilities applied.
- Arkansas Writers’ Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S. 221: lack of narrow tailoring independently suffices under strict scrutiny.
- Schneider v. State, 308 U.S. 147: collateral public harms cannot justify unnecessary suppression when direct regulation is available.

##### The selectively pursued interests are not compelling in this application

**Independent alternative holding:** Where the government burdens protected religious conduct but leaves appreciable comparable harm unregulated despite feasible controls, it has not established that its asserted interest justifies the selective restriction at the highest constitutional level. Hialeah’s substantial underinclusion independently defeats its compelling-interest showing for this scheme; the decision does not declare animal welfare or public health inherently unimportant.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy’s Part III, compelling-interest ground; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Material application:** Ordinance87-40 incorporates Florida’s unnecessary-or-cruel-killing rule as construed to reject religious necessity while accepting comparable secular reasons. Ordinance87-52 prohibits owning, keeping, possessing, sacrificing or slaughtering enumerated animals or any other animal with intent to use it for food, and limits application to killing in any ritual regardless of whether flesh or blood will actually be consumed; its separate exception allows licensed establishments to slaughter animals specifically raised for food where properly zoned or permitted under state/local law and Agriculture Department rules. Ordinance87-71 defines sacrifice as unnecessary killing, tormenting, torturing or mutilating in a ritual or ceremony not primarily for food consumption and prohibits it citywide. Ordinance87-72 requires food slaughter in a properly zoned slaughterhouse meeting health, safety and sanitation codes, but separately exempts persons or organizations slaughtering or processing for sale small numbers of hogs or cattle weekly in accordance with the state-law exemption. The distinct exclusions, comparable secular harms and available neutral controls support the holdings; 87-72’s independent validity in another setting remains reserved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The enactment-history discussion lacks a majority. No exemption regime replaces Smith, no universal general-applicability definition is adopted, and exemption from a genuinely evenhanded anti-cruelty law remains undecided. Narrow tailoring and the compelling-interest failure independently sustain invalidation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for declaratory and appropriate injunctive relief against ordinances 87-40, 87-52, 87-71 and 87-72 as presented. No damages or immunity ruling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Florida Star v. B.J.F., 491 U.S. 524: substantial unregulated comparable harm undermines the asserted exceptional justification; no broader rule is imported from a separate writing.
- Yoder, 406 U.S. 205: the asserted interest must justify the particular restriction at the required level.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

#### Thompson v. Western States Medical Center, 535 U.S. 357

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-344.
**Decided:** April 29, 2002.

##### The challenged conditions burden truthful promotion more broadly than their justification supports

**Holding:** The prescription-solicitation and particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion conditions of section 503A violate the First Amendment as applied to truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding because the Government has not established a reasonable fit between their informational burden and its asserted distinction between individualized compounding and manufacturing subject to approval. The holding applies the ordinary commercial-speech framework; it neither requires the least restrictive imaginable measure nor prevents restrictions on misleading claims, unlawful transactions, actual manufacturing conduct or narrower commercial-speech measures supported by an adequate justification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. These six Justices adopt the complete fit holding, statutory explanation and affirmance. Thomas accepts this sufficient application without endorsing the framework as the full measure of constitutional protection. This is a directly shared rationale, not an aggregation of distinct advancement findings.

**Material application:** The Court assumes the Government's combined substantial-interest and material-advancement account and finds inadequate fit; it establishes no separate advancement failure. Available production, distribution, patient-prescription and commercial-copy controls explain the mismatch without selecting a cap or finding every alternative equally effective. Truthful physician-directed information is within the six-Justice holding; the three dissenters reserve that component rather than vote to sustain it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Licensed pharmacies prepared promotional materials for distribution by mail and at medical conferences. The challenge concerns truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding. Section 503A(a) permits the identified patient's prescription through unsolicited receipt or a prescribing practitioner's approved notation that a compounded product is necessary for that patient. The ordinary route requires compounding by a licensed pharmacist in a State-licensed pharmacy or Federal facility, or by a licensed physician, on the identified patient's prescription from a licensed physician or another practitioner authorized by State law to prescribe. The separate advance-compounding route permits a licensed pharmacist or physician to prepare limited quantities before receiving the patient's valid prescription, based on a history of receiving valid prescriptions for that drug generated solely within an established relationship between the compounder and either that identified patient or the physician or other practitioner who will write the prescription. Section 503A(c) prohibits particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion while permitting promotion of compounding services; the necessity notation does not exempt a message from subsection (c).

Section 503A supplies only exemptions from section 501(a)(2)(B)'s manufacturing-practice requirement, section 502(f)(1)'s adequate-directions requirement and section 505's new-drug-approval requirement. Bulk drug substances must comply with an applicable United States Pharmacopoeia or National Formulary monograph and the United States Pharmacopoeia compounding chapter; if no monograph exists, they must be components of approved drugs or, if neither condition is met, appear on the Secretary's regulatory list. They must be manufactured by a registered establishment, including a foreign establishment registered under section 510(i), and accompanied by valid certificates of analysis. Nonbulk ingredients must comply with an applicable monograph, if one exists, and the compounding chapter.

The compounder may not use a drug product on the Secretary's published list of products withdrawn or removed because the product or a component was found unsafe or ineffective. Regular compounding, or compounding in inordinate amounts as defined by the Secretary, of essentially commercially available copies is barred; a change for an identified patient producing a significant difference determined by the prescribing practitioner is excluded from that copy category. The product must not be one identified by regulation as presenting demonstrable compounding difficulties reasonably demonstrating an adverse effect on safety or effectiveness.

The interstate alternatives are compounding in a State that has entered a memorandum with the Secretary addressing inordinate interstate distribution and providing for appropriate State-agency investigation of complaints about compounded products distributed outside that State, or, absent that memorandum, out-of-State distribution no greater than five percent of the pharmacy's or physician's total prescription orders dispensed or distributed. The statute contains no enacted twenty-percent memorandum ceiling. PET compounded drugs and radiopharmaceuticals are excluded. Mixing, reconstitution and other acts performed according to the manufacturer's approved labeling and other directions consistent with that labeling lie outside the statutory compounding definition. These distinct provisions are not interchangeable exceptions to the challenged promotion rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is affirmed, including its unreviewed determination that §353a is nonseverable. The Court creates no operating exemption with the speech conditions removed and no replacement compounding program.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### III. The Court's present change supplies the legal change required for prospective relief

**Holding:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join the present-change rule and its distinction between this continuing decree and a completed judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Denial of Rule 60(b)(5) relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with its governing legal premises reviewed as questions of law. Rufo requires a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable and a modification fitted to that change. The legal change here is the Court's ruling today. It is not the expectation that the Court might reconsider Aguilar, an increase in the expense of compliance, or a supposed earlier overruling. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest decided antecedent statutory and regulatory questions without deciding establishment. Rosenberger's actual printing benefit and Pinette and Chabad's private-expression holdings did not authorize this public teaching program. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply the directly governing rule.

The objection that Rule 60 review cannot become an untimely rehearing of the original judgment does not bar this relief. The motion addresses the decree's continuing prospective operation, and the preserved question presents the constitutional rule supporting that operation. The Court changes that rule expressly; it does not hold that the district court should have anticipated the change. Requiring a new motion solely to invoke today's decision would preserve a now-rejected prohibition without a remaining legal reason.

Rufo's limits remain. Dissatisfaction, a cheaper lawful alternative and anticipated doctrinal change are insufficient. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. A clarification of constitutional permission does not automatically erase a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation. This injunction enforces an adjudicated constitutional prohibition; the record establishes no negotiated promise independently requiring that prohibition. The Court neither reopens completed claims nor finds an independently sufficient factual change in costs.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail is applied to the present legal change and retained in its significant-change, accepted-risk and tailored-relief requirements. Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc. is applied to preserve lower-court obedience to direct precedent. Browder v. Director, Department of Corrections does not foreclose adjustment of continuing relief on the basis of the legal change actually adopted here; its limit on using a Rule 60 appeal to reopen the underlying completed judgment remains.

**Holding:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any remaining obligation must have an identified lawful basis, and any further proceedings must concern a specified material issue under the surviving law rather than require proof that future misconduct is impossible.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join this tailored relief and its public-control limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo connects modification to the proven change while preserving the lawful purposes of continuing relief. The change today eliminates the categorical site-and-monitoring ground. The district court therefore cannot simply reinstate that same ground, presume that public service and religious instruction are inseparable, or treat ordinary secular supervision as itself forbidden. It must remove the prohibition to that extent. Supported questions about actual religious instruction, diversion, religious control of public employees or another independent legal prohibition remain subject to ordinary procedure and the governing law; hypothetical misconduct does not justify preserving Aguilar's rejected inference.

No additional negotiated obligation is presumed. If an independent statutory, contractual or decree obligation is established, constitutional permission does not by itself extinguish it. Existing lawful services may continue while the mandate is implemented promptly. Children currently receiving those services should continue to receive them during implementation, with existing statutory duties preserved and no new service entitlement created. Any implementation period must address identified tasks and circumstances; the Court sets no number of days, and administrative delay cannot indefinitely preserve the rejected categorical prohibition. The district court modifies the decree under the mandate. Until it acts, the City must obey the operative injunction.

The holding permits the described program; it does not command onsite delivery, create an unconditional funding entitlement, approve a replacement program, award damages or repayment, cancel contracts, or excuse past disobedience. It makes no finding that every present implementation satisfies the required secular controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rufo's suitable-tailoring requirement controls implementation and preserves any independently lawful obligation. Its consent-decree rule is not converted into a presumption that this adjudicated injunction contains a voluntary stricter undertaking.

## Standards and Tests selection: Lemon test

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement do not cure public payment for demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation. The inquiry concerns the instructional function purchased; religious identity alone does not establish the defect, and genuinely independent private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

Actual financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control as the funded instructional function requires correction; neutral allocation, public title or a secular label cannot cure it. Religious affiliation or location, control, fungibility, possible diversion and incidental savings, singly or accumulated, do not themselves prove forbidden financing. Reliably lawful secular use or an independently protected benefit cannot be condemned categorically because the object is equipment or a library book. Meek and Wolman lose force only to the extent their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns that relationship by recipient character or presumed diversion; unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain. Effective correction must reach proved prohibited use while preserving independently lawful applications; a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official school worship and independent religious pressure

### Official school worship and independent religious pressure

**Governing question:** Officially organized school worship, delegated representative religious choices and independently coercive school prayer.

**Current rule:** Officials violate the Establishment Clause by selecting clergy, requesting invocations and benedictions, guiding their religious content and incorporating the worship into the official ceremony. Nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure that sponsorship; no separate punishment or quantified-pressure showing is required for this ground. Independently, school-directed clergy prayer is forbidden when the school's control and the occasion's importance place objecting adolescents under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the ceremony. Optional attendance and standing or remaining silent do not necessarily remove that pressure; mere offense is insufficient. The official-sponsorship prohibition also applies when a school organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event but uses student initiation or delivery or a statutory disclaimer. Those features alone do not make the worship private, and the sponsorship ground remains independent of quantified pressure. The official-worship prohibition also reaches a public university’s selection of a religious leader and incorporation of actual invocation and benediction prayers into commencement. Mature attendance, meaningful freedom to abstain, brevity, nonsectarian language and a sincere secular ceremonial purpose do not cure that official worship; independent clergy composition does not make it private forum speech. Separately, actual pressure to participate in school-directed worship in compulsory or comparably pressured settings violates the Establishment Clause; offense at a genuinely private viewpoint alone does not suffice.

A school may not make an invocation an official representative component of its own ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that component includes religious exercise. Such a mechanism is not transformed into private access by student authorship, optional attendance, possible silence or secular speech, or nonsectarian/nonproselytizing limits. The defect does not require a prayer already delivered or official editing of its words. Genuinely voluntary private prayer, independently chosen student expression, lawful secular messages and neutral private access remain protected; the holding adds no separate religious-pressure test.

## Standards and Tests selection: Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

### Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

**Governing question:** What instructions govern religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny?

**Current rule:** A law is nonneutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive burdens even without naming the religion; adverse impact or the words ritual or sacrifice alone do not establish that trigger. Substantially leaving comparable secular conduct unregulated despite its threat to the asserted interests defeats general applicability at least to that extent. Nonneutral or nongeneral restrictions require compelling interests and narrow tailoring. Appreciable comparable harm left unregulated despite feasible controls defeats the selective compelling-interest showing, and available neutral narrower controls and underinclusion can independently defeat tailoring. Smith is not replaced by a general exemption regime; all boundaries of general applicability and exemption from genuinely general anti-cruelty laws remain open. The enactment-history plurality supplies no additional controlling test. A public-employment restriction alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must receive its own neutrality/general-applicability inquiry rather than be absorbed into an employee-speech balance. Religious impact or sincerity alone does not establish targeting; lack of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime, or when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

## Standards and Tests selection: Personal arms protection, incorporation and necessary acquisition

## Standards and Tests selection: Core-Preservation Review

### Core-Preservation Review

**Governing question:** Whether a restriction burdens constitutionally protected arms activity and what justification its practical operation requires.

**Current rule:** Stage 1 asks whether the law interferes with protected keeping, bearing, acquisition, necessary ammunition or transportation of arms, with constitutional coverage determined from text, history and the Court's precedents. If protected activity is burdened, Stage 2 requires justification corresponding to the severity of the restriction's practical operation, subject to the independent categorical rule that government may not abolish meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home.

A core deprivation receives strict scrutiny: the government must establish a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and the absence of a comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative. A substantial regulation receives intermediate scrutiny: an important governmental objective, a substantial relationship between the regulation and that objective, and a restriction that does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate the protected activity. An incidental regulation imposing no meaningful interference receives deferential reasonableness review; arbitrary administration, discrimination and cumulative destruction of the right remain unconstitutional. Routine equipment specifications, administrative procedures and minor requirements qualify for that last category only when they do not materially obstruct the right. Actual operation controls: calling an effective prohibition a safety measure or administrative rule cannot reduce its level of review.

**Exceptions and limits:** The home boundary protects a meaningful core; it does not grant immunity to every weapon, person or use. Necessary ammunition and transportation receive protection as incidents of protected keeping or bearing, not through coverage of every item or transport method. That boundary, the graduated standard and the application to particular facts perform different functions.

**Application to particular persons:** The qualifying domestic-violence disability is a substantial regulation of particular persons under Wright's Core-Preservation Review and satisfies intermediate scrutiny on the developed official-use challenge. Keeping and bearing remain covered at Stage 1; at Stage 2, preventing armed recurrence is an important objective, the adjudicated violent-conduct predicate and conviction safeguards substantially connect the restriction to that objective, and the defined eligibility and relief limits constrain its burden; the personal right does not compel an official-use exception.

*Love* recognizes personal protection, *Hale* distinguishes coverage from regulation, and *Rybar* rejects a setting-based denial of the right. Keeping or bearing does not lose protection merely because it occurs at work. There is, however, no independent fundamental entitlement to an armed public job. *Wright* measures justification by practical burden. This serious disability, confined by its adjudicated conduct predicate and statutory safeguards, is a substantial regulation of particular persons rather than incidental paperwork or a general abolition of ordinary defensive arms. The developed claim concerns access through the governmental channel for armed service; no individual's ordinary home-possession application is presented.

The contrary argument confuses an individual right with immunity from a justified conduct-based restriction. A service weapon remains accessible to its possessor; official employment does not neutralize the risk established by qualifying conduct. Intermediate scrutiny is satisfied on the presented record, independently of ordinary equality review. This does not authorize disarmament for any misdemeanor, a mere allegation or an unrelated offense. An improperly classified conviction, an unavailable statutory safeguard or a genuinely different individual private-possession record remains open. Wright's prohibition of a general abolition of meaningful home possession remains intact; no public-carry licensing, training or recertification question is presented.

**Statutory scope of the official-use application:** The holding concerns firearms and ammunition in the governmental channels defined by section 925(a)(1): transportation, shipment, receipt, possession or importation of items imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for the use of the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. That provision separately withholds its exception for sections 922(d)(9), 922(g)(9), and provisions concerning firearms prohibited by section 922(p). It is not an exemption for every privately held weapon of a public employee and is distinct from the machinegun exceptions in section 922(o)(2).

Section 922(d)(9)'s supply prohibition requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction. Section 922(g)(9)'s separate language addresses interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of an item previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Neither provision receives the other's elements by implication. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with a timely renewal application when required, during indictment and until any conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded administrative process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement for an individual in this case.

The qualifying misdemeanor must be under federal or state law and have an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. Counsel or a knowing and intelligent waiver is required. If the person was entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, there must have been a jury trial or a knowing and intelligent waiver by plea or otherwise; no new jury entitlement is created.

A conviction does not count when expunged or set aside, or when the person was pardoned or had civil rights restored if the jurisdiction provides for loss of civil rights for that offense, subject to the statute's express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court does not enlarge that wording or resolve its application to an individual. These are statutory limits on the rule, not newly imposed evidentiary procedures.

A civil protective order can support a preventive disability without a criminal conviction when adjudicated domestic violence and recurrence risk, actual hearing notice and an opportunity to participate supply the danger predicate. The order-dependent restriction on possession of an ordinary pistol and necessary ammunition is a substantial regulation satisfying intermediate scrutiny on those predicates. Its fit rests on adjudicated conduct, danger and the operative order, not merely a safety label, nominal time limit or continued access for other people. Disarmament based on an allegation, ex parte process or an unrelated order is not approved.

Section 922(g)(8) independently requires hearing notice and opportunity to participate, a qualifying restraint on harassment, stalking, threats or conduct putting the intimate partner in reasonable fear of bodily injury to that partner or a child of the partner or restrained person, and either a credible physical-safety-threat finding under (C)(i) or an express prohibition on using, attempting or threatening force reasonably expected to cause bodily injury under (C)(ii). These alternatives are distinct; a finding of violence and possible recurrence is not automatically the statutory credible-threat finding.

A force-prohibiting domestic-relations order may also supply the civil danger predicate without completed assault or an express credible-threat formula when actual notice and meaningful opportunity to contest specific violent-threat evidence, a governing requirement of real nonspeculative protective necessity, and an order directed to that threatened injury support the restriction. The federal disability must depend on the operative order. On those predicates the individual disqualification is a substantial regulation satisfying intermediate scrutiny; a safety label, temporary duration or continuing access for other people alone supplies neither fit nor constitutional permission. Ordinary collateral relitigation of the order remains distinct from constitutional review of the additional federal burden. Statutory compliance alone does not conclusively establish constitutionality.

## Standards and Tests selection: Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification

### Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification

**Governing question:** What Pennsylvania may require patients to be told, offered or asked to certify in obtaining informed consent, including independent First Amendment limits.

**Current rule:** Section 3205(a)(1) may require accurate, medically material information about the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks. The three §3205(a)(2) notices may state truthfully: state materials concerning fetal development and alternatives are available and a free copy will be supplied if chosen; medical assistance for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care may be available; and the father is liable to assist in support even if he offered to pay for abortion. Assistance and legal liability are not promises of eligibility, payment or collection. Paternal-support information **may** be omitted in a rape pregnancy, including spousal rape, without a police-report condition; omission is permitted, not required, and changes no support obligation. Section 3208 publication and §3205(a)(3) chosen receipt are permissible at their objective, accurate, nonjudgmental scope. Notice of availability is distinct from receipt: patients need not accept, read or endorse materials or listed agencies. Clinicians may correct or criticize them. Section 3205(a)(4) certification may record actual receipt of lawful information, not ideological agreement, unchosen reading or compliance with an invalid prerequisite. The §3205(c) disclosure-health defense remains available on its own terms.

The First Amendment independently permits these bounded medical and ancillary disclosures, voluntary receipt and factual documentation. Payment does not strip professional speech of protection, and truth alone does not justify every compulsory statement. False, irrelevant, coercive or ideological scripts and materially different implementation remain outside the sustained duties. Accurate content does not establish the validity of a timing or exclusive-speaker condition; invalid timing and delivery obligations cannot survive through a certificate.

**Authority by component:**

- Medical content, objective publication/chosen receipt and basic certification: Casey, “Accurate material medical information,” “Statutory publication and chosen receipt of materials,” and “Certification of lawful receipt”, June 29, 1992; unanimous direct holdings at those scopes.
- Each ancillary notice and its qualification: *Casey*, “Notice of available free materials,” “Notice of potentially available medical assistance,” and “Paternal-support notice and the rape omission permission”; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter supply five rationale joins. The additional judgment votes do not broaden that rationale.
- Independent speech rules: *Casey*, the three “Independent speech challenge” holdings; Souter with White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas for medical content, chosen materials and basic certification; the same group without Blackmun for ancillary notices. Stone's judgment-only agreements supply no speech rationale.
- Dependent timing and delivery links: *Casey*, “Certification of lawful receipt,” with the five-Justice invalidation grounds in “Physician delivery of medical information” and “The mandatory 24-hour interval.”

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rape omission permission has no §3209/§3128 reporting or 90-day condition. No finding establishes universal accuracy of actual publications or agencies. No general commercial-speech classification or professional-speech immunity is adopted. Required content remains subject to the separate access framework.

**Material development:** Akron and Thornburgh are qualified only insofar as they categorically prohibit the sustained concise, accurate ancillary notices because they may encourage childbirth; their protection against misleading, coercive and unjustifiably burdensome implementation remains.
