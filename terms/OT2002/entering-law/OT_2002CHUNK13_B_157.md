#### Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, 510 U.S. 487

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1223.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration

**Holding:** Section 7114(b)(4)'s duty to supply qualifying bargaining data operates only to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law; when the asserted exception to the Privacy Act is §552a(b)(2), the question is whether FOIA itself requires disclosure. Retained Reporters Committee precedent governs the ordinary third-party public-interest inquiry, so collective-bargaining utility alone does not enlarge the incorporated FOIA balance; the Court does not decide that this precedent's restriction was inevitably compelled by FOIA's original text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this statutory coordination and precedent-based explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The labor duty concerns requested data normally maintained by the agency in its regular course of business, reasonably available and necessary for full and proper discussion, understanding and negotiation of subjects within collective bargaining, and not guidance, advice, counsel or training provided for management officials or supervisors relating to collective bargaining. These conditions are not independently disputed or newly adjudicated here. For a record contained in a system of records, §552a(b) bars agency disclosure absent a written request by, or prior written consent of, the individual to whom it pertains, or an applicable enumerated exception. A union request is not the subject individual's request or consent. Routine use under §552a(b)(3) and other exceptions are distinct and undecided; their applicability is not guaranteed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: retained and applied as statutory precedent for ordinary third-party public-interest analysis and requester neutrality; its original-text justification is not newly endorsed as inevitable, and requester identity can still matter when disclosure is opposed on a privilege ground and the requester is the person protected by that privilege, a situation absent here.
- Department of the Treasury, IRS v. FLRA: applied for respecting enacted limits on labor-agency authority; the uncontested bargaining-necessity determination is not reconsidered.
- Department of State v. Ray: preserved; the incorporated Exemption 6 inquiry remains a concrete balance, not a rule that another statute's useful purpose automatically establishes FOIA-required disclosure.

##### The added home addresses do not justify disclosure on this record

**Holding:** On these requests, official disclosure of employee home addresses would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy under Exemption 6 because the real interest in controlling dissemination of home locations outweighs the absent or negligible demonstrated addition to public understanding after names and work stations were supplied. Exemption 6 permits withholding and FOIA therefore does not require disclosure; the Privacy Act supplies the resulting prohibition against compelling release through the invoked §552a(b)(2) route, without deciding a different demonstrated transparency use, routine use, subject consent or another exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same record-specific balance and reversal of both enforcement components.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exemption 6 applies to personnel, medical and similar files whose disclosure would constitute a clearly unwarranted invasion of personal privacy; the requested employee-address records fall within that category. Exemption 7(C) instead concerns information compiled for law-enforcement purposes and disclosures that could reasonably be expected to constitute an unwarranted invasion; that different standard is not used here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Department of State v. Ray: applied for concrete privacy injury and demonstrated incremental public understanding; its confidentiality assurance is not a prerequisite or automatic exemption, and no general bar to derivative benefits or prior-wrongdoing requirement is added.
- Department of the Air Force v. Rose: applied; disclosure remains the general rule, with the enacted Exemption 6 privacy balance preserved.
- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press: applied to the relevant public interest and continued control over officially disseminated personal information despite scattered public availability; its rap-sheet sensitivity and Exemption 7(C) threshold do not replace the different record or Exemption 6 standard here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Enforcement of both the Gulfport and Lowry disclosure orders is reversed on the asserted §552a(b)(2) ground. No new necessity determination, constitutional privacy remedy or approval of a routine use is ordered; other disclosure routes must meet their own requirements.

#### J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation and Lance E. Lindblom v. Federal Bureau of Investigation

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 95-5386; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1998-02-13.

For statutory Article III standing, the authorized agency action and sovereign consent, and constitutional standing and dismissal form, see J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation and Lance E. Lindblom v. Federal Bureau of Investigation, D.C. Circuit No. 95-5386; no separate Supreme Court docket stated, decided 1998-02-13, Federal Courts — Privacy Act standing and jurisdictional dismissal.

##### The protected personal retention invasion can itself be an adverse effect

**Holding:** Actual unwanted maintenance of identified records describing this individual's protected associations, if outside §552a(e)(7)'s lawful alternatives, can itself supply the adverse personal effect required by §552a(g)(1)(D). A merely abstract or technical violation with no adverse personal consequence is insufficient; an adverse agency determination, public disclosure, lost employment or pecuniary loss is not independently required as such.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II, joined by Breyer, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas reserve this construction; Stone-Zsela does not join it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The personal adverse effect is not a finding of distress or actual damages, nor is every statutory violation sufficient. No actual nonpertinence is found.

##### Continued retention must satisfy the complete Continuing Pertinence Test

**Holding:** Section 552a(e)(7) regulates continued maintenance as well as original collection. Unless retention is expressly authorized by statute or by the individual, the information must remain pertinent to and within the scope of an authorized law-enforcement activity; lawful original collection is not conclusive permission to retain it after every qualifying connection has ceased. The Court adopts the following complete rule:

1. **Does the challenged record fall within the statutory protection?**

   The plaintiff identifies records describing their First Amendment activities and establishes that the government continues to maintain them. The court first checks whether retention is independently authorized by statute or by the individual.

2. **Can the government identify an authorized law-enforcement activity?**

   The agency must establish the legal authority for that activity. It need not prove the plaintiff committed a crime. Intelligence investigations, criminal investigations, and authorized civil enforcement may qualify.

3. **Does the information remain pertinent to that activity?**

   The court examines the actual relationship, not a hypothetical future possibility. Relevant considerations include:

   - Why the information was collected.
   - What investigative purpose it still serves.
   - Whether it preserves necessary context or evidence.
   - Whether subsequent events have changed its relevance.
   - Whether continued use or indexing exceeds the justified investigative scope.

   No single consideration automatically decides the case.

4. **What disposition and remedy does the law authorize?**

   If a qualifying connection remains, the agency may retain the information under the statutory exception. If no qualifying connection remains, the court determines the violation and available relief under the Privacy Act.

   The court separately accounts for lawful evidence-preservation and federal records-management duties.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III, joined by Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Each joins the entire four-step rule, including every listed consideration and the separate preservation/relief requirements. Scalia and Thomas reject the complete rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Authorized activity may include genuine historical intelligence value, connected or later inquiries and necessary evidentiary context; no active investigation of the plaintiff is required. General agency mission or hypothetical future usefulness is insufficient by itself. Age, closure, innocence and name retrieval do not control. The considerations are nonexclusive and impose no necessity or least-restrictive-recordkeeping test, periodic audit, deadline, universal consultation or disclosure of classified reasons.

##### The statutory judgment requires record-specific further proceedings

**Holding:** The conclusive original-collection rule cannot sustain summary judgment on Lindblom's retention claim. That component is vacated and remanded for the complete test's application to the actual records, with any independent statutory or individual authorization and lawful preservation obligation considered separately; the Court awards neither expungement nor damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V-A has Breyer, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia and Thomas oppose statutory remand.

##### Each remedial route retains its independent requirements

**Holding:** The retention restriction does not create unlimited relief: monetary liability under §552a(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4), amendment relief under (d)/(g)(1)(A)/(g)(2), access relief, and any independently permissible prospective equity retain their distinct conditions. Neither an automatic monetary award nor a general expungement entitlement follows from the Court's construction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion: Part V-B has all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees that no final award issues and joins Part V-A's separate lawful-relief and preservation directions, without adopting V-B's additional grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Money retains intentional/willful noncompliance, causally compensable actual damages and statutory-minimum questions. Amendment relief retains request, refusal, review and exemption predicates; exhausted access requests do not establish amendment exhaustion. Sections (j)/(k) cannot exempt (e)(7), though a valid (d) exemption may defeat derivative amendment relief. Prospective equity retains a lawful action, preclusion and ordinary limits; no separate APA claim is created. If authorized, a mandatory §702 decree must identify the responsible officer or successor by name or title.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate Lindblom’s statutory summary judgment and remand, 7–2, under the complete test; sustain both constitutional standing rejections and remove their with-prejudice effect unanimously. No damages, expungement, discovery reversal, new hearing, agency audit or destruction is ordered. Original collection and unreviewed components remain untouched.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Steel Co. (June 2, 1997):** Applied for constitutional power before disputed statutory merits and the distinction between substantial claims and recovery; its fully corrected reporting default does not decide this ongoing personal-custody controversy. Its confined already-controlled-merits exceptions are not used.
- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to the substantial federal claim; preserved as neither a sovereign waiver nor permission to assume personal injury.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Applied for concrete personal injury, direct traceability, likely redress and summary-judgment evidence; no particular-document or additional downstream-loss requirement is added.
- **Warth v. Seldin; Havens Realty Corp. v. Coleman:** Applied for actual personal statutory interests and the distinction from abstract illegality; neither is treated as deciding Privacy Act remedies or its adverse-effect text.
- **Arizonans for Official English:** Applied to exclude legally impossible remedies and to permit limited jurisdictional correction; no magic-word damages rule is inferred.
- **Lee v. Harcleroad:** Applied to claim-specific proof and jurisdictional dismissal; its unsupported future patient injury does not erase an established personal statutory occurrence.
- **Alexander v. Whitman:** Applied for meaningful partial redress despite uncertain ultimate recovery, preserving procedural-stage and defendant-specific limits; its controlled-merits setting is not extended.
- **FDIC v. Meyer:** Applied to distinguish sovereign consent, an authorized action and relief; its ban on direct agency Bivens damages remains intact.
- **Department of Defense v. FLRA:** Applied to the distinct Privacy Act disclosure protection and enumerated exceptions; neither its disclosure balance nor §(b)(7) is substituted for retention's three alternatives.
- **Laird v. Tatum; Meese v. Keene:** Applied respectively to subjective constitutional chill and a supported governmental mechanism for reputational injury; neither categorically excludes the personal statutory interest decided here.
- **Chamber of Commerce v. Reich:** Preserved for qualified prospective equitable restraint; no automatic bypass of the Privacy Act's remedial scheme is adopted.

The circuit decisions Albright, Doe v. FBI, Nagel and Clarkson inform the distinct statutory elements and remedial routes at their actual scope. Albright's damages questions and Doe's case-specific adverse-effect finding are not imported as findings here. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

#### J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation and Lance E. Lindblom v. Federal Bureau of Investigation

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 95-5386; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1998-02-13.

For continued-retention duties, adverse effect and statutory remedies and the case-wide mandate, see J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation and Lance E. Lindblom v. Federal Bureau of Investigation, D.C. Circuit No. 95-5386; no separate Supreme Court docket stated, decided 1998-02-13, Administrative Law — Privacy Act retention and continuing pertinence.

##### A live personal statutory controversy and an express statutory action permit merits review

**Holding:** Actual continued custody of identified records describing this claimant's protected associations supplies a concrete personal information interest directly traceable to the retaining agency; an actually requested award within §552a(g)(1)(D)/(g)(4)'s legally available monetary category can meaningfully redress the claimed invasion if its requirements are established. Jurisdiction does not depend on first proving the disputed retention violation or final damages entitlement, and the substantial statutory claim falls within the Act's express agency action and conditional sovereign consent without creating a new constitutional damages cause.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion: Part I, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees in reaching the merits without joining this rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Act’s express agency action and conditional monetary consent supply the route; §1331 is not a waiver, §702 supplies no money and no agency Bivens cause is created. Actual monetary relief was requested, but culpability, adverse effect and entitlement remain separate.

##### The separate constitutional claims lack the required supported connection

**Holding:** The Foundation's affidavits do not establish the causal mechanism connecting FBI retention to prospective grantees' or employers' knowledge and reactions; Lindblom's separately presented constitutional claim supplies no independent supported basis beyond that failed derivative theory. Both constitutional-standing rejections therefore stand, without a ruling on the First Amendment merits or a categorical exclusion of reputational or associational injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion: Part IV-A has all nine Justices. Scalia and Thomas's additional injury/imminence ground is separate and noncontrolling.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Meese’s supported public-label mechanism is absent. No categorical bar on reputational or associational injury is adopted; the actual personal statutory custody claim is distinct from subjective constitutional chill or the unsupported audience-exposure mechanism.

##### The constitutional dismissals must be without prejudice

**Holding:** A dismissal of these constitutional claims for want of Article III standing cannot carry a merits-preclusive effect. The standing rejections are sustained, but the district judgment's with-prejudice effect is vacated and the case is remanded to conform those dismissals to their jurisdictional ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion: Part IV-B has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees in the disposition only. The correction is unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Steel Co. and Bell v. Hood distinguish constitutional jurisdiction from disputed statutory merits and ultimate recovery. Lujan, Warth and Havens require a concrete personal interest with stage-appropriate proof; Meyer requires a separate authorized action and sovereign consent. Laird and Meese preserve the distinction between subjective chill and a supported mechanism for reputational harm. Arizonans and Lee permit corrective jurisdiction and require the constitutional dismissals to remain without merits prejudice; Alexander preserves meaningful partial redress without relaxing the procedural-stage requirement.

#### Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-974.
**Decided:** March 3, 1997.

##### Whether this former employee retains a live challenge to the employment rule

**Holding:** An individual prospective employment-rule challenge becomes moot when the employee voluntarily leaves covered employment without an intention to return, realistic recurrence or a surviving claim. A general relief request cannot manufacture nominal damages against a State or official-capacity defendant that is not a damages person under §1983.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous; all nine Justices join every holding.

**Material application:** No class or replacement claimant remained, and the dismissed individual-capacity claims were not preserved on appeal. State nonpersonhood under §1983 is a statutory bar distinct from waivable Eleventh Amendment immunity; a proposed consensual payment cannot supply the missing cause of action. The Attorney General’s constitutional participation under §2403(b) did not overturn the denial of State party status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general requirement to plead nominal damages by name is established. A legally available individual-capacity claim presents a different question. The Court does not fix the precise April 1990 date when mootness attached.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Preiser requires a controversy throughout review. Will and Hafer distinguish the unavailable State or official-capacity damages claim from genuine individual-capacity liability.

##### Whether limited corrective jurisdiction permits vacatur without deciding sponsor standing or constitutional merits

**Holding:** The Court may correct a lower federal court’s jurisdictional error without exercising constitutional merits jurisdiction. On the equities here, that authority requires vacating the appellate judgment and directing vacatur of the District Court declaration and dismissal for mootness, without resolving ultimate initiative-sponsor standing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The prevailing plaintiff ended the covered employment during review efforts; this was not settlement or abandonment by a reviewing loser. The later appellate merits adjudication and absence of a lawful damages residue justify clearing both unreviewable constitutional judgments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2106 does not independently create merits jurisdiction. Intervention, an appeal notice or political commitment does not confer automatic sponsor standing. The original declaration is not declared void when entered, and the Court resolves neither every intervention ruling nor Article XXVIII’s validity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate both merits judgments and dismiss the action as moot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bender and Corrick supply limited correction of jurisdictional error. Lujan and Brown Group retain constitutional injury and lawful representation requirements. Bancorp requires equitable rather than automatic vacatur; Munsingwear and Anderson support judgment clearing within that inquiry.

##### Whether state-law certification can revive a moot federal action

**Holding:** Certification of unsettled state law can assist a live federal controversy but cannot cure the absence of Article III jurisdiction. No certification is ordered in this moot case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The meaning of Article XXVIII and any constitutional challenge in future live litigation remain open. No mandatory certification requirement is adopted.

#### Major League Baseball Players Association v. Garvey, 532 U.S. 504

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-1210.
**Decided:** May 14, 2001.

##### Whether a timely petition from the later judgment permits review of its necessary antecedent ruling

**Holding:** A timely petition seeking review of the later judgment in the same litigation allows review of an antecedent ruling necessary to that judgment. The earlier mandate binds the district court but does not shield its predicate from this Court's proper later review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Stevens does not join and would obtain plenary consideration before deciding the merits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mercer and Hamilton-Brown Shoe supply the later-judgment review rule without dissolving the earlier mandate's effect below.

For Garvey's labor-arbitration review, independent alternative remedial holding and award-restoration mandate, see Labor and Employment, this case, May 14, 2001, No. 00-1210.

### Privacy Act incorporation of FOIA in federal bargaining requests

**Governing question:** When a federal labor-information duty can compel personal-record disclosure through the Privacy Act’s FOIA exception.

**Current rule:** Section 7114(b)(4) operates only to the extent disclosure is not prohibited by law. For a record in a system of records, §552a(b) prohibits disclosure absent the subject’s written request or prior written consent or an applicable enumerated exception. Under §552a(b)(2), FOIA itself must require disclosure. Collective-bargaining usefulness does not enlarge Reporters Committee’s ordinary third-party public-interest inquiry. Exemption 6 balances concrete privacy against demonstrated incremental public understanding; it permits withholding rather than commanding secrecy, while the Privacy Act supplies the prohibition through this invoked route when FOIA does not require release.

**Authority by component:**

- Labor duty, Privacy Act trigger and incorporated FOIA inquiry: Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority — The labor duty incorporates the Privacy Act's FOIA exception without alteration, 1994-02-23. Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt this statutory coordination and precedent-based explanation.
- Distinct Exemption 6 permission and resulting Privacy Act prohibition: Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority — The added home addresses do not justify disclosure on this record, 1994-02-23. Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; nine Justices adopt the same record-specific balance and reversal of both enforcement components.

**Present operation effective:** Department of Defense v. Federal Labor Relations Authority, 1994-02-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** The labor duty concerns requested, normally maintained, reasonably available data necessary for full and proper bargaining discussion, understanding and negotiation, excluding management/supervisory bargaining guidance, advice, counsel and training. Exemption 6 covers personnel, medical and similar files and a clearly unwarranted invasion; Exemption 7(C)’s different law-enforcement threshold is not substituted. Scattered public availability does not eliminate dissemination privacy. Routine use, subject consent, other exceptions and a different demonstrated transparency use remain separate. Requester neutrality does not erase a protected person’s position when disclosure is opposed on a privilege ground.

### Personal information invasion and Privacy Act adverse effect

**Governing question:** The adverse-effect requirement for an individual retention claim under §552a(g)(1)(D).

**Current rule:** Actual unwanted maintenance of identified records describing the claimant’s protected associations, if outside §552a(e)(7)’s lawful alternatives, may itself constitute the required adverse personal effect. An abstract or technical violation without personal consequence is insufficient, but a separate adverse agency determination, disclosure, employment loss or pecuniary injury is not independently necessary. Adverse effect does not itself establish actual damages under §552a(g)(4).

**Authority by component:**

- The protected personal retention invasion can itself be an adverse effect: J. Roderick MacArthur Foundation and Lance E. Lindblom v. Federal Bureau of Investigation, 1998-02-13; Breyer Part II with Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-02-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** Intentional or willful noncompliance, causally resulting compensable actual damages and the statutory minimum remain separate questions. No monetary amount or nonpecuniary-damages rule is decided.

**Related current rules:** Continued maintenance of First Amendment records under the Privacy Act states the lawful retention alternatives and complete pertinence test.

### Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

**Governing question:** Whether an individual former employee's challenge to an employment rule remains live after departure.

**Current rule:** A prospective individual challenge becomes moot after voluntary departure from covered employment when no intent to return, realistic recurrence or surviving claim is established. A general prayer cannot preserve the controversy by producing nominal damages against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom §1983 does not make a damages person. Consent to payment cannot create the missing cause of action, and certification of an unsettled state-law question cannot supply missing Article III jurisdiction.

**Authority by component:**

- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — A continuing personal controversy is absent, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the continuing-controversy and unavailable-damages limits
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — Certification cannot restore the controversy, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the certification boundary

**Present operation effective:** Arizonans for Official English, 1997-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is no categorical express-pleading requirement for nominal damages and does not decide a legally available personal-capacity damages claim. Limited authority to correct lower-court jurisdictional error is distinct from merits authority; judgment vacatur remains equitable under the existing rule. No automatic initiative-sponsor standing or mandatory certification rule is established.
