#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### Independent speech challenge to medical consent information

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not forbid requiring the accurate, material medical facts in section 3205(a)(1) as part of informed consent to this treatment. This is a bounded consent rule, not a holding that paid medical speech is categorically commercial or that any truthful statement can be compelled.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 8 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Required content is the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks, not an ideological script. Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception. Stone concurs in the judgment only and adds no rationale vote. The separately invalidated timing and exclusive-speaker conditions do not acquire approval through this speech holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Deny the independent facial medical-content speech claim at this bounded scope; materially false, irrelevant or ideological implementation is not approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Riley v. National Federation of the Blind, 487 U.S. 781 (1988): payment alone does not remove speech protection.
- Danforth: supplies the legitimate informed-consent connection.
- Wooley v. Maynard, 430 U.S. 705 (1977): distinguished because factual treatment information does not require ideological affirmation.

##### Independent speech challenge to the three ancillary notices

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not facially invalidate the concise truthful notices of available materials, possible assistance and paternal liability at the scopes separately sustained. None requires ideological assent or endorsement of a listed agency; the notice of materials remains distinct from chosen receipt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 7 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Availability means a right to review and obtain free materials if chosen, without acceptance, reading or endorsement. Assistance may be available for prenatal, childbirth and neonatal care, without eligibility or payment guarantees. Paternal liability remains despite an offer to pay for abortion and is not guaranteed collection; rape-related omission is permitted without a police report, including spousal rape. Section 3205(c) protects omission of subsection (a) information when the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. It is an information defense, not an emergency-treatment or general delay exception. Clinicians may correct or criticize information. Stone concurs in the judgments only; Blackmun does not reach these independent speech grounds.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Deny facial relief on each of the three independent speech claims at its bounded factual scope. Concrete falsehood, coercive distribution or materially different enforcement remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Riley: forecloses the compensation-based shortcut; each command is examined independently.
- Wooley: protects against compelled ideological assent, absent from the sustained commands.
- Danforth: supplies the informed-choice connection.

##### Independent speech challenge to chosen materials and lawful receipt certification

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not invalidate state publication and genuinely chosen receipt of objective materials, or a certificate confined to receipt of lawful information rather than ideological assent. This principle does not supply a vote sustaining a contested underlying notice, timing or delivery obligation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas: 8 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Receipt of a copy occurs only if the patient chooses to review it; no reading or ideological assent is compelled. A written certificate documents actual lawful receipt, not personal agreement. Stone concurs in judgment only. The independent speech ruling does not add votes to any disputed prerequisite or certification dependency.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Deny the independent speech challenge to chosen materials and basic receipt certification. False distribution, forced reading or a certificate demanding ideological agreement remains challengeable.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wooley: distinguished at voluntary receipt and factual certification scope; ideological assent remains protected.
- Danforth: applied to consent documentation.
- Riley: paid status supplies no categorical exception to speech protection.

#### Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-329.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

##### Religious viewpoint in the private publication program

**Holding:** The University may not exclude an otherwise eligible private student publication from its generally available printing benefit because its editorial viewpoint is religious. The rule applies to this privately edited, independently attributed publication program on its otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms; it does not require funding every religious activity or every subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt the program-specific viewpoint rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Recognition, facilities, distribution and money remain distinct; not every public expenditure creates a speech forum. The publication remains independently edited and attributed under required disclaimers and approved vendor-payment terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: preserves limited-forum definition while forbidding viewpoint discrimination.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches; Widmar v. Vincent: apply private religious-viewpoint equality without assuming that facility access decides every subsidy.
- Regan v. Taxation With Representation: distinguishes definition of a government financing program from discriminatory exclusion within this opened private-publication arrangement.

##### Permissible printing support on the actual program terms

**Holding:** Providing this neutral printing benefit to the otherwise eligible private religious publication does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Its secular student-expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent editorial attribution and controlled vendor payments matter together; neutrality alone is not a universal authorization of devotional financing or governmental sponsorship of worship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas join this concrete program conclusion. Lemon remains governing law; no new universal framework is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry remains; neutral availability alone neither authorizes unrestricted devotional financing nor governmental worship. Compelled-fee challenges, unrestricted religious aid and particular awards are outside review.

The denial concerned religious activity, defined by the Guidelines as primarily promoting or manifesting belief in or about deity or ultimate reality, rather than an established finding of religious-organization ineligibility. The organization rule separately concerns devotion as organizational purpose. Political restrictions separately exclude principal-purpose lobbying and candidate electioneering while permitting incidental or insubstantial advocacy, nonpartisan study/research and ideological views; those permissions are not exceptions to the religious-activity rule. Permitted ability-to-perform membership limits likewise remain distinct. Abandoned free-exercise/state individual-rights claims and unchallenged equal-protection intent findings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lemon v. Kurtzman: retains the existing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry; no universal replacement is adopted.
- Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind; Mueller v. Allen: apply neutral-benefit and private-direction distinctions within the actual program, not neutrality alone.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel v. Grumet: distinguishes denominational governmental authority and retains religious-neutrality limits.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills: preserves the actual antecedent-law remand; supplies no religious-aid merits holding here.
- Lee v. Weisman: preserves limits on governmental religious participation and coercion; this private printing benefit compels no student worship.
- Church of Scientology v. Clearwater: distinguishes RFRA burdens and exemptions from constitutional financing authority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for removal of the religious-viewpoint exclusion under otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms. Examine any properly available independent Virginia funding defense consistently with the federal viewpoint holding, without declaring applicability, validity or revival of forfeited defenses. No full payment, damages, immunity, fee or compelled-fee refund is determined.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms. It concerns financing, not merely facility access; it neither decided Agostini's public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.

#### Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 96-2412; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** 1998-05-14.

##### Purposeful, materially effective assistance to murder has no categorical publisher immunity

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not categorically bar civil liability where a publisher intentionally supplies detailed operational assistance to facilitate murder, intends criminal use, and the communication materially assists the resulting crime, subject to the applicable tort's substantive and causal requirements. This conclusion rests on the motion-only stipulated combination; publication, offensiveness, abstract advocacy, knowledge of possible misuse, foreseeability or a reader's independent imitation does not alone establish it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion is joined in full by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The stipulations govern defendants’ summary-judgment motion only. Lawful intended readership and absence of specific knowledge of the plot, victims or conspiracy are preserved. Plaintiffs still must establish every necessary fact by operative admissions or admissible proof; ordinary tort elements, causation and defenses remain. No lesser-mental-state rule, general exception to Brandenburg, prior restraint or ordinary-publication liability is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reversal of summary judgment is affirmed unanimously and the case remanded for the reserved law and facts. No liability, damages, publication ban or final factfinding follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Brandenburg:** Retained for advocacy; no general immediacy exception is created.
- **NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware:** Applied to preserve protected advocacy, association and responsibility limits against liability for others' violence.
- **Giboney and Brown v. Hartlage:** Supply the distinction between expression and its function as participation in independently unlawful conduct, without a category of unprotected dangerous ideas.
- **Dawson and Wisconsin v. Mitchell:** Preserve the requirement that expression have a legally relevant connection to misconduct rather than serve as punishment for belief.
- **Madsen and Schenck:** Their responsibility and actual-concert limits remain; the decision does not turn shared viewpoint into participation.
- **Cohen v. Cowles Media:** No categorical press exemption follows, but the general-law label alone does not establish constitutional validity.

#### Thompson v. Western States Medical Center, 535 U.S. 357

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-344.
**Decided:** April 29, 2002.

##### The challenged conditions burden truthful promotion more broadly than their justification supports

**Holding:** The prescription-solicitation and particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion conditions of section 503A violate the First Amendment as applied to truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding because the Government has not established a reasonable fit between their informational burden and its asserted distinction between individualized compounding and manufacturing subject to approval. The holding applies the ordinary commercial-speech framework; it neither requires the least restrictive imaginable measure nor prevents restrictions on misleading claims, unlawful transactions, actual manufacturing conduct or narrower commercial-speech measures supported by an adequate justification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. These six Justices adopt the complete fit holding, statutory explanation and affirmance. Thomas accepts this sufficient application without endorsing the framework as the full measure of constitutional protection. This is a directly shared rationale, not an aggregation of distinct advancement findings.

**Material application:** The Court assumes the Government's combined substantial-interest and material-advancement account and finds inadequate fit; it establishes no separate advancement failure. Available production, distribution, patient-prescription and commercial-copy controls explain the mismatch without selecting a cap or finding every alternative equally effective. Truthful physician-directed information is within the six-Justice holding; the three dissenters reserve that component rather than vote to sustain it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Licensed pharmacies prepared promotional materials for distribution by mail and at medical conferences. The challenge concerns truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding. Section 503A(a) permits the identified patient's prescription through unsolicited receipt or a prescribing practitioner's approved notation that a compounded product is necessary for that patient. The ordinary route requires compounding by a licensed pharmacist in a State-licensed pharmacy or Federal facility, or by a licensed physician, on the identified patient's prescription from a licensed physician or another practitioner authorized by State law to prescribe. The separate advance-compounding route permits a licensed pharmacist or physician to prepare limited quantities before receiving the patient's valid prescription, based on a history of receiving valid prescriptions for that drug generated solely within an established relationship between the compounder and either that identified patient or the physician or other practitioner who will write the prescription. Section 503A(c) prohibits particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion while permitting promotion of compounding services; the necessity notation does not exempt a message from subsection (c).

Section 503A supplies only exemptions from section 501(a)(2)(B)'s manufacturing-practice requirement, section 502(f)(1)'s adequate-directions requirement and section 505's new-drug-approval requirement. Bulk drug substances must comply with an applicable United States Pharmacopoeia or National Formulary monograph and the United States Pharmacopoeia compounding chapter; if no monograph exists, they must be components of approved drugs or, if neither condition is met, appear on the Secretary's regulatory list. They must be manufactured by a registered establishment, including a foreign establishment registered under section 510(i), and accompanied by valid certificates of analysis. Nonbulk ingredients must comply with an applicable monograph, if one exists, and the compounding chapter.

The compounder may not use a drug product on the Secretary's published list of products withdrawn or removed because the product or a component was found unsafe or ineffective. Regular compounding, or compounding in inordinate amounts as defined by the Secretary, of essentially commercially available copies is barred; a change for an identified patient producing a significant difference determined by the prescribing practitioner is excluded from that copy category. The product must not be one identified by regulation as presenting demonstrable compounding difficulties reasonably demonstrating an adverse effect on safety or effectiveness.

The interstate alternatives are compounding in a State that has entered a memorandum with the Secretary addressing inordinate interstate distribution and providing for appropriate State-agency investigation of complaints about compounded products distributed outside that State, or, absent that memorandum, out-of-State distribution no greater than five percent of the pharmacy's or physician's total prescription orders dispensed or distributed. The statute contains no enacted twenty-percent memorandum ceiling. PET compounded drugs and radiopharmaceuticals are excluded. Mixing, reconstitution and other acts performed according to the manufacturer's approved labeling and other directions consistent with that labeling lie outside the statutory compounding definition. These distinct provisions are not interchangeable exceptions to the challenged promotion rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit judgment is affirmed, including its unreviewed determination that §353a is nonseverable. The Court creates no operating exemption with the speech conditions removed and no replacement compounding program.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission and Board of Trustees v. Fox: applied for ordinary commercial-speech scrutiny and proportional fit; no new tier or least-restrictive-means rule is adopted.
- 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island: applied for independent examination of the burden on truthful information; its broader separate theories are not converted into a controlling categorical ban.
- Lorillard Tobacco Co. v. Reilly: applied for separate advancement and fit inquiries and attention to the actual lawful-speech burden; this decision rests on fit and assumes the Government's advancement account.
- Edenfield v. Fane: applied for justification responsive to the actual professional setting; the physician audience is part of the covered speech burden.
- Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association v. United States: applied for practical examination of the fit between a selective speech restriction and the regulatory objective; no conclusion that all compounded products are safe follows.
- Lanphere & Urbaniak v. Colorado: its benefit-condition principle remains applicable at its actual scope; selecting speech as the condition does not eliminate ordinary constitutional scrutiny.

## Standards and Tests selection: Purposeful published assistance to completed crimes

### Purposeful published assistance to completed crimes

**Governing question:** When does public distribution fail to supply categorical First Amendment immunity for assistance to a crime?

**Current rule:** Public distribution creates no categorical immunity from civil liability where a publisher intends to facilitate murder through detailed operational assistance, intends criminal use and materially assists the resulting crime, subject to the tort’s substantive and causal requirements. Knowledge of the particular plot is not indispensable to that stipulated combination. Offensiveness, publication, abstract advocacy, foreseeability, knowledge of possible misuse or a reader’s independent imitation alone does not establish purposeful participation.

**Authority by component:**

- Intentional-participation boundary and actual-assistance predicate: Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc — Purposeful, materially effective assistance to murder has no categorical publisher immunity, 1998-05-14.

**Present operation effective:** Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, 1998-05-14.

**Exceptions and limits:** The claimant must establish the necessary facts through operative admissions or admissible proof; motion-only stipulations do not establish them for trial. Brandenburg’s advocacy requirements remain unchanged. No lesser-mental-state rule, general imminence exception, prior restraint or rule for news, fiction, historical works or ordinary technical information is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** Rice v. Paladin Enterprises, Inc. — Purposeful operational assistance to crime.

## Standards and Tests selection: Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification

### Accurate abortion-consent information, optional materials and receipt certification

**Governing question:** What Pennsylvania may require patients to be told, offered or asked to certify in obtaining informed consent, including independent First Amendment limits.

**Current rule:** Section 3205(a)(1) may require accurate, medically material information about the procedure, material risks and alternatives, probable gestational age and childbirth risks. The three §3205(a)(2) notices may state truthfully: state materials concerning fetal development and alternatives are available and a free copy will be supplied if chosen; medical assistance for prenatal care, childbirth and neonatal care may be available; and the father is liable to assist in support even if he offered to pay for abortion. Assistance and legal liability are not promises of eligibility, payment or collection. Paternal-support information **may** be omitted in a rape pregnancy, including spousal rape, without a police-report condition; omission is permitted, not required, and changes no support obligation. Section 3208 publication and §3205(a)(3) chosen receipt are permissible at their objective, accurate, nonjudgmental scope. Notice of availability is distinct from receipt: patients need not accept, read or endorse materials or listed agencies. Clinicians may correct or criticize them. Section 3205(a)(4) certification may record actual receipt of lawful information, not ideological agreement, unchosen reading or compliance with an invalid prerequisite. The §3205(c) disclosure-health defense remains available on its own terms.

The First Amendment independently permits these bounded medical and ancillary disclosures, voluntary receipt and factual documentation. Payment does not strip professional speech of protection, and truth alone does not justify every compulsory statement. False, irrelevant, coercive or ideological scripts and materially different implementation remain outside the sustained duties. Accurate content does not establish the validity of a timing or exclusive-speaker condition; invalid timing and delivery obligations cannot survive through a certificate.

**Authority by component:**

- Medical content, objective publication/chosen receipt and basic certification: Casey, “Accurate material medical information,” “Statutory publication and chosen receipt of materials,” and “Certification of lawful receipt”, June 29, 1992; unanimous direct holdings at those scopes.
- Each ancillary notice and its qualification: *Casey*, “Notice of available free materials,” “Notice of potentially available medical assistance,” and “Paternal-support notice and the rape omission permission”; Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter supply five rationale joins. The additional judgment votes do not broaden that rationale.
- Independent speech rules: *Casey*, the three “Independent speech challenge” holdings; Souter with White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas for medical content, chosen materials and basic certification; the same group without Blackmun for ancillary notices. Stone's judgment-only agreements supply no speech rationale.
- Dependent timing and delivery links: *Casey*, “Certification of lawful receipt,” with the five-Justice invalidation grounds in “Physician delivery of medical information” and “The mandatory 24-hour interval.”

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rape omission permission has no §3209/§3128 reporting or 90-day condition. No finding establishes universal accuracy of actual publications or agencies. No general commercial-speech classification or professional-speech immunity is adopted. Required content remains subject to the separate access framework.

**Material development:** Akron and Thornburgh are qualified only insofar as they categorically prohibit the sustained concise, accurate ancillary notices because they may encourage childbirth; their protection against misleading, coercive and unjustifiably burdensome implementation remains.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion informed consent; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion information and compelled speech.
