## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent legal review, contemporaneous notice and preservation limits: Elder v. Holloway — Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue, 1994-02-23. Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Elder v. Holloway, 1994-02-23.

**Holdings navigation:** Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue.

## Standards and Tests selection: Whole-record review of a jury verdict under Rule 50

### Whole-record review of a jury verdict under Rule 50

**Governing question:** How a court assesses whether the record can support a jury verdict.

**Current rule:** The court considers the entire record, draws reasonable inferences for the verdict winner and makes no credibility choices or weighing of evidence. It disregards evidence favoring the movant that the jury was free to disbelieve, while considering uncontradicted and unimpeached evidence the jury was required to credit. Disinterested testimony is a relevant circumstance rather than an inflexible witness-label rule. The source of an item alone neither compels belief nor permits its exclusion.

**Authority by component:**

- Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc.; No. 99-536. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, II. Rule 50 preserves reasonable jury inferences while considering the whole record, 2000-06-12; Scalia with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-12; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** In discrimination cases, Hicks’s prima-facie-plus-falsity inference remains permissible rather than compulsory. Material falsity need not be supplemented by a special evidence category, but genuinely conclusive contrary evidence can defeat sufficiency; merely imagining another motive cannot. Ultimate persuasion remains with the plaintiff.

**Related current rules:** Claim-specific grounds in renewed Rule 50 motions supplies preservation; Appellate judgment after exclusion of trial evidence supplies the separate appellate authority and new-trial boundaries; Rejected employment explanations and circumstantial discrimination proof supplies the existing Hicks inference.

## Standards and Tests selection: State compensation limits within federal new-trial review

### State compensation limits within federal new-trial review

**Governing question:** The measure and allocation of compensatory-excessiveness review in a New York diversity action.

**Current rule:** The district court applies CPLR §5501(c)'s material-deviation-from-reasonable-compensation measure through Rule 59, retaining federal new-trial procedure and allocation of functions. Pertinent state decisions guide compensation, without fixed prices or an imported state appellate hierarchy. The court of appeals reviews for abuse of discretion, independently correcting legal error and deferring to the district judge's supported case-specific assessment; the Seventh Amendment permits that bounded review but forbids a first-instance appellate valuation of jury-tried compensation. A remittitur must offer the plaintiff reduced recovery or a new jury trial, not simply impose a substituted amount; federal additur remains forbidden.

**Authority by component:**

- State standard and coexistence with Rule 59: Gasperini — Holding I, June 24, 1996; Souter Part I for Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer and himself (seven).
- Trial/appellate allocation and lawful remittitur: the same Record, Holding II; Souter Part II for the same coalition except Stevens (six).

**Present operation effective:** Gasperini, June 24, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** This does not transplant every outcome-affecting state procedure, adopt a fixed damages cap or authorize a conditional increase under state practice. No damages amount follows from the rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc., 518 U.S. 415 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes

### Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes

**Governing question:** How equal protection governs a State’s use of sex in peremptory jury selection.

**Current rule:** The State may not strike a juror because of sex or a stereotype that sex predicts competence or impartiality. Sex classifications must substantially serve an important governmental objective; a generalized prediction of sympathy does not suffice. The objector first must make the required prima facie showing, after which the striking party must produce a genuine sex-neutral explanation, without having to establish cause. The court decides purposeful discrimination with the ultimate persuasion burden on the objector. A correlated individual characteristic may be a genuine reason but cannot serve as pretext or proxy. Established discriminatory selection requires a new trial before a lawfully selected jury, not a proportionally composed jury.

**Authority by component:**

- Prohibition, staged proof and conditional new-trial remedy: J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B — Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes; Staged proof and the remand, 1994-04-19. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt the rule and explanation. Kennedy concurs in the judgment and independently supports the operative prohibition on individual-right grounds. Blackmun’s six-Justice Opinion of the Court adopts the procedure and conditional remedy; Kennedy independently agrees with the operative inquiry and remand, producing seven votes for reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B, 1994-04-19.

**Open question:** The sex-strike rule for private parties is not decided. The earlier racial-strike rules, including the criminal-defense rule, retain their separate scope.

**Holdings navigation:** Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes; Staged proof and the remand.

## Standards and Tests selection: Claim-specific grounds in renewed Rule 50 motions

### Claim-specific grounds in renewed Rule 50 motions

**Governing question:** What a trial motion must preserve for postverdict judgment as a matter of law.

**Current rule:** The Rule 50(a)(2) motion must identify the judgment sought and supporting legal and factual grounds; Rule 50(b) ordinarily renews only those grounds. Functional presentation and adequate explicit incorporation can suffice without magic words. Pleading qualified immunity or invoking it for one claim does not itself preserve a distinct claim’s immunity defense, and arguing constitutional legality does not necessarily present immunity for a reasonable legal mistake.

**Authority by component:**

- The trial motion did not preserve search immunity: McCardle v. Haddad, 1998-01-29; Ginsburg for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-29.

**Open question:** The outer limits of exceptional review to prevent injustice remain unresolved. Immunity’s importance alone supplies no exception. Failure to preserve does not determine ultimate immunity, constitutional liability or damages.

**Holdings navigation:** McCardle v. Haddad — Qualified-immunity preservation under Rule 50.

#### J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1239.

**Decided:** 1994-04-19.

##### Purposeful sex discrimination in state peremptory strikes

**Holding:** The State may not strike a prospective juror because of sex or the assumption that sex itself predicts competence or impartiality. Under the equal-protection requirement that a sex classification substantially serve an important governmental objective, a generalized prediction that men will favor the alleged father does not justify this exclusion; genuine individual, sex-neutral reasons remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices adopt the rule and explanation. Kennedy concurs in the judgment and independently supports the operative prohibition on individual-right grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190 — applied: sex classifications require an important objective and substantial relationship.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718 — applied to unjustified sex stereotypes and equal protection of men.
- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 — extended to purposeful sex-based state peremptories; its racial rule remains intact.
- Powers v. Ohio, 499 U.S. 400 — applied for the excluded juror’s individual interest and the litigant’s ability to challenge discriminatory selection.
- Edmonson v. Leesville Concrete Co., 500 U.S. 614, and Georgia v. McCollum — relied on for juror equality within selection; the private-party sex-strike question is not decided.
- Taylor v. Louisiana, 419 U.S. 522 — distinguished: no proportionally representative petit jury is required by this decision.

##### Staged proof and the remand

**Holding:** A party challenging a peremptory strike as purposeful sex discrimination must first make the required prima facie showing; the striking party must then give a genuine sex-neutral explanation, which need not justify a challenge for cause. The trial court decides intentional discrimination with the ultimate burden on the objector; if Alabama cannot sustain the challenged strikes under that inquiry, the verdict must be set aside and a new trial held before a lawfully selected jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s six-Justice Opinion of the Court adopts the procedure and conditional remedy; Kennedy independently agrees with the operative inquiry and remand, producing seven votes for reversal and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Batson v. Kentucky, 476 U.S. 79 — its staged purpose inquiry is extended without requiring a cause-level explanation or an explanation for every strike.
- Georgia v. McCollum — its distinction between production and the objector’s ultimate persuasion burden is preserved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The case involves State strikes, not an adjudication of private-party sex strikes. No proportional petit jury, cause-level explanation for every strike or stricter general sex-classification rule is required. A genuinely neutral reason correlated with sex is not automatically forbidden, while a neutral label does not defeat a supported pretext finding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Alabama Court of Civil Appeals is reversed and the case remanded, 7–2. Apply the staged selection inquiry and order a lawfully selected new trial if the strikes cannot be sustained. No paternity finding, jury quota or automatic determination of pretext is made.

#### Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8579.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue

**Holding:** Whether the asserted federal right was clearly established when the officers acted is a legal question reviewed independently on appeal using all relevant precedent, including decisions neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. That duty does not admit a new claim or factual record, make every considered precedent controlling, or permit later decisions to supply notice retroactively; here the notice inquiry remains fixed at April 27, 1987.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither grants nor denies ultimate immunity, decides the factual or legal sufficiency of exigency, awards damages, orders new factfinding or an automatic trial, nor imposes a mandatory merits-first sequence. United States v. Al-Azzawy, 784 F.2d 890, is relevant circuit law predating the arrest; its rule concerning a compelled exit and its ultimate exigency disposition must both be evaluated. Depositions about access to guns and five days of advance arrest planning bear in different directions and are not resolved here. Florida warrants and the absence of an Idaho warrant are distinct facts; the Court does not decide that either alone disposes of the home-arrest question. The lower courts must distinguish a coerced exit from a genuinely voluntary appearance under the governing home-arrest doctrine. Ordinary preservation of claims and evidence remains separate from consideration of legal authority on this preserved claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800: applies objective clearly-established-law protection; the defense is preserved, not replaced by a new reliance-only rule.
- Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511: applies the legal character of the immunity inquiry.
- Pierce v. Underwood, 487 U.S. 552: applies independent appellate review of legal questions; this later decision does not itself supply officers with notice in April 1987.
- Davis v. Scherer, 468 U.S. 183: distinguishes a requirement that the relevant federal right be clearly established from a rule excluding uncited authority; unrelated duties do not defeat immunity.
- Hunter v. Bryant, December 16, 1991: preserves judicial determination under ordinary summary-judgment constraints using the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; its later decision date supplies no retroactive notice.
- Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: preserves the particularized objective inquiry used in current review; because it was decided after April 27, 1987, it is not itself pre-arrest notice authority.
- Payton v. New York, 445 U.S. 573: retains its warrant protection against a nonconsensual home arrest absent exigent circumstances for the lower court's application; this decision does not resolve that application.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for independent immunity review using all relevant law and the proper summary-judgment record. Ultimate immunity, exigency, Fourth Amendment liability and damages remain undecided; no automatic trial or new factfinding is ordered.

#### United States v. Virginia; Virginia v. United States, 518 U.S. 515 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 94-1941 and 94-2107.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### Whether VMI’s categorical exclusion of women satisfies equal protection

**Holding:** A State excluding persons from a public educational opportunity expressly because of sex must establish a genuine important governmental objective and a substantial relationship between that objective and the exclusion. Virginia has not carried that burden for VMI's categorical exclusion of women, including women able and willing to undertake its demanding program; the liability judgment in No. 94-2107 is affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** Accepted benefits of adversative education and predicted effects of coeducation remain findings; their legal sufficiency is independently examined. General preferences attributed to most women do not justify denying consideration to capable willing women. A genuine present purpose need not have been stated in 1839 or contemporaneous writing; litigation timing alone proves no pretext.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Demanding intermediate scrutiny is unchanged; exceedingly persuasive justification describes the State’s burden, not strict scrutiny or least restrictive means. Real physical differences and appropriate accommodations remain relevant; no universal ban on single-sex education.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Liability judgment in No. 94-2107 affirmed, 7–1.

##### Whether full performance of VWIL’s approved plan cures the denied opportunity

**Holding:** A separate program offered to cure an unconstitutional exclusion must actually remedy the denied educational opportunity, taking account of its material tangible and intangible benefits; a shared general objective or equal annual public subsidy does not alone establish adequacy. VWIL's approved design does not cure this exclusion even assuming full performance of its funding and alumni commitments, so the approval judgment in No. 94-1941 must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** The Court credits equal annual public subsidy, ordinary student cost, separate endowment and full promised alumni recruitment, placement, externship and advancement access. Even so, cumulative differences in integrated adversative/military training, accessible curriculum, facilities, resources, degree and institutional standing leave the opportunity materially unequal. The engineering alternative requires three Mary Baldwin years plus two at Washington University at private tuition without the proposed final-two-year public discount.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No breached promise or unreported implementation failure is found; no isolated disparity, fixed endowment ratio, identical campus or guaranteed career outcome is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** VWIL approval in No. 94-1941 reversed, 7–1; monitoring cannot cure a substantively inadequate design.

##### What effective relief and State choices remain

**Holding:** Virginia must promptly eliminate the established denial through admission on lawful terms or an actually adequate lawful alternative, with responsibility for lawful remedial design remaining initially with the State and implementation supervised below. If Virginia continues the same publicly supported VMI opportunity without establishing an adequate lawful alternative, qualified women must receive consideration without the categorical sex bar; the existence of VWIL or promises of future improvement cannot justify indefinite continuation of the injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Parts I–III, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Scalia dissents; Thomas does not participate.

**Material application:** State design responsibility survives within prompt effective correction; genuine privacy, physical-training and operational accommodations remain possible. A lawful change in State involvement differs from relabeling the same public institution, but no withdrawal proposal is preapproved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No abandonment of genuine qualifications, named-person admission, closure, quota, identical programs or particular physical standard is ordered. Private single-sex education, every parallel undergraduate or sex-conscious opportunity arrangement and independent Title IX claims are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand for prompt effective cure without indefinite exclusion under the rejected plan; no precise admissions date, damages, special master or newly required hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Craig v. Boren, 429 U.S. 190: applied for important-objective and substantial-relationship scrutiny; the scrutiny tier is unchanged.
- Mississippi University for Women v. Hogan, 458 U.S. 718: applied for the State's demanding burden and genuine justification for unequal allocation. Its nursing-school facts do not decide this integrated military program automatically, and its reservation of broader separate-education questions survives. A statutory admissions exemption supplies no constitutional permission.
- J.E.B. v. Alabama ex rel. T.B., 511 U.S. 127: its prohibition on using sex as a conclusive individual-capacity proxy informs this application. Its peremptory-strike procedure is not made the proof framework for college admissions.
- Kelley v. Board of Trustees of the University of Illinois, decided May 22, 1995: its genuine-objective and substantial-fit rule remains governing; its permitted sex-conscious restructuring does not create an admissions exemption or require a compensatory objective for every lawful distinction.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections, decided December 4, 1995: applied to the particular public opportunity. Material institutional differences remain relevant, but whole-institution difference supplies neither categorical immunity nor automatic liability.
- Sweatt v. Painter, 339 U.S. 629: its consideration of tangible and intangible educational opportunity informs remedial adequacy; its racial setting is not extended into a categorical ban on all single-sex education.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, decided June 12, 1995: applied for effective, violation-linked repair rather than mere program installation or generalized educational improvement; its distinct desegregation obligations are unchanged.
- Lewis v. Casey, decided June 24, 1996: its requirement of effective relief tied to the proved injury and genuine government choice of means informs the remand. Its prisoner-claim and prison-conditions statutory rules are not transferred to this admissions dispute.
No earlier holding is overruled.

#### Saucier v. Katz

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1977; judgment below, 194 F.3d 962.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### The binding remand instruction and its limited authority

**Authority of the judgment:** Souter announces a 5–4 vacatur and remand. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer join the exact instruction to apply existing objective qualified immunity separately from the force merits on proper interlocutory assumptions, leaving both grant and denial of immunity available. No other Justice joins Souter's full reasoning. His distinct objective-defense rationale, Stone-Zsela's authoritative-reliance replacement and the three-Justice concurrence's force-specific objection are not logical subsets. No controlling Marks rationale establishes a new general immunity rule or mandatory merits-first sequence. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would direct immunity.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Harlow, Anderson and Graham retain their existing operation. Agreement between Souter and dissenters creates no new controlling sequence rule. The preserved legal inquiry uses September 24, 1994 law and plaintiff-favorable interlocutory assumptions, including disputed participation; the Parker-only account is not an appellate finding. There is no ultimate force, violation, participation, immunity, causation or injury determination. First Amendment, false-arrest, supervisory and prospective-relief branches are not reopened; the federal-officer damages channel is assumed without a new cause of action or institutional remedy.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit must perform the specified existing-law inquiry. Separable legal issues may be decided on the proper assumptions; genuinely material factual disputes remain for the appropriate forum. The mandate requires no actual-authoritative-reliance proof, additional reliance submissions, automatic trial, hearing, liability finding or damages award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Graham v. Connor:** Its complete, objective, on-scene force inquiry remains the merits rule, including the circumstances and reasonable factual perceptions confronting the officer. No physical-injury prerequisite, least-dangerous-tactic command or categorical security exception is added.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald:** Its existing objective personal-immunity defense remains in force; a separate proposal to replace it is not the Court's law.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Its particularized legal-notice inquiry remains available under the remand; neither an identical-case requirement nor proof of actual authoritative reliance replaces it.
- **Hunter v. Bryant:** Its judicial legal inquiry under proper summary-judgment assumptions governs the return; the court may not choose a more favorable competing factual account for the officer.
- **Elder v. Holloway:** Its consideration of all relevant preconduct law and ordinary preservation limits remain; later decisions cannot supply September 24, 1994 notice.
- **Johnson v. Jones:** Its exclusion of evidentiary-sufficiency disputes from immediate immunity review remains applicable to disputed participation.
- **Behrens v. Pelletier:** Its distinction between a separable assumed-fact legal defense and a factual-sufficiency appeal remains; the present judgment creates no additional appellate jurisdiction.
- **Sterling v. Borough of Minersville:** Its unanimous participation-review rule applies to the supported two-officer account. Its different confidentiality and April 1997 notice holdings do not answer this force claim or supply earlier notice.
- **Plakas v. Drinski:** Its contextual force analysis remains; its supported immediate armed attack and absence of a contrary material account do not establish the facts here. Its later decision supplies no September 1994 notice.
- **Wilson v. Layne:** Its distinction between constitutional violation and conduct-date personal immunity remains, without requiring a factual twin; its 1999 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Denius v. Dunlap:** Its retention of the existing objective defense remains; its distinct disclosure claims do not settle this force application, and its 2001 decision supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Conn v. Gabbert:** Its reservation of mandatory immunity sequencing is not displaced by a controlling sequence holding here.
- **Marks v. United States:** No common narrower rationale is identified among the materially different judgment-supporting writings; it supplies no basis to combine them into a new rule.

#### Hope v. Pelzer, 536 U.S. 730

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-309.
**Decided:** June 27, 2002.

##### The accepted constitutional premise and individual-responsibility limits of this immunity review

**Holding:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Eighth Amendment characterization is accepted for the granted immunity inquiry, not newly decided after trial. May involved two hours with quarter-hour necessities; June involved roughly seven hours and greater aggravations. May-only officers cannot be assigned June conduct, and Gates’s alleged June attachment alone does not establish supervision, removal of clothing, deprivation or taunting.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer requires actual awareness, personal causal responsibility and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits but does not compel an awareness inference, and reasonable responses remain protected. Hudson retains its confined nontrivial malicious-beating holding. Rhodes and Whitley distinguish justified control from gratuitous punishment. Delaney’s assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or retrospective notice.

##### Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. An established rule can give that warning through an obvious application without a materially identical prior case; context, contrary authority and judicial hierarchy matter, and an earlier express reservation of the particular application may require greater factual specificity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** General moral disapproval is insufficient. An official need not have consulted the controlling law; state policy and administrative warnings contextualize rather than create or erase the constitutional right. Later decisions cannot supply 1995 notice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow’s objective defense and Anderson’s particularized clarity remain. Lanier permits obvious-application notice without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve conduct-date context; Saucier imposes no substitute defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law.

##### Pre-1995 law warned against the assumed gratuitous restraint on both occasions

**Holding:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White gave fair warning that knowingly prolonging painful restraint as punishment after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued violated the Eighth Amendment. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion separately covers May 11 and June 7, 1995, on the assumption of knowingly prolonged painful restraint after control and work refusal ended. May’s offered necessities do not justify that assumed gratuitous punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state restraint regulation’s return-to-work, quarter-hour necessities and log safeguards reinforce the distinction but create no federal right by themselves; compliance with some safeguards permits no needless pain. The preconduct federal report does not prove any officer received it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gates and Ort provide the specific punishment/coercion boundary; Bonner supplies circuit hierarchy. Contrary district decisions involved continued refusal, opportunities to return, necessities or shorter duration and cannot displace the superior circuit warning at the accepted premise.

##### Reversal resolves notice while preserving the ungranted responsibility issues

**Holding:** The reviewed similarity-based immunity judgment is reversed because the legal warning was sufficient on the accepted gratuitous-punishment account of both occasions. Ordinary further proceedings determine each respondent's supported acts, knowledge, causation and independent defenses; the June aggravations may not be attributed to May-only defendants or to Gates without a sufficient personal basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV. Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct rationale votes throughout Parts I–IV.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The immunity judgment is reversed, 7–2. Ordinary individual-responsibility proceedings remain; Stone-Zsela separately makes remand express. Notice is settled on the accepted account, while materially different actual facts and lawful independent defenses retain their force. Withdrawn claims remain withdrawn.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Farmer governs actual awareness and reasonable response in the remaining individual inquiry. Delaney preserves the distinction between assumed-fact legal review and supported personal liability without inventing a factual dispute.

#### McCardle v. Haddad

**Docket or dockets:** Second Circuit Nos. 96-9133(L) and 96-9503; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1998-01-29.

##### The trial motion did not preserve search immunity

**Holding:** Rule 50 requires the trial motion to identify the judgment sought and its supporting legal and factual grounds; postverdict renewal does not ordinarily permit a new ground omitted from that motion. Functional presentation, including adequate explicit incorporation, can suffice without a particular label, but pleading qualified immunity and invoking it for the stop did not preserve the distinct search-immunity ground here; no demonstrated exceptional injustice requires reaching that ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Search legality and immunity for a reasonable legal mistake are distinct. The motion expressly invoked stop immunity but identified no search-immunity ground or sufficient incorporation. The plaintiff-favorable search record supplies no compelled judgment establishing exceptional injustice; no new search-merits or ultimate immunity holding follows. The outer limits of exceptional review remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmed insofar as reviewed, leaving the unlawful-search verdict and $1 nominal judgment undisturbed. No immunity remand or new trial issues. The unappealed stop verdict and separate punitive and fee rulings are outside review; their reasoning is not adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gomez is applied to distinguish an affirmative immunity defense from an obligation courts must supply without its proper presentation.
- Elder is applied to preserve review of all relevant law on a preserved immunity issue; it does not revive an omitted ground or admit a new factual record.
- Harlow and Anderson retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity. The Court neither grants nor denies that defense on its merits.
- Long informs the rejection of claimed exceptional injustice at its actual protective-search scope; neither a valid stop nor the officer's subjective account alone establishes a lawful search.
- Crawford-El and Fletcher retain ordinary procedure and the distinction between constitutional liability and personal defenses. Their holdings create no immunity-specific Rule 50 exception or motive element for this search claim.
- Johnson, Behrens, Clanton and Smith retain their legal/factual and claim-specific limits. Their interlocutory settings do not restrict this final-judgment channel or enlarge the trial motion. Lanier and later immunity decisions supply no notice retrospectively for the March 1992 conduct.
- Allentown's distinction between a fairly included argument and an independent unpreserved objection remains confined to its actual question and governing statute; Rule 50 controls here.
- Farrar and Hensley remain unchanged. Their explained assessment of actual success permits zero without mechanical proportionality or a categorical nominal-relief presumption. The Court does not approve the lower fee rationale or decide a fee amount.

#### Gasperini v. Center for Humanities, Inc., 518 U.S. 415 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-719.
**Decided:** June 24, 1996.

##### Whether New York’s compensation standard governs federal diversity new-trial review

**Holding:** In this diversity action governed by New York damages law, the federal district court must assess excessiveness under CPLR section 5501(c)'s material-deviation measure through Rule 59. The state compensation constraint applies without importing the State's appellate hierarchy, displacing federal new-trial procedure, creating a fixed damages cap or authorizing federal additur.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** CPLR §5501(c) directs the Appellate Division’s specified itemized-money-verdict review, but authoritative New York cases apply the same material-deviation measure at trial with sparing discretion. Rule 59 preserves the federal new-trial motion and function without prescribing an immutable substantive degree of excessiveness. The state construction, not the statute’s named actor alone, supports trial application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule incorporates every outcome-affecting state procedure, appellate hierarchy, photographic tariff, state conditional increase or punitive-damages standard.

##### Which federal court applies that standard and what appellate review is permissible

**Holding:** The district judge must exercise the federal new-trial function using the state measure, while the court of appeals reviews that determination for abuse of discretion, independently correcting legal error and deferring to the district judge's supported case-specific assessment. The Seventh Amendment permits that review of trial-court discretion but does not permit the appellate court to substitute its own first-instance valuation of the jury's compensatory award; this judgment must be vacated for district reconsideration under the proper allocation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Stevens joins Part I only.

**Material application:** The unexplained district denial does not demonstrate application of the state measure, but proves no particular wrong formula. The appellate court’s first operative valuation cannot substitute for the assigned trial assessment. Comparable awards guide rather than fix price.

**Limits and questions not reached:** If remittitur is ordered, the plaintiff must choose between the reduced award and a new jury trial; an unconditional reduced judgment and federal additur are not authorized. No remittitur acceptance is established. The separate industry-evidence admissibility ruling remains undisturbed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated and remanded, 6–3, for district reconsideration of Rule 59 under the state measure and genuine abuse-of-discretion appellate review. Neither $450,000 nor $100,000 is selected; no automatic retrial. Stevens would retain the existing conditional appellate order; Scalia and Thomas would restore the district judgment against this challenge.

## Event

## Holdings

### I. Congress expressly authorized the family-care action against a State employer

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 2611(4)(A)(iii) and 2617(a)(2) unmistakably authorize an eligible employee's action for the prescribed family-care damages or equitable relief against a covered State employer in a competent federal or state court. Public-agency coverage and express private-action authorization are separate statutory predicates, both satisfied by the enacted text.

**Authority:** Part I of the opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2611 expressly includes public agencies, incorporating the definition that reaches States and their agencies. Section 2617 separately permits the employee's action against an employer, expressly including a public agency. Florida Prepaid and Kimel require unmistakable authorization distinct from constitutional power. This direct remedy text answers the clarity inquiry without depending on an inference from employer coverage alone. The statute regulates the employment relationship and authorizes its enforcement; State consent is a different source of amenability. College Savings Bank's rejection of consent inferred from ordinary activity remains intact.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid and Kimel supply the distinct clear-authorization inquiry. College Savings Bank preserves the difference between legislative authority and waiver.

### II. Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized family-care action

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against a State when both the substantive obligation and the enforcement measure fall within a valid Article I power and the State's exposure to suit is unmistakably expressed. The FMLA family-care employment duty and its enacted employee remedy satisfy that rule under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses and independently defeat Nevada's categorical immunity defense.

**Authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. O'Connor withholds this ground; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from it.

**Controlling explanation:** Florida Prepaid establishes the affirmative Article I enforcement rule; Kimel applies it to State employment, and Garrett independently sustains the specified Title I employment actions on that basis. Alden applies valid commerce authority to an expressly authorized state-court wage remedy. Family-care leave regulates the terms and continuity of actual economic employment. The employee action is an appropriate means of enforcing that obligation. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming support federal regulation of covered State employment, while the private-remedy question is answered by the later enforcement precedents.

The opposing structural distinction between regulation and a private action against the State's treasury is the objection those precedents rejected. It does not become an existing exception merely because the subject is family-care leave rather than wages, age discrimination or disability discrimination. The Court adheres to its enforcement rule and applies its separate power and authorization requirements here. Congress's commerce designation is consistent with the economic relationship but does not by itself establish power. The action rests on valid legislation, rather than Nevada's inferred consent, and Hibbs need not prove an individual equal-protection violation as an extra element of this statutory claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel and Garrett are applied to the authorized employment duty and remedy. Alden preserves the independently authorized state-court route. Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming supply the substantive employment-regulation foundation; College Savings Bank's no-implied-consent rule remains distinct.

### III. Section 5 independently sustains the bounded family-care entitlement

**Holding and operative rule:** The sex-neutral family-care entitlement in §2612(a)(1)(C), with its enacted coverage, eligibility, medical-need, scheduling and remedial qualifications, is congruent and proportional preventive enforcement of constitutional sex equality in State employment. Section 5 therefore independently sustains the expressly authorized private action, including statutory violations that would not themselves establish an individual constitutional tort.

**Authority:** Part III of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens withholds assent to this ground without adopting an invalidity holding; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Boerne v. Flores permits fitted prevention beyond separately provable constitutional violations while forbidding Congress to redefine the constitutional right. United States v. Virginia requires genuine important objectives and a substantial relationship for sex classifications; assumptions about men's and women's proper domestic roles cannot replace that justification. The relevant injury is employment exclusion and unequal leave administration sustained by those roles, rather than the mere absence of any leave benefit.

Congress's evidence included historical employment restrictions, contemporary public-employment accounts and testimony, and private-sector practices. The Massachusetts public-school account and testimony concerning public employers give the inference State-related support. Pregnancy incapacity, child-rearing and care of ill relatives remain different settings; the seven listed women-only regimes are not seven adjudicated unconstitutional family-care denials. Those limits do not erase the connected mechanism: treating women as caregivers and men as continuously available workers can reinforce exclusion in hiring, advancement and leave decisions. Equal family-care opportunity removes that proxy for both sexes.

The principal objection is that a minimum benefit reaches nondiscriminatory employers and decisions, and some evidence concerns neighboring practices. The finite combined twelve-week entitlement, ordinary unpaid status, eligibility and serious-illness requirements, certification, notice, scheduling safeguards and claim-linked remedies keep the preventive means fitted to the identified injury. Boerne allows that preventive reach without a violation in every State or a quota of findings. Garrett confirms that valid employment prevention can exceed the constitutional minimum. Popovich similarly separates fitted statutory protection from an individual constitutional tort; its distinct parental-hearing setting does not substitute for the family-care analysis performed here. Self-care and other leave entitlements lie outside the presented question and require their own constitutional analysis.

**Precedent treatment:** Boerne supplies the prevention/redefinition boundary; Virginia supplies demanding intermediate scrutiny. Garrett supplies the independent preventive-employment principle. Popovich is applied for the statutory-duty/constitutional-tort distinction and distinguished as to its court-access subject. Kimel's reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 question remains intact.

**Operative statutory qualifications supporting the family-care holding:** Employee eligibility generally requires twelve months with the employer and 1,250 service hours in the preceding twelve months, calculated under the applicable Fair Labor Standards Act standards. The Act excludes federal officers and employees covered by subchapter V of chapter 63 of Title 5, and a worker at a site with fewer than fifty employees if the employer also has fewer than fifty within seventy-five miles. Express public-agency coverage does not eliminate those employee requirements.

Section 2612(a)(1)(C), subject to provider certification under §2613, covers care for a spouse, son, daughter or parent with a serious health condition. Parent includes a biological parent or one who stood in a parental role during the employee's childhood. Son or daughter includes a biological, adopted, foster or stepchild, legal ward or child for whom the employee stands in a parental role, under eighteen or, if older, incapable of self-care because of mental or physical disability. The enacted spouse definition is husband or wife. Serious health condition requires inpatient care in a hospital, hospice or residential medical facility, or continuing treatment by a health-care provider. The entitlement totals twelve workweeks within a twelve-month period across the statutory leave categories, rather than twelve weeks per episode or relative.

Medically necessary intermittent or reduced-schedule family-care leave is permitted, and only leave actually taken reduces the total. Foreseeable planned treatment requires a reasonable scheduling effort to avoid undue operational disruption, subject to provider approval, and thirty days' notice, or practicable notice if treatment must start sooner. For that foreseeable intermittent treatment, the employer may temporarily transfer a qualified employee to an available alternative position with equivalent pay and benefits that better accommodates recurring leave.

Leave may be unpaid. An employee may elect, or an employer require, substitution of accrued vacation, personal or family leave for the specified birth, placement or family-care categories. For family-care or self-care serious-health-condition leave, accrued vacation, personal, medical or sick leave may similarly be substituted; that permission does not require paid medical or sick leave where the employer would not normally provide it. For spouses working for the same employer, combined leave may be limited to twelve weeks for birth, placement or care of a sick parent; that aggregation permission does not cover every family-care category.

An employer may require timely provider certification identifying commencement, probable duration, relevant medical facts, the need and expected time for care, and applicable treatment dates or intermittent-care need and schedule. A supported doubt permits an employer-paid second opinion from a provider not regularly employed by it; a conflict permits an employer-paid third opinion jointly chosen and binding on both sides. Reasonable subsequent recertification is permitted.

Restoration protects the former or an equivalent position and accrued preleave benefits, without creating benefit accrual during leave or a right the employee would have lacked absent leave. The restoration exception requires a salaried employee among the highest-paid ten percent within seventy-five miles, necessity to prevent substantial and grievous economic injury, notice when the employer determines that injury would occur and, if leave has begun, the employee's election not to return after notice. This is a restoration exception, not permission to deny leave generally. Group-health coverage continues on the terms that would have applied during continuous employment. Premiums paid for unpaid leave may be recovered after failure to return when the entitlement expires, except when nonreturn results from continuation, recurrence or onset of a qualifying serious health condition or circumstances beyond the employee's control; timely provider certification may be required for the serious-health-condition exception. Greater statutory, local, contractual and plan protections retain their enacted force. These qualifications define the scheme; their application to Hibbs requires the ordinary merits record.

### IV. The statutory action proceeds under its own proof and remedy requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's rejection of categorical immunity is affirmed, and the family-care action proceeds under the FMLA's coverage, liability, defense and remedy provisions. Hibbs must prove the required statutory facts and causally recoverable loss by a preponderance; an individual equal-protection violation is not an additional entry requirement under either sufficient enforcement ground.

**Authority:** Part IV of the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The two constitutional grounds establish the State's amenability to the enacted action. They leave the actual statutory dispute to ordinary adjudication, as Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Garrett and Popovich distinguish valid enforcement from proved liability. The department's assertion that leave was exhausted remains contested rather than an adjudicated fact. The same distinction requires application of the enacted loss measures and defenses to any proved violation. The surviving procedural disposition of the separate constitutional claim remains effective.

**Operative remedy qualifications:** Section 2617 permits wages, salary, benefits or other compensation denied or lost by reason of the violation. When such compensation was not denied or lost, the alternative is actual monetary loss directly resulting from the violation, such as care costs, capped at twelve weeks of wages or salary; that cap applies to the alternative, not all backpay. Prevailing-rate interest is added. Additional liquidated damages equal the compensation or alternative loss plus interest. Only upon the employer's proof of both good faith and reasonable grounds for believing the act or omission lawful may the court, in its discretion, reduce liability to principal and interest. Appropriate equitable relief includes employment, reinstatement or promotion; a judgment for the employee also carries reasonable attorney and expert fees and other costs. The limitations period runs two years from the last event constituting the alleged violation, or three years for a willful violation. A Secretary complaint under §2617(d) seeking restraint of delayed payment of the employee's specified amounts, or under §2617(b) seeking recovery of those damages, terminates the private-action right unless that action is dismissed without prejudice on the Secretary's motion.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid, Kimel, Garrett and Popovich preserve ordinary statutory proof and relief after rejection of immunity; their affirmative enforcement holdings are applied without converting them into merits awards.

**Stevens, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins statutory authorization, the Article I ground and the statutory remand. ## Holdings

### I. Probation does not erase protection against unjustified outside disclosure of intimate medical information

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Amendment protects a probationer's objectively intimate medical testing and status information against intentional disclosure to an employer or family without sufficient lawful official justification. The claimant must establish the protected disclosure and the officer's personal responsibility; the asserted official need must sufficiently connect the information, recipient and supervisory function, with scope, use and safeguards fitted to that need.

**Authority:** Part I is the opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five adopt the rule at the stated level of generality and its application to the pleaded account.

**Controlling explanation:** Sheets protects intimate information against unjustified official dissemination. Denius requires an actual functional connection and fitted safeguards for government access to medical information. Paul P. and Lawall require attention to the particular audience and use; lawful acquisition for a confidential task does not by itself justify an additional transfer. Sterling confirms the constitutional importance of intimate family exposure, while its coercive-disclosure trigger remains distinct from the actual disclosures alleged here. Doe v. SEPTA preserved a confined benefits use, which supplies no permission for an unrelated employer or family disclosure.

Probation permits genuine supervision, not every use of information acquired through that relationship. Voluntarily telling an officer about a test is not general consent to publication. Neither a limited number of recipients nor the public-safety label establishes a sufficient connection to these disclosures. Each employer or sister communication must be assessed separately; a justification for one does not justify the other. The allegations of unconfirmed status, termination pressure and absence of adequate official need therefore state the protected invasion, subject to factual proof by a preponderance. The privacy rule permits justified protective action and does not turn every administrative confidentiality breach into a constitutional violation. The guidance supplies relevant context rather than the constitutional right.

**Precedent treatment:** Sheets and Denius are applied to intimate medical disclosure in federal supervision; Paul P. and Lawall supply the recipient-and-function limits. Sterling's distinct compelled-disclosure holding is preserved. Doe's confined benefits application and Whalen's safeguarded administration remain permissible at their actual scope.

### II. The immunity judgment is set aside without a common controlling immunity rationale

**Judgment and operative effect:** The five-Justice judgment rejects the asserted legal qualified-immunity dismissal on the pleaded no-justification account and returns the claim for ordinary proceedings. The same assumed account cannot be dismissed again on the legal defense rejected by this judgment; materially different proved facts and properly preserved independent defenses retain their force.

**Authority:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reach that result under the existing objective notice defense. Stone-Zsela reaches it under his distinct proposed authoritative-reliance defense. Their common judgment binds this case; Part I independently supplies the five-Justice substantive rule. The immunity rationale remains fractured under Marks.

**Explanation of authority:** The four-Justice rationale finds that preconduct intimate-information and limited-supervision decisions gave sufficient warning on these allegations. Stone-Zsela would replace uncertainty-based immunity with protection for genuine reasonable reliance on directly authorizing binding precedent or enacted authority. One asks what a reasonable officer was objectively warned against; the other asks whether actual reliance on valid applicable permission is proved. The existing objective immunity framework remains the general governing law, while this case proceeds under the binding judgment supported by those distinct grounds.

**Precedent treatment:** Under Marks, these distinct predicates leave the immunity rationale fractured. Harlow and Anderson retain their existing force. Meyer preserves the separate question of an authorized cause of action, which recognition of the substantive right does not resolve.

- Paul P. v. Verniero supplies the connection among protected information, audience and official purpose, applied to the particular outside recipients.
- Planned Parenthood of Southern Arizona v. Lawall distinguishes necessary confidential handling from an additional transfer requiring its own justification and safeguards; that distinction applies to supervisory acquisition and outside disclosure.
- Sterling v. Borough of Minersville preserves protection against an operative intimate family-disclosure ultimatum. The present actual-disclosure claim applies the privacy interest without merging the two triggers.
- Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority and Whalen v. Roe preserve justified, safeguarded governmental information uses; those decisions do not supply the missing connection on the assumed allegations.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity; Elder v. Holloway requires all relevant preconduct authority. Clanton v. Cooper and Hope v. Pelzer preserve obvious-application notice without requiring a factual twin; Saucier v. Katz supplies no mandatory merits-first sequence.
- FDIC v. Meyer preserves the distinction between a substantive right, an individual-officer action and an unauthorized direct-agency damages action. The properly preserved source question remains independent.
- Marks v. United States supplies no common immunity rationale for the different judgment-supporting grounds.

## Law After Decision

The substantive protection now applies to unjustified intentional outside disclosure of a probationer's intimate medical testing and status information. Genuine supervisory need must fit the particular information, audience, use and safeguards. The judgment conclusively removes the asserted legal immunity dismissal on the pleaded account; ordinary factual and remedial adjudication remains. The governing objective immunity framework continues for other cases; Part II states four Justices' application to this pleaded account. Present privacy decisions do not themselves give officials notice in December 1993 or January 1994.

## Separate Writings

**The Part II opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** Existing law sufficiently warned against these disclosures on the complaint's assumed absence of a legitimate purpose. Eastwood protected against unjustified intimate intrusion and required application of law to analogous facts. Woods, Barrington and Plymouth addressed unjustified medical exposure in their own settings. Griffin permitted a search tied to valid supervision; Harris addressed supported transmission risks, and Stine upheld a related counseling condition. Those decisions supply meaningful limits rather than a general probation exception. Whalen and Nixon's approval of safeguarded uses does not justify purposeless outside disclosure. Mangels concerns accurate unlawful-drug information, and DeSanti's contrary general approach is considered at its own hierarchy and scope. The cumulative preconduct boundary, applied under Elder, Clanton and Hope's present method, supplies sufficient warning despite the lack of an identical HIV-probation case. The guidance reinforces context but creates no constitutional right, and later privacy holdings supply no retrospective notice. The four reject the legal defense finally on these assumptions while leaving actual conduct, justification and the independent remedial questions for ordinary adjudication.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment.** He joins the substantive privacy rule and would vacate the immunity dismissal by replacing Harlow and Anderson's uncertainty-based defense. After a violation under the full substantive rule, an officer would have a distinct personal defense only by identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct and proving genuine reasonable reliance on its valid, applicable permission. The officer would bear that factual showing by a preponderance; silence, title, custom and lack of a factual twin would not suffice. A faithful instruction could convey actual authority, while reasonable factual mistakes would remain part of the substantive rule. Genuine substantive discretion, source-specific absolute immunity, personal responsibility and independently authorized damages channels would remain. He would correspondingly displace Denius's contrary immunity rule and Clanton, Hope and Saucier only within that replacement's scope, preserving their substantive holdings and remedial limits. The allegation of an unestablished employment prohibition and contrary guidance permits no assumed reliance dismissal. Harper would apply his replacement to still-open direct-review claims, including earlier conduct; Reynoldsville would preserve genuine independent defenses, with final judgments and collateral authorization retaining their force. He would require separate determination of any preserved damages-source objection and then adjudication of the alleged disclosures, actual justification, responsibility, reliance and compensable injury. Lawall supplies neither earlier permission nor pre-1994 notice.

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** Current medical-privacy protection and sufficiently particular pre-1994 warning are different inquiries. The earlier cases arose in materially different investigatory, medical, prison or general-information settings, with competing approaches and a distinct supervisory relationship here. Even on the assumed absence of an adequate actual purpose, those authorities did not sufficiently settle the application to this probation officer's limited employer and family disclosures. Hodge and Denius preserve objective immunity for an unsettled application even where a present interest is recognized. The dissent considers all relevant preconduct law rather than demanding a factual twin, and it does not treat administrative guidance as constitutional notice. O'Connor and Kennedy accept the confined current interest reflected in Sheets, Denius, Sterling and Lawall but would decide this case on notice alone; their immunity votes do not find the alleged conduct lawful. All four would affirm the immunity judgment without crediting a disputed protective-purpose account.

**Scalia, further dissenting, joined by Thomas.** In addition to the notice defense, they oppose extending substantive informational privacy to these limited disclosures. Their Sheets, Paul P. and Lawall positions distinguish the actual holdings they must respect from a broader right inferred from confidentiality or official possession. The claim's intimate medical character and guidance do not by themselves supply the proposed constitutional extension. They adopt neither a universal denial of medical privacy nor a blanket probation exemption. Meyer likewise does not authorize automatic dismissal merely by treating an individual officer as a federal agency.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Tenth Circuit for ordinary adjudication of the surviving informational-privacy damages claim under the substantive rule and the judgment rejecting the asserted legal defense on the assumed account. The court must address any properly preserved objection to the individual damages channel independently, and then the supported factual and remedial issues. The separately dismissed claims and withdrawn roommate allegation remain outside the proceedings.
