## Standards and Tests selection: Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation

### Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation

**Governing question:** The distinction between an underlying federal deprivation and municipal responsibility, including final policymaking for the challenged function.

**Current rule:** A municipal employee who proves deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and the required municipal responsibility need not prove an additional, independent abuse-of-governmental-power element. The federal deprivation and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries. Employment alone establishes neither municipal liability nor a substantive right; *Canton*'s training analysis supplies attribution and causation, not the underlying constitutional guarantee. For a final-policymaker theory, the court identifies through state and local law, including custom or usage with legal force, who speaks finally for the particular entity on the challenged subject. Final operational discretion does not alone answer that question; labels, election, territory, financing or lack of commission control is individually insufficient. An Alabama sheriff's independent local office supplies County final law-enforcement authority in the criminal-investigation function addressed in McMillian, even without commission delegation or review. An attributable policy or final decision must still cause the proved federal injury. For a training or supervision omission, the plaintiff must establish a deliberate or conscious municipal choice amounting to deliberate indifference, an identified deficiency closely related to the federal injury, and actual causation. Notice can come from an established pattern or an obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; prior identical incidents are not invariably necessary. Negligence, a single employee’s error, general supervisory responsibility and hindsight that additional training might have helped are insufficient. Responsibility for police conduct requires actual supported authority over that conduct rather than cooperation alone.

Consequential examination injuries caused by a proved municipal removal-policy violation do not invariably require proof of a second examination policy. The underlying wrong, municipal causal connection, injury and recoverable measure must nevertheless be established separately for each consequence. That route does not authorize adjudication of an unpresented independent examination-policy claim or adopt a constitutional rule for such examinations.

**Authority by component:**

- Rejection of the additional element and separation of deprivation from attribution: Collins v. City of Harker Heights — No extra governmental-abuse element under §1983, February 26, 1992; Part A of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

- McMillian v. Monroe County — I. Final authority and the government represented are distinct inquiries, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg, all nine
- McMillian v. Monroe County — II. The sheriff's independent local office supplies County law-enforcement authority, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five
- McMillian v. Monroe County — III. The County claim returns for the remaining liability elements, 1997-06-02; the same five

- Deliberate omission, notice, identified deficiency and causation: Carter v. City of Philadelphia — A deliberate municipal omission must actually cause the constitutional injury, March 13, 2000; Ginsburg’s opinion joined by all seven other Associate Justices.

- Consequential injury from an established municipal wrong without an invariably required second policy: Canonical Decision Record `Wallis_v_Spencer_merits_2001-04-02.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. The independent examination theory requires a threshold determination, while consequential injury remains possible, 2001-04-02; all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Wallis, April 2, 2001, for the consequential-injury qualification; the existing Collins, McMillian and Carter attribution and fault components retain their stated authority.

**Current wording authority:** Carter, March 13, 2000, states the surviving Canton training-and-supervision requirements and applies them without adopting a separate Walker test.





**Exceptions and limits:** Other sheriff functions, other States' arrangements, individual liability and State liability or immunity require separate analysis. Institutional identity does not prove the truth of misconduct or make every official act municipal policy. Bryan County's pending reargument supplies no hiring, fault or causation change.

**Holdings navigation:** [Wallis v. Spencer — Emergency child removal and municipal responsibility](HOLDINGS.md#wallis-v-spencer).

## Standards and Tests selection: Present-offense facts changing binding punishment ranges

### Present-offense facts changing binding punishment ranges

**Governing question:** Which additional present-offense facts must receive constitutional element protections.

**Current rule:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the jury. A lawful jury waiver changes the factfinder but retains that proof standard. This rule reaches mandatory Guidelines constraints below a larger outer statutory maximum: an enforceable inner range is not discretionary merely because a higher sentence is statutorily possible. Judicial assessment remains permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions. A sentencing label, ordinary sentencing admission, notice of exposure or unused aggregate maxima on other counts supplies neither the missing element nor an inevitable lawful aggregate.

In a federal prosecution the covered aggravating fact also receives applicable grand-jury charging protection. A lawful jury waiver does not enlarge the charge or convert ordinary sentencing findings into element adjudication.

A statutory present-offense aggravating fact necessary to make death legally available likewise receives jury and reasonable-doubt protection in a jury-tried prosecution, subject to valid element admission and existing lawful waiver requirements. Eligibility is distinct from mitigation, weighing and ultimate selection among already authorized punishments.

**Authority by component:**

- Apprendi v. New Jersey; No. 99-478. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, Present-offense facts necessary for a higher maximum require element protections, 2000-06-26; Stone-Zsela with Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six).

- Canonical Decision Record `united-states-v-angle_merits_2002-06-20.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / 1. Present-offense facts changing a binding punishment range receive element protections; June 20, 2002; Souter with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg (six); supplies binding floors/ceilings and Guidelines extension.

- Canonical Decision Record `united-states-v-angle_merits_2002-06-20.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / 2. Federal charging protection accompanies the covered aggravated fact; June 20, 2002; Souter with Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg (five); supplies federal charging component.

- Canonical Decision Record `ring-v-arizona_merits_2002-06-24.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The jury must determine the fact that first authorizes death; June 24, 2002; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (seven); supplies capital-eligibility extension.

**Present operation effective:** Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, for necessary capital-eligibility aggravators; United States v. Angle, June 20, 2002, for binding floors/ceilings, Guidelines and applicable federal charging. Apprendi's June 26, 2000 maximum rule remains the earlier component.

**Exceptions and limits:** Prior-conviction facts remain undecided; no constitutional recidivism exception is created. State charging form and Fifth Amendment grand-jury incorporation, general consecutive sentencing independent of the disputed range premise, collateral retroactivity, and capital mitigation, complete weighing and ultimate selection remain outside these holdings. The Guidelines remain binding subject to the constitutional conditions; no advisory-system remedy or automatic aggravated trial is created. Sentencing and any later proceeding need independent substantive and procedural authority. Forfeited claims retain all Rule 52(b) conditions, and preserved capital-eligibility omission receives only Ring's qualified actual-verdict inquiry.

**Related current rules:** Aggravated unlawful-reentry elements and authorized maximum; Original armed carjacking and cumulative firearm punishment; Firearm-type elements under former section 924(c)(1). Those statute-specific federal charging rules remain independent of this new constitutional rule.

**Former-rule note:** Angle displaced McMillan's permission for ordinary judicial present-offense findings to raise a mandatory floor merely because the maximum is unchanged, effective June 20, 2002. Ring displaced Walton's permission for a judge without a jury to find a statutory aggravating fact necessary to authorize death, effective June 24, 2002; unrelated capital-selection propositions survive only to the extent independent of that allocation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Denial of defense summary judgment is not a plaintiff liability judgment

### Denial of defense summary judgment is not a plaintiff liability judgment

**Governing question:** Whether an earlier denial of a defendant’s summary-judgment motion establishes liability at trial.

**Current rule:** Denial of a defendant’s summary-judgment motion does not establish the plaintiff’s liability case and cannot, merely as law of the case, justify a directed liability judgment at trial. The court must apply Rule 50 to the actual trial evidence and each substantive liability requirement.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 2: Rule 50 and the separate municipal-liability inquiry: DeCarlo v. Fry — controlling Holdings, 1998-05-28.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-05-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Municipal liability still requires an attributable policy or authorized final decision causing an established federal deprivation; personal qualified immunity supplies no entity defense.

**Holdings navigation:** DeCarlo v. Fry — Administrative-delay immunity and distinct municipal claims.

#### Powell v. Nevada, 511 U.S. 79

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-8841.

**Decided:** 1994-03-30.

##### McLaughlin applies to a case still pending on direct review

**Holding:** A criminal defendant whose judgment was not final when McLaughlin issued receives that decision's prompt-probable-cause-review rule on direct review, even if the arrest or prosecution began earlier and even assuming the decision announced a new rule. The prosecution's commencement date does not permit withholding the governing rule; applying it does not itself determine the remedy for a violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's direct-review portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine adopt this proposition. Thomas separately opposes vacatur on a remedial ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Griffith v. Kentucky, 479 U.S. 314 (1987): applied directly to the nonfinal criminal judgment; the commencement-date cutoff is rejected.
- County of Riverside v. McLaughlin, 500 U.S. 44 (1991): applied to Powell without deciding whether it announced a new rule.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): its equal-application principle and separation of genuine remedy questions are preserved.

##### The four-day interval requires justification, with remedy separately determined on remand

**Holding:** A judicial probable-cause determination within forty-eight hours of a warrantless arrest generally complies with the prompt-review requirement, but the arrestee may prove that the actual delay was unreasonable—for example, because it served to gather evidence justifying arrest, arose from ill will or occurred for delay's own sake, without limiting the challenge to those examples; after forty-eight hours the government must prove a bona fide emergency or other extraordinary circumstance, and intervening weekends or the administrative convenience of combining procedures do not alone excuse the delay. Powell's four-day interval therefore requires that justification inquiry; the Court vacates for consideration under this rule and properly available remaining grounds without deciding suppression, causal product, attenuation, preservation, good-faith reliance or harmlessness.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's timing-and-remand portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; eight Justices adopt this bounded disposition and reservation of remedy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103 (1975): applied to neutral judicial review, while preserving that unlawful detention alone does not void a conviction.
- County of Riverside v. McLaughlin: applied with all timing qualifications and the government's post-forty-eight-hour burden unchanged.
- New York v. Harris, 495 U.S. 14 (1990): retained as a distinct protected-interest and remedial analogy; its home-entry facts do not settle this statement's causal source.
- Segura v. United States, 468 U.S. 796 (1984), and United States v. Crews, 445 U.S. 463 (1980): causal-product principles remain available without a new application finding here.
- Illinois v. Krull, 480 U.S. 340 (1987): its separate statutory-reliance defense remains available for lawful consideration, without a merits ruling on its predicates.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A police declaration does not perform neutral judicial review or restart the clock. Personal appearance is separate. A state-law arraignment waiver does not itself waive federal prompt-review rights; a prearrest statement alone does not establish federal harmlessness. Suppression, causal product, attenuation, properly available preservation, good-faith reliance and harmlessness remain separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Nevada Supreme Court judgment is vacated and the case remanded, 8–1. Timing and lawful justification must be addressed before any necessary remaining remedial issues. No suppression, new trial, acquittal, release, damages or capital resentencing is ordered by this disposition.


Source: `state/holdings/civil-rights.md`; section: Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477


#### Heck v. Humphrey, 512 U.S. 477

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-6188.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### Necessary invalidity on the present in-custody record

**Holding:** A state prisoner in Heck's position, in custody with a lawful opportunity to challenge the conviction, has no presently cognizable §1983 damages claim when success necessarily establishes the invalidity of that outstanding conviction or sentence, unless the judgment has undergone qualifying invalidation. The rule decides that setting; it does not make present habeas availability a universal element or decide plaintiffs who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity or whose custody has ended.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly adopt this bounded rule; the distinct sufficient rationales are not added together to obtain its support.

**Current-force modification:** Randell v. Johnson, April 25, 2001, resolves the reserved former-prisoner category to this extent: a necessarily contradictory damages claim needs no prior invalidation when the claimant never had realistic lawful custody review or lost it beyond his control despite timely pursuit. Unexcused loss of a genuinely usable opportunity retains the prerequisite; release or current federal habeas unavailability alone establishes neither the exception nor invalidation. Heck's original in-custody rule, qualifying invalidation routes and deferred accrual for claims still covered remain; independent law governs accrual outside the prerequisite.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 475 (1973): its release-channel distinction informs the new, bounded damages prerequisite; its holding is not misdescribed as already deciding damages-only claims.
- Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539 (1974): preserves civil relief compatible with the operative conviction and custody judgment.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents, 457 U.S. 496 (1982): remains the rule against general §1983 exhaustion; this decision addresses present cognizability instead.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Randell v. Johnson, judgment below 227 F.3d 300, April 25, 2001: former-prisoner realistic-opportunity exception, unexcused-loss boundary and conditional cognizability remedy.

##### Statutory coordination supports this limited prerequisite

**Holding:** For the in-custody, conviction-negating damages claim described in Part I, coordination of §1983 with the available habeas channel supports favorable invalidation as a prerequisite, with the common-law malicious-prosecution analogy implementing that coordination. This is a new, limited restriction on §1983, not conversion of the damages complaint into habeas, a general exhaustion rule, or adoption of every malicious-prosecution element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter; five Justices adopt this sufficient statutory explanation at this limited scope. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg do not join Part II.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Preiser v. Rodriguez: extends statutory coordination to the particular damages claim while preserving the distinct relief and conditions of habeas.
- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves its permissible civil-remedy category; factual overlap with prosecution does not remove a claim from that category.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: distinguishes general exhaustion from the newly adopted prerequisite; exhaustion alone cannot satisfy it.
- Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247 (1978): applies common-law remedial guidance as an aid to construing the federal remedy, not a wholesale importation of a state tort.

##### Qualifying invalidation and deferred accrual

**Holding:** The claim barred under Part I becomes cognizable only when the conviction or sentence has been reversed on direct review, expunged by executive order, declared invalid by a state tribunal authorized to do so, or called into question by a federal court's issuance of a writ of habeas corpus. That claim accrues only upon such invalidation; exhaustion, unsuccessful review, or loss of a review opportunity is not invalidation, and neither immediate accrual with equitable tolling nor a preservation stay is required for this unaccrued claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt the four invalidation routes and the accrual consequence for the barred claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carey v. Piphus: applies its common-law-informed approach to the federal remedy without imposing additional constitutional-liability elements.
- Patsy v. Board of Regents: preserves the distinction between exhaustion and existence of a cognizable damages claim.

##### Independent injuries, claim-specific analysis and this dismissal

**Holding:** A claim whose success does not necessarily establish invalidity falls outside this prerequisite even if it concerns the same investigation or trial; courts must compare the civil claim's elements with the basis of the conviction, and compensatory recovery still requires an actual compensable injury. On the accepted characterization of Heck's presented action, dismissal without prejudice stands on lack of present cognizability, without deciding misconduct, immunity, personal responsibility, preclusion, damages, or entitlement to release.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine directly adopt this boundary, application and remedy.

**Material application:** The lower courts' characterization that Heck's action necessarily attacked the conviction was not disputed in the petition or principal merits brief; the late reply established no separately preserved independent-injury count requiring remand. An unlawful-search claim can remain compatible with a conviction through independent source, inevitable discovery or harmless error, but those possibilities establish neither automatic admissibility of every search claim nor compensation for conviction injury without invalidation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wolff v. McDonnell: preserves the distinction between compatible civil relief and relief dependent on invalidating custody.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: distinguishes its opportunity-only claim; the existing §1983 holding remains intact.
- Carey v. Piphus: applies the requirement of actual compensable injury without adjudicating the amount or merits here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Former prisoners and persons who never had a realistic custody-review opportunity remain outside the question resolved. No universal constitutional malicious-prosecution action, automatic state-law preclusion, automatic habeas conversion or pending-prosecution abstention rule is adopted. Custis's distinct no-meaningful-review reservation in the ACCA channel remains intact. Recognition of a conviction's invalidation by an authorized state tribunal identifies one circumstance in which the present §1983 prerequisite is satisfied. It does not itself create jurisdiction, confer authority to reopen the underlying conviction, or prescribe grounds upon which a state tribunal must grant collateral relief. Those questions remain governed by the independently applicable law authorizing that tribunal's action.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Dismissal without prejudice is affirmed unanimously for lack of present cognizability. The barred claim has not accrued, so its limitations period is not consumed while the prerequisite remains unmet. A later otherwise proper action may follow qualifying invalidation, subject to remaining requirements; no stay, release, automatic conversion to habeas, liability, immunity or damages determination is ordered.

#### McMillian v. Monroe County

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-542.
**Decided:** June 2, 1997.

##### Final policymaking requires identification of the particular entity and function

**Holding:** Under Monell and Jett, the court identifies through state and local law, including custom or usage with legal force, the official who speaks with final policymaking authority for the particular governmental entity concerning the challenged action. Final operational discretion does not alone identify that entity; office labels, election, territory, funding and absence of commission control are each insufficient standing alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final operational discretion and the identity of the represented government are separate; County government need not consist solely of its commission.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Monell requires the institution's own policy. Pembaur preserves a single authorized final decision without treating four-Justice Part II-B as the whole Court's rationale. Praprotnik's plurality preserves legally effective custom, genuine delegation and ratification; Jett Part IV supplies the majority identification rule. Collins and Kinman separate deprivation and institutional attribution. Swint decided appellate jurisdiction; the later permitted §1292(b) route lawfully presents the merits.

##### The Alabama sheriff supplies County policy in this criminal-investigation function

**Holding:** An Alabama sheriff represents the County when setting and executing the law-enforcement policy involved in these criminal-investigation acts. The independently constituted final local office supplies that County authority without a delegation from, or power of review in, the county commission; the holding does not classify every sheriff function or every act as County policy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Material application:** The locally elected, independently final law-enforcement office, County salary and necessary equipment obligations together establish the local institution. State executive placement, immunity classifications, duties to prosecutors and courts, and §36-22-5 written-investigation directions do not displace that autonomous local authority here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No actual State instruction is assumed, and financing is not treated as investigative control. Other functions, including jails, other States, State damages liability and immunity require separate analysis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hereford and Parker v. Amerson inform Alabama structure but do not decide federal attribution. No all-functions rule follows from separate analogies to Parker v. Williams or Hess.

##### Reinstatement leaves violation, attributable policy and moving-force causation to proof

**Holding:** The County-attribution dismissal is reversed and the claim based on the sheriff's final law-enforcement policymaking is reinstated for further proceedings. McMillian must still establish a federal violation and an attributable County policy or final decision that was the moving force behind his injury; the sheriff's institutional status establishes neither the allegations' truth nor liability for every act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the County-attribution dismissal and reinstate the policy claim without a liability finding, damages or compulsory trial. Personal claims and immunity and unreviewed evidentiary rulings remain in their own posture.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Collins retains the separate deprivation and institutional inquiries; Kinman's single-final-decision route makes the claim legally possible without guaranteeing recovery.

#### Carter v. City of Philadelphia

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 98-1581; no Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** March 13, 2000.

##### Whether the Philadelphia District Attorney's own-prosecutor administration is local policy and protected by Pennsylvania's immunity

**Holding:** Pennsylvania's sovereign immunity does not bar the claims challenging this Philadelphia office's training, supervision and administrative policy. For training and supervision of the office's own prosecutors, the District Attorney supplies final local policy under Pennsylvania law; neither conclusion classifies every prosecutorial function or establishes authority over police.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only.

**Material application:** Pennsylvania law, local office control and the City's primary responsibility for judgments establish the relevant local institution. Election, title, territory, funding or final discretion alone would not suffice. The County-officer designation has a home-rule and optional-government exception; the decision does not depend on that label. Defined state intervention for resource insufficiency, conflict or judicially established failure or refusal amounting to abuse does not establish ordinary state control of training.

##### What fault, notice and causal connection a municipal training or supervision omission requires

**Holding:** Carter must prove a federal deprivation attributable to a municipal policy or legally effective custom, a deliberate or conscious training or supervision omission amounting to deliberate indifference, and an identified deficiency closely related to and actually causing his injury. Notice may arise from an established pattern or a sufficiently obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; prior identical incidents are not invariably required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Negligence, employee wrongdoing and hindsight that more training might help do not satisfy municipal fault or cause. Perjury's obvious wrongfulness alone does not show an obvious need for specialized instruction. Any police-control theory requires actual authority and causal proof; cooperation alone creates no employment relationship.

##### Whether heightened pleading can be demanded for this municipal-policy claim

**Holding:** The then-operative Rule 8 pleading standard governs this municipal claim without a judge-created demand for heightened factual particularity about internal policies before discovery. The alleged pattern and policy-related omissions suffice against the categorical dismissal grounds presented, while the substantive liability elements and ordinary procedural controls remain fully applicable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by all seven other Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 11, managed discovery and Rule 56 remain available. Municipal deliberate-indifference allegations do not automatically defeat personal qualified immunity, which depends on clearly established law at the time of the conduct. A municipality cannot borrow personal immunity; otherwise protected advocacy does not lose its functional protection merely through an allegation of dishonesty.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No misconduct, innocence, actual policy, policymaker knowledge, pattern, supervisory control over police, deliberate indifference, causation or damages is found. The Court does not classify all Pennsylvania officials or all District Attorney functions, decide a new Rule 54(b) rule, resolve the annotated State supersession procedure, or impose particular personnel safeguards. The personal and state-law rulings outside the two granted questions receive no new judgment here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reinstatement of the municipal-policy claim within the two granted questions and remand for ordinary proceedings. Liability, damages, any trial and particular safeguards remain unestablished; personal and state-law matters outside the grant receive no new disposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **McMillian v. Monroe County:** Applies its final-authority and governmental-identity inquiry to Pennsylvania's own legal structure. Its Alabama application does not determine every State or every prosecutorial function.
- **Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp.:** Applies the complete structural inquiry; treasury exposure remains highly probative rather than indispensable.
- **Regents of the University of California v. Doe:** Distinguishes a State's primary obligation with collateral indemnity from this independent local office's obligations; the antecedent structural inquiry remains necessary.
- **Monell v. Department of Social Services:** Preserves the requirement of the municipality's own policy or legally effective custom and rejects employer-based liability.
- **City of Canton v. Harris:** Applies deliberate indifference, notice, an identified deficiency and actual causal connection; no universal identical-incident prerequisite or relaxed fault standard is added.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights:** Preserves the separate inquiries into a federal deprivation and municipal responsibility.
- **Plakas v. Drinski:** Applies the distinct deprivation, attribution, fault and causal requirements and the separation between personal immunity and municipal liability.
- **Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit:** Applies ordinary municipal pleading under the Rules and preserves distinct discovery and proof controls.
- **Conley v. Gibson:** Supplies fair-notice pleading under the Rules in force for this action.
- **Buckley v. Fitzsimmons:** Preserves the functional distinction between investigation or administration and protected advocacy; classification of an individual act remains separate.
- **Fletcher v. Kalina:** Preserves otherwise protected advocacy and separate personal evidentiary acts, without a general dishonesty exception or automatic loss of qualified immunity.
- **Chalfin v. Specter, Specter v. Moak, and Duggan v. 807 Liberty Avenue:** Supply their stated Pennsylvania structural classifications; none alone decides federal attribution or every office function.
- **Walker v. City of New York:** Its lower-court elaboration does not become a separate binding Supreme Court test; Canton governs.

#### Randell v. Johnson

**Docket or dockets:** Judgment below, 227 F.3d 300; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** April 25, 2001.

##### Whether the actual civil claim necessarily contradicts an operative custody determination

**Holding:** A section 1983 claim compatible with the valid sentence or custody determination, including a claim that officials disobeyed a valid sentence's terms, requires no favorable invalidation. The court must identify the operative judgment, administrative calculation or custody adjudication and compare it with the precise civil theory; a sentence-administration label neither establishes nor avoids necessary invalidity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The screening record does not establish whether Randell's disputed credit claim enforces a valid sentence or contradicts an operative credit determination. Neither excess-time allegations nor an administrative label answers that question.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck and Edwards require the claim-specific legal-consequence comparison, while Wolff preserves compatible civil injuries and Preiser distinguishes the release channel.

##### Whether absence or faultless loss of realistic custody review permits a necessarily contradictory damages claim

**Holding:** A former prisoner's necessarily contradictory damages claim may proceed without favorable invalidation when no realistic lawful opportunity ever existed to contest the confinement before custody review became unavailable, or when timely pursuit nevertheless lost that opportunity through circumstances beyond the claimant's control. Unexcused failure to use a genuinely available opportunity retains the prerequisite; release or current federal habeas unavailability alone supplies neither this exception nor invalidation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Parts II and III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins to the exception and complete conditional directions. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg join Part I and the judgment but withhold these grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Opportunity depends on supported custody, notice, timing, access and practical ability to pursue the particular challenge. The rule does not demand exhaustion of every conceivable state procedure or repeat review after an unsuccessful meaningful opportunity. It creates no finding of diligence, abandonment, overconfinement or an available remaining procedure. Individual responsibility, constitutional violation, causation, injury, immunity, preclusion, screening and applicable limitations remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the categorical screening dismissal, unanimously, and compare the claim with the actual custody determination. If necessary invalidity exists, apply the five-Justice opportunity rule. Compatible claims and qualifying exception claims may proceed under ordinary civil requirements. A still-covered claim without qualifying invalidation is dismissed without prejudice for present noncognizability and accrues only upon invalidation by direct reversal, executive expungement, an authorized state tribunal or a federal habeas writ. No habeas conversion, exhaustion stay, invented tolling rule, automatic hearing, release or damages award follows; outside-prerequisite accrual remains governed by independent law.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Heck's former-prisoner reservation is partly resolved by this bounded exception and its unexcused-loss limit; its original in-custody rule remains. Edwards retains the same consequence-based classification and the invalidation/accrual rules for claims still covered. Spencer preserved, but did not already resolve, this civil question. Preiser supplies statutory coordination and Patsy prevents conversion of that coordination into general state-remedy exhaustion. Carey preserves proof of compensable injury, not the requested automatic per-day award.

#### Parke v. Raley, 506 U.S. 20 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-719.

**Decided:** December 1, 1992.

##### Initial production burden

**Holding:** In Kentucky’s collateral challenge to a final counseled conviction used for sentence enhancement, due process permits the Commonwealth to prove the judgment and require the defendant to produce evidence of infringement or procedural irregularity. If the defendant refutes regularity, the Commonwealth must affirmatively establish constitutional validity under Kentucky’s procedure; a missing old transcript alone, without alleged official misconduct, does not establish invalidity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238 (1969), distinguished as direct review without weakening its valid-waiver requirement. Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458, 468–469 (1938), applied to collateral regularity. Burgett v. Texas, 389 U.S. 109 (1967), preserved for constitutionally defective uncounseled convictions. Medina v. California (June 22, 1992), distinguished: its narrow competence-burden holding does not establish a controlling categorical ban on practical due-process analysis.

##### Measure of proof

**Holding:** Due process does not require the clear-and-convincing extrarecord proof demanded by the Sixth Circuit to establish validity of an old guilty plea in this collateral sentencing proceeding. Kentucky’s retention of the ultimate persuasion burden does not entail that heightened constitutional quantum; the fact of prior conviction and the validity inquiry remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Boykin is not extended into a clear-and-convincing collateral proof requirement. Zerbst supports the distinction between constitutional validity and a universally heightened burden. The Sixth Circuit’s Dunn v. Simmons, 877 F.2d 1275 (1989), cannot govern insofar as it demands that constitutional quantum and shifts the entire production burden solely because no transcript survives.

##### The 1981 plea

**Holding:** Under then-applicable 28 U.S.C. §2254(d), a competent state court’s factual determination, evidenced by written findings, an opinion or other reliable and adequate written indicia after a merits hearing involving the applicant and the State receives a presumption of correctness subject to the conditions and exceptions below; the ultimate federal question whether a plea was knowing and voluntary remains independently determined. On the established earlier warnings, contemporaneous 1981 charge form and jury warning, representation, education, unimpaired condition and testimony here, Raley’s 1981 plea was knowing and voluntary and the Sixth Circuit’s conditional new-hearing remedy is unwarranted. The presumption does not govern if the applicant establishes, it otherwise appears, or the State admits that (1) the state hearing did not resolve the merits of the factual dispute; (2) its factfinding procedure was inadequate to afford a full and fair hearing; (3) material facts were not adequately developed; (4) the state court lacked subject-matter jurisdiction or jurisdiction over the applicant; (5) an indigent applicant was denied appointed counsel in deprivation of his constitutional right; (6) the applicant did not receive a full, fair and adequate hearing; or (7) the applicant was otherwise denied due process in that proceeding. Under paragraph (8), the presumption also does not govern when the pertinent state record is produced and the federal court, considering that record as a whole, concludes that the factual determination lacks fair record support. At a federal evidentiary hearing, once the state factual determination is duly proved, and absent those circumstances, the applicant must establish its error by convincing evidence. That federal factual-review burden is distinct from Kentucky’s allocation and measure of proof of a prior plea’s constitutional validity. Inferences receive deference only when fairly supported by the facts; no presumption attaches to the ultimate federal legal conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight votes at this level. Blackmun agrees with the result in a separate writing limited to sentencing.

**Current-force modification:** The original statutory factual-presumption and independent-legal-review analysis remains controlling for proceedings governed by former §2254. AEDPA §104 replaces that scheme: new §2254(e)(1) presumes a state factual determination correct and places on the applicant the burden of rebuttal by clear and convincing evidence. New §2254(d) separately forbids granting relief on a claim adjudicated on the merits in state court unless the decision was contrary to or unreasonably applied clearly established federal law determined by this Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the state-court evidence. These new conditions are not the former eight exceptions; enactment supplies no judicial construction of their terms and does not reclassify the underlying constitutional question as a historical fact. The April 24 source admission does not decide this amendment's temporal application to a particular pending proceeding; the original adjudication is unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marshall v. Lonberger, 459 U.S. 422, 431–438 (1983), applied to supported historical inferences and independent legal review. Boykin and North Carolina v. Alford, 400 U.S. 25, 31 (1970), applied to an informed voluntary choice. No later habeas deference standard enters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The ultimate federal validity of the plea remains independently determined. No universal collateral proof quantum or categorical prohibition on collateral review is adopted. Kentucky's separate proof of the fact of prior conviction beyond a reasonable doubt and prosecution burdens on the present offense remain. Earlier experience alone does not establish knowing waiver.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit conditional-writ judgment concerning the 1981 plea; the actual evidentiary showing warrants no new hearing. The unchallenged 1979 determination is undisturbed.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §104, replacement factual-presumption rules and the new state-merits relief conditions. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

#### Cooper v. Oklahoma, 517 U.S. 348

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-5207.
**Decided:** April 16, 1996.

##### Whether the defendant may bear clear-and-convincing persuasion on trial incompetence

**Holding:** Due process forbids requiring a criminal defendant to establish trial incompetence by clear and convincing evidence, because that allocation permits trial when the greater weight of the evidence favors inability to understand the proceedings or rationally assist counsel. Defendant-preponderance persuasion remains permissible at an adequate hearing, as does a competence presumption that merely expresses that allocation without adding evidentiary weight.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Counsel, psychiatric assistance, repeated examinations, and renewed inquiry do not cure the higher ultimate persuasion burden. Timely prosecution and preventing feigning do not justify its added risk. The ultimate finding is distinct from the sufficient-doubt trigger for inquiry; competence is distinct from insanity, guilt, and waiver. The holding applies beyond capital trials and requires neither prosecution persuasion nor one exclusive due-process methodology.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Medina*'s preponderance and allocation-only presumption holdings remain controlling; its reservation of a higher burden is now resolved, without converting its divided methodological discussion into a majority rule. *Dusky*, *Pate*, and *Drope* supply functional capacity and adequate inquiry. *Addington*, *Jones*, and *A. St. P. C.* address different deprivations and directions of risk; *Leland* and *Patterson* do not equate trial competence with insanity, and *Winship*'s offense-element rule is neither applied nor narrowed.

##### Effect of the invalid burden and conditions for preserving the trial judgment

**Holding:** Findings expressly made under the invalid competency burden, and appellate sufficiency review applying it, cannot alone sustain Cooper's conviction and dependent sentence. Preservation requires a reliable retrospective determination of competence at the relevant trial period under the proper functional standard and a lawful allocation; if such an evaluation is unavailable or establishes trial incompetence, the conviction and dependent sentence must be set aside, and retrial may occur only upon competence. The State may elect lawful retrial instead of retrospective review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–III of the same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** The judge expressly used the challenged quantum. Conflicting expert and lay evidence does not establish what a proper factfinder would decide under preponderance; illness and manipulation can coexist, diagnosis does not necessarily prove legal incompetence, and factual understanding alone does not establish rational assistance. Retrospective reliability must account for elapsed time, the disputed condition, and available contemporaneous evidence; the existence of reports and hearings does not itself establish reliability.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse approval of the burden, vacate the dependent appellate affirmance, and remand for the conditional inquiry. A reliable determination sustaining competence may permit preservation through lawful further proceedings. The trial judgment is not unconditionally vacated, and no immediate retrial, acquittal, automatic release, commitment, treatment, present-competence determination, execution ruling, or deadline follows.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No feasibility proof quantum, universal hearing format, structural-error classification, or general harmless-error standard is prescribed. Competence for execution, medication, jury waiver, new commitment, and unrelated guilt or penalty issues are not decided; treatment and restoration retain separate legal predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pate* and *Drope* require meaningful retrospective evaluation rather than presumed reconstruction. *Godinez* preserves functional capacity, renewed inquiry, and the distinction from knowing and voluntary waiver. *Riggins*'s supported medication-related impairment does not establish that reconstruction is impossible here; *Tuggle* supplies no automatic retrial or universal error rule.

#### Neder v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1985.
**Decided:** 1999-06-10.

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, extends the qualified actual-verdict inquiry to its omitted statutory death-eligibility finding. The State bears the beyond-reasonable-doubt noncontribution burden, with actual-instruction, evidentiary-conflict and reliable-adjudication limits; this extension establishes no universal omitted-element or sentencing-error rule.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

##### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

#### Apprendi v. New Jersey, 530 U.S. 466 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-478.
**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### Present-offense facts necessary for a higher maximum require element protections

**Holding:** Under the Sixth Amendment's jury guarantee, applied to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment, and the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause, a fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to authorize punishment above the maximum supported by the verdict or plea is an element. It must be admitted as an element or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt; a sentencing label does not remove those requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg. Six direct rationale votes control the rule, both procedural guarantees, the Count 18 application and the bounded remedy. This is a new constitutional holding; the earlier statutory decisions did not establish it.

**Material application:** The plea's express reservation permits this claim; knowledge of possible exposure and admission of an unlawful purpose to frighten or harass do not admit the additional racial purpose. The lower court's view of strong evidence supplies neither an admission nor a jury verdict. The judgment sustaining the enhanced term is reversed and the term vacated, with the limited remand stated below. This application adopts no universal structural-error classification and does not automatically extend Neder's qualified materiality-error inquiry to every sentencing aggravator or plea defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Facts of prior conviction are not decided; there is no constitutional prior-conviction exception in this holding. Whether, or in what form, a State must charge a covered fact is not decided, and the Fifth Amendment's grand-jury clause is not applied to the States. The federal charging consequences of the earlier statutory decisions remain intact. Judicial factfinding within punishment already authorized by the conviction remains permissible. Unchanged-maximum mandatory minima, general consecutive-sentence rules, capital eligibility or selection, and materially different sentencing systems, including general Guidelines classifications, remain outside this decision. McMillan and Walton are not overruled. No valid waiver of the reserved claim occurred here; any distinct lawful-waiver question retains its own requirements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The New Jersey judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains the twelve-year Count 18 sentence. The constitutional rule adopted here requires vacatur of that enhanced sentence. On the existing admitted offense, a sentence within the five-to-ten-year range is permissible. Any further proceeding must have a basis in state law and comply with the governing plea, notice, waiver, sufficiency and double-jeopardy rules; the Court neither orders nor authorizes a new aggravated prosecution or enhancement jury merely by remanding.

The pleas and convictions remain intact. The Court orders no acquittal, withdrawal of the pleas, specific new sentence, consecutive restructuring, aggregate sentence or release. The separate Graves Act question is not decided; any parole consequence must follow the lawful sentence and that provision's own terms rather than automatic retention of the former four-year figure.

- **Mullaney v. Wilbur:** Supports examination of the fact's legal role rather than its label when necessary facts determine aggravated punishment.
- **Patterson v. New York:** Preserves legislative definition of substantive offenses without permitting a label alone to transfer a covered aggravating element away from the jury.

Ring v. Arizona, June 24, 2002, extends the rule to the statutory aggravator necessary for death eligibility and overrules Walton to that extent. Those former reservations no longer leave the covered allocations open; ordinary factfinding within independently authorized punishment, the unresolved prior-conviction and state-charging questions, and the distinction between capital eligibility and final selection remain at their stated scope.

#### United States v. Angle, 254 F.3d 514 (judgment below)

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit Nos. 96-4662, 96-4672 and 99-4187; consolidated appeals of Corey Angle and James Edward Phifer.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### Present-offense facts changing a binding punishment range receive element protections

**Holding:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to a jury, unless a lawful jury waiver permits the judge to determine the element under that same proof standard. **Proposition-level authority:** Part I-A of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Guidelines remain binding subject to the constitutional conditions. Prior-conviction questions, a general advisory-system remedy and consecutive sentencing independent of the disputed range premise are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Angle's independent findings-based sentence vacatur is affirmed unanimously; six Justices add the constitutional resentencing directions. The lawful range and supported findings within it must be determined without a judicially supplied replacement range.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Apprendi is extended to present-offense facts raising binding Guidelines ceilings or floors. McMillan is overruled insofar as ordinary present-offense sentencing findings may raise a mandatory minimum merely because the maximum is unchanged. Mistretta retains institutional approval of the Commission. Witte and Watts/Putra retain ordinary assessment within independently authorized punishment, subject to the binding-range rule.

##### Federal charging protection accompanies the covered aggravated fact

**Holding:** In a federal prosecution, a present-offense fact performing the aggravating legal work identified in Part I-A must receive the applicable grand-jury charging protection as an element. A quantity-free charge and verdict, ordinary sentencing findings, or notice of possible exposure do not themselves establish that element; a valid element admission or lawful waiver must satisfy its own governing requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins the disposition on this component without adopting the separate charging proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A valid admission or waiver must satisfy its own requirements. The mandate supplies no new charging or jury procedure.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Jones and Castillo supply federal aggravated-element charging consequences while retaining their distinct statutory holdings. Cotton confirms that the constitutional charging omission does not withdraw subject-matter jurisdiction.

**Holding:** Phifer's 292-month drug-count sentence exceeds the 240 months authorized by his quantity-free verdict and is constitutional error. Applying the established review-time rule in the settled-law-then-changed setting, the count excess is plain; substantial-rights effect and discretionary correction remain separate requirements.

##### Whether authorized removal waives immunity from state-permitted state-law claims

**Holding:** A State that authorizes voluntary removal of an action to federal court relinquishes its federal-forum immunity from state-law claims for which it has already waived immunity in its own courts, even though its law expressly reserves federal-forum immunity and does not separately authorize counsel to declare a waiver. **Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Georgia Tort Claims Act's scope and manner conditions survive. The holding does not decide removal of claims immune in the State's own courts, an otherwise valid federal claim, or other defensive participation. # Gibson-v-County-of-Washoe_merits_2003-01-14

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Gibson-v-County-of-Washoe_merits_2003-01-14.md`

## Event

Gibson v. County of Washoe, 290 F.3d 1175. Merits decision, January 14, 2003. On review of the Ninth Circuit's May 22, 2002 judgment reversing summary judgment for the County on supported mental-health policy claims and affirming judgment for the named deputies and supervising sergeant. The question is whether a County policy may cause an actionable denial of a detainee's serious medical needs despite the named officers' lack of the subjective awareness required for personal liability.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## Public Action

Affirmed, 9–0; the supported County screening/evaluation and medication-policy claims return for ordinary further proceedings, with the remaining dispositions preserved.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The supported County claims concerning screening/evaluation and medication handling return for ordinary further proceedings, including trial if the remaining procedural requirements are satisfied. Summary judgment for the named deputies and sergeant, rejection of the unsupported communications-policy theory, and the unappealed consortium and state-law dispositions remain intact.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer joined. The opinion and judgment are unanimous.

## Holdings

### The municipality's own policy may cause a constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** A municipality may be liable under §1983 for its own deliberately indifferent custodial policy that actually causes a denial of constitutionally required medical care, even when the named employees lack the personal awareness required for their liability. The claimant must prove an actual constitutional deprivation, an attributable municipal policy or legally effective custom, a deliberate municipal choice concerning a known or obvious constitutional need, and an identified defect closely related to and actually causing the injury; negligence, employment alone and pooled employee knowledge do not suffice.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court, authored by Stevens and joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls the rule, the summary-judgment application and the limited return for further proceedings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a named employee's exoneration does not collapse the separate inquiries into the deprivation and its municipal source. Monell requires the municipality's own causal policy or custom. Collins separates the underlying constitutional wrong from attribution; the absence of a custody-based duty in its voluntary-employment setting does not govern a pretrial detainee's custody. Canton requires deliberate municipal choice and a close causal connection. Carter permits notice through a pattern or a sufficiently obvious need to perform a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; identical prior injuries are not invariably necessary.

The County correctly insists on an actual deprivation and more than negligent administration. But the named deputies' lack of relevant awareness establishes neither that all required care was furnished nor that a County policy could not disable that care. Farmer's individual-awareness rule does not permit combining employees' innocent information to manufacture personal culpability. The municipal claim instead must identify and prove the County's own deliberately indifferent policy and its injury-producing operation. This preserves the underlying constitutional-injury requirement rather than substituting institutional responsibility for it.

### The supported screening and medication evidence permits further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** At summary judgment, a claimant must identify evidence permitting a reasonable finding on every required municipal-liability element. The evidence concerning delayed mental-health evaluation and handling of psychotropic medication meets that standard here; the surviving claims return under the ordinary preponderance-of-the-evidence burden, while the unsupported communications theory and the preserved personal and unappealed dispositions remain excluded.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the evidence, viewed under Rule 56, supports a distinct policy inquiry. The jail regularly held mentally ill detainees, policymakers knew of that recurring population, and the evidence concerns practices postponing evaluation until an acutely disturbed person cooperated and treating relevant medication as property rather than a signal for assessment. General knowledge of mental illness alone would not suffice. These particular practices, combined with the recurring constitutional responsibility and medication evidence, permit a finding beyond hindsight that more training might have helped.

The County remains entitled to contest policy authority or effective custom, deliberate choice, deprivation and actual cause. Death during restraint does not itself establish a medical-care violation or that a different policy would have prevented the injury. The reasonable-force and personal-awareness rulings therefore survive, but they do not decide the independent medical-policy claim. The communications theory lacks the necessary policymaker-notice support and cannot survive merely by association with the supported policies. On remand, the family must prove the surviving elements and any compensable injury; ordinary factual and remedial adjudication under those rules does not make the municipal standard unresolved.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658:** Applies the municipality's own policy-or-custom and causation requirements; employer-based liability remains forbidden.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115:** Applies the separate inquiries into constitutional deprivation and municipal attribution; distinguishes voluntary employment from actual pretrial custody.
- **City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378:** Applies demanding municipal fault, notice, identified deficiency and causal connection; no negligence or hindsight standard replaces them.
- **Carter v. City of Philadelphia, March 13, 2000:** Applies its pattern-or-obvious-recurring-need notice rule and actual-cause requirement without adding an invariably identical-prior-incident condition.
- **Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825:** Preserves individualized actual awareness and the protection for a reasonable response; neither collective awareness nor a failed reasonable response supplies personal culpability. The distinct municipal inquiry does not alter that rule.

## Law After Decision

Existing municipal and custodial law governs. A municipality cannot obtain judgment automatically from the named employees' lack of personal awareness when supported evidence independently connects an actual medical-care deprivation to its own deliberately indifferent policy. The same demanding deprivation, attribution, fault and causation requirements control analogous custodial policy claims. The decision supplies neither institutional strict liability nor a new aggregate-awareness test. Section 3626's requirements for prospective prison-conditions relief remain unchanged.

# Westside-Mothers-v-Haveman_merits_2002-12-10

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Westside-Mothers-v-Haveman_merits_2002-12-10.md`

## Event

Westside Mothers v. Haveman; judgment below, 289 F.3d 852. Merits decision, December 10, 2002, on full merits submission reviewing the Sixth Circuit's May 15, 2002 judgment reversing categorical dismissal and remanding. The questions are whether the specified Medicaid child screening, diagnostic and treatment requirements confer rights enforceable under §1983, and whether prospective relief against responsible state officials remains available despite the Spending Clause basis and voluntary participation. The plaintiffs allege unmet services, information and arrangements in Michigan's managed-care system; these are allegations rather than adjudicated individual deprivations.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The reviewed child-service and prospective-officer rulings are affirmed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0, within the reviewed child-service questions.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The live child-service claims may proceed under the rules below. The existing transportation preclusion and organizational-standing dispositions retain their stated effect; the separate provider claims remain outside this review.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine join the complete opinion. Scalia files a concurrence, joined by Kennedy, preserving an original-meaning reservation without withholding any join.

## Holdings

### 1. Eligible children may enforce the specified Medicaid benefits

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 1396a(a)(10) and (43)(A)–(C), read with §§1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), give Medicaid-eligible children under twenty-one mandatory individual rights to the defined medical assistance, information, requested screening and arrangements for corrective treatment, enforceable through §1983. The right requires the applicable eligibility, service-category, request, screening-discovered need and coverage conditions stated below; it does not establish a personal right to optimal program administration, staffing, provider rates, overall capacity or a preferred provider.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt this holding and its statutory limits.

**Statutory content and conditions:**

- Section (43)(A) requires informing persons in the State under twenty-one who have been determined eligible for medical assistance including early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services (EPSDT), about those services and the need for age-appropriate immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases.
- Section (43)(B) requires providing or arranging screening whenever requested. Section (43)(C) requires arranging, directly or by referral to appropriate agencies, organizations or individuals, corrective treatment whose need screening discloses. The request condition belongs to screening; the disclosed-need condition belongs to corrective arrangements. Screening of unwilling children is not compelled.
- Section 1396d(r)(1) requires screening at medically and dentally reasonable periodic intervals determined by the State after consultation with recognized child-health organizations, and at other medically necessary intervals. Its minimum comprises a comprehensive health and developmental history addressing physical and mental development, an unclothed physical examination, age-and-history-appropriate immunizations, laboratory tests including age-and-risk-appropriate lead assessment, and health education including anticipatory guidance. Pediatric immunization follows the separately referenced statutory vaccine schedule.
- Sections (r)(2)–(4) separately cover vision, dental and hearing services at professionally reasonable periodic and other medically necessary intervals. Their minima include diagnosis and treatment of vision defects, including eyeglasses; pain and infection relief, tooth restoration and maintenance of dental health; and diagnosis and treatment of hearing defects, including hearing aids.
- Section (r)(5) covers necessary health care, diagnosis, treatment and other measures of the kinds described in §1396d(a) to correct or ameliorate physical or mental defects, illnesses or conditions discovered through screening, whether or not the State plan otherwise covers the service. Actual statutory service-category, need and coverage conditions remain.
- A provider qualified to supply one or more EPSDT items may supply them without qualification for every item. Section (43)(D)'s reporting and participation measures are not converted into additional individual rights by this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Blessing's provision-specific inquiry. Congress identifies the eligible child and the mandatory benefit, and the service definitions supply standards courts can apply through medical evidence. Wright and Wilder show that clinical or administrative judgment does not make a defined entitlement judicially unmanageable. The officials' strongest objection treats the plan as an undertaking only to the federal administrator. But the particular child-directed benefits, together with §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10's rejection of state-plan placement as a categorical bar, defeat that objection. Suter retains its different reasonable-efforts and reporting holdings; it establishes no contrary rule for every plan provision. Gonzaga's FERPA-specific outcome does not erase Blessing or turn its four-Justice general formulation into controlling law.

Enforcement remains a separate inquiry. Blessing and Bruneau require actual congressional foreclosure, and §1396c's funding oversight supplies no sufficiently comprehensive incompatible individual remedy. Managed-care contracts do not eliminate the State's financing, informing and arrangements obligations. They establish neither an actual deprivation nor a guarantee that state employees personally render treatment. Aggregate deficiencies may prove an identified child's violation or support fitted relief; they cannot create a different personal entitlement.

### 2. Accepted federal duties may be enforced prospectively against responsible officials

**Holding and operative rule:** A valid Spending Clause enactment remains federal law when its accepted conditions govern a participating State. Officials actually connected to a continuing violation of these individual federal rights may be ordered to comply prospectively; relief must address the proved future violation and may not award retrospective State compensation or compel obedience to state law.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Ex parte Young's connected-official route. Pennhurst's funding analogy explains clear notice and acceptance, while Bennett rejects reducing an enacted condition to an ordinary bilateral promise. Michigan's voluntary choice to participate does not make compliance voluntary while it participates. The State's real interest and implementation discretion therefore do not sustain categorical dismissal.

Verizon requires examination of the actual defendant, continuing federal violation and remedy. Coeur d'Alene rejects an additional generalized sovereign-interest barrier. Medicaid's general funding supervision supplies no exclusive remedial sequence displacing this action. Allegations of a continuing deprivation suffice against the categorical defense at this stage; coercive relief requires proof. Edelman permits the prospective costs of lawful compliance but bars retrospective treasury compensation, and Pennhurst separately bars state-law commands. The source-of-law conclusion, individual right, §1983 channel and immunity boundary remain distinct. Neither voluntary participation nor this officer action establishes consent to direct State damages, and the Court announces no separate private cause under the Supremacy Clause.

### 3. The live child-service claims return for adjudication under those rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit is affirmed insofar as it rejected the categorical defenses and allowed the reviewed child-entitlement action to proceed. On remand each live claimant must establish standing and eligibility, the applicable statutory version and obligation, concrete deprivation, responsible official, continuing breach and causation before a court awards relief fitted to that violation.

**Authority:** All nine join this application and remand in the same Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Dismissal prevented findings on actual service failures and the proper scope of relief. Blessing requires matching the asserted right to the claimant, conduct, provision and remedy. An established statewide pattern may support relief connected to proved violations, but a statistical shortfall alone warrants neither a new personal right nor automatic redesign of the system. These are factual and remedial tasks under the rule just stated.

The December 1999 transportation-assistance preclusion ruling was not appealed and remains effective. The Michigan League for Human Services' unappealed dismissal, Michigan Welfare Rights Organization's affirmed dismissal and the professional associations' restored standing retain the posture fixed below. The distinct provider and professional-association claims lie outside the granted child-service questions; this decision adds no individual provider-rate entitlement under §1396a(a)(30)(A).

## Precedent Treatment

- Blessing v. Freestone: applies particular-right, foreclosure and fitted-remedy inquiries; the general program-adequacy exclusion remains.
- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District: applies the distinction between an existing right and actual remedial foreclosure; Medicaid is not treated as substantively identical to Title IX.
- Gonzaga University v. Doe: distinguishes the FERPA entitlement and preserves its outcome and the nonmajority status of its broader rights formulation.
- Suter v. Artist M.: preserves its actual reasonable-efforts, reporting and separate implied-action holdings and its independent case-plan question; no categorical state-plan bar is applied.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority; Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association: apply defined statutory entitlement and manageable qualitative application; regulations may elaborate an existing right but do not create the right held here.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman; Bennett v. Kentucky Department of Education: preserve clear notice and acceptance while rejecting a contract-only account of enacted law; Pennhurst's separate state-law-relief bar remains.
- Ex parte Young; Edelman v. Jordan; Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe; Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission: apply the connected-official, ongoing-federal-violation and prospective-remedy rules, preserving retrospective and state-law limits.
- Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas: its distinct express-authorization and Fourteenth Amendment enforcement holdings do not alter this statutory right or prospective-officer route.

## Law After Decision

Eligible children may enforce the identified mandatory Medicaid child benefits under §1983, subject to their exact statutory conditions and proof of violation. Responsible officials remain amenable to prospective compliance relief. This provision-specific application adds the child-service holding while preserving the existing right, foreclosure and remedy framework; it creates no right to general administrative perfection.

That reservation does not support treating a federal enactment as nonlaw or refusing the established inquiry into these defined benefits. They adopt no additional restriction on the Court's operative rule or relief.
