#### International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc. v. Lee, 505 U.S. 672 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-155.
**Decided:** June 26, 1992.

For the separate distribution judgment, see Lee v. International Society for Krishna Consciousness, Inc., June 26, 1992, No. 91-339, under this subject. The companions use the same entering-law baseline.

##### Whether continuous or repetitive solicitation and immediate personal receipt may be prohibited in the controlled terminal interiors

**Authority of the judgment:** The per curiam judgment affirms denial of relief, 6–3, with Stone, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas supporting. White's nonpublic-forum opinion has four supporters: White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas. Stone assumes public-forum protection and Kennedy applies it after classifying the common areas as public; their separate Ward applications do not join White's classification or reasonableness test. No controlling validity rationale or airport classification exists under Marks: different classification predicates and rules are not demonstrated logical subsets merely because one is more demanding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment binds the parties only within the immediate-payment construction. Requests for later support, general donation appeals, distribution, literature sales, airline-leased areas and secure operational areas are not resolved by a controlling rationale. No exception to viewpoint neutrality or new category of unprotected solicitation is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit's denial of declaratory and injunctive relief remains effective for continuous or repetitive solicitation coupled with immediate personal receipt. No additional injunction or remand is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marks preserves the absence of a demonstrated common ground. Perry and Cornelius retain their forum doctrine; Ward retains its content-neutral tailoring requirements. Kokinda is not converted into a majority airport-classification rule, and no precedent is overruled.

#### Edenfield v. Fane, 507 U.S. 761 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1594.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

##### Real advancement must be established in the relevant professional setting

**Holding:** When truthful, nonmisleading solicitation concerns lawful CPA services, the State must demonstrate that the harms supporting its restriction are real and that the restriction alleviates them to a material degree. Florida’s unsupported predictions about direct solicitation of business clients do not satisfy that burden; the ruling neither requires empirical studies in every case nor immunizes deceptive or coercive solicitation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No strict scrutiny, universal empirical-studies requirement, or least-restrictive-means rule is adopted. Fraud, coercion and materially different professional settings remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the judgment and injunction against the challenged CPA business-solicitation prohibition; no damages or new licensing rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: applies the real-advancement burden.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469: retains reasonable fit without least restrictive means.
- Ohralik v. Ohio State Bar Assn., 436 U.S. 447: distinguished by professional setting, vulnerability and pressure.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: alternative time/place/manner characterization cannot cure deficient advancement.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: consistent application; no expansion to strict scrutiny.

#### City of Ladue v. Gilleo, 512 U.S. 43

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1856.

**Decided:** 1994-06-13.

##### The replacement prohibition leaves no adequate residential channel

**Holding:** A city violates the First and Fourteenth Amendments when, even assuming content neutrality, it virtually eliminates residents' signs expressing their own views at home without adequate alternative channels preserving that distinctive communicative opportunity. Ladue's replacement prohibition fails that requirement; the judgment protecting Gilleo's residential expression is affirmed, while otherwise lawful controls of size, number, placement, illumination and safety remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the same residential-channel holding and limited remedy; no fractured-opinion inference is necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding, that the exemptions are content neutral. It does not decide their independent validity, a formal severability determination concerning the exemptions, the unpreserved six-square-foot substitute, or any new ordinance. The old variance procedure is not restored. The separate fee adjustment is outside this merits review. No absolute right to any size, number, illumination or placement of sign is recognized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Linmark Associates v. Willingboro, 431 U.S. 85: applies its inquiry into whether costlier or less effective alternatives adequately replace signs; its reservation concerning genuinely neutral regulation remains.
- Members of City Council v. Taxpayers for Vincent, 466 U.S. 789: distinguishes its public-property setting and preserved private channels; its recognition of substantial aesthetic interests remains intact.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: applies the requirement of adequate alternative channels while preserving its rejection of a general least-restrictive-means requirement.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: preserves attention to actual burden and fit without importing a rule against incremental regulation; the present holding does not resolve every exemption's content classification.
- Metromedia v. San Diego, 453 U.S. 490: preserves its fractured billboard decision and the distinction between regulating a medium's physical effects and suppressing protected expression; no unified rationale is attributed to all its writers.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's judgment and the injunction protecting Gilleo's residential expression are affirmed. No exemption is deleted, old variance procedure restored, replacement ordinance imposed or separate fee ruling altered.

#### Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-329.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

##### Religious viewpoint in the private publication program

**Holding:** The University may not exclude an otherwise eligible private student publication from its generally available printing benefit because its editorial viewpoint is religious. The rule applies to this privately edited, independently attributed publication program on its otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms; it does not require funding every religious activity or every subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt the program-specific viewpoint rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Recognition, facilities, distribution and money remain distinct; not every public expenditure creates a speech forum. The publication remains independently edited and attributed under required disclaimers and approved vendor-payment terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: preserves limited-forum definition while forbidding viewpoint discrimination.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches; Widmar v. Vincent: apply private religious-viewpoint equality without assuming that facility access decides every subsidy.
- Regan v. Taxation With Representation: distinguishes definition of a government financing program from discriminatory exclusion within this opened private-publication arrangement.

##### Permissible printing support on the actual program terms

**Holding:** Providing this neutral printing benefit to the otherwise eligible private religious publication does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Its secular student-expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent editorial attribution and controlled vendor payments matter together; neutrality alone is not a universal authorization of devotional financing or governmental sponsorship of worship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas join this concrete program conclusion. Lemon remains governing law; no new universal framework is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry remains; neutral availability alone neither authorizes unrestricted devotional financing nor governmental worship. Compelled-fee challenges, unrestricted religious aid and particular awards are outside review.

The denial concerned religious activity, defined by the Guidelines as primarily promoting or manifesting belief in or about deity or ultimate reality, rather than an established finding of religious-organization ineligibility. The organization rule separately concerns devotion as organizational purpose. Political restrictions separately exclude principal-purpose lobbying and candidate electioneering while permitting incidental or insubstantial advocacy, nonpartisan study/research and ideological views; those permissions are not exceptions to the religious-activity rule. Permitted ability-to-perform membership limits likewise remain distinct. Abandoned free-exercise/state individual-rights claims and unchallenged equal-protection intent findings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lemon v. Kurtzman: retains the existing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry; no universal replacement is adopted.
- Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind; Mueller v. Allen: apply neutral-benefit and private-direction distinctions within the actual program, not neutrality alone.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel v. Grumet: distinguishes denominational governmental authority and retains religious-neutrality limits.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills: preserves the actual antecedent-law remand; supplies no religious-aid merits holding here.
- Lee v. Weisman: preserves limits on governmental religious participation and coercion; this private printing benefit compels no student worship.
- Church of Scientology v. Clearwater: distinguishes RFRA burdens and exemptions from constitutional financing authority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for removal of the religious-viewpoint exclusion under otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms. Examine any properly available independent Virginia funding defense consistently with the federal viewpoint holding, without declaring applicability, validity or revival of forfeited defenses. No full payment, damages, immunity, fee or compelled-fee refund is determined.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms. It concerns financing, not merely facility access; it neither decided Agostini's public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.

#### 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island, 517 U.S. 484

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1140.
**Decided:** May 13, 1996.

##### Material advancement and reasonable fit of truthful-price restrictions

**Holding:** Even under ordinary *Central Hudson* review and treating temperance as substantial, Rhode Island has not demonstrated that these truthful-price restrictions directly and materially reduce consumption. The holding concerns this record and restriction, not every possible effect of advertising, prices, or demand; supported prediction remains permissible without a mandatory state-specific numerical study or experimental certainty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five. Stevens affirmatively adopts this independently sufficient conditional application without accepting consumer ignorance as legitimate or ordinary scrutiny as the complete constitutional account.

**Material application:** The State's search-cost/price-dispersion mechanism and opposing experts' concessions of price responsiveness and theoretical plausibility are material but do not overcome the trial finding of no significant consumption effect. General-advertising studies with control problems, missing estimates connecting this restriction's projected price change to consumption, and possible market-share gains without aggregate consumption gains fail to establish material advancement. Constitutional sufficiency is independently assessed without turning appellate economic inference into a new trial finding.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming material advancement, continuously suppressing this category of truthful retail-price information is disproportionate to the temperance objective on this record. Concrete direct price and tax alternatives expose inadequately justified fit; they are neither mandatory replacements nor a least-restrictive-means rule.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven. O'Connor and Breyer assume advancement; Souter adopts both failures; Stevens's application remains conditional. Scalia's judgment vote and Thomas's categorical theory supply no additional balancing votes.

**Material application:** Interstate-shopping concerns do not explain why the State's indirect price mechanism avoids the same leakage or justify the informational burden. Section 3-8-7 permits merchandise-attached or merchandise-placed price signs or tags inside licensed premises under departmental rules; Regulation 32 forbids references visible from outside Class A premises. Section 3-8-8.1 separately excepts recognized, authorized trade journals. These exceptions and lawful nonprice promotion neither automatically defeat nor establish fit; the challenged category is complete subject to those exact exceptions, rather than a ban on all alcohol speech.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary *Central Hudson/Fox* review remains operative. No majority adopts a special-care or heightened-review tier, categorical anti-ignorance rule, universal alternatives checklist, all-sources-at-once requirement, or independent interstate-information immunity. False or misleading speech, unlawful promotions, and direct alcohol controls retain their own governing law; the Court selects no tax or minimum-price program and decides no antitrust dispute.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Edenfield*, *Ibanez*, *Lanphere*, and *Rubin* require proof responsive to the actual restriction. *Fox* supplies reasonable proportional fit; *Discovery Network* and *Rubin* support concrete comparisons without least-restrictive-means review. *Edge* retains its record-specific meaningful-reduction holding; *Florida Bar*'s invalidation does not prohibit supported prediction. *Midcal* and *324 Liquor* invalidate particular price arrangements, not every state-imposed price control. Subsequent searching scrutiny denies automatic *Posadas* authorization here, but broader repudiation of its paternalism and greater-power reasoning has only four votes. *Queensgate* remains a summary merits disposition confined by *Mandel* to what it necessarily decided, rather than a rule that comparable restrictions invariably survive later developed advancement-and-fit doctrine. *Bigelow* supplies no separate adopted interstate holding.

##### Effect of the Twenty-first Amendment on speech protection

**Holding:** Alcohol-control authority supplies no additional presumption relaxing First Amendment protection for these off-premises truthful-price restrictions. Authority over lawful sales does not itself validate suppression of accurate information, and accepting a license does not surrender speech rights.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-A, unanimous; this agreement does not attribute balancing to Scalia or Thomas.

##### The broader speech rule and LaRue's enhanced-deference rationale

**Holding:** The Twenty-first Amendment does not qualify the First Amendment's protection of speech. *LaRue*'s reliance on the Twenty-first Amendment to enhance deference is disavowed; its holding remains supported by ordinary police powers over the regulated conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; six. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor and Breyer do not join this broader rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *LaRue*'s identified rationale loses force, but its conduct holding and *Bellanca*'s conduct result remain intact. The broader rule concerns speech protection, not invalidity of every direct alcohol restriction.

##### Operative relief on both preserved speech claims

**Holding:** Reversal requires implementation of the district court's constitutional declaration against the challenged prohibitions in §§3-8-7 and 3-8-8.1 and Regulation 32; the completed record requires no new merits trial. The Court issues no additional injunction directive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part V-B, joined by all seven other Associates; eight. Reversal on both plaintiffs' First Amendment claims is unanimous; Stone-Zsela would additionally direct a prospective injunction, which is not the mandate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the operative October 24 First Circuit judgment and remand to implement the declaration. The paid $400 fine and unappealed administrative order are not reopened; no damages or refund is awarded. Peoples' waived Commerce Clause claim remains outside review. General enforcement authority in §3-8-8 and every other alcohol-advertising rule are not independently invalidated.

#### Settle v. Dickson County School Board

**Docket or dockets:** Sixth Circuit No. 93-6207; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 25, 1996.

##### What genuine curricular grounds may limit a religious research topic

**Holding:** A teacher may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns, and a student's religious convictions create no exemption from them. But curricular discretion does not make categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal prohibition on academic religious subjects, a legitimate ground merely because it is described as educational judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The teacher-only submission is not presumed publicly attributable to the school or an open forum. Objective inquiry may be distinguished from devotion or personal testimony. Genuine factual or evaluative mistakes can remain pedagogical judgments, while a legally impermissible categorical religious ban does not become valid as an honest academic mistake. The full policy permits objective, respectful, balanced academic religious treatment without promoting tenets or demeaning belief and accommodates student-initiated belief or nonbelief; neither provision guarantees approval.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal curricular viewpoint neutrality, unrestricted topic choice, religion-based exemption, perfect-accuracy duty or independent Free Exercise, Establishment or equal-protection holding.

##### Whether the summary-judgment record establishes lawful cause or the same-decision defense

**Holding:** Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible pedagogical justification when supported evidence permits a material inference that a student's work was rejected for an impermissible religious reason rather than legitimate application of assignment requirements. Under Mt. Healthy, the student must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role; defendants may then prove by a preponderance that the same adverse decision would have occurred without that conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Failure to secure permission was supported but its independent causal effect was disputed by the next-day no-penalty alternative, mixed reasons and original-choice answer, which also covered Buddha and Muhammed. Faith alone does not prove inability to research. Familiarity does not prove absence of new inquiry. Source requirements must reflect permitted secondary and outside sources, not a new Bible-only or school-library-only premise. Comparator descriptions are relevant without proving identical circumstances.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No pretext, hostility, final causation, liability or automatic defeat of a proved same-decision defense is found. Waters supplies its shared genuine-ground distinction, not an imported employment investigation code. Graduation proves no universal mootness; actual capacities, municipal attribution, immunity and remedies remain open. Conduct-date immunity notice concerns March–April 1991, not later decisions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated and remanded, 9–0, for ordinary proceedings on the free-speech claim. No compulsory approval, reading, grading, credit, removal of the zero, damages, new discovery procedure or trial; another supported lawful disposition remains possible.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier, 484 U.S. 260:** Applies legitimate pedagogical control to this graded research assignment, preserving genuine method and quality decisions and reasonable academic error. Its public-attribution rationale is not automatically transferred to a teacher-only submission.
- **Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503:** Preserves student expression without creating unrestricted topic choice or displacing genuine assignment requirements.
- **Epperson v. Arkansas, 393 U.S. 97:** Applies the distinction between curricular authority and constitutional immunity, and between objective academic study of religion and governmental religious instruction. No new religion-clause claim is adjudicated.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District, June 7, 1993; Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, June 29, 1995; and Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue, December 11, 1995:** Preserve the distinction between genuine program boundaries and religious-viewpoint exclusion in their actual settings. They do not classify this assignment as a forum or establish a right to a particular grade.
- **Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992, and Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21, February 12, 1996:** Preserve their limits on official worship and coercive religious participation without creating a blanket prohibition on academic religious subjects.
- **Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994:** Applies the genuine-reliance and causation distinction at its shared scope. Its separate investigation approaches and public-employment balance are not adopted as a classroom test.
- **Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274:** Applies the claimant's protected-conduct and substantial-or-motivating-factor burden and defendants' separate preponderance same-decision defense. A possible lawful reason is not sufficient proof, and partial improper motivation does not automatically establish entitlement to relief.

No precedent is overruled.

#### Arkansas Educational Television Commission v. Forbes

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-779.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### 1. This selectively composed candidate debate is a nonpublic forum

**Holding:** A public broadcaster's candidate debate is a nonpublic forum when its actual program retains editorial selection and has not been opened generally to every qualified candidate. Exclusions must be reasonable in light of the program's journalistic purpose and viewpoint neutral; neither public ownership nor the label journalism supplies immunity from those requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. That six-Justice coalition controls both holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The candidates express private views, but the actual program retains individual editorial selection. A generally opened debate presents a different question. Neither public identity nor the broadcaster’s own label establishes immunity from viewpoint review.

##### 2. The supported exclusion is reasonable and viewpoint neutral

**Holding:** A genuine assessment of public support and journalistic significance may justify exclusion from this selective debate when it reasonably serves the program's purpose and is not a pretext for viewpoint discrimination. The established criteria and application satisfy that rule here; the Constitution imposes no additional prerequisite that future criteria be published.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The supported public-support and journalistic criteria bear on the program, and the verdict rejects political pressure and viewpoint disagreement. Ballot qualification alone does not require admission. Sham criteria, incumbent favoritism, viewpoint pretext and inconsistent denial under actual open-access terms remain prohibited; no numerical formula or universal written policy is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed, 6–3, with directions to enter judgment for the Commission on the reviewed forum claim. No future criteria-publication condition, damages, immunity ruling or compulsory broadcast is ordered; the abandoned statutory-access claim is not revived.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Cornelius and Perry Education:** Applied to actual selective access and reasonable, viewpoint-neutral restrictions.
- **Rosenberger:** Preserves private viewpoints within the actual program rather than compelling universal access.
- **Good News/Good Sports and Bronx Household:** Preserve actual comparators and genuine forum boundaries; the planned selective debate differs from broad rental access.
- **Denver Area:** No universal public-broadcast classification or government editorial immunity is drawn from its separate components or incompatible rationales.


#### Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1835.
**Decided:** 1999-02-17.

##### Assigned instructional production and the asserted personal speech right

**Holding:** A public-school teacher's selection, production, and direction of material as part of the assigned school instructional program constitute institutional curricular expression subject to the school system's lawful control; the artistic discretion entrusted to the teacher does not, on this claim, create a personal First Amendment right against curricular and related employment control. This rule concerns the assigned school production, and does not authorize punishment of separate citizen speech or an independently unconstitutional classification or exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's complete opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, supplies five direct rationale votes.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier: applies the distinction between personal student expression and school-sponsored educational activity; its student-speech rule is not converted into an instructor's personal control over institutional content.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board, June 25, 1996: preserves genuine curricular authority and the prohibition on categorical religious exclusion masquerading as pedagogy; distinguishes the student's limited interest within an assignment from the teacher's asserted interest in administering the program.
- Jeffries v. Harleston, November 17, 1997: distinguishes the unaffiliated citizen address expressly outside assigned official work; its protection, actual-cause rule, and limited mandate remain intact.
- Brewster v. Board of Education, August 17, 1998: distinguishes the particular internal attendance-error report and conduct-date immunity question; no general rule withdrawing protection from workplace reports follows.
- Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994: preserves its limited majority propositions on genuine lawful grounds and mistaken accounts of protected employee speech; supplies no personal curricular entitlement or universal investigation procedure.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle: preserves the claimant's protected-conduct and substantial-or-motivating-factor showing and the employer's separate preponderance same-decision defense; no defense is found proved here.
- Pickering v. Board of Education, Connick v. Myers, and Givhan v. Western Line Consolidated School District: retain contextual public-employee speech protection, including private speech; the Court adopts no categorical workplace or private-concern exclusion.
- Epperson v. Arkansas and Keyishian v. Board of Regents: preserve independently applicable constitutional limits on educational authority; the curricular label does not establish immunity from them.

No precedent is overruled.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The teacher's delegated selection and direction of this school instructional production do not supply the personal speech entitlement claimed against curricular and related employment control. That limited institutional-content rule leaves student expression, separate citizen speech, independent constitutional prohibitions, and other sources of employment protection in place. The decision adopts no universal rule for government employees' assigned speech and no constitutional safe harbor based on any conceivable pedagogical reason.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The dismissal of the granted First Amendment claim remains in force. The Court orders no further remedy and does not reopen claims outside the grant.

#### U.S. West, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 98-9518; judgment below, 182 F.3d 1224. No Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** February 7, 2000.

##### Whether the marketing-selected use condition requires independent commercial-speech scrutiny

**Holding:** A rule conditioning use of information on its intended use for truthful, lawful commercial solicitation receives commercial-speech review even when the carrier lawfully possesses the information and other marketing remains available. Protecting confidential calling records against unknowing use outside the customer's chosen service relationship is a substantial interest, but the government must independently establish material advancement and reasonable fit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, unanimous; Stevens and Kennedy join only this part.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The information is confidential, individually identifiable service-derived CPNI, including calling patterns and billing information; subscriber lists are excluded and aggregate information receives separate treatment. Corporate or carrier status, lawful custody, an unchallenged statutory baseline and other marketing opportunities do not eliminate speech review. Neither competition nor a new constitutional data-ownership right supplies an alternative sufficient ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lanphere and United Reporting supply the speech-selected-use trigger. Went For It preserves both substantial privacy and the need for supported application. Paul P. and Condon remain confined to their different questions.

##### Whether affirmative approval materially advances confidentiality for new-category marketing

**Holding:** On this record, requiring approval before using confidential service-derived CPNI to market an unsubscribed service category directly and materially protects against unknowing repurposing outside the existing relationship. The service-related and distinct research permissions do not defeat that application; this holding neither treats all internal handling as harmful nor validates every collateral CPNI restriction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The existing-relationship permissions concern marketing within subscribed local, interexchange and commercial-mobile categories and sharing among affiliates that already supply those categories when the customer subscribes to more than one. The distinct permissions cover inside-wiring installation, maintenance and repair; mobile-service health-effects research; and former adjunct-to-basic services. Section 222(d)'s separate permissions cover service initiation, rendering, billing and collection; protection against fraudulent, abusive or unlawful service use and protection of carrier rights and property; and customer-approved inbound telemarketing, referral or administrative services during that customer-initiated call, not indefinite later marketing. Those permissions do not reproduce unconsented new-category marketing. The integrated-firm privacy assessment is counterevidence, not a finding that every later purpose falls within the original relationship. No external disclosure or individual injury is found; confidentiality rather than suppression of lawful product knowledge is essential to the holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Edenfield's real-harm and material-advancement burden is applied without a mandatory-study rule. Discovery Network, Rubin and Greater New Orleans require the actual permission comparison; Lanphere and Went For It are distinguished at their public-information setting without weakening them.

##### Whether the flexible affirmative-approval mechanism reasonably fits the protected interest

**Holding:** Oral, written or electronic affirmative approval, lasting until revoked or limited and preceded by intelligible notice, reasonably and proportionately fits the confidentiality interest in this challenged new-category use. The government need not select opt-out merely because it burdens the carrier less, but neither opt-out's general constitutional invalidity nor a least-restrictive-means requirement follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, supported by the same seven Justices: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Approval can be oral, written or electronic and lasts until revoked or limited. The carrier must prove compliant oral approval and keep notice and approval records at least one year. Prior intelligible, nonmisleading notice identifies information, recipients, purposes, confidentiality rights and duty, precise approval/denial steps, revocation or limitation, and that refusal leaves subscribed service unaffected. Written notice must be legible and readily apparent, and any partial translation requires translation of the whole. Solicitation must be proximate to notice; it may describe tailored-offering benefits and possible disclosure on affirmative written request, but may not encourage freezing third-party access. The removed same-document rule is distinct. Mixed response evidence and expense remain real burdens; silence, nonresponse and the outbound hang-up/no-further-call category do not establish informed refusal or consent. Other marketing channels mitigate rather than erase the burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fox supplies proportional fit without least restrictive means. Edenfield, Lanphere and Went For It retain the burden of a supported application; Greater New Orleans does not transform every less burdensome alternative into a constitutional command.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the First Amendment ground for vacatur of the challenged approval application. The separate statutory question and bounded implementation remedy appear under Administrative Law — Telecommunications customer approval, U.S. West, February 7, 2000, Tenth Circuit No. 98-9518.

#### Henerey v. City of St. Charles, School District

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 98-3439; no Supreme Court docket number supplied.

**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### 1. This supervised campaign is governed by the school-sponsored-expression standard

**Holding:** A school-created student-government campaign administered through advisers, campaign rules and material approval as an educational exercise is school-sponsored expression governed primarily by Hazelwood's legitimate-pedagogical-reason standard. **Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Parts I-II, unanimous.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, Parts I-II, unanimous.

**Material application:** The stated policy calls for submission by the preceding school day but expressly permits more than one school day for difficult material; it is not a guarantee of approval within twenty-four hours. Approval of a slogan does not approve every object carrying it.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The defense summary judgment is affirmed on the actual approval-process ground, without a remand, installation in office or damages award.

#### Trans Union Corp. v. Federal Trade Commission

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 00-1141 (245 F.3d 809 and 267 F.3d 1138).
**Decided:** June 24, 2002.

##### The identified marketing use receives independent speech scrutiny

**Holding:** Restricting sale of these credit-derived lists for commercial targeting requires independent review under the governing commercial-speech standard, even though the agency lawfully possesses the data and can market through other channels. The government must establish a substantial interest, direct material advancement and a reasonable proportional fit; corporate status, payment and lawful possession do not themselves answer those questions or classify every publication as commercial speech.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous; Thomas joins the complete sufficient application while reserving a broader theoretical view.

**Material application:** The marketed names and addresses were selected through credit-file attributes, so membership could communicate the selected financial trait. The commercial classification rests on that targeting function, not profit or corporate identity alone.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory classification and administrative-law claims outside the granted speech review, public-concern publication and independently acquired information are not decided. The following statutory permissions and duties bound all three holdings in this entry.

These are the statute's permissions and duties, not findings that any consumer, reporting agency, recipient or offer complied.

**Consumer direction and financial purposes.** Section 604(a)(2), 15 U.S.C. §1681b(a)(2), permits furnishing in accordance with the consumer's written instructions; silence or failure to opt out is not an instruction. Section 604(c)(1)(A) separately permits consumer authorization for the agency to provide that report to that recipient in a credit or insurance transaction not initiated by the consumer. That clause does not itself require a writing and does not authorize unrelated marketing generally. For the credit and insurance routes, the agency must have reason to believe the recipient intends use for credit extension, account review or collection involving the consumer, or insurance underwriting involving the consumer. Under section 604(a)(3)(F), the agency must have reason to believe that the recipient has a legitimate business need in connection with a consumer-initiated transaction or to review an account for continued compliance with its terms. Section 603(m) excludes a relationship holder's review of the consumer's existing account or insurance policy, or collection of that account, from the unsolicited-transaction definition; it does not create a general new-marketing permission.

**Offers without affirmative authorization.** Section 604(c)(1)(B) permits the unsolicited credit or insurance route only when the transaction consists of a firm offer, the agency has complied with subsection (e), and no effective subsection (e) election excludes the consumer's name and address. Under section 603(l), the offer must be honored if the consumer meets the specific consumer-report criteria used for selection. Further conditions may require application information establishing satisfaction of specific creditworthiness or insurability criteria fixed before selection for deciding whether to extend the offered credit or insurance; verification of continuing satisfaction of selection criteria through a report, application or other relevant creditworthiness or insurability information, or verification of application information to determine satisfaction of the applicable creditworthiness or insurability criteria; and collateral required before selection and disclosed in the offer. These are conditional offers, not guaranteed extensions, and the statute supplies no minimum economic value or favorable-rate requirement.

**Information and use.** Under section 604(c)(2), that route permits only names and addresses, a nonunique identifier used solely to verify identity, and other information that does not identify the consumer's relationship or experience with a particular creditor or other entity. The last category and list membership can disclose a financial trait; the permission is not confined to two address fields. Section 604(c)(3) separately bars furnishing unsolicited-transaction inquiry records except section 609(a)(5)'s disclosure to the consumer, on request with proper identification, of inquiries identifying that consumer during the preceding year. Section 604(f) separately requires an authorized furnishing purpose and a prospective user's general or specific purpose certification under section 607. A permitted transfer does not authorize unrestricted subsequent use.

**Consumer elections and agency duties.** Section 604(e) allows notice through the agency's notification system or its signed election form. Exclusion begins five business days after notice and applies to affiliates. System-only notice lasts two years beginning five business days after notice, unless cancelled sooner; a signed-form election continues until cancellation through the system. The agency must explain the two-year limit after system notice and provide the signed form within five business days if requested during that notice. Its system must include a toll-free telephone number and allow notice with appropriate identification. Publication at least annually in a general-circulation publication for the service area must describe possible file use and give the exclusion address and toll-free number; the original deadline was 365 days after September 30, 1996. A qualifying system and publication maintained for affiliates satisfies those obligations for them; nationwide reporting agencies must maintain the system jointly. These safeguards do not make an election immediately effective, a telephone election perpetual, or general publication proof of individual awareness.

**Separate duties of users.** Section 615(d), 15 U.S.C. §1681m(d), requires each written solicitation based on the firm-offer route to provide a clear and conspicuous statement disclosing use of report information, satisfaction of selection criteria, any applicable selection or further creditworthiness or insurability criteria or collateral conditions that may prevent extension, the right to prohibit unsolicited-transaction use, and how to exercise that right through the system, including its address and toll-free number. Selection, extension and collateral criteria must be retained for three years from the offer. These are separate user duties, not additional express elements of section 604(c)(1)(B); the written-solicitation trigger neither guarantees notice before first disclosure nor covers every oral communication. Federal and state unfair- or deceptive-practice authority remains. Section 615(c)'s distinct liability defense requires proof by a preponderance that reasonable compliance procedures were maintained at the time of the alleged violation; it does not erase those duties or authorize an otherwise impermissible section 604 purpose.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Central Hudson supplies commercial-speech scrutiny, and Fox requires reasonable proportional fit without a least-restrictive-means rule. U.S. West (February 7, 2000) requires independent scrutiny despite lawful custody and alternative channels; its different FCC approval mechanism is not imported into the FCRA.

##### Preventing unrelated credit-file repurposing materially protects confidentiality

**Holding:** Protecting confidential financial information against unrelated commercial repurposing is substantial, and prohibiting the identified nonconsensual sale of credit-derived marketing classifications directly and materially advances that interest. The authorization, financial-purpose and firm-offer permissions, with the actual conditions stated above, do not defeat that conclusion even though permitted prescreening can disclose the same financial trait without affirmative authorization.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the unanimous opinion of the Court (Stevens).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule does not assume universal compliance or consumer awareness. Financial prescreening may disclose the same trait before individual notice and without affirmative authorization; its actual purpose, offer, information and use constraints remain essential to the distinction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Discovery Network and Greater New Orleans require comparison of the actual permissions with the prohibited harm. The constrained financial transaction distinguishes this scheme from an inconsistent suppression of comparable information; the Court does not require elimination of every unchosen disclosure.

##### The actual statutory arrangement reasonably fits this application

**Holding:** The FCRA restriction on these unrelated credit-derived marketing lists reasonably fits its confidentiality interest when assessed with the separate consumer-direction routes and the defined financial-purpose, conditional-offer, information, election and user-duty limits; the D.C. Circuit's denial of relief on this First Amendment challenge is affirmed. Fox does not require extending a comparable opt-out regime to unrelated marketing merely because it would burden the seller less, and this holding neither declares opt-out generally inadequate nor makes written authorization constitutionally necessary for every information use.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the unanimous opinion of the Court (Stevens).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The judgment establishes no general constitutional inadequacy of opt-out and no universal written-consent requirement. The FTC order remains confined to the identified lists and does not extend to consented uses, independently collected public information or other statutory permissions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The denial of relief from the FTC order is affirmed at the reviewed First Amendment application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fox permits a proportionate distinction without requiring the seller’s less burdensome opt-out alternative. Lorillard preserves actual-burden review and the separate advancement and fit inquiries; other marketing channels mitigate rather than erase the burden. Thompson’s inadequately justified drug-promotion condition is distinguished from regulation of the confidential disclosure itself. The broader separate theory in 44 Liquormart is not adopted, and Reno v. Condon supplies no independent answer to the speech claim.

**Controlling construction:** The category rule permits use to provide or market subscribed local, interexchange and commercial-mobile services. Affiliates supplying already subscribed categories may share when the customer subscribes to more than one such category; that permission is absent otherwise. New-category marketing requires approval, with separate permissions for inside-wiring installation, maintenance and repair, mobile-service health-effects research, and former adjunct-to-basic services. CPNI includes confidential individual calling patterns and billing information; subscriber-list information is excluded and aggregate information has separate treatment. Use required by law or approved by the customer is distinct from use for the originating service or services necessary to its provision, including directories. Original section 222(d) separately permits service initiation, rendering, billing and collection; protection of carrier rights or property and users or other carriers against fraudulent, abusive or unlawful use or subscription; and customer-approved inbound telemarketing, referral or administration during the customer's own call. The last permission does not authorize indefinite later marketing.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Prior one-time notice must identify the information, specific recipients, purposes, rights, carrier duty, precise approval/denial steps, withdrawal rights and the continuing effect until affirmative revocation or limitation; it must state that refusal does not affect subscribed services. Notice must be comprehensible and nonmisleading. Written notice must be legible, sufficiently large and readily apparent; translating any part requires translating all. Notice may describe tailored-offering benefits and possible compelled disclosure on an affirmative written request, but may not encourage freezing third-party access. Solicitation must be proximate to notice. The carrier bears proof of compliant oral approval, and notice and approval records must be kept at least one year.

**Effective transition:** FCC 99-223 retains affirmative approval, separately removes the winback restriction in paragraph 187 and the separate-document notice restriction in paragraph 188, and makes amended information-collection rules dependent on OMB approval and later effective-date publication under paragraphs 185 and 190. No such later publication is established. Paragraph 189 postpones enforcement of amended section 64.2009(a) and (c) until eight months after release. Publication is not immediate effectiveness of all equipment, information-service or safeguard amendments, and reversal does not revive removed provisions.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is no universal rule for data ownership, all targeted marketing, all internal handling or all CPNI restrictions. Separate equipment/information-service and competitor-tracking provisions are not independently upheld or given invented approval exceptions. Takings and other arbitrary-and-capricious claims remain open; no compliance or sanction is decided.

**Related current rules:** Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions supplies the independent constitutional standard. Wireless-location and emergency subscriber information states the later express statutory requirements rather than treating general approval as permission to disregard them.

## Date-eligible published noncontrolling positions

### 2002-12-10 — Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Planned-Parenthood-v-ACLA_merits_2002-12-10.md`.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the contextual-threat rule and its application, the named-plaintiff specific-intent injunction, the attribution and notice limits, and the removal of the possession restriction. He supports damages vacatur and remand, but would require subjective recklessness rather than purpose or knowledge: awareness of a substantial risk that the communication will be understood as a serious threat of unlawful violence, followed by conscious disregard. Objective foreseeability is insufficient because it exposes speakers to an audience reaction they did not consciously risk; an intent to carry out violence is unnecessary. In his view this minimum preserves political advocacy while reaching deliberately risked coded intimidation. He would require review of the actual jury instructions and findings under that floor and a new trial on affected liability and damages if the error was not harmless. The separate specific-intent findings independently exceed his floor for the confined prospective commands and cannot repair the jury verdict. The punitive award remains subject to the existing reconsideration.

He would confine the injunction's protected-person coverage to the named plaintiffs, with actual notice, attributable conduct and the specified threatening intent, and would remove coverage extending beyond them. He does not join the Court's related-person extension. His vote to vacate paragraph 4's derivative asset restraint supplies no additional rationale. Independent RICO questions remain outside review. His different constitutional threshold and protected-person limit do not become the Court's rules.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that an implicit public communication can be a threat and join the attribution, notice, remedial-connection and private-possession limits. They would reverse the reviewed threat liability outright and dissolve its speech injunction because independent constitutional review does not establish the speakers' own threatened violence. Claiborne protects forceful political condemnation and social pressure despite fear of unrelated violence. The lawful-accountability language, the absence of violence following many posters, the unestablished abortion connection of the Patterson killing and the lack of proof attributing prior murders to these defendants undermine the inference the Court draws. Their position does not require literal threats, private delivery, ability to attack or a confession; it requires a sufficient connection between the message and threatened violence attributable to its speaker. The judge's subjective findings cannot create missing threatening content. They also support dissolution of the dependent asset restraint and reject using security to preserve the liability they would reverse.

Kennedy separately joins the Court's purpose-or-knowledge constitutional rule while rejecting its application. Scalia and Thomas accept FACE's enacted intentional-act requirements but do not join the new constitutional formula, because their antecedent no-threat ground resolves the case. Their shared judgment therefore does not imply identical culpability positions.

### 2003-03-25 — American Amusement Machine Association v. Kendrick

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/American-Amusement-Machine-Association-v-Kendrick_merits_2003-03-25.md`.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and in the judgment.** She joins the protected-expression and preliminary-relief holdings and agrees that the violence provisions must be preliminarily enjoined. She does not join a rule importing the complete adult content-scrutiny framework into every restriction directed only to minors. Her Reno position permits genuine minors-directed protection when its protected reach is sufficiently confined, and her Free Speech Coalition position allows supported enforcement predictions. Even granting that greater regulatory latitude here, the studies do not adequately identify the covered narratives as the harmful category asserted, and the required physical parental attendance is not justified by the showing. Ginsberg supplies no missing violence-category premise. A substantially better-supported and genuinely confined law would require its own assessment under her approach.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's opinion and would presume correction of any ultimately proved common unconstitutional violent-game restriction for the persons it governs unless the City establishes a narrower complete cure. That proposed remedial presumption remains bounded by the injunction's proper jurisdiction and the ordinary preliminary and final proof requirements. The fitted decree should identify the restrained provisions and their severability, require the responsible enforcers to receive notice and conform their enforcement instructions, and be enforced against continued covered sanctions. His presumption adds no final liability finding to the present preliminary relief.

### 2003-04-07 — Virginia v. Black

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Virginia-v-Black_merits_2003-04-07.md`.

**Part III of the lead opinion: Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer; Stone-Zsela joins its facial-proof analysis.** These Justices conclude that the proof provision in its model-instruction operation reaches substantial protected expression. Cross burning has political and ritual uses, and treating the act alone as sufficient permits conviction across a substantial identifiable range of those uses. The problem reaches beyond a single erroneous instruction because the model instruction remains undisavowed. General proof directions do not remove its specific permission. The state court has yet to supply a final construction, and construction or severance remains for the distinct Elliott and O'Mara remands. Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer affirm Black's indictment dismissal on that present facial ground. Stone-Zsela concurs in that part of the judgment without joining its indictment rationale.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part; Thomas joins the facial-construction portion only.** Scalia accepts the confined offense, rejects present facial invalidation and would obtain an authoritative state construction. Prima facie evidence may be sufficient when unrebutted while leaving the jury obliged to weigh contrary evidence; possible misuse alone does not establish substantial overbreadth. Thomas joins that construction objection. Scalia separately concludes that Black's by-itself instruction leaves intolerable uncertainty whether the jury considered rebuttal notwithstanding Instructions 6 and 8. He would retain conviction vacatur, vacate the indictment dismissal and permit a retrial under a valid construction, subject to ordinary restrictions. Thomas does not join this instructional conclusion or retrial disposition.

**Souter, concurring in the judgment as to Black and dissenting as to Elliott and O'Mara, joined by Kennedy and Ginsburg.** They would resolve the open content-selection question against this prohibition. The combination of a symbol-specific offense and evidentiary privilege presents a realistic danger that suppressing the associated ideology partly explains the selection. A cross can convey ideology, intimidation or both. The proof provision tilts doubtful cases toward punishment, and a general intimidation law can protect safety without this selection. They would affirm every reviewed conviction vacatur and indictment dismissal. Later removal of the proof clause cannot validate prosecutions under a law constitutionally defective when the conduct occurred.

**Thomas, dissenting.** Thomas principally treats intentional cross burning as terrorizing conduct by a particular means. Alternatively, its history gives the permissive inference a rational basis under Allen, while the whole charge preserves the prosecution's burden and the jury's ability to reject the inference. He would reverse the reviewed constitutional grounds for vacatur and dismissal for each respondent and remand for remaining preserved matters, including O'Mara's plea consequences. His position requires statutory intimidating intent and does not make every political or ritual cross burning criminal.
