#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

#### Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville, 508 U.S. 656 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1721.
**Decided:** June 14, 1993.

##### Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury

**Holding:** An association may challenge a governmental barrier preventing members who are able and ready to bid from competing on equal terms, without proving that a member would have won a contract, when a member has individual standing, the interest is germane to the association’s purpose, and neither the claim nor the requested relief requires individual-member participation. The unchallenged allegations accepted here establish the required connection; genuinely disputed elements remain subject to stage-appropriate proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Turner v. Fouche, 396 U.S. 346 — equal eligibility applied.
- Bakke, 438 U.S. 265 — opportunity injury distinguished from guaranteed selection.
- Warth, 422 U.S. 490 — concrete causal connection retained.
- Hunt, 432 U.S. 333 — association requirements retained.
- Lujan, June 12, 1992 — concrete injury and stage-specific proof preserved.

##### The replacement continues the controversy

**Holding:** Repeal does not moot this dispute because the successor’s sheltered-market method continues to exclude the association’s members from particular competitions on the challenged basis. Changes in groups, percentages and methods do not remove that injury; a replacement actually ending it could produce a different result.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, The replacement continues the controversy portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Stage-appropriate proof remains necessary for genuinely disputed elements. No equal-protection merits rule, guaranteed contract or categorical rule about every replacement enactment is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for merits and lawful relief concerning the operative ordinance; no automatic reinstatement of the repealed-ordinance injunction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Mesquite, 455 U.S. 283 — voluntary cessation applied.
- Diffenderfer, 404 U.S. 412 — distinguished because the challenged injury continues.

#### Bennett v. Spear

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-813.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether irrigators plead personal injury, traceability and redressability

**Holding:** Irrigators who allege loss of their project water from a Biological Opinion that the operating agency has announced it will follow sufficiently plead concrete injury, causation and likely redress through removal of that constraint. Standing must later be supported by the evidence appropriate to each litigation stage; a citizen-suit authorization does not replace Article III injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Material application:** The Bureau’s announced compliance connects the completed Service action to petitioners’ own alleged loss despite retained operational discretion. The possibility of other eventual choices does not require certainty of a changed allocation or defeat pleading-stage redress.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No biological error or final factual standing determination is made; the statutory review routes and relief remain separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed ESA/APA dismissal is reversed and remanded for claim-specific proceedings; the unchallenged NEPA dismissal is undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lujan’s personal-interest and stage-of-proof rules control, not its noncontrolling absent-agency application. Humane Society’s missing link to an already completed transfer is distinguished. Tracy permits cognizable economic injury without dispensing with other requirements, and Arizonans requires a continuing controversy without establishing a loss of this alleged injury.

For Bennett v. Spear, No. 95-813, decided March 19, 1997, see Environmental Law, “Endangered Species Act citizen-suit channels,” for the statutory channels and retained conditions, and Administrative Law, “Review of completed biological consultation,” for APA interests and finality.

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co., No. 98-470, May 17, 1999: resolves the bounded nonmerits sequencing question by permitting straightforward personal-jurisdiction dismissal before a difficult subject-matter inquiry, subject to the stated state-law and statutory limits; the contested-merits prohibition remains unchanged.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.
- **Church of Scientology v. United States:** Meaningful partial relief remains enough; no effectual relief of that kind is available on these allegations.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.
- **Bennett v. Spear:** Supported pleading-stage causal and remedial links remain sufficient; its ongoing constraint differs from the completed reporting deprivation here.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Applied for the live-controversy requirement and limited corrective authority to vacate without reaching merits.
- **Norton v. Mathews; Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech:** Their already-controlled merits and distinct jurisdictional settings remain intact; neither decides this unresolved EPCRA dispute.
- **Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation:** Its different statutory wording and enforcement scheme do not settle the merits question the Court leaves open.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western:** Its RCRA cause-and-remedy limits remain statute-specific; it supplies no automatic EPCRA current-violation rule.

#### Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1474.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

##### A disputed federal tribal title question does not create a sovereign-lands exception to prospective officer relief

**Holding:** The Eleventh Amendment does not bar a claim for prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against officials actually connected to an alleged ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights merely because adjudicating that violation requires resolving a disputed federal title question or affects important sovereign interests. The decree must address proved unlawful future official conduct and may not bind the immune State's title, award damages or retrospective compensation, execute against state property or require the State itself to convey title.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Material application:** The pleaded land-regulating officials have a concrete connection to the alleged interference; a general law-obedience duty would not suffice. The actual request for exclusive use and exclusion of regulation is acknowledged, but the Ninth Circuit withheld a decree binding Idaho’s title and the case remains at the pleading stage.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither generalized state-interest balancing nor a special sovereign-lands exception bars the route. The Tribe must establish the federal right, ongoing violation and lawful remedy. No ownership, navigability, prestatehood disposition or separate United States action is adjudicated; no general Article I abrogation rule is announced.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm permission for the prospective-officer claims, 5–4; leave the State, agency and direct quiet-title or retrospective-relief dismissals undisturbed, 9–0. Further merits and tailored-remedy proceedings are permitted, without automatic injunction or property transfer.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young supplies the connected-officer route; Tindal and Treasure Salvors distinguish officer conduct relief from sovereign title. Oneida supplies federal-question character. Edelman, Green and Pennhurst retain retrospective and state-law limits. Seminole’s actual IGRA officer holding is consistent but supplies neither a universal statutory route nor an abrogation rule, and no IGRA sequence is imported.

#### Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission of Maryland / United States v. Public Service Commission of Maryland, 535 U.S. 635

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 00-1531 and 00-1711.
**Decided:** May 20, 2002.

For Verizon Maryland and the consolidated United States case, May 20, 2002, Nos. 00-1531 and 00-1711, on §1331 and the later-administrative-order distinction, see Federal Courts.

##### Whether prospective officer relief defeats categorical immunity and indispensability dismissal

**Holding:** A claim to restrain responsible commissioners from continuing enforcement of an order alleged to violate federal law may proceed through the prospective officer-relief route. Private intercarrier financial consequences do not by themselves convert that restraint into retrospective compensation from the State, and the commission's absence does not categorically make such relief impossible.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part III, joined by all nine Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The connected-officer route concerns alleged ongoing federal violation, not a merits finding. No restrictive statutory substitute sequence is evaded. A declaration affecting accrued private obligations and a direction concerning private refunds require separate examination; no refund is awarded. Direct Commission amenability, regulatory-participation consent, direct-State authorization and the private statutory cause remain undecided. Participation alone supplies none of those predicates.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate both judgments and remand, with eight Associate votes on that form; Stone-Zsela would reverse while joining the rules. The lower courts must adjudicate federal compliance and the actual party, preclusion and remedy questions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young supplies the prospective connected-officer route. Seminole Tribe preserves its enacted remedial limits; Coeur d'Alene rejects generalized sovereign-interest balancing as an additional bar. Schacht separates jurisdiction and immunity. Florida Prepaid permits necessary unchanged-judgment alternatives and preserves its distinct Article I rule. College Savings Bank and Lapides do not turn this participation into actual consent. Edelman bars retrospective State compensation and Pennhurst bars state-law commands; private financial effects alone trigger neither prohibition.

#### Gonzaga University v. Doe, 536 U.S. 273

**Docket or dockets:** No. 01-679.
**Decided:** 2002-06-20.

##### Whether FERPA nondisclosure provisions establish the asserted Section 1983 entitlement

**Authority of the judgment:** Reverse and remand the FERPA-based federal judgment, 6–3: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer support; Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg oppose. No single full rationale or new general rights formula commands a majority, and no controlling broader rationale is established under Marks. The statute-specific proposition below has six direct votes.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** FERPA's education-record nondisclosure provisions do not establish the individual judicially enforceable entitlement asserted in this Section 1983 action. The FERPA-based federal judgment must therefore be reversed; the state-action determination, excluded from review, and independent state-law judgments are unaffected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s lead opinion, joined by O’Connor, Scalia and Kennedy, and Breyer’s concurrence in the judgment, joined by Souter, agree on the absence of this asserted FERPA entitlement. Their separate institution-focused and FERPA-specific administrative explanations are not combined into a new general formula.

**Current force:** The asserted nondisclosure claim cannot proceed through Section 1983. Right creation, Section 1983 enforcement, implied statutory actions and incompatible remedial displacement retain their separate inquiries.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The conclusion concerns these nondisclosure provisions. It does not categorically exclude funding statutes or equate the absence of an implied action with the absence of a Section 1983 right. The four-Justice lead opinion supplies no controlling general requirement of congressional intent to create a private remedy. No additional damages ruling follows this threshold disposition; the excluded state-action issue and independent state-law judgments remain intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Blessing and Bruneau remain distinct provision-specific right and enforcement inquiries; Owasso's reservation of enforcement is resolved for these nondisclosure provisions. No general rule supported only by the four-Justice lead is elevated into controlling law.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the FERPA-based federal judgment and remand consistently with rejection of that claim, without reopening state action or the independent state-law judgments.

#### Suter v. Artist M., 503 U.S. 347 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1488.
**Decided:** March 25, 1992.

For this case’s other questions, see *Suter v. Artist M.*, March 25, 1992, No. 90-1488: whether the separate b.h. consent decree moots or precludes this statutory-enforcement controversy (Federal Courts — Mootness and decree scope).

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates the asserted individual reasonable-efforts right enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(15), read in the Adoption Assistance and Child Welfare Act's plan and administrative-enforcement structure, does not confer on these beneficiaries an individual right enforceable through §1983 to compel the asserted reasonable efforts to prevent removal or reunify families. An approved plan's reasonable-efforts commitment, §671(a)(3)'s statewide-operation requirement, and the record's caseworker deficiencies do not supply that missing federal right.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this rule and reverse the §1983 ruling under §671(a)(15).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every state-plan provision unenforceable or find that administrative funding withdrawal comprehensively forecloses an otherwise existing §1983 right. The absence of an individual right, the existence of an action, and the scope of relief remain separate inquiries.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Maine v. Thiboutot, 448 U.S. 1 (1980) — preserves statutory-right enforcement under §1983; a violation of federal law still must invade an enforceable right.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority, 479 U.S. 418 (1987) — distinguishes its defined rent and utility entitlement; its enforceability holding and separate foreclosure inquiry remain operative.
- Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association, 496 U.S. 498 (1990) — distinguishes its substantive reimbursement obligation and supplied benchmarks; neither its Medicaid holding nor its rejection of automatic foreclosure by funding withdrawal is overruled.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981) — applies its clear-notice principle to the asserted funding obligation; no categorical immunity for state-plan provisions is created.

##### Whether §671(a)(15) creates a separate implied private action

**Holding:** The Act does not create an implied private cause of action to enforce §671(a)(15)'s reasonable-efforts clause because the claimants have not established congressional intent to create that remedy. This conclusion concerns the distinct action asserted directly under the Act and does not make failure to imply an action a bar to an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this independently necessary ground for rejecting the second enforcement route sustained below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to imply a private action does not foreclose an otherwise available enacted §1983 remedy; other statutory provisions require their own examination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort v. Ash, 422 U.S. 66 (1975) — applies its inquiry into an implied private remedy, with congressional intent decisive.
- Transamerica Mortgage Advisors, Inc. v. Lewis, 444 U.S. 11 (1979) — applies its requirement of congressional intent to create the particular remedy asserted; statutory purpose alone is insufficient.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992, decision — preserves the distinction between the existence of a cognizable action and appropriate relief within it; no Title IX holding is narrowed.

##### Whether the asserted §671(a)(9) reporting claim is enforceable through §1983

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9)'s requirement that a state plan provide for reporting to the appropriate court or law-enforcement agency when a state agency has reason to believe a child's home or institution is unsuitable because of neglect, abuse, or exploitation, and the child's care is paid for in whole or in part under Title IV-E or IV-B, does not confer on these respondents a private federal right enforceable through §1983 on the claim presented. The reporting ground therefore cannot independently sustain the challenged caseworker injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-A, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this separate statutory holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reporting obligation and administrative enforcement remain. Section 671(a)(16)’s case-plan and review claim is not decided through this reporting holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wright, 479 U.S. 418 — applies the need to identify a privately enforceable statutory right; its distinct rent holding remains in force.
- Wilder, 496 U.S. 498 — applies provision-specific examination of a binding individual entitlement; no general rule against enforcement of plan provisions replaces it.

##### Whether §671(a)(9) creates the asserted separate implied action

**Holding:** Section 671(a)(9) does not create an implied private action for these respondents to obtain the challenged caseworker relief: its reporting requirement and the Act's enforcement structure do not establish congressional intent to authorize that action. Rejection of this distinct statutory vehicle does not decide private enforcement of §671(a)(16).

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Opinion of the Court, Part IV-B, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the ground rejecting direct implied enforcement of the reporting claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 671(a)(16), read with §675(1) and §675(5)(B), remains open as to enforceable right, implied action, violation and additional lawful relief. No constitutional claim is revived or nationwide three-day deadline imposed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s judgment is reversed as to §671(a)(15) and (9) enforcement through both asserted routes; the three-working-day assignment/reassignment injunction and ancillary weekly monitoring order are vacated, 6–3. Any blanket resolution of the independent §671(a)(16) claim is vacated and that claim remanded for provision-specific consideration, 9–0, without keeping the existing injunction alive on an unadjudicated ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cort, 422 U.S. 66, and Transamerica, 444 U.S. 11 — apply the congressional-intent requirement to the distinct implied reporting action.
- Franklin, February 26, 1992, decision — applies its action/remedy distinction without changing the established Title IX action or relief available within it.

#### Blessing v. Freestone

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1441.
**Decided:** April 21, 1997.

##### What a claimant must identify before invoking §1983 for a statutory violation

**Holding:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit the claimant, creating a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement for judicial enforcement. A beneficial statute, mandatory administrative duty, or useful remedy does not alone establish that right; qualitative terms are not universally unenforceable merely because they require judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous. Scalia and Kennedy's additional original-meaning reservation withholds no join and supplies no controlling limit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Wright* and *Wilder* supply defined individual entitlements, not indivisible program promises. *Livadas* separates right, enforcement channel, foreclosure, defendant, and remedy. *Suter* retains provision-specific adjudication; *Pennhurst* requires clear notice without categorical state-plan immunity.

##### Whether Title IV-D creates an individual entitlement to substantial compliance or general administrative adequacy

**Holding:** Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure creates no personal right to systemwide compliance. The same theory does not become an individual right when framed as sufficient staffing or satisfactory computer administration as such; genuinely distinct claims under particular provisions require separate assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 654(3)'s organizational and staffing duties establish no beneficiary entitlement to a specified staff complement, caseload, or worker. Former and amended §654(16) and related §654a management requirements do not make the administrative system itself an individual entitlement. Aggregate compliance could coexist with a violation of an independently existing family-specific right.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shortages, backlogs, and accounting defects may prove an established violation or inform fitted relief, but cannot create a right. No distinct standardized-computer, confidentiality, or individual transmission claim is established, and no particular clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 is decided. Regulatory elaboration of an existing right is not categorically excluded.

##### Whether general federal administration forecloses all otherwise existing §1983 claims

**Holding:** An individual federal right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that route or provides a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme. Title IV-D audits, corrective plans, and funding sanctions alone do not establish chapterwide foreclosure; the actual provision and any specific incompatible remedy require separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The federal oversight tools regulate funding and offer no general family-specific administrative forum. Their detail and significance do not alone show incompatibility with §1983.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Livadas*, *Wright*, and *Wilder* keep foreclosure separate from right creation. *Sea Clammers* and *Smith v. Robinson* retain their incompatible-remedy holdings, distinguished from this general oversight arrangement.

##### What effect state-plan placement has under §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10

**Holding:** A Social Security Act provision cannot be denied enforceability merely because it specifies a state plan or its contents. **Holding:** A criminal definition substantially reaching nonobscene depictions produced without exploiting an actual child cannot be sustained merely because the depicted person appears to be a minor. **Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Those six directly support both rules and the statutory qualifications below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual-child exploitation, obscenity and independently unlawful solicitation retain their own elements. The Government's grooming, demand and proof concerns do not justify this substantial protected reach; restricting the provision to realistic images does not cure the defect or supply a serious-value protection.

##### Presentation does not transform a protected work into contraband

**Holding:** Section 2256(8)(D) is independently substantially overbroad because promotional presentation can make a nonobscene, nonexploitative work prohibited material in connected distribution and possession offenses, including for persons unconnected with the presentation. A court may not save this definition by rewriting it as a separately defined intentional unlawful-offer offense.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six directly join Part II and this independent invalidity ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A minor is under eighteen. Sexually explicit conduct covers actual or simulated genital-genital, oral-genital, anal-genital or oral-anal intercourse, whether between persons of the same or opposite sex, bestiality, masturbation, sadistic or masochistic abuse and lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area; mere nudity does not itself establish the last category. Section 2252A(c) applies only to (a)(1)–(4), requires actual adult performers at production, and requires that the defendant did not advertise, promote, present, describe or distribute the work as depicting minors. It excludes wholly generated images and ordinary possession under (a)(5). Section 2252A(d), separately, concerns possession of fewer than three images: prompt, good-faith reasonable destruction steps or reporting the images to law enforcement and affording access to each image, without retaining any image or copy or permitting anyone other than law enforcement access to one. It requires reasonable destruction steps, not successful destruction, and provides no right to retain protected expression. The unchallenged (C) definition concerns a recognizable actual person who was a minor at the relevant creation, adaptation or modification or whose image as a minor was used; proof of the person's actual identity is unnecessary. These provisions do not cure the challenged protected reach.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The invalidation of §§2256(8)(B) and (D) is affirmed for substantial overbreadth. No separate vagueness judgment is entered. Actual-child and obscenity prohibitions retain independent operation; the identifiable-actual-minor definition in (C) and a differently enacted unlawful-offer offense are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **New York v. Ferber, 458 U.S. 747, and Osborne v. Ohio, 495 U.S. 103:** Their actual-child injury predicates are preserved; they are not extended to an invented image or an adult performance solely because it resembles a child.
- **Miller v. California, 413 U.S. 15:** Obscenity remains an independent category with its own elements, not a substitute apparent-age rule.
- **Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union:** Its protected-adult-expression and substantial-overbreadth reasoning applies; its distinction between genuine severance and judicial rewriting defeats the proposed new offense limits.
- **United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc.:** Its content-based scrutiny and governmental justification burden govern the fit of the challenged criminal restrictions.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.:** Knowledge of depiction characteristics remains distinct from constitutional authority to suppress those characteristics; this case changes no surviving factual-knowledge requirement.
- **Broadrick v. Oklahoma, 413 U.S. 601:** Substantial protected reach, rather than a conceivable unusual application, supports facial relief here.
- **Ginzburg v. United States, 383 U.S. 463:** Pandering may inform an obscenity inquiry; it does not independently make a protected nonobscene work contraband for all possessors.

## Event

Westside Mothers v. Haveman; judgment below, 289 F.3d 852. Merits decision, December 10, 2002, on full merits submission reviewing the Sixth Circuit's May 15, 2002 judgment reversing categorical dismissal and remanding. The questions are whether the specified Medicaid child screening, diagnostic and treatment requirements confer rights enforceable under §1983, and whether prospective relief against responsible state officials remains available despite the Spending Clause basis and voluntary participation. The plaintiffs allege unmet services, information and arrangements in Michigan's managed-care system; these are allegations rather than adjudicated individual deprivations.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The reviewed child-service and prospective-officer rulings are affirmed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0, within the reviewed child-service questions.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The live child-service claims may proceed under the rules below. The existing transportation preclusion and organizational-standing dispositions retain their stated effect; the separate provider claims remain outside this review.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine join the complete opinion. Scalia files a concurrence, joined by Kennedy, preserving an original-meaning reservation without withholding any join.

## Holdings

### 1. Eligible children may enforce the specified Medicaid benefits

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 1396a(a)(10) and (43)(A)–(C), read with §§1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), give Medicaid-eligible children under twenty-one mandatory individual rights to the defined medical assistance, information, requested screening and arrangements for corrective treatment, enforceable through §1983. The right requires the applicable eligibility, service-category, request, screening-discovered need and coverage conditions stated below; it does not establish a personal right to optimal program administration, staffing, provider rates, overall capacity or a preferred provider.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt this holding and its statutory limits.

**Statutory content and conditions:**

- Section (43)(A) requires informing persons in the State under twenty-one who have been determined eligible for medical assistance including early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services (EPSDT), about those services and the need for age-appropriate immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases.
- Section (43)(B) requires providing or arranging screening whenever requested. Section (43)(C) requires arranging, directly or by referral to appropriate agencies, organizations or individuals, corrective treatment whose need screening discloses. The request condition belongs to screening; the disclosed-need condition belongs to corrective arrangements. Screening of unwilling children is not compelled.
- Section 1396d(r)(1) requires screening at medically and dentally reasonable periodic intervals determined by the State after consultation with recognized child-health organizations, and at other medically necessary intervals. Its minimum comprises a comprehensive health and developmental history addressing physical and mental development, an unclothed physical examination, age-and-history-appropriate immunizations, laboratory tests including age-and-risk-appropriate lead assessment, and health education including anticipatory guidance. Pediatric immunization follows the separately referenced statutory vaccine schedule.
- Sections (r)(2)–(4) separately cover vision, dental and hearing services at professionally reasonable periodic and other medically necessary intervals. Their minima include diagnosis and treatment of vision defects, including eyeglasses; pain and infection relief, tooth restoration and maintenance of dental health; and diagnosis and treatment of hearing defects, including hearing aids.
- Section (r)(5) covers necessary health care, diagnosis, treatment and other measures of the kinds described in §1396d(a) to correct or ameliorate physical or mental defects, illnesses or conditions discovered through screening, whether or not the State plan otherwise covers the service. Actual statutory service-category, need and coverage conditions remain.
- A provider qualified to supply one or more EPSDT items may supply them without qualification for every item. Section (43)(D)'s reporting and participation measures are not converted into additional individual rights by this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Blessing's provision-specific inquiry. Congress identifies the eligible child and the mandatory benefit, and the service definitions supply standards courts can apply through medical evidence. Wright and Wilder show that clinical or administrative judgment does not make a defined entitlement judicially unmanageable. The officials' strongest objection treats the plan as an undertaking only to the federal administrator. But the particular child-directed benefits, together with §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10's rejection of state-plan placement as a categorical bar, defeat that objection. Suter retains its different reasonable-efforts and reporting holdings; it establishes no contrary rule for every plan provision. Gonzaga's FERPA-specific outcome does not erase Blessing or turn its four-Justice general formulation into controlling law.

Enforcement remains a separate inquiry. Blessing and Bruneau require actual congressional foreclosure, and §1396c's funding oversight supplies no sufficiently comprehensive incompatible individual remedy. Managed-care contracts do not eliminate the State's financing, informing and arrangements obligations. They establish neither an actual deprivation nor a guarantee that state employees personally render treatment. Aggregate deficiencies may prove an identified child's violation or support fitted relief; they cannot create a different personal entitlement.

### 2. Accepted federal duties may be enforced prospectively against responsible officials

**Holding and operative rule:** A valid Spending Clause enactment remains federal law when its accepted conditions govern a participating State. Officials actually connected to a continuing violation of these individual federal rights may be ordered to comply prospectively; relief must address the proved future violation and may not award retrospective State compensation or compel obedience to state law.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Ex parte Young's connected-official route. Pennhurst's funding analogy explains clear notice and acceptance, while Bennett rejects reducing an enacted condition to an ordinary bilateral promise. Michigan's voluntary choice to participate does not make compliance voluntary while it participates. The State's real interest and implementation discretion therefore do not sustain categorical dismissal.

Verizon requires examination of the actual defendant, continuing federal violation and remedy. Coeur d'Alene rejects an additional generalized sovereign-interest barrier. Medicaid's general funding supervision supplies no exclusive remedial sequence displacing this action. Allegations of a continuing deprivation suffice against the categorical defense at this stage; coercive relief requires proof. Edelman permits the prospective costs of lawful compliance but bars retrospective treasury compensation, and Pennhurst separately bars state-law commands. The source-of-law conclusion, individual right, §1983 channel and immunity boundary remain distinct. Neither voluntary participation nor this officer action establishes consent to direct State damages, and the Court announces no separate private cause under the Supremacy Clause.

### 3. The live child-service claims return for adjudication under those rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit is affirmed insofar as it rejected the categorical defenses and allowed the reviewed child-entitlement action to proceed. On remand each live claimant must establish standing and eligibility, the applicable statutory version and obligation, concrete deprivation, responsible official, continuing breach and causation before a court awards relief fitted to that violation.

**Authority:** All nine join this application and remand in the same Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Dismissal prevented findings on actual service failures and the proper scope of relief. Blessing requires matching the asserted right to the claimant, conduct, provision and remedy. An established statewide pattern may support relief connected to proved violations, but a statistical shortfall alone warrants neither a new personal right nor automatic redesign of the system. These are factual and remedial tasks under the rule just stated.

The December 1999 transportation-assistance preclusion ruling was not appealed and remains effective. The Michigan League for Human Services' unappealed dismissal, Michigan Welfare Rights Organization's affirmed dismissal and the professional associations' restored standing retain the posture fixed below. The distinct provider and professional-association claims lie outside the granted child-service questions; this decision adds no individual provider-rate entitlement under §1396a(a)(30)(A).

## Precedent Treatment

## Event

Montiel v. Davis. Direct-appeal merits decision, January 13, 2003. On appeal under 28 U.S.C. §1253 from the three-judge District Court for the Southern District of Alabama, No. 01-0447-BH-S, which granted summary judgment for defendants on July 8, 2002, 215 F. ## Participation

## Public Action

## Judgment & Remedy

The denial of relief on the population, constitutional racial, and statutory legislative claims stands within the plaintiffs' own electoral exposure. The requested invalidation and replacement of the legislative plans are denied. The severed congressional and education-board proceedings are unaffected.

## Opinion Topology

Stone-Zsela delivered the opinion of the Court. The complete-opinion coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer join the complete opinion. O'Connor and Kennedy additionally join Parts I and III. Scalia and Thomas additionally join Part I. Thomas filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Scalia.

## Holdings

### Population equality and prohibited purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** A state legislative maximum population deviation below ten percent does not alone establish a prima facie violation, but remains challengeable through proof of arbitrary or discriminatory weighting. Where the governing claim turns on discriminatory purpose, that purpose need only be a substantial or motivating factor; once that predicate is proved, the government must prove by a preponderance that materially the same decision would have occurred without it.

**Authority:** Part I, adopted by all nine Justices, directly controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Voinovich applies substantial equality without making smaller deviations immune; Reynolds and Brown reject a demand for mathematical exactness. Maximum deviation adds the greatest departure above and below the ideal district population. The Senate's 9.78% and House's 9.93% figures describe that plan-wide range, not a separate allowance for each voter. Below the threshold, plaintiffs must connect their evidence to unlawful weighting. County splits, demographics, and more equal alternatives can be relevant, but do not prove that connection merely by existing. The reported submissions add no material evidence establishing the claimed arbitrary or discriminatory operation. The lower court's suggestion that an improper purpose must be exclusive is incorrect under Arlington Heights, Brooks, and Page. That error does not require remand because the independent evidentiary deficiency remains under the correct rule, and no excluded material proof is identified. Political explanations receive no categorical immunity; they are assessed against the evidence. The challenged judgment stands on the absence of the necessary showing.

### Territorial design and independent constitutional injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Racial consideration or predominance in drawing a shared territorial district does not alone establish an equal-protection deprivation. An independently asserted exclusion, intentional impairment of electoral opportunity, material restriction on participation, or inferior civic status must satisfy its own elements; voters of every race receive the same protection.

**Authority:** Part II, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, supplies an independently sufficient majority ground for the constitutional racial judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Shaw, Johnson v. Miller, Easley, and Robertson distinguish the composition of a common electorate from government action that impairs a protected civic interest. A design-only claim identifies motive, shape, racial composition, or district assignment without establishing that independent operation. Intentional dilution requires discriminatory purpose, actual material impairment of electoral opportunity, and causation. Unequal participation requires a real impediment to political activity, not merely placement in a less favorable district. Such an injury need not await a second governmental act or an electoral defeat. Here the population figures, boundaries, and suppositions establish neither the asserted unlawful weighting nor another concrete impairment. Race's correlation with party affiliation can be evidence; a partisan label neither proves a racial wrong nor excuses one proved. The asserted white-voter claim receives the same legal protection as any other racial claim, but does not acquire a different injury rule. This constitutional ground creates no additional prerequisite for an independent Voting Rights Act claim.

### Independent statutory proof

**Holding and operative rule:** A §2 district-dilution claim requires the relevant cohesion and opposing-bloc causal showing and unequal electoral opportunity under the statute; racial population figures and the availability of another map do not establish those requirements by themselves. The statutory claim here fails on its independent evidentiary deficiency, regardless of the disposition of the constitutional design theory.

**Authority:** Part III, adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, controls by seven direct votes. Scalia and Thomas support the statutory judgment on another ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Gingles identifies the causal voting conditions, Voinovich places the initial burden on challengers, and De Grandy requires an independent examination of actual opportunity without maximizing districts. Section 2 protects citizens of every race and uses a results inquiry distinct from constitutional purpose. The population and boundary evidence does not establish the requisite cohesion, opposing-bloc mechanism, and unequal opportunity. Current law permits one protected group to establish capacity in a lawful, geographically compact district either through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Those routes remain available, but no renewed capacity ruling answers this separate deficiency. Balderas preserves a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground rather than ordering remand for an immaterial error in another part of the analysis. Preclearance does not adjudicate these independent statutory or constitutional claims. The Court rejects the requested statutory relief on the record presented.

# Current-term public authority, available before 2003-03-25

Effective date: 2003-03-17


## Event

National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell; review of 272 F.3d 104, Second Circuit No. 99-9450. October Term 2002. Decided March 17, 2003, after full merits submission. The Court reviews vacatur of a preliminary injunction against Vermont's requirement that mercury-containing products, including lamps, and their packaging disclose mercury content and lawful disposal. The questions concern compelled commercial disclosure and interstate-commerce burdens. The accuracy and commercial character of the information are conceded; manufacturers assert production and distribution costs and a practical incentive to label nationally.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at merits submission and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms vacatur of the preliminary injunction and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** Affirmed, 9–0. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. The preliminary injunction remains vacated; Vermont may enforce the challenged local-sale requirement while litigation proceeds. A supported final challenge or renewed request based on materially different evidence remains subject to the applicable procedural and proof requirements. This interlocutory judgment does not finally resolve every application of the law.

## Opinion Topology

Kennedy delivers the opinion of the Court.

| Part | Controlling subject | Justices joining the reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| I | Bounded factual-disclosure rule and application | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer (8) |
| II | Territorial reach of the local-sale command | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer (9) |
| III | Pike review and failure to establish likely success | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer (7) |

Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment and joins Parts II and III. Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurs in part and in the judgment; they join Parts I and II and agree with the Part III judgment on an assumed-Pike basis.

## Holdings

### I. Truthful product and disposal facts may serve a substantial health purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may require truthful, noncontroversial information about a commercial product's mercury content and lawful disposal when the disclosure is reasonably related to a substantial health or environmental purpose and is neither unjustified nor unduly burdensome. Separate proof of consumer deception is unnecessary in this product-and-hazard setting; the conceded content, connection and burden shown here do not establish likely success on the speech challenge.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by the eight Associates identified above, controls the disclosure rule and application. Stone-Zsela agrees with the judgment on separate grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that useful facts about a purchased product may be required for its safe handling and disposal. Zauderer distinguishes justified factual disclosure from suppression of commercial speech while preserving objections to unjustified or unduly burdensome demands. Its reason for treating disclosure differently supports this defined extension beyond deception. Ibanez requires attention to the actual justification and burden rather than accepting an official characterization.

Mercury content is not apparent to purchasers, and the disposal information connects the covered product to lawful handling. Accuracy and commercial character are conceded. The State's substantial health and environmental purpose and the information's direct relation to it support the requirement; the present burdens do not show likely constitutional invalidity. Requiring deception as an indispensable additional condition would exclude useful product facts serving the same informational function.

United Foods concerns compelled financing of private promotion and expressly reserves factual disclosure and distinct health or safety purposes. It neither invalidates this label nor exempts all commercial mandates from scrutiny. The message's actual content, commercial connection, justification and burden govern. Disputed ideology does not become noncontroversial product information merely because government calls it factual. These conditions resolve the request for interim relief while preserving review of a materially different supported application.

### II. A local-sale requirement does not become an outside command through production choices

**Holding and operative rule:** A labeling rule governing goods sold or distributed for use in Vermont does not directly regulate independent transactions outside Vermont merely because manufacturers prefer nationwide compliance to segregating local goods. The rule's actual legal command, rather than that production preference alone, determines whether it directly controls outside commerce.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II is unanimous.

**Controlling explanation:** Healy and Brown-Forman address direct outside-price control. Vermont's rule commands labels for its own market and does not condition access to that market on labels placed on independent outside sales. Manufacturers may experience costs elsewhere and may choose a uniform label, but those economic effects do not themselves extend Vermont's legal command to outside transactions. This distinction answers the direct-control claim without making the costs irrelevant. The asserted burdens are addressed separately in Part III.

### III. Evenhanded regulation remains subject to Pike review

**Holding and operative rule:** Pike review does not require a preliminary showing that a burden falls disparately on interstate commerce: a challenger to an evenhanded local measure must substantiate the interstate burden and show it clearly excessive in relation to the measure's putative local benefits. The identified production and distribution costs and speculative incompatible labels do not establish likely success under that standard on this preliminary record.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, forming a seven-Justice majority. Scalia and Thomas agree only with the judgment on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** Pike addresses burdens of evenhanded regulation. A categorical disparate-burden threshold would remove the inquiry precisely where that rule calls for one. The Court therefore rejects that threshold in the appellate reasoning while affirming its disposition.

The asserted costs matter, but they must be assessed against the disposal information's concrete local benefit. A firm's preference for uniform national production does not itself establish clear excess, and no actual conflicting sister-state requirement is shown. The present evidence thus fails to establish likely success warranting interim restraint. A supported record of conflict or disruption would require assessment of the actual burden rather than speculation or a categorical exemption. RCRA's preservation of state environmental authority supplies no automatic congressional authorization of an otherwise unconstitutional interstate burden; the judgment rests on the ordinary constitutional analysis.

## Precedent Treatment

- Zauderer v. Office of Disciplinary Counsel, 471 U.S. 626, is extended to the defined truthful product-content and lawful-disposal setting without requiring separate deception proof; its justification and undue-burden limits remain operative.
- Ibanez v. Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation preserves actual justification and burden review; the State's description of a mandate does not settle its character or validity.
- United States v. United Foods, Inc., 533 U.S. 405, is distinguished as a private promotional-financing case whose express factual-disclosure and health-purpose reservation leaves this issue open.
- Healy v. Beer Institute, 491 U.S. 324, and Brown-Forman Distillers Corp. v. New York State Liquor Authority retain their direct outside-control rule; local compliance costs and a voluntary nationwide production choice do not alone satisfy it.
- Pike v. Bruce Church, Inc., 397 U.S. 137, governs evenhanded interstate burdens without a categorical disparate-burden prerequisite. Its clear-excess standard, applied to actual costs and putative benefits, defeats the present likelihood-of-success showing.

## Law After Decision

The bounded disclosure rule reaches truthful, noncontroversial mercury-content and lawful-disposal facts serving a substantial health or environmental purpose, with reasonable relationship, justification and burden limits. The legal reach of a local-sale command and its interstate practical burdens remain separate inquiries. Evenhandedness does not foreclose Pike review. The current record permits enforcement while the litigation continues.

## Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela joins Parts II and III and concurs in the disclosure judgment. His disclosure rule requires a legitimate health or environmental objective rather than the Court's substantial-purpose threshold. The State must identify that objective and a reasonable connection; the challenger must substantiate burden or misleading operation and establish entitlement to interim relief. Accurate, nonmisleading purchased-product and disposal facts qualify without deception proof, subject to justification and undue-burden limits. Actual content and function distinguish those facts from ideological affirmation, misleading presentation of disputed propositions or unrelated advocacy. Zauderer supports the extension; United Foods, Barnette and Wooley address different compulsion. Vermont's requirement satisfies his rule on the present evidence.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, fully joins Parts I and II. Assuming Pike governs, they agree that the asserted costs and speculative incompatible labels fail to establish likely success. They withhold assent from Part III's affirmative balancing rationale while supporting the same interim disposition.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns with the preliminary restraint vacated. Vermont may enforce the challenged requirement. A renewed interim request must present materially different supporting evidence satisfying the ordinary requirements; repetition that uniform national production is cheaper is insufficient. Remaining final proceedings must apply the controlling disclosure conditions and commerce rules to the actual record, and any proved violation must receive relief fitted to the offending requirement.

## Source Notes

National Electrical Manufacturers Association v. Sorrell, 272 F.3d 104; 72 F. Supp. 2d 449; Vt. Stat. Ann. tit. 10, §6621d; 42 U.S.C. §6929. The required information's accuracy and commercial character are conceded. The legal command concerns goods sold or distributed for use in Vermont, and the preliminary record establishes no actual conflicting sister-state labeling mandate.




# Current-term public authority, available before 2003-03-31

## Source: terms/OT2002/records/A-Womans-Choice-v-Newman_merits_2003-03-24.md
Effective date: 2003-03-24


## Event

A Woman's Choice–East Side Women's Clinic v. Newman. Plenary merits decision, March 24, 2003. On review of the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 305 F.3d 684, No. 01-2107, reversing the permanent injunction reported at 132 F. Supp. 2d 1150. The questions concern Indiana’s compulsory in-person communication of advance abortion information, review of trial and comparative evidence before enforcement, and preservation of separable informed-consent duties.

The statute’s medical-emergency exception excuses its informed-consent conditions. As construed by Indiana’s Supreme Court, it applies when the attending physician, exercising good-faith clinical judgment concerning the patient’s life and physical and mental health, finds pregnancy complications that make prompt therapeutic abortion medically necessary to address a significant threat. Severe but temporary conditions for which abortion is not medically necessary are excluded. The challenged added encounter burdens patients who remain subject to attendance after that exception operates.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

Reversed and remanded for restoration and implementation of the limited in-person-delivery injunction, 6–3.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

Restore and implement the permanent injunction against compulsory in-person delivery of the covered advance information. The independently remaining eighteen-hour interval is outside this reviewed injunction component; leaving it in place does not decide its constitutionality. Accurate material information, qualified communication, genuine clinical assessment and voluntary informed consent remain. A receipt certificate or dependent penalty cannot reinstate the enjoined physical-presence condition.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. The complete coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Kennedy files a dissenting opinion. Scalia files a dissenting opinion, joined by Thomas.

## Holdings

### I. The complete standard and ordinary review rules govern prospective proof

**Holding and operative rule:** A compulsory abortion-information delivery condition is governed by the complete Casey framework, including meaningful-interference review even when no substantial obstacle is proved. Supported historical trial findings receive Rule 52(a) protection, while constitutional sufficiency and application are independently reviewed; reliable comparative experience may establish prospective injury when material similarities and differences are considered.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court’s opinion; the six-Justice whole-opinion coalition identified in Opinion Topology joins this rule in full.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey requires identification of the legal duty and baseline, actual function and purpose, attributable interference and affected population, the corresponding substantive protection, clinical judgment and truthful communication, and fitted relief. Challengers prove duty, causation, affected population and triggering interference. Baseline violations, objectively proved obstructive purpose and substantial obstacles independently fail; other severe interference requires compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated permissible reasons and fit, including an answer to an identified concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative. Below meaningful interference that heightened access inquiry ends, while independent constitutional requirements remain.

Fargo and Mazurek preserve this complete inquiry. Neither Salerno’s universal-invalidity condition nor a fixed numerical fraction displaces it. A court need not await completed local injuries, demand a national study or prescribe one regression method. Comparative evidence is not automatically conclusive either: geography, provider availability, income, transportation and clinical practice can affect transferability. Respect for underlying trial findings does not convert a contested prediction into an established Indiana effect. The Court resolves this case on the separate concrete extra-visit burden, without resolving the disputed projected percentage.

### II. This additional physical encounter lacks sufficient justification

**Holding and operative rule:** When compulsory prior physical attendance adds meaningful travel, work, childcare, scheduling or similar burdens beyond communicating accurate information, the State must substantiate the added encounter’s permissible function and answer a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving that function. Indiana’s uniform advance-presence requirement fails that inquiry on this record because remote communication followed by clinical examination and consent verification at the procedure visit adequately addresses the stated functions, and the State has not identified a material deficiency.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by the same six Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey distinguishes useful information from burdensome timing and delivery; Akron’s protection against unsupported attendance restrictions survives at that scope. Indiana may provide accurate information favoring childbirth, and legislative opposition to abortion alone proves no obstructive purpose. But patients who otherwise need only the procedure visit must make a concrete additional encounter. That added burden exists without assuming every projected decline in abortions reflects prevention rather than persuasion, travel elsewhere or voluntary choice.

The State identifies personal communication, nonverbal responses, misunderstanding and coercion. Those are legitimate concerns, but generalized claims do not explain why reliable advance remote communication, opportunities for questions and later individualized clinical review cannot adequately address them. The performing clinician remains responsible for actual consent and may delay care when consent is unresolved. The alternative therefore preserves clinical responsibility while removing an unsupported uniform trip. Reasonable experience and predictions may substantiate regulation; no new study or numerical benefit calculation is invariably required. Courts may not select a preferred policy once justification and fit are shown. Here that necessary answer is absent. Different restrictions supported by actual clinical need remain governed by the same rule, not a universal entitlement to remote medical treatment.

### III. The delivery-only injunction supplies complete relief on this appeal

**Holding and operative rule:** The compulsory in-person delivery condition and enforcement dependent on it must remain enjoined, while legally separable accurate disclosure and genuine consent duties remain enforceable. The independent eighteen-hour interval is outside this appeal’s reviewed injunction component and is not sustained by the Court’s limited judgment.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by the same six Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey’s remedy principle removes the obligation that fails and preserves lawful requirements. The existing decree does precisely that: it prevents the unnecessary advance physical encounter without requiring a procedure or a clinically necessary consultation to occur remotely, or forbidding an in-person visit requested by the patient. Lawful receipt certification may document information actually supplied; it cannot enforce physical attendance after that obligation is enjoined. The established meaningful-interference record is sufficient, so restoration does not require a new trial directed to the disputed statewide percentage. Any preserved challenge to the interval must receive the governing analysis in its proper posture.

## Precedent Treatment

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey supplies the complete framework and separates lawful accurate content from unsupported timing and delivery burdens; its clinical and remedial qualifications remain.
- Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer confirms that absence of a substantial obstacle does not end review of meaningful interference.
- Mazurek v. Armstrong preserves concrete-burden proof and the full corresponding justification inquiry.
- Akron v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health retains its protection against unsupported waiting and delivery restrictions at the scope preserved by Casey, without barring accurate permissible information.
- United States v. Salerno does not impose universal invalidity or completed local injury as prerequisites to this Casey-governed relief; its treatment in other constitutional fields is unchanged.

## Law After Decision

Comparable compulsory delivery restrictions require justification of the added physical encounter against its established burden and concrete adequate alternatives. Accurate information and lawful persuasion do not themselves justify an unnecessary trip. Prospective proof remains available under ordinary evidentiary rules, without a universal causal percentage or demand that patients first lose access. Indiana’s separate interval receives no constitutional approval from this judgment.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, dissenting.** He retains protected choice but rejects the residual justification rule adopted in Casey and applied here. In his view, personal communication and an opportunity to observe misunderstanding are reasonably related to informed consent. Comparative abortion declines do not establish the number of women prevented from obtaining care, and the record does not justify overriding this reasonable delivery arrangement under his approach. He would affirm reversal of the injunction. He neither treats every decline as voluntary persuasion nor requires completed local injury in every prospective case.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** They retain their Casey, Miller, Voinovich and Stenberg disagreement with the substantive constitutional abortion right. They would sustain this reasonable informed-consent regulation and affirm reversal of the injunction on that distinct foundation. Their position does not deny the extra burdens, replace supported trial findings or attribute the Court’s earlier contrary rule to a different Casey decision. It supplies no controlling law for lower courts.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for restoration and implementation of the delivery-only permanent injunction. The decree preserves the remaining duties at their existing scope and bars enforcement that would recreate compulsory advance physical attendance. No additional evidentiary trial is required to establish the already sufficient meaningful-interference ground.

Newman, 671 N.E.2d 104, 108–111 (Ind. 1996), construing the medical-emergency exception; Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 52(a). The projected ten-to-thirteen-percent reduction is not a finding that all such procedures were prevented; the judgment does not depend on that inference.




# Current-term public authority, available before 2003-06-16

## Source: terms/OT2002/records/Scheidler-v-National-Organization-for-Women-Inc_merits_2003-02-26.md
Effective date: 2003-02-26


## Event

Scheidler v. National Organization for Women, Inc.; consolidated Nos. 01-1118 and 01-1119. October Term 2002. Argued December 4, 2002; decided February 26, 2003. The Court reviews the Seventh Circuit's affirmance of civil RICO liability and a nationwide injunction arising from clinic blockades, threats and other interference. The granted questions concern obtaining property under the extortion predicates and private RICO equitable authority. The United States supports the business-control extortion theory but opposes private RICO injunctions on a separate statutory ground.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the reviewed RICO judgment and vacates its associated treble damages and nationwide injunction in both consolidated dockets.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Judgment:** RICO judgment reversed and dependent damages and injunction vacated, 8–1. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer. **Opposing:** Stevens, who sustains business-control acquisition and dissents from the announced predicate-failure basis for eliminating the judgment and relief. The remand is for implementation: enter the corrected judgment, remove dependent monetary liability and dissolve the RICO restraint. Any implementation issue concerning relief already collected belongs in the proper remedial proceeding.

## Opinion Topology

Scalia delivers the opinion of the Court.

| Part or writing | Joins | Authority or judgment relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Parts I, II and IV: acquisition, related predicates and consequential relief | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Eight-Justice controlling opinion. |
| Part III: physical violence in furtherance of a qualifying plan | O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Seven-Justice controlling opinion. Stone-Zsela concurs in the disposition rejecting the four findings as an independent basis for this judgment, without joining this rationale or adding a ground. |
| Ginsburg concurrence | Breyer | Both join the complete Court opinion. |
| Stevens dissent | No joins | Opposes the acquisition holding and resulting RICO reversal. |

## Holdings

### I. Extortion requires acquisition as well as deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** Hobbs Act extortion through wrongful force, violence or fear requires obtaining another's property, not merely coercing the owner to stop exercising it; attempted extortion must likewise seek the required acquisition. Intangible property, temporary acquisition and noncommercial use can qualify without profit or formal title, but the clinic-shutdown and service-interference theory here identifies compelled inactivity rather than property acquired by defendants or another person.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that what the victim loses and what the defendant obtains are separate statutory questions. Enmons and the distinction between extortion and coercion preserve the acquisition requirement. The earlier NOW decision rejected an economic-motive requirement; it did not decide that these trial predicates satisfied every other element. Civil plaintiffs still must prove their invoked predicates by a preponderance.

Respondents and the Government characterize the coercive veto over clinic operations as acquisition of the owner's exclusive business-control right. Stevens accepts that theory. But its asserted acquisition consists entirely of the owner's forced cessation of business. Calling that deprivation transferred control would collapse extortion into general coercion. Carpenter involved actual appropriation of business information and does not erase the distinction. The Court excludes neither intangible interests generally nor ideologically motivated acquisition; the problem is the obtaining alleged here. The Government's qualified willingness to return the mixed liberty/property charge for examination does not cure this defect in the business-control theory itself, and respondents made no equivalent concession. The statutory failure resolves the extortion judgment without a new trial on deprivation alone.

### II. The related state, travel and conspiracy categories do not supply the missing acquisition

**Holding and operative rule:** State extortion invoked as a RICO predicate must satisfy the generic obtaining-property requirement, and attempts or agreements directed only to the same nonacquisitive coercion do not cure that failure. The actual Travel Act and attempted-travel predicates, characterized as furthering this extortion undertaking, fall with it; a broader instructional list is not an adjudicated independent unlawful-activity basis.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by the same eight Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that different labels cannot substitute for the federal category's elements. Nardello supplies generic extortion analysis, and Taylor supports attention to the category rather than local nomenclature. State force, fear and value conditions do not supply acquisition absent from the undertaking. The travel charge mentioned extortion, arson and crimes of violence, but the reviewed predicates furthered extortion; the broader list alone establishes neither arson nor another qualifying unlawful activity. A crime of violence is not universally a substitute for the Travel Act's specified unlawful activity.

Salinas removes the requirement that every conspirator personally commit two predicates but retains knowing agreement toward a qualifying substantive undertaking. It cannot turn agreement to the rejected coercive-deprivation object into extortion. Nor does the direction to bypass separate conspiracy questions after Claim I liability supply an independent affirmative verdict. The existing judgment therefore lacks a surviving predicate merely by relabeling the same undertaking.

### III. The four physical-violence findings do not establish an independent Hobbs offense

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1951(a)'s physical-violence language requires violence or threats in furtherance of a plan or purpose to violate the section through robbery or extortion, including its inchoate forms; it does not independently federalize every act of violence affecting commerce. The four findings under interrogatory 4(e) cannot sustain this RICO judgment as freestanding Hobbs offenses after the extortion undertaking fails, and no distinct robbery undertaking is established here.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; seven direct joins. Stone-Zsela supports the disposition on this component without joining its rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory connection cannot be omitted. The four acts or threats were separately listed in the contemporaneous verdict and must be addressed. The instruction listed physical violence without connecting that category to a robbery or extortion plan. Its breadth cannot expand the enacted offense. The rejected acquisition theory therefore cannot be rescued merely by describing its acts as violence affecting commerce.

The verdict's combined pattern, commerce, causation and damage answers also do not segregate those four incidents into a separately sustainable recovery. The finding that extortion involved more than blockades or sit-ins concerns the extortion interrogatories, not independent validation of 4(e).

### IV. Failure of the liability basis removes the dependent relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the reviewed RICO judgment lacks the required predicates, its associated treble damages and nationwide injunction must be removed. General private equitable authority under §1964 remains undecided because failure of the underlying violation independently disposes of this decree.

**Authority:** Scalia's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer; eight direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a remedy requires its own valid liability basis. The Government's structural objection to private injunctions and respondents' broader reading of §1964 need not be resolved once this RICO judgment fails. ACLA adjudicated different threatening conduct under its own authority and expressly left the RICO questions open. Its threat-meaning rule and independent intent findings cannot supply acquisition here. FACE likewise remains a separate source with its own conditions; this judgment cannot be converted into relief under that statute. Implementation removes the RICO restraint and monetary liability rather than authorizing a new search for hypothetical predicates. Other claims retain only the effect of their own judgments and lawful procedural existence.

## Precedent Treatment

- National Organization for Women, Inc. v. Scheidler, 510 U.S. 249, retains its no-economic-motive holding and independent element and remedy requirements; its pleading-stage decision did not establish acquisition at trial.
- United States v. Enmons, 410 U.S. 396, preserves the distinction between coercion and extortion requiring property acquisition.
- United States v. Nardello, 393 U.S. 286, and Taylor v. United States, 495 U.S. 575, supply generic-category analysis rather than reliance on state labels.
- Carpenter v. United States, 484 U.S. 19, recognizes genuinely appropriated intangible business property and does not dispense with acquisition.
- Salinas v. United States (December 2, 1997) preserves the qualifying substantive object despite dispensing with personal commission of two predicate acts.
- Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette v. ACLA (December 10, 2002) retains its source-specific threat rule and fitted remedy; neither establishes the statutory predicates or private RICO authority presented here.

## Law After Decision

Extortion requires acquisition, including acquisition of a genuinely obtainable intangible interest, rather than deprivation alone. Generic state extortion and dependent travel or conspiracy theories must satisfy that element. Section 1951(a)'s violence language remains tied to a qualifying robbery or extortion plan. The reviewed RICO damages and injunction fail; private RICO equitable authority remains open for a case requiring its decision.

## Separate Writings

Stevens dissents because exclusive control over business assets is itself valuable property. Coercion that forces surrender of that control can obtain it even when the actor seeks closure rather than profit or subsequent operation. He relies on Tropiano and Arena's treatment of business control, Carpenter's recognition of intangible property, and the established appellate construction he would leave to Congress to change. He accepts that the violence must further robbery or extortion, but finds a qualifying extortion undertaking here. He regards the acquisition as supported and opposes the Court's predicate-failure basis for eliminating the judgment and relief. His dissent supplies no separate affirmative holding on private equitable authority.

Ginsburg, joined by Breyer, concurs fully and emphasizes the consequences of a broader construction for protest activity subject to RICO's severe remedies. Congress's targeted FACE protection does not justify stretching extortion to cover every unlawful interference. Their distinction preserves liability for conduct that satisfies the actual statute and creates no immunity for violence or obstruction.

## Procedure After Action

The lower courts must conform the judgments to the reversal, remove the dependent treble-damages liability and dissolve the nationwide RICO injunction. The remand implements that result and does not order a new trial on the rejected deprivation-only theory.
