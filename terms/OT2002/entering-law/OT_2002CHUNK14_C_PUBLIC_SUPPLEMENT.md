# Gibson-v-County-of-Washoe_merits_2003-01-14

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Gibson-v-County-of-Washoe_merits_2003-01-14.md`

## Event

Gibson v. County of Washoe, 290 F.3d 1175. Merits decision, January 14, 2003. On review of the Ninth Circuit's May 22, 2002 judgment reversing summary judgment for the County on supported mental-health policy claims and affirming judgment for the named deputies and supervising sergeant. The question is whether a County policy may cause an actionable denial of a detainee's serious medical needs despite the named officers' lack of the subjective awareness required for personal liability.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## Public Action

Affirmed, 9–0; the supported County screening/evaluation and medication-policy claims return for ordinary further proceedings, with the remaining dispositions preserved.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The supported County claims concerning screening/evaluation and medication handling return for ordinary further proceedings, including trial if the remaining procedural requirements are satisfied. Summary judgment for the named deputies and sergeant, rejection of the unsupported communications-policy theory, and the unappealed consortium and state-law dispositions remain intact.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivered the opinion of the Court, in which Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer joined. The opinion and judgment are unanimous.

## Holdings

### The municipality's own policy may cause a constitutional deprivation

**Holding and operative rule:** A municipality may be liable under §1983 for its own deliberately indifferent custodial policy that actually causes a denial of constitutionally required medical care, even when the named employees lack the personal awareness required for their liability. The claimant must prove an actual constitutional deprivation, an attributable municipal policy or legally effective custom, a deliberate municipal choice concerning a known or obvious constitutional need, and an identified defect closely related to and actually causing the injury; negligence, employment alone and pooled employee knowledge do not suffice.

**Authority:** The unanimous opinion of the Court, authored by Stevens and joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls the rule, the summary-judgment application and the limited return for further proceedings.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a named employee's exoneration does not collapse the separate inquiries into the deprivation and its municipal source. Monell requires the municipality's own causal policy or custom. Collins separates the underlying constitutional wrong from attribution; the absence of a custody-based duty in its voluntary-employment setting does not govern a pretrial detainee's custody. Canton requires deliberate municipal choice and a close causal connection. Carter permits notice through a pattern or a sufficiently obvious need to perform a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; identical prior injuries are not invariably necessary.

The County correctly insists on an actual deprivation and more than negligent administration. But the named deputies' lack of relevant awareness establishes neither that all required care was furnished nor that a County policy could not disable that care. Farmer's individual-awareness rule does not permit combining employees' innocent information to manufacture personal culpability. The municipal claim instead must identify and prove the County's own deliberately indifferent policy and its injury-producing operation. This preserves the underlying constitutional-injury requirement rather than substituting institutional responsibility for it.

### The supported screening and medication evidence permits further proceedings

**Holding and operative rule:** At summary judgment, a claimant must identify evidence permitting a reasonable finding on every required municipal-liability element. The evidence concerning delayed mental-health evaluation and handling of psychotropic medication meets that standard here; the surviving claims return under the ordinary preponderance-of-the-evidence burden, while the unsupported communications theory and the preserved personal and unappealed dispositions remain excluded.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the evidence, viewed under Rule 56, supports a distinct policy inquiry. The jail regularly held mentally ill detainees, policymakers knew of that recurring population, and the evidence concerns practices postponing evaluation until an acutely disturbed person cooperated and treating relevant medication as property rather than a signal for assessment. General knowledge of mental illness alone would not suffice. These particular practices, combined with the recurring constitutional responsibility and medication evidence, permit a finding beyond hindsight that more training might have helped.

The County remains entitled to contest policy authority or effective custom, deliberate choice, deprivation and actual cause. Death during restraint does not itself establish a medical-care violation or that a different policy would have prevented the injury. The reasonable-force and personal-awareness rulings therefore survive, but they do not decide the independent medical-policy claim. The communications theory lacks the necessary policymaker-notice support and cannot survive merely by association with the supported policies. On remand, the family must prove the surviving elements and any compensable injury; ordinary factual and remedial adjudication under those rules does not make the municipal standard unresolved.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658:** Applies the municipality's own policy-or-custom and causation requirements; employer-based liability remains forbidden.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115:** Applies the separate inquiries into constitutional deprivation and municipal attribution; distinguishes voluntary employment from actual pretrial custody.
- **City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378:** Applies demanding municipal fault, notice, identified deficiency and causal connection; no negligence or hindsight standard replaces them.
- **Carter v. City of Philadelphia, March 13, 2000:** Applies its pattern-or-obvious-recurring-need notice rule and actual-cause requirement without adding an invariably identical-prior-incident condition.
- **Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825:** Preserves individualized actual awareness and the protection for a reasonable response; neither collective awareness nor a failed reasonable response supplies personal culpability. The distinct municipal inquiry does not alter that rule.

## Law After Decision

Existing municipal and custodial law governs. A municipality cannot obtain judgment automatically from the named employees' lack of personal awareness when supported evidence independently connects an actual medical-care deprivation to its own deliberately indifferent policy. The same demanding deprivation, attribution, fault and causation requirements control analogous custodial policy claims. The decision supplies neither institutional strict liability nor a new aggregate-awareness test. Section 3626's requirements for prospective prison-conditions relief remain unchanged.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for ordinary proceedings on the supported County screening/evaluation and medication-policy claims. The trial issues must identify those surviving policies. The family bears the ordinary preponderance burden on the required liability elements and must establish compensable loss under the applicable damages rules; the County may contest each. The affirmed personal judgments and the excluded communications, consortium and state-law dispositions remain effective.

# Westside-Mothers-v-Haveman_merits_2002-12-10

Source: `terms/OT2002/records/Westside-Mothers-v-Haveman_merits_2002-12-10.md`

## Event

Westside Mothers v. Haveman; judgment below, 289 F.3d 852. Merits decision, December 10, 2002, on full merits submission reviewing the Sixth Circuit's May 15, 2002 judgment reversing categorical dismissal and remanding. The questions are whether the specified Medicaid child screening, diagnostic and treatment requirements confer rights enforceable under §1983, and whether prospective relief against responsible state officials remains available despite the Spending Clause basis and voluntary participation. The plaintiffs allege unmet services, information and arrangements in Michigan's managed-care system; these are allegations rather than adjudicated individual deprivations.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated.

## Public Action

The reviewed child-service and prospective-officer rulings are affirmed, and the case is remanded for further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0, within the reviewed child-service questions.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The live child-service claims may proceed under the rules below. The existing transportation preclusion and organizational-standing dispositions retain their stated effect; the separate provider claims remain outside this review.

## Opinion Topology

Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine join the complete opinion. Scalia files a concurrence, joined by Kennedy, preserving an original-meaning reservation without withholding any join.

## Holdings

### 1. Eligible children may enforce the specified Medicaid benefits

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 1396a(a)(10) and (43)(A)–(C), read with §§1396d(a)(4)(B) and (r), give Medicaid-eligible children under twenty-one mandatory individual rights to the defined medical assistance, information, requested screening and arrangements for corrective treatment, enforceable through §1983. The right requires the applicable eligibility, service-category, request, screening-discovered need and coverage conditions stated below; it does not establish a personal right to optimal program administration, staffing, provider rates, overall capacity or a preferred provider.

**Authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt this holding and its statutory limits.

**Statutory content and conditions:**

- Section (43)(A) requires informing persons in the State under twenty-one who have been determined eligible for medical assistance including early and periodic screening, diagnostic and treatment services (EPSDT), about those services and the need for age-appropriate immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases.
- Section (43)(B) requires providing or arranging screening whenever requested. Section (43)(C) requires arranging, directly or by referral to appropriate agencies, organizations or individuals, corrective treatment whose need screening discloses. The request condition belongs to screening; the disclosed-need condition belongs to corrective arrangements. Screening of unwilling children is not compelled.
- Section 1396d(r)(1) requires screening at medically and dentally reasonable periodic intervals determined by the State after consultation with recognized child-health organizations, and at other medically necessary intervals. Its minimum comprises a comprehensive health and developmental history addressing physical and mental development, an unclothed physical examination, age-and-history-appropriate immunizations, laboratory tests including age-and-risk-appropriate lead assessment, and health education including anticipatory guidance. Pediatric immunization follows the separately referenced statutory vaccine schedule.
- Sections (r)(2)–(4) separately cover vision, dental and hearing services at professionally reasonable periodic and other medically necessary intervals. Their minima include diagnosis and treatment of vision defects, including eyeglasses; pain and infection relief, tooth restoration and maintenance of dental health; and diagnosis and treatment of hearing defects, including hearing aids.
- Section (r)(5) covers necessary health care, diagnosis, treatment and other measures of the kinds described in §1396d(a) to correct or ameliorate physical or mental defects, illnesses or conditions discovered through screening, whether or not the State plan otherwise covers the service. Actual statutory service-category, need and coverage conditions remain.
- A provider qualified to supply one or more EPSDT items may supply them without qualification for every item. Section (43)(D)'s reporting and participation measures are not converted into additional individual rights by this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Blessing's provision-specific inquiry. Congress identifies the eligible child and the mandatory benefit, and the service definitions supply standards courts can apply through medical evidence. Wright and Wilder show that clinical or administrative judgment does not make a defined entitlement judicially unmanageable. The officials' strongest objection treats the plan as an undertaking only to the federal administrator. But the particular child-directed benefits, together with §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10's rejection of state-plan placement as a categorical bar, defeat that objection. Suter retains its different reasonable-efforts and reporting holdings; it establishes no contrary rule for every plan provision. Gonzaga's FERPA-specific outcome does not erase Blessing or turn its four-Justice general formulation into controlling law.

Enforcement remains a separate inquiry. Blessing and Bruneau require actual congressional foreclosure, and §1396c's funding oversight supplies no sufficiently comprehensive incompatible individual remedy. Managed-care contracts do not eliminate the State's financing, informing and arrangements obligations. They establish neither an actual deprivation nor a guarantee that state employees personally render treatment. Aggregate deficiencies may prove an identified child's violation or support fitted relief; they cannot create a different personal entitlement.

### 2. Accepted federal duties may be enforced prospectively against responsible officials

**Holding and operative rule:** A valid Spending Clause enactment remains federal law when its accepted conditions govern a participating State. Officials actually connected to a continuing violation of these individual federal rights may be ordered to comply prospectively; relief must address the proved future violation and may not award retrospective State compensation or compel obedience to state law.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Opinion of the Court directly adopts this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Ex parte Young's connected-official route. Pennhurst's funding analogy explains clear notice and acceptance, while Bennett rejects reducing an enacted condition to an ordinary bilateral promise. Michigan's voluntary choice to participate does not make compliance voluntary while it participates. The State's real interest and implementation discretion therefore do not sustain categorical dismissal.

Verizon requires examination of the actual defendant, continuing federal violation and remedy. Coeur d'Alene rejects an additional generalized sovereign-interest barrier. Medicaid's general funding supervision supplies no exclusive remedial sequence displacing this action. Allegations of a continuing deprivation suffice against the categorical defense at this stage; coercive relief requires proof. Edelman permits the prospective costs of lawful compliance but bars retrospective treasury compensation, and Pennhurst separately bars state-law commands. The source-of-law conclusion, individual right, §1983 channel and immunity boundary remain distinct. Neither voluntary participation nor this officer action establishes consent to direct State damages, and the Court announces no separate private cause under the Supremacy Clause.

### 3. The live child-service claims return for adjudication under those rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit is affirmed insofar as it rejected the categorical defenses and allowed the reviewed child-entitlement action to proceed. On remand each live claimant must establish standing and eligibility, the applicable statutory version and obligation, concrete deprivation, responsible official, continuing breach and causation before a court awards relief fitted to that violation.

**Authority:** All nine join this application and remand in the same Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Dismissal prevented findings on actual service failures and the proper scope of relief. Blessing requires matching the asserted right to the claimant, conduct, provision and remedy. An established statewide pattern may support relief connected to proved violations, but a statistical shortfall alone warrants neither a new personal right nor automatic redesign of the system. These are factual and remedial tasks under the rule just stated.

The December 1999 transportation-assistance preclusion ruling was not appealed and remains effective. The Michigan League for Human Services' unappealed dismissal, Michigan Welfare Rights Organization's affirmed dismissal and the professional associations' restored standing retain the posture fixed below. The distinct provider and professional-association claims lie outside the granted child-service questions; this decision adds no individual provider-rate entitlement under §1396a(a)(30)(A).

## Precedent Treatment

- Blessing v. Freestone: applies particular-right, foreclosure and fitted-remedy inquiries; the general program-adequacy exclusion remains.
- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District: applies the distinction between an existing right and actual remedial foreclosure; Medicaid is not treated as substantively identical to Title IX.
- Gonzaga University v. Doe: distinguishes the FERPA entitlement and preserves its outcome and the nonmajority status of its broader rights formulation.
- Suter v. Artist M.: preserves its actual reasonable-efforts, reporting and separate implied-action holdings and its independent case-plan question; no categorical state-plan bar is applied.
- Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority; Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association: apply defined statutory entitlement and manageable qualitative application; regulations may elaborate an existing right but do not create the right held here.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman; Bennett v. Kentucky Department of Education: preserve clear notice and acceptance while rejecting a contract-only account of enacted law; Pennhurst's separate state-law-relief bar remains.
- Ex parte Young; Edelman v. Jordan; Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe; Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission: apply the connected-official, ongoing-federal-violation and prospective-remedy rules, preserving retrospective and state-law limits.
- Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas: its distinct express-authorization and Fourteenth Amendment enforcement holdings do not alter this statutory right or prospective-officer route.

## Law After Decision

Eligible children may enforce the identified mandatory Medicaid child benefits under §1983, subject to their exact statutory conditions and proof of violation. Responsible officials remain amenable to prospective compliance relief. This provision-specific application adds the child-service holding while preserving the existing right, foreclosure and remedy framework; it creates no right to general administrative perfection.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy, concurring.** They join the opinion under the governing statutory-right precedents. As in their Blessing concurrence, they retain the original-meaning reservation concerning whether beneficiaries of a funding undertaking possess rights of the kind §1983 was enacted to enforce. That reservation does not support treating a federal enactment as nonlaw or refusing the established inquiry into these defined benefits. They adopt no additional restriction on the Court's operative rule or relief.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for provision-specific adjudication of the live child-service claims and any properly supported prospective relief. The lower court must determine the actual unmet needs, causation, class scope and terms of any decree under the controlling rules.
