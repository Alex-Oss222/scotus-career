### Monge v. California, No. 97-6146

Merits decision, June 26, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 28, 1998. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of California, reviewing 16 Cal. 4th 826, 941 P.2d 1121 (1997). The question is whether the Double Jeopardy Clause bars another attempt to establish the serious-prior-felony allegation after the first proof failed to establish its necessary personal-conduct qualification.

The jury convicted Monge of using a minor to sell marijuana, selling or transporting marijuana, and possessing marijuana for sale. The information gave notice of a serious-prior-felony allegation and a distinct prior-prison-term allegation. Monge waived a jury on those allegations. At the bifurcated bench proceeding, the State introduced a prison record establishing an assault conviction and imprisonment, but not necessarily the required personal use of a dangerous or deadly weapon or personal infliction of great bodily injury. The prosecutor's assertion about use of a stick was not evidence of that missing fact. The trial court found both allegations true and imposed eleven years: a selected five-year term doubled to ten, plus one year for the separate prison-term allegation. A three-year term on count two was stayed and a two-year term on count three ran concurrently.

The State conceded on appeal that the first record did not prove the serious-prior qualification beyond a reasonable doubt. The Court of Appeal found insufficiency and barred retrial. The California Supreme Court reversed that double-jeopardy ruling, permitting another proof proceeding. The seven-year unenhanced maximum on count one, fourteen-year qualifying-prior maximum, selected five-year term and independent one-year enhancement remain distinct.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg.

The judgment rejecting the federal double-jeopardy bar is affirmed. California may, but is not required to, attempt renewed proof of the serious-prior allegation under applicable state law and safeguards. The unsupported first finding is not reinstated; neither qualifying personal-conduct alternative is found true, no burden is reduced and no enhanced sentence is directed. The drug convictions and independent prior-prison-term enhancement are not invalidated. Independent defenses and applicable sentencing constraints remain.

The dissenters would reverse permission to retry either qualifying alternative for the same failed serious-prior allegation and remand for lawful sentencing without its doubling effect, while preserving the separate enhancement and convictions. Neither disposition fixes an aggregate sentence, release date or the treatment of stayed or concurrent counts.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer. They join Parts I–II in full: the classification of this proceeding as a noncapital determination of recidivist status, the conclusion that jeopardy does not attach, and the bounded remedy permitting renewed proof.

Justice Scalia files a dissent, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, addressing the additional personal-conduct qualification's constitutional function as an element and the resulting finality of acquittal. Justice Stevens files an additional dissent on the finality of this failed trial-like aggravating adjudication even if it is treated as genuine noncapital sentencing.

Stevens joins Scalia's case-confined element ground and separately gives a broader finality ground. His join adopts no general rule for every fact that increases a punishment maximum; the common dissent addresses this additional personal-conduct qualification and the completed failed adjudication. Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg do not join an extension of Bullington to genuine noncapital sentencing merely because trial safeguards were afforded. No combination of dissenting rationales creates a Court holding.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. This noncapital recidivist-status determination does not receive offense-acquittal finality

**Holding and operative rule:** The Double Jeopardy Clause does not bar renewed proof of the serious character of Monge's prior conviction in this noncapital sentencing proceeding after the first proof was insufficient. Here the required personal-conduct qualification determines recidivist status for punishment of the current offense, rather than adjudicating a new offense or a capital acquittal-like determination; neither the availability of trial safeguards nor the increase in the punishment ceiling alone makes this particular determination an offense acquittal.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer; five direct joins to this classification and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** DiFrancesco distinguishes ordinary sentencing determinations from final acquittals of offenses. Bullington and Rumsey preserve acquittal-like protection for their capital adjudications; the Court does not erase that exception or make a statutory label conclusive. The present allegation concerns the serious character of an existing conviction as a basis for punishment of the current drug offense. Renewed proof neither prosecutes the old assault again nor reopens the current drug verdicts.

The strongest contrary argument is that the generic conviction does not establish the required personal use or infliction, and that this additional fact authorizes a higher maximum after a completed criminal-burden hearing. The Court accepts those premises. It nevertheless classifies this inquiry into the prior conviction's qualifying character as noncapital recidivist sentencing, not a new offense adjudication. California's jury, confrontation, evidence and reasonable-doubt safeguards govern the proceeding; their provision does not itself create federal acquittal finality. Legislative characterization alone could not remove protection from an actual offense.

Caspari left these direct-review merits open. Almendarez-Torres construed the federal reentry statute to make its prior conviction an aggravated-offense element; it adopted neither a general constitutional recidivism exception nor a general maximum-increase rule. Its statutory construction does not decide California's different classification. Witte and Watts address sentencing within conviction-authorized punishment and do not independently answer the increased-ceiling question here. The Court therefore decides this particular classification directly rather than treating those cases as foreclosing the argument.

The holding supplies no comprehensive classification of all maximum-increasing facts, mandatory minima, unchanged-range findings or prior-conviction procedures. It decides no independent indictment, jury or due-process claim, changes no actual jury waiver, and permits no relabeling of an actual offense to evade jeopardy.

##### II. Permission to prove the allegation again neither repairs the first record nor directs an enhancement

**Holding and operative rule:** The California judgment permitting another proof proceeding is affirmed because the disputed adjudication does not receive offense-acquittal protection, not because the State's first evidence was sufficient or its failure excusable. Any renewed proceeding must satisfy applicable law and safeguards; no true finding, automatic doubling, aggregate sentence, or invalidation of the independent prior-prison-term enhancement or drug convictions follows from this judgment.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion; the same five Justices join the complete remedy and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The State conceded that the original prison record failed to prove personal weapon use or personal infliction of great bodily injury beyond a reasonable doubt. A valid generic assault conviction remains distinct from the unproved serious-felony qualification. The Court makes no new finding about the assault and does not treat the prosecutor's assertion as evidence.

Burks, as applied in Hinds, bars a second prosecution opportunity to supply missing proof after actual insufficiency in a constitutionally protected offense adjudication. That consequence is mandatory when its predicate exists. The present judgment rests on nonattachment to this recidivist-status determination; it creates no trial-error exception, proof-repair permission after an offense acquittal, or authority to reopen a protected capital acquittal. Watts's different-burden evidentiary-use rule supplies no such permission either.

The State may attempt renewed proof only under governing state law. The Court neither requires it to do so nor predicts success, and it does not decide whether another independent defense or sentencing constraint limits that course. Count one's seven-year unenhanced maximum is not a fixed lawful aggregate sentence; the selected term, doubled term, independent year, stayed count and concurrent count retain their distinct treatment. Those matters remain for lawful proceedings below.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. DiFrancesco:** Applied to distinguish ordinary sentence reconsideration from final offense acquittal, without making a sentencing label dispositive.
- **Bullington v. Missouri; Arizona v. Rumsey:** Preserved for acquittal-like capital adjudications; the Court declines to extend their protection to this noncapital recidivist-status determination.
- **Caspari v. Bohlen:** Its habeas new-rule holding remains intact; its reservation of the direct-review merits is answered only for the proceeding now presented.
- **Almendarez-Torres v. United States:** Its statutory aggravated-reentry element holding remains controlling. It supplies neither a general constitutional recidivism exception nor an already controlling constitutional maximum-increase rule; no part is overruled.
- **Witte v. United States; United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra:** Retain their bounds concerning ordinary sentencing within conviction-based authority and their independent constitutional reservations. They do not independently decide this increased-maximum classification or permit retrial after an offense acquittal.
- **McMillan v. Pennsylvania:** Its unchanged-maximum setting does not resolve this different ceiling increase; its limited holding is undisturbed.
- **Burks v. United States; United States v. Hinds:** Preserve acquittal and the bar on renewed proof after actual insufficiency in a protected offense adjudication. The result here depends on classification and nonattachment, not a new exception to that consequence.
- **United States v. Dixon; Grady v. Corbin:** Dixon's retained Grady rule remains unchanged. Those same-offense rules do not themselves classify the disputed enhancement proceeding as a prosecution to which jeopardy attaches.

No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The previously unresolved direct-review issue is resolved for this noncapital serious-prior qualification: failed proof does not receive offense-acquittal finality, so the Double Jeopardy Clause does not forbid another lawful proof proceeding. The State still must prove the qualifying facts under governing safeguards, and the initial unsupported finding is not restored. Actual offense acquittals, acquittal-like capital determinations, the independent convictions and enhancement, and unpresented jury, indictment and due-process questions retain their separate rules. No universal constitutional recidivism exception or rule for every maximum-increasing fact is adopted. The dissenters' element-function and additional sentencing-finality grounds are noncontrolling.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, dissenting.** The additional personal-conduct fact was not established by the old conviction and was indispensable to authority for the greater punishment maximum. It therefore performs the constitutional work of an offense element. Calling it the serious character of a prior conviction does not remove that function. Ordinary reliance on criminal history within already authorized punishment is different from adjudicating a new fact needed to enlarge the available range. McMillan's unchanged ceiling and Witte's and Watts's ordinary sentencing uses do not answer this case. Almendarez-Torres resolved its federal statute without a controlling constitutional exception, and California's classification now requires deciding the constitutional question within the preserved double-jeopardy claim.

California afforded a completed reasonable-doubt adjudication and conceded that its proof failed. Once the allegation is recognized as an element for this finality inquiry, Burks and Hinds bar another opportunity to supply the missing prosecution evidence. Either personal weapon use or personal infliction of great bodily injury was an alternative way to qualify the same failed allegation; switching alternatives cannot avoid the bar. These Justices would reverse and require lawful sentencing without its doubling effect. They do not invalidate the generic assault conviction, the drug convictions or the separate prison-term enhancement, prescribe an aggregate term, or adjudicate a separate indictment or jury defect. This is an element-function ground, not a holding that trial procedures alone extend Bullington to genuine noncapital sentencing.

**Stevens, additionally dissenting.** He joins that confined element conclusion but also considers the State's failed aggravating adjudication final even if the proceeding remains genuine sentencing. The State chose a trial-like determination, had a complete opportunity to prove the prescribed allegation beyond a reasonable doubt, and failed to establish the condition producing additional punishment. Bullington's acquittal-finality principle and Burks's prohibition on repeated attempts to repair insufficient prosecution proof should not turn on whether the possible penalty is death. His Caspari position likewise would protect a completed status adjudication without making a changed maximum a necessary condition. State procedural generosity alone creates no federal right, but the adjudication's substantive punitive consequence, criminal burden and completed proof failure together justify protection. He would reverse renewed-proof permission on this additional ground and order the same bounded sentencing remand. Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg do not join this extension to genuine noncapital sentencing.

#### Source Notes

The factual and lower-court account in [524 U.S. 721, 724–729](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-524/pdf/USREPORTS-524-721.pdf) supplies the convictions, separate enhancement allegations, jury waiver, evidence introduced, concession of insufficiency, California proceedings and distinct sentencing figures. The cited account does not establish the unreported contents of the appendix prison record or complete California opinions. The reported concession and procedural issue do not require a finding about the assault's actual personal conduct; none is made.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the California Supreme Court for further proceedings consistent with the affirmed rejection of the federal double-jeopardy bar. A renewed serious-prior proof proceeding is permitted only as otherwise lawful; it is not commanded. The unsupported first serious-prior finding and doubled term are not reinstated by this decision, and no substitute sentence or release order is entered. Supreme Court review of this event is complete; the remaining state proceedings retain their independent legal constraints.

**End of entry: Monge v. California, Merits decision, June 26, 1998.**

### Calderon v. Thompson, No. 97-215

Merits decision, April 29, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 9, 1997. On review of the Ninth Circuit’s August 3, 1997 recall of its mandate in Thompson’s first federal habeas proceeding. California seeks reversal and reinstatement of the June 11 mandate denying relief.

Thompson was convicted in 1983 of murder and rape; the rape special circumstance supported death eligibility. His January 1990 federal application produced rape-related relief and vacation of the death sentence in 1995, while the murder claim was denied. A circuit panel reversed the granted relief. After January 17, 1997 notice concerning rejection of the en banc suggestion, a March 12 request for belated consideration described a law-clerk communication failure; another judge supported it, and the panel author rejected it. The mandate issued June 11.

The en banc majority later reported a separate court-initiated recall request shortly after issuance. That initiative preceded the prisoner’s July new-evidence motion but was not an entered recall or stay. The August 3 recall, after the circuit’s stated wait for state proceedings, rested on the first application’s claims and evidence and excluded the later Leitch account. It restored conditional rape-conviction and rape-special-circumstance relief and the associated death-sentence vacation, directed further murder review, and vacated the then-entered execution order.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded with instructions, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Reinstatement of the mandate denying the first application removes the recall-dependent direction to enter a partial rape-related writ unless California elected to retry within a reasonable time, the associated death-sentence vacation, the further-review direction concerning murder, and the execution-order vacatur. No new execution date, self-executing revival of an expired date, judgment on a distinct later application or displacement of an independent lawful protection follows.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part I only | Reverses recall and restores the prior mandate | Part I: review channel, first-application record and temporal law; Parts II–III: recall standard, gateway applications and remedy |
| Dissent | Stevens | Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Would affirm the recall | Exceptional institutional correction; no independent innocence or ineffective-assistance holding |

#### Holdings

##### The recall judgment is reviewable through the ordinary channel

**Controlling proposition:** Felker’s §2244(b)(3)(E) restriction on review of permission to file a successive district-court application does not bar ordinary review of this separate mandate-recall judgment confined to the first application’s claims and evidence. Under Lindh and Carriger, the identified amended §2254 merits and replacement factual-presumption provisions do not govern this capital application filed in January 1990, outside established chapter 154 qualification; other statutory provisions retain their own temporal and procedural predicates.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor’s opinion is joined by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the legal channel must match the action reviewed. Felker concerns a distinct authorization decision; the rejection of Thompson’s separate July motion does not settle the court-initiated recall. Its limitation to the first application excludes Leitch’s later parole account. Calling action court-initiated would not permit a disguised successive application to evade its governing statute, but that basis is not established here.

Lindh’s provision-specific temporal construction, as Carriger applies it to already pending capital applications, preserves the former merits and factual-finding rules here. No California chapter 154 qualification is established. This conclusion does not exempt every later filing from AEDPA or decide a hearing provision’s independent temporal reach.

##### Completed habeas merits cannot be reopened on ordinary reconsideration alone

**Controlling proposition:** When recall reopens the completed merits of a state prisoner’s habeas proceeding, rather than correcting a genuinely different defect in judicial process, exceptional recall requires the applicable miscarriage-of-justice showing: Schlup’s crime-innocence gateway for an attack on a conviction, or Sawyer’s distinct gateway for an eligibility-only attack. A clerical correction, fraud, a mandate that does not express the court’s judgment or another genuinely different process defect remains distinct; capital punishment or disagreement with the prior merits result alone is insufficient.

**Authority:** Parts II–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, including the author. These five directly adopt this rule, its application and the remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the circuit’s action reopened completed merits and abused its exceptional recall discretion. Hazel-Atlas recognizes protection of the judicial process on its demonstrated fraud predicate, while Ohio Power illustrates exceptional authority over the Court’s own procedure. Neither makes every declined rehearing perpetually reopenable. Carlisle forbids using inherent power to bypass actual governing constraints; Lonchar protects required process without creating a right to en banc review.

The January notice and actual March request and rejection allowed institutional attention before the June mandate. The problem was not first discovered immediately before execution. The separate June initiative confirms that the full court acted independently of the prisoner’s later motion, but it neither suspended nor recalled the mandate. The ensuing delay, while the State proceeded in reliance on finality, is not justified merely by invoking the stated wait for state proceedings.

Most decisively, the August action restored rape-related relief and reopened murder review. It changed the completed substantive adjudication. No finding of dishonest purpose, universal judicial awareness or unlawful March rejection is necessary. The Court therefore extends the appropriate innocence gateway to this category of merits recall, giving effect to finality and comity while retaining genuine process correction. It does not hold that Schlup or Sawyer already governed every recall or that every court-initiated recall is a successive district-court application.

##### The augmented first-application record meets neither distinct gateway

**Controlling proposition:** A conviction attack under Schlup requires reliable new evidence assessed with the whole probative record showing that, more likely than not, no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict beyond a reasonable doubt. An eligibility-only attack under Sawyer requires clear and convincing evidence that, absent constitutional error, no reasonable juror would find the necessary rape special circumstance and other state-law eligibility predicates; Thompson’s first-application record satisfies neither showing.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice Parts II–III coalition directly adopts both component-specific applications. No fractured-decision synthesis is involved.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court considers the augmented evidence together. The added Fink and Del Frate material includes specific informant histories, benefits, dishonesty assessments, Del Frate’s earlier association with Leitch and correspondence between his account and newspaper reports. It further weakens testimony already substantially impeached at trial; it is not disregarded merely because it impeaches. But the added information does not require every reasonable juror to reject the accounts in light of the remaining evidence.

Root disputes the handcuff inference, bruising chronology and sparse sperm. His opinions provide no alternative cause for the crushing injury, select neither douching nor low sperm count, and do not establish consent. Semen supports intercourse rather than force by itself. Restraint evidence, altered clothing, gagging, remaining confession accounts and Thompson’s inconsistent explanations nevertheless permit a reasonable adverse conclusion on the augmented record. Conflicting accounts of which wrist was injured support no inference. The excluded later Leitch account does not enter this assessment.

Carriger requires coherent whole-record analysis, not remand in every gateway dispute. Former §2254(d) protects qualifying state findings after the required hearing and adequate written indicia, subject to its exceptions; paragraph (8) requires the pertinent produced state record as a whole. Marshall preserves that historical and credibility protection. The Court does not assume a state finding on every added item, treat a federal panel’s findings as state findings, or give the original verdict conclusive force. A prior finding cannot conclusively resolve evidence it never assessed.

Evidence may be new relative to trial without postdating the mandate; no AEDPA due-diligence condition is imported into Schlup. Ordinary Strickland prejudice, mitigation and doubts about the actual verdict do not substitute for either gateway. Neither ineffective-assistance merits nor any excluded claim is independently decided.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Felker v. Turpin:** Applied channel by channel. The successive-application authorization-review bar does not decide this distinct recall judgment.
- **Lindh v. Murphy; Carriger v. Stewart:** Applied to the identified pre-AEDPA capital-application provisions; no blanket exemption or automatic gateway remand follows.
- **Schlup v. Delo:** Extended to conviction-innocence review for this category of completed-merits recall, retaining reliable new evidence, the whole record and its probability standard.
- **Sawyer v. Whitley:** Extended separately to an eligibility-only recall claim, retaining clear and convincing proof concerning state-law death eligibility rather than mitigation or sentence selection.
- **Hazel-Atlas Glass Co. v. Hartford-Empire Co.:** Its exceptional process-protection power remains, with the actual fraud predicate distinguished.
- **United States v. Ohio Power Co.:** Its exceptional procedural reopening remains; it supplies no general entitlement to revisit refused rehearing.
- **Carlisle v. United States:** Applied to inherent authority exercised consistently with governing rules; no statute is held to abolish every recall.
- **Lonchar v. Thomas:** Required first-application process remains protected; discretionary en banc reconsideration is not made mandatory.
- **Marshall v. Lonberger:** State historical and credibility findings retain former §2254(d)’s protection only with its prerequisites and exceptions.
- **Strickland v. Washington:** Performance and reasonable-probability prejudice remain distinct from gateway innocence; no independent ineffective-assistance merits holding issues.

#### Law After Decision

Exceptional recall authority survives. For completed state-prisoner habeas merits reopened on the ground presented here, the appropriate crime-innocence or capital-eligibility gateway is now required. Genuinely different process correction retains its distinct grounds. Neither the seriousness of the sentence nor ordinary merits disagreement supplies the gateway, and the two gateway burdens do not merge. The first-application denial mandate is restored without adjudication of an independent later claim or protection.

#### Separate Positions

**Stevens, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** These Justices would affirm the circuit’s exceptional correction of its own first-application process. They acknowledge the January notice, March belated-call request and rejection, June mandate and later delay. Those facts defeat a theory of a clerical lapse first discovered on the eve of execution. But the supported clerk-transition failure, actual effort to obtain consideration and independent June recall initiative establish a concrete institutional basis beyond regret about a merits result. The panel’s refusal does not extinguish the full court’s exceptional authority. The first-record limitation and explanation for awaiting state proceedings suffice under restrained abuse-of-discretion review, without making the delay necessary or every judge blameless.

Hazel-Atlas supports judicial-process protection while its fraud predicate remains distinct; Ohio Power confirms that procedural finality does not abolish all exceptional correction. Carlisle forbids circumvention of an actual rule but supplies no rule eliminating this power. Lonchar protects proper process without requiring en banc consideration. On that ground the dissent would not impose Schlup or Sawyer as a prerequisite to recall. It makes no finding that Thompson satisfies either gateway, proves Strickland prejudice or is entitled to every merits proposition stated below.

Affirmance would preserve the direction to enter the partial rape-related writ unless California elected to retry within a reasonable time, the associated death-sentence vacation, further review of murder and the then-entered execution-order vacatur. It would not create a fixed election or trial deadline, unconditional release, a murder acquittal or an independent Supreme Court ineffective-assistance holding.

#### Source Notes

The reported lower-court chronology, first-application evidence and conditional decree are recited at 523 U.S. 538. The January/March correspondence is a published account attributed to the lower dissent; the separate June initiative is reported by the en banc majority. Neither establishes every judge’s knowledge or an earlier entered recall. The reasonable-time condition concerns election to retry and supplies no fixed deadline. Conflicting left/right descriptions of a wrist injury are immaterial to the holding and supply no factual inference.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Ninth Circuit must reinstate its June 11, 1997 mandate denying the first application. The recall-dependent conditional partial-writ and additional review directions no longer govern. No execution date or result on a distinct later application is supplied by this judgment.

**End of entry: Calderon v. Thompson, Merits decision, April 29, 1998.**
