## 2002-10-15 — Ford Motor Co. v. McCauley; No. 01-896.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents. He would vacate the consolidated-complaint dismissal and require findings on the value of lawful individual equitable relief. He treats that complaint as an independent action: reinstatement would restore the forum defendants sought for still-disputed claims, rather than merely improve the reasoning of an otherwise satisfactory judgment. Deposit Guaranty National Bank v. Roper supports examining a concrete continuing stake despite nominal success. The premise would fail if the complaint had no independent legal effect or the appeal merely sought to evade protected remands.

On valuation, he would count an injunction’s reasonable, unavoidable cost to the defendant when the individual plaintiff could lawfully obtain that relief. The class label does not change the statutory value of an individual controversy. Snyder v. Harris and Zahn v. International Paper Co. continue to forbid aggregation of separate rebate rights; Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission supports valuing the object actually in dispute. Combined costs of separate claims, hypothetical classwide relief, litigation expenses and avoidable administrative choices cannot establish the amount. The present findings do not establish either equitable entitlement or actual qualifying cost.

His remand would require dismissal if the individual controversy still failed §1332. It would leave the separate state-action remands untouched under §1447(d), and would not decide contract liability, certification or supplemental jurisdiction over other class members.

## 2002-11-04 — Early v. Packer; No. 01-1765.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees with the governing legal rules but dissents from their application. He regards the judge’s public repetition of the majority’s criticism as giving that criticism judicial force. Coupled with individualized pressure on an exhausted juror and disclosure of the 11-to-1 division, the intervention communicated a demand to yield even without naming the desired verdict. Permission to disagree could not neutralize that combined pressure.

Applying Lowenfield’s contextual rule, he finds actual coercion and separately concludes under Williams that the state assessment was objectively unreasonable, not merely less persuasive. He rejects both a constitutional citation requirement and reliance on Jenkins or Gypsum as independent state constitutional commands. His ground is the sustained exercise of judicial authority in this record, not a categorical ban on supplemental instructions, numerical knowledge or legitimate health inquiries. He would preserve a conditional writ allowing California a reasonable period to retry only the counts whose verdicts followed the coercive intervention, leaving earlier sealed verdicts and acquittals untouched.

## 2002-11-04 — Popovich v. Cuyahoga County Court of Common Pleas; judgment below, 276 F.3d 808; no Supreme Court docket supplied.

Justice Thomas, joined by Justice Scalia, dissents. They accept the constitutional right to an adequate initial custody hearing and Congress’s clear authorization of suit, but find the preventive damages regime insufficiently fitted insofar as it reaches beyond an actual constitutional denial or penalty. Their Boerne and Garrett positions distinguish enforcement of a protected opportunity to be heard from substituting a broader statutory accommodation right for that constitutional standard.

Effective participation might already be supplied by counsel or other means even if a claimant could establish some additional statutory accommodation duty. The need to investigate disability, legitimate choices among effective assistance and reasons for delay therefore matter to the constitutional predicate. Their objection is not that all preventive legislation requires an individual constitutional tort or a numerical record of adjudicated violations. It concerns the fit of this damages regime. They would confine the remand to a properly preserved statutory application enforcing an actual denial of meaningful hearing participation or an unconstitutional penalty for requesting it, and would not sustain the existing general verdict.

## 2002-11-04 — Woodford v. Visciotti; No. 02-137.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the governing rules but dissents from the result. He agrees that occasional use of probable does not independently establish a contrary-to violation. His objection concerns what the state court actually did with the mitigating account. The missing coherent history of abuse, instability and functional impairment, combined with counsel’s damaging presentation, could affect a conscientious juror’s sentencing judgment even though it did not excuse the crimes. Scattered guilt-phase brain-injury evidence did not necessarily perform that explanatory work.

He gives the killing, attempted killing and prior violence their full aggravating force, but concludes that the combined impairment of the mitigation presentation undermines confidence in the sentence. In his view, treating the grave offenses as a sufficient answer to that impairment materially understates lawful mitigation and makes the state application objectively unreasonable under Williams as well as prejudicial under Strickland. The deliberations and questions reinforce the materiality of the missing account without proving any juror’s hypothetical choice.

He would preserve conditional sentence relief, allowing a reasonable period for constitutionally adequate resentencing or a lawful noncapital disposition. Any properly preserved unresolved defense that counsel performed adequately must be decided before implementing a writ dependent on deficiency. The convictions remain intact. He adopts no categorical prejudice rule for abuse, brain injury, lengthy deliberation or capital cases.

## 2002-11-05 — Syngenta Crop Protection, Inc. v. Henson; No. 01-757.

Justice Stevens concurs and joins the entire Court opinion. He would go further in treating United States v. New York Telephone Co. Rather than merely distinguish its expansive construction of the All Writs Act, he would overrule that construction. His own dissent in that case treated the Act as a procedural means of exercising jurisdiction already granted, using means consistent with established legal usages, rather than a general power to help the Government or litigants achieve their objectives. The effort to derive removal authority from that decision illustrates, in his view, why its overbroad construction should be repudiated. No other Justice joins this proposal, and it does not change his full agreement with the removal rule or the judgment.

## 2002-12-10 — Abdur'Rahman v. Bell; No. 01-9094; October Term 2002.

The four-Justice Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, locates corrective authority in the original appeals that were before the Sixth Circuit when the motion and remand requests were presented, together with its receipt of the transferred proceeding. Sections 1291 and 2253 supplied the original appellate channel; §2106 permits appropriate remand of a case lawfully before the appellate court, and §1631 does not require a transferee to retain a request outside the transferred category. The district court's pending-appeal discussion recognized an indicative-ruling and remand route. The circuit therefore could allow ordinary consideration of a separable Rule 60 request without an appeal from every interlocutory transfer. Its refusal of that route rested on the same categorical classification. The plurality would correct that refusal and the improper routing, while preserving the completed merits judgment and actual authorization denial. Because that original channel supplies the requested procedural return, its reasoning does not require reversal of the separate transfer-appeal dismissal or certificate rulings, which it leaves undisturbed without adopting nonfinality. Hohn supplies this Court's review of the judicial acts; it does not itself supply the circuit's original appellate authority.

Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I, III and IV and the default-component remand, and dissents from leaving the distinct transfer appeal and certificate rulings undisturbed. The district court conclusively refused Rule 60 jurisdiction over the asserted procedural request, and that final disposition was timely challenged. He would recognize the Sixth Circuit's authority to review that refusal as such. A separate actual authorization denial cannot insulate the judicial classification and certificate orders. He applies the procedural rule only to the ten defaulted-claim requests and the express merits exclusion to the four merits-treated claims and cumulative merits prayer. Timing, Rule 39 and failure to appeal remain matters for ordinary Rule 60 adjudication. He would also vacate the transfer-appeal dismissal and certificate refusals. His final-order route supplies no fifth vote for the plurality's original-appellate route, and the availability of that route supplies no basis to abandon his finality position.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurs in the judicial-review and authorization-bar holdings and the renewed-merits exclusion, but dissents from the default-component vacatur. Review of the circuit's jurisdictional dismissal does not make the underlying transfer final or appealable. The transferee could examine its authority, and the original appeal had completed the constitutional adjudication without a challenge to the default ruling. Felker's channel limits and Calderon's finality requirements therefore support affirming dismissal and leaving the refusal of original-appellate relief undisturbed. O'Connor and Kennedy adopt no categorical prohibition on every genuine Rule 60 process motion; the possibility of extraordinary circumstances does not establish entitlement here.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, adds that this request reopens finally disposed claims and expressly seeks renewed merits and cumulative adjudication. A later legal objection to default is different from a clerical mistake or independently established fraud on the federal court. Stewart's reserved premature claim and Slack's without-prejudice exhaustion return do not justify treating this final-claim attack as an untouched first application. He would retain the successive channel as an additional ground, without purporting to classify every conceivable Rule 60 correction.

## 2002-12-10 — Planned Parenthood of the Columbia/Willamette, Inc. v. American Coalition of Life Activists; judgment below, 290 F.3d 1058.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the contextual-threat rule and its application, the named-plaintiff specific-intent injunction, the attribution and notice limits, and the removal of the possession restriction. He supports damages vacatur and remand, but would require subjective recklessness rather than purpose or knowledge: awareness of a substantial risk that the communication will be understood as a serious threat of unlawful violence, followed by conscious disregard. Objective foreseeability is insufficient because it exposes speakers to an audience reaction they did not consciously risk; an intent to carry out violence is unnecessary. In his view this minimum preserves political advocacy while reaching deliberately risked coded intimidation. He would require review of the actual jury instructions and findings under that floor and a new trial on affected liability and damages if the error was not harmless. The separate specific-intent findings independently exceed his floor for the confined prospective commands and cannot repair the jury verdict. The punitive award remains subject to the existing reconsideration.

He would confine the injunction's protected-person coverage to the named plaintiffs, with actual notice, attributable conduct and the specified threatening intent, and would remove coverage extending beyond them. He does not join the Court's related-person extension. His vote to vacate paragraph 4's derivative asset restraint supplies no additional rationale. Independent RICO questions remain outside review. His different constitutional threshold and protected-person limit do not become the Court's rules.

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree that an implicit public communication can be a threat and join the attribution, notice, remedial-connection and private-possession limits. They would reverse the reviewed threat liability outright and dissolve its speech injunction because independent constitutional review does not establish the speakers' own threatened violence. Claiborne protects forceful political condemnation and social pressure despite fear of unrelated violence. The lawful-accountability language, the absence of violence following many posters, the unestablished abortion connection of the Patterson killing and the lack of proof attributing prior murders to these defendants undermine the inference the Court draws. Their position does not require literal threats, private delivery, ability to attack or a confession; it requires a sufficient connection between the message and threatened violence attributable to its speaker. The judge's subjective findings cannot create missing threatening content. They also support dissolution of the dependent asset restraint and reject using security to preserve the liability they would reverse.

Kennedy separately joins the Court's purpose-or-knowledge constitutional rule while rejecting its application. Scalia and Thomas accept FACE's enacted intentional-act requirements but do not join the new constitutional formula, because their antecedent no-threat ground resolves the case. Their shared judgment therefore does not imply identical culpability positions.

## 2003-01-14 — Craigmiles v. Giles; 312 F.3d 220; no Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens would affirm the existing-business injunction under ordinary rational-basis review. His approach in Beach and Nordlinger asks whether an impartial legislature could pursue the asserted purpose and whether that purpose rationally explains the actual inequality. He accepts that the curriculum teaches some merchandising; he rejects the conclusion that this limited content explains exclusion from the entire retail activity until the seller completes the substantially unrelated professional qualification. The trial findings concerning the stores' limited work, casket quality and the entry burden supply the basis for that conclusion. A private benefit cannot justify itself merely because the statute creates it. His objection does not demand the least restrictive fraud law or an optimally efficient course: it concerns the relationship of the whole prerequisite to the actual trade. Bare preservation of incumbent rents supplies no independent legitimate justification in his view.

**Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** The Chief Justice would affirm on both due-process and equal-protection grounds. Beach and Lee Optical permit imperfect economic lines, but Moreno and Cleburne preserve a legitimate-public-end requirement. He would extend that limit to bare protection of incumbent sellers from harmless competition. Incidental incumbent benefits, consumer protection, redistribution, aid to a distressed industry and Nordlinger's protection of established commitments remain distinguishable legitimate objectives.

He accepts the merchandising instruction, the 37 examination questions and the possible value of professional discipline. On this combined record, however, the stores perform none of the defining services that principally occupy the education and apprenticeship; the burial choices and quality findings also undermine the proposed casket-safety explanation. Discipline's usefulness does not itself connect those unrelated entry prerequisites to the admitted retail activity. Challengers retain the burden of negating reasonably conceivable legitimate explanations; no empirical proof, least restrictive system or specially optimized curriculum is constitutionally required.

He would retain relief against this separable qualification and presume equivalent protection for materially equivalent merchandise-only activity unless the State establishes that a narrower decree fully cures the proved violation. The trial findings and remedial jurisdiction must define the covered activity. Bodily care, funeral arrangements, advance-need transactions and lawful sales regulation remain outside that invalidation. The State could not obtain narrower relief by merely promising discretionary nonenforcement against the named stores. His rule would govern still-open direct-review cases under Harper, subject to Reynoldsville's independent procedural, immunity and remedial limits; final judgments remain closed and collateral relief requires its own authorization.

## 2003-01-14 — Johnson v. City of Cincinnati; 310 F.3d 484; no Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The Chief Justice joins the ordinary-public-passage and parent-authorized-caregiving rules, fee affirmance and component-specific remand. Movement supplies Au France's complete constitutional ground, so he would not decide the additional attorney-intimacy theory. That position neither adopts the lower court's theory nor supplies a vote for its rejection.

If a live claim supports relief against the proved categorical exclusion policy, he would presume relief against that policy for the persons it governs unless the City establishes a narrower complete cure. This proposal does not make the fee dispute a source of prospective jurisdiction. The court must first assess Burnett, actual legal enforceability, remaining exposure and the status of damages. Laidlaw's demanding cessation burden remains distinct from entitlement to an injunction. If an injunction is warranted, he would require withdrawal of operative exclusion notices and enforcement directions, with compliance verifiable through those records and later contrary enforcement subject to enforcement proceedings. If the prospective component is moot, its disposition must preserve live fee or damages controversies. No present finding establishes the conditional prospective predicate or enters those remedial commands.

Under Harper, he would apply both constitutional rules to the parties and every case still open on direct review. Reynoldsville preserves independently valid immunity, procedural and remedial limits; reliance alone cannot justify selective prospectivity. Final judgments remain closed, and collateral relief requires its own governing authorization.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** These Justices agree that the fee appeal is live, that professional confidentiality alone does not establish intimate association, and that prospective relief and requested damages need separate treatment. They reject the new fundamental-movement and caregiving extensions and would also vacate the appellate affirmance of the fee award.

Kent and Papachristou do not establish the claimed substantive right against geographically bounded drug-control restrictions. Saenz concerns interstate citizenship, Morales decided enforcement guidance, and Glucksberg requires an established protected interest before heightened review. Under Beach's ordinary rational review, the City may connect exclusion from drug-concentrated zones to reducing recurring crime. The durations, offense predicates, administrative review and resident, employment and service variances supply genuine limits; the City need not prove that every future visit would be criminal. They impose no §1983 exhaustion requirement and do not treat conviction as forfeiting every constitutional protection.

Troxel's parental protection likewise does not automatically transfer an independent entitlement to a grandmother against this conduct-based restriction. Moore and Roberts do not make every difficulty maintaining a relationship unconstitutional. They accept the actual disruption but reject the proposed extension, while Thomas preserves strict scrutiny for an established infringement of a parent's own fundamental right. Because Au France's district-court double-jeopardy ground remains outside the grant, their disposition would require consideration of the remaining lawful fee basis and any necessary allocation, rather than unconditional elimination of all fees or complete judgment for the City.

## 2003-01-14 — Sattazahn v. Pennsylvania; No. 01-7574.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the double-jeopardy holding and would independently reach the same sentence-only result under due process. Pennsylvania made life the conclusive consequence of its completed unsuccessful capital opportunity. Requiring surrender of that final limit to correct guilt-phase error burdens meaningful appellate review and upsets the adjudicated liberty interest without a new lawful basis. Guilt retrial and life punishment remain available; the appeal alone cannot recreate capital exposure the completed process ended. Hicks supplies protection for an actual sentencing entitlement, while this application develops its operation in the distinct final-life setting. The position does not presume prosecutorial vindictiveness from a harsher sentence, preserve every favorable sentencing term after reversal, or convert the jury's division into an acquittal. The other four majority Justices rest on the sufficient double-jeopardy ground and do not join this independent theory.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** They would affirm rejection of both federal challenges. Bullington and Rumsey protect an adjudication that death is unavailable on the merits. Here neither the jury nor the judge found the State's aggravation proof insufficient or selected life on the merits. The statutory consequence of deadlock therefore supplies no federal acquittal finality once the defendant obtains reversal of the underlying conviction for trial error. Poland preserves that distinction, and Richardson permits retrial of unresolved allegations. The State must still obtain the lawful findings and comply with the procedures governing the new capital proceeding. Hicks protects the entitlement state law actually confers, but the supplied law establishes no life ceiling independently surviving reversal of the conviction. Their due-process rejection concerns that asserted entitlement, not every possible state-created sentencing interest. O'Connor and Kennedy do not adopt the separate element-based explanation below.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, additionally dissenting.** Even if a necessary present-offense aggravator performs the constitutional work of an element, a hung jury has not rejected it. Their Monge position concerns a completed failure of proof in an element adjudication; Apprendi, Angle, Harris and Ring supply present-offense factfinding protections without treating deadlock as acquittal. They therefore agree with the common dissent and explain why its result survives this limited element analysis. They adopt no universal prior-conviction exception, proposition that every aggravator is a separate offense, or rule that eligibility requires selection of death. O'Connor and Kennedy join no part of this additional writing.

## 2003-01-15 — Barnhart v. Peabody Coal Co.; Nos. 01-705 and 01-715.

**Scalia, joined by O’Connor and Thomas, dissenting.** The dissent would affirm because §9706(a) grants initial-assignment authority in the same time-bounded mandate that requires its exercise. The fixed as-of allocation and express correction and reassignment provisions reinforce closure on October 1, 1993. An initial assignment of a previously unassigned beneficiary differs from a correction authorized under another provision, and the unassigned-beneficiary funding system supplies the statutory destination for those costs. Brock permits statutory context to establish expiration; Good involved separate continuing forfeiture authority and a distinct five-year limitations period. The dissent therefore does not rest on a universal rule that every mandatory agency deadline disables late action. It rejects the Court’s reading of this scheme and would preserve the timing-based judgments. Revenue or fairness cannot enlarge a source of authority Congress bounded by time.

## 2003-01-15 — Eldred v. Ashcroft; No. 01-618.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Part II-A’s rejection of categorical First Amendment immunity but would invalidate the challenged existing-term increment under the Copyright Clause. An extension must genuinely and rationally serve progress; challengers must show that asserted purposes do not connect the additional exclusion to that function. He does not demand empirical proof of a new work-for-work bargain, optimal policy, or immutable original endpoints. Here completed creation cannot be induced; longer lives do not justify added postdeath protection for those works; and generalized harmonization and preservation claims do not explain the undifferentiated increment, which imposes no new dissemination or preservation obligation. Feist supplies a real constitutional boundary; Sony and Graham distinguish the exclusive right from the public end it serves. A supported systemic transition or conditioned preservation program could differ. Delayed verbatim dissemination is a real speech burden, but his Copyright Clause ground supplies complete relief without a universal independent speech tier. He preserves Campbell’s actual fair-use analysis.

He would reverse, declare the added twenty years invalid within the challenged application, and remand for severability and prospective relief against federal enforcement. Relief would presumptively cover the proved unconstitutional existing-term extension unless the defendant establishes that a narrower cure fully remedies it, within severability and the court’s authority over that defendant. The decree would use preextension expiration dates: lawful earlier terms survive, and works with unexpired earlier terms do not immediately enter the public domain. He would leave the new-work term intact and award no damages or personal judgments against absent owners. Under Harper the rule would govern the parties and cases still on direct review; Reynoldsville preserves independent immunity, procedural, and remedial limits, while reliance alone would not justify selective prospectivity. Final judgments would remain closed, and collateral relief would require its own authorization. Private infringement disputes would apply the constitutional rule through their own parties, defenses, and procedures.

**Stevens, dissenting.** He would reverse as to existing copyrights because completed creation cannot be induced by prolonging an existing federal bargain at the public’s expense. General parity, dissemination, and preservation rationales do not justify a blanket further restriction on the access originally promised. Initial establishment of the federal copyright system differs from enlarging an existing federal grant, and repeated legislation cannot itself establish constitutional power. Feist’s real constitutional boundary and Campbell’s and Quality King’s operative access limits support this objection. He would remand for relief confined to the existing-term application, without independently deciding First Amendment invalidity, a permissible new-work term, public-domain restoration, or remedies for older extensions. His existing-bargain ground is distinct from Stone-Zsela’s acceptance of genuinely justified later adjustments and Breyer’s assessment of this increment’s burdens and benefits.

**Breyer, dissenting.** He would reverse the challenged existing-term application under the Copyright Clause read in light of expressive interests. Meaningful legislative leeway remains, but a substantial additional restraint on use of already-created material must have an adequate copyright-related public justification. The reasons offered do not sufficiently connect this increment of exclusion to progress. Corley’s attention to functional benefit and actual expressive burden and Quality King’s preservation of operative limits support that assessment. His ground does not require a measured new-creation incentive for every work and does not impose a universal independent First Amendment tier. He would remand for relief confined to the existing-term challenge. This ground adopts no new economic calculation, aggregate royalty estimate, orphan-work count, or empirical preservation finding.

## 2003-01-15 — Newman v. Sathyavaglswaran. Judgment below: 287 F.3d 786; Ninth Circuit No. 00-55504.

Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joins Parts I and II and Part III's integrated Mathews inquiry, ordinary practicable-opportunity principle with its actual-urgency or impracticability qualification, and factual, responsibility, defense and remedy remit. He would hold that an established procedure irreversibly defeating a retained objection ordinarily requires reasonable efforts at advance notice and a practicable opportunity to object. The parents retain the burden of proving deprivation without adequate process; the State must substantiate an asserted urgency, unavailable contact information or other necessity for bypassing that ordinary safeguard with actual circumstances. Mathews gives substantial value to advance protection of an irreversible choice, while allowing medical timing to constrain its form. Logan and Zinermon distinguish this foreseeable procedure from the random acts in Parratt and Hudson; Patsy separately rejects exhaustion. In his view the missing operational facts determine necessity and liability, and do not prevent stating the ordinary safeguard now. He requires neither a trial-type hearing nor an absolute veto of necessary autopsy, and honors valid donor directions and authorized consent. The lower court should develop contact opportunities, retrieval windows, donor directions, authorization and the decision procedure, then determine the practicable protection withheld and causal responsibility. The parents must prove their entitlement, deprivation, defendant responsibility and compensable injury by a preponderance; applicable immunity and other preserved defenses remain separate. The two retrospective removals establish no entitlement to a prospective decree against the later consent regime. Under Harper his rule would govern the parties and cases still open on direct review; Reynoldsville Casket preserves independent procedural, immunity and remedial limits without selective prospectivity based on reliance alone. Final judgments remain closed, and collateral relief requires its own authorization. The safeguard itself determines neither 1997 immunity nor institutional attribution or damages. His particular ordinary-notice presumption and necessity substantiation are not the Court's additional holding.

## 2003-01-21 — United States v. Jimenez Recio; No. 01-1184.

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He agrees that §846 does not make a conspiracy end automatically upon secret government seizure. He would nevertheless preserve the defendants’ appellate victory. Rule 30 required a timely instructional objection before jury retirement, but the Government made none, affirmatively accepted pre-seizure joinder as its closing burden, and first attacked the circuit rule on rehearing after not challenging it in its initial appellate presentation. ## 2003-01-27 — FCC v. NextWave Personal Communications, Inc.; Nos. 01-653 and 01-657, consolidated; October Term 2002.

## 2003-02-24 — Planned Parenthood of the Rocky Mountains Services, Corp. v. Owens; review of 287 F.3d 910; Tenth Circuit No. 00-1385

## 2003-02-25 — California First Amendment Coalition v. Woodford; judgment below, 299 F.3d 868; October Term 2002.

Stone-Zsela joins the opinion and would additionally apply ordinary qualified-closure safeguards. The juvenile-predicate rule instead concerns the procedure authorizing punishment: it applies to nonfinal direct-review cases under Griffith, while final collateral claims retain Teague's procedural-rule limits and the governing habeas statutes. His account does not itself reopen absent persons' judgments.

He makes no independent proportionality determination. He would first require the state court to resolve the disputed appellate-preservation question under the actual appellate rules and their application to this case. The established trial objection cannot alone settle that distinct question. He withholds assent to the additional reviewability conclusion and proportionality ground while joining the complete relief afforded by the Court's other holdings.

**Stevens, concurring, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** They would also hold the 206-month term grossly disproportionate. Ewing requires assessment of the present offense, serious history and actual term together. Both prior juvenile aggravated-sodomy adjudications receive substantial weight, but cannot replace the current voluntary close-in-age act. The thirteen-to-fifteen-month treatment of the same act and history with opposite-sex participants is strong comparative evidence, not an automatic sentence-ratio rule. Solem preserves an enforceable limit despite recidivism, and Henderson requires attention to the actual offense, offender and release conditions. These Justices find the extraordinary term excessive even with the serious history included; they propose sentence-only relief and a lawful replacement, without selecting a numerical term.

They also conclude that the trial ruling permits review of this claim. The written motion and sentencing argument raised the Eighth Amendment. Under K.S.A. §21-4721(c)(1), as construed in Clemons, a presumptive-sentence challenge could not receive state appellate review. Largent, Costarelli and Nash therefore identify the trial court as the highest court in which this particular decision could be had under §1257. The appellate statement that the issue was not raised does not erase that independently available route.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Kennedy and, as to the juvenile discussion, Breyer.** They join the equality rule and its sentence vacatur but reject the separate juvenile-factfinder ground. McKeiver validates the juvenile adjudicative process within its setting, and Monge recognizes the distinct recidivist context. Apprendi reserved that context. They would treat the valid adjudication as an established offender-status fact usable in a structured adult sentencing system, rather than require its renewed allocation as an element. They rely on that affirmative ground, not a supposed existing constitutional exception.

O'Connor and Kennedy also reject the proposed proportionality application. Their Ewing position gives exceptional deference to legislative judgments about incapacitation after repeated serious offending. The present minor-conduct setting and two serious juvenile priors distinguish the nonviolent property offense in Ewing. They would cure the sex-based disparity under equality while rejecting the additional inference of gross disproportionality. Breyer does not join that portion.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They join the Court's nonjury-predicate rule and actual-admission inquiry. They would nevertheless uphold the sex condition on reduced punishment. Under their Romer and Bowers reasoning, the legislature may encourage opposite-sex adolescent development while discouraging the specified same-sex conduct through a conduct-based moral judgment, especially in laws protecting minors. They regard the distinction as rational despite its imperfect fit. They also reject the asserted constitutional right to proportionality in prison duration on their Harmelin and Ewing grounds, rather than adopt the other dissent's proportionality balancing.

**Thomas, concurring.** He joins the narrower nonjury-predicate rule and retains the broader position expressed in his Apprendi concurrence: a fact legally necessary to aggravate punishment is an element even when it concerns a prior adjudication. Monge's double-jeopardy classification does not settle that jury-and-proof question. The required fact is the aggravating predicate the adult law uses, not every underlying fact of a valid juvenile judgment.

## 2003-06-26 — Nike, Inc. v. Kasky; No. 02-575; 539 U.S. 654.

**Souter, concurring in the termination of review; Stevens and Ginsburg join the alternative prudential ground.** Assuming jurisdiction, they would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. The present pleading posture combines difficult questions about public debate, deceptive commercial factual representations, fault and private enforcement. Communication-specific development can clarify the relationship between those questions, and the objection that the private-enforcement theory was not pressed or passed upon below adds reason to await a more developed adjudication. They take the asserted expressive burden seriously but find that those considerations justify ending this writ. Souter leaves statutory finality and Article III undecided; Stevens and Ginsburg also join the Court's separate finality holding. This prudential position is not a rule that every speech case must await trial.

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by O'Connor.** They would retain review. Cox's fourth category asks whether reversal on the federal issue would preclude the relevant action and whether postponement would seriously erode federal policy. A complete First Amendment reversal could end this action, and the possibility of narrower correction does not negate that route. The state court has fixed the challenged legal classification while the action burdens participation in a current public controversy. Later correction cannot fully restore speech withheld during that debate. Jefferson and Pierce County preserve the practical exception rather than foreclose it whenever liability proceedings remain.

They also distinguish Nike's claimed present appellate injury from whether Kasky could have initiated an action in federal court. ASARCO permits attention to the defendant's own injury from the state adjudication. In their view, neither the state plaintiff's standing nor the prudential concerns justify ending review. The mixed-expression problem requires room for public debate while retaining lawful regulation of actual commercial deception. Their dissent supports immediate review of those questions; it does not adjudicate the truth, protection or liability consequences of every communication.

**Kennedy, dissenting.** He would retain the case and dissents from terminating review. He does not join Breyer's opinion.

## 2003-06-26 — Old Person v. Brown; Ninth Circuit No. 02-35171; October Term 2002.

Thomas, joined by Scalia, concurs in the judgment on the statutory construction maintained in Holder, De Grandy, Page, and Balderas. He concludes that §2 does not authorize the asserted district-allocation claim and therefore does not join the Court's evaluation of Gingles, proportionality, or totality. ## 2003-06-26 — Stogner v. California; No. 01-1757; 539 U.S. 607.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the completed-bar rule, its Calder basis and dismissal of both counts. He would also treat the rule as substantive for otherwise proper collateral review because it withdraws authority to restore criminal exposure, rather than merely changing trial procedure. Griffith governs covered nonfinal direct-review cases; Teague's substantive distinction, in his view, permits invocation in an otherwise proper collateral proceeding. That position does not itself reopen final convictions or dispense with jurisdiction, default, limitations or independent statutory habeas requirements. Carmell's evidentiary rule and Rogers's judicial fair-warning inquiry support attention to legal operation without already deciding this revival question. Sattazahn's defined capital-finality holding remains distinct.

**Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They would reject both granted categorical challenges. On their reading, Calder's categories compare substantive criminality, the offense's degree, prescribed punishment and necessary guilt proof with the law governing the act. Section 803(g) changes the period for enforcing the same criminal prohibition, without changing those features. An expired limit supplies important statutory immunity, but does not become an acquittal or a substantive element merely because prosecution formerly was barred. They therefore regard revival as outside the Clause's received categories. Carmell's alteration of legally necessary proof and Rogers's removal of an acknowledged offense boundary do not require the same result here.

They also reject the claim that expiration alone creates a constitutionally irrevocable due-process immunity. Brown describes the effect of the then-operative statute, not an absolute constitutional right to repose. The original criminality supplies warning, while the reporting and independent-corroboration safeguards bear on fairness. Their proposed affirmance would permit proceedings only under the statute's actual requirements and would preserve the lower court's fact-specific inquiry into preaccusation delay. It would not treat allegations of qualifying conduct or corroboration as established, or decide a claim of actual defense prejudice on facts not determined in this record.

## 2003-06-26 — Wiggins v. Smith, No. 02-311; 539 U.S. 510.

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, dissenting.** They would affirm denial of relief because Maryland's assessment remained within the range permitted by §2254(d)(1). Strickland allows reasonable limits on investigation, and the state court could reasonably view counsel's existing records and their principalship strategy as an adequately informed choice in the actual context, even if additional investigation would have been better. The statutory inquiry is not a fresh selection of the preferred professional judgment. Their disagreement concerns whether Wiggins has established objective unreasonableness; they do not endorse an automatic defense whenever counsel invokes strategy, demand a factually identical precedent, treat Williams as the source of a new earlier duty, or presume that a complete investigation occurred. Because this statutory ground is sufficient for denial, they do not independently decide actual deficiency or prejudice. Their votes therefore supply neither a no-deficiency nor a no-prejudice holding.

## 2003-06-30 — Bragg v. West Virginia Coal Association; 248 F.3d 275; historical petition No. 01-619.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court’s retained-duty construction, connected-officer route and rejection of participation-based waiver. He would add that, after a common unlawful practice is proved, the remedy must cure it throughout its demonstrated reach unless the violator shows that a narrower lawful order fully cures the wrong. This proposed presumption operates only within statutory authority, standing, the proved federal breach and ordinary equitable entitlement. Survival of immunity supplies none of those findings. He agrees that the general valley-fill injunction cannot simply be reissued, and that the lower courts must use supported, undisturbed findings and resolve genuinely remaining issues before identifying correction, schedule and verification through permit records and required findings. His broader cure presumption would apply in nonfinal equitable proceedings, without automatically reopening final decrees, and has no other join.

## 2003-06-30 — Burdine v. Johnson (Cockrell v. Burdine); Fifth Circuit No. 99-21034; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court in full and would require the habeas court to identify the existing order, effective mandate, stays, and compliance dates and require the State to demonstrate actual compliance through trial and custody records. An unperformed promise would not suffice. If the enforceable condition is unmet, the court must order the relief required against custody under the invalid judgment, subject to a separately lawful custody basis. His additional verification direction protects the existing conditional remedy from being replaced by a new 120-day period or left unenforced without proof of compliance; it does not determine the separate enforcement controversy.

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justices Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Parts I–II: the former-law framework, acceptance of the actual-sleep finding, independent constitutional classification, and Cronic's distinct absence category. They would reverse the presumption-based writ under Teague because the accepted intermittent record does not sufficiently identify the missing critical segment or comparably defined function to make this application compelled by 1987 law. Hamilton, Herring, and Geders involved identifiable missing assistance functions. To these Justices, the majority's functional analogy supports a plausible extension but does not establish a dictated application on intervals whose extent and boundaries remain incompletely defined. Their objection does not require an identical prior fact pattern or treat any awake advocacy as curing proved absence.

Under Caspari, they would examine and reject both contemporary exceptions. The proposed presumption concerns the method of establishing trial unreliability rather than conduct, persons, or punishment beyond the State's substantive power. Nor does this new boundary for dispensing with prejudice proof itself establish a watershed procedure essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness; Gideon's fundamental guarantee and O'Dell's confined watershed holding do not make every extension watershed. They would set aside this presumption-based relief and return any properly pending individual Strickland sleeping-counsel theory and other unadjudicated claims for their own lawful adjudication, leaving their constitutional elements to that process.

## 2003-06-30 — Eichorn v. AT&T Corp.; 248 F.3d 131; Third Circuit No. 99-5791.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I, III and IV. He would return the independent-party antitrust claims for a supported ancillarity and competitive-effects inquiry. Copperweld answers genuine internal coordination, but independent employers’ naked labor allocation remains subject to per se treatment. A restraint must be subordinate and reasonably necessary to a legitimate transaction before it receives the rule of reason. Defendants invoking ancillarity must substantiate that relationship and necessity; plaintiffs must establish agreement, cognizable injury and the applicable competitive harm, and may meet a supported justification with lack of fit or materially less restrictive alternatives. California Dental and NYNEX require the actual competitive inquiry, State Oil preserves the distinction among restraint categories, and Microsoft rejects justification by an integration label. The eight-month restriction’s relation to a six-month bridging deadline and its covered hiring opportunities require more than selection of the continuity inference on summary judgment. He would affirm dismissal if the complete record conclusively established genuine ancillarity and no cognizable anticompetitive effect under that inquiry; he does not treat those predicates as established here.

He agrees that the combined pension evidence permits a §510 trial inference without establishing liability. For requested plan restoration he would require identification of the §502 route, defendant authority over plan status, the protected right and the causal correction. Varity permits genuine individual restoration within operative plan terms, while a separate adequate ERISA remedy ordinarily makes additional §502(a)(3) equity inappropriate. Great-West distinguishes a personal debt from a lien or constructive trust in specifically identifiable property held through the required equitable connection; other equitable remedies retain their own predicates. His antitrust disagreement and additional remedial explanation do not change the unanimous ERISA return.

## 2003-06-30 — Holland ex rel. Overdorff v. Harrington; 268 F.3d 1179; Tenth Circuit No. 99-1373.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief Justice joins the opinion and judgment applying existing law but proposes a different personal defense after a proved constitutional violation. He would require an official claiming legal reliance to prove, by a preponderance of the evidence, identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct, genuine reliance, and an objectively reasonable basis for treating that authority as valid and applicable. Courts would independently decide its legal content. Silence, customary tactics, a warrant's general authorization and a supervisor's assurance would not suffice.

He reasons that Graham already accounts for urgency, available information and reasonable factual mistakes in determining whether force is unlawful. After unreasonable force is proved, uncertainty about a general prohibition should not itself defeat accountability. He would replace Harlow's and Anderson's contrary defense, overrule Denius's holding that actual reliance is unnecessary, and displace Wilson and Hope to that extent while preserving their substantive protections and individual-responsibility holdings. Saucier did not previously adopt his replacement. Defendants would receive a fair opportunity to establish any qualifying reliance. Under Harper the new defense would apply in this and other nonfinal civil cases; Reynoldsville would preserve independently valid defenses, remedial requirements and finality rather than selective nonapplication based on generalized reliance. His proposal concerns personal damages defenses after an established violation and supplies no new cause of action or institutional liability.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They join Parts I and III but would affirm deployment immunity and the dependent dismissals. On the residents' assumed facts, April 1996 law did not make the initial choice of armed control at this multi-occupant property sufficiently clearly unlawful. Graham, Garner and Summers supplied contextual limits; McDonald and Baker addressed direct threats and continued restraint after the need for control, not the distinct advance tactical decision. The known children, low offense severity and time to plan make the constitutional objection substantial, but do not remove that legal uncertainty. Further remand is therefore unnecessary on their independent notice ground. They accept the planning-review rule and reject reliance on disputed intelligence; they also agree that continued direct threats after control cannot receive the same immunity and that Davis's separate claim proceeds.

## 2003-06-30 — United States v. Gatewood; 230 F.3d 186; Sixth Circuit No. 98-5138.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court’s whole opinion and would apply its new element protection through explicit safeguards against prejudicial use of criminal status. A specific informed and legally adequate admission must establish every fact for which it substitutes; an admission of one status does not establish disputed chronology, conduct or identity and does not cure an omitted indictment allegation. Under Old Chief’s Rule 403 comparison, an actually available unconditional admission proving the complete status element must be weighed against the prejudicial name or nature of the conviction, while a partial admission or a genuine separate evidentiary purpose requires its own analysis. A separately authorized enhancement phase is required if a combined proceeding would create an intolerable risk to determination of present guilt. He preserves Monge and the applicable charging, sufficiency and double-jeopardy rules for any independently authorized further proceeding, without borrowing his separate due-process theory in Sattazahn as controlling law. No other Justice joins these additional implementation directions.

**O’Connor, joined by Kennedy and Breyer, dissenting.** They would affirm both sentencing rulings. Their Limon position treats valid adjudicated offender history as usable in structured adult sentencing, even in the juvenile setting; they maintain that distinct ground for adult convictions here. Limon’s controlling juvenile-maximum rule leaves this adult question open. A valid robbery conviction, already obtained through criminal adjudication, supplies a recidivist baseline that Congress may use at sentencing. Section 3559(c)(3)(A) then gives the defendant a genuine exception requiring proof of the complete absence of weapon use or threatened use and of death or serious bodily injury. Patterson preserves the distinction between an element and an affirmative defense; the prior adjudication and the statute’s structure place this exception on the latter side. Parke supports respect for final judgments but is not itself authority for the ultimate clear-and-convincing quantum. Cooper’s competence-to-stand-trial protection does not determine the burden for this recidivist exception. The dissent’s recidivist distinction covers the mandatory floor on the kidnapping counts as well as the robbery maximum. Unavailable old witnesses and denial of participation neither establish the enacted exclusion nor reopen final guilt. Their ground does not require an invented conclusion that the old Arkansas judgments necessarily adjudicated every weapon or injury circumstance.
