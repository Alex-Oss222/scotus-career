**Case Name:** Reno v. Flores  
**Citation or Docket:** 507 U.S. 292; No. 91-905.  
**October Term:** 1992.  
**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, pre-decision preparation; certiorari. The listed historical merits proceeding is retained as the reboot's working procedural premise, not as a newly rendered simulated grant.  
**Simulated Event Date:** 1993-03-23, the historical decision date used as the simulation calendar date.  
**Question Presented / Granted Scope:** Whether the 1988 INS juvenile custody regulation is facially invalid under substantive or procedural due process or exceeds the Attorney General’s statutory authority because it ordinarily permits release only to parents, guardians, or specified adult relatives.  
**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit en banc, 942 F.2d 1352 (1991), affirming an injunction requiring release to other responsible adults and automatic immigration-judge hearings.  
**Relief Requested:** The Government seeks reversal of the classwide injunction; the juvenile class seeks its continuation.

**Material Record:** The class consists of minors arrested on suspicion of deportability. The 1988 regulation permits release in an order of preference to parents, legal guardians, and specified adult relatives, with an unusual-and-compelling-circumstances avenue for other custodians. Initial custody and deportability determinations may be reviewed by an immigration judge on request. The notice form states that review may be requested. A November 1987 agreement separately governs conditions, requiring prompt placement in licensed or equivalent juvenile facilities. This case is not a factual adjudication that every detention is brief, every notice understood, or every institution safe. The facial challenge proceeds against the regulation and the operative conditions agreement, not against an invented record of uniformly punitive confinement.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The facial posture under United States v. Salerno is material. Identify the actual liberty interest in institutional confinement without assuming that minors have no liberty because they must be in someone’s custody. Equally, an interest in freedom from unnecessary confinement does not automatically establish a right to placement with any proposed unrelated adult. Individual inability to request review, excessive delay, abuse, and unlawful extended detention remain distinct questions. Statutory authority should be addressed before unnecessary constitutional expansion.

**Relevant Dates:** Conditions agreement: November 30, 1987. Regulation published: May 17, 1988. Argument: October 13, 1992. No later settlement or later detention statute enters this packet.

##### Issue 1: Statutory authority and custody purpose

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Then-operative 8 U.S.C. § 1252(a)(1), 8 C.F.R. §§ 242.24 and 242.2; Carlson v. Landon, 342 U.S. 524 (1952), and the received administrative-law framework, including Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. NRDC, 467 U.S. 837 (1984). Statutory discretion remains bounded by the immigration purpose and constitutional limits.

**Open Point:** Whether generic custodian categories reasonably implement the detention delegation and whether the regulation requires an unlawful custody purpose.

##### Issue 2: Liberty, process, and facial relief

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** The Fifth Amendment; Salerno, 481 U.S. 739; Schall v. Martin, 467 U.S. 253 (1984); Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S. 584 (1979); Youngberg v. Romeo, 457 U.S. 307 (1982); Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319 (1976); Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71 (1992). The protected interest, substantive basis for confinement, and adequacy of procedures are separate inquiries.

**Open Point:** Whether the scheme is invalid across its facial reach, and whether review on request is constitutionally insufficient for the entire class on the supplied record.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Government:** The regulation supplies a custodial preference grounded in ordinary family relationships and child safety, with institutional placement where a suitable release is unavailable. The INS is not equipped to perform an unrestricted child-placement program. Review is available, and the facial record does not show that every minor cannot request it.

**Class and below:** Institutional confinement burdens liberty even when the institution is described as care. Safe responsible adults can offer a less restrictive alternative, and minors may not understand or be able to invoke paper hearing rights. Administrative economy cannot justify unnecessary custody.

**Limits:** Government must still demonstrate lawful authority in an individual detention challenge. The class cannot obtain a universal placement rule merely from proof that some applications may be defective.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the broad facial injunction if lawful applications exist and the scheme does not invariably deny meaningful review; preserve properly presented as-applied claims. Alternatively, sustain a specifically established classwide statutory or procedural defect with relief limited to that defect. An automatic hearing requirement or expanded custodian category must be tied to an established legal violation, not a preferred child-welfare policy.

**Question Presented / Granted Scope:** Whether the FSIA’s first commercial-activity exception reaches the Nelsons’ intentional-tort claims arising from Saudi detention and torture, and their separately pleaded negligent-failure-to-warn claims arising from recruitment in the United States.  
**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eleventh Circuit, 923 F.2d 1528 (1991), reversing dismissal for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction.

The complaint also alleges that the defendants failed during recruitment to warn of foreseeable retaliation. The claims seek damages for personal injuries, not enforcement of an unpaid contractual benefit. No individual-officer statutory claim is before the Court.

##### Issue 1: FSIA commercial activity and the basis of each action

##### Issue 2: Separate merits and preclusion questions

**Limits:** An employment connection alone cannot make every sovereign act commercial. An independently pleaded local commercial tort must still satisfy ordinary duty, causation, and preclusion requirements.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Limits:** An aggravator need not be mathematically precise, and state-of-mind findings are not inherently subjective in the constitutional sense. A court may not invent a new state-law definition to save the existing one.
