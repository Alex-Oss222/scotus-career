**Limits:** An aggravator need not be mathematically precise, and state-of-mind findings are not inherently subjective in the constitutional sense. A court may not invent a new state-law definition to save the existing one.

General allegations that the train was too fast for the time and place do not establish that distinction. The warning-device rules do not preempt this claim because their operative project predicates are absent.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress chose covering, which requires comparing the subject of the actual state duty with the federal rule rather than equating any speed-related duty with the whole regulated field. Section 213.9 fixes maximum speeds by track class. Contemporaneous rulemaking treated population density and crossing-specific variables as outside that track-safety calculation. Stone would therefore distinguish a demand to recalibrate the track maximum from an actually presented duty addressing an uncovered crossing condition. That is his proposed resolution of the scope question, not a claim that earlier Supreme Court law already adopted it. The packet describes ordinary time-and-place speed allegations but does not establish a preserved, distinct uncovered theory, so a limited remand must resolve that point without assuming an additional claim exists. If the duty is only an ordinary demand for slower operation on the federally classified track, the speed claim remains preempted. For warnings, general planning and an abandoned project do not satisfy §§ 646.214(b)(3)–(4)’s operative conditions. Coverage and each statutory local-hazard condition remain independent legal inquiries; nonconflict alone does not save a covered claim. [Published record and contemporaneous regulatory discussion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/658/) supply source context, not an entering holding in this case.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general exemption for negligence at crossings, no statewide local-hazard exception, and no evasion of express coverage through different phrasing of the same duty. The specific-individual-hazard issue remains reserved. If the existing record supplies no preserved uncovered crossing theory, the proposed remand ends in retention of speed preemption.
