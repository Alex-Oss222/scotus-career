##### Issue 1: Collateral-order appellate jurisdiction

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Section 1291 and Cohen v. **Open Point:** Whether denial of the asserted sovereign immunity satisfies Cohen, without using appealability to establish the underlying immunity.

**Limits:** The order must conclusively deny a genuine immunity claim. The appeal need not determine contract liability, and allowing it establishes neither entity status nor a constitutional immunity extending beyond the doctrine actually invoked.

**Limits and alternatives:** Market evidence need not be ritualistic, but economic power and probability cannot be presumed from wrongdoing alone. The other verdict theories require separate review.

##### Issue 1: Dangerous probability is an element

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved legal challenge to the instruction and general verdict.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Swift and the enacted offense; reject the Ninth Circuit’s substitution of an inference of bad intent for proof of threatened market power.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Attempted monopolization requires anticompetitive conduct, specific intent to monopolize, and a dangerous probability of achieving monopoly power in a relevant market. The same conduct may support more than one inference, but it cannot eliminate the probability requirement.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A firm may harm a rival without having the ability to monopolize the competitive field. Congress did not create a general federal tort of unfair commercial conduct in § 2.
