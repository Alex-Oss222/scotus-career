**Question Presented / Granted Scope:** Whether the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments permit execution following a constitutionally valid capital conviction when later evidence is offered to establish factual innocence, and whether the second federal habeas proceeding supports further proceedings or a stay on that basis.  
**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, 954 F.2d 1029 (1992), vacating the district court’s stay of execution and rejecting a freestanding innocence ground for federal habeas relief.  
**Relief Requested:** Herrera seeks reversal and an opportunity for judicial examination of the new evidence, with protection against execution while the claim is lawfully considered; Texas seeks affirmance and finality of the existing judgment.

**Material Record:** Herrera was convicted in January 1982 of murdering Officer Carrisalez and sentenced to death; he pleaded guilty to Officer Rucker’s murder that July. The trial proof included eyewitness identification, Carrisalez’s identification, the vehicle connection, physical evidence, and an incriminating letter. Later affidavits attributed the killings to Herrera’s brother, who died in 1984. Two reported the brother’s confessions; another, from his son, claimed eyewitness knowledge dating from childhood. These are disputed assertions, not findings of innocence. The second state application presented part of the evidence and was denied. The second federal petition added material, including the son’s affidavit, and a suppression allegation. The district court dismissed most claims as abuse of the writ, initially rejected the Brady claim, reconsidered to allow a hearing on that claim, and stayed execution to permit state presentation of the innocence materials. It did not conduct an innocence hearing or find the affidavits credible.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case concerns postconviction relief, not a renewed presumption of innocence or a sufficiency challenge to the trial record. Identify separately cognizability, Teague, exhaustion of materially new evidence, abuse of the writ, entitlement to a hearing, and entitlement to a stay. The appellate rejection of the Brady theory does not prove either suppression or innocence. Some material was added only on rehearing; it cannot be treated as a developed district-court record. Review of the stay does not authorize an immediate acquittal.

**Relevant Dates:** Offenses: September 1981. Conviction: January 1982. First federal proceedings ended in 1990; new state proceedings in 1990–1991. Present federal petition: February 1992. Argument: October 7, 1992.

##### Issue 1: Cognizability and the proposed constitutional injury

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** 28 U.S.C. §§ 2241 and 2254 authorize relief from custody contrary to federal law. Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293 (1963), distinguishes newly discovered evidence alone from a federal constitutional violation. Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 (1979), addresses legal sufficiency of the trial evidence. The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments, In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358 (1970), and capital reliability decisions supply the constitutional arguments. Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436 (1986), and Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478 (1986), recognize innocence-related gateways in different procedural settings.

**Open Point:** Whether execution of a person who can now establish factual innocence is itself a cognizable constitutional violation, rather than innocence functioning only as a gateway to another violation.

##### Issue 2: Collateral-review and interim-relief gates

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 (1989), Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 (1989), McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467 (1991), Rose v. Lundy, 455 U.S. 509 (1982), and the pre-AEDPA habeas statutes govern retroactivity, abuse, and exhaustion. Townsend, as limited by Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1 (1992), governs factual development. Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 (1983), and Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320 (1990), require a legally substantial basis for an execution stay.

**Open Point:** Whether a freestanding innocence rule would fall within the substantive exception to Teague, what reliable showing would warrant factual development, and which exhaustion or successive-petition objections remain operative.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below and Texas:** A fair trial supplies the lawful conviction; habeas does not ordinarily reopen guilt merely because witnesses offer late and contradictory accounts. The evidence is hearsay-heavy, delayed, and inconsistent with substantial trial proof. Clemency and available state processes address late innocence assertions. The Government as amicus supports affirmance.

**Herrera:** Executing a person who can establish that he did not commit the crime is a present constitutional wrong independent of a particular trial error. Paper comparison is not a substitute for testing an asserted eyewitness and corroborating accounts. A trial’s constitutional regularity cannot make reliably established innocence irrelevant.

**Limits and alternatives:** Recognition of a claim does not establish its truth. Texas’s finality interest supports a demanding showing, not a power to execute an adjudicated innocent person. Conversely, humane concern does not dispense with the statutory channel, exhaustion, or the standards for reopening factual proceedings.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm if no freestanding claim exists, or if a legally recognized claim is facially insufficient under its applicable standard. Reverse or vacate the categorical cognizability ruling and remand for separate treatment of exhaustion, abuse, and factual sufficiency. A hearing or stay requires its own findings; neither follows automatically from accepting the constitutional theory. A finding of innocence, a new trial, and an execution bar are distinct possible consequences and cannot be ordered interchangeably on these affidavits.

##### Issue 1: Capital innocence, procedural reach, and a source-bound remand

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches cognizability as necessary to review the appellate legal ground. He separately recognizes exhaustion and successive-petition limits and rejects the proposition that the district court already found a hearing warranted on innocence.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve an open constitutional question by a candid extension of the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments, and identify its retroactivity consequence expressly. Townsend’s exclusion of new evidence unconnected to a constitutional wrong remains intact; the proposed rule identifies the present execution itself as the wrong.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A capital prisoner who establishes factual innocence by clear and convincing evidence may not be executed for that crime. A properly channeled § 2254 claim alleging that violation is cognizable even without an additional trial error. The existing verdict remains entitled to substantial weight; an unsupported assertion or a mere possibility of acquittal does not meet the rule.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defect in categorical noncognizability is that it makes a constitutionally regular trial authorize a punishment even after its factual predicate is reliably disproved. Stone proposes a substantive limitation on whom the State may execute, not a general second criminal trial. He would apply Penry’s substantive Teague exception to that limitation openly, rather than silently calling a new evidentiary procedure old law. The new component is recognition of the substantive claim and a clear-and-convincing merits burden. The existing trial burden, ordinary sufficiency review, exhaustion, abuse rules, and hearing requirements remain. Finality is protected by the demanding showing and the lawful channel, but reliance on executing an actually innocent person is not protected. The delayed affidavits require critical examination; their allegations are not enough to award the writ, and their conflicting accounts are not judicial findings that can be resolved through this brief.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The proposal is limited to execution. It does not settle every noncapital freestanding claim, erase lawful custody on other convictions, require hearings on every affidavit, or revive the independently rejected Brady claim. No approved fallback exists.

##### Issue 1: Retroactivity

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Teague, 489 U.S. 288, and Butler v. McKellar, 494 U.S. 407 (1990), distinguish rules dictated when the conviction became final from new rules. Penry, 492 U.S. 302, recognizes that a capital mitigation claim may be an application of earlier law. Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 (1990), limits treating every request for a different sentencing instruction as compelled by Lockett.

**Open Point:** Whether the requested ability to give effect to these particular mitigating characteristics was dictated in 1984.

##### Issue 2: Mitigation within the special issues

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Lockett v. Ohio, 438 U.S. 586 (1978), and Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982), forbid excluding relevant mitigation from the sentencing decision. Jurek v. Texas, 428 U.S. 262 (1976), sustained Texas’s framework on the understanding that relevant mitigating evidence could be considered. Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990), frames instructional review in terms of a reasonable likelihood of an impermissible application.

**Open Point:** Whether evidence with a culpability-reducing significance independent of future danger could receive effect through the questions actually submitted; the role of the reasonable-likelihood formulation must not erase the earlier substantive requirement.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below and Texas:** Youth and favorable character permit a negative answer on future dangerousness and an assessment of deliberateness. Jurek approved the scheme. At minimum, reasonable jurists in 1984 could regard that mechanism as sufficient, making the proposed additional requirement new.

**Graham:** Evidence may reduce moral culpability even when it does not negate deliberate action or predict rehabilitation. Lockett and Eddings already required the sentencer to be able to act on that force; admission without an authorized sentencing effect is inadequate. The lower court’s distinction between ordinary mitigation and uniquely severe permanent handicaps has no basis in those cases.

**Necessary limits:** No entitlement to a favorable sentence follows from offering mitigation. A State may structure sentencing and require legal instructions; it may not make material mitigation incapable of affecting the choice.

Affirm on Teague without deciding the disputed merits, affirm on a conclusion that the special issues adequately accommodated the evidence, or reverse after finding the claim dictated by the pre-finality authorities and the instruction constitutionally insufficient. A new procedural rule would require an independently applicable Teague exception; severity of punishment alone is not one. Sentencing relief does not reopen guilt.

##### Issue 1: Severance and fair trial

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure 8(b) and 14 distinguish authorized joinder from relief against prejudice. Richardson v. #### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Government and below:** Rule 14 contains no automatic severance command. Conflicting accounts do not relieve the Government of proving each defendant guilty. The petitioners identify no actual compromise of confrontation, admissibility, or individualized verdict assessment on this record.

**Limits:** Administrative economy cannot excuse an uncured violation of a trial right. Conversely, a better chance of acquittal in isolation is not the measure of prejudice.

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved Rule 14 objections, assuming proper joinder and applying abuse-of-discretion review.
