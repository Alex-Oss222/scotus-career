## Controlling holdings

#### Adjudicatory consent does not authorize these monetary assessments

**Holding and operative rule:** The McCarran Amendment subjects the United States to the state procedures governing the water adjudication, but does not specifically waive immunity from Idaho’s challenged filing assessments. Its express exclusion of judgments for costs and the blurred line between these assessments and former costs reinforce that conclusion; participation in the adjudication does not itself create liability for the charges.

**Authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that consent to adjudicate water rights does not itself consent to this monetary exaction. Eagle County gives the McCarran Amendment its intended comprehensive reach, so the Government cannot escape ordinary state procedural requirements merely by calling them nonsubstantive. That conclusion does not answer the distinct question of payment. Shaw and the monetary-consent principle applied in Nordic Village require attention to the obligation Congress actually authorized. Section666 contains no specific authorization for these charges and expressly prohibits judgments for costs. Idaho’s system collects in advance expenses formerly taxed at the end of adjudication, blurring the ordinary distinction between fees and costs. That history reinforces the absence of sufficiently specific monetary consent; the Court need not classify every fee as a cost. The State’s practical financing interest cannot enlarge the waiver. The United States remains a party whose water rights may be determined under the applicable law. The Court does not announce immunity from every procedural requirement or decide charges imposed under a different express waiver.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. District Court in and for Eagle County, 401 U.S.520: comprehensive adjudicatory waiver preserved, not converted to universal payment consent.
- Library of Congress v. Shaw, 478 U.S.310: applies specificity of monetary consent.
- United States v. Nordic Village, February25 1992, simulated: particular monetary-waiver principle applied by analogy; bankruptcy holding unchanged.

## Material published noncontrolling positions

Stone, joined by Stevens, agrees that the assessments cannot be imposed. Idaho finances the adjudication by substituting the challenged assessment for conventional costs; Congress expressly excluded judgments for costs. That textual ground suffices. The concurrence would not turn the result into a general rule that every procedural fee requires a separately phrased waiver. Nor would it permit the Government to avoid ordinary state procedures or the comprehensive adjudication itself. The concurrence supports the cost-characterization ground and judgment, but does not join the Court’s broader monetary-waiver reasoning. Stevens’ position is consistent with his Nordic Village dissent because the present statute contains an express exclusion.
