## Controlling holdings

#### The court may decide a necessary antecedent question of governing law

**Holding and operative rule:** In this live dispute, the court of appeals could consider whether the statute authorizing the challenged activity remained in force even though the parties initially assumed it did, after giving them a fair opportunity to brief the issue. This authority to identify governing law does not impose a general duty to raise every unargued issue or dispense with adversarial fairness.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the parties’ initial framing did not compel a court to assume the existence of the law on which their dispute depended. Kamen distinguishes a properly presented claim from every legal argument bearing on its resolution, and Arcadia recognizes the need to decide a question logically antecedent to the one the parties emphasize. The insurance agents and federal defendants remained adverse over the validity of the Comptroller’s action, so statutory existence was a governing-law question within a concrete controversy. The court of appeals requested argument and supplemental briefing, rather than resolving an unexpected premise without an opportunity to respond. Its consideration of the issue was therefore permissible even though its answer was wrong. The Court rejects neither party presentation in general nor the need for fair procedure. It does not decide whether a court would be required to raise this question in different circumstances or authorize advisory resolution of unrelated legal issues.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Kamen v. Kemper Financial Services, Inc., 500 U.S.90: a presented claim permits consideration of the governing legal arguments.
- Arcadia v. Ohio Power Co., 498 U.S.73: a necessary antecedent question may be resolved.
- Swift & Co. v. Hocking Valley Railway Co., 243 U.S.281: parties’ legal assumptions do not bind a court’s statement of governing law.

#### The enacted statutory structure establishes that the insurance authorization survived

**Holding and operative rule:** The 1916 insurance-agency provision amended §13 of the Federal Reserve Act rather than Revised Statutes §5202, notwithstanding the misplaced quotation marks in the printed text. The 1918 amendment of §5202 therefore did not repeal it; omission from a nonpositive-law Code edition cannot overcome the governing Statutes at Large.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the full enactment, rather than two isolated quotation marks, identifies the amended provision. The 1916 Act restated §13 of the Federal Reserve Act and carried forward language within that section referring to §5202. Its arrangement, neighboring Federal Reserve powers and consistent references to the Act establish where the operative amendment begins and ends. Treating the printer’s marks as controlling would detach the insurance authorization and neighboring provisions from the structure Congress enacted. Hammock permits punctuation to yield to that demonstrated statutory meaning, without creating a general judicial power to improve legislation. The 1918 Act amended §5202 by adding another exception and thus did not repeal the separate insurance provision in §13. The later Code omission supplies no contrary enactment because that title was not positive law. The enrolled-bill principle concerns authenticity, which nobody disputes here. The Court need not rely on legislative history, agency deference or the desirability of bank insurance sales to establish survival.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Hammock v. Loan & Trust Co., 105 U.S.77: punctuation read in the whole enacted context where that context establishes the legal meaning.
- Marshall Field & Co. v. Clark, 143 U.S.649: enrolled-bill authenticity distinguished from interpretation of an agreed authentic text.
- Posadas v. National City Bank, 296 U.S.497: a description of §5202 amendments does not decide this repeal question.
- Commissioner v. First Security Bank of Utah, 405 U.S.394: prior assumed validity is not treated as a holding resolving statutory survival.
- Germain, March 9, 1992: entered fidelity to operative statutory language and coexistence preserved.
