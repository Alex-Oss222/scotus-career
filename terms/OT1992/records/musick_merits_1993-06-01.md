## Controlling holdings

#### Contribution is available among persons jointly liable under the received Rule10b-5 action

**Holding and operative rule:** Persons jointly liable for the same violation under the established implied Rule10b-5 action may seek contribution, subject to proof of their shared underlying liability and the applicable allocation requirements. The rule is a bounded incident of that received action and does not create general professional liability, an automatic aiding-and-abetting rule, indemnity, or contribution under unrelated statutes.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that defining this incident of the established implied remedy calls for the same confined method used to define its other boundaries. Lampf looks to closely analogous express securities provisions, and §§9(e) and18(b) provide contribution among persons answerable for comparable misconduct. Blue Chip Stamps and Ernst & Ernst likewise show that the received action must have judicially identified limits rather than an undefined remedial scope. Northwest Airlines and Texas Industries concern attempts to add contribution to different congressionally supplied schemes; they do not settle every incident of this already judicially developed action. The dissent’s new-action objection has force, but the Court concludes that allocation among persons already liable is part of defining that existing liability, not a new substantive prohibition. The insurers must still establish the defendants’ own shared liability and a permissible allocation. Neither participation in a professional engagement nor payment of a settlement proves those predicates.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Lampf, Pleva, Lipkind, Prupis & Petigrow v. Gilbertson, 501 U.S.350: close express securities analogies applied to define an incident of the received action.
- Blue Chip Stamps v. Manor Drug Stores, 421 U.S.723: bounded judicial definition of the established action applied.
- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S.185: limits on implied securities liability preserved.
- Northwest Airlines, Inc. v. Transport Workers Union, 451 U.S.77: different express remedial scheme distinguished; no universal contribution presumption announced.
- Texas Industries, Inc. v. Radcliff Materials, Inc., 451 U.S.630: different statutory scheme distinguished.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February26,1992: existing-action/remedy distinction preserved; no freestanding professional cause created.

## Continuity and published noncontrolling positions

**Dissent — Thomas; joined by Blackmun, O'Connor:** Thomas, joined by Blackmun and O’Connor, distinguishes the accepted private action of an injured investor from a new action by a wrongdoer seeking reimbursement. Northwest Airlines and Texas Industries require congressional authorization rather than a judicial preference for fair allocation. The existence of contribution in other express securities sections shows that Congress knew how to provide it; it does not itself enact a contribution right under §10(b). Lampf’s task of defining a limitation period for a received action does not necessarily justify creating a distinct claimant and claim. The dissent would reject the asserted federal contribution right without deciding whether these professionals committed securities violations or fixing anyone’s share of loss. Its disagreement concerns remedial authority, not a general rule immunizing advisers from their own otherwise established liability. It is noncontrolling.
