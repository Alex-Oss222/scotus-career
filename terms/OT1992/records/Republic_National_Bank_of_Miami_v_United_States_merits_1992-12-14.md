## Record facts and source corrections

The United States sought forfeiture under 21 U.S.C. §881(a)(6). Initial arrest of the real property perfected in rem jurisdiction. Republic asserted an $800,000 mortgage interest. In May 1988 the parties agreed to a $1,050,000 sale, with claims transferred to the proceeds held by the Marshal. The District Court entered forfeiture on March 15, 1990. The bank timely appealed without a stay or supersedeas bond. On April 20, 1990, the Government transferred proceeds to the Assets Forfeiture Fund in the Treasury. The Eleventh Circuit dismissed on June 10, 1991 without deciding the bank’s actual-versus-constructive-knowledge and other merits objections. No fraudulent-removal finding, conclusive lien entitlement, existing payment judgment or later disposition is supplied.

## Law of the decision

#### Removal does not itself terminate perfected forfeiture jurisdiction

**Holding and operative rule:** Once valid attachment has established in rem jurisdiction, removal of the property or substituted proceeds does not by itself defeat appellate jurisdiction. The bank’s failure to obtain a stay or bond therefore does not foreclose this appeal where a favorable judgment could still provide lawful effectual relief; an entirely useless judgment presents a distinct limit.

**Authority:** Parts I, II and IV of Blackmun’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; eight Justices adopt this rule. Thomas supports the judgment on a different statutory ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that initial control of the property and continuing custody are different requirements. The Rio Grande rejects the proposition that removal automatically destroys jurisdiction already acquired, and The Little Charles identifies practical inability to effectuate a decree as a different concern. The Brig Ann addresses abandonment before proceedings began; Taylor v. Carryl concerns the original seizure needed to obtain jurisdiction. Neither establishes the Government’s proposed perpetual-custody rule. Here the parties substituted identifiable sale proceeds for property validly attached, and the Government then placed those proceeds in its own Treasury account. The bank’s failure to secure a stay did not undo the original attachment. Church of Scientology likewise confirms that possible effectual relief can preserve review despite an intervening event. The statutory-payment holding supplies that possibility here. The Court reserves a case where a judgment would be entirely useless, the amendment’s temporal application, and every question about whether this bank should prevail on the forfeiture merits.

**Precedent treatment:**

- The Rio Grande, 23 Wall. 458 — applied; removal alone does not terminate jurisdiction properly acquired.
- The Little Charles, 26 F. Cas. 979 — applied; the ability to effectuate relief is distinct from uninterrupted custody.
- The Brig Ann, 9 Cranch 289 — distinguished; abandonment before institution differs from removal after valid attachment.
- Taylor v. Carryl, 20 How. 583 — distinguished; initial jurisdiction does not establish perpetual custody as a condition.
- Church of Scientology of California v. United States, November 16, 1992 — applied by analogy on effectual relief; its IRS-copy rule supplies no payment authority.

#### The return statute and judgment appropriation permit effective relief upon success

**Holding and operative rule:** If the bank prevails in the underlying forfeiture and obtains the requisite judgment, 28 U.S.C. §2465 supplies a substantive right to return of the substituted proceeds; 31 U.S.C. §1304 and 28 U.S.C. §2414 supply the judgment-payment route when payment is not otherwise provided, the Comptroller General certifies payment, and the obligation satisfies the statutory final-judgment and payable-judgment requirements. That conditional lawful route defeats the Government’s claim that this appeal can produce no effective relief, without ordering payment now or recognizing judicial spending power apart from an applicable appropriation.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela’s statutory-payment opinion, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six express joins and independently controls. Stevens expressly accepts the same sufficient statutory proposition in his own writing, making seven supporters without an invented formal join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress has supplied a lawful means of effectuating a favorable forfeiture judgment. Section 2465 commands return when the claimant prevails, and sale of the attached property does not eliminate that right; the proceeds stand in its place. Section 1304 appropriates sums for qualifying final judgments when payment is not otherwise provided and the certification and statutory-payment conditions are met. Section 2414 supplies the relevant final district-court judgment route. Richmond cautions that the Judgment Fund is not an all-purpose judicial account: a substantive statutory right must support payment. Section 2465 supplies that right here if the bank succeeds. The Government’s Treasury transfer therefore does not make relief legally impossible. Knote’s restriction on payment without legislative authority need not be qualified because the necessary statutory route exists. The Court does not decide whether disputed funds may ever be returned without an appropriation, whether another payment source applies, or whether the bank is entitled to prevail. No money is awarded today.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Office of Personnel Management v. Richmond, 496 U.S. 414 — applied; a substantive statutory entitlement and applicable appropriation, not equity alone, are required for this payment route.
- Knote v. United States, 95 U.S. 149 — preserved; the statutory resolution leaves the broader constitutional question about contested forfeiture proceeds undecided.
