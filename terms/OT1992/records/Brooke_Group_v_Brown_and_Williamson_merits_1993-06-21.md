**Questions and relief:** What primary-line predatory-pricing proof Robinson-Patman requires, whether oligopolistic recoupment is possible, and whether this evidence permits the necessary inference. Liggett seeks reversal of the judgment as a matter of law and restoration of its verdict or further proceedings. Brown & Williamson seeks affirmance.

**Material facts and record limits:** Liggett developed lower-priced generic cigarettes. Brown & Williamson's rebate campaign allegedly used below-cost sales to discipline Liggett, increase generic prices, and preserve branded-cigarette profits. Evidence supporting the theory included a history of parallel profitable pricing, internal strategy documents, subsequent parallel price increases, and expert testimony that costs and promotions did not account for those increases. Contrary evidence included expanding generic output, excess capacity, new lower-price products, Reynolds' incentives and actions favoring greater generic volume, discounts separating list from net prices, and executives' testimony disputing coordination. Brown & Williamson bore its own predatory losses while any later oligopolistic gains would be shared. The jury received a competitive-injury and recoupment instruction. On judgment-as-a-matter-of-law review, evidence and reasonable inferences favor Liggett; below-cost pricing and intent can be treated as sufficiently supported without deciding the remaining competitive mechanism.

The judgment-as-a-matter-of-law standard prevents credibility reweighing but still requires evidence of every legally necessary link in the claimed recoupment mechanism, including recovery of the predator's own losses through sufficiently durable anticompetitive returns. The district court identified lack of competitive injury, antitrust injury, and causation as separate grounds. ##### Issue 1: A market mechanism, not a judicial presumption of virtue

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory competitive-injury and sufficiency questions, viewing the evidence favorably to Liggett and accepting sufficient evidence of below-cost pricing and purpose for the analysis.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Clarify the statutory competitive-injury requirement using Matsushita and Cargill while preserving Robinson-Patman's reasonable-possibility language and Utah Pie's allowance for supported circumstantial proof. Applying recoupment logic to this primary-line claim is a proposed doctrinal clarification, not an assertion that those earlier decisions already resolved the identical question.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A primary-line below-cost discriminatory-pricing claim requires evidence permitting a reasonable inference that the strategy had a reasonable prospect of recouping its losses through the alleged anticompetitive mechanism. Tacit oligopolistic coordination is a legally possible mechanism. Actual success or monopoly by the defendant is not required, but intention, concentration, and injury to a rival alone are insufficient. The appellate judgment must be reconsidered without a categorical oligopoly bar and with the evidence and reasonable inferences evaluated under the proper standard.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The record supports below-cost sales and a capacity to impose losses on Liggett that it was unwilling to sustain. A history of parallel profitable pricing, the documented discipline strategy, subsequent parallel increases, and expert testimony that promotions and costs did not consume those increases support a possible inference of durable higher generic prices and recovery through protected branded profits. The evidence must be considered together; growth in generic output does not itself establish a competitive price, and an ultimately incomplete scheme may have presented the statutory prospect of injury when undertaken. Stone does not treat these disputed inferences as findings or require the reviewing court to accept a conclusory expert opinion.

**Counterevidence and Remaining Recoupment Inquiry:** Excess capacity, expanding generics, new lower-price products, Reynolds' contrary incentives, the difference between list and net prices, and Brown & Williamson's need to recover its own losses despite sharing gains with other firms seriously challenge the alleged mechanism. The remand must test the ability to discipline the rival, sustain coordinated supracompetitive generic prices, and recover the predator's own losses over a sufficient period, including the evidence of incremental returns. Intent documents and concentration cannot fill a missing link. Stone's choice to remand is a judgment about the need for correct review of the whole record, not a claim that the Master or prior precedent compels liability. Utah Pie is not reduced to protection of a competitor; Matsushita and Cargill do not authorize silently requiring actual recoupment. Relevant facts concerning substitution, entry, coordination incentives, duration, and net prices inform the existing inquiry without becoming a new mandatory checklist.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No monopoly-only condition is added to Robinson-Patman. The statute’s primary-line requirement remains distinct from Sherman Act attempted monopoly, secondary-line discrimination, and other exclusionary conduct. No numerical market-share safe harbor or universal price-only antitrust rule is created.

## Controlling holdings

#### Below-cost pricing must carry a prospect of recoupment

**Holding and operative rule:** A primary-line predatory-pricing claim requires pricing below an appropriate measure of the defendant’s costs and a reasonable prospect of recovering the predatory investment through anticompetitive returns. Tacit oligopolistic coordination is a legally possible mechanism; actual success, an express conspiracy or a single-firm monopoly is not indispensable, but intent, concentration and injury to a rival alone are insufficient.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, Below-cost pricing must carry a prospect of recoupment portion; O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute protects competition rather than insulating a competitor from low prices. Brown Shoe supplies that distinction, and Matsushita and Cargill explain why below-cost losses injure competition only if a plausible later return can make the predatory strategy worthwhile. Robinson-Patman’s primary-line standard requires a reasonable prospect of that recovery, not proof that recoupment already occurred or automatic importation of the Sherman Act’s dangerous-probability formulation. Utah Pie permits supported circumstantial proof of competitive injury; it does not establish liability merely from discriminatory prices and harm to a smaller rival. Oligopolistic coordination is not categorically excluded, because durable supracompetitive returns may in principle arise without one firm obtaining a monopoly. But the evidence must connect the strategy to recovery of the defendant’s own losses, including the consequences of sharing subsequent gains with rivals. The Court need not select an exclusive accounting measure of cost because the separate recoupment requirement resolves this appeal.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brown Shoe, 370 U.S.294 — competition/competitor distinction applied.
- Matsushita, 475 U.S.574 — recoupment logic applied without requiring an express conspiracy here.
- Cargill, 479 U.S.104 — low prices distinguished from actionable predation.
- Utah Pie, 386 U.S.685 — read consistently with competitive injury; not treated as rival-protection liability.
- Spectrum Sports, January 25, 1993 — intent cannot replace competitive danger; Sherman Act elements not transplanted wholesale.

#### This record lacks the necessary recoupment showing

**Holding and operative rule:** Even crediting below-cost pricing and predatory purpose and drawing reasonable inferences for Liggett, the evidence does not permit a reasonable jury to find the required prospect of Brown & Williamson recouping its losses through the alleged coordinated pricing mechanism. The failure of that necessary element sustains judgment as a matter of law without a categorical oligopoly exemption.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, This record lacks the necessary recoupment showing portion; O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the evidence must establish the links in the proposed market mechanism, not merely a hostile plan. Liggett points to parallel profitable pricing, internal discipline documents, later list-price increases and expert testimony about costs and promotions. Those materials support intent and a proposed strategy, but the required inference also concerns durable anticompetitive returns sufficient to repay Brown & Williamson’s own investment. Expanding generic output, excess capacity, lower-price entry, discounts separating list from net prices and Reynolds’ contrary incentives undermine that connection. The predator would bear its own losses while sharing any coordinated gains. Matsushita permits scrutiny of whether the proposed mechanism has evidentiary support; it does not authorize choosing between witnesses merely because judges prefer one. On the whole record, the missing recovery showing cannot be supplied by intent or concentration. The Court therefore affirms on competitive injury and does not decide the district court’s independent antitrust-injury and causation grounds or declare that oligopoly recoupment can never be proved.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Matsushita, 475 U.S.574 — economically plausible mechanism must have evidentiary support.
- Cargill, 479 U.S.104 — rival losses do not alone establish competitive harm.
- Utah Pie, 386 U.S.685 — circumstantial proof permitted, but necessary recovery inference absent here.
