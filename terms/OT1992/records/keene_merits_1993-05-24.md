Question: Whether §1500 bars the reviewed compensation actions because factually overlapping suits seeking overlapping relief were pending elsewhere when each action was filed, notwithstanding their later dismissal.

Keene sought recovery from the United States for asbestos-related liabilities through actions filed while related suits remained pending elsewhere. Keene I was filed while its third-party action in Miller remained pending; Keene II preceded dismissal of its district-court suit by five days. The other suits later ended. The Claims Court dismissed, and the Federal Circuit affirmed en banc. ## Controlling holdings

#### Section1500 jurisdiction is measured when the action is filed

**Holding and operative rule:** Section1500 bars the reviewed Claims Court action when, at filing, a suit for or in respect to the same claim is pending elsewhere against the United States or a covered federal actor; later dismissal of that other suit does not cure the original action’s jurisdictional defect. Claims Court decisions inconsistent with that filing-time requirement cannot support jurisdiction here; the Court does not decide the reversed order of filing.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that jurisdiction is tested when the action begins. Mollan supplies that ordinary rule, and Corona Coal applies the statutory prohibition to an existing claim in another forum. The words describing a pending suit identify the condition that must be measured at that point, not a defect the plaintiff can retroactively erase. The dissent’s election approach addresses the practical hardship of divided jurisdiction, but it changes the statutory limit into a discretionary or continuing case-management rule. Congress’s choice may force difficult decisions about forum and timing; courts cannot supply a relation-back cure because those decisions are difficult. Brown and Hossein cannot survive to the extent they permit the inconsistent cure, while cases about a later-filed outside action address a sequence not presented. The Court affirms jurisdictional dismissal without reaching the underlying asbestos compensation theories or predicting the timeliness of a future action.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Mollan v. Torrance, 9 Wheat.537: filing-time jurisdiction applied.
- Corona Coal Co. v. United States, 263 U.S.537: statutory pendency bar applied.
- Brown v. United States, 175 Ct.Cl.343, 358 F.2d1002, and Hossein v. United States, 218 Ct.Cl.727: disapproved insofar as inconsistent with filing-time jurisdiction.
- Tecon Engineers, Inc. v. United States, 170 Ct.Cl.389, 343 F.2d943: reversed filing sequence reserved, not overruled here.

#### Overlapping facts and relief cannot be separated merely by changing legal theories

**Holding and operative rule:** For the claims presented, §1500 applies when the suits arise from substantially the same operative facts and seek overlapping relief, even if they plead different legal theories or those theories cannot be brought in the same forum. The Court leaves open suits seeking wholly different relief and does not adopt the Federal Circuit’s broader disposition of those precedents.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute cannot be avoided by assigning different legal labels to the same operative events and overlapping request for money. British American Tobacco supplies the established factual comparison, and the words for or in respect to a claim reject an artificially narrow identity requirement. Matson and the statutory history confirm that Congress addressed parallel routes against the Government and its agents, routes that often could not have been combined in one tribunal. Keene’s proposed claim-splitting limitation would therefore disable the statute in the circumstances it was enacted to reach. The factual overlap found below is not displaced by the different theories of compensation. Casman and Boston Five involved materially different requests for relief and need not be resolved to decide these suits. The Court neither rules that all factual overlap is sufficient regardless of relief nor adjudicates the merits of the monetary claims.

**Precedent treatment:**

- British American Tobacco Co. v. United States, 89 Ct.Cl.438: operative-facts construction adopted for overlapping relief.
- Matson Navigation Co. v. United States, 284 U.S.352: historical statutory setting applied; different causes do not negate the bar.
- Casman v. United States, 135 Ct.Cl.647, and Boston Five Cents Savings Bank v. United States, 864 F.2d137: wholly different relief question reserved, not overruled here.

#### The dismissals do not depend on a new rule requiring prospective-only treatment

**Holding and operative rule:** Keene’s reviewed suits fail under established filing-time and factual-identity principles; the Federal Circuit’s broader repudiation of precedents unnecessary to this result does not make the controlling rule new as applied here. The Court therefore rejects the requested prospective-only treatment without deciding the general availability of pure prospectivity.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Keene cannot obtain jurisdiction by characterizing this result as the retroactive announcement of an unexpected rule. The actual suits were filed while other actions on the same operative facts and overlapping relief remained pending. Corona Coal and British American Tobacco already supplied the necessary principles. The Federal Circuit also addressed cases involving a reversed filing order or wholly distinct relief, but those broader questions do not decide Keene’s actions and the Court does not adopt their resolution. Brown and Hossein are inconsistent with the filing-time rule, yet they do not supply a settled rule entitling these suits to proceed. Chevron Oil’s proposed prospective-treatment inquiry therefore need not be resolved: the premise of a new controlling rule for this case is absent. The Court likewise declines to answer hypothetical tolling questions about another complaint that has not been presented.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Chevron Oil Co. v. Huson, 404 U.S.97: prospective-only request rejected at its new-rule premise; general availability not decided.
- Corona Coal Co. v. United States, 263 U.S.537, and British American Tobacco Co. v. United States, 89 Ct.Cl.438: existing governing principles applied.

## Continuity and published noncontrolling positions

**Dissent — Stevens; no other joins:** Stevens reads the pendency language in light of the statute’s purpose of preventing simultaneous pursuit of duplicative claims. Once the other action has ended, that purpose no longer requires closing the remaining forum. He objects that a rigid filing-time rule can defeat adjudication even though neither court is then considering a competing suit and each forum initially had different jurisdictional limits. The lower decisions permitting a plaintiff to remove the obstacle before adjudication reinforce his treatment of the statute as an election rather than an irrevocable trap. He would not approve duplicate recovery or concurrent adjudication; his disagreement concerns the ability to proceed after the competing action ends. The dissent supplies no current exception to the Court’s filing-date rule.
