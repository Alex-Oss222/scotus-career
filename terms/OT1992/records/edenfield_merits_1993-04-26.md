## Controlling holdings

#### Real advancement must be established in the relevant professional setting

**Holding and operative rule:** When truthful, nonmisleading solicitation concerns lawful CPA services, the State must demonstrate that the harms supporting its restriction are real and that the restriction alleviates them to a material degree. Florida’s unsupported predictions about direct solicitation of business clients do not satisfy that burden; the ruling neither requires empirical studies in every case nor immunizes deceptive or coercive solicitation.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the State must connect its legitimate concerns to the speech it suppresses. Central Hudson requires more than a substantial interest stated in the abstract; Fox preserves a reasonable fit rather than demanding the least restrictive imaginable rule. Florida’s interests in preventing fraud, protecting privacy and maintaining independence are substantial, but the record does not establish the asserted threat from CPA business solicitation. The Board’s generalized affidavit does not show that such contacts undermine professional judgment, and the professional evidence points the other way. Ohralik permitted prophylaxis against personal solicitation by lawyers in circumstances involving vulnerable clients and pressure for immediate commitment. Business executives considering accounting services can control the time and setting and need not decide immediately. That difference defeats the proposed analogy, not every professional prophylactic rule. Nor can labeling the prohibition a time, place and manner restriction cure its failure materially to advance the asserted interests, even assuming that alternative framework applies. Fraud, coercion and materially different settings remain open.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S.557: applies the real-advancement burden.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S.469: retains reasonable fit without least restrictive means.
- Ohralik v. Ohio State Bar Assn., 436 U.S.447: distinguished by professional setting, vulnerability and pressure.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S.781: alternative time/place/manner characterization cannot cure deficient advancement.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993, simulated: consistent application; no expansion to strict scrutiny.

## Material published noncontrolling positions

Blackmun joins the opinion and judgment but maintains that truthful, nonmisleading commercial speech deserves more protection than the intermediate framework affords; that broader view is not adopted. O’Connor dissents. Her Shapero and Peel positions support attention to the culture and responsibilities of a profession, not simply the sophistication of a particular prospective client. She would allow Florida to protect independence and prevent pressure through a prophylactic restriction without waiting for proven abuse. In her view the Court understates experience-based risks and permits an injunction extending to business solicitation generally on a record centered on Fane’s proposed conduct. She would reverse; no Justice joins her writing.
