# Canonical Decision Record — Herrera v. Collins

**Reporter locator:** 506 U.S. 390.

### Event, posture and material record

On certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Fifth Circuit, 954 F.2d 1029 (February 18, 1992), which vacated an execution stay in Herrera's second federal habeas proceeding. The question is whether a freestanding assertion of innocence based on later evidence warrants federal habeas proceedings or a stay after a constitutionally valid capital trial. Herrera seeks judicial examination and protection against execution, not a direct acquittal on an accepted innocence finding. Texas and the United States defend the existing judgment, finality and established review channels.

Herrera was convicted in January 1982 of Carrisalez's murder and pleaded guilty to Rucker's murder in July. The evidence includes identification by Hernandez and Carrisalez, the car registered to Herrera's girlfriend, his possession of its keys, physical evidence and his incriminating letter. New affidavits attributed the murders to his brother, who died in 1984. Villarreal and Palacios reported confessions; the brother's son claimed eyewitness knowledge, and Ybarra reported another confession. Their contents remain allegations. The son was nine at the time. Some materials were supplied only in federal court, with another affidavit added on appellate rehearing. They are not developed district-court findings.

The District Court rejected claims 2–5 as abuse of the writ; initially rejected the Brady branch, reconsidered that branch to permit a hearing, and stayed execution to allow state presentation of new innocence material. It did not hold an innocence hearing or find affidavits credible. The Fifth Circuit found no particularized suppression allegation, upheld abuse treatment of claims 2–5, rejected innocence alone as cognizable, vacated the stay and vacated the certificate of probable cause on claims 2–5. Supreme Court scope does not reopen those independent rejected claims. No special hypothetical later stay-and-abeyance regime applies.

### Participation and issue-specific entering law

Nine participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. No recusal or other nonparticipation is established. Quorum is six; judgment threshold five.

Keeney (May 4) retains Townsend's deliberate-bypass qualification for inadequate state development of material facts on a properly presented claim; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not defeat a necessary federal hearing. Coleman claim default and McCleskey abuse, statutory exhaustion and §2254(d) factual presumptions remain separate. This does not presume a hearing merely because an affidavit exists.

Sawyer, effective June 22, 1992, requires clear and convincing evidence that absent constitutional error no reasonable juror would find death eligibility. Its express limits leave crime innocence and freestanding innocence unresolved. Sawyer's majority includes White, Kennedy, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; the broader Stevens/Blackmun/O'Connor judgment concurrence is a published position, not law. Townsend distinguishes new evidence alone from unconstitutional custody; Jackson reviews trial-record sufficiency. Ford and Beck supply competing arguments about substantive execution injury and capital reliability, not an already decided freestanding right.

Teague/Penry govern novelty and substantive exceptions; a proposed protected-class theory must be separately justified rather than mislabeled old law. Sections 2241/2254 authorize custody review only on federal grounds. Barefoot/Delo govern successive-petition stays, with Gomez's April 21 equitable-delay holding retained at its specific independent scope. Dobbs's January 19 rule requires justified accurate-record treatment but establishes no innocence claim or general hearing right. The limited holding in Dobbs does not alter Keeney, Sawyer, Jackson, Teague, exhaustion or abuse.

### Stone's position

Stone votes to vacate the categorical innocence exclusion and remand through the Fifth Circuit for proper claim identification, exhaustion and abuse analysis, and, when material, Townsend/Keeney factual development. His separate dissent proposes clear-and-convincing affirmative factual innocence as a substantive Eighth/Fourteenth execution bar, with an expressly proposed Penry substantive-exception extension under Teague. He does not join Blackmun's lower probable-innocence burden. He refuses an automatic hearing, an indefinite stay, an innocence finding, acquittal, automatic new trial or the view that resentencing cures proved crime innocence. Temporary relief requires Barefoot/Delo findings. The separately rejected Brady theory and claims 2–5 are not before this limited Supreme Court question; no substantive Stone choice on their merits is inferred.

## Law of the decision

#### This record does not establish entitlement to federal innocence relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Herrera's delayed and materially inconsistent affidavits, considered with the identification, physical and documentary evidence supporting his convictions, do not establish entitlement to the requested federal habeas relief or justify the district court's innocence-based stay, even assuming an exceptionally persuasive post-trial showing could constitutionally bar execution. This record-specific rejection establishes no uniform freestanding-innocence burden and does not decide whether such a constitutional claim exists.

**Authority:** O'Connor's plurality opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, and White's express agreement with its reasons for finding this submission insufficient in his concurrence in the judgment: five Justices adopt this limited record conclusion. White does not join the plurality's broader discussion or an unspecified extraordinarily high threshold. No whole opinion commands five votes; no Marks synthesis establishes a common general test.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that these papers do not warrant the federal relief requested. Jackson distinguishes constitutional sufficiency of the trial record from a fresh determination of guilt; Herrera does not show that the original conviction failed that requirement. His later affidavits must nevertheless be assessed together with the existing evidence for purposes of the assumed claim. The identifications, connection to the automobile, physical evidence and incriminating letter remain substantial. The delayed accounts attributing both murders to his deceased brother conflict over important circumstances and leave the guilty plea to Rucker's murder unexplained. Their possible probative value does not make this submission a persuasive affirmative demonstration of innocence. The District Court made no credibility finding or innocence-hearing determination to which this Court could defer. Barefoot and Delo require substantial grounds for relief before a stay on a successive petition; that requirement is unmet here. The Court does not adjudicate the affidavits false in every respect, define a general innocence standard, or authorize execution of a person whose factual innocence has been established.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307 — trial-record sufficiency remains distinct; White uses its rational-factfinder formulation only as a minimum for an assumed new-evidence claim, without a five-Justice adoption of that general burden.
- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 — applied to require a substantial legal basis for an execution stay.
- Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320 — applied to the substantial-ground requirement on a second or successive federal petition.
- Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293, and Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, May 4, 1992 — preserved; necessary factual development of a properly available claim remains distinct from legal sufficiency, cognizability, exhaustion and abuse.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, June 22, 1992 — distinguished; its capital-eligibility gateway does not decide freestanding guilt innocence or supply its merits burden.

#### Limits and Questions Not Reached

The federal habeas disposition does not determine the authority of a State to establish independent postconviction procedures for newly discovered evidence, actual innocence, or scientific developments undermining evidence relied upon at trial. Neither the existence nor the scope of such a state statutory remedy is before the Court. No general federal entitlement to a hearing upon any subsequent scientific disagreement is established.

## Precedent treatment and law after decision

The Court does not overrule Townsend, recognize a general new claim, replace Jackson or import Sawyer's eligibility gateway as a freestanding guilt-innocence standard. The plurality's broader cognizability, state-deadline and clemency discussion has four votes. White's distinct minimum burden is noncontrolling. The case-specific insufficient-showing determination and ordinary substantial-ground stay requirement support the affirmance.

Effective January 25, 1993, this submission does not justify the requested federal relief even upon the assumption of an exceptional constitutional innocence claim. The existence and precise burden of such a claim remain unresolved by a controlling rationale. No logical subset is established between an undefined extraordinary threshold and White's rational-factfinder minimum, so Marks supplies no general standard. Townsend/Keeney hearings on properly available materially undeveloped claims, statutory exhaustion, McCleskey abuse, Teague and the distinct Sawyer gateway retain their own operation.

## Continuity

O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, emphasizes in a separate concurrence that execution of a person established to be factually innocent would be constitutionally intolerable, but that the difficult question of a freestanding federal remedy need not be decided on this submission. The plurality's account of ordinary habeas practice and Texas's late-new-trial and clemency procedures does not attract White's join and supplies no new five-Justice holding on categorical cognizability or the adequacy of clemency.

White concurs in the judgment alone. Assuming that a persuasive showing of innocence could bar execution despite an expired new-evidence deadline, he would require at least a showing that no rational trier could find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt upon the old and new evidence together. He expressly agrees that Herrera's proof falls short for the reasons in the plurality. This does not adopt an established freestanding claim or a definitive burden.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, also joins the plurality and separately rejects a constitutional entitlement to reopen a lawfully completed conviction on newly discovered innocence evidence alone. Townsend, historical procedural practice and finality support his position; the arguendo disposition does not cause him to accept a new constitutional right. His categorical position has two votes.

Blackmun dissents, joined by Stevens and Souter in the legal discussion and proposed remand. He would recognize an independent Eighth and Fourteenth Amendment claim because execution of a person shown to be innocent is itself a constitutional injury. Ford and Johnson show that a valid judgment does not resolve every later objection to execution; Beck connects capital punishment to reliable guilt determinations. He would require the prisoner to show probable actual innocence on the whole evidence, exceeding a mere likelihood that a juror would entertain reasonable doubt. He rejects leaving that constitutional claim conclusively to clemency and would remand for district-court consideration of threshold factual and procedural requirements, without declaring Herrera innocent. Blackmun alone adds his broader criticism of restrictions on capital review; Stevens and Souter do not join that concluding commentary.

Stone-Zsela dissents separately. He would vacate only the categorical exclusion of a properly presented capital-innocence claim, preserving exhaustion and then-governing abuse-of-the-writ rules. His proposed merits burden is clear and convincing affirmative proof, on the relevant old and new evidence together, that the prisoner did not commit the crime for which execution is sought. Ford and Beck support extending substantive protection; Penry's exception for substantive limits on capital punishment would extend to that protected class. This is a proposed extension, not a claim that those cases already decided freestanding innocence. Clemency is additional protection, not a conclusive answer to an established constitutional prohibition. Allegations provisionally credited must be capable of satisfying that burden before factual development becomes material. Townsend and Keeney then retain deliberate bypass, while ordinary counsel negligence alone does not bar a necessary hearing. Any temporary stay requires an express Barefoot/Delo showing; no indefinite stay follows. If innocence is proved, execution for that crime is barred and another sentencing hearing cannot cure the defect. His proposal neither orders acquittal nor disturbs independent lawful custody, revives the Brady claim, changes the trial burden or resolves all noncapital claims.

All separate theories remain published noncontrolling positions. Neither the probable-innocence standard nor Stone's clear-and-convincing standard enters current law. Scalia/Thomas's categorical rejection is likewise noncontrolling. The narrow shared insufficiency holding does not decide future petitions presenting stronger evidence.

Supreme Court merits review concludes with affirmance. The Fifth Circuit's vacation of the district-court stay remains operative. The Court orders no renewed federal stay, new hearing, retrial, sentence reduction or release. The reported state stay pending this review is not converted into an indefinite federal order; no subsequent execution, date or state action is established.
