## Controlling holdings

#### Administrative exhaustion must precede the original FTCA action

**Holding and operative rule:** An FTCA claimant must present the claim to the appropriate federal agency and receive its final written denial, sent by certified or registered mail, before instituting the action; after six months without final disposition, the claimant may instead elect to treat the inaction as final denial. Exhaustion achieved after filing does not cure the original premature action, even when little litigation has occurred; §2675(a) expressly excepts claims asserted under the Federal Rules by third-party complaint, crossclaim or counterclaim.

**Authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute fixes the order in which the claimant and the courts must proceed. Presenting a claim and awaiting the agency’s disposition are steps preceding institution of the action, not conditions that may be completed while it remains pending. Hallstrom enforces a comparable statutory sequence and rejects an invitation to replace it with an assessment of practical progress in the litigation. McCarthy preserves the primacy of an express congressional command, while excusing a different, judicially imposed exhaustion requirement where Congress supplied none. Administrative settlement and orderly processing also require a rule that does not turn on how quickly the parties litigate after an unlawful filing. McNeil’s lack of counsel does not authorize a different sequence: Haines concerns reading pleadings fairly, not dispensing with statutory procedure. This conclusion addresses the original action alone. The Court does not decide whether later papers commenced another action or how any limitations defense to that action should be resolved.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Hallstrom v. Tillamook County, 493 U.S.20: applied to enforce a congressionally fixed prefiling sequence.
- McCarthy v. Madigan, March 4, 1992: statutory primacy applied; its damages-only BOP exception is distinguished.
- Haines v. Kerner, 404 U.S.519: liberal pleading construction preserved; it does not excuse the prefiling requirement.
