## Neutral event, facts, posture and scope

No. 91-6646. Argued November 4, 1992; decided November 16, 1992. Certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 918 F.2d 848 (November 14, 1990), rehearing denied September 9, 1991. The March 2, 1992 grant was unqualified. The petition’s four questions concern the admission of other-acts evidence to prove intent, the offer to stipulate concerning intent, Rule 403 prejudice balancing, and the claimed circuit disagreement. Separate expert-testimony, evidentiary-sufficiency and sentencing issues considered below were not questions in that petition. Historical reporter locator: 506 U.S. 19.

Hadley was tried on severed federal sexual-abuse counts and convicted on counts VII–IX concerning one complainant, with acquittals on X–XI. He disputed the acts and offered to stipulate concerning intent if the acts were established. The offer was not accepted. Evidence of earlier acts against other children was admitted. The Ninth Circuit sustained its nonpropensity relevance and Rule 403 balance; the Supreme Court’s grant brought the identified other-acts issues here.

## Continuity: published separate positions

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents from the dismissal and would vacate and remand on the preserved Rules 404(b) and 403 issue. He does not treat the defense’s offer concerning intent as an accepted stipulation or as removal of an element. The prosecution must still prove intent, but its actual need for remote other-victim testimony bears on probative value. Huddleston v. United States identifies proper purpose, relevance, balancing and limiting instructions as separate protections; it does not itself command reversal.

For attempted abuse, the question after independent proof of preliminary conduct is whether the conduct was intended to culminate in penetration. Stone regards the charged incident involving the same complainant approximately one month earlier as a closer source of that inference. For the completed act and contact counts, a court must identify the remaining uncertainty about mental state and explain how the other testimony addresses it without relying on propensity. The Ninth Circuit performed a Rule 403 analysis; his objection concerns its adequacy, not its absence. Similarity does not by itself explain the independent value of inflammatory accounts of remote abuse. The limiting instruction matters, but does not settle whether unfair prejudice substantially outweighs probative value. He would require reconsideration under ordinary abuse-of-discretion review and harmless-error review if admission proves erroneous. He would neither order an automatic acquittal nor decide expert testimony, sufficiency, sentencing, or any question under later sex-offense evidence legislation. No other Justice joins. This is a proposed application in dissent, not a rule adopted by the Court.
