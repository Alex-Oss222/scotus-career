## Controlling holdings

#### Member income protection depends on Indian-country residence and source

**Holding and operative rule:** Absent express congressional authorization, a State may not tax a tribal member’s income earned in Indian country when the member resides there; tribal employment alone does not establish that protection. The residence inquiry includes §1151 reservations, dependent Indian communities and allotments whose Indian titles have not been extinguished, including rights-of-way, rather than only an undiminished formal reservation.

**Authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the place where a tribal member lives is an indispensable part of the inquiry. McClanahan protected a member who both lived and earned the relevant income on the reservation. The lower court therefore could not extend that result to every member employee merely because the employer was the Tribe. But Oklahoma’s competing reservation-only rule is also mistaken. Congress’s Indian-country definition includes dependent communities and qualifying allotments, and Potawatomi confirms the significance of federally protected tribal land beyond a formal reservation label. The residence inquiry can consequently be resolved on an applicable allotment without deciding every contested reservation boundary. Oklahoma identifies no express congressional permission for these taxes; its failure to assume jurisdiction under the applicable Public Law 280 procedures supplies none. Yakima concerns a different, expressly authorized fee-land levy. The Court remands for the missing residence predicates and leaves open any independently supported protection of tribal self-government for members living outside Indian country.

**Precedent treatment:**

- McClanahan v. Arizona State Tax Commission, 411 U.S.164: applied with both residence and income-source predicates.
- Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Citizen Band Potawatomi Indian Tribe, 498 U.S.505: applied against a reservation-label-only geography.
- County of Yakima v. Confederated Tribes, January 14, 1992: distinguished; limited land-tax authorization supplies no income-tax authority.

#### The challenged vehicle levies cannot be sustained merely as off-country use charges

**Holding and operative rule:** For tribal members residing in Indian country, the challenged vehicle excise and registration levies fall within the prohibition on state property taxation absent congressional authorization; collecting the same forbidden charges from later purchasers does not save them. Their application to the established residents must be reconsidered on remand; the Court does not decide a genuinely apportioned off-country road-use charge or every tax connected to a vehicle sale.

**Authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the substance of these levies, not their labels, controls. Moe and Colville prohibit the state vehicle property taxes imposed on protected resident tribal members. Oklahoma’s excise is distinct from its sales tax, and the registration levy is imposed in place of personal property taxation. The State has not shown that either assessment measures the amount of travel outside Indian country. Exempting nonresident vehicles further weakens the claim that these are simply charges for using the roads. Requiring a later purchaser to pay the accumulated amounts for the tribal-registration period burdens the same protected ownership and cannot accomplish indirectly what the State cannot do directly. The ruling nevertheless requires the resident-member predicate absent from the lower court’s analysis. It does not transform tribal registration into immunity for every owner, invalidate a demonstrated and properly tailored road-use charge, or decide the different position of nonmembers.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Moe v. Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes, 425 U.S.463: applied to member vehicle property taxation.
- Washington v. Confederated Tribes of the Colville Indian Reservation, 447 U.S.134: applied to the substance of the vehicle levies; genuinely tailored off-country use charges remain distinct.
