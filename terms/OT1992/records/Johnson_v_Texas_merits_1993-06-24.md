**Court and judgment under review:** Texas Court of Criminal Appeals, 773 S.W.2d 322: capital conviction and death sentence affirmed; mitigation claim addressed on rehearing.

**Questions and relief:** Whether the former special issues permitted meaningful mitigating effect for Johnson’s youth-related reduced culpability, not merely its predictive relevance to future danger. Johnson seeks reversal of his death sentence and a constitutionally adequate sentencing proceeding. Texas seeks affirmance.

**Material facts and record limits:** Johnson was nineteen at the time of the murder. The penalty jury answered whether his conduct was deliberate with a reasonable expectation that death would result and whether he probably would commit future criminal violence. Jurors were told to consider all aggravating and mitigating evidence in answering those issues. His father described his troubled adolescence, substance use, and family losses. The questions did not separately ask whether his youth reduced culpability sufficiently to warrant a sentence less than death even if both prescribed answers were yes.

## Controlling holdings

#### Youth’s independent culpability significance needs an effective sentencing route

**Holding and operative rule:** Considering the charge, evidence and trial as a whole, there is a reasonable likelihood that the special issues prevented Johnson’s jury from giving effect to his youth-related reduced culpability when it truthfully found deliberateness and future danger. Under Graham, Eddings, Penry and Boyde, admission of the evidence and partial relevance to future danger do not supply the missing route; mere possibility of a restrictive reading would be insufficient.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, Youth’s independent culpability significance needs an effective sentencing route portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that its decision in Graham governs this application. Eddings recognizes that immaturity can reduce culpability independently of a prediction about later conduct. Johnson was nineteen, and his father’s account of troubled adolescence, substance use and family losses supplies a concrete mitigating argument. Texas correctly observes that youth can be transient and can reduce a prediction of future violence. But the general instruction to consider mitigating evidence remained tied to answering deliberateness and future-danger questions; it did not authorize a lesser sentence when both answers remained yes. Penry distinguishes admitting evidence from allowing its mitigating force, and Boyde requires a reasonable likelihood of preclusion in the charge and trial as a whole. That standard is satisfied, rather than merely conceivably implicated, on this record. Jurek’s conditional general approval remains, but cannot displace Graham’s effective-consideration rule. No categorical age prohibition, favorable evidentiary weight or unrestricted mercy instruction is required.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993 — independent youth-culpability holding applied as binding merits law.
- Eddings, 455 U.S.104 — immaturity’s independent culpability significance applied.
- Lockett, 438 U.S.586 — material mitigation cannot be legally excluded from effect.
