## Controlling holdings

#### The appellate court could reach the future-effects claim

**Holding and operative rule:** The attempted future-effects proof and related appellate objections gave the court of appeals a sufficient record-based basis to treat future health risk as part of this pro se litigation. That conclusion acknowledges ambiguity and does not authorize new claims on appeal or reopen the independent immediate-symptom and damages-immunity rulings.

**Authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, The appellate court could reach the future-effects claim portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court rejects the officials’ contention that the court of appeals invented a wholly new claim. The record is not perfectly clear, and the Court does not manufacture an express amended pleading. McKinney sought to establish actual and potential effects of the smoke exposure, encountered exclusion of potential-effects evidence, and challenged the relevant rulings on appeal. That course gives a sufficient basis for the appellate court’s reading of the case. The seriousness of custodial safety alone would not cure a failure to present a claim; it is the attempted proof and related objections that matter here. The returned proceeding remains narrower than the entire original suit. The directed verdict concerning deliberate indifference to existing symptoms and the separate protection against damages were left intact below and are not reopened. Affirming this procedural scope permits consideration of the future-risk theory under governing substantive law, without deciding that the current facts establish either a constitutional violation or a continuing need for an injunction.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Ordinary claim-scope and appellate-review principles — applied to the actual attempted proof; no new exception for important claims.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S.294 — governing substantive requirements retained on the returned claim.

#### Preventive protection does not require existing disease

**Holding and operative rule:** A prisoner may establish an Eighth Amendment violation by proving deliberate indifference to involuntary exposure posing an unreasonable risk of serious future harm. He must prove both the objective gravity of the exposure—including seriousness, likelihood and contemporary society’s unwillingness to tolerate the risk—and the separate subjective requirement under Wilson, assessed against current conditions.

**Authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, Preventive protection does not require existing disease portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the State’s duty of custodial safety is not postponed until preventable disease develops. Estelle requires protection against serious medical neglect, while Hutto demonstrates that unsafe confinement conditions can require correction before their full harm occurs. Rhodes supplies the objective seriousness requirement, and Wilson separately requires deliberate indifference. Scientific evidence of risk is necessary to the factual inquiry but is not the entire constitutional standard: McKinney must also establish that society does not tolerate forcing unwilling persons to face this danger. The officials’ proposed categorical present-injury requirement would prevent relief precisely while prevention may still work. That does not make every exposure or negligent response unconstitutional. Transfer to a different institution and the new smoking policy may change exposure, culpability and prospective need; actual implementation must be examined. The Court affirms an opportunity to prove the claim, not a finding of violation, a damages entitlement, or a judicial command to ban smoking throughout Nevada’s prisons.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Estelle, 429 U.S.97 — serious custodial needs and culpability retained.
- Hutto, 437 U.S.678 — protection from unsafe conditions applied.
- Rhodes, 452 U.S.337 — objective seriousness retained.
- Wilson, 501 U.S.294 — deliberate indifference remains a separate requirement.
- DeShaney, 489 U.S.189 — custodial safety obligation supplies context.
- Hudson, February 25, 1992 — nontrivial malicious beating rule remains distinct; no universal conditions standard imported.
