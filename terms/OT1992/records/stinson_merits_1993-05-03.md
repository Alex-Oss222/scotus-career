## Controlling holdings

#### Consistent interpretive commentary is authoritative

**Holding and operative rule:** Guidelines commentary interpreting or explaining a guideline is authoritative unless it violates the Constitution or a federal statute, or is inconsistent with, or a plainly erroneous reading of, that guideline. Commentary cannot amend contrary guideline text; a prior judicial interpretation does not alone defeat a later, permissible Commission interpretation.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Commission’s permissible explanation of its guideline carries more than advisory force. Mistretta establishes the Commission’s delegated role in the sentencing system. Section3553(b) expressly gives commentary a role in identifying matters the Commission considered, and section1B1.7 explains its interpretive function. Williams confirms the authority of a prohibitory departure policy within that structure; the present decision addresses interpretive commentary more broadly. Seminole Rock provides the appropriate analogy because the Commission explains a rule it promulgated. The absence of congressional review for commentary amendments does not make every explanation optional, but it does preserve the boundary between interpretation and amendment. A contrary statute, constitutional command or guideline controls, and a plainly erroneous reading receives no authority. The Eleventh Circuit’s earlier construction is therefore not a sufficient reason to disregard a later consistent explanation. This holding concerns interpretive and explanatory commentary; it does not allow the Commission to change binding text through an inconsistent note or decide whether every later explanation applies to every earlier sentence.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Mistretta v. United States, 488 U.S.361: applies the delegated institutional framework.
- Bowles v. Seminole Rock & Sand Co., 325 U.S.410: extends consistency-limited interpretive authority to Guidelines commentary.
- Williams v. United States, March9 1992, simulated: preserves prohibitory-policy authority and extends analysis to interpretive commentary without altering §3742(f) review.

#### The possession exclusion is a permissible interpretation, with temporal application reserved

**Holding and operative rule:** Amendment433’s exclusion of unlawful firearm possession by a felon is a permissible interpretation of the crime-of-violence guideline and controls defendants to whom it applies. The appellate judgment sustaining a categorical possession rule is vacated; the governing-version and temporal objection remains for remand, together with properly presented independent sentencing grounds and the consequences of any guideline error.

**Authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Commission’s possession exclusion fits within the guideline it explains. The crime-of-violence definition includes a force clause and a serious-potential-risk clause, with listed offenses informing the latter. Possession by a prohibited person does not necessarily involve force, and treating possession alone as outside the definition is not an inconsistent or plainly erroneous construction of that risk language. Under the interpretive-authority rule adopted today, the appellate court could not preserve its categorical contrary construction merely because it preceded the commentary. That error requires vacating its judgment. It does not establish that every defendant sentenced earlier receives the benefit of every later amendment. The Government’s distinct temporal argument was not resolved below and lies beyond the authority question reviewed, so the lower court must address it in identifying the applicable rule. Williams continues to govern the remedial consequences of an incorrect guideline application. The Court orders neither a specified replacement sentence nor reopening of final cases, and does not treat the other counts as automatically sufficient alternative predicates.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Seminole Rock, 325 U.S.410: applies the consistency test to Amendment433.
- Williams, March9 1992, simulated: preserves statutory error and remand analysis; no automatic replacement sentence.

## Material published noncontrolling positions

Stone concurs in the judgment, independently reading the applicable guideline to exclude felon possession alone. He preserves Williams’ rule for departure policy statements and the statutory role of commentary in assessing considered departure factors. Those rules do not establish mandatory deference to every later interpretation of a classification provision. Guidelines undergo congressional review under §994(p), while commentary changes do not; judicial interpretation must remain independently justified. Under a Skidmore-type approach the Commission’s expertise, consistency, text and reasoning receive substantial weight. Stone does not limit crime of violence to offenses with force elements: the serious-risk clause must be read with burglary of a dwelling, arson, extortion and use of explosives. Possession alone does not establish comparable inherent risk merely because a prohibited possessor might later misuse the firearm. That legal error, rather than a request simply to reconsider commentary already considered, supports vacatur. An explanation can illuminate what original text meant without retroactively creating a rule; a substantive later change must satisfy its own temporal conditions. The lower court must identify the governing version, address the preserved temporal argument and any properly supported alternative ground, and apply Williams to the error’s consequences. No particular sentence or final-case reopening follows. No associate joins this writing; its persuasive-weight framework is noncontrolling.
