Question: Whether offering a firearm as consideration for drugs constitutes use during and in relation to drug trafficking under the charged version of §924(c)(1).

## Controlling holdings

#### Offering a firearm in exchange for drugs is use within the charged statute

**Holding and operative rule:** Offering a firearm as consideration in a drug exchange constitutes using the firearm under the applicable §924(c)(1), even though the firearm is employed as merchandise rather than fired, brandished or otherwise employed as a weapon. The separate during-and-in-relation-to requirement must also be met; the statute is not satisfied by possession alone under this holding.

**Authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a firearm is used when it is deliberately employed as the consideration offered to obtain drugs. Perrin directs attention to the ordinary meaning of the enacted word, and Timbers requires the surrounding statute to inform that reading. Use is not limited to the object’s most familiar purpose, and §924(d)’s treatment of firearm transactions confirms that Congress can describe employment in exchange as firearm use. That contextual confirmation does not import the forfeiture provision’s distinct elements or burden of proof. The dissent’s weapon-only reading would add a limitation absent from this criminal provision. The separate relation-to requirement supplies an additional limit rather than a reason to narrow use to discharge or threats. Bass and Moskal preserve lenity after genuine unresolved ambiguity; the Court finds none after these ordinary tools. This ruling concerns the offered exchange proved here, not uncharged carrying, mere possession or later statutory amendments.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Perrin v. United States, 444 U.S.37: ordinary meaning applied.
- United Savings Association v. Timbers of Inwood Forest, 484 U.S.365: whole-statute context applied.
- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S.336, and Moskal v. United States, 498 U.S.103: residual lenity preserved; no remaining ambiguity found.
- Deal v. United States, May17,1993: conviction sequence remains distinct; it does not decide use.

#### The offered firearm has the required connection to the trafficking crime

**Holding and operative rule:** The firearm must have a purpose or effect with respect to the trafficking crime, rather than being present by accident or coincidence; at minimum, it must facilitate or have the potential to facilitate that crime. Offering the firearm as the consideration for the charged drug transaction establishes that relationship here without deciding that facilitation alone is sufficient in every other setting.

**Authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the gun’s role in this transaction was direct. It was the item offered to procure the cocaine, not an object coincidentally present while a separate offense occurred. The phrase in relation to prevents an unrelated firearm use from triggering the mandatory penalty, and the transaction here meets that limit because the proposed exchange itself advances the charged trafficking. The statutory inquiry remains separate from whether the conduct counts as use; neither requirement may swallow the other. Stewart and Long describe the facilitative relationship as a useful minimum, but the Court need not decide its outer boundary when the firearm supplies the proposed payment. Nor does the officer’s failure to complete the barter defeat the nexus to the conspiracy and attempt actually charged. Blackmun’s concurrence confirms that no universal facilitation-only sufficiency rule is adopted. The decision does not sustain the conviction through carrying, which the indictment did not charge.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Stewart, 779 F.2d538: facilitation formulation used as a minimum connection, not a universal sufficiency rule.
- United States v. Long, 905 F.2d1572: facilitative relationship formulation applied within the proved exchange setting.

## Continuity and published noncontrolling positions

**Concurrence — Blackmun; no other joins:** Blackmun joins the Court’s construction and its application to the offered exchange. He emphasizes that this firearm was tendered as the consideration for the cocaine, so its relationship to the charged trafficking was established without deciding every marginal form of facilitation. He does not understand the opinion’s minimum formulation to make facilitation alone sufficient in every case. That reservation preserves the separate relation-to element and leaves its outer boundary open; it does not withhold a vote from the result or from the Court’s rule for this transaction.

**Dissent — Scalia; joined by Stevens, Souter:** Scalia, joined by Stevens and Souter, reads the entire phrase in its ordinary setting: using a firearm means employing it as a weapon, not merely doing something useful with it. The argument does not restrict weapon use to firing; threats or striking can be weapon employment. The broader transaction language in the forfeiture provision addresses different objects and does not establish the criminal element in §924(c). Treating the gun as merchandise lacks the contextual weapon use charged here, even if trading it assists the drug transaction. The separate relation-to requirement cannot cure that missing element. The dissent would reverse the firearm-use conviction and cannot sustain it on carrying, which was not charged. It does not control.

**Dissent — Stone-Zsela; no other joins:** Stone-Zsela accepts the force of the Government’s instrumental-use reading but finds that the criminal phrase in context addresses employment as a weapon. Perrin and Timbers require contextual ordinary meaning; Bass and Moskal call for lenity only after the ordinary tools leave genuine uncertainty. The independent connection to trafficking does not answer whether the gun was used in the charged sense. Threats, intimidation or striking may qualify without firing, whereas merchandise-only barter does not. He would remove the firearm count and its thirty-year mandatory term and remand for the resulting sentence correction. The separate drug convictions remain, and the remand would not authorize conviction for uncharged carrying or a new trial to cure the rejected legal theory. This position is noncontrolling.
