## Controlling holdings

#### An applied federal rule governs still-open direct review

**Holding and operative rule:** When this Court applies a federal rule to the parties before it, that rule governs all still-open direct-review cases concerning events within its substantive scope, whether those events predate or postdate announcement. A State cannot use selective prospectivity to withhold the rule; Chevron Oil is displaced to the extent it permits that practice, without reopening final judgments or deciding every conceivable prospective disposition.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, An applied federal rule governs still-open direct review portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that similarly situated pending litigants receive the same governing federal law. Griffith rejects selective application on criminal direct review, and Beam supplies the civil foundation when the Court already applied the rule to its own litigants. Davis applied federal nondiscrimination law to the pension taxation before it; Michigan’s willingness to refund did not reserve the rule’s temporal operation. Virginia therefore cannot use Chevron Oil to preserve the same discrimination for other pending claimants. Reliance and fiscal disruption are substantial practical concerns, but they cannot make the substantive federal rule vary among contemporaneous open cases. The state refund interpretation here depends on the rejected prospectivity premise and is not an independent state ground insulating that error. The Court distinguishes finality and remedy from choice of governing law. It does not reopen closed judgments, calculate refunds, or decide a hypothetical case in which the Court expressly reserves application to the parties before it.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Davis, 489 U.S.803 — nondiscrimination rule applied to pending claims.
- Griffith, 479 U.S.314 — equal direct-review application extended to this civil setting.
- Beam, 501 U.S.529 — its rejection of selective application clarified through a direct majority rule.
- Chevron Oil, 404 U.S.97 — displaced only insofar as it permits selective prospectivity after application to the original litigants.

#### Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate

**Holding and operative rule:** The state courts must apply Davis and determine the correction state law and federal due process require. If the State compelled payment before an adequate opportunity to contest the tax, meaningful backward-looking relief is required; lawful refunds, retroactive nondiscriminatory assessments or a combination may suffice, while a meaningful prepayment opportunity presents a different remedial setting.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Retroactivity and the lawful tax remedy are separate portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that application of the federal rule does not mechanically select a refund amount. McKesson requires meaningful backward-looking correction when a State collects first and provides no adequate prepayment opportunity to challenge an unconstitutional discriminatory tax. The State may have more than one lawful means of correcting the discrimination, including refunds, constitutionally permissible assessment of previously favored taxpayers, or a combination. Davis similarly distinguishes the nondiscrimination command from the appropriate corrective mechanism. The present record requires the state courts to examine their procedures and actual remedial authority rather than assume either an automatic full refund or unconditional retention of the taxes. Genuine procedural bars and remedial considerations may operate consistently with federal law, but they cannot merely reintroduce selective prospectivity under another name. The Court does not impose liability on absent taxpayers without lawful process or invent a state appropriation. It reverses the federal-law premise and remands the remedy for determination under the proper sources.

**Precedent treatment:**

- McKesson, 496 U.S.18 — pay-first systems require meaningful backward-looking relief; alternatives preserved.
- Davis, 489 U.S.803 — correction of unequal taxation distinguished from a particular refund formula.
- Beam, 501 U.S.529 — procedural and remedial questions kept distinct from temporal governing law.
