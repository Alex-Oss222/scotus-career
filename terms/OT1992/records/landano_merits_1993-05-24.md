## Controlling holdings

#### Confidentiality requires an assurance or a supported inference, not an FBI label

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Exemption7(D), a source is confidential when the information was furnished under an express assurance or circumstances supporting a reasonable inference of confidentiality; an assurance of absolute secrecy is unnecessary. The Government bears the burden and cannot presume every FBI criminal-investigation source confidential, but may rely on a supported, rebuttable inference tied to particular circumstances or a properly defined category, including the crime and the source’s relationship to it.

**Authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory word confidential describes the circumstances of a source’s cooperation, not simply the identity of the investigating agency. A person may expect restricted law-enforcement sharing while reasonably expecting that the information will not become publicly available. The FBI’s sources nevertheless range too widely to support one inference for everyone. Reporters Committee approved categorical treatment under a different privacy provision and does not remove the factual predicate in Exemption7(D). At the same time, requiring separate direct testimony for every source would disregard circumstances that themselves support the inference. Payment to an informant, the violence of a crime, or a witness’s relationship to it may support an appropriately confined category when the agency explains the connection. The requester remains free to challenge that inference with nonprotected evidence. The Court reserves whether later public testimony waives protection and does not decide the prisoner’s guilt, Brady claim or entitlement to every withheld page.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Department of Justice v. Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, 489 U.S.749: distinguished; privacy categories do not establish every source’s confidentiality.
- John Doe Agency v. John Doe Corp., 493 U.S.146: compilation analysis preserved; it does not eliminate the confidentiality requirement.
- Department of State v. Ray, December16,1991: distinct Exemption6 privacy holding preserved.

#### Source identity and criminal-source information receive distinct statutory protection

**Holding and operative rule:** Exemption7(D) protects identity when law-enforcement disclosure could reasonably be expected to reveal a confidential source, including an agency, authority or private institution furnishing information confidentially; it also protects information furnished by a confidential source in a criminal investigation conducted by a criminal-law-enforcement authority. The statute separately covers a lawful national-security intelligence investigation; reasonably segregable nonexempt material remains subject to release, but redacting identity alone does not remove protection for covered source information.

**Authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the two statutory protections must be applied according to their different objects. One prevents disclosure that could reasonably reveal the confidential source; the other protects information furnished by such a source in the specified criminal-investigation setting. Landano therefore cannot obtain protected source information merely by proposing deletion of the source’s name. Nor can the FBI withhold an entire file simply because it contains material from one protected source: confidentiality must first be established and segregation must address material outside the exemption. John Doe Agency requires attention to the statutory law-enforcement compilation requirement rather than the original location of the paper, while Rose preserves FOIA’s disclosure duty for nonexempt material. The present remand concerns FBI criminal-investigation records, not the scope of the separate national-security clause. Protected submissions and in-camera examination may assist the court, but no universal requirement to use either procedure is imposed.

**Precedent treatment:**

- John Doe Agency v. John Doe Corp., 493 U.S.146: statutory compilation requirement applied.
- Department of Air Force v. Rose, 425 U.S.352: nonexempt segregable disclosure preserved, without overriding protected source information.
