## Controlling holdings

#### The individual-determination channel does not foreclose all rule challenges

**Holding and operative rule:** Section1255a(f) channels review of individual legalization determinations but does not categorically eliminate district-court review of a challenge to generally applicable regulations where no individual determination supplies an effective review route. A regulatory label alone establishes neither ripeness nor a right to district-court reconsideration of an individual denial.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, The individual-determination channel does not foreclose all rule challenges portion; Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the object of the challenge matters to the statutory channel. McNary distinguishes a systemic challenge from review of an individual application determination under a related legalization statute. Section1255a(f) likewise does not clearly prohibit every challenge to a regulation. But an applicant whose claim depends on a formal denial ordinarily must follow the limited administrative and judicial path Congress specified, including review connected to a deportation order. A plaintiff cannot avoid that structure merely by calling an individual eligibility dispute a policy challenge. Conversely, refusal to accept papers can prevent an application record and formal denial from ever arising. Reading that refusal as necessarily foreclosing all review would require an indication Congress has not supplied. Michigan Academy’s presumption favors a review-preserving construction in that circumstance; it does not prove the facts of any claimant’s case. The Court leaves eligibility, substantive validity of the challenged rules and the ultimate remedy outside this threshold disposition.

**Precedent treatment:**

- McNary, 498 U.S.479 — systemic-review distinction applied; individual denials remain distinct.
- Bowen v. Michigan Academy, 476 U.S.667 — presumption of review applied, not converted into a factual burden.
- Section1255a(f)(2) — late-filing denial bar preserved; no general permission to evade it by never filing.

#### A concrete application barrier must be established

**Holding and operative rule:** An actual refusal to accept a timely application under the challenged rule supplies concrete application sufficient for a ripe district-court challenge where front-desking blocks the individual-review route. Mere awareness of an adverse rule is insufficient on this record; further facts may establish that the front-desking policy substantially caused a comparable inability to apply even without a personal front-desk rejection.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, A concrete application barrier must be established portion; Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a benefits regulation does not necessarily injure every person who reads it. Abbott Laboratories asks whether the controversy is fit for decision and what hardship postponement creates. Here some persons might not otherwise qualify or might have used designated assistance that bypassed front-desking. The existing record does not identify particular class members actually refused acceptance with sufficient precision. Those who were refused encountered the regulation directly and lost the administrative record needed for ordinary review. The Government cannot create that absence and then insist on a formal denial as the only judicial doorway. The Court nevertheless does not equate every decision not to apply with a government refusal. Further evidence might show that the policy substantially caused nonapplication in a sufficiently concrete way; that possibility remains open. The district courts must establish the affected claimants and adequate class representation before choosing relief. No present finding establishes legalization eligibility for every class member.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Abbott Laboratories, 387 U.S.136 — fitness and hardship applied to concrete effects.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S.871 — concrete application and immediately operative burdens distinguished.
- McNary, 498 U.S.479 — effective-review concern applied to refused filings.

## Published separate writings and continuity

O’Connor concurs in the judgment. She regards the request to extend an already expired application period as ripe: closure has occurred, and its effect is not contingent. But §1255a(a)(1)(A) requires a timely application. Promulgating an erroneous benefits regulation, without more, is not the affirmative misconduct needed to justify this broad equitable extension. Irwin, Hansen and Pangilinan preserve statutory conditions. She agrees that actual front-desking may support processing a timely application and further inquiry; she does not adopt the Court’s ripeness analysis or a categorical total-review bar.

Stevens, joined by White and Blackmun, dissents. The rules concretely discouraged eligible persons from applying during a finite statutory opportunity. Under Abbott and McNary, the policy challenges and class relief should not depend on proving an individual futile visit to the front desk. They would preserve an effective opportunity to apply while leaving individual eligibility for administrative determination.
