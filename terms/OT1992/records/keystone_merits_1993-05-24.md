## Event and chronology

Question: Whether an employer’s transfer of unencumbered property in satisfaction of pension-funding obligations is a prohibited sale or exchange under §4975(c)(1)(A).

Keystone transferred unencumbered truck terminals and real property to its pension trust and credited their values against minimum funding obligations. It treated the transfers as sales or exchanges for income-tax purposes. The Commissioner assessed excise-tax deficiencies; the Tax Court and Fifth Circuit rejected the sale-or-exchange classification. The stated property values are not contested here. Classification, rather than a new valuation or independently adjudicated exemption, is the question reviewed.

## Controlling holdings

#### The established tax meaning includes property exchanged for release of a funding duty

**Holding and operative rule:** In §4975(c)(1)(A), the established tax meaning of sale or exchange includes an employer’s transfer of property in satisfaction of an enforceable pension-funding obligation. An unencumbered transfer meeting that condition is not excluded merely because it is called a contribution.

**Authority:** Blackmun’s Part III-A; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress used a familiar tax expression against an established legal background. Hammel recognizes that sale or exchange can encompass a disposition discharging an obligation, even when the transaction does not resemble a voluntary cash sale. Sorenson supports reading repeated tax language consistently where the statutory setting warrants it. Keystone receives release from its funding obligation in exchange for the property transferred, and indeed treated the disposition as a sale or exchange in reporting its own income-tax consequences. Atlantic Cleaners warns against mechanically transferring a meaning between different settings, but the prohibited-transaction provision’s breadth supplies no reason to exclude this exchange. The dissent’s concern about voluntary contributions does not alter the consideration furnished when an existing obligation is discharged. This rationale independently supports classification of the reviewed transfers. It does not decide every gratuitous contribution, settle an unpresented exemption, or calculate the remaining excise-tax consequences.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Helvering v. Hammel, 311 U.S.504: settled tax treatment of obligation-discharging transfers applied.
- Sorenson v. Secretary of Treasury, 475 U.S.851: consistent tax-language construction applied.
- Atlantic Cleaners & Dyers, Inc. v. United States, 286 U.S.427: contextual qualification preserved; it does not justify a different meaning here.
- Commissioner v. Lester, 366 U.S.299: internal consistency of tax language supports the established-meaning rationale.
- Albernaz v. United States, 450 U.S.333: prior settled construction informs the meaning Congress selected.

#### Discharge supplies consideration even without importing a technical tax meaning

**Holding and operative rule:** Independently of a settled technical tax meaning, transferring property to discharge an employer’s pension-funding duty is an exchange under §4975(c)(1)(A): the plan receives property and the employer receives release from its obligation. Section4975(f)(3)’s inclusion of property subject to a mortgage or similar lien assumed by the plan or placed by a disqualified person within ten years does not exclude other exchanges supported by consideration.

**Authority:** Blackmun’s Parts III-C and III-D; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the actual legal effects of this transfer suffice even without relying on a specialized tax usage. The plan obtains property; the employer obtains satisfaction of a duty it otherwise must perform. Those reciprocal legal benefits constitute an exchange under the ordinary meaning of the enacted words. Keystone’s reading of the encumbrance provision mistakes an express inclusion for an exhaustive definition. Section4975(f)(3) ensures coverage when a plan assumes a mortgage or similar lien or the disqualified person placed the lien within the stated ten-year period; it does not say that a transfer discharging a separate funding obligation is excluded. The statutory text thus answers the strongest structural objection without relying on policy hostility toward property funding. The Court classifies the transfers presented and leaves the application of any properly available exemption, correction provision and remaining tax requirement to its own conditions. It makes no new finding about the property’s value.

**Precedent treatment:**

- This independently sufficient ground rests directly on §4975(c)(1)(A) and (f)(3); it requires no additional treatment of an earlier decision.

## Continuity and published noncontrolling positions

**Dissent — Stevens; no other joins:** Stevens would affirm because income-tax treatment does not necessarily settle the different prohibited-transaction provision. Both voluntary property contributions and transfers satisfying minimum funding can produce income-tax gain; borrowing that classification selectively gives insufficient weight to ERISA’s context. The statutory emphasis on encumbered transfers and the distinction between genuine plan funding and transactions extracting plan assets support the narrower reading. Trustees remain responsible for deciding whether to accept suitable property, which can benefit a plan rather than threaten it. Atlantic Cleaners cautions that identical words may bear different meanings in different settings. This disagreement concerns statutory classification, not a finding that Keystone’s properties were misvalued, a general exemption for every employer transaction, or an order computing tax. The dissent does not control.

Scalia and Stone withhold the policy portion; their limited joins do not withhold support from the ordinary-exchange holding. No separate opinion supplies another controlling rule.
