## Event and chronology

Gilmore v. Taylor; No. 91-1738. Argued 1993-03-02; decided 1993-06-07. Historical reporter locator: 508 U.S.333. Review of the Seventh Circuit, 954 F.2d 441 (1992), reversing denial of federal habeas relief and directing a conditional writ.

Question: Whether Teague permits collateral relief under Falconer’s rule for the interaction of Illinois murder and voluntary-manslaughter instructions.

Taylor received a murder sentence of thirty-five years after a trial at which he relied on provocation and related mitigation. The charge permitted a murder verdict upon the stated elements without requiring jurors first to resolve the separate manslaughter instructions. Illinois discretionary review ended June 4, 1987, and Taylor did not seek certiorari. The lower court noted that choosing that date or the later expiration of the certiorari period made no difference because Reddick and Falconer came after both. The Court likewise has no need to decide that date question. The Seventh Circuit treated Falconer as an old rule and ordered a writ unless Illinois elected to retry Taylor within 120 days. Reddick held as a matter of state law that the prosecution must disprove the mitigating mental state beyond a reasonable doubt. Falconer instead addresses the federal sequencing defect that could let jurors convict of murder without considering the lesser-offense path. The State concedes error under Falconer but preserves its Teague defense. The separate home-invasion sentence is not reviewed.

## Controlling holdings

#### Falconer announces a new rule for this collateral proceeding

**Holding and operative rule:** Falconer’s specific requirement governing the interaction of these murder and voluntary-manslaughter instructions was not dictated by precedent at Taylor’s finality and is therefore a new rule under Teague. The State’s concession of error under Falconer does not waive its preserved retroactivity objection, and this conclusion does not approve the charge on direct review or exclude all defense-instruction errors from due process.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a general protection against an unfair charge did not dictate Falconer’s particular result at finality. Cupp requires consideration of the whole instruction and trial, but does not settle every relationship between an offense instruction and a state affirmative defense. Patterson and Martin show that the constitutional burden on offense elements does not automatically govern every fact relevant to an affirmative defense. The strongest contrary argument is that these instructions permitted jurors to convict without considering the only meaningful route to manslaughter. That concern may support a constitutional rule; it does not establish that existing precedent compelled this specific rule. The entered Estelle decision preserves reasonable-likelihood review without supplying a finality-era Falconer holding. Graham’s capital-mitigation result rested on its own earlier capital authorities and does not make this noncapital sequencing rule old. Connecticut v. Johnson’s plurality concerns presumptions affecting elemental proof, not this defense sequence. Boyde’s later capital-instruction analysis does not establish that Falconer was dictated at 1987 finality. Judicial disagreement alone is not the test. The State preserved Teague despite conceding Falconer error, and the Court resolves only that threshold.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S.288: finality-era dictated-by-precedent inquiry applied.
- Cupp v. Naughten, 414 U.S.141: whole-charge fairness preserved but not treated as dictating this specific rule.
- Connecticut v. Johnson, 460 U.S.73: plurality discussion of elemental presumptions distinguished; it is not treated as a controlling holding compelling Falconer.
- Boyde v. California, 494 U.S.370: later capital-instruction reasonable-likelihood analysis distinguished; its date does not itself supply a 1987 finality-era source.

- Estelle v. McGuire, December 4, 1991: the entered whole-charge reasonable-likelihood holding preserved; it neither decides Falconer nor supplies a source existing at 1987 finality.
- Graham v. Collins, January 25, 1993: the entered capital-mitigation holding distinguished on its earlier capital authorities.

#### Neither then-existing Teague exception permits this new instructional rule

**Holding and operative rule:** The Falconer rule regulates trial procedure rather than removing conduct or a class of persons from the State’s substantive power to punish, and it is not a watershed procedural rule essential to the fundamental accuracy and fairness of the criminal process. Neither exception recognized by Teague at this event’s date permits its retroactive use to obtain the reviewed habeas relief.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the character of the new rule prevents reliance on either Teague exception. The rule changes how the jury considers a defense within the trial; it does not make Taylor’s conduct constitutionally immune from punishment or exclude a class of persons from the penalty at issue. The substantive exception therefore does not apply. The watershed exception remains part of the governing law, but it is reserved for exceptional procedural changes fundamental to the accuracy and fairness of the criminal process. The possibility of a serious instructional error is not itself enough to place every new instruction rule within that category. The dissent emphasizes that jurors may have missed the only basis for a lesser offense. The majority does not dismiss that concern; it concludes that this specific sequencing rule does not reconstruct the process at the exceptional level Teague requires. No harmless-error ruling is necessary after the retroactivity bar, and the Court does not import any later elimination or narrowing of the exception.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Teague, 489 U.S.288: both the substantive and watershed exceptions applied in their event-date form.
- Sawyer v. Smith, 497 U.S.227: the demanding watershed category applied; importance of a procedural protection alone is insufficient.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: the entered preserved-Doyle Chapman holding remains distinct; harmlessness is not reached here.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana, June 1, 1993: the absence of a valid reasonable-doubt verdict is a different instructional defect and does not bypass Teague in this case.

## Continuity and published noncontrolling positions

Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, joins the Court and emphasizes that Eddings and related capital-sentencing cases do not automatically dictate the sequencing of murder and affirmative-defense instructions in a noncapital trial. Patterson and Martin allow distinctions that the proposed analogy would obscure. Their concurrence adds no holding that every defense-instruction error is constitutionally irrelevant. Blackmun, joined by Stevens, dissents. Cupp’s protection against a fundamentally unfair charge, in their view, reaches instructions that permit jurors to return the greater verdict without evaluating the only basis for a lesser offense. The accused’s admission to the killing cannot fairly be separated from his offered explanation by a misleading route through the charge. They also regard the protection as within Teague’s watershed exception even if the rule is new, because preventing that bypass bears directly on reliable adjudication of the grade of guilt. They would preserve the Seventh Circuit’s conditional writ requiring Illinois to elect retrial within 120 days; that remedy is not the Court’s mandate.

Supreme Court review ends by reversal of the conditional-writ judgment. The grant of relief on Falconer is removed; no retrial, release date, separate sentence determination or new habeas claim is ordered.
