## Event, record and chronology

Delo, Superintendent, Potosi Correctional Center v. Blair, No. A-69; historical reporter locator 509 U.S. 823. October Term 1992. Walter Blair's execution was scheduled in Missouri; he filed a successive federal habeas petition raising a claim of actual innocence, supported by seven affidavits recanting or contradicting trial testimony. The District Court, finding the facts "mirror those in Herrera," nonetheless granted a stay; the Eighth Circuit affirmed the stay and indicated a need for "more detailed study" over approximately five weeks. The State applied to vacate that stay.

## Participation and entering law

Eight Justices participate: Chief Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas. White retired after the June 28, 1993 decisions and does not participate; Ginsburg is not yet seated. Six constitute a quorum; five votes suffice to resolve the application.

The controlling entering law is the simulated Herrera v. Collins, effective January 25, 1993. That decision's controlling explanation holds that even assuming a freestanding actual-innocence claim exists, a petitioner's affidavits must be assessed together with the existing trial evidence, and their "possible probative value does not make [a] submission a persuasive affirmative demonstration of innocence"; it further holds, applying Barefoot and Delo, that "substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted" are required before a stay may issue on a second or successive petition. Stone-Zsela dissented separately in that case: he would recognize the categorical availability of a properly presented capital-innocence claim (preserving exhaustion and then-governing abuse-of-the-writ rules), proposing a merits burden of clear and convincing affirmative proof that the prisoner did not commit the crime — but his dissent itself preserved the stay-specific gatekeeping rule, stating that "[a]ny temporary stay requires an express Barefoot/Delo showing; no indefinite stay follows." That stay standard, not the broader disagreement over the categorical merits question, controls this application.

## Stone's position

Blair's application presents the identical procedural question Stone's own Herrera dissent already answered for stays: a temporary stay requires an express Barefoot/Delo showing of substantial grounds, regardless of one's view on the broader, unresolved question whether a freestanding innocence claim should categorically be recognized. The Supreme Court's own characterization of Blair's papers — indistinguishable from Herrera's own rejected showing — means Blair's affidavits present nothing beyond what Stone's Herrera dissent already treated as insufficient to warrant relief on the merits, and a fortiori insufficient to clear the lower, threshold showing required merely to justify a stay. Stone therefore joins the per curiam vacating the stay. He writes separately, in a brief individual statement, to note that this vacatur is fully consistent with his Herrera dissent: recognizing the category of claim in principle does not relax the express substantial-grounds showing his own opinion requires before any stay may issue, and no such showing is made on this record. His statement does not readjudicate Herrera, does not foreclose the categorical question for a petitioner who does make the required showing, and does not itself find the affidavits false.

## Law of the decision

### A temporary stay requires an express Barefoot/Delo showing

**Holding and operative rule:** It is an abuse of discretion for a federal court to maintain a stay of execution on a second or successive habeas petition raising claims that are, for all relevant purposes, indistinguishable from those already rejected as insufficient in Herrera v. Collins. No substantial grounds upon which relief might be granted have been shown on this record.

**Authority:** Per curiam opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas; five participating Justices adopt this application of the Herrera/Barefoot/Delo stay standard.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the District Court's own finding — that the facts here mirror those in Herrera — forecloses the stay rather than justifying it, because Herrera's own affidavits, considered together with the existing trial evidence, were held insufficient to make a persuasive affirmative demonstration of innocence. The Eighth Circuit's stated need for "more detailed study" does not identify any respect in which Blair's showing exceeds what Herrera's own papers presented and what this Court already found wanting. Barefoot and Delo require substantial grounds before a stay may issue on a successive petition; an indistinguishable showing cannot supply what an already-rejected showing lacked. The Court does not find the affidavits false, does not resolve the categorical availability of a freestanding innocence claim on a stronger record, and does not disturb any other pending proceeding.

**Precedent treatment:**
- Herrera v. Collins (simulated, effective January 25, 1993): applied; its controlling explanation that an indistinguishable affidavit showing fails to make a persuasive affirmative demonstration of innocence governs this application directly.
- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880: applied for the substantial-grounds threshold governing a stay pending appellate or collateral review.
- Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320: applied, as in Herrera itself, to the substantial-grounds requirement on a second or successive federal petition.

### Stone-Zsela's separate statement

**Holding and operative rule:** Recognizing, as a categorical matter, that a properly presented and sufficiently supported capital-innocence claim may warrant relief does not relax the independent, express showing required before any stay may issue; that stay-specific showing is absent here for the same reason it was absent in Herrera.

**Authority:** Separate statement of Stone-Zsela, unjoined, concurring in the judgment vacating the stay.

**Controlling explanation:** Stone's Herrera dissent already reserved the stay question from the broader categorical disagreement: "[a]ny temporary stay requires an express Barefoot/Delo showing; no indefinite stay follows." Because Blair's papers are, on the Court's own uncontested characterization, indistinguishable from Herrera's own insufficient showing, they fail that express showing regardless of which side of the categorical dispute a Justice occupies. This statement does not represent a change in Stone's Herrera position and does not foreclose a stay, or eventual relief, on a materially stronger future record.

### Souter's and the dissent's separate positions

Justice Souter states he would deny the application to vacate, preferring to allow the Eighth Circuit's contemplated further study to proceed before the stay is disturbed; he does not join Blackmun and Stevens's dissent. Justices Blackmun and Stevens dissent, consistent with their Herrera dissent's more receptive view of successive-petition innocence claims, and would deny the application and preserve the stay pending the Eighth Circuit's review. No opinion resolves the merits of Blair's affidavits; the disagreement concerns only whether the existing showing and the Eighth Circuit's stated intention to study it further meet the stay threshold.
