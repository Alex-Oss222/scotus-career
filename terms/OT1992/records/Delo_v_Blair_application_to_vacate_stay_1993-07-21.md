## Event, record and chronology

Delo, Superintendent, Potosi Correctional Center v. Blair, No. A-69; historical reporter locator 509 U.S. 823. October Term 1992. Walter Blair's execution was scheduled in Missouri; he filed a successive federal habeas petition raising a claim of actual innocence, supported by seven affidavits recanting or contradicting trial testimony. The District Court, finding the facts "mirror those in Herrera," nonetheless granted a stay; the Eighth Circuit affirmed the stay and indicated a need for "more detailed study" over approximately five weeks. The State applied to vacate that stay.

**Stage before:** Resolved in version 1.0 as a 5–3 grant of the application; that resolution is withdrawn for the reasoning defect described above, not for any change in the underlying sources. **Stage after:** The application to vacate is denied by an equally divided Court. The Eighth Circuit's stay remains in effect, and its announced further study of the record may proceed below. No other claim, execution order, or custody disposition is entered by this Court.

**Effective-date insertion:** The event remains at its originally planned position, July 21, 1993, within the already-processed chunk 11 range, before DeBoer v. DeBoer (July 26, 1993). No later chunk 11 event depends on Blair's outcome.

## Participation and entering law

Eight Justices participate: Chief Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas. White retired after the June 28, 1993 decisions and does not participate; Ginsburg is not yet seated. Six constitute a quorum; five votes are required to grant the State's application. Four votes are not enough; the application fails on a 4–4 division, and the status quo (the existing stay) persists, exactly as an equally divided Court's judgment produces no disturbance of the order under review.

The controlling entering law is the simulated Herrera v. Collins, effective January 25, 1993. Its **judgment** affirmed the Fifth Circuit 5–4 (White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas affirming; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter dissenting), holding that Herrera's own affidavits, considered with the trial evidence, did not warrant federal relief and did not meet the Barefoot/Delo substantial-grounds threshold for a stay on that submission. **Stone-Zsela dissented from that judgment.** He did not join the majority's finding that Herrera's showing was insufficient; he voted to vacate the Fifth Circuit's categorical exclusion of the claim and to remand for exhaustion and abuse-of-the-writ analysis and, where material, Townsend/Keeney factual development, under his own proposed clear-and-convincing-affirmative-proof merits standard. He wrote that "[a]llegations provisionally credited must be capable of satisfying that burden before factual development becomes material" and that "[a]ny temporary stay requires an express Barefoot/Delo showing; no indefinite stay follows" — a reservation against automatic or indefinite stays, not a finding that Herrera's own affidavits, or affidavits of that general kind, fail the threshold. Stone's dissent sided with Blackmun, Stevens, and Souter in the remand camp, differing from them only in the merits standard he would apply on remand.

## Stone's position

Blair's application presents claims the Court itself characterizes as indistinguishable from Herrera's own. Stone's already-committed Herrera position is not that this kind of showing is too weak to warrant further proceedings — it is the opposite: he dissented from the Herrera majority precisely because he thought the Fifth Circuit's categorical exclusion of such a claim, without exhaustion/abuse analysis and possible factual development, was wrong. The Eighth Circuit's stay here, coupled with its stated intention to conduct "more detailed study" of the seven affidavits, is materially the same kind of process Stone wanted for Herrera's claim rather than summary rejection. Vacating that stay now, before the Eighth Circuit completes the examination it has already committed to, would cut off exactly the process Stone's Herrera dissent sought. Applying his own position consistently, Stone would deny the State's application to vacate and allow the Eighth Circuit's study to proceed, subject to his own reservation that any continuation of relief must eventually rest on an express Barefoot/Delo showing rather than become an indefinite stay. This is not a new position invented for Blair; it is his existing Herrera dissent applied to a claim the Court itself says is indistinguishable from Herrera's.

## Public action, judgment and opinion

By an equally divided Court, 4–4, the application to vacate the Eighth Circuit's stay of execution is denied. No opinion commands a majority; each side states its own view. The denial has no precedential effect, in keeping with the ordinary rule that an equally divided Court's disposition decides only the case before it.

## Legal effect of the disposition (no controlling holding)

### An equally divided Court leaves the existing stay in place

**Legal effect, not a holding:** Where the Court divides 4–4 on an application to vacate a lower court's stay of execution, the application fails for want of the five votes required to grant it, and the stay under review remains in effect. This produces no precedent and decides nothing beyond the disposition of this application; it is not a controlling proposition adopted by any coalition.

**Basis:** No opinion for the Court; this is the ordinary legal consequence of an equally divided vote.

**Explanation:** A movant seeking to disturb an existing order must secure a majority to do so; a tie is not a majority. The Eighth Circuit's stay, and its announced intention to examine the seven affidavits, the State's responses, and the district court's reasoning in greater detail, therefore continues without alteration by this Court. Nothing in this disposition finds the affidavits credible, incredible, sufficient, or insufficient; it leaves that assessment to the ordinary course of the proceedings below.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Herrera v. Collins (simulated, effective January 25, 1993): its controlling insufficiency holding governs Herrera's own record and commanded five votes there; it does not itself resolve Blair's application, because Stone-Zsela's vote here is not available to the four Justices who would extend Herrera's result, and no other Justice's vote has changed.
- Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880, and Delo v. Stokes, 495 U.S. 320: remain the governing substantial-grounds standard for a stay on a successive petition; this disposition does not relax or extend that standard, and does not decide whether Blair's showing would satisfy it on a developed record.

### Stone-Zsela's separate statement

**Position, not a holding:** A Justice's already-committed dissent in a materially indistinguishable case governs his own vote in a later application presenting the same kind of claim, absent a reason to depart from it; here, no such reason is presented, and the Eighth Circuit's own announced process for developing the record is consistent with, not contrary to, that dissent. This is Stone's individual, unjoined position, not a proposition this Court adopts.

**Source:** Separate statement of Stone-Zsela, unjoined, stating he would deny the application.

**Reasoning:** In Herrera, Stone would have vacated the categorical exclusion of a claim of this kind and remanded for exhaustion, abuse-of-the-writ analysis, and, where material, factual development, rather than summarily reject it. Blair's claim is, on the Court's own characterization, indistinguishable from Herrera's. The Eighth Circuit has already undertaken to examine the affidavits, the State's responses, and the district court's reasoning more closely — the kind of process Stone's Herrera dissent sought rather than opposed. Vacating the stay now would foreclose that process before it occurs. Consistency with his own Herrera position, not a new view adopted for this case, leads Stone to deny the application. This statement does not find the affidavits sufficient on the merits, does not order any particular outcome below, and does not represent a change from his Herrera dissent, which itself cautioned that "no indefinite stay follows" from his framework — a continued stay must eventually rest on an actual showing, not persist indefinitely without one.

### The statement of the Justices who would grant the application

Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas would vacate the stay, on the view that Blair's papers present nothing beyond what Herrera's own affidavits presented and what the Herrera majority already found insufficient to make "a persuasive affirmative demonstration of innocence," and that the Eighth Circuit's stated need for further study identifies no respect in which Blair's showing exceeds Herrera's. This view does not command a majority and decides nothing.

### Blackmun, Stevens, and Souter

Blackmun, joined by Stevens, would deny the application, consistent with their Herrera dissent's view that a claim of this kind warrants district-court consideration of the threshold factual and procedural requirements before being rejected outright. Souter separately states he would deny the application, preferring to allow the Eighth Circuit's contemplated further study to proceed before the stay is disturbed, consistent with his own Herrera dissent joining Blackmun's remand.

## Adaptive audit annex

**Correction of version 1.0's defect:** Version 1.0 reasoned that because Blair's showing was indistinguishable from Herrera's, and because Stone's Herrera dissent required "an express Barefoot/Delo showing" for any stay, Blair's showing must fail that showing too, "a fortiori" from its failing Herrera's higher merits burden. Two errors: (1) a showing's failure to satisfy a demanding merits burden does not itself establish failure to satisfy a materially lower interim-relief threshold — that requires its own application, not an inference from the higher standard; and (2) Stone's Herrera dissent never found Herrera's affidavits insufficient on the merits at all — he dissented from that very finding and voted to remand. Attributing an adverse merits finding to Stone that his own dissent does not contain, and then extending it "a fortiori" to a lower threshold, compounded one unsupported inference on another. This version replaces both with Stone's actual recorded position: he voted to remand Herrera's materially identical claim rather than reject it, and that position, applied to Blair, supports denying the application to vacate rather than granting it.

**Historical reconciliation:** History vacated the stay 5–3, under a Court whose Chief (historically Rehnquist) is replaced by Stone-Zsela in this simulation; the other eight Justices' historical positions are unchanged: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas would grant vacatur; Blackmun and Stevens dissent; Souter separately would deny. This simulation departs from history only at the Chief's own vote, which follows from Stone's own already-committed, independently reasoned Herrera dissent rather than from adopting the historical Chief's position by default. That single changed vote converts a 5–3 disposition into a 4–4 tie, which under ordinary Supreme Court practice does not carry the movant's application — a materially different practical result (the stay survives) driven by a single Justice's documented, consistent jurisprudence rather than by an invented departure.
