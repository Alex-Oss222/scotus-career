## Controlling holdings

#### Section 506(b) supplies interest on these oversecured arrearages

**Holding and operative rule:** For an allowed secured claim whose collateral value, after recovery under §506(c), exceeds the claim, §506(b) allows interest within that surplus even if the agreement does not provide interest on arrearages. In these plans the parties accept application through confirmation; reasonable fees, costs and charges remain subject to the separate requirement that the agreement provide for them.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory entitlement to interest does not depend on an interest-on-arrearages term in the notes. Ron Pair reads §506(b) to distinguish interest from the following category of reasonable fees, costs and charges provided by agreement. These claims are oversecured, and the arrearages form part of the allowed secured obligation. Interest is therefore available within the value remaining after the recovery contemplated by §506(c). The debtors’ cure argument does not remove that entitlement: §1322(b)(5) permits a method of treating default, but contains no exception displacing §506(b). Nobelman protects the holder’s mortgage rights while recognizing that independent Code provisions continue to operate; it does not make contract silence decisive here. The parties agree on the provision’s preconfirmation application for these plans, and the Court does not announce a universal temporal endpoint for every case. It awards no unagreed fee and decides no undersecured-claim question. Calculation must preserve the distinction between this accrual and later present-value protection.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S.235: statutory interest distinguished from agreement-dependent reasonable fees, costs and charges.
- Nobelman v. American Savings Bank, June 1, 1993: protected mortgage rights coexist with independent Code provisions; contract silence does not displace §506(b).

#### Deferred cure payments must preserve present value under the applicable confirmation alternative

**Holding and operative rule:** When a plan provides for an allowed secured arrearage claim through deferred payments, the creditor objects and the debtor retains the collateral, §1325(a)(5)(B) requires lien retention and distributions worth at least the allowed claim as of the plan’s effective date. Cure and maintenance under §1322(b)(5) do not exempt those arrearages from that requirement; creditor acceptance or surrender remains a distinct statutory alternative.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that these plans provide for the arrearage claims by prescribing how they will be paid. Section 1325(a)(5) is not confined to claims whose contractual rights are otherwise modified. Wade objects and the debtors retain the collateral, so the applicable alternative requires retention of the lien and distributions having the necessary value as of the plan’s effective date. Deferred dollars do not alone supply present value; the payment stream must include the value protection the statute requires. The cure provision permits correction of default within a reasonable time while maintaining payments on obligations extending beyond the plan, but it does not suspend the confirmation requirements. The contrary view would turn the cure label into an exception absent from the text. Nobelman’s anti-modification holding addresses a different provision and does not supply that exception. The Court decides no rate, no treatment of all undersecured mortgages and no automatic entitlement to every fee. The calculation must not duplicate recovery for the same period.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Nobelman, June 1, 1993: the anti-modification rule and express cure authority retained without erasing the separate confirmation requirement.
- Ron Pair, 489 U.S.235: distinct Bankruptcy Code provisions receive their own operative effect; the present-value holding rests on §1325(a)(5)(B), not §506(b) alone.
