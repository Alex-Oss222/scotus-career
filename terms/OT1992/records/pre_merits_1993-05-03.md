## Controlling holdings

#### A single suit must be objectively baseless before motive defeats petitioning protection

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the sham theory presented, a lawsuit loses Noerr protection only if it is objectively baseless, so that no reasonable litigant could realistically expect success on the merits, and then is shown to use the governmental process itself, rather than its outcome, as an anticompetitive weapon. The copyright suit had an objectively reasonable basis, ending this inquiry without motive discovery; even a sham finding would leave the ordinary antitrust elements to be proved.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an unsuccessful suit is not a sham merely because its filer sought a competitive advantage. Noerr and Pennington protect efforts to obtain government action, and Omni distinguishes the requested result from abuse of the process itself. California Motor Transport preserves that latter inquiry; it does not permit an adverse motive alone to strip a reasonably grounded suit of protection. Bill Johnson’s likewise separates baseless litigation from reasonably founded claims. The studios’ copyright theory addressed an unsettled public-performance question and presented a realistic prospect of success, even though it ultimately failed. Objective probable cause therefore defeats the sham theory here without discovery into motive or an inquiry into whether an expected victory would be economically profitable. Only after objective baselessness is established does the process-versus-outcome inquiry arise. The Court does not resolve alleged fraud in obtaining government action, a pattern of serial proceedings or independently unlawful conduct outside this theory. Nor does loss of immunity establish antitrust liability without its substantive elements.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Eastern Railroad Presidents Conference v. Noerr Motor Freight, Inc., 365 U.S.127, and United Mine Workers v. Pennington, 381 U.S.657: applies protection for government-directed competitive advocacy.
- City of Columbia v. Omni Outdoor Advertising, Inc., 499 U.S.365: applies process/outcome distinction.
- California Motor Transport Co. v. Trucking Unlimited, 404 U.S.508: clarifies sham litigation without deciding every pattern or fraud case.
- Bill Johnson’s Restaurants, Inc. v. NLRB, 461 U.S.731: applies distinction between baseless and reasonably grounded suits.
- Spectrum Sports, January25 1993, simulated: ordinary antitrust elements remain necessary; no new exemption from their proof.

## Material published noncontrolling positions

Souter joins the Court. Objective probable cause provides a useful threshold, but the decision does not incorporate every detail of malicious-prosecution law or Rule11 practice into antitrust immunity. Stevens, joined by O’Connor, agrees that this tenable copyright action cannot sustain the counterclaim. He regards the broader formulation as unnecessary to that result and warns that objectively plausible proceedings can sometimes be used as part of abusive conduct. A pattern, imposed burdens or conduct distinct from a genuine attempt to obtain relief may require a different inquiry. The concurrence would decide this record without foreclosing those possibilities. Neither separate writing changes the seven-Justice rule governing the single suit and theory actually reviewed.
