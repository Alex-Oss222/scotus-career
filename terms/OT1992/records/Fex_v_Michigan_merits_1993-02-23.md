The IAD, adopted as federal law at 84 Stat. 1397 and codified in the then-current 18 U.S.C. Appendix, governs. Article III(a) applies during a term of imprisonment to an untried indictment, information or complaint in another party State on which a detainer has been lodged. The prisoner must cause delivery to prosecutor and appropriate court of written notice of the place of imprisonment and a request for final disposition, accompanied by the custodian's certificate stating the term, time served and remaining, good time earned, parole eligibility and parole-agency decisions. Article III(b) requires the custodian promptly to forward notice, request and certificate by registered or certified mail with return receipt requested. Article III(a)'s necessary-or-reasonable good-cause continuance requires an open-court showing and presence of prisoner or counsel; Article VI(a) excludes the period of inability to stand trial as determined by the court with jurisdiction. ## Controlling holdings

### Article III(a): actual receipt starts the trial period

**Holding and operative rule.** When a prisoner invokes Article III while serving a term of imprisonment and an untried charge in another participating State is the subject of a lodged detainer, the 180-day trial period begins only when the required written notice and request for final disposition have actually reached both the prosecuting officer and the appropriate court in that State. Delivery to the custodian alone does not start it; a necessary or reasonable continuance may be allowed for good cause shown in open court with the prisoner or counsel present, Article VI(a) tolls the period while the prisoner is unable to stand trial as determined by the court having jurisdiction, and Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice when the applicable period is violated.

**Authority.** Scalia's opinion, joined by White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six votes for this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the compact starts the prosecution period on receipt by the officials who must act on the request. Article III(a) names the prosecutor and court as the recipients; Article III(b) separately makes the custodian responsible for forwarding the papers and accompanying certificate. Requiring a return receipt reinforces a definite, verifiable starting point. Carchman v. Nash supplies the treatment of this congressionally approved compact as federal law and requires attention to its operative terms. Houston v. Lack protects a prisoner's own appeal filing upon delivery to prison authorities; it does not determine when another jurisdiction's period for bringing a prosecution begins. The contrary interpretation addresses a serious risk of official delay, but it could consume the receiving State's trial period before that State receives the request. Article IX's liberal-construction direction does not eliminate the named recipients. Because trial began 177 days after both recipients received Fex's papers, Article III(a) does not require dismissal. The Court leaves any separate notice violation, independent constitutional remedy, and the availability of a continuance first sought after expiration unresolved.

**Precedent treatment.**

- Carchman v. Nash, 473 U.S. 716 (1985): applied for the compact’s federal-law character and attention to operative language; its exclusion of probation-violation detainers remains intact.
- Houston v. Lack, 487 U.S. 266 (1988): distinguished because a prisoner’s own appeal-filing deadline differs from commencement of the receiving State’s prosecution period; its mailbox rule remains in force within its setting.

## Continuity and material published noncontrolling positions

**Blackmun, joined by Stevens, dissenting on Article III(a).** The proper custodial submission completes the act the compact requires from a prisoner. Article III(b) channels the request through officials whom the prisoner cannot supervise; reading Article III(a) together with that prescribed channel and Article IX places transmission risk on the participating governments. Houston v. Lack supplies the analogous protection for a prisoner who has surrendered the paper to the mandatory intermediary. Blackmun's Carchman opinion recognizes the serious burdens associated with unresolved detainers, which explain why prompt disposition matters independently of ultimate conviction. The actual-receipt rule permits an indefinite official delay after the prisoner has done what the compact asks. The prosecutor's interest in adequate trial preparation is substantial, but a necessary or reasonable continuance remains possible upon Article III(a)'s actual good-cause showing in open court with the prisoner or counsel present. These Justices would reverse and remand under the custodial trigger, preserving the compact's actual exclusions rather than granting an automatic extension. They do not decide whether a continuance can first be obtained after the period has expired.

**Stone, separately dissenting on Article III(a).** Stone would begin the period when the prisoner gives the responsible custodial official the required written notice and final-disposition request for transmission through Article III(b). The custodian's later preparation of the required certificate or forwarding does not defer that date. The claimant must establish both a qualifying request and its delivery date under ordinary evidentiary rules: an oral inquiry, a writing missing the required notice or request, or an unexplained delivery to an unrelated official does not suffice. The prosecutor and court remain entitled to receive the papers. Stone acknowledges that their actual receipt offers a clear administrative date and preserves their entire preparation period, but reads the mandatory custodial channel and Article IX to assign transmission risk to the governments that operate it. Under his proposed rule the responsible custodian must record receipt, forward request and certificate promptly, and preserve delivery records. He would reverse and remand to apply the September 7 submission date and decide any Article III good-cause continuance or Article VI exclusion on its own predicates. If the lawful period expired before trial, Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice. Delay creates no automatic extension; bad faith is not required; no damages remedy or constitutional override is proposed.

Controlling law: actual receipt by both receiving-State officials starts Article III(a)'s period, subject to the compact's separate continuance/exclusion rules. Procedural terminal action: affirmance and partial dismissal of grant, with no Supreme Court remand. Published noncontrolling positions: Blackmun/Stevens favor qualifying custodial handoff; Stone independently favors that trigger with explicit certificate-preparation, proof, official-record and conditional-remedy limits.
