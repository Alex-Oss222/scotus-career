Question: Whether Rule 23(c) may make closure of this trial-like preliminary hearing the default unless the accused requests public proceedings.

A reporter and newspaper challenged denial of access to preliminary hearings under Rule 23(c). The Puerto Rico Supreme Court sustained the rule, relying on local practice and its view of preliminary hearings. The hearings determine whether an accused will be held for trial before a neutral magistrate, with counsel, cross-examination and the opportunity to present a defense. The governing rule allows exclusion of witnesses; that is distinct from blanket exclusion of the press and public. The lower court also recognized opening upon implied waiver by the accused or circumstances making the asserted privacy protection unnecessary. Those qualifications do not impose the constitutionally required burden and findings on the party seeking closure and do not cure the default. This is additional source detail within the unchanged challenge to default closure, not a new claim or a revision of the neutral access framework.

## Controlling holdings

#### Trial-like preliminary hearings require qualified public access

**Holding and operative rule:** The First Amendment’s qualified right of access applies to these adversarial preliminary hearings; the history inquiry concerns this type of proceeding, not only Puerto Rico’s own practice. Closure requires specific findings supporting an overriding interest and a narrowly tailored order after reasonable alternatives are considered; where fair-trial prejudice is invoked, there must be a substantial probability of prejudice that closure would prevent and no adequate reasonable alternative.

**Authority:** Per curiam Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a defendant’s preference cannot replace the constitutional justification for closing a judicial proceeding. Press-Enterprise II applies experience and function to preliminary hearings, and these hearings contain the adversarial safeguards that make public observation useful: a neutral decisionmaker, counsel, cross-examination and an opportunity to present a defense. Globe Newspaper rejects a categorical substitute for the particularized inquiry. A jurisdiction cannot defeat that inquiry merely by pointing to its own tradition of closure, for the relevant experience concerns the kind of hearing across the country. Fair-trial concerns remain substantial, but their existence must be demonstrated with the required probability of prejudice and inadequacy of reasonable alternatives. Witness safety can likewise support a properly justified and limited protective order. Rule 23(c)’s default therefore cannot stand. The Court does not establish access to every pretrial activity, open grand-jury proceedings, or order disclosure of material covered by a valid narrow protection.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Richmond Newspapers, Inc. v. Virginia, 448 U.S.555: access’s public oversight function applied.
- Globe Newspaper Co. v. Superior Court, 457 U.S.596: categorical closure rejected in favor of a particularized justification.
- Press-Enterprise Co. v. Superior Court (II), 478 U.S.1: trial-like preliminary-hearing access and the fair-trial closure requirements applied.
