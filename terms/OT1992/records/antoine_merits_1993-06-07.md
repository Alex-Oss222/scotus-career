## Controlling holdings

#### The ministerial transcript function does not carry absolute judicial immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** Court reporters performing the statutory task of accurately recording or producing a verbatim transcript do not receive absolute judicial immunity merely because their work is important or integral to a judicial proceeding. A claimant must establish the historically and functionally justified immunity; this ministerial task does not involve the discretionary adjudication that supports judicial immunity.

**Authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that immunity follows the protected function rather than proximity to the judge. Butz requires the claimant to justify absolute protection, and Forrester distinguishes adjudicative decisions from administrative functions even when a judge performs them. Section 753 requires accurate verbatim preservation of the proceedings; it does not authorize a reporter to decide what the legal record ought to contain. The proposed analogy to common-law judges taking notes fails because those judges exercised discretion in deciding what to record for their own adjudication. A modern reporter’s skill and institutional importance do not transform the assigned task into judicial decisionmaking. Burns likewise requires actual historical and functional support rather than a general fear of litigation. The respondents have not established that support here. The Court removes only absolute judicial immunity. It does not determine whether an authorized federal damages action exists, whether Antoine suffered an actionable deprivation, or whether causation, other defenses or damages can be proved.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Forrester v. White, 484 U.S.219: functional distinction between adjudication and administration applied to reporting.
- Butz v. Economou, 438 U.S.478: burden of justifying absolute immunity and functional comparison applied.
- Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S.478: historical and functional justification required; importance alone is insufficient.
- Imbler v. Pachtman, 424 U.S.409: immunity for adjudicative functions distinguished from the ministerial transcript task.
- Wyatt v. Cole, May 18, 1992: entered private-party qualified-immunity holding preserved without extension to the defense decided here.
