## Controlling holdings

#### Antecedent legal grounds must be considered on this remand

**Holding and operative rule:** The live reimbursement claim should return for adversarial consideration of its statutory and regulatory basis and any independently dispositive funding limits before this Court decides establishment. The lower courts must expressly decide whether to entertain the district’s forfeited defenses, considering the family’s opportunity to respond and prejudice; avoidance does not automatically revive abandoned grounds.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, Antecedent legal grounds must be considered on this remand portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an antecedent-law remand is appropriate in this particular posture. Graduation does not extinguish the family’s claim for expenses, and the parties’ exhaustion stipulation leaves that dispute available for review. But the district litigated below on the federal constitutional objection and indicated it otherwise would have furnished the interpreter. Adickes therefore gives the preservation objection real weight. Singleton nevertheless recognizes discretion to address an unpassed-on issue where the circumstances justify it, and Ashwander counsels against unnecessary constitutional adjudication. The IDEA, reimbursement authority and the federal funding regulation present identifiable questions affecting the requested relief. They require adversarial attention, not an assumption that the family lacks an entitlement or that every alternative defense was preserved. A regulation merely implementing establishment limits may not avoid the constitutional question at all. The Court orders that examination while leaving both reimbursement and the Establishment Clause merits unresolved, and without reviving the Free Exercise issue omitted from the petition.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Adickes, 398 U.S.144 — ordinary preservation rule acknowledged, not bypassed silently.
- Singleton, 428 U.S.106 — discretion exercised for this antecedent-law remand.
- Ashwander, 297 U.S.288 — constitutional-necessity principle applied without an automatic forfeiture exception.
- Burlington, 471 U.S.359 — statutory reimbursement context retained; no automatic award.

#### Lack of a duty and an affirmative prohibition are different

**Holding and operative rule:** On remand, absence of a federal mandate to supply the interpreter cannot by itself establish that local provision or reimbursement is prohibited. The courts must determine the actual operation of IDEA, 34 C.F.R.§76.532(a)(1) and any properly entertained state-law defense, distinguishing a bar on religious worship, instruction or proselytization funding from a regulation implementing only the constitutional limit.

**Authority:** O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, Lack of a duty and an affirmative prohibition are different portion; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that identifying the source of relief requires more precision than asking whether federal law compels the district to provide this service. The district’s local policy and its litigation representations make authorization distinct from obligation. Burlington establishes a statutory setting for reimbursement, but the Court has not adjudicated entitlement under it on this record. Section76.532(a)(1) restricts use of federal funds for religious worship, instruction or proselytization; its scope and the Government’s contention that it implements the Establishment Clause must be examined rather than presumed. An interpretation simply restating the constitutional restriction may leave that question unavoidable. Any Arizona constitutional defense also requires a lawful route consistent with preservation and the federal claim. The district cannot obtain an unexplained merits victory by changing theories late, and the family must have a fair opportunity to answer. This remand neither orders a monitoring scheme nor decides that the interpreter is religious instruction attributable to government.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Burlington, 471 U.S.359 — reimbursement authority distinguished from an automatic entitlement.
- Mueller, 463 U.S.388; Witters, 474 U.S.481; Meek, 421 U.S.349; Wolman, 433 U.S.229; Ball, 473 U.S.373 — no merits treatment by the Court; competing aid precedents remain available.
