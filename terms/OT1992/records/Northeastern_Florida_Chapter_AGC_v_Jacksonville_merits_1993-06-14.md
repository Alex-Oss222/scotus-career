## Event, chronology and neutral record

October Term 1992. Argued 1993-02-22; event effective 1993-06-14. The official reporter supplies these dates. The supplied merits-calendar premise provides the certiorari review; no new petition poll or grant is invented.

**Court and judgment under review:** Eleventh Circuit, 951 F.2d 1217: district judgment vacated and dismissal without prejudice directed for lack of standing.

**Questions and relief:** Whether ready and able competitors must prove a winning bid to challenge unequal bidding conditions, and whether replacement moots this controversy. The contractors’ association seeks reversal of the standing dismissal and further proceedings on its equal-protection challenge. Jacksonville seeks dismissal for lack of standing or mootness.

**Material facts and record limits:** Jacksonville’s original ordinance reserved ten percent of city contracting expenditures for designated minority business enterprises. The association alleged that members regularly bid for city work and would have bid for the reserved contracts but for the restrictions. Those allegations were not challenged in the parties’ summary-judgment submissions. In October 1992 the city replaced the ordinance with a program containing several methods, including a sheltered market reserving contracts for certified Black- and female-owned firms. The new program changed coverage and percentages but retained a mechanism excluding other firms from competing for particular awards.

**Threshold and preservation:** Standing and mootness are the only merits-reach questions presented here. The district court ruled on equal protection, but the court of appeals did not. Association standing concerns injury to participating members, not ideological objection to affirmative action. The continuing barrier must be identified in the replacement program rather than presumed from its title.

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Eleventh Circuit's standing dismissal, reject mootness because the replacement preserves the challenged kind of competitive barrier, and REMAND.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Injury from unequal competition

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach Article III standing on the unchallenged allegations about members' existing city-contracting activity and willingness to bid. Do not reach the constitutional merits of the preference.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply inherited competitive-injury and association-standing principles, identifying the injury as unequal treatment in a concrete competition rather than loss of an award the plaintiff would otherwise necessarily have won.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** An association with otherwise qualifying representational standing may challenge a governmental competitive barrier when its members are able and ready to compete and the challenged rule prevents competition on equal terms. They need not prove that a member would have won a particular contract. The allegations accepted in this posture establish the required connection to city contracting.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The city imposes the barrier, and prospective relief against it can remove the unequal terms. Reconstructing the winning bid in a competition a member was excluded from entering mistakes the ultimate benefit for the process injury. This does not dispense with evidence when allegations are genuinely disputed or with the ordinary requirements of causation, redressability, and association standing. The principle protects access to review of a genuinely contested process without deciding which side is right on the merits.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** General opposition to affirmative action is not injury. Readiness must be genuine, not an abstract possibility manufactured for litigation. The merits court must determine whether the racial distinction is justified; jurisdiction does not imply invalidity or entitlement to a contract.

##### Issue 2: Replacement ordinance and the surviving controversy

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Determine mootness independently of the merits. Examine what the replacement authorizes rather than treating repeal as conclusive.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply City of Mesquite's voluntary-cessation principle while retaining the distinction between continuing unequal treatment and a genuinely different controversy.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Replacement does not moot this dispute where the successor's sheltered-market method continues to reserve particular competitions to the preferred categories and thereby preserves the kind of unequal competitive treatment the association challenges. Not every revision of a contracting program necessarily has that effect.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Changes in coverage, percentages, and available methods matter, but do not eliminate the continuing exclusionary method. A plaintiff need not lose the opportunity for review merely because the city changes the form while retaining the challenged barrier. Further proceedings must address the current program and the relief that can still redress the members' injury.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** A remedial or access-expanding description neither defeats standing nor establishes the program's constitutional justification. Unequal opportunity to compete is a cognizable injury; it does not itself establish that a racial distinction is unjustified. Individual contract allocations are not categorically excluded from Stone's position permitting supported repair, and their individualized character does not by itself select an additional scrutiny tier. This threshold appeal adopts no new merits framework: the actual governing law, including Croson where applicable, and the program's authority, evidence, operation, and burdens remain for the proper merits proceeding. ## Controlling holdings

#### Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury

**Holding and operative rule:** An association may challenge a governmental barrier preventing members who are able and ready to bid from competing on equal terms, without proving that a member would have won a contract, when a member has individual standing, the interest is germane to the association’s purpose, and neither the claim nor the requested relief requires individual-member participation. The unchallenged allegations accepted here establish the required connection; genuinely disputed elements remain subject to stage-appropriate proof.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, Equal opportunity to compete is a concrete injury portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the injury is denial of equal treatment in competition, not eventual loss of a contract. Turner protects eligibility for consideration, and Bakke likewise distinguishes a chance to compete from a guaranteed benefit. Members regularly engaged in city contracting allege that they would bid on reserved work if allowed. The ordinance itself prevents that participation, and relief removing the barrier would redress the injury. Warth does not require proof of a successful bid in a competition the government closed to the bidder; it requires a concrete connection between the plaintiff and the challenged conduct. Hunt still governs the association’s representation of its members. Lujan’s evidentiary rules remain: the parties did not contest these allegations in their summary-judgment submissions, and the Court does not excuse missing proof when an element is genuinely disputed. Nothing in this threshold decision determines whether the preference is constitutionally justified.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Bakke, 438 U.S.265 — opportunity injury distinguished from guaranteed selection.
- Warth, 422 U.S.490 — concrete causal connection retained.
- Hunt, 432 U.S.333 — association requirements retained.
- Lujan, June 12, 1992 — concrete injury and stage-specific proof preserved.

#### The replacement continues the controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** Repeal does not moot this dispute because the successor’s sheltered-market method continues to exclude the association’s members from particular competitions on the challenged basis. Changes in groups, percentages and methods do not remove that injury; a replacement actually ending it could produce a different result.

**Authority:** Thomas’s Opinion of the Court, The replacement continues the controversy portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the operative program must be examined for the injury actually alleged. City of Mesquite rejects automatic mootness from voluntary repeal when the challenged conduct continues or can recur. Jacksonville’s new program changes details but retains a sheltered market in which specified firms alone may compete. The association continues to confront the unequal opportunity that gives it standing. The city’s argument that the old ordinance no longer exists identifies a change in legal instrument, not disappearance of the injury. Diffenderfer involved a change removing the operative statutory basis for the challenged treatment; it does not make every legislative revision conclusive. The conclusion is confined to the replacement mechanism shown here. On remand the courts must address the current program and relief capable of redressing the injury, rather than automatically restoring every provision of an injunction against the old ordinance. The constitutional merits of either preference remain undecided.

**Precedent treatment:**

- City of Mesquite, 455 U.S.283 — voluntary cessation applied.
- Diffenderfer, 404 U.S.412 — distinguished because the challenged injury continues.
