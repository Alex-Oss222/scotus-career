## Event and chronology

**Hartford Fire Insurance v. California**, No.91-1111 and91-1128; October Term 1992. Merits disposition effective 1993-06-28; argued 1993-02-23. On writ of certiorari to Ninth Circuit, Inre Insurance Antitrust Litigation, 938 F.2d 919 (1991).

**Question:** Whether domestic insurers lose McCarran-Ferguson antitrust protection by alleged agreement with foreign reinsurers; which pleaded conduct falls within the boycott exception; and whether international comity bars the Sherman Act claims against the London defendants. No. 91-1111 presents the two McCarran-Ferguson questions. No. 91-1128 presents extraterritorial application to the foreign conduct. These are distinct reviewed components of the same listed consolidated matter.

**Material record:** Nineteen States and private plaintiffs filed 36 complaints alleging concerted changes in commercial general liability insurance and reinsurance, including policy forms, pollution exclusions, and terms of coverage. The allegations include withholding broader reinsurance business to compel adoption of disfavored terms, as well as joint drafting without alleged refusal to deal. Other counts concern London reinsurers’ coordinated terms for American risks. These are allegations on motions to dismiss, not proven cartels. The London defendants invoke a British regulatory regime permitting their practices; they do not identify British law requiring conduct that American law forbids.

**Relief sought:** Domestic defendants seek reinstatement of dismissals under the insurance exemption. The London defendants seek dismissal of the foreign-conduct claims on comity grounds. State and private plaintiffs seek continued litigation of the challenged conspiracies.

**Render form: full.** Basis: Distinct five Justice boycott and foreign conduct coalitions; component-specific dispositions and limits of comity rule.

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** In No. 91-1111, AFFIRM IN PART and REVERSE IN PART on the reviewed domestic-insurance components: reject automatic loss of exemption through association with foreign reinsurers, permit the sufficiently pleaded boycott counts, and reverse reinstatement of California VII and Connecticut VI against the domestic defendants. #### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Activity-specific exemption

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory ground supporting the domestic dismissals; do not infer the foreign defendants’ regulatory status from an assumption made to test the domestic theory.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Royal Drug’s activity-centered distinction and the enacted exemption, rejecting an overextended analogy to entity-based immunities.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** An otherwise qualifying insurance activity does not lose its § 2(b) protection merely because a foreign reinsurer participates in the alleged agreement. Whether the particular activity is the business of insurance and is regulated by state law remains essential.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress protected a defined activity, not a class of persons that forfeits status by association. The participation of an insurance reinsurer differs from the noninsurance pharmacy agreements addressed in Royal Drug. This does not establish that every foreign or domestic act is exempt; it defeats the categorical rationale used below.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No blanket foreign-reinsurer exemption and no extension of Fabe’s different statutory clause.

##### Issue 2: Boycott and the different complaint counts

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply event-date pleading law, accepting the relevant allegations without finding that the defendants actually acted together.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Clarify the statutory boycott boundary through Barry and the ordinary function of coercive refusal, not a new antitrust exemption.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Refusal of collateral or unrelated business to compel acceptance of terms in the targeted transaction can constitute a § 3(b) boycott. Mere joint insistence on terms for that same transaction does not, without more, turn every exempt concerted insurance practice into a boycott.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged threat to cut off broader reinsurance, including otherwise acceptable business, permits proof of collateral pressure in California I through IV and Connecticut I and II. Their sufficiency is not a finding that every piece of reinsurance is unrelated to the insurer’s overall risk; defendants may contest that operational premise. California VII and Connecticut VI, by contrast, allege drafting of model umbrella and excess terms without the material refusal-to-deal mechanism. With the categorical foreign-association theory rejected, those counts lack the reviewed basis for defeating the domestic exemption.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Boycott is an exception to an otherwise established exemption, not the definition of every restraint. No categorical holding about all coercion or intimidation is necessary. Alleged conduct, agreement, and actual antitrust injury remain to be proved.

##### Issue 3: American market effects and the asserted foreign conflict

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The nonfrivolous federal claim supplies judicial power; separately examine substantive coverage and the requested comity restriction.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply established foreign-effects authority and reject the asserted bar on the particular allegations. Reserve a universal theory of comity rather than making absence of impossibility the answer to every future case.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The pleaded London conduct, deliberately directed at substantial changes in American insurance coverage, may be adjudicated under the Sherman Act. British permission for that conduct, without a conflicting legal duty or another established source requiring nonapplication here, does not displace the American rule.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The foreign market and the United Kingdom’s regulatory interest are genuine. They do not alone explain why conduct aimed at American buyers must be insulated from an otherwise applicable federal statute. Congress’s § 6a boundaries remain controlling where applicable, including both the required direct, substantial, reasonably foreseeable domestic effect and the requirement that the qualifying effect give rise to the asserted claim. The alleged changes in American coverage supply the connection asserted here. Stone does not resolve the provision’s reach beyond what is necessary on these allegations or omit its claim-connection requirement. He does not derive power from importance or disregard a true conflicting command. The pleaded American operation and the statutory source, not a free-floating national preference, justify continued adjudication.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general holding that courts can never account for comity absent impossibility, no authority over wholly foreign injury lacking the required connection, and no determination that the plaintiffs have proved a Sherman Act violation.

## Controlling holdings

#### Insurance exemption follows activity rather than association

**Holding and operative rule:** Otherwise qualifying business of insurance regulated by state law does not lose McCarran-Ferguson § 2(b) antitrust protection merely because domestic insurers agree with foreign reinsurers. Whether the activity itself qualifies and is state-regulated remains essential; neither domestic nor foreign nationality establishes that condition categorically.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the exemption’s object is the activity Congress described. Royal Drug distinguishes the business of insurance from all business done by insurance companies; its discussion of agreements with outsiders does not create an entity-based contamination rule. Pireno likewise examines risk transfer, the policy relationship and whether the practice remains within the insurance industry. The alleged agreements here concededly concern the business of insurance, and foreign reinsurers are not pharmacies or other parties wholly outside that industry. Even assuming their own activities lack state regulation, the domestic activity does not automatically lose otherwise established protection through association. The contrary analogy to exemptions for designated agricultural producers overlooks different statutory language. This ruling removes one appellate rationale without foreclosing the boycott exception or deciding the actual regulatory status of every reinsurer. Fabe’s separate first-clause protection of state insurance-regulatory law remains distinct. Qualifying activity, actual regulation and applicable statutory exceptions must still be evaluated.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Group Life & Health Insurance v. Royal Drug, 440 U.S.205: activity/entity distinction applied; loose outsider analogy confined to its context.
- Union Labor Life v. Pireno, 458 U.S.119: practice-specific criteria retained.
- SEC v. National Securities, 393 U.S.453: regulation of insurance business distinguished from regulation of companies.
- Case-Swayne v. Sunkist, 389 U.S.384, and United States v. Borden, 308 U.S.188: producer/entity-based exemptions distinguished.
- Entered Fabe, June 11, 1993: separate first-clause holding not expanded into the antitrust proviso.

#### Collateral refusals distinguish boycott from agreement on terms

**Holding and operative rule:** A concerted refusal of collateral or unrelated transactions to compel desired terms in a targeted transaction can constitute a McCarran-Ferguson § 3(b) boycott; a mere joint insistence on terms of the refused transaction does not, without more, do so. The pleaded broader withdrawals permit California I–IV/Connecticut I–II to proceed, subject to proof and whether other business genuinely affects the reinsured risk; California VII/Connecticut VI allege no refusal mechanism and cannot defeat domestic exemption on the reviewed grounds.

**Authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the boycott exception must leave room for the activity exemption Congress enacted. Barry involved withholding alternative insurance to force acceptance of another insurer’s terms, while Eastern States used unrelated dealings to compel a collateral trade restriction. Those examples distinguish coercive leverage from a collective demand about the very risk or transaction offered. Reinsurance terms depend on the underlying coverage, so refusing to reinsure an objectionable primary policy is not automatically collateral. The complaints nevertheless allege broader withdrawal even from otherwise acceptable business and linked demands that may cross that boundary. McLain and Conley require crediting that permissible pleaded account at this stage, not deciding the operational facts against the plaintiffs. Defendants may show that the other business genuinely affected the proposed risk. The model umbrella/excess counts contain no corresponding refusal allegation. The Court decides neither actual conspiracy nor antitrust injury, and supplies no categorical definition of every independent coercion or intimidation claim.

**Precedent treatment:**

- St. Paul Fire&Marine v. Barry, 438 U.S.531: collateral-pressure application retained; not limited to boycotts of competitors.
- Eastern States Retail Lumber v. United States, 234 U.S.600: unrelated trade used as leverage illustrates the boundary.
- United States v. South-Eastern Underwriters, 322 U.S.533: coercive exclusion distinguished from mere agreement on terms; not abrogated.
- McLain v. Real Estate Board, 444 U.S.232, and Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S.41: then-operative pleading standard applied; no actual liability finding.

#### The asserted British permission does not bar these American-market claims

**Holding and operative rule:** The pleaded London conduct intended to produce, and producing, substantial effects on American insurance coverage may be adjudicated under the Sherman Act; British permission without an incompatible duty or another established ground requiring nonapplication here does not warrant the asserted comity dismissal. The Court reserves a universal comity rule and §6a’s precise applicability, while preserving its qualifying-effects and claim-connection requirements wherever applicable.

**Authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that these allegations connect the challenged foreign conduct to the American market the statute protects. Alcoa and Matsushita recognize the Sherman Act’s reach to intended substantial domestic effects, and Continental Ore does not equate foreign legality with immunity from that law. The London defendants allegedly coordinated coverage restrictions aimed at American buyers; those alleged changes supply both the domestic effect and the asserted injury connection. Assuming §6a applies and adds requirements, the alleged direct, substantial and reasonably foreseeable domestic effect gives rise to these asserted claims. The Court does not decide the provision’s wider reach or every interaction with import commerce. British regulation and sovereign interests deserve consideration, but the defendants identify no British command inconsistent with American compliance. The contrary prescriptive-comity approach would displace established statutory reach on these allegations without a sufficient legal basis. The Court leaves broader comity questions open and requires proof below. Federal subject-matter power, substantive coverage and ultimate antitrust liability remain separate; none is established merely by labeling foreign practices objectionable.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Aluminum Co.of America, 148 F.2d 416, and Matsushita, 475 U.S.574: intended substantial domestic effects standard applied.
- Continental Ore v. Union Carbide, 370 U.S.690: foreign permission does not itself immunize conduct.
- SociétéNationale Industrielle Aérospatiale, 482 U.S.522: actual foreign/domestic conflict considered; Blackmun’s separate analysis identified as not the whole Court’s holding.
- Charming Betsy, 2 Cranch 64: interpretive canon retained; does not require the asserted dismissal here.
- Aramco, 499 U.S.244, and entered Sale, June 21, 1993: source-specific geographic scope preserved; different statutes do not erase established Sherman Act reach.
