## Blair: material record defect requiring correction

The Blair record's **Stone's position** section says the characterization of Blair's papers as indistinguishable from Herrera's means they present nothing beyond what Stone's Herrera dissent had already treated as insufficient on the merits, and are therefore “a fortiori insufficient to clear the lower, threshold showing required merely to justify a stay.” Its **Validation and durable commitment** section also identifies Stone's dissent as controlling authority for the stay question. These statements require correction for three connected reasons:

1. Failure to satisfy a higher merits burden does not itself establish failure to satisfy a lower interim-relief threshold. A separate application of the substantial-grounds requirement is needed. The existing inference cannot establish that conclusion simply by calling the threshold lower.
2. The earlier [Herrera Decision Record](../records/Herrera_v_Collins_merits_1993-01-25.md), **Stone fixed core and final participation** and **Continuity**, records Stone's proposed clear-and-convincing merits burden, his vote to vacate the categorical exclusion and remand through separate procedural gates, and his requirement of express Barefoot/Delo findings before a temporary stay. It does not record Stone adopting the majority's finding that Herrera's particular submission was insufficient or deciding that no properly supported temporary stay could issue after the required inquiry. Reservation of a stay requirement is not an adverse application of it. Blair's asserted prior Stone conclusion is therefore unsupported by the earlier record.
3. Herrera's actual controlling record-specific insufficiency proposition has five supporters—White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas—and preserves the ordinary stay requirement. Stone's dissent is not Court law. The two sources must remain distinct even if a corrected Blair decision can apply the controlling Herrera holding to reach the same result. This close pass may not replace the recorded basis for Stone's fifth vote with a new rationale.

The Blair record explicitly counts Stone among five per curiam supporters on an eight-member Court. Its public holding and stay vacatur therefore depend on the vote whose recorded derivation is defective. The [chunk 11 public render](../output/OT_1992CHUNK11.md) repeats the five-Justice authority and says the stay showing was absent for the same reason it was absent in Herrera; agreement between the two files does not resolve the underlying defect. The record's express account of a user-directed assembly is preserved, but does not authorize a close pass to supply a missing adjudicative premise.

**Required resolution:** A user-authorized correction of Blair's Decision Record must distinguish the controlling Herrera holding from Stone's separate position, supply a supported application of the stay threshold without the invalid higher-to-lower-burden inference, and confirm the resulting judgment and exact proposition-level joins. Any dependent Render Input, render and workspace correction must follow that corrected record. The outcome need not necessarily change; this pass neither decides that it must change nor constructs a substitute justification. The missing item is a valid corrected adjudicative account, not a demand that the seven affidavits necessarily be recovered. Those materials' nonrecovery remains the record's stated source limitation.

The defect blocks Blair's Holdings incorporation and final publication, not Alexander or the nonprecedential treatment of DeBoer. Herrera's existing candidate entry and present-force statement remain unchanged. No purported Blair rule or noncontrolling separate statement is silently substituted for it.

## Alexander: authority, scope and earlier law

Each of Alexander's three Holding and Proposition-level authority fields is copied from its own record. Part I has six supporters: Souter, Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas. Part II has five: Souter, Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy. Part III has the same six as Part I; the other three accept alternative Excessive Fines relief without joining the coverage rationale. All nine leave the convictions undisturbed. No judgment-only agreement is counted as a rationale join, and no Marks synthesis is needed.

The First Amendment location preserves both the limited classification and the independent examination of lawful expressive inventory. The criminal-procedure location preserves the distinct Excessive Fines inquiry. Natural docket/date cross-references connect the locations without duplicating a proposition. The record identifies 509 U.S. 544 as a historical source locator, not the report of the altered decision; the candidate therefore uses the case name, actual docket and decision date as its authority anchor.

The added entries preserve the difference between judicial forfeiture and administrative destruction, statutory nexus and constitutional review, injury and available correction, and coverage and ultimate excessiveness. They adopt neither categorical inventory immunity, Stone's additional scrutiny method, his proposed excessiveness considerations nor a numerical formula. The mandate does not order return of destroyed stock, create a damages action, restore the business, reopen the convictions or make a final excessiveness finding.

Alexander completes the supplied June 28 group using June 25 entering law. Austin is an uncoordinated same-day decision, not prior authority for Alexander; their civil and criminal forfeiture holdings coexist prospectively. Alexander applies Simon & Schuster's distinction between selective burdens on expression and neutral forfeiture, preserves the actual R.A.V. overbreadth rule, and distinguishes Mitchell's punishment of independently criminal conduct from the unresolved lawful-inventory question. It overrules no precedent and changes none of those earlier holdings' present force. No current-force rewrite or later-authority backlink is warranted for those routine applications and distinctions.
