## Saudi Arabia v. Nelson

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Saudi Arabia v. Nelson; No. 91-522; 1993-03-23. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** REVERSED. Eight Justices rejected jurisdiction over the intentional-tort claims, with different rationales; six rejected jurisdiction over the failure-to-warn claims.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter’s opinion was joined by Rehnquist, O’Connor, Scalia, and Thomas; Kennedy joined except its final paragraph addressing warnings. White concurred in the judgment, joined by Blackmun. Kennedy concurred in part and dissented in part, with Blackmun and Stevens joining Parts I-B and II. Blackmun also wrote separately; Stevens dissented.

**Historical Holding:** The intentional-tort claims were based on sovereign police conduct rather than qualifying United States commercial activity. The Court also rejected the warning theory as a reformulation of the sovereign torts. Separate opinions disputed the latter conclusion and identified unresolved merits and preclusion questions.

**Historical Authority:** 507 U.S. 349 (1993); a historical Supreme Court merits decision. Its authority in the historical legal system does not make it an effective decision of this reboot.

**Historical Sources:** [Published opinions, disposition, and opinion-joinder statement](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/349/).
