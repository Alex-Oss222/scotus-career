#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the judgment insofar as it sustains the forfeiture without a proper Excessive Fines inquiry, and REMAND; reject the categorical prior-restraint ground.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require examination of the punitive forfeiture’s extent and relationship to the proved offenses, preserving correction of any forfeiture that exceeds statutory or constitutional limits. Leave the independently sustained convictions undisturbed by this remedial ruling. Do not choose a new dollar cap or adjudicate the ultimate proportion on an undeveloped analysis.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Prior restraint and punishment of an expressive enterprise

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved issue within the review posture stated in Section I.

**Stone’s Legal Position and Treatment of Precedent:** Apply and explain existing sources; do not announce a general replacement doctrine.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A forfeiture imposed after valid criminal adjudication under a generally applicable enterprise statute is not a prior restraint merely because it reaches an expressive business, where it forbids no future publication; the sanction still must satisfy the statute and independent constitutional limits.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone recognizes the severity of destroying an expressive enterprise without converting every punishment affecting a publisher into a prospective speech injunction. The Government must rely on the adjudicated criminal enterprise and the statute’s actual nexus, not on the undesirability of lawful books. Rejecting the prior-restraint category supplies no automatic answer to excessiveness or to a genuinely established claim of targeting protected expression as such.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No authority to seize presumptively protected expressive inventory before adjudication on the same theory, no future-publication ban, and no categorical constitutional approval of unlimited enterprise forfeiture.

##### Issue 2: Punitive criminal forfeiture and excessiveness

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved issue within the review posture stated in Section I.

**Stone’s Legal Position and Treatment of Precedent:** Apply and explain existing sources; do not announce a general replacement doctrine.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** This punitive criminal forfeiture is subject to the Excessive Fines Clause, and the judgment cannot stand without a proper inquiry into whether the exaction is excessive in relation to the offenses supporting it.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone separates the finding of a valid criminal offense from authorization to take every asset connected in some way to the enterprise. The First Amendment objection cannot be dismissed simply by calling the sanction forfeiture, but neither does the expressive character of an asset by itself resolve the fine question. A remand is required because the necessary constitutional analysis was not supplied; the Court does not invent its result.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic finding that this forfeiture is excessive, no later Bajakajian formula imported as entering law, and no assumption that same-date Austin has already been decided.

It supplies no governing rule, factual finding, approval history, or adopted Court holding. #### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Deference to the temporary appellate stay and a successive petition

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve only the application to vacate the appellate stay, after applying the actual effective habeas law. Do not decide the habeas merits.

**Stone’s Legal Position and Treatment of Precedent:** Apply and explain existing sources; do not announce a general replacement doctrine.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A State does not establish clear abuse merely by identifying a successive petition when a nonfrivolous innocence contention and its procedural availability require examination; this limited appellate review period should not be displaced on the abbreviated presentation.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone distinguishes permitting a court to examine a claim from accepting the claim. The affidavits are not adjudicated facts, but the State’s analogy and insistence on finality do not by themselves establish that the lower court acted outside its discretion. He would preserve the limited opportunity to determine whether the claim is legally available and factually substantial. An actual controlling simulated holding that eliminates the asserted basis would require a renewed case-specific position, not an Engine-invented exception.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic stay for every successive petition; no finding of actual innocence; no adoption of a freestanding innocence rule through emergency disposition; no assumed Herrera result.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Federal review, the child’s claim, and interstate custody enforcement

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve A-64’s stay entitlement only. The child’s federal theory is considered to the extent needed for the provisional review assessment; no plenary custody merits holding follows.

**Stone’s Legal Position and Treatment of Precedent:** Apply and explain existing sources; do not announce a general replacement doctrine.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The child’s claimed harm from transfer does not, on this record, establish a sufficient prospect of federal reversal of Michigan’s enforcement obligation to warrant a stay; A-64 should be denied without adjudicating an unpresented companion application.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone recognizes the child as a rights-holder and treats the claimed disruption as serious. He nevertheless finds no demonstrated federal defect that would authorize disregarding the Iowa determination on the theory that a different home may be better. He accepts next-friend presentation for this narrow application in the absence of an established representation defect, rather than treating counsel’s assertion as proof of a separate substantive right. A stay cannot become a de facto new custody adjudication outside the governing review channel.
