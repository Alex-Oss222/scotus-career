##### Issue 1: Prior restraint and punishment of an expressive enterprise

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Near v. Minnesota, 283 U.S. 697 (1931), protects against prior censorship; Arcara v. Cloud Books, Inc., 478 U.S. 697 (1986), distinguishes neutral sanctions for unlawful conduct from regulation aimed at protected expression. Fort Wayne Books, Inc. v. Indiana, 489 U.S. 46 (1989), requires adjudication before removal of presumptively protected material on an obscenity theory and distinguishes that pretrial question from postconviction RICO consequences. Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo, 418 U.S. 241 (1974), protects editorial choice but does not immunize a publisher from every neutral criminal sanction. The prior-restraint classification is not the entirety of First Amendment review. [Record and authority sources](#sources-67).

**Open Point:** Whether this postconviction forfeiture is equivalent to prohibited prospective censorship, and what remains after that characterization is rejected.

##### Issue 2: Punitive criminal forfeiture and excessiveness

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** The Eighth Amendment prohibits excessive fines imposed by the Federal Government. Browning-Ferris Industries v. Kelco Disposal, 492 U.S. 257 (1989), distinguishes a private civil award from governmental punitive extraction. In rem tradition or a forfeiture label does not eliminate the need to identify the actual punitive exaction. Entering sources do not supply a universal numerical ratio for this RICO forfeiture. The forfeiture’s statutory basis, offense relationship, punitive extent, and the claimant’s constitutional objection require separate examination. [Record and authority sources](#sources-67).

**Open Point:** Whether the lower court’s treatment omitted a constitutionally required excessiveness inquiry, without deciding its final outcome here.

The lower court did not provide the required separate Excessive Fines analysis. [Record and authority sources](#sources-67).
