##### Issue 1: Prior restraint and punishment of an expressive enterprise

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Near v. Minnesota, 283 U.S. 697 (1931), protects against prior censorship; Arcara v. Cloud Books, Inc., 478 U.S. 697 (1986), distinguishes neutral sanctions for unlawful conduct from regulation aimed at protected expression. Fort Wayne Books, Inc. v. Indiana, 489 U.S. 46 (1989), requires adjudication before removal of presumptively protected material on an obscenity theory and distinguishes that pretrial question from postconviction RICO consequences. Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo, 418 U.S. 241 (1974), protects editorial choice but does not immunize a publisher from every neutral criminal sanction. The prior-restraint classification is not the entirety of First Amendment review. [Record and authority sources](#sources-67).

**Open Point:** Whether this postconviction forfeiture is equivalent to prohibited prospective censorship, and what remains after that characterization is rejected.

##### Issue 2: Punitive criminal forfeiture and excessiveness

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** The Eighth Amendment prohibits excessive fines imposed by the Federal Government. Browning-Ferris Industries v. Kelco Disposal, 492 U.S. 257 (1989), distinguishes a private civil award from governmental punitive extraction. In rem tradition or a forfeiture label does not eliminate the need to identify the actual punitive exaction. Entering sources do not supply a universal numerical ratio for this RICO forfeiture. The forfeiture’s statutory basis, offense relationship, punitive extent, and the claimant’s constitutional objection require separate examination. [Record and authority sources](#sources-67).

**Open Point:** Whether the lower court’s treatment omitted a constitutionally required excessiveness inquiry, without deciding its final outcome here.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Alexander argues that extinguishing a publishing and distribution enterprise and destroying lawful materials suppresses expression far beyond the proved obscenity offenses. The Government responds that RICO imposes a generally applicable postconviction sanction on a criminal enterprise, not a censorship licensing scheme, and that the assets satisfy the forfeiture statute. That answer addresses the prior-restraint characterization but does not automatically justify any amount or scope of punishment. The lower court did not provide the required separate Excessive Fines analysis. [Record and authority sources](#sources-67).

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reject the categorical prior-restraint claim because the sanction follows adjudication and forbids no future publication, while examining independent constitutional limits on the sanction. Vacate the forfeiture judgment for a properly sourced excessiveness inquiry; identify any unsupported statutory nexus or impermissible targeting of lawful expression rather than presume it. Do not void the obscenity convictions merely from an excessive forfeiture.

##### Issue 1: Deference to the temporary appellate stay and a successive petition

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** 28 U.S.C. §§ 2251 and 1651 provide source-bound authority to preserve review of custody and execution. Barefoot v. Estelle, 463 U.S. 880 (1983), requires a legally substantial basis for capital habeas process; death alone supplies no automatic stay. Delo v. Johnson, 485 U.S. 945, 947 (1988), and Wainwright v. Spenkelink, 442 U.S. 901, 905 (1979); those statements are persuasive descriptions, not mislabeled holdings of the Court. [Record and authority sources](#sources-68).

**Open Point:** Whether the State has shown that the short stay lacks a lawful substantial basis, in light of the actual simulated innocence law and the affidavits.

##### Issue 1: Federal review, the child’s claim, and interstate custody enforcement

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** 28 U.S.C. §§ 1257 and 2101(f) provide the potential state-judgment review and stay channels.
