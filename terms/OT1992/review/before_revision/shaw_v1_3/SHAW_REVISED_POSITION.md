#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM dismissal of the equal-protection claim against the state defendants. Leave dismissal of the federal defendants undisturbed on its separate statutory and jurisdictional grounds.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Grant no injunction against the map on the racial-design-only theory. Decide neither that federal law required these boundaries nor that the plan satisfies every other constitutional or statutory requirement. This disposition rejects the asserted legal theory; it does not establish facts supporting an unpleaded deprivation or direct a trial on the map's overall validity. Ordinary rules governing a properly sought amendment, any other claim, and preclusion continue to apply. Preserving a category of claim is not a promise that these litigants can relitigate a final judgment regardless of those rules.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The actual pleading question and the separate federal dismissal

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state claim was dismissed before trial. Assume deliberate use of race and subordination of ordinary districting considerations, as alleged. The question is whether that alleged design, without a further constitutional deprivation, states the equal-protection claim asserted here. The reported complaint did not assert dilution. [Procedural record, 509 U.S. 636–640](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/630/).

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary pleading law while resolving the sufficiency of the proposed constitutional theory. Do not decide disputed purpose by imagining highways, communities, or political compromises inconsistent with the allegations. Do not recast the substantive question as a universal Article III rule: the voters complain about a state arrangement that actually assigns them to constituencies.

**Federal defendants:** The Voting Rights Act's §14(b) review channel and the separate restriction on review of the Attorney General's preclearance determination support leaving that dismissal undisturbed. This does not insulate the State's plan from an otherwise cognizable challenge. [Morris v. Gressette, 432 U.S. 491, 501–507 (1977)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/432/491/).

##### Issue 2: Equal political membership, including for these plaintiffs

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Deliberate or predominant consideration of race in constructing territorial constituencies does not, standing alone, deny a resident equal political membership. Equal protection remains available against racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic status, and other constitutional impairments under their governing rules.

**Essential Reasoning:** District lines affect people: they assign residents to constituencies and change political circumstances. The relevant distinction is not between land and persons. It is between that assignment and the imposition of unequal rights, unequal political opportunity, or inferior civic treatment. Within a territorial district all residents remain constituents and participate in the same election regardless of their individual race. A demographic objective alone establishes neither separate racial ballots nor racial eligibility for office or representation.

Neither race has a constitutional entitlement to a preferred racial composition, a representative of the same race, unchanged boundaries, or electoral success. Conversely, a white plaintiff's personal right cannot be answered by statewide white political strength. Stone does not adopt the lower court's statewide-representation rationale or presume that a remedial purpose disposes of any injury. The same inquiry applies to a Black resident in a majority-white district and a white resident in a majority-Black district.

**Boundary:** Participation means more than possession of a ballot. The claimed deprivation need not be financial, mathematically measurable, or capable of changing an election winner. Conversely, electoral defeat, demographic imbalance, or disagreement with a representative does not alone establish a deprivation. The governing source identifies the protected opportunity and the showing required. Lack of dilution does not automatically defeat every electoral equal-protection claim. It matters here because no other impairment is identified beyond racial design itself.

##### Issue 3: The strongest contrary analogy and the limits of precedent

**Contrary argument:** State-directed racial assignment can be understood as unequal personal treatment in itself, regardless of vote loss. It can also encourage racial stereotypes and imply that a representative serves a racial group. Those concerns cannot be answered merely by calling the plan territorial or remedial.

**Stone's distinction:** In the contracting setting addressed by *Croson*, race altered an applicant's opportunity to compete. Territorial design, standing alone, does not establish an analogous differential legal opportunity among a district's residents. Stone recognizes that extending the classification principle to constituency assignment is a substantial competing interpretation; he rejects that extension here because it treats the disputed injury as established by the same racial design said to cause it. This does not decide whether a distinct burden is justified or rewrite *Croson* for contracts. [City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469, 493–494 (1989)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/488/469/).

*Wright v. Rockefeller* presents a real antecedent obstacle: the Court considered allegations of racially segregated districts and sustained a finding that the alleged racial purpose had not been established. Stone cannot dismiss that history as irrelevant. Its resolution did not, however, hold that every proved racial territorial design necessarily violates equal protection. He addresses the injury question that *Wright* did not resolve on the assumed facts here. [376 U.S. 52, 53–58 (1964)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/376/52/).

*United Jewish Organizations* supplies narrower affirmative support: its judgment sustained deliberate racial consideration in the particular redistricting dispute. Its fractured opinions do not establish Stone's entire theory, validate every remedial map, or make statewide racial proportionality an answer to an individual deprivation. Stone treats it as evidence that racial consideration and unconstitutional electoral discrimination are not invariably identical. [430 U.S. 144, 161–168, 179–180 (1977)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/430/144/).

**Extent of doctrinal choice:** Before this case is decided, there is no existing *Shaw* holding to overrule. Stone is declining the proposed extension of the assignment-injury theory in this appeal. He is not installing his broader §9 justification rule in every field or treating it as inherited law. Any future departure from controlling classification precedent must be presented and justified in the case requiring it.

##### Issue 4: Geography cannot confer immunity from actual inequality

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Boundary drawing remains subject to the prohibitions governing the deprivation it accomplishes. It cannot lawfully be used to remove a racial population from the franchise or governing institutions, dilute voting opportunity with the constitutionally required purpose, or impose inferior civic membership.

**Independent constitutional and statutory sources:** Each claim retains its own elements. Equal protection, the Fifteenth Amendment, and statutory voting protections do not merge into one injury test. An independently sufficient statutory deprivation requires no additional constitutional violation and no discriminatory purpose that the statute does not demand. The territorial-injury rule supplies no additional gate for those claims and creates no general restriction on standing.

*Gomillion* illustrates the distinction: the alleged boundary change excluded nearly all Black municipal voters while excluding no white voters and deprived those removed of the municipal franchise. The majority proceeded under the Fifteenth Amendment; Justice Whittaker separately used equal protection. The geography was evidence and a mechanism of an identified wrong. [364 U.S. 339, 341–348, 349 (1960)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/364/339/).

*White v. Regester* confirms that participation can be impaired even though voters retain ballots. Its local institutional and historical inquiry concerned exclusion from effective political participation, not a guaranteed racial share of offices. [412 U.S. 755, 765–770 (1973)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/412/755/).

**Inferior civic status:** An operative state rule designating one race as the district's rightful membership, or an official practice withholding ordinary representative access from residents because of race, would present a different claim. The court must identify the challenged governmental treatment and its connection to the claimant. Stone does not infer such treatment merely from composition, irregular shape, or an expectation about a representative's future conduct. An isolated statement may be evidence of governmental action or purpose; it does not automatically prove a constitutional status injury.

The distinction between personal unequal treatment and generalized stigmatic objection has an antecedent in *Allen v. Wright*. That standing decision does not itself determine the merits of this districting theory. Stone uses the distinction to explain why noneconomic harm can matter without making every objection to racial policy actionable. [468 U.S. 737, 755–756 (1984)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/468/737/).

##### Issue 5: Racial knowledge and independent statutory protection

**Stone's Legal Position:** Legislatures may examine racial electoral conditions, discharge valid statutory duties, and pursue supported inclusion within their lawful responsibilities. Keep three propositions separate: racial evidence may be relevant; a statute may require specified corrective action when its conditions hold; this particular plan satisfies the applicable requirements. The first does not prove the third.

*Gingles* calls for examination of demonstrated electoral behavior, including cohesion and majority bloc voting, within amended §2's results inquiry. Those questions do not require officials to assume that every member of a race shares a preference. Its statutory elements are not universal pleading requirements for every voting claim. Constitutional purpose requirements remain separate. [Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30, 43–51 (1986)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/478/30/).

**Coalition and crossover questions:** Where the applicable statutory rule recognizes a combined-group claim, the court assesses the protected groups' combined opportunity on evidence of actual cohesion and common bloc opposition, together with every other applicable requirement. Claims involving crossover support likewise receive the inquiry belonging to the governing statutory protection. Racial identity alone establishes no individual's political preference. Cohesion alone neither proves liability nor displaces applicable size, geographic, or other requirements.

This paragraph preserves source-specific inquiry; it does not decide that §2 requires coalition, crossover, or influence districts. *Gingles* left related submajority questions unresolved. No new population threshold, automatic aggregation rule, seat entitlement, or statutory duty is adopted in this appeal. Any actually effective earlier simulated ruling in *Growe* or *Voinovich* must be incorporated on its own terms; a prepared Stone position or historical comparator supplies no substitute. [Gingles, 478 U.S. 46–47 n.12](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/478/30/).

Section 5's nonretrogression principle is not a command to maximize majority-minority districts. Preclearance does not certify compliance with all other law. Stone makes no finding that the federal officials demanded the precise challenged lines. [Beer v. United States, 425 U.S. 130, 140–142 (1976)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/425/130/).

##### Issue 6: Supported congressional prevention and lawful implementation

**Stone's Legal Position:** In the voting setting, Congress may establish valid remedial or preventive categories under the applicable Reconstruction Amendment enforcement power. Appropriate legislation may protect beyond the constitutional minimum applicable to an individual act; a completed constitutional violation by every covered jurisdiction is not a universal prerequisite. Coverage and compliance then follow the enacted conditions. [South Carolina v. Katzenbach, 383 U.S. 301, 324–331 (1966)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/383/301/); [Katzenbach v. Morgan, 384 U.S. 641, 650–656 (1966)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/384/641/).

**Support and fit:** Demonstrated patterns of constitutional violations, risks of recurrence, and methods of evasion can support preventive categories fitted to those conditions. These describe a supported route to legislative action, not a new exclusive evidentiary test. Apply the governing enforcement-power doctrine, including its treatment of legislative judgment and inference; require no universal formal findings, specified study, or separate local adjudication. *City of Rome* sustained the prohibition of certain discriminatory effects even assuming the underlying constitutional prohibition required discriminatory purpose. [446 U.S. 156, 173–178 (1980)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/446/156/).

**Implementation:** An institution administering a valid statute may rely on the legislative determinations supporting its category. It must still establish that its challenged action falls within statutory authority and satisfies the coverage and application-specific obligations the law imposes. It need not reconstruct Congress's investigation in every application. A jurisdiction acting independently cannot claim Congress's distinct enforcement power merely by describing its policy as preventive. The validity of legislation, its application, and an independently adopted policy remain different questions. [Croson, 488 U.S. 488–491](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/488/469/).

**Continued protection:** When continued authority is properly at issue, assess the relevant conditions, recurrence, evasion, and material misfit through the governing statute and constitutional doctrine. Reduced discrimination may reflect successful prevention; success proves neither automatic obsolescence nor permanent necessity. Coverage, statutory bailout, and constitutional challenges retain their own procedures and burdens. This explanation creates no automatic sunset, recurring judicial reauthorization requirement, or permission to disregard an otherwise applicable statute. [City of Rome, 446 U.S. 180–182](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/446/156/).

**Reach in Shaw:** These authorities explain why statutory protection and lawful prevention need not await an adjudicated constitutional wrong in every application. They do not establish that federal law required this map, that North Carolina met every application-specific condition, or that the validity of every preventive category is presented here.

##### Issue 7: Permission, duty, and the limits of this judgment

**Connection to Stone's §9:** Impartial government may address supported exclusion within lawful authority. Stone's stated corrective permission depends on objective support, an appropriate relationship between the condition and the measure, and permissible operation and burdens. Under his preferred framework, satisfaction of the defined permission resolves that equal-treatment justification without a second balancing inquiry or an additional scrutiny tier. A claimant may contest the permission's conditions, its actual implementation, or an independent deprivation. These are principles of his jurisprudence, not findings about North Carolina.

**Precedent and reserved doctrinal change:** Those principles describe Stone's broader position on justification. Because this claim fails at the asserted-injury stage, this judgment does not replace the scrutiny required by precedent for a different racial classification. Governing 1993 law includes *Croson*'s strict scrutiny of the state contracting classification and *Metro Broadcasting*'s different treatment of the congressional measures presented there. A departure from a controlling rule must be expressly identified and justified under Master §16 in a case presenting it; this dismissal does not silently accomplish that change. [Croson, 488 U.S. 493–494](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/488/469/); [Metro Broadcasting, 497 U.S. 547, 563–566 (1990)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/547/).

Where an actual claim calls for justification, the government must make the showing the applicable rule requires. Here Stone rejects design alone as the asserted deprivation without first requiring the State to prove federal compulsion. Making absence of injury depend on establishing that defense would collapse two distinct questions. Constitutional permission can extend beyond statutory compulsion; neither permission nor compulsion is inferred for every racial map.

**Independent limits:** Neither public justification, corrective permission, nor valid statutory implementation authorizes an independently prohibited constitutional deprivation, overrides a statutory restriction, or supplies authority for an otherwise unauthorized act. Permission to adopt a lawful measure does not itself impose a statutory duty or authorize a court to order it. The present judgment decides none of those additional questions by treating them as consequences of dismissal.

##### Issue 8: A usable rule under 1993 pleading law

**Practical sequence:**

1. Identify the governmental act, the asserted right, and the deprivation alleged. Read the substance, not just the plaintiff's chosen label.
2. Accept factual allegations and reasonable favorable inferences under ordinary pleading law. Require no expert report, completed voting analysis, replacement map, or proof of a changed election winner before discovery. *Conley* supplies the applicable notice-pleading approach. [355 U.S. 41, 45–48 (1957)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/355/41/).
3. Apply the elements of the actual claim. Exclusion, intentional constitutional dilution, statutory results liability, and personal civic inequality retain their distinct inquiries. Do not add racial predominance to every claim.
4. Address justification where that claim permits it, with its proper burdens. A remedial label alone answers nothing.
5. Tie relief to the established violation and the court's authority. A discriminatory access practice may call for correction of that practice; redrawing boundaries requires a connection between the map and the established wrong.

**Application:** On the theory presented here, even fully accepting the alleged racial design does not supply the additional deprivation Stone's rule recognizes. Dismissal is substantive, not punishment for failing to plead detailed evidence or use special words. If an actual complaint alleges another impairment, the court cannot rename it racial-design-only and dismiss it under this rule.

**Amendment:** Where leave is sought to allege a legally sufficient deprivation, Rule 15 and *Foman* govern the request, including futility, prejudice, and other recognized grounds. Stone does not direct an amendment or invent supporting facts in this appeal. [Foman v. Davis, 371 U.S. 178, 182 (1962)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/371/178/).

##### Issue 9: Consequences and the available disposition

**Consequence of the selected rule:** Some deliberately racial maps may survive this particular claim even when their objectives or boundaries attract objection. Stone's position does not promise that another doctrine will invalidate them. Shape, demographics, legislative statements, and departures from ordinary criteria remain evidence where relevant, rather than independent aesthetic or predominance tests.

**Application to this appeal:** The user's selected premise supports affirming dismissal of this equal-protection theory. Striking the map solely for its racial design would contradict that premise. An independently cognizable deprivation, properly presented and established, could support tailored relief under its own law. Alternatively, recognizing assignment itself as an injury would change Stone's selected rule; even that approach would ordinarily permit litigation after reversal of dismissal, not establish this map's final invalidity on the allegations alone.

**Stone's concluding statement:** I would affirm dismissal of the equal-protection claim. Accepting the alleged racial purpose and departures from ordinary districting considerations, the complaint identifies no unequal electoral participation or other constitutional deprivation beyond the racial construction of territorial constituencies itself. That construction does not alone deprive a resident of equal political membership. This conclusion preserves claims of racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, and legally inferior civic status under the doctrines governing those injuries. Independent statutory claims likewise retain their enacted elements without an additional constitutional-injury requirement. The judgment neither depends on federal compulsion nor adjudicates the map's compliance with every other legal requirement.
