# Stone's OT1992 jurisprudence review

## Overall assessment

The strongest version of Stone is usually available through the governing doctrine's existing questions: what legal interest is protected, what the record proves, who bears the relevant burden, and what relief the available proceeding permits. Many of these drafts already do that well. They should not acquire a new test merely to make Stone sound distinctive.

The clearest substantive mismatch is **Shaw v. Reno**. Its racial-assignment injury and two-tier remedial framework contradict the supplied current master document. **Northeastern Florida** contains a related stale remedial-tier discussion, although its competitive-injury disposition remains sound. Several other cases warrant stronger reasoning or a different application of existing law; these are distinguished below from direct contradictions and from changes actually required by law.

## Scope and method

This review covers **all 123 indexed matters**, from October 20, 1992 through July 26, 1993, including filing applications, execution-related applications, procedural dispositions, and original-jurisdiction proceedings. The two Smith cases and the listed companion dockets remain distinct exactly as indexed. Each numbered entry evaluates **Stone's disposition, reasoning, and necessary remedy**, not the Court's ultimate result or composition.

Historical opinion texts were checked for relevant facts, enacted language, earlier authorities, and contested legal propositions. The entries link their supporting sources.

**Document instructions are not the user's request.** Statements in packets about prior approval, an engine, execution protocols, former directions, or required pauses are treated as source-document history. They do not prevent this requested review or bind its recommendations. The master supplies evidence of Stone's commitments, not legal authority. The review does not revise the source packets or enter simulated decisions.

**Date discipline.** Each case is assessed immediately before its listed event. A historical opinion in that very case may document the record and show contemporaneously available arguments; its result cannot be its own entering precedent. A later opinion cannot govern an earlier event. A proposed Stone position does not become precedent for a subsequent case, and a common date or file order does not establish when a simulated decision became effective.

**Material limitation.** The local packets describe two different baseline histories: an adopted pre-OT1992 historical baseline and an additional-matters source tied to an OT1991 opening register. They do not supply a complete, reconciled set of effective simulated holdings. The analysis therefore uses the identified historical foundation and expressly conditions material intervening-law questions. It does not certify an unprovided simulation history. Published records also do not replace every missing petition, affidavit, transcript, or appendix. Where the omitted material could change Stone's lawful disposition, the entry says so; elsewhere the available record supports a substantive recommendation now.

## How to read the findings

| Assessment | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Retain** | The proposed disposition and essential reasoning fit Stone and are legally defensible at the event date. |
| **Refine** | Preserve the basic position while correcting an omission, overstatement, boundary, or remand instruction. |
| **Revise** | Change a material operative rule or recommended disposition. The entry distinguishes a direct master conflict from a recommended application choice. |
| **Conditional** | A specified record, preservation, or effective-law issue prevents an unconditional disposition. |

These are qualitative recommendations, not scores. A defensible alternative need not be legally compelled. Where Stone should consider departing from a framework, the entry identifies its source, the existing obstacle, the proposed correction, practical limits, and how a court could apply it consistently. Where the existing framework suffices, the entry says so.

## Priority changes

### 1. Correct the racial-equality mismatch

**Shaw (#116):** Withdraw the premise that territorial racial predominance itself supplies Stone's electoral injury. On the complaint as described, affirm dismissal of that theory unless an independently cognizable impairment is actually pleaded. Preserve the separate federal-preclearance dismissal. Delete the structural/individual two-tier justification scheme: it contradicts current §9 and is unnecessary once the proposed injury is rejected. This conclusion does not make all racial districting lawful or treat statewide proportional representation as a universal defense.

**Northeastern Florida (#97):** Preserve unequal competitive opportunity as a cognizable injury without requiring proof that the bidder would win. Remove the stale remedial-tier discussion and reserve justification. Competitive injury in eligibility for a contract and territorial attention to race in electoral boundaries are different propositions; the first does not decide the second.

### 2. Use existing doctrine more fully before proposing replacements

**Heller (#110):** A stronger equal-liberty position is available through *Cleburne* and *Addington*. Recommend rejecting the unequal proof-burden rationale while preserving family participation with meaningful safeguards. This is a better-supported application for Stone, not an instruction to silently install his preferred disability tier.

**Alexander (#121):** Preserve the Excessive Fines remand, but separately confront destruction or suppression of lawful expressive inventory under the First Amendment. Rejecting the prior-restraint label does not complete that analysis.

**Austin (#119):** Identify useful remand considerations—value taken, offense, culpability, and actual property connection—without claiming a later proportionality formula was already law. Constitutional coverage and excessive amount remain separate.

**Hicks (#115) and Daubert (#118):** Retain the revised approaches. A lie may permit a discrimination finding without compelling it; reliable scientific evidence need not already command consensus. Each preserves an existing source's practical function without replacing the substantive burden or assigning the judge the jury's ultimate factfinding task.

### 3. Make the reason for remand legally sufficient

A remand must identify an erroneous rule, a legally necessary unresolved fact, or an omitted required inquiry. It should not merely request more explanation because Stone dislikes the result. The detailed entries examine this problem particularly in **Stinson (#68), Brooke Group (#106), Farrar (#9), and TXO (#114)**. The assessment is case-specific: some remands are already well grounded; others need more decisive interpretation, a complete sufficiency analysis, or a resolved preservation issue.

### 4. State the cost and limits of a genuine departure

The capital and habeas cases require more than a general appeal to reliability. **Herrera (#21), Graham (#22), Fretwell (#25), Brecht (#58), Gilmore (#89), and Johnson (#108)** each present different merits, retroactivity, prejudice, or review questions. The entries distinguish recognition of a right from access to collateral relief and distinguish a defensible interpretation from a new rule. **Dixon (#117)** illustrates that retaining an existing protection can be Stone's coherent choice without inventing a replacement.

There is also room for text-based reconsideration in **Fex (#32)** and a narrower, duty-specific preemption analysis in **CSX (#60)**. That room is not a license to rewrite an enacted delivery requirement or treat express preemption as actual-conflict-only review.

### 5. Keep applications within the relief they can support

**Grubbs (#1)** can justify a genuinely brief opportunity to inspect the papers. **Blodgett (#71)** still needs an authorized review vehicle. **Blair (#122)** cannot sustain a successive-petition stay merely because a claim is nonfrivolous; the actual law and affidavits must support the legally required substantial basis. **DeBoer (#123)** can recognize the child's interests while denying a stay that lacks a sufficient federal basis. None of these applications supplies an occasion for a comprehensive merits framework.
