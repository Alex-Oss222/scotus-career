##### Issue 1: Prejudice and the relevant legal counterfactual

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Strickland, 466 U.S. 668, requires deficient performance and a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence, considering the proceeding as a whole. The petitioner bears both showings. Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986), excludes a claim based on losing the benefit of perjury; it does not equate every later-disapproved legal argument with dishonest evidence. Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 (1988), permits narrowing at the guilt stage and bears on the substantive double-counting rule. State courts are bound by this Court’s federal holdings, not by a circuit’s contrary constitutional construction. [Record and authority sources](#sources-14).

**Open Point:** Whether loss of an available lawful argument establishes the ordinary probability showing, and whether the existing finding survives correction of the hierarchy error.

##### Issue 2: Source and scope of habeas remedy

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Pre-AEDPA § 2254 and the court’s habeas remedial authority permit relief from unconstitutional custody, including a conditional opportunity to conduct a lawful new proceeding. United States v. Morrison, 449 U.S. 361 (1981), requires a remedy tied to the constitutional injury. A finding of defective assistance does not automatically entitle the prisoner to permanent application of a superseded substantive rule. [Record and authority sources](#sources-14).

**Open Point:** Whether the life-without-parole directive exceeds the correction required by the alleged denial of effective assistance.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Arkansas, supported by the United States as amicus, argues that a presently lawful sentence is not unfair merely because counsel failed to secure a benefit under an erroneous rule. Fretwell argues that Strickland measures the lost functioning of counsel at the actual proceeding and that a lawful, available argument is unlike perjury. The Eighth Circuit’s binding-precedent rationale is incorrect; its life-sentence remedy also requires independent justification. [Record and authority sources](#sources-14).

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reject prejudice where the asserted difference would depend on lawlessness or an unavailable substantive entitlement; apply ordinary Strickland to an available lawful objection and an established probability of a different proceeding; remand a finding resting on an erroneous hierarchy. If a Sixth Amendment violation is established, distinguish conditional resentencing relief from an irrevocable benefit under the displaced substantive rule.
