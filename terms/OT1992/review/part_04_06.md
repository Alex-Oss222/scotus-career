# Stone jurisprudence review — cases 37–72

## Scope and source treatment

This part reviews the proposed positions in CHUNK4_STONE, CHUNK5_STONE, and CHUNK6_STONE against their paired NEUTRAL records and the Master, especially §§1–4 and 14–19 and the relevant domains. Embedded approval language and directions are documentary history, not instructions for this review. References to published opinions in the case being reviewed establish the reported record, arguments, and available reasoning; their ultimate holdings are not assumed to be law already controlling Stone before that event. Statements identified below as proposed resolutions answer then-open questions. No later doctrine is imported. All dates are the packets’ simulated event dates.

The packets do not supply the complete effective simulated decision register or all underlying briefs and appendices. Accordingly, pre-OT1992 historical authorities supply the working baseline; a demonstrably different earlier simulated holding would require the identified issue to be reconsidered. Same-day opinions cannot silently supply one another’s premises. Source line references below identify the existing Stone reasoning, rather than purporting to cite it as law.

## 37. Leatherman v. Tarrant County

**Date:** March 3, 1993. **Verdict: Retain.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 17–35.

Stone proposes reversal of the heightened pleading dismissal and remand under ordinary Rule 8. This closely matches Master §§3–4 and Courts: the elements of municipal liability do not create immunity from suit or a special pleading rule. His retention of *Monell*’s institutional connection and *Canton*’s failure-to-train requirements also respects the Master’s express municipal-liability boundary.

The entering sources are Rules 8(a), 9(b), and 12(b)(6), *Conley*, *Monell*, *Owen*, and *Canton*. They provide an adequate legal basis without replacing substantive municipal liability. The reported claims concerned allegedly unconstitutional home searches; the case ended on pleading rather than a finding that training policy caused an injury. [Leatherman opinion and record, 507 U.S. 163, 164–169](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/163/).

**Recommended reasoning and extent:** Require fair notice of the municipal claim and its grounds under the rules then in force; refuse an additional particularity demand derived solely from litigation expense. Courts can enforce Rule 11, control discovery, and require evidence at summary judgment. Those mechanisms must serve their own standards, rather than restore the disallowed pleading burden indirectly. This is consistently administrable because the same procedural rules govern comparable civil actions. Stone should go no further into individual-officer immunity, respondeat superior, or ultimate damages. Reversal and remand completely correct the error presented; broader constitutional innovation would add nothing necessary.

## 38. Reves v. Ernst & Young

**Date:** March 3, 1993. **Verdict: Refine; retain reversal and remand.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 53–71.

Stone accepts an operation-or-management boundary but finds a triable question about the accountants’ actual directional role. That is consistent with Master §§2, 4, and 17: use the legal source reaching private misconduct, distinguish professional services from participation in enterprise affairs, and leave supported factual disputes to the factfinder. His point is not that an audit with serious consequences is automatically management.

Section 1962(c)’s precise participation requirement was the question to be resolved. *Sedima* and *Turkette* supplied broad RICO principles; neither had already settled this particular test. Stone should therefore call the operation-or-management rule his construction of the statute, not a preexisting Supreme Court requirement. The reported evidence included the firm’s initial selection of the plant valuation and financial-statement assumptions; the opposing account described ordinary auditing and a failure to warn management. Under *Anderson*, permissible inferences favor the nonmovant at summary judgment. [Reves, 507 U.S. 170, 177–179, 185–194](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/170/).

**Recommended rule:** An outside professional participates in conducting enterprise affairs when evidence shows that the professional assumed or exercised a decisionmaking function for the enterprise, including indirect direction; merely providing advice, drafting at management’s direction, or performing a deficient audit does not itself suffice. Asset importance supports the inference but is not a substitute for evidence about who made the decision. Engagement terms, correspondence, testimony, and actual authority make this inquiry workable.

Stone could adopt a broader reading of “participate” and invoke the enacted liberal-construction clause, but that expansion is unnecessary if his independently accepted directional test permits this claim to proceed. The cleaner course preserves both liability for actual managerial participation and a predictable distinction from professional negligence. Remand must leave predicate acts, culpability, injury, and causation unproved. No general aiding-and-abetting liability follows.

## 39. Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor)

**Date:** March 8, 1993. **Verdict: Retain.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 89–107.

Stone would remove the NLRA-preemption basis for the injunction against this project’s labor agreement. His distinction between buying a construction project and imposing sovereign labor policy follows Master Federalism and Statutory Interpretation: identify the actual statutory displacement and the government’s actual conduct.

*Garmon* and *Machinists* constrain state regulation; *Gould* prevents procurement from becoming a disguised sanction for violations elsewhere. NLRA §§8(e) and 8(f) matter because Congress permits specified agreements in construction. MWRA’s ownership, financing, supervision, and performance-related labor-stability objective give the private-proprietor analogy concrete support. [Boston Harbor, 507 U.S. 218, 224–233](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/218/).

**Recommended rule and extent:** The asserted labor-preemption doctrines do not displace a project-specific purchasing condition directed to performance of the government’s own transaction where a private owner could lawfully choose comparable terms. The project boundary, relationship to timely performance, and sanctions actually imposed provide manageable criteria. Government ownership alone cannot immunize a contractual requirement that functions as general regulation or reaches unrelated employment.

No framework departure is needed. Stone should reverse and remand on the NLRA ground while leaving any independent preserved challenge available. He need not create a universal market-participant exemption, decide that every project labor agreement is valid, or require courts to judge whether the purchasing choice was optimal. Protecting lawful public bargaining discretion is itself faithful to his jurisprudence.

## 40. Smith v. United States, 507 U.S. 197

**Date:** March 8, 1993. **Verdict: Refine the explanation; affirmance remains defensible.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 125–143.

Stone dismisses the Antarctic FTCA claim because the waiver’s territorial and choice-of-law structure does not supply the action. That fits Master §§3–4 and Statutory Interpretation, but “missing law or venue” overstates this claimant’s difficulty. She resided and sued in Oregon, so §1402(b) supplied venue. A hypothetical venue gap for nonresident claimants is, at most, contextual support, not a threshold defect here.

The genuine interpretive contest is between *Spelar*’s foreign-sovereignty description of “foreign country,” §2674’s private-person analogy, and §1346(b)’s direction to apply the law of the place of the act or omission, explained in *Richards*. All alleged acts occurred in Antarctica. Oregon law was a proposed conflicts solution, not nonexistent law. [Smith, 507 U.S. 197, 200–204, 209–214](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/197/).

**Recommended reasoning:** Explain why the place-of-act requirement does not authorize selecting the plaintiff’s domicile law when the relevant conduct occurred in sovereignless territory. Treat §2680(k) as a contextual territorial limitation, without asserting that “country” unambiguously means every foreign landmass. A broader reading excluding Antarctica from §2680(k) is legally arguable, but it still needs an authorized, stable choice-of-law rule; humanitarian purpose supplies none. The maritime history also prevents an absolute claim that the FTCA never reached conduct outside United States territory.

Retain dismissal if Stone finds that structural reading stronger. Its limit is this FTCA claim, not all overseas governmental accountability. No merits finding of reasonable care or universal overseas immunity follows. This refinement preserves source discipline while squarely acknowledging the serious alternative.

## 41. Ortega-Rodriguez v. United States

**Date:** March 8, 1993. **Verdict: Retain, with candid identification of the new boundary.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 163–181.

Stone would vacate automatic dismissal of the appeal after flight ended before appellate review began. This matches Master §§3, 16, and 19: a court may protect proceedings it administers, but an appellate remedy should not become punishment untethered to its source.

*Molinaro* and *Estelle v. Dorrough* support disentitlement where fugitivity affects the appellate process. Their rationales do not already resolve every instance of completed preappeal flight. Stone’s appellate-nexus requirement is a principled limitation of that doctrine, correcting the lower court’s broader rule rather than overruling a Supreme Court holding on identical facts. The reported record did not contain the necessary appellate assessment. [Ortega-Rodriguez, 507 U.S. 234, 239–252](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/234/).

**Recommended rule:** Completed flight permits appellate dismissal only where identified effects on review, enforceability, or a potential retrial make that sanction a reasonable response. Recapture defeats a simple assumption that judgment cannot be enforced, although it does not erase demonstrated prejudice caused by delay. Require an explanation linking the particular effect to dismissal and consider available narrower responses within existing discretion; do not invent a universal least-restrictive-sanction test.

The rule is workable through dates, lost evidence, enforceability, and actual disruption. Flight remains punishable through its own legal channels. Stone should reinstate consideration of the appeal by vacating the dismissal and remanding, leaving both the conviction’s merits and any adequately supported disentitlement determination open.

## 42. Reiter v. Cooper

**Date:** March 8, 1993. **Verdict: Retain.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 201–231.

Stone preserves the shipper’s unreasonable-rate claim while retaining the filed tariff as the initial lawful charge. This is a strong example of Master §4: a statutory defense or counterclaim, administrative competence, limitations, and final judgment are distinct questions. Neither insolvency nor perceived unfairness cancels the filed-rate rule.

The then-operative §11705(b)(3), *Maislin*, *Bull*, and *Western Pacific* support the coexistence of tariff enforcement, statutory reparation, same-transaction recoupment, and the ICC’s rate expertise. Rule 8(c) permits correction of a mislabeled counterclaim; Rule 54(b) governs separate final judgment. [Reiter, 507 U.S. 258, 263–270](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/258/).

**Recommended rule and application:** Treat the shipper’s substantive claim according to its elements rather than its caption. A qualifying same-transaction recoupment claim does not become an unlimited affirmative recovery after limitations expire. Refer the rate-reasonableness issue through the established administrative process while retaining the judicial action where appropriate. Before allowing collection to become independently final, address the relationship of the claims and the concrete risk that insolvency makes repayment unavailable.

These familiar mechanisms supply predictable administration without a new payment-first requirement or blanket stay. Stone should reverse and remand for that sequence, with no finding that these rates are unreasonable. His lawful room to protect the shippers is effective case management within Rule 54(b), not an equitable override of Congress’s tariff system. No doctrinal replacement is warranted.

## 43. Delo v. Lashley

**Date:** March 8, 1993. **Verdict: Retain the narrow instructional disposition; refine the record discussion.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 251–281.

Stone would reject the Eighth Amendment theory requiring a particular mitigating instruction without supporting evidence, while preserving a distinct ineffective-assistance claim. Master Criminal Procedure requires meaningful mitigation and competent representation, but also accepts governing burdens and rejects invented factual findings. That combination supports his narrow position.

*Lockett*, *Eddings*, and *Penry* protect consideration of mitigation presented; *Hopper* and *Walton* permit evidentiary predicates and mitigation burdens without relieving the prosecution of proving aggravators. Counsel declined to make a supporting proffer, while potential juvenile-record consequences were never resolved by the trial judge. The record cannot therefore be described as a judicial ruling compelling Lashley to testify. [Delo, 507 U.S. 272, 274–279](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/92-409.ZPC.html).

**Recommended clarification:** A some-evidence threshold may be satisfied through available witnesses, records, or other lawful evidence; it is not a demand to prove an exhaustive negative. Lashley’s youth and concerns about juvenile information deserve acknowledgment, but they do not by themselves establish the specific mitigating circumstance on this record. The court must not infer other crimes from silence, and the unpresented innocence-instruction theory should remain undecided.

Stone can go further by expressly preserving adjudication of counsel’s failure to investigate or proffer evidence under *Strickland*. He should not decide that claim without its record or treat rejection of this instruction as a finding of effective representation. Reverse only the identified instructional ground and return remaining claims for proper proceedings. A categorical presumption of no significant criminal history would require a separate change to *Walton*’s allocation, inadequately justified here.

## 44. Demos v. Storrie

**Date:** March 8, 1993. **Verdict: Retain as an exercise of discretion, with the precedent distinction made explicit.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 301–319.

Stone would deny fee relief for the present abusive petition but decline a prospective bar on every future civil certiorari filing. That preserves the Master’s court-access concern without inventing an unconditional entitlement to discretionary Supreme Court review or treating unsuccessful litigation alone as unconstitutional exclusion.

Rule 39.8 and the abuse-control precedents permit denial of IFP privileges. The reported history included an existing extraordinary-writ restriction and repeated later certiorari applications. [Demos, 507 U.S. 290–291](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/507bv.pdf#page=370). *McDonald*, *Sindram*, and the earlier *In re Demos* establish authority to address abuse; they do not require Stone to select the broadest available restriction in each new matter.

**Recommended rule and extent:** Say that the Court possesses established abuse-control authority, but that Stone would exercise its discretion through individualized screening here. Do not claim that a prospective condition is legally impossible merely because the future petition has not yet been filed. Conversely, a discretionary power is not a command to impose the sanction. This formulation avoids silently overruling prior abuse-control orders while preserving access to an exceptional meritorious petition.

If a different effective simulated *Martin* decision mandates a broader approach, Stone must identify and address it under §16. He should deny only the present IFP request, specify the ordinary opportunity for a compliant fee-paid submission, and leave later petitions to their proper treatment. Familiar screening remains administrable; unsupported claims of superior efficiency should be omitted.

## 45. Reno v. Flores

**Date:** March 23, 1993. **Verdict: Refine substantially; retain the narrow facial disposition on the supplied record.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 339–357.

Stone would reverse facial invalidation, preserve the conditions agreement, and leave actual unlawful custody or ineffective review open. His restraint accords with Master §§3 and 19, but the reasoning must not make minors’ substantive liberty merely a reservation for a future case. The Immigration and Procedural Due Process domains require him to explain why the present rule permits lawful custody before using the existence of lawful applications to reject the facial remedy.

Section 1252(a)(1) provides custody authority and discretion, *Carlson* informs its scope, and *Chevron* is the entering administrative framework. *Salerno*, *Schall*, *Parham*, and *Youngberg* distinguish legitimate temporary custody from arbitrary institutional confinement and calibrate procedures. They do not establish that every preferred-family category or every requested safeguard is constitutionally necessary. The actual regulation also includes parental designation and an exceptional-release avenue; licensed-care requirements materially shape the facial record. [Flores, 507 U.S. 292, 297–299, 315–319](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/292/).

**Recommended reasoning:** Children have an independent interest in freedom from institutional custody; ordinary parental custody is not a reason to erase it. Temporary placement while the government verifies a safe custodian and assures attendance can be lawful. Familial categories can organize that inquiry, but a category cannot transform protection into indefinite confinement or replace meaningful neutral review. A facial challenger must identify the rule actually attacked: the general availability of emergency custody does not answer a narrower challenge to a mandatory rule of continued detention. Stone should expressly distinguish the class’s demand for release to all responsible adults from a properly supported challenge to a particular mandatory barrier.

**Lawful room to go further:** Apply *Mathews* to notice and access using facts about age, language, representation, and ability to request review. That existing test permits effective assistance or an automatic review safeguard where a demonstrated inability makes request-only review illusory; it does not command a universal hearing schedule on the present record. The practical rule is review of statutory basis, safe placement, attendance risk, duration, and realistic alternatives, with reasons responsive to the child’s circumstances. Fixed invented deadlines or a universal new proof burden would require a separately defended doctrinal change.

The full injunction requiring an added release category is broader than the supplied showing supports, so retain reversal and remand. Preserve the 1987 conditions agreement and genuinely pleaded implementation claims. Stone should neither certify every detention lawful nor narrow relief so far that a proven common defect must be relitigated child by child. No later settlement, detention statute, or later immigration holding belongs in the analysis.

## 46. Saudi Arabia v. Nelson

**Date:** March 23, 1993. **Verdict: Retain the split claim treatment; refine the jurisdictional boundary.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 375–405.

Stone would dismiss intentional-tort claims based on Saudi police detention and torture, while allowing the independent United States recruitment-omission claims past the asserted immunity bar. Master §14 expressly distinguishes a torture prohibition from jurisdiction over a foreign sovereign. Thus refusing to invent a torture exception is alignment, not a retreat from his constitutional floor.

FSIA §§1603–1606 supply the operative framework. *Weltover* requires attention to the nature of the relevant activity; *Bancec* separates substantive liability from immunity. The warning theory’s factual basis is recruitment in the American labor market, not merely a commercial motive for police action. The difficult issue is whether that omission is an independent basis of the claim. [Nelson, 507 U.S. 349, 359–363, 370–376](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/349/).

**Recommended rule:** For each claim and defendant, identify the conduct constituting the alleged breach, its commercial nature, the defendant’s legally attributable participation, and its substantial United States contact. A recruiter’s allegedly tortious omission during commercial recruitment can qualify even where another sovereign act contributed to the injury. Merely relabeling detention as a failure to announce a future detention cannot.

That route is legally stronger than importing the FTCA’s differently worded intentional-tort exception wholesale. It is also narrower than treating American recruitment as jurisdiction over every later employment injury. An independently actionable commercial undertaking supplies the limiting principle; applicable tort law must still establish duty, knowledge, causation, and defenses. *Sheridan* additionally shows why independent negligence and an immune intentional act are not invariably the same claim. [Sheridan v. United States, 487 U.S. 392](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/487/392/).

Stone should avoid suggesting either that pleading a duty conclusively establishes disputed jurisdictional facts or that the claimant must win the entire tort case before obtaining jurisdiction. On the alleged record, allow the warning claims to proceed to the appropriate merits and preclusion inquiries, including the HCA judgment; direct no liability or damages. This is a reasoned resolution of the statute’s contested scope, not adoption of the historical Nelson decision as entering law. Going further by deeming all employer retaliation commercial would lose the statutory distinction between purpose and nature and is unnecessary to preserve the genuine recruitment claim.

## 47. Cincinnati v. Discovery Network

**Date:** March 24, 1993. **Verdict: Retain.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 423–441.

Stone would maintain relief against the selective removal of commercial-publication newsracks. Master Speech and §17 support this application: truthful commercial information is protected, and the government’s distinction must actually serve its asserted physical safety and aesthetic aims.

*Central Hudson* and *Fox* govern commercial speech, while *Ward* applies only to genuinely content-neutral restrictions. The city removed 62 commercial racks while leaving roughly 1,500–2,000 others, without identifying a corresponding difference in obstruction or appearance. The legal defect is the unsupported fit of the chosen content distinction, not a numerical requirement that every regulation eliminate a specified fraction of a problem. [Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410, 417–430](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/410/).

**Recommended rule and extent:** A commercial classification cannot establish reasonable fit merely by asserting that commercial speech has lesser value when the interest concerns physical features unrelated to that classification. The government may regulate incrementally, but it must explain the selected line through the interest its rule actually addresses. Dimensions, placement, maintenance, and obstruction provide administrable criteria for future regulation; they illustrate workable options rather than impose a least-restrictive-means test.

Stone should affirm the existing relief and leave lawful regulatory choices to the city. He need not abolish the commercial-speech category, demand strict scrutiny for all advertising restrictions, or prohibit incremental safety measures. Existing doctrine gives his principle full effect; a broader replacement would complicate regulation without correcting an additional defect presented here.

## 48. Pioneer Investment Services Co. v. Brunswick Associates Limited Partnership

**Date:** March 24, 1993. **Verdict: Retain; identify the interpretive formulation as this case’s resolution.** Local position: CHUNK4_STONE, lines 459–477.

Stone would allow the late proofs of claim because the particular neglect is excusable, while attributing counsel’s actions to the creditors. This matches Master §§2, 17, and 18: apply the text’s genuine discretion through identifiable circumstances without creating a general exemption for attorney mistakes.

Bankruptcy Rule 9006(b)(1) contains the relevant authorization, subject to its express exceptions; *Link* supports ordinary attribution of counsel’s conduct. Whether “neglect” includes carelessness and how to assess excusability are the disputed interpretive questions. The NEUTRAL packet’s familiar factor formulation should not be represented as an already controlling Supreme Court holding before this event. The unusual notice and short delay are concrete case facts. [Pioneer, 507 U.S. 380, 388–399](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/380/).

**Recommended rule:** Neglect includes inadvertence; excusability depends on prejudice, length and operational effect of delay, its explanation including control, and good faith, assessed together. The applicant must explain the failure, and lack of prejudice alone need not excuse an unexplained missed deadline. The rule’s equitable language supports this interpretation; no repudiation of *Link* is necessary.

Require reasoned application of those factors with appropriate appellate review of discretion. That permits predictable distinctions between confusing official notice and an ordinary plainly communicated deadline, without pretending every factual combination yields a mechanical answer. Affirm the allowance here, reserve claim validity and amount, and do not extend this permission to deadlines whose own governing rules prohibit enlargement.

## 49. United States v. McDermott

**Date:** March 24, 1993. **Verdict: Refine; retain federal priority.** Local position: CHUNK5_STONE, lines 17–35.

Stone gives the noticed federal tax lien priority in property acquired after notice, notwithstanding the judgment creditor’s earlier docketing. Master §§4 and 18 support separating state-created property rights from federal priority; settled commercial expectations matter but cannot override the enacted lien scheme.

Sections 6321–6323, *Glass City Bank*, and *New Britain* supply the baseline. The omitted difficulty is *United States v. Vermont*: a general lien on all existing property need not be separately levied on a particular asset to become choate. Stone should distinguish that proposition from a lien reaching property the debtor does not yet own. He must also acknowledge that the federal lien attaches to the later-acquired property at acquisition; its earlier notice establishes priority under the statutory scheme, not earlier ownership of the asset. [McDermott, 507 U.S. 447, 449–455](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/447/).

**Recommended rule:** Against the judgment-lien category at issue, compare federal notice with the time the competing lien becomes complete as to the disputed property. Resolve the after-acquired-property question openly instead of suggesting the exact comparison was already settled by *New Britain*. The rule is administrable through filing, assessment, and acquisition dates and preserves other express §6323 priorities.

Stone should reverse the competing priority determination and apply that rule to the verified dates. A private-creditor-first alternative is arguable, but does not better explain the property component of choateness and the statutory notice structure. No wider federal preference over every state lien follows.

## 50. Arave v. Creech

**Date:** March 30, 1993. **Verdict: Retain.** Local position: CHUNK5_STONE, lines 55–73.

Stone rejects Idaho’s “cold-blooded, pitiless slayer” construction as inadequate to narrow the utter-disregard aggravator. This directly fits Master Criminal Procedure’s demand for reliable, nonarbitrary capital selection and §§16–17’s insistence that a rule operate through discernible criteria. It does not require Stone to abolish capital punishment or invalidate mental-state aggravators generally.

*Godfrey*, *Maynard*, *Zant*, *Walton*, and *Jeffers* provide the entering framework. The State may cure statutory vagueness through an authoritative narrowing construction, and courts can find mental states using objective evidence. Stone’s objection must therefore be that this actual construction leaves the eligibility boundary unclear, not that every evaluative term or mental-state finding is forbidden. The independent resentencing grounds also remain legally consequential. [Arave, 507 U.S. 463, 470–478, 482–489](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/463/).

**Recommended rule and extent:** An aggravator must identify a distinguishable class through its elements and authoritative construction, allowing trial findings and appellate review to explain why the defendant falls within it. Emotional detachment, lack of pity, premeditation, and rage cannot be interchanged without explaining which condition is required. A later precise state construction could be evaluated separately, subject to its temporal and procedural limits; Stone should not write that state-law replacement himself.

Affirm the vagueness determination and preserve the already-required resentencing. Idaho may rely on other valid, supported aggravators with required mitigation. This is a demanding application of the received framework, not a new capital test or a decision of the reserved as-applied question.

## 51. Delaware v. New York

**Date:** March 30, 1993. **Verdict: Retain.** Local position: CHUNK5_STONE, lines 91–121.

Stone would identify the actual debtor, follow recorded-address priority and then incorporation priority, and reject headquarters or unsupported statistical substitutes. That accords with Master §16’s protection of settled property and transactional rules, Interstate Relations, and §4’s distinction between identifying a debt and allocating sovereign power over it.

*Texas v. New Jersey* and *Pennsylvania v. New York* supply the established priority sequence. The law creating the obligation determines whether an issuer has discharged its debt and an intermediary now owes the beneficial owner. The interstate rule cannot itself manufacture either relationship. [Delaware, 507 U.S. 490, 497–509](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/490/).

**Recommended rule and extent:** Determine the actual obligor and creditor first; apply primary priority where legally adequate evidence establishes the creditor’s relevant address; otherwise use the debtor’s incorporation state, subject to a later superior primary claim. Reliable reconstruction is permissible, but a model distributing money according to likely commercial concentration changes the legal priority rather than proves an address.

The existing system already supplies clear, repeatable criteria. Replacing incorporation with headquarters creates factual uncertainty and disrupts settled allocation without a demonstrated legal defect. Stone should sustain the pertinent exceptions, reject the offered statistical substitution, and return the accounting and address proof to the Master. He should not award every disputed dollar to Delaware or decide an intermediary’s independent ownership claim outside the submitted issues. This is a case where preserving the framework is more coherent than devising a distribution Stone considers fairer.

## 52. Conroy v. Aniskoff

**Date:** March 31, 1993. **Verdict: Retain.** Local position: CHUNK5_STONE, lines 141–159.

Stone applies military-service tolling without requiring proof that service impaired redemption. The position follows Master Statutory Interpretation: statutory language and neighboring provisions define the permission, and judicial sympathy or skepticism about the claimant’s need cannot add an omitted condition.

The then-effective §525 of the Soldiers’ and Sailors’ Civil Relief Act excludes qualifying service from the redemption-period calculation. Other provisions condition relief on impairment or confer discretion. *Boone v. Lightner* construed a different protection and does not transfer its conditions into this one. [Conroy, 507 U.S. 511, 514–518](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/511/).

**Recommended rule and extent:** Verify qualifying service and the redemption period, exclude the service interval, and then apply the remaining state-law requirements. The dates supply a predictable rule without fact-intensive litigation over whether this officer was too busy or too financially constrained to redeem. A person can qualify even when military service did not cause the default; Congress’s categorical line makes that result lawful.

Reverse and remand for the corrected calculation, leaving the tax obligation and any other properly presented title or procedural issue intact. Stone should not enlarge the provision into tax forgiveness or a general exemption from limitations for every military-related dispute. He also should not narrow the enacted protection merely because a hardship test might seem fairer in one instance. No doctrinal change or constitutional ruling is needed.

## 53. United States v. Texas

**Date:** April 5, 1993. **Verdict: Refine the historical account; retain reversal and remand.** Local position: CHUNK5_STONE, lines 179–197.

Stone treats the Debt Collection Act’s exclusion of States from mandatory statutory interest as distinct from abolishing an independently grounded common-law claim. That fits Master §§4 and 18 and Statutory Interpretation. The qualification is that the exact reach of state interest liability was contested; he should not say Congress in 1982 necessarily preserved every feature later articulated in *West Virginia*.

Sections 3701 and 3717 define the statutory obligation. *Board of Commissioners* recognizes common-law interest principles, *West Virginia* addresses States’ obligations to the United States, and *Isbrandtsen* supplies the presumption against implied displacement. The food-stamp debt’s statutory setting must also be considered. [Texas, 507 U.S. 529, 534–539, 541–544](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/529/).

**Recommended rule:** The exclusion removes the Act’s mandatory assessment mechanism; it does not by itself extinguish common-law interest on a matured federal debt. Explain why the preexisting federal-debt principles extend to this obligation, including the compensation for delay rationale, rather than derive liability from congressional silence alone. Preserve whatever qualifications the applicable common-law rule actually permits; do not substitute unrestricted equitable balancing where precedent has rejected it.

Remand for interest entitlement and calculation under that source, not automatic application of §3717’s rate and penalties. Due date, amount outstanding, delay, and legally relevant equitable circumstances make the inquiry manageable. Broader creation of federal common law whenever federal money is affected would conflict with Stone’s method and is unnecessary.

## 54. United States v. Green

**Date:** April 5, 1993. **Verdict: Retain; correct the authority’s caption.** Local position: CHUNK5_STONE, lines 217–247.

Stone ends review after Green’s death and reserves the interrogation question. This squarely follows Master §3 and Courts: a compelling constitutional issue does not survive the loss of a live controversy merely because the arguments have already been heard.

The proper source correction is **Mintzes v. Buchanon, 471 U.S. 154 (1985)**, not “Mintzes v. Clark” as the NEUTRAL packet states. The reported death occurred on March 24, 1993. The relevant procedural object is the grant of certiorari and petition; it is not an automatic vacatur of the lower judgment or adjudication of abatement. [Green, 507 U.S. 545](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/507bv.pdf#page=625).

**Recommended disposition and extent:** Vacate the grant and dismiss the petition as moot. Do not announce an alternative holding about whether resolution of an earlier prosecution ends *Edwards* protection during continuous custody. A recurring issue is still advisory in this proceeding when no requested criminal relief can operate against the deceased respondent.

There is no lawful reason for Stone to go further on the merits, and no doctrinal replacement would improve this disposition. The practical rule is straightforward: identify the supervening event, whether any live relief remains, and the exact order being vacated. This preserves future adjudication in a live case while avoiding an unsupported extension of *Munsingwear* or later interrogation doctrine.

## 55. Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins

**Date:** April 20, 1993. **Verdict: Retain and make the proxy inquiry concrete.** Local position: CHUNK5_STONE, lines 267–285.

Stone distinguishes interference with service-based pension vesting from discrimination because of age, while preserving proof of an age proxy and the ordinary *Thurston* willfulness standard. ## Prioritized findings for integration

1. **Stinson requires the clearest revision.** Preserve the existing independent-reading language, but make its legal conclusion decisive enough to justify vacatur; confront *Williams*, §3553(b), and §1B1.7 before declining the extension of mandatory regulatory deference.
2. **CSX permits a narrower, source-based approach to speed preemption.** Preserve the warning-device result. Examine whether the actual speed claim targets crossing conditions outside the track-class regulation’s coverage; remand rather than manufacture that theory or direct negligence liability.
3. **Flores needs a fuller account of liberty within the facial analysis.** The present family-placement remedy need not stand, but temporary lawful custody does not automatically answer every narrower mandatory-detention challenge. Apply ordinary due-process doctrine to a demonstrated inability to obtain review.
4. **Smith’s venue explanation needs correction.** Oregon supplied venue for this claimant. The serious issue is the FTCA’s territorial and choice-of-law structure; any nonresident venue gap is only contextual.
5. **Nelson and Reves have defensible, principled room for Stone’s positions.** Preserve independent recruitment duties and actual outsider direction respectively, with source-specific elements and factual limits; neither requires categorical immunity or boundless private liability.
6. **Brecht’s Chapman position is legally available at the event.** It requires a reasoned choice on the unresolved collateral question, not a claim that earlier practice conclusively settled that question. Withrow must use the actual effective same-day law, not assume Stone’s Brecht proposal was adopted.
7. **Correct Green’s citation to Mintzes v. Buchanon.** The NEUTRAL packet’s “Mintzes v. Clark” caption is wrong.
8. **Preserve the many aligned positions.** The Master often calls for using the existing procedural, statutory, or immunity framework with precision. A different result is not warranted simply to make Stone more distinctive. Current-event formulations in Pioneer, PRE, Olano, and similar packets should be identified as proposed resolutions rather than imported entering holdings.
