## Shaw: the selected rule and its consequences

The former Shaw supplement recognized a racial-assignment injury and proposed two remedial tiers. The replacement implements the user's expressly selected rule: **affirm dismissal of the racial-design-only equal-protection claim**, while preserving challenges to exclusion, intentional dilution, unequal participation, and imposed civic inequality under their applicable doctrines.

This does not declare the map compliant with every legal requirement. Invalidation solely because of racial territorial design would contradict the supplied baseline. A properly presented and established independent deprivation could support relief under its own rule. Recognizing assignment itself as the injury would instead change the baseline and, at this pleading posture, allow further adjudication rather than establish final map invalidity. [Reported posture, Shaw, 509 U.S. 630, 636–642, 658 (1993)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/630/).

The revision adds:

- Direct treatment of the *Wright* and *Croson* counterarguments, and the limits of fractured *UJO* reasoning.
- Ordinary notice pleading, substance over labels, and Rule 15 amendment treatment.
- An identifiable governmental-treatment requirement for the proposed civic-status category, without demanding economic loss or a changed election result.
- Separate inquiries into injury, justification, statutory compulsion, and compliance.
- Relief tied to the established wrong; a representative-access violation does not automatically require a new map.

## Hicks v1.3 and the OT1991 dependency review

The user's attached Hicks proposal replaces the former permissible-inference rule. Stone now assigns the employer the burden of persuasion on the absence of prohibited discriminatory cause only after the employee proves the prima facie case and affirmatively disproves every material explanation the employer offered. The replacement states the departure from *Burdine*, its Title VII basis, the limits of Rule 301 and *Price Waterhouse*, the treatment of multiple reasons and factual mistakes, fair notice, possible focused reopening, and the independent treatment of §1983 and remedies.
