## 109. Godinez v. Moran

**Date:** June 24, 1993. **Assessment: REFINE; retain reversal and remand.**

**Existing position and alignment.** Stone rejects a categorically higher competence threshold for a plea or counsel waiver, but insists on separate capacity and knowing, intelligent, voluntary waiver inquiries. That fits §§3–4 and Criminal Procedure: a competence label cannot do the work of an actual waiver. His remand also correctly avoids treating medication, psychiatric reports, or short answers as individually conclusive. The record includes explicit waiver findings that must be evaluated, not ignored. Only the sentence for the ex-wife's murder had been replaced with life without parole; the two other death sentences remained. The draft's singular reference to a reduced death sentence should identify that count to avoid suggesting all capital exposure had disappeared. [Reported proceedings](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/389/).

**1993 law and available reach.** *Dusky*, *Pate*, *Drope*, *Faretta*, *Boykin*, and *Westbrook* supply the relevant protections; the equivalence of capacity standards is the question being resolved, not an already settled Supreme Court rule. *Westbrook*, 384 U.S. 150 (1966), supports an additional waiver inquiry without necessarily demanding a higher mental-capacity category. Declining an uninvoked *Teague* defense is available under *Collins v. Youngblood*, 497 U.S. 37, 41 (1990).

**Recommended reasoning and administration.** Retain the common capacity threshold, expressly preserving its **rational**, present understanding requirement. Require the remand court to decide whether the contemporaneous circumstances called for further inquiry, whether a reliable retrospective determination remains possible, and whether each actual relinquishment was informed and voluntary. Old reports cannot answer a materially changed mental condition. This implements existing inquiries through the plea record and relevant medical evidence; it requires neither a hearing at every plea nor an intelligence or legal-skill threshold. A new universal competence tier offers less practical protection than enforcing these existing distinctions. Do not predecide relief before applying pre-AEDPA habeas rules to the findings.

## 110. Heller v. Doe

**Date:** June 24, 1993. **Assessment: REVISE the proof-burden analysis; retain the separate family-participation analysis.**

**Existing position and alignment.** Stone would uphold both procedural distinctions while preserving individual commitment safeguards. His refusal to install his preferred disability scrutiny tier through an issue raised late is consistent with §§3 and 6. His rejection of automatic family control is consistent with Procedural Due Process. The weakness is substantive: the draft accepts easier diagnosis as a sufficient reason to give one class less protection against confinement without adequately examining what the burden of proof protects.

**A stronger position is available within received law.** *Cleburne* permits rational-basis review that rejects irrelevant differences and prejudice; it does not require a new scrutiny tier or a trial-style legislative record. *Addington* establishes a due-process minimum and explains the allocation of error risk when liberty is at stake. Meeting that minimum does not independently answer whether unequal safeguards are rational. Differences in diagnosing a condition do not automatically justify different proof standards for dangerousness, treatment benefit, and necessity of institutional placement. [Cleburne, 473 U.S. 432 (1985)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/473/432/); [Addington, 441 U.S. 418 (1979)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/441/418/).

**Recommended disposition.** Stone should affirm the invalidation of the unequal proof burdens, reverse the categorical invalidation of family participation, and remand for an appropriately limited remedy and unresolved matters. This is a defensible application choice, not a result compelled by rational-basis doctrine. The strongest opposing argument is that different diagnostic histories and error risks permit the legislature to calibrate safeguards differently. Stone must answer that argument directly: on this scheme, easier identification of one condition does not adequately explain lowering protection across all the commitment predicates when the loss of liberty remains comparable. The contemporaneous disagreement in the published case shows that this route was available in 1993; that disagreement is persuasive analysis, not entering precedent. [Heller record and separate analyses](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/312/).

That answer must encompass the State's additional rationales: a longer behavioral history may improve prediction of dangerousness, and different treatment may be less intrusive. Stone should acknowledge the first as a plausible clinical distinction, then explain why it does not sufficiently justify assigning the individual a greater risk of erroneous institutional confinement. He should reject the second insofar as it discounts comparable loss of liberty on an unsupported categorical assumption about the intrusiveness of treatment. His position concerns the justification for the overall allocation of error risk, not a demand for a separate empirical demonstration for every statutory predicate. Reasonable legislative generalizations remain permissible; no heightened proof requirement or mandatory legislative record is added.

The **equal-protection challenge to differential family-party status** needs its own answer as well. Stone can sustain that distinction because a lifelong developmental condition and continuing family involvement plausibly make relatives' participation especially informative. That rationale concerns gathering relevant information rather than valuing one person's liberty less. The separate *Mathews* conclusion is that such participation does not itself deprive the subject of effective contest before a neutral adjudicator. Neither conclusion makes family preferences evidence of dangerousness or establishes that every actual hearing was fair.

**Practical rule and limit.** An otherwise permissible distinction between conditions must rationally explain **the particular procedural disadvantage imposed**. No presumption that all disability distinctions are invalid, no required scientific study, and no new substantive proof element follows. Family members may provide evidence and participate where counsel, contest, and independent adjudication remain effective; family convenience proves no commitment predicate. On remedy, identify lawful severability and state choices for equalizing safeguards; do not invent a national beyond-reasonable-doubt commitment rule or immediately release everyone without examining the applicable orders. This better preserves Stone's equal-liberty principle without importing his unadopted intermediate tier.

## 111. Buckley v. Fitzsimmons

**Date:** June 24, 1993. **Assessment: REFINE; retain reversal of absolute immunity and remand.**

**Alignment.** The distinction between investigative fabrication, press statements, and advocacy closely follows Stone's Remedies domain. His refusal to turn reversal into damages also respects §§4 and 19. The pleaded bootprint fabrication occurred in evidence gathering before probable cause; press statements have a separate function. [Buckley procedural and factual account](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/259/).

**Historical framework.** *Imbler*, 424 U.S. 409 (1976), protects advocacy even against allegations of wrongdoing; *Burns* and *Forrester* make function decisive. The law does not contain Stone's general exception for knowing, material official falsification in protected advocacy or testimony. The investigative route resolves the presented immunity question without changing that broader rule. [Burns, 500 U.S. 478 (1991)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/500/478/).

**Refinement and realistic extent.** Replace any implication that Stone endorses immunity for deliberate advocacy falsification with a precise reservation: this judgment decides the investigative acts and press conference, and does not resolve his proposed exception in a case where absolute protection would otherwise attach. Identify function from what the prosecutor was doing; absence of probable cause is relevant here, not an automatic rule stripping immunity from every unsuccessful charging decision. Later presentation cannot immunize independently actionable earlier fabrication.

If the pleaded act were instead classified as advocacy, Stone's alternative would require an express *Imbler* modification: knowing material falsification, an independently actionable deprivation, and causation, with ordinary pleading and proof requirements. Its legal argument is that §1983's accountability function and adjudicative accuracy are undermined by protecting deliberate corruption; its serious obstacle is statutory stare decisis and the established protection against vexatious retrials. That broader change is unnecessary here. Qualified immunity under *Harlow/Anderson*, witness immunity, and whether press statements establish more than reputational injury remain separate. A function-specific remand is predictable and preserves the strongest lawful present remedy.

## 112. Shalala v. Schaefer

**Date:** June 24, 1993. **Assessment: RETAIN.**

Stone would sustain the fee award's timeliness on the missing separate-judgment ground and distinguish sentence-four from sentence-six remands. This aligns with statutory fidelity, practical access, and remedial discipline. The district court's failure to enter the required judgment cannot silently start and expire a statutory fee period. [Case record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/292/).

The relevant sources are 42 U.S.C. §405(g), 28 U.S.C. §2412(d), then-effective Rule 58, the governmental appeal period, and *Melkonyan v. Sullivan*, 501 U.S. 89 (1991). A sentence-four remand ends the civil action by judgment; a sentence-six remand involves the statute's specified grounds and later return. *Sullivan v. Hudson*, 490 U.S. 877 (1989), requires explanation rather than a categorical declaration that all administrative work is compensable or all such work is excluded.

Stone can clarify that obtaining the sentence-four judgment supplies prevailing-party status without waiting for eventual benefits, while retaining EAJA eligibility, substantial justification, and amount inquiries. The precise prevailing-party and clock application is the resolution he proposes here, not a same-case precedent already binding him. Judgment entry and expiration of appeal time provide administrable events. No later 150-day deemed-entry rule belongs in this case. Expanding administrative-stage fees beyond the authorized scope would sacrifice the statutory principle rather than preserve it.

## 113. United States v. Edge Broadcasting Co.

**Date:** June 25, 1993. **Assessment: RETAIN, with a precise advancement explanation.**

The draft sustains the restriction under *Central Hudson* and *Fox*, rejecting a categorical vice exception. That is compatible with Stone's protection of truthful commercial speech and his acceptance of ordinary lawful regulation. Most of Edge's audience was in Virginia, but its contribution to listening in the affected North Carolina area was substantial enough to require actual assessment. Neither percentage alone decides fit. [Record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/418/).

The predecision framework requires a substantial interest, direct advancement, and a reasonable fit; it does not require perfect effectiveness or the least restrictive conceivable rule. *Posadas*, 478 U.S. 328 (1986), is an obstacle to any sweeping claim that all vice-advertising restrictions were already forbidden, but need not be extended to dispose of this dispute. *Central Hudson*, 447 U.S. 557 (1980), and *Fox*, 492 U.S. 469 (1989), permit this narrower analysis.

Stone should explain why reducing this station's contribution materially advances the accommodated policy despite competing cross-border messages, and why licensing location is reasonably related to that interest despite burdening lawful Virginia listeners. Broadcaster status and federalism do not independently establish either proposition. The draft already identifies the relevant facts and can reasonably sustain the line. A stronger speech-protective application remains legally available if the full record undermines advancement, but the master does not compel it. Retain reversal and remand for any preserved unresolved equality claim; reserve materially different applications. No general ban on as-applied challenges or overruling of *Posadas* is needed.

## 114. TXO Production Corp. v. Alliance Resources

**Date:** June 25, 1993. **Assessment: REFINE; retain a supported punitive-only vacatur and remand.**

Stone's concern about arbitrary punishment, wealth-based redistribution, and outsider hostility aligns with §§10, 17, and Remedies. He properly recognizes potential harm without turning $19,000 in compensation into a mathematical ceiling. The $10 million award and the actual guidance and review require examination. [TXO record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/443/).

*Haslip* supports due-process review of punitive discretion, but neither imposes a universal checklist nor requires jurors to explain their calculations. Stone should remove any suggestion that the constitutional defect is the absence of proof of what the jury privately thought. The question is whether the permissible evidentiary basis, instructions, and judicial review adequately constrain the amount. A reviewing court may uphold a general verdict on its supported record without reconstructing juror deliberations. [Haslip, 499 U.S. 1 (1991)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/499/1/).

The specific instructional objections also present a genuine preservation dispute. *Yee*, 503 U.S. 519 (1992), permits new arguments supporting a preserved claim; it does not automatically defeat an independent state procedural default. Stone should identify the objections actually presented, distinguish an argument from a new claim, and explain why the challenged review remains reachable. Otherwise confine his holding to the preserved adequacy/excessiveness challenge.

**Practical direction:** require the state court to identify supported threatened harm, culpability, and legitimate deterrence reasons, confront the instruction's redistribution risk, and determine whether ordinary state procedures can correct the award. A new punitive trial follows only if necessary. Do not import later *BMW* guideposts, a single-digit presumption, a compulsory special verdict, or federal review of every punitive award. Stone may demand meaningful review more rigorously than the draft's opponents without manufacturing findings or a dollar cap.

## 115. St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks

**Date:** June 25, 1993. **Assessment: RETAIN.**

The revised draft accurately balances Stone's commitments: circumstantial proof must remain usable, and falsity is not automatically discrimination. Vacatur and remand under a permissible-inference rule fits §§8, 17, Courts, and Statutory Interpretation. The packet's references to former user directions are drafting history; the position stands on its reasoning in this review.

*McDonnell Douglas*, *Burdine*, and *Aikens* preserve the employee's ultimate persuasion burden while requiring a specific employer explanation at production. *Burdine* permits proof through discrediting that explanation. Stone can reject both compulsory liability and a universal additional-direct-evidence requirement without a new burden shift. [Burdine, 450 U.S. 248 (1981)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/450/248/).

The trial court disbelieved the disciplinary explanations but found no racial motive; the appellate court stopped at a compulsory inference rather than completing review. Stone should require Rule 52(a) review of the actual finding, giving prima facie evidence and proven falsity their full inferential force. A personal-animosity explanation must be grounded in the record and fairly open to contest. The plaintiff need not refute invented lawful motives. [Hicks proceedings](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/502/).

The rule is usable at trial: the factfinder may infer discrimination from the complete circumstantial record, but is not commanded to do so. Stronger language that pretext can itself suffice is warranted; automatic judgment for Hicks is not. Keep the Title VII and §1983 defendants and remedies distinct, accepting their unchallenged common intentional-discrimination analysis only for this case. Later employment cases provide no necessary authority.

## 116. Shaw v. Reno

**Date:** June 28, 1993. **Assessment: REVISE substantially; the draft conflicts with the current master on both injury and justification.**

**What conflicts.** The draft would revive a racial-assignment claim based on personally experienced racial predominance in districting, then use intermediate scrutiny for structural repair and strict scrutiny for individual allocations. The current Elections domain expressly says territorial racial predominance alone is not an electoral deprivation; a claimant must identify exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, or another independently cognizable impairment. Current §9 also allows supported repair affecting individual contracts, admissions, or positions without making individuality select a stricter tier. These are direct differences in Stone's stated rules, not nuances of application.

**What remains sound.** Preserve the independent dismissal of the federal-preclearance claims under the Act's review arrangements and *Morris v. Gressette*, 432 U.S. 491 (1977). Preserve the distinction between §2, §5, and constitutional claims, and between allegation and proof. Reject racial entitlement to representation, automatic proportionality, and automatic approval from DOJ preclearance. [Shaw record and questions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/630/).

**The better-grounded 1993 path.** Before *Shaw* itself is decided, no controlling Supreme Court holding requires the new nondilution assignment injury. *Gomillion*, 364 U.S. 339 (1960), concerns actual exclusion through boundaries; *United Jewish Organizations* permits meaningful racial attention in districting and does not make race awareness alone unlawful. Its fractured opinions must not be restated as a universal immunity or a proportional-representation entitlement. [UJO, 430 U.S. 144 (1977)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/430/144/).

The contrary position has antecedent support worth addressing: *Wright v. Rockefeller* entertained a racial-gerrymandering challenge but resolved it on failure to establish the alleged racial purpose. Stone should acknowledge that assumption without treating it as a holding that territorial racial predominance alone establishes injury. His proposed distinction concerns the unestablished impairment in this complaint, not the proposition that districting can never discriminate. [Wright, 376 U.S. 52 (1964)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/376/52/).

Stone should **affirm the state-claim dismissal insofar as the pleaded theory rests on racial composition or predominance without an independently cognizable electoral impairment**. The described complaint supplies no one-person-one-vote or partisan-entrenchment theory to substitute. This is a rejection of the proposed merits theory, not a declaration that racial injuries never establish Article III standing. He should not rely on whites' statewide proportional success as an answer to every possible local injury. If the actual complaint contains a separately sufficient impairment omitted from the packet, address that pleaded claim under its own elements; do not invent one to preserve the draft's remand.

**Proposed operative reasoning.** Territorial boundaries assign all residents of a constituency to the same election regardless of their individual race. Racial attention in choosing those boundaries can prove a prohibited practice, but does not itself establish unequal eligibility or impaired participation. A voter must identify the impairment the governing constitutional or statutory source prohibits. This rule keeps *Gomillion*-type exclusion and intentional dilution fully actionable while avoiding a free-standing judicial compactness or racial-predominance cause of action.

**How far to change doctrine.** Rejecting this newly proposed claim does not require overruling an existing *Shaw* precedent. It also makes a replacement racial-justification framework unnecessary here. Delete the two tiers from Stone's proposed holding. If a later, properly presented case requires his §9 rule against contrary *Croson/Wygant* doctrine, he must openly justify that change through the constitutional repair authorities, operative burdens, and statutory stare decisis where applicable; *UJO* and *Fordice* do not already establish the entire master framework. The present case is not a sound vehicle to decide contracts or admissions.

**Administration.** Require a specified source, personal impairment, causation, and source-specific proof. Map shape and demographics remain evidence, not triggers detached from harm. This gives courts an identifiable legal injury to adjudicate and avoids litigating whether every irregular boundary expresses a racial message. The draft's claims that earlier user instructions dictated its result are source-document history, not instructions overriding this review request.

## 117. United States v. Dixon

**Date:** June 28, 1993. **Assessment: RETAIN; sharpen the count-by-count rule.**

Stone would retain *Grady*, bar Dixon's drug prosecution and Foster's five later counts, and leave the completed contempt proceedings alone. This fits finality, protection against repeated punishment proceedings, and §16's demand for a reason to overrule beyond disagreement.

The entering rule is *Grady v. Corbin*, which supplements *Blockburger* in successive prosecutions; *Felix* distinguishes evidentiary overlap and an independently punishable conspiracy. Nonsummary punitive contempt cannot lose its criminal character because counsel for a protected person initiated it. [Grady, 495 U.S. 508 (1990)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/495/508/); [Dixon/Foster charges and proceedings](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/688/).

State the rule accurately: the later prosecution is barred when proving an essential element would require proving conduct constituting an offense already prosecuted. Identify the conduct constituting each earlier contempt offense, rather than saying any reused evidence suffices. Dixon's possession-with-intent conduct and Foster's two assaults were prosecuted; the three threatened acts had already been tried with acquittals. Adding intent to kill to one later assault charge does not avoid the retained rule when the prosecuted assault supplies the necessary conduct. *Ashe* remains independently available where necessary, without making a redundant factual-estoppel ruling here.

The best objection is administrability and coordination between protection-order proceedings and ordinary prosecution. Stone should acknowledge those costs and explain why charging instruments, the incorporated order, and prior adjudicated conduct permit a workable comparison here. Neither compulsory joinder of an entire episode nor a same-evidence test follows. Maintaining this established protection is a legally realistic choice; no new Stone framework is needed.

## 118. Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals

**Date:** June 28, 1993. **Assessment: RETAIN; make the preliminary proof burden explicit.**

This is a strong example of source-grounded doctrinal correction. Stone would displace an indispensable general-acceptance prerequisite with flexible reliability and relevance review under the enacted Rules. That aligns with §§2, 17–18 and Courts: novelty is neither disqualification nor proof, and judicial screening must preserve the jury's different function.

*Frye* is lower-court common law, not a Supreme Court construction of Rule 702 insulated by statutory stare decisis. The 1993 text requires scientific knowledge and assistance to the factfinder; Rule 104(a) assigns preliminary admissibility to the judge. The proponent's preliminary foundation is established by a **preponderance**, supported by *Bourjaily*, rather than an invented heightened proof threshold. [Bourjaily, 483 U.S. 171 (1987)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/483/171/); [Daubert proffers and original Rules](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/579/).

The proposed correction is better grounded because it follows the Rules' knowledge-and-assistance requirements while allowing reliable work before consensus develops. Explain the relevance of testing, data, peer scrutiny, error information where meaningful, and fit in the particular proffer. No single consideration controls every case. Require an intelligible admissibility explanation; do not adjudicate scientific truth or impose a universal statistical significance threshold.

Vacate and remand for renewed screening and summary-judgment consideration. An animal study or unpublished reanalysis needs a defensible connection to the human-causation inference; neither must be admitted merely because the old rule was wrong. The civil-rights reservation is appropriate but can be shorter: no voting test or legislative-fact regime is decided. Do not import later Rule 702 amendments, *Joiner*, or *Kumho Tire*. This change has an administrable judicial task and a bounded remand.

## 119. Austin v. United States

**Date:** June 28, 1993. **Assessment: REFINE; retain coverage reversal and give modest remand guidance.**

Stone correctly treats punitive function as more important than a civil in-rem caption. The draft aligns with Property, Forfeiture, and Remedies: statutory nexus, constitutional coverage, and excessiveness are different questions. *Browning-Ferris*, 492 U.S. 257 (1989), distinguishes private damages from government exactions; *Calero-Toledo*, 416 U.S. 663 (1974), and the statute's culpability-sensitive provisions make punishment a serious question. *Halper* offers an analogy without transplanting double-jeopardy law. [Austin statute and record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/602/).

The coverage holding is an open constitutional application, not an inherited same-case rule. It is source-grounded: a constitutional limit on excessive fines would be easily evaded if government could impose a punitive property loss under a civil caption.

**Room to go further.** The remand can identify what the lower court must examine without announcing a comprehensive test: the property and interest taken, supported value, seriousness and extent of the offense, the owner's relevant culpability, and the property's actual role in wrongdoing. Explain that facilitation sufficient under the statute does not by itself answer whether the amount taken is excessive. These are proposed, nonexclusive implementation considerations derived from the Clause's comparison of exaction and wrongdoing, not settled 1993 guideposts.

Retain reversal and remand without automatic return, a mandatory ratio, or invented valuation. The government retains statutory enforcement authority within constitutional limits. State incorporation, innocent-owner doctrine, and other constitutional claims remain outside the decision. Administrability improves through explicit findings and a reasoned comparison, not by claiming that later *Bajakajian* already governs.

## 120. Hartford Fire Insurance v. California

**Date:** June 28, 1993. **Assessment: RETAIN; clarify the foreign-commerce reservation.**

The component-specific disposition follows Stone's source separation and ordinary-law commitments. Insurance status is not blanket immunity; participation by a foreign reinsurer does not automatically defeat an exemption for otherwise qualifying activity. *Royal Drug* and *Pireno* support examination of the activity, risk arrangement, and state regulation, while §3(b)'s boycott exception requires its own analysis.

The collateral-refusal boundary is a defensible statutory clarification informed by *Barry*, 438 U.S. 531 (1978), rather than a reason to call every joint demand a boycott. Preserve the distinction between the broader-pressure allegations and the model-language counts. The filed complaints and reviewed questions support the draft's separate treatment of the domestic and London components. [Hartford Fire record and count structure](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/764/).

Stone can allow the London claims to proceed on deliberately directed American effects while rejecting the proposition that British permission necessarily defeats American law. He should keep distinct prescriptive reach, subject-matter jurisdiction, and any comity limitation. If §6a applies, address **all** applicable requirements, including the connection between the qualifying domestic effect and the asserted claim; listing directness, substantiality, and foreseeability alone is not the complete statute. It is also permissible to explain why a disputed §6a issue is unnecessary on this posture.

Retain the docket-specific partial affirmance/reversal for domestic counts and affirmance of continued proceedings on the foreign claims. No antitrust violation is yet proved. A transaction-and-count analysis is more predictable than an entity-wide exemption or an unrestricted judicial balancing of national interests. Broader comity questions and unreviewed state-law claims remain for an actual need to decide them.

## 121. Alexander v. United States

**Date:** June 28, 1993. **Assessment: REFINE substantially; retain forfeiture vacatur, add an express First Amendment inquiry.**

The draft properly separates postconviction punishment from a categorical prior restraint and recognizes that punitive criminal forfeiture receives Excessive Fines review. But it risks making the Eighth Amendment remand carry the entire protection for lawful expression. The master Speech domain protects publication against sanctions that burden that function; rejecting the prior-restraint label does not finish the First Amendment analysis.

**Available law.** *Arcara* involved a sanction for nonexpressive unlawful conduct and left room for scrutiny where expression or singling out expressive activity draws the sanction. *Fort Wayne Books* did not settle the constitutionality of every postconviction forfeiture of lawful stock. Their distinctions give Stone a narrower source-grounded route than either immunizing all publishers or describing every punishment as prior censorship. [Arcara, 478 U.S. 697 (1986)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/478/697/); [Fort Wayne Books, 489 U.S. 46 (1989)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/489/46/).

**Proposed refinement.** A valid obscenity conviction permits the punishment and forfeiture the law authorizes, but does not itself establish that suppressing or destroying lawful expressive inventory is constitutionally permissible. On remand distinguish the transfer of a forfeitable economic interest from elimination of protected expression; examine statutory nexus and whether ordinary asset realization or other lawful means can accomplish the punishment without gratuitous suppression. The reported destruction of inventory makes this a concrete question, not a hypothetical future-publication dispute. [Alexander record](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/544/).

**Trigger and standard.** Stone should expressly apply *Arcara*'s distinction: an ordinary sanction for nonexpressive misconduct does not trigger special First Amendment scrutiny merely because a publisher loses assets. Here expressive predicates drew the sanction, and removal of lawful inventory presents a distinct expressive burden. For a genuinely content-neutral incidental burden within that trigger, use *O'Brien*: lawful authority, an important or substantial interest unrelated to suppressing expression, and an incidental restriction no greater than essential to that interest. If the challenged measure instead suppresses works because of their protected content, the content-based rule demands compelling justification and narrow tailoring. Ordinary asset forfeiture receives no universal least-restrictive-means requirement. [O'Brien, 391 U.S. 367 (1968)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/391/367/).

Applying that distinction to this postconviction enterprise sanction is Stone's proposed constitutional application, not a previously settled protected-inventory exception. The opposing argument—that valid obscenity predicates and neutral RICO forfeiture fully account for the burden—must be answered through the particular scope and operation, not the mere presence of books. The remand must also distinguish the judicial forfeiture order from the Government's subsequent administrative choice to destroy stock. Determine which injury the challenged judgment caused, what aspects are properly reviewable here, and what correction is still available. This appeal does not independently authorize damages for destruction. Lawful expressive assets are not immune from every neutral economic forfeiture, and obscenity convictions are not reopened merely because forfeiture is overbroad.

Retain vacatur and remand for both separately necessary inquiries, with any correction tailored to assets and relief actually available; do not pretend destroyed inventory can simply be returned. An Excessive Fines inquiry remains independent, without borrowing same-day *Austin* as effective law or importing a later numerical/proportionality formula.

## 122. Delo v. Blair

**Date:** July 21, 1993. **Assessment: CONDITIONAL; the proposed denial requires a legally substantial basis, not just nonfrivolousness.**

Stone would leave the appellate stay in place while innocence and procedural availability receive examination. That fits his reliability and meaningful-review commitments. The packet correctly identifies seven affidavits, a third habeas petition, and an approximately five-week stay; their number is not proof of innocence. [Published application record](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/93A-69.ZPC.html).

*Barefoot*, 463 U.S. 880 (1983), *Delo v. Stokes*, 495 U.S. 320 (1990), and *McCleskey*, 499 U.S. 467 (1991), constrain successive capital proceedings. The clear-abuse wording invoked in the packet comes from separate writings and should not be labeled an unqualified controlling majority rule. The existence and scope of an earlier effective simulated *Herrera* ruling materially affect the available claim; neither its historical result nor Stone's prepared position can fill that gap.

The proposed holding says a nonfrivolous innocence contention warrants examination. That is too weak if understood as the substantive basis for sustaining the whole stay: nonfrivolousness does not replace the substantial-ground requirement in this posture. Stone should identify what admissible or potentially testable evidence could satisfy the applicable merits/gateway rule, why the successive-petition bar does not clearly foreclose it, and why the existing review interval is within lawful discretion.

Retain denial **if those conditions hold**. If the necessary affidavits or effective law cannot be assessed, only a genuinely necessary, promptly reconsidered preservation interval is presently defensible; absence of a record does not prove entitlement to five weeks. No freestanding innocence rule, factual exoneration, automatic hearing, or habeas writ should emerge from this application. The uncertainty is case-specific and material, not an excuse to avoid reviewing the proposed rationale.

## 123. DeBoer v. DeBoer

**Date:** July 26, 1993. **Assessment: RETAIN; state next-friend standing affirmatively.**

The denial of the child's A-64 stay is consistent with Stone's family-integrity principle as bounded by jurisdiction and legally established relationships. The child's attachment and claimed disruption matter, but do not extinguish the biological parents' unextinguished legal interests or give Michigan authority to relitigate custody simply because it prefers another placement. The packet correctly keeps the DeBoers' A-65 application separate. [A-64 record](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/93A-64.ZA.html).

The channels are §§1257 and 2101(f), the applicable interstate-custody framework including §1738A where applicable, and the ordinary stay requirements. *Stanley*, *Santosky*, and *Lehr* protect family interests without creating a free-standing Supreme Court best-interests appeal. *Whitmore*, 495 U.S. 149 (1990), requires a proper next friend; it is preferable to identify the child's inability to litigate personally and the representative's adequate dedication rather than accept representation merely because no defect was proved.

Stone should distinguish a legally identifiable defect in custody enforcement or representation from serious harm caused by implementing a lawful judgment. Recognizing a new child-specific substantive veto over transfer would require reconciling parental rights, finality, and interstate jurisdiction on a far fuller merits record. This emergency application does not justify that step. A temporary transition safeguard could be considered only through an available channel, with a concrete legal basis and necessity; attachment alone supplies neither. Deny A-64, recognize the child as a rights-holder, and make no new custody decree. That boundary is administrable without dismissing the child's interests as irrelevant.
