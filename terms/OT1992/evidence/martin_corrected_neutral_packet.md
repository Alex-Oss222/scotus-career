## No. 92-5584: Martin v. District of Columbia Court of Appeals and others

**Actual route:** Petition for certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, No. 91-7176, concerning D.D.C. Civil Action No. 89-2789. The named respondent is the local court, but this petition is not direct review of a local-court judgment. The petition invokes 28 U.S.C. §1254(1).

**Judgment below:** The attached October 4, 1991 D.D.C. order dismisses under Rule 12(b)(1), with prejudice, for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction under D.C. Court of Appeals v. Feldman, 460 U.S. 462 (1983). It notes analogous Maryland/Virginia federal suits dismissed for want of jurisdiction. A May 8, 1992 D.C. Circuit order (Wald, D.H. Ginsburg, Sentelle) summarily affirms substantially for the district court's reasons. July 24 rehearing orders follow. Appendix pages 1a, 3a, 4a–5a supply these sources; the printed appendix is not continuously paginated.

**Present IFP request:** An August 1992 motion and financial affidavit seek filing without prepayment. No source supplied here contradicts indigence. A district judge had allowed IFP on appeal in 1989; that does not conclusively determine this Court's separate motion.

**Questions actually raised:** The petition asks about applying the ADA to bar admission, federal jurisdiction over a prolonged and eventually adverse bar-admission proceeding, abstention/comity, and judicial immunity from declaratory or injunctive relief. The petition alleges it passed the D.C. bar examination and that a years-long investigation relied on disputed medical-history characterizations. Those are petitioner's allegations, not new Supreme Court findings.

**Material contents:**

- At pp. 33–42, petitioner invokes the ADA and Civil Rights Act of 1991 as intervening law and advances a retroactivity theory. He mainly quotes employment provisions and legislative commentary. No merits conclusion on ADA coverage, retroactivity, preservation, or immunity follows from this packet.
- At pp. 43–48, petitioner disputes the lower courts' Feldman application. At p.44 he quotes complaint paragraph 60 as challenging a purported general classification differentiating adverse neuropsychiatric history from physical impairments. He says that classification can be reviewed independently of his individual admission result. His overall demand also seeks redress for his own denial and associated damages.
- At pp.50–58, he distinguishes review of a particular admission decision from challenges to general rules, extended delay, and injunctive/declaratory relief; he invokes pre-divergence bar-admission cases and immunity authorities. Some characterizations are substantially overstated, but disagreement or poor presentation does not itself establish Rule 39.8 frivolousness.
- A long factual account and collateral litigation occupy much of the petition. The petition incorporates references to earlier files, including No.91-5476; their contents are not reproduced in full. The complete operative complaint is absent from this recovered component, apart from quoted passages. Thus the full relation between every original count and the individual admission decision is not independently reconstructed here.

**Distinct lawful paths:** (1) Grant this IFP motion and process the petition normally, because at least the general-rule/jurisdiction and prospective statutory arguments have an arguable legal basis that is not resolved by a numerical filing history; this decides neither certiorari nor any merits issue. (2) Deny this IFP motion if the Court concludes, from the particular filing rather than prior denials alone, that all presented claims are frivolous repetitions of a personal collateral attack and that the new statutes provide no arguable distinct ground. The latter requires addressing the quoted general-rule claim and distinct forms of relief rather than treating the district court's jurisdictional dismissal as proof of frivolousness. The record supports ordinary denial of certiorari without necessarily supporting fee denial, but a petition-stage disposition is outside the current prepared event.

## No. 92-5618: Martin v. McDermott and Delaware Volunteer Legal Services

**Actual route:** August 3, 1992 certiorari petition to the Delaware Supreme Court, No.448,1991, invoking §1257(a). Attached orders establish the relevant lower proceeding and the requested recusal.

**Judgment below:** The May 26, 1992 Delaware Supreme Court order affirms a jurisdictional dismissal. The case began as a Justice of the Peace debt action. The purported Superior Court appeal changed both defendants and claims, adding a legal-services entity and interference-with-contract/Rule11 theories. Under 10 Del. C. §9570, as construed by Cooper's Home Furnishings v. Smith, 250 A.2d 507 (Del. Super.1969), and Dzedzej v. Prusinski, 259 A.2d 384 (Del. Super.1969), a de novo appeal must retain the same action and parties. The state supreme court therefore held the Superior Court lacked jurisdiction. It separately characterized the trial-judge recusal claim as meritless and moot. Rehearing was denied June5 for failure to satisfy the specified state rule's grounds.

**Petition's issue:** Whether a judge affiliated with a federally funded law school must recuse when the applicant alleges the school discriminated against him. The petition describes commencement appearances, honors/gifts, professional associations, and a disputed report of an adverse comment attributed to the judge. Some Delaware appellate judges recused; substitute judges were designated. The petition quotes Aetna Life Insurance Co. v. Lavoie, 475 U.S.813 (1986), about constitutionally disqualifying interests. It does not demonstrate that the challenged judge had a direct personal pecuniary interest like Aetna's, nor attack the independent state-law jurisdictional ground in a developed federal argument. Petitioner's allegations of school misconduct remain disputed allegations.

**Present IFP motion:** The August3 motion seeks fee waiver; it says fees below were paid and refers to a financial statement. The recovered component does not independently reproduce every financial exhibit. No evidence that its poverty claim is false has been identified.

**Distinct lawful paths:** (1) Deny IFP under Rule39.8 on the conclusion that the actual petition's due-process recusal theory supplies no arguable avenue past the adequate independent jurisdictional ground; alleged general professional association does not itself establish the constitutional conflict claimed. This is a fee determination, not adoption of all the state court's rhetoric or a Supreme Court merits judgment. (2) Permit IFP and dispose of the petition through ordinary processing; the strongest contrary point is that the direct constitutional challenge to the decisionmaker need not be treated as frivolous simply because the claim ultimately fails, and the petitioner should not be required to pay merely for access. The court can choose ordinary petition denial without fee sanctions.

## Entering law and procedural limits

All nine Justices are seated; no established nonparticipation applies. Five decide a filing motion. This is an ordinary motion to the Court under Rule39, not interim relief. Standing State's interim-application referral rule does not itself require a fictitious personal presentation of an IFP motion. No argument or certiorari grant is implied.

**Simulated Zatko:** `state/HOLDINGS.md:1221-1240`, November4,1991. White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter joined the rule allowing discretionary fee denial where the current submissions are frivolous and extreme repetitive abuse is established. It creates no numerical threshold, no conclusive effect of earlier denials, and no blanket prospective filing restriction. The seventeen identified fee decisions and Martin's documented pre-Zatko history are established. No subsequent warning, fee-compliance default, or nine intervening Supreme Court decisions is established. Those events cannot be imported from another timeline. Published noncontrolling positions: `terms/OT1991/output/OT_1991CHUNK1.md:128-146` records Stevens's objection, joined by Blackmun, that history cannot replace current-paper review and a financial barrier can exclude a later colorable claim. Thomas took no part in Zatko.

**Simulated Denton:** `state/HOLDINGS.md:1244-1286`; `state/STANDARDS_AND_TESTS.md:144-160`, Present operation effective May4,1992. O'Connor's opinion, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas among others, preserves the difference between arguable claims and clearly baseless factual allegations. Stevens and Blackmun agree with the factual-screening principle in their separate writing. This is a §1915(d) rule, not an automatic transposition of every remedial feature into Supreme Court Rule39; its distinction between implausibility and frivolousness is materially relevant.

**Pre-divergence authorities read in full:**

- In re McDonald, 489 U.S.180 (1989), official [PDF](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep489/usrep489180/usrep489180.pdf), locally `tmp/usrep489180.pdf`: targeted restriction on extraordinary writs; concrete repeated claim and lack of appropriate extraordinary-writ grounds. Blackmun and Stevens joined Brennan's dissent favoring individualized access and rejecting prospective exclusion. White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy participated in the per curiam majority.
- In re Sindram, 498 U.S.177 (1991), official [PDF](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep498/usrep498177/usrep498177.pdf), locally `tmp/usrep498177.pdf`/`.txt`: continued extraordinary-writ abuse where present legal claims repeat earlier unsuccessful presentations; the opinion emphasizes the special unlimited timing of extraordinary writs and preserves other types of IFP access. Blackmun and Stevens joined Marshall's dissent; Blackmun separately stresses inadequate case-specific justification. White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter participated in the per curiam majority. Thomas was not on the Court.
- In re Amendment to Rule39, 500 U.S.13 (1991), official [PDF](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep500/usrep500013/usrep500013.pdf), locally `tmp/usrep500013.pdf`: Rule39.8 effective July1,1991 permits denial if the particular petition is frivolous or malicious. Stevens, joined by Blackmun, separately emphasizes that denying certiorari is easier and legally distinct from finding frivolity.

**Prospective civil-certiorari restriction:** Extension from extraordinary writs to all future civil petitions is a separate question. The strongest supporting position invokes institutional authority and the established pre-Zatko repetitive history; a source-defined earlier-warning requirement has not been established as a universal legal prerequisite. The strongest contrary position invokes the narrower simulated Zatko law, time-limited ordinary certiorari, and the absence of verified post-Zatko escalation. An order against unknown future petitions is not entailed by either present motion's disposition. A decision declining that remedy here need not adopt a categorical constitutional prohibition on future restrictions.
