## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

### Current motion No.92-5584

**Path A — permit fee-waived processing:** The petition identifies a purported general classification and expressly separates prospective/declaratory relief from redress for the particular bar admission. Feldman itself distinguishes general rules from review of particular judgments. The new statutory arguments are overstated and may fail for coverage, preservation, jurisdiction, or temporal reasons, but the supplied orders do not independently settle each claim's frivolity. The actual current paper therefore does more than recite dissatisfaction with an earlier result. IFP leaves certiorari subject to ordinary denial.

**Strongest counterargument:** The petition's lengthy account remains overwhelmingly a personal collateral attack and repeats a litigation campaign. The D.C. Circuit found the jurisdictional answer clear enough for summary affirmance. The applicant chiefly invokes employment statutes without showing an employer relationship. A court need not accept relabeling to evade Feldman.

**Resolution and join barriers:** Those points permit denial of certiorari and dismissal of claims without establishing that every severable general/prospective question lacks an arguable legal basis. The narrow commitment does not find the allegations true, hold that a general rule actually exists, recognize a cause of action, or require review. White/O'Connor/Scalia/Kennedy/Souter/Thomas would refuse a rationale finding broad ADA liability or invalidating judicial immunity. Blackmun/Stevens can additionally rely on their existing preference for ordinary petition screening.

**Path B — deny fee status:** Supported only if the present paper is found wholly frivolous or malicious, rather than merely unlikely to secure certiorari. The record does not establish malice; the asserted general-rule passage and forms of relief prevent the unqualified frivolity determination provisionally. No fee denial may rest solely on the count of earlier petitions.

### Current motion No.92-5618

**Path A — deny this motion:** The recovered appendix shows a final judgment on the independent state-law limit on de novo appellate jurisdiction. The certiorari question instead attacks the trial judge's professional association and uses Aetna without demonstrating a comparable personal interest. The current paper does not explain a federal defect in the dispositive jurisdictional ground or show how the requested disqualification would eliminate it. White/O'Connor/Scalia/Kennedy/Souter/Thomas accept a limited Rule39.8 denial on that present defect, coupled with the established prior filing history.

**Strongest counterargument:** A constitutionally impartial tribunal is a distinct requirement, and a state court's declaration that its own jurisdictional ruling makes bias moot does not automatically answer the due-process challenge. A mistaken or underdeveloped claim is not necessarily frivolous; a pro se litigant should receive ordinary petition processing.

**Resolution and narrower positions:** The six fee-denial commitments depend on the lack of a concrete constitutionally disqualifying interest and the unchanged jurisdictional obstacle together; neither a mere commencement appearance nor the state court's adjective alone is dispositive. Their disposition is fee-only and leaves paid filing available. Blackmun and Stevens accept the stronger individualized-access objection and would grant IFP without granting certiorari or requiring reversal. These six would not join any proposition that all judge/law-school affiliations are immune from due-process scrutiny.

### Prospective civil-certiorari restriction

**Path A — impose restriction:** McDonald and Sindram recognize institutional authority to protect resources and preserve paid access. Their reasoning is not necessarily limited forever to extraordinary writs, and no universal warning requirement has been established. The pre-Zatko record demonstrates extraordinary filing volume.

**Strongest counterargument:** Those cases establish targeted remedies on concrete repeated claims; Sindram specifically discusses extraordinary writs' absence of deadlines. The simulated Court previously confined its order to pending submissions. There is no established subsequent warning, nine intervening simulated denials, or proved breach of that order, and one present filing articulates distinct claims. The particular-file control remains available.

**Resolution and join barriers:** All eight decline extension here. For White/O'Connor/Scalia/Kennedy/Souter/Thomas, the minimum compatible formulation reserves authority and states inadequate demonstrated necessity on this record, rather than declaring every prospective restriction unlawful. For Blackmun/Stevens, a separate position may retain their broader objection that an unknown future filing should not be rejected on earlier history. No Court majority can be inferred from addition of those broader reasons to the six Justices' narrower ground; exact proposition compatibility must be tested after Stone enters.
