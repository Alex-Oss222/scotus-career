#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the Eighth Circuit’s judgment and REMAND for application of ordinary Strickland prejudice without treating circuit precedent as binding on the state court.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The remand must determine whether the existing finding of a likely sustained objection remains supported independent of the erroneous hierarchy. If so, afford conditional relief from the death sentence through lawful resentencing, not an automatic federal direction of life without parole. This remedial consequence is part of the user-directed reviewed proposal.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Prejudice and the relevant legal counterfactual

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved issue within the review posture stated in Section I.

**Stone’s Legal Position and Treatment of Precedent:** Resolve the temporal application of Strickland's prejudice requirement while retaining its concern with a lawful, reliable proceeding. Stone rejects an additional free-standing entitlement screen that automatically erases a legally supportable argument because a court later rejects it. He does not treat every statistically probable favorable error as constitutional prejudice, and he corrects the lower court's treatment of federal circuit authority as binding on a state court.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Later rejection of a legal argument does not automatically erase prejudice from counsel’s deficient failure to present it, but prejudice cannot be proved by falsely treating circuit precedent as binding on a state court.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The strongest objection is that Strickland excludes the effects of lawless decisionmaking, Nix rejects a claimed benefit from perjury, and Kimmelman requires a meritorious underlying Fourth Amendment claim. Stone accepts those constraints: prejudice cannot rest on juror nullification, fabricated evidence, or a ruling forbidden by authority controlling the original state proceeding. The temporal question here is different. A professionally supportable constitutional objection, grounded in then-existing judicial reasoning and legally open for adoption by the state court, is part of effective advocacy even if its interpretation is later rejected. Later doctrinal correction does not automatically establish that losing the original opportunity made no constitutional difference.

Collins supplied persuasive circuit reasoning, not a command binding Arkansas. The remand must therefore reassess the existing finding of a likely sustained objection under the correct hierarchy: identify the argument counsel could properly have made, test it against then-controlling state and Supreme Court law, and assess whether there is a reasonable probability of a different lawful sentencing result. The court must not simply assume obedience to Collins, substitute a numerical odds test for Strickland, or erase the original finding without examining its support. This preserves Strickland's two-part inquiry while giving its temporal counterfactual a legally bounded application. [Strickland](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/466/668/); [Nix](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/475/157/); [Kimmelman](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/477/365/).

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No revival of Collins as current substantive law and no presumption that every omitted argument changes the outcome.

##### Issue 2: Source and scope of habeas remedy

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the remedial issue only to define the consequence of a supported prejudice finding on remand.

**Stone’s Legal Position and Treatment of Precedent:** Apply and explain existing sources; do not announce a general replacement doctrine.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Any established Sixth Amendment violation here warrants a valid replacement sentencing proceeding, not an automatic federal award of the sentence counsel might once have obtained under Collins.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A supported Sixth Amendment finding concerns the fairness and reliability of the original sentencing proceeding; it does not freeze the interpretation in Collins for a new one. Resentencing must use then-applicable law and respect any independently controlling restriction on renewed aggravating findings or available punishment. Stone neither directs life without parole automatically nor authorizes a previously rejected aggravator without that separate legal inquiry.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No judicial invention of new aggravating findings; no treatment of this remand as an authorization to execute before the constitutional claim is resolved.

It supplies no governing rule, factual finding, approval history, or adopted Court holding. ## Fex v. Michigan

#### A. STATUS

**Approval Status:** USER-DIRECTED REVIEW REVISION. Revised under the user's instruction to make the necessary changes. The disposition and reasoning remain Stone's proposed simulated position, not a rendered judgment or an adopted simulated holding.  
**Approval Date or Turn:** Current user-directed implementation turn.  
**Current-Through Baseline:** The sources and cutoff in Section I, subject to actual event-date revalidation.  
**Conditions:** None beyond the event-date, source-state, and record conditions stated in Section I.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone’s Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Michigan judgment and REMAND for application of Article III's clock from delivery of a qualifying request to the responsible custodial official.

**Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply the earlier custodial-delivery date, then determine any Article III good-cause continuance or Article VI exclusion under its own requirements. If the lawful 180-day period expired before trial, Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice. Do not invent a grace period for forwarding, require proof of official bad faith, or award an unpresented damages remedy.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Receipt trigger and statutory consequence

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved issue within the review posture stated in Section I.

**Stone’s Legal Position and Treatment of Precedent:** Resolve an open compact-interpretation question by reading Article III(a)'s prisoner-caused delivery together with Article III(b)'s prescribed custodial forwarding channel and Article IX's direction of liberal construction. No prior Supreme Court actual-receipt holding is overruled. The alternative is grounded in the Agreement's text and allocation of duties, not a constitutional override of the compact.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When a prisoner gives the responsible custodial official the written notice and final-disposition request required by Article III for transmission through Article III(b), the prisoner has completed the statutory act that starts the 180-day period. The custodian's subsequent preparation of the required certificate or transmission of the request does not defer that date. The claimant must establish a qualifying request and its delivery date under ordinary evidentiary rules.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Article III(a) names the prosecutor and court as ultimate recipients, and Article III(b)'s return-receipt mechanism supports the competing actual-receipt reading. That reading gives prosecutors a clock that begins when they can act, a serious administrability advantage. Stone nevertheless reads the prisoner's duty in the context of the official forwarding channel the Agreement prescribes: the prisoner causes the required delivery by making the prescribed submission to the official charged with forwarding it. Treating the official's performance as an additional triggering condition lets that intermediary suspend the prisoner's statutory protection for an indefinite time after the prisoner has done what the Agreement requires.

Stone assigns that transmission risk to the governments that created and operate the channel. The responsible custodian must record receipt, promptly forward the request and certificate, and preserve delivery records. The prosecutor and court remain the required recipients; the rule changes the clock's trigger, not their entitlement to receive the papers. A prisoner cannot start it with an oral inquiry, an incomplete writing that lacks the required notice or request, or an unexplained delivery to an unrelated official. Good-cause continuances and applicable statutory exclusions remain available on their actual predicates; delay alone supplies no automatic extension.

Here the reported custodial submission was September 7, 1988; receipt by the prosecutor and court was September 26; trial began March 22, 1989. The interval was 196 days from submission and 177 from recipient receipt. The earlier trigger therefore changes the proposed disposition, while remand preserves the required inquiry into lawful exclusions before dismissal. [Contemporaneous record and competing interpretations](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/43/).

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** This rule addresses the trigger for a qualifying prisoner request under Article III, not an unpresented constitutional speedy-trial claim or another detainer procedure. Stone does not decide a separate question beyond the actual grant, presume misconduct, or impose a new negligence standard. The revised construction is his proposed simulated rule, not a rendered historical holding.

It supplies no governing rule, factual finding, approval history, or adopted Court holding. #### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Dilution and discriminatory purpose

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone assumes the influence theory only to resolve its deficient proof and does not reach the unpresented racial-classification claim.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Gingles and ordinary purpose proof without creating a per se ban or a remedial-necessity burden for all state majority-minority districts.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2's requirements for compelling creation of a majority-minority district do not become a statutory prohibition against a State choosing one without that compulsion. The claimant must prove the asserted violation. Here the conceded absence of the required majority bloc-voting mechanism defeats the presented dilution theory; neither awareness of federal-law obligations nor opponents' speculation by itself establishes prohibited discriminatory intent.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute protects electoral opportunity, not a judicially selected distribution of influence. The mapmaker’s possible political objectives cannot be converted into a racial-purpose finding without evidence that supports that inference. State redistricting authority and federal remedial authority are different: restrictions on what a federal court may order do not become a prohibition on every state choice.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No ruling that all influence claims fail or that every race-conscious map is constitutional. Distinguishing a statutory duty from statutory permission does not resolve an independently presented equal-protection assignment claim or select its scrutiny tier. No two-lane merits framework is introduced into this earlier, differently presented case.

##### Issue 2: Population justification

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the separately preserved equality claim after rejecting the other grounds.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the inherited prima facie threshold and state justification burden.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Deviations exceeding ten percent require justification, but preservation of county boundaries is not legally unavailable merely because that threshold is crossed.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The threshold changes the burden; it does not supply the final constitutional answer. The district court must assess the actual plan’s connection to the state policy and the magnitude of the departures. The State must substantiate the county-boundary policy's actual connection to the deviations and the consistency of its implementation. A label alone does not carry the burden; neither does crossing ten percent make the justification legally unavailable.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding that the particular deviations are justified before that inquiry is performed.
