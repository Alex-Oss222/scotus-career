#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE and REMAND.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside the federal plans to the extent they improperly displaced the timely state process. The district court should defer to the state plans subject to any properly presented, proven federal defect. It may retain jurisdiction and establish justified implementation safeguards if a genuine election deadline or violation requires action, but it may not recreate its preferred minority district without the statutory showing.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: State judicial redistricting

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the direct appeal and distinguishes remedial deference from absence of federal jurisdiction.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Germano and Reynolds to the actual state institutions engaged in redistricting.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal court must afford timely, lawful state redistricting a genuine opportunity, including work by a state court; it may not displace that process merely because it prefers its own plan.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The State’s responsibility is not confined to its legislature. Here the state court was actively correcting defects and producing plans. The federal panel’s deadline for legislative action did not erase that work. A reserve federal power to correct federal violations remains, but the record must justify its exercise.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No universal first-filed rule and no immunity of state plans from federal review.

##### Issue 2: A source-bound § 2 remedy

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone independently addresses the statutory foundation of the federal minority district and the live-plan question.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Extend the functional Gingles predicates to the presented fragmentation theory, rather than inventing a right to a racially proportional district.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal § 2 remedy cannot rest on the demographic aggregation of minority groups without proof of the relevant political cohesion and majority bloc voting, and an attack on a superseded plan does not prove the replacement unlawful.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Size alone does not show common electoral preferences or the majority’s ability to defeat them. The missing evidence matters because it establishes the causal mechanism of dilution, not because each group must always litigate separately. Stone would not categorically forbid a genuine coalition claim; he finds the statutory predicates absent on this record.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No categorical answer to every coalition or influence-district theory, and no adjudication of an unpleaded challenge to a replacement plan. Cohesion must be proved under the governing statutory inquiry; no particular statistical technique is made universally mandatory. A later evidentiary decision supplies no law to this February 1993 event. Any effect on later listed cases arises only from an actually adopted, effective simulated holding, not this supplement.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE and REMAND, rejecting the stated § 2 and purpose grounds while requiring proper analysis of the population deviations.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court should evaluate the county-boundary explanation under Mahan and Brown, not impose categorical invalidity above ten percent. No instruction to draw a particular racial configuration follows.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Dilution and discriminatory purpose

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone assumes the influence theory only to resolve its deficient proof and does not reach the unpresented racial-classification claim.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Gingles and ordinary purpose proof without creating a per se ban or a remedial-necessity burden for all state majority-minority districts.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2's requirements for compelling creation of a majority-minority district do not become a statutory prohibition against a State choosing one without that compulsion. The claimant must prove the asserted violation. Here the conceded absence of the required majority bloc-voting mechanism defeats the presented dilution theory; neither awareness of federal-law obligations nor opponents' speculation by itself establishes prohibited discriminatory intent.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute protects electoral opportunity, not a judicially selected distribution of influence. The mapmaker’s possible political objectives cannot be converted into a racial-purpose finding without evidence that supports that inference. State redistricting authority and federal remedial authority are different: restrictions on what a federal court may order do not become a prohibition on every state choice.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No ruling that all influence claims fail or that every race-conscious map is constitutional. Distinguishing a statutory duty from statutory permission does not resolve an independently presented equal-protection assignment claim or select its scrutiny tier. No two-lane merits framework is introduced into this earlier, differently presented case.

##### Issue 2: Population justification

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the separately preserved equality claim after rejecting the other grounds.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the inherited prima facie threshold and state justification burden.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Deviations exceeding ten percent require justification, but preservation of county boundaries is not legally unavailable merely because that threshold is crossed.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The threshold changes the burden; it does not supply the final constitutional answer. The district court must assess the actual plan’s connection to the state policy and the magnitude of the departures.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding that the particular deviations are justified before that inquiry is performed.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the summary judgment on § 1962(c) and REMAND, while accepting a properly bounded operation-or-management requirement.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit further proceedings to determine whether the firm actually made enterprise-management decisions and whether the other RICO elements are established. Do not direct a liability verdict or treat ordinary negligent auditing as racketeering.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Function and the summary-judgment record

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides statutory scope and then tests the record under Rule 56, rather than treating the lower court’s ultimate characterization as an undisputed fact.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the open statutory question by requiring some participation in direction of enterprise affairs, then apply that interpretation without a professional-status exemption.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1962(c) reaches an outsider who actually takes part in directing the enterprise’s affairs. Evidence that an accountant independently selected the enterprise’s decisive asset-valuation and acquisition assumptions can satisfy that participation requirement; the supported dispute here cannot be resolved against the noteholders on summary judgment.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The word conduct and the distinction between enterprise affairs and one’s own services support a directional role, but the words directly or indirectly prevent limiting it to titled executives. The defect is not the proposed statutory boundary but treating the accountant’s function as necessarily nonmanagerial despite evidence of assumed decisionmaking. Stone relies on the record descriptions of independent choices as evidence to be tested, not as findings that fraud occurred. The liberal-construction clause confirms that a lawful boundary must not become a categorical outsider immunity.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The firm may prevail after factual testing. No general aiding-and-abetting rule, conspiracy holding, strict professional liability, or dispensation from predicate-act and causation requirements is announced.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE and REMAND on the NLRA preemption issue in both consolidated dockets.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the NLRA preemption basis for enjoining Bid Specification 13.1 and return the litigation for any remaining properly preserved grounds. Do not declare every aspect of the agreement valid under every statute.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A purchase is not automatically sovereign regulation

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the labor-preemption ground necessary to the preliminary-injunction judgment.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the regulatory scope of Garmon and Machinists and distinguish Gould by actual operation.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Federal labor preemption directed at state regulation does not prohibit this project-specific contracting condition where the State acts as owner and purchaser on terms a private proprietor could lawfully pursue, absent a congressional displacement of that proprietary conduct.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** MWRA owns, pays for, and supervises the construction whose labor stability the agreement addresses. Unlike a debarment program punishing labor violations elsewhere, the condition governs performance of the transaction being purchased. The point is not that all contracts escape federal law; it is that this conduct does not enter the regulatory category the asserted preemption rules displace. NLRA construction provisions reinforce the private analogue.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Government may not use procurement to impose general labor policy outside its own transaction. Independent NLRA prohibitions and other statutes remain fully applicable.
