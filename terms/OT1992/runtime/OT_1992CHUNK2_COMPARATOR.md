## Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy; No. 91-1010; 1993-01-12. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 8 to 1.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** White wrote for Rehnquist, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas. Blackmun concurred separately. Stevens dissented.

## Herrera v. Collins

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Herrera v. Collins; No. 91-7328; 1993-01-25. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** AFFIRMED, 6–3 in the judgment.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote for himself, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. O’Connor, joined by Kennedy, concurred; Scalia, joined by Thomas, also concurred. White concurred in the judgment. Blackmun dissented, with Stevens and Souter joining Parts I through IV of his opinion.

**Historical Holding:** The Court rejected the requested federal habeas relief. It treated new evidence of innocence, absent an independent constitutional violation, as ordinarily insufficient for habeas, and assumed without deciding that a truly persuasive post-trial showing in a capital case might support relief if no state avenue existed. Herrera’s showing did not meet the extraordinarily high threshold that hypothetical claim would require. It did not hold that Herrera had proved innocence, and its assumption was not an unqualified recognition of a freestanding claim.

**Historical Authority:** 506 U.S. 390 (1993); a historical Supreme Court merits decision. Its authority in the historical legal system does not make it an effective decision of this reboot.

**Historical Sources:** [Published opinions, disposition, and opinion-joinder statement](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/506/390/).

## Zafiro v. United States

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Zafiro v. United States; No. 91-6824; 1993-01-25. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** AFFIRMED, 9–0 in the judgment.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor wrote for eight Justices. Stevens concurred in the judgment.

**Historical Holding:** Rule 14 does not make mutually exclusive defenses prejudicial per se. District courts assess serious risks to specific rights or reliable verdicts and tailor remedies; lesser safeguards often suffice. The preserved severance claims of Garcia, Soto, and Martinez did not establish reversible prejudice.

**Historical Petitioner-Specific Treatment:** At 506 U.S. 537, the Court expressly stated that Gloria Zafiro had not appealed the denial of her severance motion and that her claim therefore was not properly before it. The overall affirmance was not a merits adjudication of her own severance claim. Her trial testimony remained relevant to the other petitioners’ claims.

**Historical Authority:** 506 U.S. 534 (1993); a historical Supreme Court merits decision. Its authority in the historical legal system does not make it an effective decision of this reboot.

## Spectrum Sports v. McQuillan

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Spectrum Sports v. McQuillan; No. 91-10; 1993-01-25. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** REVERSED AND REMANDED, 9–0.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** White delivered the unanimous opinion.

**Historical Holding:** Attempted monopolization requires specific intent and a dangerous probability of monopolization, evaluated with reference to a relevant market and economic power. Predatory conduct alone cannot replace those requirements. The general verdict required remand because it could rest on the defective attempt theory.
