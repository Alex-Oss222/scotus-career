#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the Fifth Circuit judgment insofar as it categorically excludes a freestanding capital-innocence claim, and REMAND. Do not direct an acquittal, make a credibility finding, or reinstate an indefinite district-court stay.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand through the Fifth Circuit for the district court to identify the properly presented claim and apply the existing exhaustion and abuse rules. Stone would preserve a temporary stay only for the period reasonably necessary to decide whether the petition presents substantial grounds for relief, with an express judicial determination under Barefoot and Delo. If exhaustion is lacking, use the lawful pre-AEDPA disposition rather than inventing a later stay-and-abeyance system. Factual development follows only if the pleaded evidence, credited provisionally and considered with the trial record, could satisfy the proposed demanding merits burden and the governing hearing rules. No finding of innocence is made here.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Capital innocence, procedural reach, and a source-bound remand

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches cognizability as necessary to review the appellate legal ground. He separately recognizes exhaustion and successive-petition limits and rejects the proposition that the district court already found a hearing warranted on innocence.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve an open constitutional question by a candid extension of the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments, and identify its retroactivity consequence expressly. Townsend’s exclusion of new evidence unconnected to a constitutional wrong remains intact; the proposed rule identifies the present execution itself as the wrong.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A capital prisoner who establishes factual innocence by clear and convincing evidence may not be executed for that crime. A properly channeled § 2254 claim alleging that violation is cognizable even without an additional trial error. The existing verdict remains entitled to substantial weight; an unsupported assertion or a mere possibility of acquittal does not meet the rule.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defect in categorical noncognizability is that it makes a constitutionally regular trial authorize a punishment even after its factual predicate is reliably disproved. Stone proposes a substantive limitation on whom the State may execute, not a general second criminal trial. He would apply Penry’s substantive Teague exception to that limitation openly, rather than silently calling a new evidentiary procedure old law. The new component is recognition of the substantive claim and a clear-and-convincing merits burden. The existing trial burden, ordinary sufficiency review, exhaustion, abuse rules, and hearing requirements remain. Finality is protected by the demanding showing and the lawful channel, but reliance on executing an actually innocent person is not protected. The delayed affidavits require critical examination; their allegations are not enough to award the writ, and their conflicting accounts are not judicial findings that can be resolved through this brief.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The proposal is limited to execution. It does not settle every noncapital freestanding claim, erase lawful custody on other convictions, require hearings on every affidavit, or revive the independently rejected Brady claim. No approved fallback exists.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the denial of relief on the mitigation claim and REMAND for a conditional writ directed to the death sentence.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow Texas a reasonable period fixed by the district court to provide a new sentencing proceeding with an effective vehicle for all relevant mitigation or replace the death sentence with a lawful noncapital sentence. Preserve the conviction and other independently valid custody. Do not order the jury to assign any specified weight to youth.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Teague and meaningful mitigation

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides Teague first, treats the claim as preserved, and reaches only the mitigation question fairly presented.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing Lockett–Eddings principle differently from the Fifth Circuit, without proposing to abolish Teague or to announce a categorical juvenile-execution rule.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The pre-1984 requirement that the sentencer be able to give effect to relevant mitigation is not satisfied where the only authorized sentencing questions omit the evidence’s independent bearing on culpability. Applying that principle to Graham’s youth and background is not a new rule.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Jurek’s approval was tied to meaningful consideration, not immunity for every application of the special issues. A juror could believe a young defendant less culpable while still finding deliberate conduct and future danger. The questions then provide no lawful route for that mitigating judgment. Stone rejects the lower court’s proposed requirement of an unusually severe and permanent condition because Eddings itself treated youth and disturbed background as material. Penry explains the same limitation but is not used as a lawmaking event retroactively inserted into 1984. The structure and evidence create a reasonable likelihood of foreclosing material mitigation. No burden, Teague exception, or general sentencing discretion is silently changed.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding concerns this submitted mechanism and evidence, not a mandatory instruction in every capital case or an entitlement to mercy unconnected with mitigation. Later Johnson must use the actual simulated outcome of this case, not this proposed position.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the judgment as to the severance issue for all four petitioners.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No new trial is required on this ground. Preserve Rule 14 authority to sever, redact, instruct, or use another effective safeguard in cases presenting actual prejudice.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Case-specific prejudice, not a defense-label rule

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved Rule 14 objections, assuming proper joinder and applying abuse-of-discretion review.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the rule’s contextual prejudice inquiry and reject an unsupported per se extension.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Mutually antagonistic defenses do not require severance without a serious risk to a specific trial right or to a reliable individualized verdict. Here the claimed incompatibility and record do not establish an uncured risk requiring separate trials.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The relevant question is what the joint trial prevented this jury from fairly deciding. The Government still bore the burden for each defendant, and the instructions directed separate consideration. Blame shifting alone is ordinary adversarial evidence, not a legal transfer of the prosecution burden. Bruton-type evidence or an inability to compartmentalize highly prejudicial proof can require a different remedy, but the Court cannot supply those facts where the petitioners have not shown them.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No categorical presumption that instructions always cure prejudice; no entitlement to severance merely because separate trials might improve a defendant’s prospects.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE and REMAND.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower court must determine whether further trial proceedings or another legally supported disposition is appropriate under the correct attempted-monopolization elements and any properly preserved independent ground. The ambiguous general verdict cannot be sustained simply by choosing a theory it might have adopted.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Dangerous probability is an element

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved legal challenge to the instruction and general verdict.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Swift and the enacted offense; reject the Ninth Circuit’s substitution of an inference of bad intent for proof of threatened market power.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Attempted monopolization requires anticompetitive conduct, specific intent to monopolize, and a dangerous probability of achieving monopoly power in a relevant market. The same conduct may support more than one inference, but it cannot eliminate the probability requirement.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A firm may harm a rival without having the ability to monopolize the competitive field. Congress did not create a general federal tort of unfair commercial conduct in § 2. Market conditions, alternatives, barriers, and the defendant’s economic position are relevant to the statutory probability question, not an optional afterthought. The erroneous instruction matters because the verdict does not identify another sufficient ground.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No categorical immunity for small firms, no ban on circumstantial proof, and no determination of the remaining claims or the ultimate factual merits.
