**Authoring Provenance:** The supplied current Master Jurisprudence informs Stone's methodology and the case-specific reviewed proposal below. It supplies no governing law, factual finding, or adopted simulated holding. The review in review/part_01_03.md documents the source and precedent analysis.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the Fifth Circuit judgment insofar as it categorically excludes a freestanding capital-innocence claim, and REMAND. Do not direct an acquittal, make a credibility finding, or reinstate an indefinite district-court stay.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand through the Fifth Circuit for the district court to identify the properly presented claim and apply exhaustion and abuse-of-the-writ rules before proceeding to the proposed merits standard. If material factual development is necessary, apply *Townsend* and the entered *Keeney* rule: ordinary counsel negligence alone is not a bar, but deliberate bypass remains disqualifying. A temporary stay requires the applicable express *Barefoot* and *Delo* determination; no indefinite preservation order or later stay-and-abeyance system is supplied. If the prisoner proves factual innocence under this rule, execution for that crime is barred; merely repeating the capital sentencing proceeding cannot cure that substantive defect. This remand itself makes no innocence finding, directs no acquittal, and leaves independently lawful custody or another conviction undisturbed.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Capital innocence, procedural reach, and a source-bound remand

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches cognizability as necessary to review the appellate legal ground. He separately recognizes exhaustion and successive-petition limits and rejects the proposition that the district court already found a hearing warranted on innocence.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Adopt a new substantive constitutional limitation under the Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments. Ford demonstrates that a valid conviction does not conclusively authorize execution when a constitutionally disqualifying condition exists at execution; Beck connects capital punishment with reliable guilt adjudication. Extending those principles to established factual innocence is Stone's reasoned proposal, not a claim that either case already decided this question. Townsend's rule against habeas relief for newly discovered evidence alone remains: this claim alleges that executing an innocent person is itself the constitutional wrong. [Ford](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/477/399/); [Beck](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/447/625/).

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A capital prisoner who establishes by clear and convincing evidence, considering the relevant old and new evidence together, that he did not commit the crime for which execution is sought may not be executed for that crime. A properly presented § 2254 claim alleging that substantive violation is cognizable without an additional trial error. The burden requires affirmative proof of factual innocence, not merely a new reasonable-doubt argument or a possibility of acquittal.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The existing verdict commands substantial weight, and delayed evidence may be less reliable than the original trial: witnesses disappear, memories weaken, and exculpatory accounts become difficult to test. Those considerations justify a demanding merits burden and enforceable procedural rules. They do not explain why a reliable demonstration that the prisoner did not commit the crime could leave execution constitutionally permissible. Clemency provides an additional corrective route but cannot conclusively adjudicate away an independently established constitutional prohibition.

Stone proposes the clear-and-convincing burden here; he does not import Sawyer's different death-eligibility gateway as though it already governed freestanding guilt innocence. Requiring the whole relevant record prevents isolated affidavits from erasing the original evidence. The present papers do not establish innocence. On remand, allegations provisionally credited must be capable of meeting this demanding burden in light of the trial record before factual development becomes material. If a properly presented claim then turns on material facts inadequately developed in state proceedings, the entered *Keeney* holding preserves *Townsend*'s deliberate-bypass qualification: ordinary counsel negligence alone does not bar the necessary hearing. That rule does not excuse exhaustion or abuse, establish credibility, or make every late affidavit hearing-worthy.

Teague requires an explicit further step. Penry describes an exception for substantive limits on the State's power to impose capital punishment on a class of persons. Stone would extend that rationale to persons affirmatively shown not to have committed the capital crime: the rule removes the State's authority to execute them, rather than prescribing an improved trial procedure. That extension is contested and newly proposed; it is not an already settled innocence exception. The remedial hearing needed to prove membership in the protected class does not change the substantive nature of the execution prohibition. Exhaustion and pre-AEDPA abuse-of-the-writ rules remain independently applicable. [Penry](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/492/302/).

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The proposed substantive protection is limited here to execution. It does not decide every noncapital freestanding claim, require a hearing on every affidavit, revive the separately rejected Brady claim, or alter the trial burden of proof. A remedy for other custody requires its own properly presented legal basis.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the denial of relief on the mitigation claim and REMAND for a conditional writ directed to the death sentence.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow Texas a reasonable period fixed by the district court to provide a new sentencing proceeding with an effective vehicle for all relevant mitigation or replace the death sentence with a lawful noncapital sentence. Preserve the conviction and other independently valid custody. Do not order the jury to assign any specified weight to youth.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Teague and meaningful mitigation

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides Teague first, treats the claim as preserved, and reaches only the mitigation question fairly presented.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Teague's requirement that the result be dictated by the law when Graham's conviction became final in 1984, not merely supported by a plausible later extension. Stone concludes that Jurek's qualified approval, Lockett, and Eddings together already required an effective route for this material mitigation. He does not abolish Teague, classify a new mitigation procedure as substantive, or announce a categorical bar on juvenile execution.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The pre-1984 requirement that the sentencer be able to give effect to relevant mitigation is not satisfied where the only authorized sentencing questions omit the evidence’s independent bearing on culpability. Applying that principle to Graham’s youth and background is not a new rule.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The strongest contrary argument is that Jurek approved the Texas special issues and treated age and future conduct as relevant within them, leaving reasonable judges room to regard this application as permissible. Stone must answer that point to call the rule old. His answer is that Jurek's facial approval depended on the Texas procedure permitting meaningful mitigating effect; it did not authorize exclusion of a material use of mitigation. Eddings, decided before finality, specifically required consideration of youth and disturbed background as mitigating circumstances. On Graham's submitted evidence, a juror could accept reduced personal culpability from youth and background while still finding deliberate conduct and future danger. Admission of the evidence and permission to use it for prediction do not supply a lawful route for that different mitigating judgment.

Stone therefore reads the pre-1984 decisions as dictating the narrower functional requirement: a reasonable likelihood that the submitted questions exclude a material mitigating use requires an effective corrective instruction or procedure. He does not infer a constitutional right to a fourth, unrestricted mercy question whenever counsel can imagine another favorable characterization. The evidence must support a material mitigating judgment that the authorized answers cannot express; jurors remain free to reject it or give it little weight.

Franklin's later discussion of prison conduct does not establish that all background evidence fits the special issues; the conduct there bore directly on future dangerousness. Saffle's later rejection of a claim to free-floating sympathy does not eliminate the antecedent duty to give effect to relevant mitigating evidence. These later opinions help distinguish the arguments presented in 1993 but supply no law retroactively inserted into 1984. If this reasoning required a genuinely new procedural rule, ordinary Teague would bar relief absent an applicable exception; Stone instead concludes that the identified pre-finality decisions already dictate this limited result. [Jurek](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/428/262/); [Eddings](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/455/104/).

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding concerns this submitted mechanism and evidence, not a mandatory instruction in every capital case or an entitlement to mercy unconnected with mitigation. Later Johnson must use the actual simulated outcome of this case, not this proposed position.
