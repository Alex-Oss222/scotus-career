## Growe v. Emison

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Growe v. Emison; No. 91-1420; 1993-02-23. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** REVERSED AND REMANDED, 9–0.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Scalia delivered the unanimous opinion.

**Historical Holding:** The federal court should have deferred to the timely state judicial redistricting process. Gingles predicates also apply to the single-member fragmentation claim at issue, and the record did not establish the cohesion and bloc voting needed for the federal minority-district remedy.

**Historical Authority:** 507 U.S. 25 (1993); a historical Supreme Court merits decision. Its authority in the historical legal system does not make it an effective decision of this reboot.

**Historical Sources:** [Published opinions, disposition, and opinion-joinder statement](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/25/).

## Itel Containers International Corp. v. Huddleston

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Itel Containers International Corp. v. Huddleston; No. 91-321; 1993-02-23. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** AFFIRMED, 8–1.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy wrote for Rehnquist, White, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Thomas, and himself; Scalia joined except Parts IV and V and concurred in part and in the judgment. Blackmun dissented.

**Historical Holding:** The conventions and federal regulations did not preempt the tax, and the tax survived Commerce Clause and Import-Export Clause review as applied to the local leases. The Court distinguished taxation by reason of importation from nondiscriminatory local commercial taxation.

**Historical Authority:** 507 U.S. 60 (1993); a historical Supreme Court merits decision. Its authority in the historical legal system does not make it an effective decision of this reboot.

## Voinovich v. Quilter

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** REVERSED AND REMANDED, 9–0.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor delivered the unanimous opinion.

**Historical Holding:** Section 2 does not categorically prohibit majority-minority districts, and the plaintiffs failed the necessary bloc-voting showing. ## Reves v. Ernst & Young

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Reves v. Ernst & Young; No. 91-886; 1993-03-03. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** AFFIRMED, 7–2.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Blackmun wrote for Rehnquist, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, and himself, with Scalia and Thomas joining except Part IV-A. Souter dissented, joined by White.

**Historical Holding:** The Court required participation in operation or management of the enterprise, allowed that outsiders and lower-level participants can qualify, and held the accounting work insufficient on the record. The dissent disputed both the statutory reading and the treatment of evidence suggesting managerial choices.

## Building & Construction Trades Council v. Associated Builders & Contractors (Boston Harbor)

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Historical Holding:** The NLRA did not preempt MWRA’s project labor agreement requirement because the authority acted as a proprietor rather than as a regulator, and the relevant private conduct was permitted by federal labor law.
