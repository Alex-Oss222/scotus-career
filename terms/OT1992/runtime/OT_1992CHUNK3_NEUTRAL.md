## Growe v. Emison

### SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Growe v. Emison  
**Citation or Docket:** 507 U.S. 25; No. 91-1420.  
**October Term:** 1992.  
**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, pre-decision preparation; direct appeal under 28 U.S.C. § 1253 from a three-judge district court. The listed historical merits proceeding is retained as the reboot's working procedural premise, not as a newly rendered simulated grant.  
**Simulated Event Date:** 1993-02-23, the historical decision date used as the simulation calendar date.  
**Question Presented / Granted Scope:** Whether the federal court should have deferred to Minnesota’s timely state judicial redistricting process, and whether its minority district could be required under Voting Rights Act § 2 without the relevant Gingles predicates.  
**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Three-judge District of Minnesota, 782 F. Supp. 427 (1992), imposing congressional and legislative districts and displacing state-court efforts.  
**Relief Requested:** Minnesota officials seek reversal of the federal redistricting judgment; appellees seek preservation of the federal plans and relief for alleged malapportionment and minority vote dilution.

**Material Record:** Following the 1990 census, voters brought parallel state and federal redistricting actions. The legislature’s plan contained drafting defects. The state court corrected defects and continued work while legislative efforts proceeded. The federal panel imposed a deadline, declined deference, and temporarily restrained the state proceedings. After a gubernatorial veto, the state court adopted a legislative plan and proceeded with congressional hearings. The federal panel adopted its own plans, including a district combining several minority groups without developed evidence of their common political cohesion or of majority bloc voting. The § 2 attack on the superseded 1983 plan did not itself establish a defect in the new state plan.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Section 1253 supplies direct review of the three-judge court’s injunctive judgment. The priority issue concerns deference to a State’s timely lawful process, including its judiciary, not a jurisdictional rule that first filing always controls. A federal statutory violation in an old plan does not automatically establish a current controversy over the replacement. No factual finding of cohesion can be supplied by aggregating racial population totals.

**Relevant Dates:** Census: 1990. Parallel proceedings and legislative activity: 1991–1992. Argument: November 2, 1992.

##### Issue 1: State responsibility and federal remedial restraint

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Article I, § 4; the Fourteenth Amendment; 28 U.S.C. §§ 1253 and 2284. Scott v. Germano, 381 U.S. 407 (1965), recognizes primary state responsibility and deference to adequate state redistricting efforts. Reynolds v. Sims, 377 U.S. 533 (1964), requires population equality while preserving an initial state opportunity to remedy defects.

**Open Point:** When a federal court may displace a state judicial plan and whether a timely state process can be disregarded merely because the legislature has not itself completed redistricting.

##### Issue 2: Section 2 and minority opportunity

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** The 1982 version of Voting Rights Act § 2, then 42 U.S.C. § 1973, uses a results and totality inquiry. Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986), identifies size and compactness, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting as predicates for the dilution claim it considered.

**Open Point:** How those predicates apply to fragmentation in single-member districts and to the aggregation of distinct minority groups; whether the live replacement plan is shown to violate § 2.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Minnesota:** The state court was proceeding in time for the election, and state judicial action is as much state redistricting as legislative action. The federal panel had no basis to invent a § 2 remedy without proof of a current violation or the required electoral conditions.

**Appellees and below:** Federal responsibility to ensure lawful elections justified prompt intervention after legislative defects and delay. The proposed minority district was defended as necessary to preserve minority voting opportunity.

**Concessions and limits:** Deference is not abdication if state processes cannot timely produce lawful districts. Conversely, a federal preference for a particular map is not a federal violation. Political cohesion among groups requires proof; population addition is not proof.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reverse the displacement and remand for deference if the state process was timely and the asserted federal violation unproved. It may sustain federal intervention only to the extent the existing record and proper legal standard establish a genuine need to protect timely, lawful elections; federal preference for a different map is insufficient. A § 2 remedy requires a live claim and the necessary statutory predicates, and unsupported remedy components may be set aside independently. A remand could preserve federal jurisdiction over a properly presented defect in the replacement plan, without transferring the old plan's alleged defect automatically to its successor.

**Question Presented / Granted Scope:** Whether Tennessee’s sales tax on receipts from leases of cargo containers delivered in Tennessee, used exclusively in international shipping, violates the Commerce, Import-Export, or Supremacy Clause in light of the Container Conventions and federal rules.  
**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Tennessee Supreme Court, 814 S.W.2d 29 (1991), affirming rejection of federal challenges after a state-law reduction of the assessment.

The assessment was reduced on state-law grounds, leaving the federal challenge to the remaining tax. The 1956 and 1972 Container Conventions protect qualifying containers from duties tied to importation. **Citation or Docket:** 507 U.S. 146; No. 91-1618.

**Question Presented / Granted Scope:** Whether Ohio’s legislative plan violated Voting Rights Act § 2 or the Fifteenth Amendment by creating majority-minority districts, and whether population deviations exceeding ten percent were treated under the proper Fourteenth Amendment standard.

**Material Record:** The apportionment board adopted a 1991 plan and a revised 1992 plan. Challengers contended that concentrating Black voters in majority-minority districts reduced their influence elsewhere. The district court required Ohio to justify those districts as necessary to remedy a § 2 violation. It found no racially polarized voting, a finding accepted by the challengers. It also inferred unlawful intent partly from the mapmaker’s attention to federal requirements and possession of opponents’ speculative documents. ##### Issue 1: Statutory dilution and purpose

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Voting Rights Act § 2 and Gingles, 478 U.S. 30, require proof of dilution under the relevant predicates and totality, not a per se prohibition of majority-minority districts. The Fifteenth Amendment purpose inquiry requires evidence of prohibited discrimination; lawful attention to federal obligations is not itself that purpose.

**Open Point:** Whether an influence-dilution claim is generally cognizable remains open; the record’s conceded absence of racially polarized voting may resolve this claim without deciding that broader point.

##### Issue 2: Population equality

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Reynolds, 377 U.S. 533; Mahan v. Howell, 410 U.S. 315 (1973); Brown v. Thomson, 462 U.S. 835 (1983). A total deviation above ten percent establishes a prima facie case requiring state justification, not automatic invalidity. **Open Point:** Whether the actual deviations reasonably advance Ohio’s policy and remain within constitutional bounds.

**Ohio and the United States as amicus:** Section 2 does not place on a State the initial burden to prove that every majority-minority district is indispensable. The absent bloc-voting predicate defeats this theory. The intent finding draws unsupported inferences, and county integrity is a legally available population justification.

**Limits:** Rejection of these racial-dilution theories does not establish every possible race-conscious plan’s validity. Ohio must actually carry its burden on population departures.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

If the district court imposed an unauthorized statutory prohibition or shifted the initial burden, the Court may set aside that ruling; a preserved statutory ground could be sustained only if its required predicates are established. The intent finding is separately reviewable for clear error, not invalid solely because the map is race conscious or defensible solely because compliance is asserted. The population component may be remanded for evaluation of Ohio's justification if the district court wrongly treated the deviation threshold as dispositive. These separable paths do not establish a general constitutional license for every race-conscious map or authorize an unpresented classification claim.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Eighth Circuit, 937 F.2d 1310 (1991), affirming summary judgment for the accounting firm on the civil RICO claim.

##### Issue 1: Participation in enterprise affairs

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** 18 U.S.C. §§ 1962(c) and 1964(c); RICO’s enacted liberal-construction clause; Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co., 473 U.S. 479 (1985); United States v. Turkette, 452 U.S. 576 (1981). The statute reaches persons employed by or associated with an enterprise who conduct or participate directly or indirectly in its affairs through a racketeering pattern. Rule 56, Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986), and Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), govern the record posture.

**Open Point:** Whether some role in directing enterprise affairs is required, whether outsiders can perform that role, and whether the record presents a genuine dispute about the accountants’ actual function.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Firm and below:** Performing an audit and giving deficient advice does not amount to directing the cooperative. The operation-or-management requirement protects the distinction between providing services and conducting an enterprise’s affairs.

**Noteholders:** The language includes indirect participation and should not create a professional-services exemption. Their evidence permits a finding that the firm selected the valuation and acquisition assumptions itself, a managerial function rather than independent checking.

**United States as amicus:** Urges reversal and a broader understanding of statutory participation.

**Limits:** Association, professional status, and importance to the enterprise neither establish nor defeat liability. The relevant act, culpability, and injury must satisfy their separate statutory elements.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Adopt an operation-or-management interpretation and affirm if no reasonable factfinder could find such participation; adopt it and reverse if the nonmovants’ evidence supports an actual managerial role; or reject the requirement under the statutory language. A reversal for a fact dispute permits further proceedings, not automatic RICO damages.

##### Issue 1: Regulation versus proprietary purchasing

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** NLRA §§ 8(e) and 8(f) accommodate specified construction-industry agreements. San Diego Building Trades Council v. Garmon, 359 U.S. 236 (1959), and Machinists v. Wisconsin Employment Relations Commission, 427 U.S. 132 (1976), constrain state regulation of labor relations. Wisconsin Department of Industry v. Gould Inc., 475 U.S. 282 (1986), rejects use of procurement as a disguised regulatory sanction.

**Open Point:** Whether the government’s actual project-specific transaction is regulation within those preemption rules, rather than ordinary proprietary action of a kind available to a private owner.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**MWRA and project parties:** The authority seeks the same stability a private owner could bargain for on a complex project. It uses contractual terms within a purchase it owns and finances, not sanctions against unrelated market conduct. The United States as amicus urges reversal.

**Association and below:** A public purchaser’s economic leverage can alter collective bargaining and restrict nonunion competition; calling the condition contractual should not defeat federal labor policy.

**Limits:** Neither a procurement label nor government ownership is conclusive. Scope, connection to the purchase, and use of coercive sovereign powers matter. A condition reaching unrelated projects could present regulation rather than participation.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reverse the preemption ruling if this is genuine proprietary conduct Congress did not displace. Affirm if the condition operates as a regulatory scheme forbidden by Garmon or Machinists. The judgment should leave independent unreviewed objections and necessary preliminary-injunction proceedings to the lower courts.
