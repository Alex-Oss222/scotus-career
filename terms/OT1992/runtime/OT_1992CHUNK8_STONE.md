#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the judgment insofar as it sustains the death sentence and REMAND for a constitutionally adequate capital sentencing proceeding or a lawful noncapital sentence.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the guilt determination intact. Require a sentencing vehicle that permits the jury to give mitigating effect to youth-related reduced culpability without answering an unrelated factual question falsely. Do not order acquittal, automatically impose life regardless of state sentencing law, or prescribe the exact wording of every future charge.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Reduced culpability is not identical to reduced future danger

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved merits on direct review, subject to incorporation of any actual earlier simulated Graham holding.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Lockett, Eddings, Penry, and Boyde to this record; distinguish Jurek’s general approval rather than overrule the whole Texas system.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The instructions were inadequate where a juror could accept that Johnson’s youth reduced his culpability yet truthfully answer both prescribed issues in the direction requiring death. Admission of the evidence and its partial relevance to dangerousness do not supply the missing sentencing effect.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Immaturity may mitigate responsibility for a completed act even if it also supports a prediction of near-term danger. Nothing in the general instruction authorizes a juror to express that judgment through a no answer to a question the juror believes factually requires yes. The case requires a meaningful vehicle for the mitigation presented, not unconstrained mercy or consideration in every imaginable manner.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No categorical age bar is adopted. Texas retains authority to structure sentencing and require proof of aggravation; the defect is the missing legal route for the particular constitutionally relevant mitigation.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Ninth Circuit judgment insofar as it rests on a higher constitutional competence standard and REMAND for further consideration under the proper, separately stated capacity and waiver inquiries.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the conditional-writ direction tied to the higher standard. On remand, address the existing claims and findings concerning reason to conduct further inquiry and whether the actual waivers were knowing, intelligent, and voluntary. The disposition does not independently affirm every conviction or sentence, revive the death sentence already reduced on direct review, or treat the psychiatric reports as conclusive for all later moments.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Dusky does not dispense with actual informed choice

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone declines to introduce the unpreserved Teague defense and decides the fully litigated legal question. He separates that question from unresolved application under the proper standard.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Dusky and the received waiver cases, rejecting a separate higher competence threshold while preserving the independent waiver requirements.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The constitutional capacity standard is Dusky for standing trial, pleading guilty, and waiving counsel. A court must also establish that the particular relinquishment is knowing, intelligent, and voluntary, and must undertake a competence inquiry when circumstances give reason for doubt.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Capacity to understand the proceeding is not legal skill, and self-representation cannot depend on professional competence. Conversely, a defendant’s ability to consult with counsel does not prove that a later decision is informed and uncoerced. The trial judge made express findings, but the medication and other circumstances must be evaluated for the work the distinct doctrines assign them.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic hearing is required for every plea or waiver. No automatic validity follows from monosyllabic answers or old reports. States may adopt additional protections, and this case does not decide competence for execution or a later separate standard for conducting a trial without counsel.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Sixth Circuit as to the challenged proof-burden and family-participation rulings, and REMAND for proceedings on matters not resolved by those holdings.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the categorical federal injunction against these two procedural choices. Preserve the class members’ rights to the statutory commitment predicates, clear and convincing proof at minimum, independent adjudication, counsel and other applicable safeguards, and challenges to unfair implementation. Do not authorize continued confinement without a lawful current basis.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The preserved equality claim

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone declines to decide the heightened-scrutiny theory first advanced at this stage. The preserved rational-basis challenge and the separate procedural claim remain reviewable.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the received rational-basis standard rather than silently substitute Stone’s personal preferred disability classification or demand a new legislative evidentiary record.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Kentucky may use clear and convincing proof for the developmental-disability category while supplying an additional beyond-reasonable-doubt safeguard for mental illness, where differences in diagnostic history and error risk reasonably support the distinction. The preserved claim does not establish that this classification is irrational.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The liberty interest is equally serious for both groups. That does not require every protective procedure to be identical when both satisfy the federal minimum and a rational difference bears on error. The challengers identify plausible reasons for more equal treatment, but a permissible legislative judgment does not become unconstitutional because Stone might prefer that policy. No finding of individual dangerousness follows from the classification.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No new scrutiny tier is adopted and no liberty-discount for disability is approved. Addington’s floor and the substantive predicates of confinement remain independent requirements.

##### Issue 2: Participation is not adjudicative control

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the Mathews challenge independently of equal protection.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Mathews to the statutory permission, without adopting an irrebuttable assumption that family interests coincide with the adult’s interests.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Family or guardian party status does not by itself violate due process where the subject retains a meaningful opportunity to contest the evidence before an independent decisionmaker.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The private liberty interest is substantial. Family information can bear on functional ability and care, and adverse witnesses or parties are ordinarily tested through the adjudicative process rather than excluded by constitutional category. Conflict or inconvenience cannot establish commitment; the statutory predicates and burden still govern. The record does not establish that party status necessarily eliminates those safeguards.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** A concrete proceeding in which relatives control the result, suppress the adult’s position, or prevent fair testing remains challengeable. The holding does not make family preference a substitute for proof.

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the absolute-immunity judgment for the alleged preliminary investigative fabrication and press-conference statements, and REMAND.

##### Issue 1: A future prosecution does not transform prior investigation

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the claimed absolute protection, accepting the pleaded factual account for that purpose.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Police-like work remains investigative when a prosecutor performs it. A press conference informs the public and has no independent adjudicative function.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Chronology is relevant to function, not a universal rule that all post-indictment acts are protected or all earlier acts unprotected. The Court does not decide qualified immunity, witness immunity, or damages on these allegations.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the as-applied First Amendment judgment and REMAND, without adopting a categorical power to ban all advertising for activities government may prohibit.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the First Amendment basis for the injunction against application to Edge. Address any genuinely unresolved, preserved alternative claim below. #### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A meaningful reduction can satisfy the existing test

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone assumes the first Central Hudson inquiry in Edge’s favor and decides advancement and fit on the record rather than a categorical vice theory.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Central Hudson and Fox. Do not replace the test, add a no-as-applied-challenges rule, or overrule Posadas in a case that does not require it.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The federal licensing-State rule, as applied here, materially supports North Carolina’s lottery policy through a meaningful reduction in advertising reaching its residents and is a reasonable accommodation of adjacent States’ different choices.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The station’s substantial share of listening in the affected North Carolina counties prevents treating its contribution as negligible merely because most of its total audience is elsewhere. Congress need not eradicate every cross-border message to regulate a meaningful source. Licensing location is connected to the community served and the policy accommodated, unlike a speech distinction wholly unrelated to the asserted harm. The lawful Virginia audience is a real burden considered in fit, not a reason to disregard the North Carolina audience.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** This reasoning does not bar record-specific as-applied challenges, allow viewpoint discrimination, or make commercial speech unprotected whenever the underlying activity may be prohibited. Federalism identifies the interest; it does not replace proof of advancement and fit.

Review must confront that risk instead of treating the existence of some bad conduct as sufficient for any sum.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the Eighth Circuit's judgment and REMAND for further proceedings under the permissible-inference rule. This sets aside its compulsory-liability rule without directing judgment for either party.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The court of appeals must review the district court's actual discrimination finding under Rule 52(a), on the complete record and without a mandatory requirement of additional direct or independent evidence after pretext. The related judgment on Hicks's § 1983 claim against Long returns with the appeal under the unchallenged common purposeful-discrimination framework, without merging the two causes of action. Any supported, preserved defect in the factual findings or opportunity to contest a proposed explanation must receive the correction ordinary appellate and trial procedure authorizes. Further factfinding, if needed, belongs below; this disposition neither reinstates the employer's judgment conclusively nor awards Hicks relief automatically.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Production, persuasion, and the inference from falsity

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the legal consequence of disbelieving the employer's reasons after a bench trial. Preserve review of whether discrimination was actually proved. For this case, accept the parties' unchallenged premise that the same purposeful-discrimination analysis applies to the Title VII claim against St. Mary's and the § 1983 employment-discrimination claim against Long; do not announce that the statutes are interchangeable.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Clarify McDonnell Douglas, Burdine, and Aikens. Preserve the employer's burden of production and the plaintiff's ultimate burden of persuasion. Reject both an irrebuttable rule of liability from falsity and an additional substantive element requiring direct or separately independent proof of discrimination.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** After a plaintiff establishes a prima facie case and the employer meets its burden of producing a specific nondiscriminatory explanation, proof that the explanation is false permits a finding of intentional discrimination and may be sufficient to support that finding. It does not compel liability in every case. The factfinder may infer the forbidden motive from the prima facie evidence, the discredited explanation, and the surrounding record without demanding an admission or a separate category of proof.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The ultimate statutory question is discrimination because of race, not dishonesty alone. But the prima facie evidence and the employer's lie remain evidence after the presumption ceases to control; they do not disappear when the employer produces an explanation. A requirement that a plaintiff then supply independent direct proof would defeat the framework's circumstantial method. Conversely, the factfinder must remain able to evaluate the whole record rather than treat every disproved explanation as conclusive proof of the particular forbidden motive. Here the employer produced disciplinary reasons, the judge disbelieved them, and the appellate court stopped at that falsity finding instead of completing review of the ultimate finding.

**Doctrinal Components:** The prima facie presumption, the employer's production obligation, and the plaintiff's ultimate persuasion burden remain intact. The clarification concerns the inferential force of pretext at the final stage, not a new burden shift. Burdine's recognition of indirect proof is preserved as an adequate means of persuasion, not converted into either mandatory liability or mandatory extra proof. An employer that never satisfies its production obligation receives no benefit from this rule. No controlling Supreme Court holding is treated as already requiring the court of appeals' compulsory inference.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The employer need not persuade the court of its explanation merely to satisfy production. Credibility and the ultimate inference are evaluated on the whole record. The rule does not authorize courts to hypothesize facts, disregard proven falsity because direct evidence is absent, or presume a lawful motive merely because one can be imagined.

##### Issue 2: Review of the actual finding and fair opportunity to respond

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Identify the work left for remand, without deciding a factual appeal that the court of appeals did not complete.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Rule 52(a) and the inherited right to a fair opportunity to address the evidence. Distinguish an inference supported by the litigated record from an invented or unfairly introduced explanation.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Rejection of automatic liability leaves the trial court's finding subject to genuine clear-error review. A lawful nondiscriminatory inference must be grounded in the record; the plaintiff cannot be required to disprove every imaginable motive. The same evidence that established the prima facie case and discredited the stated reasons must receive its full permissible force.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The district court's personal-animosity explanation is a matter for review, not a new fact established by this supplement. The appellate court must determine whether the record supports the actual finding and whether any properly preserved procedural objection requires correction. Neither failure to compel discrimination nor the possibility of a different motive ends that inquiry.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic new trial, no instruction to find discrimination, and no new universal restriction on the use of circumstantial evidence. Later employment-discrimination decisions supply neither the rule nor the factual assessment in this 1993 case.
