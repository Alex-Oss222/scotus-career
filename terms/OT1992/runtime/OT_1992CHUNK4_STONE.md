#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the facial invalidation and REMAND, on a narrow basis that preserves individual and properly supported systemic challenges to unlawful custody or ineffective review.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside the requirement to add all responsible adults as a mandatory release category and the automatic-hearing injunction insofar as founded solely on facial invalidity. Leave the 1987 conditions agreement intact. The lower court may address properly pleaded and supported implementation claims under ordinary procedure; this disposition is not a finding that every detention, notice, or delay is lawful.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Statutory reach and the facial record

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone addresses statutory authorization first, recognizes the minors’ liberty interest, and applies the facial-review posture instead of deciding hypothetical individual cases.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing statute and facial-challenge doctrine; neither abolish agency deference nor convert the immigration label into immunity from review.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The regulation is not facially unlawful merely because it prefers designated family custodians and provides review on request. It admits lawful temporary placements serving immigration and child-safety purposes, while constitutional limits remain enforceable in actual applications.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute permits custody pending proceedings and does not compel release to any willing adult. Safe placement with relatives and temporary licensed care are permissible implementations. Foucha and Youngberg prevent describing institutional custody as outside liberty altogether, but that recognition does not itself dictate the class’s chosen placement mechanism. The record does not establish universal inability to invoke review, and the exceptional-release provision prevents treating every suitable nonrelative as categorically excluded. Stone does not presume that these protections work in every case; he declines a facial remedy broader than the demonstrated defect.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No authority for indefinite confinement, punishment, unreviewable detention, or disregard of a child’s demonstrated inability to obtain review. The validity of a particular prolonged detention remains open.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE as to jurisdiction over the detention-and-torture intentional-tort claims; AFFIRM as to jurisdiction over the independently pleaded United States recruitment failure-to-warn claims; REMAND.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Dismiss the intentional-tort claims against the sovereign defendants for want of the asserted FSIA exception. Permit lower-court consideration of the warning claims’ substantive sufficiency and the HCA judgment’s preclusive effect. No damages or finding of liability is directed.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Sovereign police conduct

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides jurisdiction before tort merits and reaches only the asserted statutory exception.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Weltover’s nature-of-the-act analysis, not an exception inferred from the gravity of abuse.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The intentional-tort claims based on detention and torture through Saudi police powers are not based on commercial activity carried on in the United States merely because commercial recruitment preceded them.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The legal nature of the coercive act is distinct from a possible commercial retaliatory purpose. Immunity under this statute is not a holding that torture is lawful. The Court cannot create an exception Congress did not enact from the strength of the merits allegation.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No decision on other statutory exceptions or claims against individual actors not before the Court.

##### Issue 2: Independent commercial omission

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone separately tests the pleaded recruitment omission under the same jurisdictional text, leaving merits and preclusion to the proper stage.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the statutory exception claim by claim; do not import a broader arising-out-of bar from a different immunity statute.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A genuine claim that a recruiter negligently omitted a legally required warning during commercial recruitment carried on in the United States falls within the commercial-activity exception, though it may fail under substantive law or preclusion.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged omission is conduct a private recruiter could perform and has its own United States connection. Identifying that conduct does not guarantee a duty existed. The lower court must determine that issue and the prior judgment’s effect rather than treating a potentially weak tort claim as jurisdictionally nonexistent. The same injury can arise from legally distinct alleged acts, each of which must satisfy its own source.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No jurisdiction merely from artful labels; a claim lacking an independent commercial act remains barred. No conclusion that these warning claims ultimately survive.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Maintain relief against the selective commercial-handbill rack prohibition. Leave the City free to enact lawful content-neutral location, size, number, safety, or maintenance rules.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The chosen distinction must serve the asserted interest

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the commercial-speech question on the accepted classification and does not require strict scrutiny for every commercial regulation.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Central Hudson and Fox; reject the proposed content-neutral characterization.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A categorical distinction between commercial and newspaper racks fails the required fit where both create the same physical harms and the City does not show why the selected category meaningfully corresponds to its safety and aesthetic objectives.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The small reduction is not constitutionally insufficient merely because it is small. The defect is the unexplained content-based selection among physically equivalent sources of the asserted harm. Incremental regulation remains permissible, but a lower speech-value label does not establish that fit. Neutral physical criteria illustrate available lawful regulation without becoming a least-restrictive-means command.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No categorical ban on commercial classifications and no requirement that all city safety rules address every source of harm simultaneously.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Ninth Circuit’s determination that the aggravator, as construed, is insufficiently definite; leave the independent resentencing grounds undisturbed.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** On resentencing, Idaho may use constitutionally valid, properly supported aggravators and permit the mitigation already required by the unreviewed judgment. Stone would not authorize use of this formulation as an eligibility gate. The conviction remains intact.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: A narrowing construction must actually narrow

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the limited facial question and expressly preserves the independent grounds requiring resentencing.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Godfrey, Maynard, and Walton differently from the State’s proposed application; no new capital-sentencing test is proposed.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The cold-blooded, pitiless-slayer formulation does not provide a sufficiently stable distinction to cure the utter-disregard aggravator’s vagueness.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone accepts that mental state can be found objectively and therefore does not rest on a rule forbidding mental-state aggravators. The problem is the undefined boundary among emotional detachment, absence of sympathy, calculated intent, and furious violence. Pitilessness adds condemnation but little narrowing. A federal court cannot supply a precise absence-of-all-emotion element that the State’s actual authoritative formulation did not establish. The construction fails the received demand for guided, reviewable eligibility selection.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No categorical rejection of judicial narrowing constructions or all mental-state aggravators. No determination of Creech’s as-applied claim or authority to disregard the existing resentencing order.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE and REMAND.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reconsider the sufficiency of the actual age-discrimination evidence under the corrected distinction. If liability remains supported, assess willfulness under Thurston without added requirements. Preserve independently valid non-ADEA claims and remedies.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Correlation is not the statutory cause

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the age-motivation and willfulness questions separately and leaves other statutory claims intact.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the ADEA’s protected-trait requirement and retain Thurston; no later causation doctrine is imported.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Service-based pension interference alone does not establish action because of age, though evidence of age motivation or an age proxy may do so. A knowing or reckless ADEA violation can be willful in an individual discharge case without proof of outrageousness or a separate heightened motive.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statute’s elements cannot be replaced by a conclusion that the employer acted badly. But correcting that error does not erase other evidence cited below. On willfulness, covert action is not less culpable than an announced policy; the received knowledge-or-recklessness standard already distinguishes ordinary from enhanced liability. No new burden of independent evidence beyond the governing intentional-discrimination framework is imposed.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No determination of disparate-impact availability, no categorical pension safe harbor, and no automatic reinstatement or elimination of liquidated damages before the remand analysis.
