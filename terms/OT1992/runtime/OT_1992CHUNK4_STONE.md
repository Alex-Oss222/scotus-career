#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Function and the summary-judgment record

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone decides statutory scope and then tests the record under Rule 56, rather than treating the lower court’s ultimate characterization as an undisputed fact.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the open statutory question by requiring some participation in direction of enterprise affairs, then apply that interpretation without a professional-status exemption.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1962(c) reaches an outsider who assumes or exercises a role in directing enterprise affairs. Evidence that the firm itself selected asset-valuation and acquisition assumptions as enterprise decisions, rather than merely implementing management’s choices, permits a reasonable inference of that role on this summary-judgment record.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone proposes an operation-or-management boundary as the resolution of this open statutory question. Conduct concerns direction, and directly or indirectly prevents limiting the provision to titled executives. The importance of accounting work to an enterprise does not alone make the accountant a manager: ordinary advice, drafting, or an inadequate audit performed under management’s direction remains insufficient. The record descriptions of independent valuation and acquisition choices nevertheless create a supported dispute about the firm’s actual function. Under Anderson and Rule 56, that dispute and reasonable inferences belong to the nonmoving noteholders at this stage. Engagement records and testimony can distinguish assumed decisionmaking from professional services; none establishes fraud, predicate acts, culpability, or causation by itself. The liberal-construction clause supports no professional-status immunity but does not eliminate the directional requirement.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The firm may prevail after factual testing. No general aiding-and-abetting rule, conspiracy holding, strict professional liability, or dispensation from predicate-act and causation requirements is announced.

##### Issue 1: Territorial scope of the FTCA waiver

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved issue within the review posture stated in Section I.

**Stone’s Legal Position and Treatment of Precedent:** Resolve the disputed territorial meaning of § 2680(k) through the FTCA’s enacted liability and choice-of-law structure; do not treat a missing venue as a fact of this case.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The FTCA does not supply this Antarctic tort action; its territorial exception and place-of-law structure do not authorize a judicially created liability regime for that location.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The claimant resided in Oregon and brought suit there, so § 1402(b) supplies residence-based venue. Nothing in this record supplies an Oregon act or an enacted direction to borrow the claimant’s domiciliary law. Although foreign country can plausibly mean a territory under foreign sovereignty, Spelar and the statute’s place-based liability design support including this foreign territory within the exception. Stone therefore rejects the proposed conflicts solution on statutory grounds. A hypothetical nonresident’s venue difficulty is contextual only, and the FTCA’s maritime history prevents treating extraterritoriality alone as an absolute bar. [Richards](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/369/1/) supplies the pre-event choice-of-law baseline.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No conclusion that an offshore location defeats every constitutional or statutory channel; no disposition of the unlisted gun-use Smith matter.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The regulation as a whole is not facially unlawful merely because it organizes release around family custodians and review on request. ##### Issue 1: Meaning of neglect and equitable excusability

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved issue within the review posture stated in Section I.

**Stone’s Legal Position and Treatment of Precedent:** Resolve the open interpretation of Rule 9006(b)(1) through its language and an equitable assessment of excusability, while retaining Link’s attribution of counsel’s acts to the client.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Counsel’s inadvertence may fall within neglect, and the unusual notice, modest delay, and lack of demonstrated prejudice support excusing it here, with counsel’s conduct attributed to the creditors.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Neglect includes inadvertence or carelessness as well as circumstances outside the actor’s control, but excusable requires an assessment of the actual failure. Stone proposes consideration of prejudice, length and procedural effect of delay, the reason for it including control, and good faith as the resolution of this disputed rule, not as an already settled Supreme Court test. The unusually placed and ambiguous bar-date notice, short delay, and absence of identified prejudice support relief here with counsel’s conduct attributed to the creditors under Link. An applicant must explain the missed deadline; lack of prejudice or good faith alone does not excuse ordinary disregard of a clear notice.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No relief from deadlines governed by different express limits and no guarantee that good faith alone suffices.
