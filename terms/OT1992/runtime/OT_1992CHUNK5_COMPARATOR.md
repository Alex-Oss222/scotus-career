## Brecht v. Abrahamson

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Brecht v. Abrahamson; No. 91-7358; 1993-04-21. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** AFFIRMED, 5–4.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote for Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and himself. Stevens concurred. White dissented, joined by Blackmun and, except for the footnote and Part III, Souter. Blackmun, O’Connor, and Souter also wrote dissenting opinions.

**Historical Holding:** The Court adopted Kotteakos for constitutional trial error on federal habeas review and found no substantial and injurious effect here. It did not reclassify Doyle as nonconstitutional or hold that structural defects are ordinary trial error.

**Historical Authority:** 507 U.S. 619 (1993); a historical Supreme Court merits decision. Its authority in the historical legal system does not make it an effective decision of this reboot.

**Historical Sources:** [Published opinions, disposition, and opinion-joinder statement](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/619/).

## Withrow v. Williams

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Withrow v. Williams; No. 91-1030; 1993-04-21. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** AFFIRMED IN PART, REVERSED IN PART, and REMANDED. The Miranda-review holding was 5–4; the improper adjudication of the involuntariness claim was unanimous.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter wrote Part III for the unanimous Court and Parts I, II, and IV for White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, and himself. O’Connor, joined by Rehnquist, and Scalia, joined by Thomas, each concurred in part and dissented in part.

**Historical Holding:** Powell did not bar the Miranda claim. The district court erred in deciding the independent voluntariness claim without pleading, consent, or an opportunity for the State to present evidence.

## CSX Transportation, Inc. v. Easterwood

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** CSX Transportation, Inc. v. Easterwood; Nos. 91-790 and 91-1206; 1993-04-21. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** AFFIRMED. Parts I and II were unanimous; the application in Parts III and IV drew a 7–2 division.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** White wrote for the Court. Rehnquist, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, and Kennedy joined Parts III and IV. Thomas concurred in part and dissented in part, joined by Souter.

**Historical Holding:** Federal regulations preempted the ordinary excessive-speed claim but did not preempt the warning-device claim on this record. The Court required substantial coverage of the relevant subject and reserved a specific-individual-hazard question.

**Historical Authority:** 507 U.S. 658 (1993); a historical Supreme Court merits decision. Its authority in the historical legal system does not make it an effective decision of this reboot.

**Historical Sources:** [Published opinions, disposition, and opinion-joinder statement](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/658/).

## United States v. Olano

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** REVERSED AND REMANDED, 6–3.

## United States v. California
