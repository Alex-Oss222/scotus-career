## Reno v. Flores

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Reno v. Flores; No. 91-905; 1993-03-23. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** REVERSED AND REMANDED, 7–2.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Scalia wrote for Rehnquist, White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and himself. O’Connor concurred, joined by Souter. Stevens dissented, joined by Blackmun.

**Historical Holding:** The regulation survived the facial substantive-due-process, procedural-due-process, and statutory challenges. The Court upheld its custodian preferences and review-on-request structure in the facial posture. O’Connor emphasized the juveniles’ protected interest against institutional confinement.

## Saudi Arabia v. Nelson

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Saudi Arabia v. Nelson; No. 91-522; 1993-03-23. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** REVERSED. Eight Justices rejected jurisdiction over the intentional-tort claims, with different rationales; six rejected jurisdiction over the failure-to-warn claims.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter’s opinion was joined by Rehnquist, O’Connor, Scalia, and Thomas; Kennedy joined except its final paragraph addressing warnings. White concurred in the judgment, joined by Blackmun. Kennedy concurred in part and dissented in part, with Blackmun and Stevens joining Parts I-B and II. Blackmun also wrote separately; Stevens dissented.

**Historical Holding:** The intentional-tort claims were based on sovereign police conduct rather than qualifying United States commercial activity. The Court also rejected the warning theory as a reformulation of the sovereign torts. Separate opinions disputed the latter conclusion and identified unresolved merits and preclusion questions.

**Historical Authority:** 507 U.S. 349 (1993); a historical Supreme Court merits decision. Its authority in the historical legal system does not make it an effective decision of this reboot.

**Historical Sources:** [Published opinions, disposition, and opinion-joinder statement](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/349/).

## Cincinnati v. Discovery Network

**Case:** Cincinnati v. Discovery Network; No. 91-1200; 1993-03-24. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** AFFIRMED, 6–3.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens wrote for Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and himself. Blackmun concurred. Rehnquist dissented, joined by White and Thomas.

**Historical Holding:** The selective ban failed the reasonable-fit requirement because the commercial distinction was unrelated to the asserted physical harms. It was not a content-neutral time-place-manner regulation.

## Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins

### SECTION III — HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

**Case:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins; No. 91-1600; 1993-04-20. This supplement is introduced only after provisional non-Stone modeling is frozen. It is not simulated precedent and supplies no Stone position.

#### A. MERITS COMPARATOR

**Historical Outcome and Vote:** VACATED AND REMANDED, 9–0.

**Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor delivered the unanimous opinion. Kennedy concurred, joined by Rehnquist and Thomas.

**Historical Holding:** Age and years of service are analytically distinct; pension interference alone does not establish ADEA disparate treatment, but proxy or dual-motive evidence can matter. Thurston’s willfulness standard applies to individual decisions as well as formal policies. The remaining evidence required reconsideration.

**Historical Authority:** 507 U.S. 604 (1993); a historical Supreme Court merits decision. Its authority in the historical legal system does not make it an effective decision of this reboot.

**Historical Sources:** [Published opinions, disposition, and opinion-joinder statement](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/604/).
