## Soldal v. Cook County

### SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Soldal v. Cook County  
**Citation or Docket:** 506 U.S. 56; No. 91-6516.  
**October Term:** 1992.  
**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, pre-decision preparation; certiorari. The listed historical merits proceeding is retained as the reboot's working procedural premise, not as a newly rendered simulated grant.  
**Simulated Event Date:** 1992-12-08, the historical decision date used as the simulation calendar date.  
**Question Presented / Granted Scope:** Whether forcible removal of a mobile home with alleged police participation implicates the Fourth Amendment even without a separate invasion of privacy or liberty.  
**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Seventh Circuit, en banc, 942 F.2d 1073 (1991), affirming summary judgment for the defendants on the federal claims.  
**Relief Requested:** The Soldals seek reversal and further proceedings on their § 1983 unreasonable-seizure claim. Respondents seek affirmance, including on the alternative ground that the evidence does not establish state action.

**Material Record:** Private park employees disconnected and towed away the family’s mobile home while eviction litigation remained pending. Deputies allegedly prevented resistance despite knowing that no eviction order existed. A state judge ordered the home returned; it had been damaged. The district court found insufficient proof of conspiracy, but the court of appeals treated joint participation as sufficiently supported for summary-judgment purposes and rejected Fourth Amendment coverage. That procedural assumption is not a final finding against every defendant.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The alternative state-action ground can support affirmance without a cross-petition because it would not enlarge the judgment. The record must be viewed favorably to the family. Municipal policy, individual causation, immunity, damages, and ultimate reasonableness are not established merely by finding a seizure. A state-law remedy does not automatically answer a separately pleaded Fourth Amendment claim.

**Relevant Dates:** Eviction challenged: September 4, 1987; return ordered September 9, 1987; a later lawful eviction occurred in December 1987. Argument: October 5, 1992.

##### Issue 1: Seizure and attribution

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** The Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments and 42 U.S.C. § 1983 govern. United States v. Jacobsen, 466 U.S. 109 (1984), distinguishes infringement of privacy from meaningful interference with possessory interests. United States v. Place, 462 U.S. 696 (1983), and Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321 (1987), separately scrutinize seizures. Adickes v. S.H. Kress & Co., 398 U.S. 144 (1970), supplies joint-action principles. Monell, 436 U.S. 658 (1978), still limits municipal liability.

**Open Point:** Whether the civil setting or absence of a privacy intrusion removes this alleged taking of a home from Fourth Amendment coverage; whether the summary-judgment record permits attribution.

##### Issue 2: Separate merits and remedial inquiries

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Fourth Amendment coverage does not establish unreasonableness. Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 (1989), directs use of an applicable specific constitutional protection rather than displacement by generalized substantive due process. Parratt v. Taylor, 451 U.S. 527 (1981), concerns a distinct procedural-due-process inquiry.

**Open Point:** The lower court did not adjudicate the seizure’s reasonableness under the proper Fourth Amendment framework.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The en banc court treated a deprivation of property unaccompanied by privacy or liberty injury as outside the Fourth Amendment, particularly in private eviction litigation.

**Family:** The text protects houses and effects against seizures independently of searches; police participation supplies the governmental connection.

**Defendants:** A contrary rule could federalize routine repossessions; the park, not the deputies, physically removed the home, and state procedures already protected possession.

**Limits and alternatives:** A private repossession alone is not state action. Coverage leaves reasonableness, causation, municipal policy, and immunities to their own rules. The state-action argument remains an alternative ground rather than an additional appeal.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance is possible on a supported absence of state action, or on the proposed restricted reading of seizure coverage. Reversal on coverage returns the case for reasonableness and the remaining § 1983 requirements; it does not direct damages. A court accepting the summary-judgment inference of joint action need not finally determine that inference. The separate due-process theory need not be reached if the specific Fourth Amendment route suffices.

## Farrar v. Hobby

### SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Farrar v. Hobby  
**Citation or Docket:** 506 U.S. 103; No. 91-990.  
**October Term:** 1992.  
**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, pre-decision preparation; certiorari. The listed historical merits proceeding is retained as the reboot's working procedural premise, not as a newly rendered simulated grant.

**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fifth Circuit, 941 F.2d 1311 (1991), reversing the attorney-fee award because the nominal-damages plaintiff was not a prevailing party.  
**Relief Requested:** The estate seeks restoration of fee eligibility and reversal of the appellate fee ruling. Hobby defends denial of fees, including the asserted lack of meaningful success.

**Material Record:** The school operators sought $17 million from public officials over the school’s closure. After litigation and an earlier appeal, the operative judgment awarded one dollar against Hobby for a due-process violation without proven compensatory injury. The district court awarded approximately $280,000 in attorney’s fees. The current dispute does not reopen the nominal judgment or establish that Hobby caused the school’s financial losses.

##### Issue 1: Prevailing-party eligibility

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Section 1988 permits a reasonable attorney’s fee to a prevailing party in covered civil-rights litigation. Carey v. Piphus, 435 U.S. 247 (1978), recognizes nominal damages for a proved procedural violation without compensatory injury. Hewitt v. Helms, 482 U.S. 755 (1987), Rhodes v. Stewart, 488 U.S. 1 (1988), and Texas State Teachers Assn. v. Garland Independent School District, 489 U.S. 782 (1989), distinguish actual relief altering the parties’ legal relationship from an advisory success.

**Open Point:** Whether enforceable nominal relief satisfies the statutory threshold irrespective of its monetary size.

##### Issue 2: Reasonable amount and scope of review

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Hensley v. Eckerhart, 461 U.S. 424 (1983), makes degree of success central to fee reasonableness and requires an explained exercise of discretion. City of Riverside v. Rivera, 477 U.S. 561 (1986), does not impose proportionality between fees and damages.

**Open Point:** Whether this record permits immediate rejection of all fees, or whether the lower courts should address reasonableness after correction of the eligibility rule.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** The Fifth Circuit treated the nominal recovery as too insubstantial to make the estate a prevailing party.

**Estate:** The judgment compels Hobby to pay and vindicates an adjudicated right; Congress did not prescribe a minimum recovery. Civil-rights litigation can matter without compensatory damages.

**Hobby:** The action sought substantial compensation and obtained essentially none; a large fee would reward near-total failure.

**Necessary distinction:** Eligibility does not compel the full lodestar or any particular positive fee. Conversely, the absence of compensatory damages does not itself establish the absence of a legally operative judgment. The amount issue was not the basis of the decision below.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can affirm on the ineligibility theory; reverse that theory and remand for a reasonable-fee decision; or find eligibility but affirm denial on an adequately presented alternative amount ground. A remand does not automatically reinstate the existing award. Any decision on amount must distinguish limited monetary success, the actual claim vindicated, and demonstrated nonmonetary significance rather than presume public benefit.

**Case Name:** District of Columbia v. Greater Washington Board of Trade

##### Issue 1: ERISA coverage, exemption, and preemption

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** ERISA §§ 3, 4(b)(3), and 514(a), 29 U.S.C. §§ 1002, 1003(b)(3), 1144(a), distinguish covered plans, arrangements maintained solely to comply with workers’ compensation law, and laws relating to covered plans. **Concessions and limits:** The District’s relation concession narrows the controversy. Preemption of this provision is not a ruling that incidental costs from taxes, wages, or ordinary contract law are sufficient. The United States supported the employer’s preemption position.

Affirm if statutory reference to a covered plan remains dispositive despite separate administration. Reverse if the statutory exemption permits the independently administered obligation and the contrary reading is rejected through a candid treatment of the inherited cases. Any judgment should distinguish the covered-plan application from obligations measured solely by exempt plans. Neither route authorizes a general judicial revision of ERISA’s text.

##### Issue 1: Collateral-order appellate jurisdiction

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Section 1291 and Cohen v. Beneficial Industrial Loan Corp., 337 U.S. 541 (1949), permit review of the small class of conclusive, separate orders effectively unreviewable after final judgment. Coopers & Lybrand v. **Open Point:** Whether denial of the asserted sovereign immunity satisfies Cohen, without using appealability to establish the underlying immunity.

**Firm:** Premature appeals fragment litigation, and an entity whose governmental character is disputed should not obtain delay merely by asserting immunity.

**Limits:** The order must conclusively deny a genuine immunity claim. The appeal need not determine contract liability, and allowing it establishes neither entity status nor a constitutional immunity extending beyond the doctrine actually invoked.

Reverse and remand for appellate consideration of the immunity claim if Cohen is met. Affirm dismissal if eventual review adequately protects the claimed right or the relevant category falls outside Cohen. Neither disposition properly awards damages, dismisses the contract suit on immunity merits, or decides congressional abrogation in the abstract.

##### Issue 1: Judicial review of the Senate’s trial procedure

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Article I, § 3, cl. 6 assigns the Senate the sole power to try impeachments and separately specifies oath, presidential-trial presiding officer, and voting requirements. Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 (1962), identifies textual commitment and lack of judicially manageable standards as political-question grounds. Powell v. **Open Point:** Whether this particular challenge to evidence-taking procedure is committed to the Senate, rather than whether every imaginable impeachment-related constitutional claim is nonjusticiable.

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** The Senate’s rule permits a committee to receive evidence while reserving final judgment to the Senate. Constitutional text, impeachment practice, and the distinction between legislative impeachment and ordinary criminal adjudication supply the interpretive materials.

**Open Point:** If judicial review is available, whether “try” requires every Senator personally to receive live testimony rather than evaluate the committee record.

**Alternative:** Even if reviewable, the full Senate made the judgment on an evidentiary record after argument.

**Limits:** Institutional sensitivity alone is not a political question. A choice to reject this procedure claim does not require ruling on noncompliance with explicit voting or oath requirements.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirm the political-question dismissal on the particular Rule XI challenge. Alternatively, find review available and reject the claim on a permissible construction of “try,” carefully distinguishing a merits judgment from a jurisdictional dismissal. A contrary merits ruling would require separate consideration of authority and relief rather than automatic reinstatement. No route needs an advisory decision on hypothetical sham proceedings.

## Bray v. Alexandria Women's Health Clinic

### SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

#### A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

**Case Name:** Bray v. Alexandria Women's Health Clinic  
**Citation or Docket:** 506 U.S. 263; No. 90-985.  
**October Term:** 1992.  
**Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, pre-decision preparation; certiorari. The listed historical merits proceeding is retained as the reboot's working procedural premise, not as a newly rendered simulated grant.

**Question Presented / Granted Scope:** Whether the first clause of 42 U.S.C. § 1985(3) reaches the private clinic-blockade conspiracy proved here, including the required class-based animus and interference with a right protected against private encroachment; what follows for fees and the injunction’s state-law foundation.  
**Court and Judgment Under Review:** Fourth Circuit, 914 F.2d 582 (1990), affirming the injunction and federal fee award following the district court’s judgment, 726 F. Supp. 1483 (E.D. Va. 1989).  
**Relief Requested:** The organizers seek reversal of federal conspiracy liability and associated relief. Clinics and supporting organizations defend the injunction and fees; the existing state trespass and nuisance judgments supply an alternative remedial basis.

**Material Record:** Operation Rescue and individual organizers coordinated physical blockades and trespasses at clinics in the Washington metropolitan area. The district court found liability under § 1985(3) on a theory of interference with women’s interstate travel, and separately under state trespass and nuisance law. The injunction barred trespass and obstruction at specified clinics. The federal fee-and-cost award was $27,687.55.

**Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The dispute concerns private actors, not a proved state-action conspiracy. ##### Issue 1: Private conspiracy under the deprivation clause

**Other Governing Law and Material Authority:** Section 1985(3); Griffin v. Breckenridge, 403 U.S. 88 (1971), requires class-based invidious purpose and recognizes some private conspiracies. United Brotherhood of Carpenters v. Scott, 463 U.S. 825 (1983), requires the right invoked against a purely private conspiracy to be protected against private encroachment and the conspiracy to target that right. The abortion liberty protected by Roe and Casey is a constraint on government; those decisions do not themselves establish private conspiracy liability.

**Open Point:** Whether the proved targeting meets Griffin, and independently whether the travel right rather than only the locally available service was the object.

Ordinary preservation rules constrain a new hindrance-clause theory.

**Open Point:** Whether the existing state judgments independently sustain the injunction, and whether any further proceedings on a new theory are procedurally available.

#### C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Below:** Interference with access by interstate patients and discrimination against women seeking abortion supported federal liability; trespass and nuisance separately justified restrictions.

**Organizers:** Opposition to abortion is not automatically hostility to women, and local blockades aimed at stopping abortions do not become attacks on interstate travel merely because some patients traveled. They also contest introduction of the unpleaded hindrance clause.

**Clinics:** The deliberate obstruction of a sex-specific liberty and the practical ability to travel to obtain it can establish discriminatory interference; state-law judgments independently support effective protection.

**SG:** The United States urged rejection of the federal deprivation-clause theory.

**Limits:** The lawfulness of opposing abortion and the unlawfulness of trespass are separate. No party’s preferred abortion policy supplies a statutory element. Federal access legislation not yet enacted cannot be used.

#### D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A supported deprivation-clause violation can sustain covered federal relief and fee consideration. Failure of either an indispensable animus or privately protected-right requirement defeats that theory. The Court can then vacate the federal fee award and remand to assess the already adjudicated state grounds without deciding an unpleaded hindrance claim. It cannot convert the constitutional abortion right into a direct prohibition on all private interference or dismiss the entire action for want of jurisdiction solely because the federal claim loses.
