#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Statutory exclusion versus displacement of common law

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved issue within the review posture stated in Section I.

**Stone’s Legal Position and Treatment of Precedent:** Apply the compensation-for-delay principles of Royal Indemnity and West Virginia to the mature federal food-stamp debt, and determine whether the 1982 Act displaced that common-law obligation.

**Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Act’s exclusion of states from § 3717’s mandatory interest mechanism does not eliminate an otherwise applicable federal common-law obligation to compensate the United States for delayed payment of a matured debt. Those principles extend to the food-stamp debt presented here.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone does not infer a claim solely from congressional silence. Royal Indemnity supplies the federal principle that interest compensates delayed payment of a matured obligation, and West Virginia confirms its application to a state’s debt to the United States. That same reason applies to Texas’s food-stamp obligation: withholding payment deprives the federal creditor of the money during the delay. The exact reach of state interest liability was contested when Congress enacted the Debt Collection Act in 1982; West Virginia was decided in 1987, so it cannot be treated as a settled 1982 legislative premise. The exclusion nevertheless removes states from the new mandatory statutory mechanism without language displacing the independently justified common-law obligation. Remand applies that obligation’s lawful rate, period, and qualifications; West Virginia does not authorize generalized balancing simply because a state is the debtor. [West Virginia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/479/305/) supplies the pre-event state-debt authority.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No freestanding federal common law merely because federal money is involved; no automatic statutory rate.

Finality, federalism, and retrial costs are legitimate reasons to consider Kotteakos, not interests the law excludes. For this preserved Doyle trial error properly before the habeas court, Stone nevertheless would keep the State’s Chapman burden: the error concerns the adjudication’s use of protected silence, and uncertainty about its effect should remain with the party responsible for that violation. Existing exhaustion, preservation, and other habeas gates protect finality, while Chapman already permits denial when the error did not contribute to the verdict. This choice accepts a greater possibility of retrial where prejudice remains uncertain; it does not deny the costs or rest on an assertion that stare decisis compelled the answer. The reviewing court must weigh permissible impeachment and inculpatory evidence against the credibility defense under that chosen allocation. [Chapman](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/386/18/) and [Kotteakos](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/328/750/) supply the competing pre-event models.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Do not extend an exception beyond its rationale

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the reviewability question without treating Powell as a jurisdictional withdrawal.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply § 2254 and decline the proposed extension of Powell.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A full and fair state opportunity to litigate Miranda does not itself foreclose federal habeas review of a conviction alleged to rest on statements obtained in violation of its safeguards.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Powell’s exclusionary-rule calculus does not transfer automatically to a privilege governing compelled self-incrimination at trial. Miranda provides a structured protection connected to reliable and voluntary statements; removing it from habeas would invite parallel voluntariness litigation and abandon its distinct function. This is preservation of a review channel, not an assumption that the underlying claim wins.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No ruling on every prophylactic rule or a general reopening of Fourth Amendment exclusionary claims.

##### Issue 2: The unpleaded voluntariness theory

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone independently addresses the district court’s adjudication of a claim the State had no fair opportunity to litigate.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing notice and Rule 15 principles.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A habeas court may not grant relief on this unpleaded, unconsented involuntariness theory without allowing the State to present its case.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The custody timing question and whether later statements were overborne can depend on different evidence. A warning violation does not automatically establish coercion of all subsequent statements. Protecting the prisoner’s rights does not authorize one-sided factual adjudication.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding that the later statements were voluntary; that question remains for proper proceedings if the claim is lawfully presented.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Compare the exact duty and the operative federal rule

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches each preemption question separately and keeps liability merits outside the decision.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the disputed extent of express FRSA coverage under § 434. Distinguish the subject covered by § 213.9’s track-class speed maximum from a properly presented crossing condition that the federal rules leave uncovered; do not replace coverage preemption with an actual-conflict-only test.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 213.9 preempts a state duty that re-evaluates the federally prescribed track-safety speed maximum. A preserved duty addressing a distinct crossing condition may proceed only if its subject is not substantially covered by the operative federal regulations. General allegations that the train was too fast for the time and place do not establish that distinction. The warning-device rules do not preempt this claim because their operative project predicates are absent.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress chose covering, which requires comparing the subject of the actual state duty with the federal rule rather than equating any speed-related duty with the whole regulated field. Section 213.9 fixes maximum speeds by track class. Contemporaneous rulemaking treated population density and crossing-specific variables as outside that track-safety calculation. Stone would therefore distinguish a demand to recalibrate the track maximum from an actually presented duty addressing an uncovered crossing condition. That is his proposed resolution of the scope question, not a claim that earlier Supreme Court law already adopted it. The packet describes ordinary time-and-place speed allegations but does not establish a preserved, distinct uncovered theory, so a limited remand must resolve that point without assuming an additional claim exists. If the duty is only an ordinary demand for slower operation on the federally classified track, the speed claim remains preempted. For warnings, general planning and an abandoned project do not satisfy §§ 646.214(b)(3)–(4)’s operative conditions. Coverage and each statutory local-hazard condition remain independent legal inquiries; nonconflict alone does not save a covered claim. [Published record and contemporaneous regulatory discussion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/507/658/) supply source context, not an entering holding in this case.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general exemption for negligence at crossings, no statewide local-hazard exception, and no evasion of express coverage through different phrasing of the same duty. The specific-individual-hazard issue remains reserved. If the existing record supplies no preserved uncovered crossing theory, the proposed remand ends in retention of speed preemption.
