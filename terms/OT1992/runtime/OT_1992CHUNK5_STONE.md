#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the Seventh Circuit judgment and REMAND for application of Chapman.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The court of appeals should review the constitutional error under Chapman rather than automatically reinstate the district court’s writ. If the State cannot show harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, a conditional writ should permit retrial within a reasonable period or end custody on the invalid conviction. No acquittal is directed.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Retain the constitutional harmless-error rule

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the conceded Doyle violation and its collateral harmless-error standard; no default or retroactivity issue is used to avoid the question.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Retain the received Chapman practice and reject the proposed new Kotteakos substitution; acknowledge that the precise collateral distinction has not previously been squarely resolved.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federal habeas court reviewing this preserved Doyle trial error should apply Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt rule, with its existing allocation, rather than impose a less protective prejudice standard solely because review is collateral.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 2254 does not itself make this constitutional error harmless. Chapman already accommodates the cost of retrial when the error did not contribute to the result. The asserted finality interests support existing channel and preservation rules but do not establish why the consequence of the same preserved trial violation should depend on which reviewing court finally recognizes it. The wrong concerns the fairness of using protected silence, not merely police deterrence. Stone changes neither the underlying right nor the trial/structural distinction. On the record, permissible impeachment and inculpatory evidence matter, but their effect must be assessed against the credibility defense under the correct rule.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic writ, no reopening of defaulted claims without their own exception, and no holding that every procedural rule of direct appeal must govern collateral review.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM IN PART, REVERSE IN PART, and REMAND: retain federal review of the Miranda claim; reverse the post-warning involuntariness ruling made without proper litigation.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court may determine the consequence of the properly presented Miranda violation under the effective harmless-error law. An amended or otherwise properly presented voluntariness claim requires the ordinary procedural gates and a fair chance for both sides to address it. Do not order categorical suppression of all later statements or automatic release from this record.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Do not extend an exception beyond its rationale

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the reviewability question without treating Powell as a jurisdictional withdrawal.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply § 2254 and decline the proposed extension of Powell.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A full and fair state opportunity to litigate Miranda does not itself foreclose federal habeas review of a conviction alleged to rest on statements obtained in violation of its safeguards.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Powell’s exclusionary-rule calculus does not transfer automatically to a privilege governing compelled self-incrimination at trial. Miranda provides a structured protection connected to reliable and voluntary statements; removing it from habeas would invite parallel voluntariness litigation and abandon its distinct function. This is preservation of a review channel, not an assumption that the underlying claim wins.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No ruling on every prophylactic rule or a general reopening of Fourth Amendment exclusionary claims.

##### Issue 2: The unpleaded voluntariness theory

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone independently addresses the district court’s adjudication of a claim the State had no fair opportunity to litigate.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing notice and Rule 15 principles.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A habeas court may not grant relief on this unpleaded, unconsented involuntariness theory without allowing the State to present its case.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The custody timing question and whether later statements were overborne can depend on different evidence. A warning violation does not automatically establish coercion of all subsequent statements. Protecting the prisoner’s rights does not authorize one-sided factual adjudication.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No finding that the later statements were voluntary; that question remains for proper proceedings if the claim is lawfully presented.

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the Eleventh Circuit in both cross-petitions: ordinary excessive-speed claim preempted; warning-device claim not preempted on this record.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Continue proceedings on the warning-device claim without a finding of negligence. Leave the speed claim barred to the extent actually decided; do not resolve an unpresented specific-individual-hazard claim.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Compare the exact duty and the operative federal rule

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches each preemption question separately and keeps liability merits outside the decision.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the express FRSA coverage standard and its savings provisions; no new free-floating federalism exception.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The speed regulations substantially cover the ordinary operating-speed duty, while the invoked warning-device rules do not displace this claim because their operative project predicates are not shown at this crossing.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress chose covering, not mere relation to a regulated field. The federal speed framework selects an operating standard that a negligence verdict cannot replace through an ordinary excessive-speed theory. The warning program, by contrast, does not establish the required device through general planning alone, and the abandoned project does not supply an installed federally prescribed warning arrangement. The local-hazard exception keeps its statutory limits.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No conclusion that every federally funded crossing is identically situated, no universal ceiling from regulatory activity alone, and no decision of the reserved specific-hazard duty.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE and REMAND.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside the new-trial disposition founded solely on the per se alternate-presence rule. The lower court may address any preserved remaining appellate issues. No automatic reinstatement of unrelated counts beyond the scope of the appellate judgment is assumed.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Rule 52(b) has distinct requirements

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone accepts the Government’s concessions of plain error and reaches substantial rights; he does not manufacture a waiver holding.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Clarify the rule’s existing structure using Atkinson and Young, not a new general appellate merits test.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Forfeited error may be corrected only when it is plain, affects substantial rights, and warrants exercise of the court’s remedial discretion because it seriously affects the fairness, integrity, or public reputation of proceedings. The defendant ordinarily carries the prejudice showing. This record does not justify per se prejudice from the instructed alternates’ mere presence.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Each proposition performs a different job: error identifies the violated rule, plainness limits correction of unpreserved matters, substantial rights addresses effect, and Atkinson guides permission to remedy. A valid intentional waiver differs from nonassertion. Deliberative privacy protects against influence, but the Court cannot assume that the alternates disobeyed instructions or changed the verdict without evidence or a justified legal presumption.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No holding that every error requires outcome proof, no denial of relief for structural or legitimately presumed-prejudice categories, and no actual-innocence-only limit on remedial discretion.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No recovery on the asserted federal common-law or subrogation theories. Leave the completed partial refund intact and do not adjudicate the correctness of the underlying California classification.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Economic cost is not legal incidence or a cause of action

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the asserted federal restitution source and the alternative subrogation route separately.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply New Mexico and traditional derivative-claim principles; reject creation of federal common law from federal financial interest alone.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Contractual reimbursement of a private contractor’s state taxes creates neither a direct federal tax immunity nor an independent federal common-law refund action for state-law assessment errors. The derivative subrogation claim does not shed the contractor’s pre-existing limitations defect.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Government chose the reimbursement arrangement and could direct the contractor’s timely remedies. That relationship does not make California the recipient of unlawfully diverted federal money or create an implied contractual duty to the Treasury. On the alternative theory, a subrogee cannot acquire a better claim simply by invoking sovereign status after the underlying right has become impaired. The federal immunity from certain state limitations is not authority to revive a claim already burdened when asserted through this derivative route.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule permitting direct taxation of the United States and no restriction on a refund remedy Congress validly creates. California’s tax calculation is not approved on its merits.
