#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** REVERSE the Ninth Circuit judgment insofar as it rests on a higher constitutional competence standard and REMAND for further consideration under the proper, separately stated capacity and waiver inquiries.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the conditional-writ direction tied to the higher standard. On remand, address the existing claims and findings concerning reason to conduct further inquiry and whether the actual waivers were knowing, intelligent, and voluntary. The disposition does not independently affirm every conviction or sentence, revive the death sentence for the ex-wife's murder already reduced to life without parole on direct review (the other two death sentences remained), or treat the psychiatric reports as conclusive for all later moments.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Dusky does not dispense with actual informed choice

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone declines to introduce the unpreserved Teague defense and decides the fully litigated legal question. He separates that question from unresolved application under the proper standard.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve the open common-capacity question through Dusky and the received waiver cases, rejecting a separate higher competence threshold while preserving the independent waiver requirements. The equivalence of standards is the resolution proposed here, not a previously settled same-case rule.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The constitutional capacity standard Stone adopts is Dusky for standing trial, pleading guilty, and waiving counsel: sufficient present ability to consult with counsel with rational understanding, and rational as well as factual understanding of the proceedings. A court must also establish that the particular relinquishment is knowing, intelligent, and voluntary, and must undertake a competence inquiry when circumstances give reason for doubt.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Capacity to understand the proceeding is not legal skill, and self-representation cannot depend on professional competence. Conversely, a defendant’s ability to consult with counsel does not prove that a later decision is informed and uncoerced. The trial judge made express findings, but the medication and other circumstances must be evaluated for the work the distinct doctrines assign them. On remand determine whether contemporaneous circumstances required further inquiry, whether any retrospective determination can still be reliable, and whether each actual relinquishment was informed and voluntary. Earlier reports cannot conclusively answer a materially changed condition. Apply pre-AEDPA habeas treatment of the state findings before selecting relief.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No automatic hearing is required for every plea or waiver. No automatic validity follows from monosyllabic answers or old reports. States may adopt additional protections, and this case does not decide competence for execution or a later separate standard for conducting a trial without counsel.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the invalidation of the unequal proof burdens, REVERSE the categorical invalidation of family or guardian participation, and REMAND for a limited remedy and unresolved matters.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Address severability and the State's lawful choices for equalizing safeguards. This judgment does not establish a national beyond-reasonable-doubt commitment standard or require immediate release of every class member. Each continuing confinement needs its own lawful basis. Family participation remains subject to counsel, effective contest, and independent adjudication.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The preserved equality claim and allocation of error risk

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Do not reach the heightened-scrutiny theory first advanced at this stage. Decide the preserved rational-basis challenge under Cleburne and the separate due-process question under its own sources.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply received rational-basis review to the particular procedural disadvantage. Stone's preferred disability tier is not the governing rule in this appeal. This conclusion is his application of an available framework, not a result compelled by it.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Kentucky's reasons for treating the two conditions differently do not rationally explain the lower overall protection against erroneous institutional confinement imposed on this class. Satisfaction of Addington's federal minimum does not independently answer the equality objection to unequal safeguards. [Cleburne, 473 U.S. 432 (1985)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/473/432/); [Addington, 441 U.S. 418 (1979)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/441/418/).

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The State's diagnostic argument concerns the identification of a condition, while commitment also requires judgments about dangerousness, treatment benefit, and institutional placement. Stone accepts that a longer behavioral history may improve some predictions and that reasonable legislative generalizations are permissible. He rejects the conclusion that those differences sufficiently explain assigning the individual a greater risk of erroneous confinement across the scheme as a whole. A categorical claim that one group's treatment is less intrusive also does not adequately account for the comparable loss of physical liberty. This reasoning addresses all of the State's explanations rather than requiring a separate empirical demonstration for each predicate. It does not require formal legislative findings, a scientific study, or governmental proof under an unstated higher tier.

**Contrary application:** Different diagnostic histories, prediction risks, and treatment may be understood to permit different safeguards. Stone acknowledges that rational-basis argument. His conclusion concerns its connection to the actual disadvantage—allocation of the risk of mistaken confinement—and does not declare every distinction between the conditions irrational. [Record and contemporaneous analyses, 509 U.S. 312 (1993)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/312/).

**Boundary:** Equal protection requires a rational relationship to the particular procedural difference, not identical procedures in every setting. Addington's minimum and the substantive predicates of commitment remain independent. No new scrutiny tier or categorical liberty discount for disability is adopted.

##### Issue 2: Differential family-party status and fair adjudication

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the equality challenge to family-party status separately from its Mathews procedural consequences.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A lifelong developmental condition and continuing family involvement plausibly make relatives' participation especially informative, supplying a rational basis for the participation distinction. Under Mathews, party status does not by itself violate due process where the subject can effectively contest the evidence with counsel before an independent decisionmaker.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Gathering relevant information differs from assigning a person's liberty less weight. Relatives' knowledge can be tested in the proceeding; their preferences, conflicts, or convenience establish no commitment predicate. Stone does not presume that a family member and the adult share interests. A neutral adjudicator must apply the lawful burden to the statutory grounds, and the adult must have an effective opportunity to answer adverse evidence. [Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319 (1976)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/424/319/).

**Boundary:** A proceeding in which relatives control the outcome, suppress the adult's position, or defeat effective contest remains challengeable. Neither ruling establishes that every individual hearing was fair.

## St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks

### SECTION II — STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

#### A. STATUS

**Case:** St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks; No. 92-602; event 1993-06-25.  
**Version:** OT1992 user-supplied Hicks revision, v1.3.  
**Approval Status:** USER-DIRECTED REVISION. The user supplied and authorized this replacement proof rule. It is Stone's proposed position, not a rendered simulated decision.  
**Direction Source:** The user's attached Hicks proposal dated June 25, 1993, treated as substantive direction rather than as an independent source of law.  
**Current-Through Baseline:** The user-authorized pre-OT1992 historical baseline, independently date-eligible law, and actual simulated law effective immediately before the 1993-06-25 event. The supplied OT1991 holdings register has been checked. Its fractured *United States v. Burke* tax judgment establishes no controlling Title VII proof rule and does not alter the allocation adopted here. Earlier same-day law counts only where its effective sequence is established.  
**Conditions:** Revalidate material changes in effective simulated law, record, or review scope. This authoring revision enters no simulated judgment.  

**Authoring Provenance:** Master Jurisprudence informs the implementation below, not governing law or a separate Engine input. The legal basis is Title VII's prohibition, the inherited *McDonnell Douglas–Burdine* method, ordinary civil proof principles, and the expressly defended limited departure stated below. Production, persuasion, factual findings, source-specific causes of action, and remedy remain separate.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the Eighth Circuit's judgment and REMAND with instructions to set aside the District Court's judgment on the Title VII claim and reconsider that claim under the allocation stated below. The Eighth Circuit's direction to enter judgment for Hicks is displaced; the employer receives an opportunity to carry the newly assigned burden of persuasion.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Findings not independently erroneous or infected by the former allocation remain part of the case. The trial court may allow a focused supplemental presentation when fairness under the new burden requires it, with an equivalent opportunity for Hicks to respond; the rule creates no routine second trial or entitlement to successive explanations. The related § 1983 judgment is vacated insofar as it rested on automatic liability and returns for source-specific reconsideration. The new Title VII allocation does not automatically govern that distinct claim. The remand directs no judgment for either party and establishes no remedy before liability, defenses, and resulting loss are resolved.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The trigger and the shifted burden of persuasion

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the legal consequence under Title VII after the employee proves the prima facie case and affirmatively proves that every material nondiscriminatory explanation offered for the challenged decision was not an actual reason. The employer's burden at the ordinary explanation stage remains production. The employee bears the burden of proving both predicates by a preponderance; equipoise on either predicate does not activate the shift.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Retain the initial *McDonnell Douglas* and *Burdine* structure but make an express, limited departure from *Burdine*'s proposition that the employee always retains the ultimate burden of persuasion. The departure arises only after the employee affirmatively establishes the specified prima facie and all-material-explanations-disproved predicate. It rejects both automatic liability from falsity and a regime that leaves every residual uncertainty with an employee who has successfully disproved the employer's complete material account.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** When an employee proves the applicable prima facie case and affirmatively proves, by a preponderance of the evidence, that every material nondiscriminatory explanation offered for the challenged decision was not an actual reason for it, the employer bears the burden of proving, by a preponderance of the evidence, the absence of the prohibited discriminatory cause. Producing another plausible explanation does not discharge that burden. If the evidence on discriminatory causation remains evenly balanced, the employee prevails on that issue.

**Essential Reasoning:** The rule assigns uncertainty only after a materially strengthened evidentiary showing. A prima facie case supplies an initial inference, and affirmative disproof of the employer's entire material account adds a further basis for inferring prohibited discrimination while removing the explanation the employer chose to present. The employer ordinarily has superior access to its decisionmakers and internal process. Leaving all remaining uncertainty with the employee at that point would weaken the indirect method of proof by allowing an employer to rely on explanations the employee has proved did not motivate the decision. The rebuttal opportunity preserves the possibility of another nonprohibited cause; the shift does not declare that every false explanation logically proves racial motive or punish dishonesty as an independent Title VII wrong.

**Doctrinal Components:** Protected status and an adverse decision do not alone establish the prima facie case. The employer need not persuade at the production stage. The employee must persuade the factfinder that the stated material reasons did not actually account for the challenged action. If one genuine material explanation remains, this particular trigger is not satisfied, although that does not defeat an otherwise sufficient discrimination claim or ordinary proof that a prohibited consideration also influenced the action under the governing causation rule.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The trigger concerns actual motivation. An employer may genuinely act on inaccurate information; proof that the information was wrong does not necessarily disprove actual reliance on it. Peripheral discrepancies do not activate the rule. A factfinder may nevertheless determine that asserted performance concerns did not motivate the action even if some underlying incidents occurred. Deliberate fabrication strengthens the inference, but no separate perjury finding is required.

##### Issue 2: What the employer must prove and how the rule operates

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Define the shifted burden without converting it into a demand that management prove wisdom, legality under every statute, or a particular documentary form.

**Stone's Legal Position:** The employer must prove what caused this decision and must establish the absence of the discrimination alleged under Title VII in the sense required by the governing substantive causation rule. It may rely on the whole admissible record, including evidence introduced by Hicks. Contemporaneous records, sequence, actual comparisons, and credible testimony may suffice; no document, confession, expert study, or fixed form of corroboration is invariably required.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Showing that the employer could have acted for a legitimate reason is insufficient. A suggestion of personal hostility is likewise insufficient without evidence of its source and operation because hostility may itself be racially motivated. A later explanation remains subject to examination in light of the earlier false account and the employee's opportunity to contest it. The court may consider supported evidence beyond a party's preferred wording, but it may not invent a motive or rely decisively on a new factual theory without fair notice and an opportunity to respond.

**Essential Reasoning and Administration:** Courts identify the actual role of each offered reason rather than count labels. The employee need not disprove every imaginable lawful motive. Parties must know which material explanations are offered and receive a meaningful opportunity to test them. Future cases ordinarily present the relevant evidence in one proceeding. Reopening in this case must be tied to fairness under the changed allocation, not to a desire to replace rejected explanations.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Existing rules governing disclosure, credibility, and litigation misconduct continue to apply. The employer need not prove its conduct prudent or lawful under every other source. The allocation does not supply a same-decision defense, change the substantive causation rule, establish damages, or authorize successive attempts to explain the action.

##### Issue 3: Legal basis and the limited departure from Burdine

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** State candidly why Stone may alter the proof allocation and what authority does not do that work.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Federal Rule of Evidence 301 does not itself authorize a persuasion shift; its ordinary rule leaves persuasion where it began. Congress did not expressly enact a post-pretext allocation. Stone adopts this rule as a Title VII-specific construction of the proof method under § 703(a)(1). *Price Waterhouse* demonstrates that Title VII proof can include a judicially recognized employer persuasion burden, but its different predicate does not dictate this rule. The legal justification is the combined discriminatory inference, the statutory function of indirect proof, and the employer's superior access to its own discredited decision process.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** *Burdine* remains controlling through the ordinary explanation stage. Stone departs only from its perpetual-assignment proposition after the employee proves the defined trigger. The alternative is better grounded because it preserves a genuine rebuttal, ties the shift to proof rather than suspicion, and assigns residual uncertainty to the party best situated to explain a process whose complete material account has been disproved.

**Essential Reasoning and Administrability:** The predicates and consequences are ascertainable: identify the prima facie elements; identify the material reasons actually offered; decide by a preponderance whether each was an actual reason; and, only if all are disproved, decide whether the employer proves the absence of prohibited cause. Separate findings on those steps permit consistent trial administration and appellate review. The rule avoids both a conclusive pretext presumption and an undefined demand for additional independent evidence.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The underlying claim, defenses, and remedies retain their own sources. Any available mixed-motive or same-decision defense must satisfy the law governing the claim and period. Stone does not assume that the Civil Rights Act of 1991 applies to employment actions taken in 1984, and the supplied OT1991 *Burke* judgment supplies no controlling answer to that retroactivity or proof question.

##### Issue 4: Application to Hicks and the separate § 1983 claim

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply the new allocation to the existing findings without treating allegations, possible motives, or historical descriptions as adjudicated facts beyond their proper status.

**Stone's Legal Position:** The District Court found the prima facie case established and rejected the defendants' stated explanations for the demotion and discharge. Those findings require application of the new allocation to each challenged decision insofar as they establish the trigger defined above. The former conclusion that Hicks failed to persuade the court of racial motivation does not answer whether the employer can now prove the absence of the prohibited discriminatory cause.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** On remand, the court must assess the entire record, including comparative discipline evidence and evidence tending against discrimination. Possible personal hostility and the bare existence of workplace violations do not resolve causation. If the employer carries its burden, it prevails on the disputed Title VII causation issue. If it fails, including at equipoise, that issue is resolved for Hicks; judgment and relief then depend on independently applicable defenses and remaining requirements.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The changed burden may justify a focused supplemental presentation, but valid existing findings remain relevant. The court must not treat a merely conceivable lawful reason as proof that the same decision actually would have occurred. It must give both sides a fair opportunity to address any supported explanation on which the result will turn.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Title VII's new allocation does not automatically govern § 1983. The defendants, elements, defenses, and remedies remain source-specific. Vacatur of the related judgment reaches only the extent to which it rested on automatic liability or an allocation that must be reconsidered; no universal equivalence between the causes of action is adopted.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The actual pleading question and the separate federal dismissal

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state claim was dismissed before trial. Assume deliberate use of race and subordination of ordinary districting considerations, as alleged. The question is whether that alleged design, without a further constitutional deprivation, states the equal-protection claim asserted here. The reported complaint did not assert dilution. [Procedural record, 509 U.S. 636–640](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/509/630/).

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary pleading law while resolving the sufficiency of the proposed constitutional theory. Do not decide disputed purpose by imagining highways, communities, or political compromises inconsistent with the allegations. Do not recast the substantive question as a universal Article III rule: the voters complain about a state arrangement that actually assigns them to constituencies.

**Federal defendants:** The Voting Rights Act's §14(b) review channel and the separate restriction on review of the Attorney General's preclearance determination support leaving that dismissal undisturbed. This does not insulate the State's plan from an otherwise cognizable challenge. [Morris v. Gressette, 432 U.S. 491, 501–507 (1977)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/432/491/).

##### Issue 2: Equal political membership, including for these plaintiffs

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Deliberate or predominant consideration of race in constructing territorial constituencies does not, standing alone, deny a resident equal political membership. Equal protection remains available against racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic status, and other constitutional impairments under their governing rules.

**Historical foundation: expansion and correction through law.** Stone understands equal political membership against a history of contested enlargement, reversals, and renewed protection. Constitutional amendments, state reforms, federal statutes, and judicial decisions have changed both the electorate and the legal safeguards of participation. The authority and scope of each change matter. An earlier exclusion cannot survive a later constitutional prohibition merely because it was once customary.

**Slavery, property, and political agency:** The original Constitution's three-fifths formula counted enslaved people for apportioning House seats and direct taxes without conferring voting rights on them. Compared with counting only the free population, that arrangement enlarged slaveholding States' representation while leaving enslaved people excluded from choosing representatives. Being included in an apportionment count did not itself secure equal political agency. Voting qualifications otherwise varied by State; the original Constitution linked House electors' qualifications to those for the most numerous branch of the state legislature. This was not a uniform national franchise for all white men or a single national landownership rule. [U.S. Const., original Art. I, §2, cls. 1, 3](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript).

**Reconstruction changed the governing constitutional commitments:** The Thirteenth Amendment abolished slavery, subject to its criminal-punishment exception. The Fourteenth secured citizenship and equal-protection guarantees and replaced the three-fifths apportionment formula. The Fifteenth prohibited denial or abridgment of voting rights on account of race, color, or previous servitude and empowered Congress to enforce that prohibition. These changes repudiated central features of the earlier order. They did not immediately achieve universal suffrage or remove every practical barrier; the Fourteenth Amendment's §2 itself retained a male, age-qualified provision addressing disenfranchisement and apportionment. [U.S. Const., amends. XIII–XV](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/amendments-11-27).

**States participated in both expansion and retreat:** New Jersey's 1776 constitution required fifty pounds of clear estate and permitted qualifying women and free Black men to vote; its 1807 legislation restricted the electorate to free white male citizens. Wyoming Territory extended suffrage to women in 1869 and retained it upon statehood in 1890. Such changes show why state tradition must be examined at the relevant time and under the governing legal source. They also prevent describing democratic inclusion as an inevitable or uninterrupted progression. [New Jersey Constitution of 1776, art. IV](https://www.nj.gov/state/archives/documentary-treasures-nj-state-constitutions-1776.shtml); [New Jersey's voting history](https://www.nj.gov/nj/about/history/short_history.shtml); [Wyoming suffrage history](https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2019/08/15/rightfully-hers-woman-suffrage-before-the-19th-amendment/).

**Later guarantees addressed further barriers:** The Nineteenth Amendment prohibited sex-based voting exclusion. The Twenty-Fourth prohibited poll-tax barriers in the federal elections it specifies; the Twenty-Sixth protected citizens eighteen and older against age-based voting exclusion. These amendments, proposed by Congress and ratified by the States, supplied defined guarantees rather than a promise that every group would hold a proportional share of office. The removal of one legal disqualification did not establish that all other barriers had disappeared. [U.S. Const., amends. XIX, XXIV, XXVI](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/amendments-11-27).

**Harper supplies a judicial foundation for correcting inherited restrictions:** *Harper* held that wealth or payment of a tax could not determine eligibility to vote in state elections and overruled *Breedlove* insofar as it permitted a tax-payment prerequisite. The Court expressly recognized that understandings of equal treatment can change, while grounding its decision in equal protection and the irrelevance of wealth to voting qualifications. Stone draws from that reasoning a duty to assess inherited restrictions under the constitutional guarantee and to acknowledge a doctrinal correction when required. Changing public preferences alone do not authorize him to replace the governing rule. [Harper v. Virginia Board of Elections, 383 U.S. 663, 665–670 (1966)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/383/663/).

**Reynolds connects representation to citizens' equal voting power:** *Reynolds* rejected history alone and economic or other group interests as justifications for departures from population-based state legislative representation. It also explained how population changes can render inherited arrangements unequal. Its state-apportionment holding preserves the distinction from the federal Senate's express constitutional structure. It supplies no entitlement to proportional racial officeholding or representation by someone of the same race. [Reynolds v. Sims, 377 U.S. 533, 566–568, 571–580 (1964)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/377/533/).

**Federal enforcement responded to repeated failures:** *South Carolina v. Katzenbach* examined the passage and erosion of Reconstruction enforcement, discriminatory substitutions, and the inadequacy of earlier remedies in sustaining stronger congressional protection under the Fifteenth Amendment. This history supports lawful preventive action when formal guarantees have failed in practice. Federal institutions also participated in earlier failures, and *Harper* itself corrected a prior federal judicial decision. Neither level of government receives a presumption that its conduct advances equality. [383 U.S. 301, 308–315, 324–334 (1966)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/383/301/).

**Effect on Shaw:** This history informs Stone's understanding of inclusion and explains why lawful protection may require attention to documented racial electoral conditions. It does not establish that this map was required or justified, replace any claim's elements, or permit historical generalizations to defeat adequately pleaded facts. White plaintiffs retain personal equal-protection rights; past racial dominance neither cancels an actual injury nor proves that white officeholders serve their interests. The proposed dismissal continues to rest on the specific distinction between territorial racial design alone and an actionable deprivation defended below.

**Essential Reasoning:** District lines affect people: they assign residents to constituencies and change political circumstances. The inquiry concerns the treatment imposed through that assignment. Within a territorial district all residents remain constituents and participate in the same election regardless of individual race. The representative's constituency includes residents of every race. A demographic objective does not itself prescribe racial eligibility for voting or office, make a candidate the legal representative of one race, or require citizens to associate or vote along racial lines.

Those features explain Stone's proposed distinction, but common formal access is not a complete constitutional answer. An arrangement can impose racial separation or unequal participation within a shared institution. The analysis must therefore examine what the alleged government action does, including any official designation or restriction, under the doctrine protecting the interest involved. Stone's conclusion concerns territorial racial design alone; it does not treat possession of a ballot as proof of equal civic treatment.

**Every plaintiff's individual right:** Equal protection belongs to persons. *Shelley* explains that its guarantees are personal; *Croson* applies that principle to scrutiny of state racial classifications. A white plaintiff's personal deprivation cannot be answered by statewide white political strength or supposed benefits from representatives serving other constituencies. Stone rejects the lower court's statewide-representation rationale as a complete answer and does not presume that a remedial purpose resolves an injury. The same inquiry applies to a Black resident in a majority-white district and a white resident in a majority-Black district. [Shelley v. Kraemer, 334 U.S. 1, 21–22 (1948)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/334/1/); [Croson, 488 U.S. 493–494](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/488/469/).

Neither race has a constitutional entitlement to a preferred racial composition, a representative of the same race, unchanged boundaries, or electoral success. Denying those entitlements does not diminish either plaintiff's protection against an actual personal inequality.

**Boundary:** The deprivation need not be financial, mathematically measurable, or capable of changing an election winner. A classification or separation can itself impose a constitutional injury under the governing doctrine; no additional downstream loss is universally required. Here, an identified deprivation beyond design means a legally sufficient explanation of unequal treatment, not a mandatory second governmental act or a separate numerical injury. The boundaries themselves can accomplish a prohibited wrong. Electoral defeat, demographic imbalance, or disagreement with a representative does not alone establish one, and lack of dilution does not automatically defeat every electoral equal-protection claim.

##### Issue 3: The strongest contrary analogy and the limits of precedent

**Contrary argument:** State-directed racial assignment can be understood as unequal personal treatment in itself, regardless of vote loss. It can also encourage racial stereotypes and imply that a representative serves a racial group. This argument is not circular merely because it locates the injury in the assignment itself. The dispute is whether this governmental act has the constitutional character of prohibited racial separation. Calling the plan territorial or remedial does not settle that question.

**Croson and the proposed distinction:** The contracting rule in *Croson* allocated competitive opportunities using applicants' racial identities. Its scrutiny does not turn on proven financial loss or disappear because the government asserts a benign objective. Its broader concern with personal racial classification presents a substantial objection here. Stone must defend the scope of that principle, not dismiss the case as involving a different subject. [City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469, 493–505 (1989)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/488/469/).

Stone's affirmative reason is that the asserted demographic design organizes a common electorate without, by that fact alone, assigning residents different terms of political membership according to race. Drawing a district to affect which voters can form an effective electoral coalition does not necessarily establish a racial qualification for that coalition, its candidate, or the representative's constituency. Equal electoral membership protects participation on constitutionally equal terms; it does not guarantee association within a preferred collection of neighboring voters. A discriminatory allocation of political opportunity or an official racial separation remains examinable even if implemented entirely through boundaries.

Stone recognizes the opposing inference: purposeful sorting may itself express separation and encourage racial representation. He declines to make that inference a necessary legal consequence of every deliberate demographic design. Electoral evidence about exclusion can make racial information relevant without legally assigning anyone a political belief. The choice is to distinguish the use of that information in composing an electorate from state-imposed unequal civic treatment, while leaving the latter fully subject to its governing law. This is Stone's interpretation, supported only in part by the districting precedents; equal eligibility alone neither proves it nor answers the objections below.

**Wright's unresolved question:** *Wright v. Rockefeller* sustained a finding that the alleged racial purpose had not been established. The challengers did not rest their theory on population inequality, and the dissents treated racial segregation of districts as a constitutional wrong. Stone cannot cite the case as holding proven racial districting lawful or requiring dilution in every case. Its disposition left unresolved the legal consequence of the racial design assumed here. [376 U.S. 52, 53–58, 58–74 (1964)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/376/52/).

**UJO's qualified support:** *United Jewish Organizations* sustained the particular plan through fractured reasoning. White's discussion relied on the absence of stigma, exclusion, and dilution, but also on white voters' countywide representation and sound districting principles. Stewart distinguished racial awareness from prohibited discrimination while preserving scrutiny of segregation and impaired participation; his reasoning also relied on the Voting Rights Act context. Brennan reserved broader questions and doubted that aggregate white representation fully answered the Hasidic individuals' complaint. [430 U.S. 144, 165–180, including Brennan's concurrence at 171 n.1 (1977)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/430/144/).

Stone adopts the limited proposition that deliberate racial consideration and unconstitutional electoral discrimination are not invariably identical. He does not adopt aggregate white representation as a complete defense, presume ordinary districting criteria were respected here, or treat a remedial label as sufficient. His conclusion without a finding of federal compulsion requires his own injury analysis; no controlling comprehensive *UJO* test already supplies it.

**Extent of doctrinal choice:** Before this case is decided, there is no existing *Shaw* holding to overrule. That does not dispose of contrary earlier principles. Stone defends a contested interpretation of their application to districting while retaining their protections against personal racial inequality. He is not installing his broader §9 justification rule in every field or treating it as inherited law. Any departure from a controlling classification rule must be identified and justified under Master §16 in a case requiring it.

##### Issue 4: Geography cannot confer immunity from actual inequality

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Boundary drawing remains subject to the prohibitions governing the deprivation it accomplishes. It cannot lawfully be used to remove a racial population from the franchise or governing institutions, dilute voting opportunity with the constitutionally required purpose, or impose inferior civic membership.

**Independent constitutional and statutory sources:** Each claim retains its own elements. Equal protection, the Fifteenth Amendment, and statutory voting protections do not merge into one injury test. An independently sufficient statutory deprivation requires no additional constitutional violation and no discriminatory purpose that the statute does not demand. The territorial-injury rule supplies no additional gate for those claims and creates no general restriction on standing.

**Gomillion and its broader concurrence:** The majority addressed boundaries that removed nearly all Black municipal voters, no white voters, and the excluded residents' municipal franchise. It proceeded under the Fifteenth Amendment. Whittaker's concurrence separately reasoned that racial fencing from one political division into another could violate equal protection even assuming equivalent voting privileges in the destination. That reasoning directly challenges Stone's proposed distinction. [364 U.S. 339, 341–349 (1960)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/364/339/).

Stone preserves protection against racial expulsion from municipal membership. He declines to extend Whittaker's segregation analysis to every deliberate racial adjustment of congressional constituencies. The concurrence is not the majority holding, but its reasoning deserves an answer: for Stone, demographic composition alone does not make the resulting constituency a racially exclusive institution or place residents of another race outside its political membership. If the boundaries accomplish an identified racial exclusion or other prohibited inequality, they remain actionable. The actual municipal expulsion supports this distinction, but does not eliminate the broader interpretive disagreement.

*White v. Regester* confirms that participation can be impaired even though voters retain ballots. Its local institutional and historical inquiry concerned exclusion from effective political participation, not a guaranteed racial share of offices. [412 U.S. 755, 765–770 (1973)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/412/755/).

**Anderson: an electoral injury without lost eligibility:** *Anderson v. Martin* invalidated compulsory racial labels beside candidates' names on official ballots. Formally equal candidacy and voter choice did not cure the State's use of its election machinery to encourage racial discrimination, even though it labeled both white and Black candidates. No proof of a changed election winner was necessary to that conclusion. [375 U.S. 399, 402–404 (1964)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/375/399/).

Stone preserves that protection. A mandatory racial cue at the act of voting gives race an official role in choosing among candidates. A district's demographic objective, even deliberately pursued, does not necessarily communicate an equivalent official direction to evaluate candidates by race. This distinction concerns how the challenged act operates; it is not a finding that North Carolina's motives were benign. An allegation of comparable official electoral discrimination must receive the inquiry belonging to it. *Anderson* prevents Stone from making formal eligibility or separate downstream harm indispensable to every claim; it does not itself decide the districting question.

**McLaurin: unequal treatment within a common institution:** *McLaurin* invalidated government-imposed racial seating arrangements despite use of the same classroom, library, and cafeteria. The restrictions impaired participation and interaction. Its holding supplies a concrete analogy for personal institutional inequality without requiring financial loss, and refutes any universal inference from shared facilities to equal treatment. It does not itself decide how constituency boundaries should be treated. [339 U.S. 637, 640–642 (1950)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/339/637/).

**Inferior civic status and its limits:** An operative state rule designating one race as a district's rightful political membership, or an official practice denying residents because of race access to an otherwise available constituent service or participation process, would present a different claim. These are applications Stone would examine under the governing equal-protection and other applicable rules, not holdings that the cited cases already decided those precise hypotheticals. The court identifies the governmental treatment, the claimant's connection to it, and the constitutional or statutory elements. Civic status describes the protected concern; it creates no freestanding cause of action or new scrutiny tier.

An official statement can itself implement a discriminatory designation or provide evidence of one; its legal role depends on content, authority, and context. No separate written restriction, repeated statement, lost vote, or economic effect is invariably necessary. Stone nevertheless declines to infer an imposed racial membership rule solely from the demographic objective, unusual shape, or speculation about a representative's future conduct. This is a substantive limit on the theory, not a heightened pleading demand.

An access claim must also identify the protected opportunity. *Minnesota Board v. Knight* rejects a general constitutional entitlement to force policymakers to listen or respond. It does not immunize an independently actionable racial exclusion, but disagreement, an unanswered request, or lack of a personal audience does not alone establish inferior civic status. [465 U.S. 271, 283–287 (1984)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/465/271/).

**Standing remains separate from the merits:** *Allen v. Wright* distinguishes personally experienced unequal treatment from generalized stigmatic objection; it does not decide whether racial district assignment constitutes such treatment. *Heckler v. Mathews* recognized a personal equality interest sufficient for standing despite the inability to obtain increased benefits, then upheld the challenged classification on the merits. These decisions explain why noneconomic injury can matter without deciding this districting claim or establishing a constitutional violation whenever standing exists. [Allen, 468 U.S. 737, 755–757 (1984)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/468/737/); [Heckler, 465 U.S. 728, 737–741, 744–751 (1984)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/465/728/).

##### Issue 5: Racial knowledge and independent statutory protection

**Stone's Legal Position:** Legislatures may examine racial electoral conditions, discharge valid statutory duties, and pursue supported inclusion within their lawful responsibilities. Keep three propositions separate: racial evidence may be relevant; a statute may require specified corrective action when its conditions hold; this particular plan satisfies the applicable requirements. The first does not prove the third.

*Gingles* calls for examination of demonstrated electoral behavior, including cohesion and majority bloc voting, within amended §2's results inquiry. Those questions do not require officials to assume that every member of a race shares a preference. Its statutory elements are not universal pleading requirements for every voting claim. Constitutional purpose requirements remain separate. [Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30, 43–51 (1986)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/478/30/).

**Coalition and crossover questions:** Where the applicable statutory rule recognizes a combined-group claim, the court assesses the protected groups' combined opportunity on evidence of actual cohesion and common bloc opposition, together with every other applicable requirement. Claims involving crossover support likewise receive the inquiry belonging to the governing statutory protection. Racial identity alone establishes no individual's political preference. Cohesion alone neither proves liability nor displaces applicable size, geographic, or other requirements.

This paragraph preserves source-specific inquiry; it does not decide that §2 requires coalition, crossover, or influence districts. *Gingles* left related submajority questions unresolved. No new population threshold, automatic aggregation rule, seat entitlement, or statutory duty is adopted in this appeal. Any actually effective earlier simulated ruling in *Growe* or *Voinovich* must be incorporated on its own terms; a prepared Stone position or historical comparator supplies no substitute. [Gingles, 478 U.S. 46–47 n.12](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/478/30/).

Section 5's nonretrogression principle is not a command to maximize majority-minority districts. Preclearance does not certify compliance with all other law. Stone makes no finding that the federal officials demanded the precise challenged lines. [Beer v. United States, 425 U.S. 130, 140–142 (1976)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/425/130/).

##### Issue 6: Supported congressional prevention and lawful implementation

**Stone's Legal Position:** In the voting setting, Congress may establish valid remedial or preventive categories under the applicable Reconstruction Amendment enforcement power. Appropriate legislation may protect beyond the constitutional minimum applicable to an individual act; a completed constitutional violation by every covered jurisdiction is not a universal prerequisite. Coverage and compliance then follow the enacted conditions. [South Carolina v. Katzenbach, 383 U.S. 301, 324–331 (1966)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/383/301/); [Katzenbach v. Morgan, 384 U.S. 641, 650–656 (1966)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/384/641/).

**Support and fit:** Demonstrated patterns of constitutional violations, risks of recurrence, and methods of evasion can support preventive categories fitted to those conditions. These describe a supported route to legislative action, not a new exclusive evidentiary test. Apply the governing enforcement-power doctrine, including its treatment of legislative judgment and inference; require no universal formal findings, specified study, or separate local adjudication. *City of Rome* sustained the prohibition of certain discriminatory effects even assuming the underlying constitutional prohibition required discriminatory purpose. [446 U.S. 156, 173–178 (1980)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/446/156/).

**Implementation:** An institution administering a valid statute may rely on the legislative determinations supporting its category. It must still establish that its challenged action falls within statutory authority and satisfies the coverage and application-specific obligations the law imposes. It need not reconstruct Congress's investigation in every application. A jurisdiction acting independently cannot claim Congress's distinct enforcement power merely by describing its policy as preventive. The validity of legislation, its application, and an independently adopted policy remain different questions. [Croson, 488 U.S. 488–491](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/488/469/).

**Continued protection:** When continued authority is properly at issue, assess the relevant conditions, recurrence, evasion, and material misfit through the governing statute and constitutional doctrine. Reduced discrimination may reflect successful prevention; success proves neither automatic obsolescence nor permanent necessity. Coverage, statutory bailout, and constitutional challenges retain their own procedures and burdens. This explanation creates no automatic sunset, recurring judicial reauthorization requirement, or permission to disregard an otherwise applicable statute. [City of Rome, 446 U.S. 180–182](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/446/156/).

**Reach in Shaw:** These authorities explain why statutory protection and lawful prevention need not await an adjudicated constitutional wrong in every application. They do not establish that federal law required this map, that North Carolina met every application-specific condition, or that the validity of every preventive category is presented here.

##### Issue 7: Permission, duty, and the limits of this judgment

**Connection to Stone's §9:** Impartial government may address supported exclusion within lawful authority. Stone's stated corrective permission depends on objective support, an appropriate relationship between the condition and the measure, and permissible operation and burdens. Under his preferred framework, satisfaction of the defined permission resolves that equal-treatment justification without a second balancing inquiry or an additional scrutiny tier. A claimant may contest the permission's conditions, its actual implementation, or an independent deprivation. These are principles of his jurisprudence, not findings about North Carolina.

**Precedent and reserved doctrinal change:** Those principles describe Stone's broader position on justification. Because this claim fails at the asserted-injury stage, this judgment does not replace the scrutiny required by precedent for a different racial classification. Governing 1993 law includes *Croson*'s strict scrutiny of the state contracting classification and *Metro Broadcasting*'s different treatment of the congressional measures presented there. A departure from a controlling rule must be expressly identified and justified under Master §16 in a case presenting it; this dismissal does not silently accomplish that change. [Croson, 488 U.S. 493–494](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/488/469/); [Metro Broadcasting, 497 U.S. 547, 563–566 (1990)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/497/547/).

Where an actual claim calls for justification, the government must make the showing the applicable rule requires. Here Stone rejects design alone as the asserted deprivation without first requiring the State to prove federal compulsion. Making absence of injury depend on establishing that defense would collapse two distinct questions. Constitutional permission can extend beyond statutory compulsion; neither permission nor compulsion is inferred for every racial map.

**Independent limits:** Neither public justification, corrective permission, nor valid statutory implementation authorizes an independently prohibited constitutional deprivation, overrides a statutory restriction, or supplies authority for an otherwise unauthorized act. Permission to adopt a lawful measure does not itself impose a statutory duty or authorize a court to order it. The present judgment decides none of those additional questions by treating them as consequences of dismissal.

##### Issue 8: A usable rule under 1993 pleading law

**Practical sequence:**

1. Identify the governmental act, the asserted right, and the deprivation alleged. Read the substance, not just the plaintiff's chosen label.
2. Accept factual allegations and reasonable favorable inferences under ordinary pleading law. Require no expert report, completed voting analysis, replacement map, or proof of a changed election winner before discovery. *Conley* supplies the applicable notice-pleading approach. [355 U.S. 41, 45–48 (1957)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/355/41/).
3. Apply the elements of the actual claim. Exclusion, intentional constitutional dilution, statutory results liability, and personal civic inequality retain their distinct inquiries. Where the governing doctrine recognizes official classification or separation itself as an injury, require no additional downstream loss. Do not add racial predominance to every claim.
4. Address justification where that claim permits it, with its proper burdens. A remedial label alone answers nothing.
5. Tie relief to the established violation and the court's authority. A discriminatory access practice may call for correction of that practice; redrawing boundaries requires a connection between the map and the established wrong.

**Application:** On the theory presented here, even fully accepting the alleged racial design does not supply the additional deprivation Stone's rule recognizes. Dismissal is substantive, not punishment for failing to plead detailed evidence or use special words. If an actual complaint alleges another impairment, the court cannot rename it racial-design-only and dismiss it under this rule.

**Amendment:** Where leave is sought to allege a legally sufficient deprivation, Rule 15 and *Foman* govern the request, including futility, prejudice, and other recognized grounds. Stone does not direct an amendment or invent supporting facts in this appeal. [Foman v. Davis, 371 U.S. 178, 182 (1962)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/371/178/).

##### Issue 9: Consequences and the available disposition

**Consequence of the selected rule:** Some deliberately racial maps may survive this particular claim even when their objectives or boundaries attract objection. Stone's position does not promise that another doctrine will invalidate them. Shape, demographics, legislative statements, and departures from ordinary criteria remain evidence where relevant, rather than independent aesthetic or predominance tests.

**Application to this appeal:** The user's selected premise supports affirming dismissal of this equal-protection theory. Striking the map solely for its racial design would contradict that premise. An independently cognizable deprivation, properly presented and established, could support tailored relief under its own law. Alternatively, recognizing assignment itself as an injury would change Stone's selected rule; even that approach would ordinarily permit litigation after reversal of dismissal, not establish this map's final invalidity on the allegations alone.

**Stone's concluding statement:** I would affirm dismissal of the equal-protection claim. Accepting the alleged racial purpose and departures from ordinary districting considerations, the complaint identifies no unequal electoral participation or other constitutional deprivation beyond the racial construction of territorial constituencies itself. That construction does not alone deprive a resident of equal political membership. This conclusion preserves claims of racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, and legally inferior civic status under the doctrines governing those injuries. Independent statutory claims likewise retain their enacted elements without an additional constitutional-injury requirement. The judgment neither depends on federal compulsion nor adjudicates the map's compliance with every other legal requirement.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** AFFIRM the District of Columbia Court of Appeals: bar the later cocaine prosecution against Dixon and all five counts of Foster’s later indictment.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the dismissal of Dixon’s substantive indictment in place and require dismissal of Foster I, II, III, IV, and V. The original contempt dispositions remain undisturbed. No further merits trial on these barred charges is permitted; no separate Ashe determination is necessary because the retained successive-prosecution rule fully disposes of the threat counts.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Reach and the nature of contempt

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the reviewable double-jeopardy question before renewed trial. Both proceedings imposed criminal punishment after adjudication; neither is a continuing civil sanction avoidable by compliance.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply criminal-contempt and double-jeopardy principles; reject a categorical exemption based on the identity of the initiating lawyer or the order’s protective purpose.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nonsummary criminal-contempt prosecution places the accused in jeopardy. Its punitive character is not erased because private counsel initiated it or because the order protected a person from violence.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Foster faced adjudication under the court’s criminal authority, with convictions, acquittals, and imprisonment. The prosecutor’s institutional identity does not transform that completed criminal trial into a private civil dispute. Protective orders remain enforceable; the constitutional question concerns the additional criminal trial after the first has ended.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Civil coercive proceedings, summary courtroom contempt, and different sovereigns are not resolved by this holding.

##### Issue 2: Retain Grady and apply it count by count

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the Government’s challenge to the governing successive-prosecution rule because its retention controls the remaining Foster counts.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Grady, Brown, and Felix rather than overrule the inherited rule. No replacement test or burden is proposed.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Under the retained Grady rule, a subsequent prosecution is barred when establishing an essential element would require proving conduct constituting an offense for which the defendant has already been prosecuted. On this record, that rule bars Dixon’s drug count and Foster’s entire five-count indictment.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Dixon: the contempt adjudication already proved possession with intent to distribute, not merely a failure to report. Foster I: the same November assault is the criminal act already punished. Foster II, III, and IV: the prosecution would retry the same three alleged threats for which the first criminal trial ended in acquittal. Foster V: adding intent to kill does not avoid Grady when the already-prosecuted May assault supplies the criminal act required by the new charge. Felix’s conspiracy holding concerns an independently punishable agreement and is not a warrant for serial trials of these acts. Stone takes the Government’s manageability objection seriously but finds the conduct-versus-evidence distinction administrable here; an inconvenient consequence for prosecutorial coordination does not demonstrate that the precedent is unworkable. Finality after completed trials weighs against withdrawing this protection solely to enlarge the second prosecution.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** This is not compulsory joinder of all charges from an episode. Blockburger remains operative, mere evidentiary overlap remains insufficient, conspiracy remains governed by Felix, and Ashe remains an independent rule where necessary.

Earlier same-day law counts only where its effective sequence is established. This is an authoring baseline, not certification against an unsupplied live decision record.  
**Conditions:** Revalidate material changes in effective simulated law, record, or review scope. This authoring revision enters no simulated judgment.  

**Authoring Provenance:** Master Jurisprudence informs the implementation below, not governing law or a separate Engine input. Evidence follows its legal question and procedural setting; relevant considerations do not become a mandatory checklist merely by being listed.

#### B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

**Stone's Proposed Judgment or Disposition:** VACATE the Ninth Circuit's judgment and REMAND for application of the event-date Federal Rules of Evidence rather than a mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite.

**Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The lower courts must assess the scientific foundation and fit of the material expert proffers under the proper rule and reconsider summary judgment on the admissible record. The district court may receive the submissions needed to conduct that inquiry fairly. Neither admission of all plaintiffs' experts nor a finding that Bendectin caused either child's injuries is directed. Renewed summary judgment remains possible if the properly evaluated record cannot support the necessary substantive proof.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: The Rules displace a consensus prerequisite

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the source and content of the federal scientific-evidence admissibility rule in this diversity action. Leave disputed scientific findings and state-law causation to their proper adjudicative stages.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Interpret the original Rules and recognize displacement of an incompatible lower-court common-law prerequisite. Do not describe Frye as a Supreme Court interpretation of Rule 702 that must be overruled.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** General acceptance is not an indispensable condition of admissibility under Rule 702. Under Rules 104(a) and 702, the proponent must establish by a preponderance of the evidence the preliminary foundation for reliable scientific knowledge that is relevant to, and will assist the factfinder on, the question actually in dispute. Scientific novelty neither excludes evidence automatically nor proves it reliable.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Rule 702's enacted language requires knowledge and assistance, not consensus as an independent veto. The trial judge must examine whether the reasoning and method have a defensible scientific basis and whether they can support the offered inference in this case. The preliminary foundation is assessed under the inherited Rule 104(a) framework and Bourjaily v. United States, 483 U.S. 171, 175–176 (1987); this is not a finding of ultimate causation or a shift in the substantive trial burden.

**Doctrinal Components:** The displaced component is Frye's mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite. The replacement is the Rules' reliability-and-relevance inquiry. Acceptance remains a potentially useful consideration. Expert qualification, the proponent's preliminary burden, Rule 403, substantive causation requirements, and summary-judgment sufficiency remain distinct and intact. Reliance on the old screening rule supports fair reconsideration, not automatic admission or an award of damages. No later wording of Rule 702 is used as 1993 enacted law.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** The case does not establish the treatment of every kind of technical or other specialized expertise, nor a comprehensive standard of appellate review for every evidentiary ruling.

##### Issue 2: Flexible scrutiny of method, data, and fit

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** State the guidance needed for remand, not a resolution of the scientific merits.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Use nonexclusive considerations within the Rules' required foundation inquiry. Do not invent a universal four-element test or a requirement that every method produce the same kind of error estimate.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Validation and testing, peer scrutiny, known limitations and error information where meaningful, standards governing a technique, data quality, and acceptance may illuminate whether a proffer is scientifically reliable. Their relevance and weight depend on the proffer. The method must be suited to the legal and factual question; no consideration mechanically establishes or defeats admissibility in every case.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An unpublished reanalysis may be reliable, but must explain its data, choices, and inference. An animal study requires a defensible connection to the human-causation proposition for which it is offered. Publication or impressive credentials cannot cure an unsupported inference. Equally, disagreement with the scientific majority does not itself establish that a method is unsound. Cross-examination and contrary evidence test admissible proof; they do not excuse the judge from assessing its foundation first.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Data quality is relevant to a method's foundation, not a newly mandatory checklist item for all expert testimony. Litigation origin is not a categorical bar. Admissibility does not establish the truth of an expert's conclusion.

##### Issue 3: Boundary concerning empirical civil-rights adjudication

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reserve issues outside this scientific-expert-evidence appeal. This reservation prevents the proposed holding from deciding unpresented voting or constitutional cases.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Keep expert admissibility, adjudicative proof, legislative facts, and substantive civil-rights standards separate.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The reliability-and-relevance inquiry does not make scientific consensus a condition of enforcing civil rights, prescribe one statistical technique for all voting cases, or itself decide how a court evaluates legislative facts. When a court is presented with scientific expert testimony, the applicable evidence rule screens that testimony; the constitutional or statutory claim independently determines what facts matter and what must be proved.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Methodological validation, error information, data selection, and fit can improve empirical adjudication without supplying an equal-protection violation, political cohesion, racial predominance, or the proper scrutiny tier. None of those substantive questions is presented by the Bendectin record. The inquiry remains evenhanded toward the parties rather than becoming a special evidentiary preference for or against civil-rights claims.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No ruling on legislative-fact review, a universal significance level, a required regression method, or the admissibility of a particular voting analysis. No earlier calendared case receives this proposed rule retroactively by authoring fiat. Its use in another matter requires an actually effective simulated decision and that matter's own procedural and evidentiary requirements.

#### C. ISSUE POSITIONS

##### Issue 1: Activity-specific exemption

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory ground supporting the domestic dismissals; do not infer the foreign defendants’ regulatory status from an assumption made to test the domestic theory.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Royal Drug’s activity-centered distinction and the enacted exemption, rejecting an overextended analogy to entity-based immunities.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** An otherwise qualifying insurance activity does not lose its § 2(b) protection merely because a foreign reinsurer participates in the alleged agreement. Whether the particular activity is the business of insurance and is regulated by state law remains essential.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress protected a defined activity, not a class of persons that forfeits status by association. The participation of an insurance reinsurer differs from the noninsurance pharmacy agreements addressed in Royal Drug. **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No blanket foreign-reinsurer exemption and no extension of Fabe’s different statutory clause.

##### Issue 2: Boycott and the different complaint counts

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply event-date pleading law, accepting the relevant allegations without finding that the defendants actually acted together.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Clarify the statutory boycott boundary through Barry and the ordinary function of coercive refusal, not a new antitrust exemption.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** Refusal of collateral or unrelated business to compel acceptance of terms in the targeted transaction can constitute a § 3(b) boycott. Mere joint insistence on terms for that same transaction does not, without more, turn every exempt concerted insurance practice into a boycott.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged threat to cut off broader reinsurance, including otherwise acceptable business, permits proof of collateral pressure in California I through IV and Connecticut I and II. Their sufficiency is not a finding that every piece of reinsurance is unrelated to the insurer’s overall risk; defendants may contest that operational premise. California VII and Connecticut VI, by contrast, allege drafting of model umbrella and excess terms without the material refusal-to-deal mechanism. With the categorical foreign-association theory rejected, those counts lack the reviewed basis for defeating the domestic exemption.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** Boycott is an exception to an otherwise established exemption, not the definition of every restraint. No categorical holding about all coercion or intimidation is necessary. Alleged conduct, agreement, and actual antitrust injury remain to be proved.

##### Issue 3: American market effects and the asserted foreign conflict

**Threshold and Merits Reach:** The nonfrivolous federal claim supplies judicial power; separately examine substantive coverage and the requested comity restriction.

**Stone's Legal Position:** Apply established foreign-effects authority and reject the asserted bar on the particular allegations. Reserve a universal theory of comity rather than making absence of impossibility the answer to every future case.

**Stone's Proposed Holding:** The pleaded London conduct, deliberately directed at substantial changes in American insurance coverage, may be adjudicated under the Sherman Act. British permission for that conduct, without a conflicting legal duty or another established source requiring nonapplication here, does not displace the American rule.

**Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The foreign market and the United Kingdom’s regulatory interest are genuine. They do not alone explain why conduct aimed at American buyers must be insulated from an otherwise applicable federal statute. Congress’s § 6a boundaries remain controlling where applicable, including both the required direct, substantial, reasonably foreseeable domestic effect and the requirement that the qualifying effect give rise to the asserted claim. The alleged changes in American coverage supply the connection asserted here. Stone does not resolve the provision’s reach beyond what is necessary on these allegations or omit its claim-connection requirement. He does not derive power from importance or disregard a true conflicting command. The pleaded American operation and the statutory source, not a free-floating national preference, justify continued adjudication.

**Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general holding that courts can never account for comity absent impossibility, no authority over wholly foreign injury lacking the required connection, and no determination that the plaintiffs have proved a Sherman Act violation.
