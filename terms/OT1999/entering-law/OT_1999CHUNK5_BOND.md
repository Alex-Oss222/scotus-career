#### Minnesota v. Dickerson, 508 U.S. 366 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2019.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Minnesota v. Dickerson, June 7, 1993, No. 91-2019, see Federal Courts — Collateral consequences of diversion.

##### Authorized touch can supply probable cause without an additional search

**Holding:** An officer lawfully conducting a protective patdown may seize contraband when the authorized touching itself supplies probable cause of its incriminating character and the officer has lawful access to it. The officer may not manipulate or otherwise explore a nonthreatening object beyond the frisk to obtain that probable cause; immediate recognition requires probable cause, not certainty.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Part II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Terry v. Ohio, 392 U.S. 1: the protective purpose limits the scope of the frisk.
- Michigan v. Long, 463 U.S. 1032: seizure of contraband found within an authorized protective search applied.
- Horton v. California, 496 U.S. 128: lawful access and immediately apparent incriminating character extended to lawful touch.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: probable cause may not be manufactured through a separate unauthorized search.
- Soldal v. Cook County, December 8, 1992: independent possessory protection preserved; no enlargement of a frisk follows.

##### Manipulation after excluding a weapon requires suppression here

**Holding:** When the accepted findings establish that the officer identified contraband only by squeezing and manipulating an object after determining that it was not a weapon, the examination exceeds Terry and cannot justify the seizure. That independent ground sustains suppression here despite the state court’s erroneous categorical rejection of tactile recognition.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Parts III and IV; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Probable cause must arise from authorized contact, with lawful access; exploratory touching is not a new exception. The diversion record’s identified legal uses, rather than an invented conviction, support review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm suppression on the accepted finding of manipulation after the weapon concern ended; no further evidentiary opportunity for the State is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Terry, 392 U.S. 1: protective authority ended when the object was known not to be a weapon.
- Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: information obtained by the additional exploration cannot validate it retrospectively.

#### Minnesota v. Dickerson, 508 U.S. 366 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2019.

##### The legally consequential diversion record keeps the case live

**Holding:** Completion and dismissal under Minnesota’s §152.18 diversion procedure do not moot this review where reversal could restore a retained record legally usable in later proceedings. The conclusion rests on those identified statutory consequences, not on an invented conviction or a universal presumption about every dismissed charge.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Part I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The statutory diversion record’s identified state-law and federal criminal-history uses supply the continuing consequences; no conviction or universal presumption about dismissed prosecutions is assumed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reject mootness and reach the suppression dispute; the separate Fourth Amendment disposition is recorded under Criminal Procedure.

### Contraband recognized through a lawful protective patdown

**Governing question:** What instructions govern contraband recognized through a lawful protective patdown?

**Current rule:** During a lawful protective patdown, authorized touching that itself supplies probable cause of incriminating character permits seizure of contraband to which the officer has lawful access. Immediate recognition requires probable cause, not certainty. Manipulating or exploring a nonthreatening object beyond the protective frisk to obtain that cause is an additional unauthorized search; identification resulting only from such manipulation cannot justify the seizure. No exploratory-touch exception follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Authorized touch can supply probable cause without an additional search: Minnesota v. Dickerson — Authorized touch can supply probable cause without an additional search, 1993-06-07. White’s Part II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Manipulation after excluding a weapon requires suppression here: Minnesota v. Dickerson — Manipulation after excluding a weapon requires suppression here, 1993-06-07. White’s Parts III and IV; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-06-07; Minnesota v. Dickerson, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Minnesota v. Dickerson, 508 U.S. 366 (1993); Minnesota v. Dickerson, 508 U.S. 366 (1993).

### Privacy in a rented vehicle and personal luggage

**Governing question:** Fourth Amendment interests of an unlisted driver and in separately protected closed luggage.

**Current rule:** Absence from the rental agreement does not alone defeat privacy where the driver possesses and controls the vehicle with the authorized renter’s permission and without theft, fraud or comparable wrongful possession. The actual legitimate relationship, right to exclude, owner’s retained rights and effective termination of possession must be assessed; permission alone does not establish privacy. Lack of vehicle privacy does not extinguish an independently established legitimate interest in the driver’s closed luggage, supported by ownership or lawful control and measures preserving privacy.

**Authority by component:**

- Vehicle privacy and the rental agreement: Vehicle privacy and the rental agreement, 1995-05-30. Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined in this proposition by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine directly adopt it.
- Independent privacy in closed personal luggage: Independent privacy in closed personal luggage, 1995-05-30. Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined on this separate proposition by all eight Associate Justices; nine directly adopt it.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-05-30; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** A recognized interest does not defeat probable-cause search within its container scope, lawful consent, standardized noninvestigatory inventory after lawful custody or another established warrant exception. Effective repossession and wrongful possession remain material. Suppression follows only after the personal interests and preserved justifications are resolved, not automatically from rejection of the rental-list rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Vehicle privacy and the rental agreement; Independent privacy in closed personal luggage.

### Traffic-stop completion, search consent and prior illegality

**Governing question:** Whether a traffic encounter continues as a seizure and whether ensuing consent permits a search.

**Current rule:** An officer’s private decision to give a warning does not invalidate an exit order during objectively lawful traffic business. When that justification ends, continued detention needs independent legal grounds. Determine objectively whether the person could decline further requests or end the encounter from the complete circumstances; return of a license alone neither ends a seizure nor authorizes its continuation. The Fourth Amendment requires no universal free-to-go warning before requesting consent. Consent must nevertheless be voluntary under all the circumstances. If unlawful detention preceded or accompanied consent, assess its causal independence from that illegality separately; voluntariness alone is not attenuation, and prior illegality does not make all later consent incurably tainted.

**Authority by component:**

- Lawful traffic business and objective continuation: Ohio v. Robinette — Ohio v. Robinette — II. Objective legality during and after a traffic stop, November 18, 1996; O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer on Part II.A, with Stone-Zsela additionally joining II.B.
- No indispensable warning and separate voluntariness/attenuation inquiries: Robinette — Ohio v. Robinette — III. No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct; O’Connor, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Robinette, November 18, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** A missing release signal remains relevant evidence. Neither the officer’s undisclosed willingness to permit refusal nor the individual’s subjective impression alone resolves seizure. Independent state law may require greater protection. No universal appellate-review rule for consent questions follows.

**Related current rules:** Objective probable cause for an ordinary initial traffic stop governs the stop’s initial justification; the present rule governs completion and consent.

**Holdings navigation:** Ohio v. Robinette (1996-11-18) — Objective legality during and after a traffic stop; Ohio v. Robinette (1996-11-18) — No indispensable federal release warning; voluntary consent and attenuation remain distinct.

## Effective current-term public law and positions

- **Flippo v. West Virginia — October 18, 1999; unanimous per curiam.** The Court rejects a categorical homicide-scene exception. Actual emergency aid, victim/assailant searches, protective action and evidence preservation retain their own predicates and scope; an ended emergency does not authorize the subsequent prolonged evidence search. Visibility and an unlocked condition do not expose a closed briefcase's contents. Mincey and Thompson supply the categorical rule, Hicks the observation/search distinction, and Dickerson the authorized-intrusion boundary. The judgment is reversed and remanded for preserved and legally supported independent exceptions and harmlessness, without finding those alternatives false or ordering unconditional suppression, acquittal or a new trial. [Record](../records/flippo-v-west-virginia_merits_1999-10-18.md)

- **Illinois v. Wardlow — January 12, 2000; Stone-Zsela opinion.** All nine join the governing Terry totality rule: flight and a demonstrated narcotics setting may be considered, location alone is insufficient, and no universal rule follows from any flight plus a high-crime label. Ornelas supplies independent ultimate review with historical-fact respect and grounded local inferences; the discovered gun supplies no retrospective justification. Five—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—find this actual pre-stop combination insufficiently particularized and affirm suppression. Recognition is a permissible inference, not an admission; the testimony supplies no marked vehicles, actual crowd, observed transaction or pre-stop contraband. The holding creates no third-factor requirement, flight exclusion or innocent-motive finding. Frisk and bag-search validity remain outside review. Laidlaw's same-day processing supplies no entering authority here. [Record](../records/illinois-v-wardlow_merits_2000-01-12.md)
