## Schlup v. Delo
Source: [canonical federal-courts volume](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md).

#### Schlup v. Delo

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-7901.
**Decided:** January 23, 1995.

##### Crime innocence invokes a demanding probability gateway

**Holding:** A prisoner seeking to overcome otherwise applicable successive or abusive habeas barriers to independent constitutional claims through innocence of the offense must present reliable new evidence and show, considering the probative record as a whole, that it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would find guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. This procedural gateway does not itself establish innocence, prove any constitutional violation or shift the ultimate burden on the underlying claims; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing rule remains confined to its distinct death-eligibility setting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's gateway and all-evidence portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Murray v. Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: applies and specifies its probability-based crime-innocence exception without changing cause and prejudice.
- Sawyer v. Whitley, 505 U.S. 333: preserves the clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway and resolves the crime-innocence question that decision left open; it is not overruled or given a categorical impeachment exclusion.
- McCleskey v. Zant, 499 U.S. 467: preserves successive/abusive-claim barriers and the exceptional innocence route, without treating potential constitutional merit as sufficient to pass it.
- Kuhlmann v. Wilson, 477 U.S. 436: uses the relevant plurality discussion of the whole probative record as persuasive guidance, preserving its actual authority limit.
- Jackson v. Virginia, 443 U.S. 307: distinguishes trial-record legal sufficiency from the likely effect of an augmented record on reasonable jurors; its sufficiency rule remains intact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court considers all relevant probative evidence, including evidence excluded at trial or becoming available afterward; trial-admissibility rules do not categorically delimit the gateway record, and even improperly admitted trial evidence may have probative force subject to reliability. The court must assess reliability and likely credibility, not accept every affidavit as true or treat the original verdict as a conclusive answer. Timing, delay and inconsistency may matter; no categorical rule excludes impeachment evidence or discredits a witness solely because the witness is a prisoner. A mere possibility of acquittal, a juror who might disagree, ordinary constitutional-merits prejudice or the reviewing judge's personal reasonable doubt is insufficient. The reasonable juror fairly considers the evidence and follows the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt instruction.

##### This legal error requires a bounded remand despite the statutory refusal permission

**Holding:** The courts' use of the rejected clear-and-convincing crime gateway and reliance on the original jury's credibility function require vacatur and renewed assessment on this record; §2244(b)'s permission to refuse a qualifying subsequent petition does not itself supply an adequate independent ground to sustain that legally mistaken refusal. The Court remands through the Eighth Circuit to the District Court for application of the probability rule and a reasoned determination of necessary factual development, without deciding whether every successful innocence showing compels review, choosing a general appellate standard, or ordering a hearing, writ, retrial, release or stay automatically.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's statutory-refusal and remand portion, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela supplies a sixth judgment vote without joining the separate §2244(b) rationale.

**Current-force modification:** AEDPA §106 replaces former §2244(b)'s refusal permission for successive §2254 district-court applications. A claim already presented must be dismissed. A new claim must be dismissed unless it rests on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence and facts that, if proved and considered with the whole evidence, establish by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt. Advance authorization by a three-judge court-of-appeals panel requires a prima facie showing; authorization does not remove the district court's duty to dismiss a claim unless the applicant proves the statutory requirements. This is an independent statutory condition, not a replacement of Schlup's probability gateway or a ruling that the gateway itself satisfies the new text. The original former-law remand stands. Pending-case application is not generally resolved; Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, applies the post-enactment authorization-review bar while reserving whether §§2244(b)(1)–(2) directly bind original habeas consideration in this Court.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McCleskey, 499 U.S. 467: applies the distinction between conditional consideration of new, nonabusive grounds and legally constrained refusal of successive or abusive grounds; neither permission nor exception becomes an unconditional command.
- Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes, 504 U.S. 1, and Townsend v. Sain, 372 U.S. 293: preserve necessary factual development within their properly presented, available-claim setting and the deliberate-bypass qualification; ordinary counsel negligence alone does not forfeit a required hearing there, but no successive/abusive gateway is eliminated.
- Carrier, 477 U.S. 478: requires use of the selected crime-innocence rule before the present refusal can be sustained on its innocence assessment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2244(b) provides:

> (b) When after an evidentiary hearing on the merits of a material factual issue, or after a hearing on the merits of an issue of law, a person in custody pursuant to the judgment of a State court has been denied by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States release from custody or other remedy on an application for a writ of habeas corpus, a subsequent application for a writ of habeas corpus in behalf of such person need not be entertained by a court of the United States or a justice or judge of the United States unless the application alleges and is predicated on a factual or other ground not adjudicated on the hearing of the earlier application for the writ, and unless the court, justice, or judge is satisfied that the applicant has not on the earlier application deliberately withheld the newly asserted ground or otherwise abused the writ.

The prior-hearing alternatives are a factual evidentiary merits hearing or a legal merits hearing, each followed by a federal denial of release or other habeas remedy to a person held under a state judgment. Both unless conditions must be preserved: the later application must allege and rest on an unadjudicated factual or other ground, and the court must be satisfied that the newly asserted ground was not deliberately withheld or otherwise an abuse. New evidence is not automatically a new ground; absence of deliberate withholding does not eliminate every other kind of abuse. McCleskey requires consideration when both conditions are met and other barriers, including nonexhaustion and procedural default, are absent. The State must plead abuse with clarity and particularity by identifying prior writ history and newly raised claims and alleging abuse before the prisoner's burden arises. Cause and prejudice and the exceptional innocence route remain separate; cause and prejudice is not pressed in this review. Subsection (a)'s separate wording is not inserted into subsection (b), and no new subsection (c) predicate is established.

The videotape was trial evidence. Green's asserted prompt call, Faherty's movement account and other later identification accounts must be assessed with the existing record; the Court finds no witness truthful or false and does not find the gateway satisfied. Proper claim classification, exhaustion, default, abuse, retroactivity and a genuinely sufficient independent merits ground retain their separate operation. Remand is not mandatory constitutional-merits review. Any necessary live testimony is for a reasoned determination under the applicable rules, not an automatic consequence of affidavits or vacatur. The merits of the suppression and ineffective-assistance claims, a freestanding innocence entitlement and any ultimate guilt determination remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit judgment is vacated and remanded through that court to the District Court for the probability gateway and a reasoned decision on necessary factual development. No gateway satisfaction, automatic hearing, constitutional-merits review, writ, retrial, release or stay is ordered.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §106, replacement §2244(b)(1)–(4) district-court successive-application mechanism; see also Felker v. Turpin, June 28, 1996, No. 95-8836, distinct original jurisdiction and reserved direct statutory reach. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

## Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)
Source: [canonical federal-courts volume](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md).

#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied for the evidentiary distinction between pleading and summary judgment; its separate APA programmatic-review holding is not enlarged.
- Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, 477 U.S. 317 (1986), and Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986): applied to require evidence of challenged essential elements while preserving genuine material disputes and reasonable inferences.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): applied; aesthetic injury is cognizable, but an organization's special interest cannot replace injury to a member.
- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.
- Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95 (1983): applied for the distinction between past exposure and a present or imminent injury supporting prospective relief.
- Whitmore v. Arkansas, 495 U.S. 149 (1990): applied for actual or imminent rather than conjectural injury.
- Lujan v. National Wildlife Federation, 497 U.S. 871 (1990): applied to specific affected use; no categorical bar to geographically transmitted injury is established.
- Japan Whaling Association v. American Cetacean Society, 478 U.S. 221 (1986): distinguished because its whale-watching and studying members alleged an adverse effect on their own activities; concrete professional or ecological injury remains possible.

##### Whether ESA citizen-suit permission and an asserted procedural right replace threatened concrete injury

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.
- Massachusetts v. Mellon, 262 U.S. 447 (1923), and Ex parte Levitt, 302 U.S. 633 (1937): applied for the distinction between direct injury and an undifferentiated public grievance.
- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.
- Valley Forge Christian College v. Americans United for Separation of Church and State, Inc., 454 U.S. 464 (1982), and Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied; a claimed right to lawful government conduct alone does not satisfy Article III.
- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975), and Sierra Club v. Morton, 405 U.S. 727 (1972): preserved for Congress's power to recognize injuries and broaden protected interests while retaining the plaintiff's own concrete injury.
- Trafficante v. Metropolitan Life Insurance Co., 409 U.S. 205 (1972), and Hardin v. Kentucky Utilities Co., 390 U.S. 1 (1968): distinguished as statutory recognition of concrete personal interests, not authorization of a wholly abstract grievance.

## Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña
Source: [canonical federal-courts volume](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md).

#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Civil Rights — Governmental racial classifications (The common substantial-justification standard; Vacatur and bounded remand under the common standard).

##### Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing

**Holding:** A bidder able and ready to compete suffers a personal injury when a governmental racial preference denies equal competitive opportunity; it need not prove that it would have received the contract. Prospective relief also requires a real, sufficiently imminent renewed exposure to that preference, which Adarand's repeated bidding and continuing opportunity to encounter the challenged incentive establish here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Standing portion of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all nine adopt the holding and its reasoning.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Standing proves no entitlement to the contract, damages or waiver of sovereign immunity and does not establish unconstitutional discrimination.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Northeastern Florida Chapter, AGC v. Jacksonville (June 14, 1993): applies its equal-opportunity injury rule; no guaranteed contract award is required.
- Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife (June 12, 1992): retains concrete injury, causal connection and stage-appropriate proof; prospective relief requires sufficiently imminent renewed exposure.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Tenth Circuit judgment and remand, 9–0, for application of the common standard. No program redesign, injunction, damages or award of a subcontract is ordered.

## Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996)
Source: [canonical federal-courts volume](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md).

#### Felker v. Turpin, 518 U.S. 651 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-8836; associated application A-890.
**Decided:** June 28, 1996.

##### Whether §2244(b)(3)(E) bars review of circuit authorization while preserving original habeas

**Holding:** Section 2244(b)(3)(E) bars ordinary certiorari review of a court of appeals' grant or denial of authorization to file a second or successive district-court habeas application; the present certiorari request must be dismissed for lack of jurisdiction. The provision does not repeal the distinct original habeas authority retained by §2241, which permits independent consideration of this petition without affirming or reviewing the circuit's authorization judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part I-A, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 2241 still names the Court and its Justices; §2244(b)(3)(A) concerns advance authorization for district filing, and the amended transfer rule does not name this Court. Direct filing is procedurally original but constitutionally appellate; §1651 aids existing jurisdiction rather than creates it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unpresented certification, interlocutory and supervisory channels are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ordinary certiorari dismissed for want of jurisdiction, 9–0, without affirming the authorization judgment.

##### When the new authorization-review bar governs

**Holding:** The authorization and review proceedings begun after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 enactment are governed by its specified authorization-review bar. That conclusion does not decide the temporal reach of every Title I provision or apply a new substantive restriction backward merely because a petition is pending.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine support the channel result; O’Connor’s Part I-B has Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six. Thomas, Scalia and Kennedy use a distinct relevant-activity approach.

**Material application:** Landgraf’s provision-specific temporal sequence governs the majority reasoning; a procedural/jurisdictional label alone does not answer retroactivity. The May authorization and subsequent review proceedings began after April 24.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No holding determines every Title I provision’s temporal reach or Georgia’s chapter 154 qualification. Chapter 154’s express pending-case direction does not automatically govern other amendments.

##### What successive restrictions and discretionary conditions govern an original petition

**Holding:** The district-court authorization mechanism does not govern filing this original application. Sections 2244(b)(1)–(2) inform the Court's consideration of repeated applications, but whether those substantive provisions bind this Court directly is reserved. Retained jurisdiction does not itself entitle an applicant to an original writ.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** For statutory second/successive §2254 applications, (b)(1) dismisses previously presented claims. New claims under (b)(2) require either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both previously undiscoverable facts despite due diligence and facts which, if proved with the whole evidence, clearly and convincingly establish that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would find guilt. A request to announce a new rule is not the first exception. Before district filing, (b)(3) requires circuit permission by a three-judge panel upon prima facie showing within thirty days; the determination permits no appeal, rehearing or certiorari. Under (b)(4), district dismissal is required unless the applicant actually establishes the conditions, despite authorization.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether (b)(1)–(2) bind this Court directly remains reserved; they inform the Court’s consideration. Rule 20 separately requires reasons for bypassing district court, exhaustion or satisfaction of §2254(b), exceptional circumstances and unavailability of adequate relief in another form or court. Section 2254(b)(1) permits exhaustion, absence of available state corrective process or circumstances rendering it ineffective; (b)(2) permits, but does not require, denial on merits despite nonexhaustion; counsel’s express waiver is necessary under (b)(3). No exhaustion, process adequacy, waiver or affirmative diligence-failure fact is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Original relief denied, 9–0, on the exceptional-original-showing ground; no return, transfer or hearing is ordered.

##### Whether the channel and successive scheme suspend meaningful review or dictate an unlawful outcome here

**Holding:** The specified review restriction and consideration of the operative successive scheme do not suspend constitutionally sufficient habeas review or prescribe a forbidden judicial outcome as applied to these claims. The original petition receives independent examination and fails on an adequate traditional original-writ ground, without deciding that every future statutory exclusion or every nominal surviving remedy is constitutional.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s opinion, Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** The retained original route supplies actual independent claim examination and a sufficient traditional denial ground. Klein distinguishes changed law from a command to falsify facts or decide an unchanged rule in a prescribed manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No approval of every nominal forum, future exclusion of otherwise sufficient innocence claims, all AEDPA, §§2254(d)–(e), or unpresented supervisory restriction.

##### Whether the submitted medical evidence establishes the Schlup gateway

**Holding:** This submission does not establish Schlup's gateway: reliable new evidence, assessed with all probative evidence, making it more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict. That conclusion does not adjudicate the separately pleaded constitutional innocence theory or make the gateway and AEDPA's successive conditions interchangeable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part IV-A, all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela withholds this additional application.

**Material application:** Detailed new three-to-five-day estimates and physiological criticisms are considered with Whitaker’s independently favored earlier death estimate, his acknowledgment that the later interval was possible, and the disappearance, car, call and clothing evidence. The submission does not eliminate reasonable adverse assessment at Schlup’s probability threshold. No affidavit is declared false and other-offense evidence retains its motive/intent/state-of-mind limit.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Gateway passage would permit consideration of a trial claim, not prove it, establish statutory (b)(2)(B), or decide a distinct constitutional innocence entitlement.

##### Whether the separately pleaded constitutional innocence claim warrants exceptional original relief

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming a possible constitutional claim based on actual innocence, this considered submission does not establish exceptional circumstances warranting the Court's original habeas intervention. Original relief is denied without establishing a universal freestanding-innocence burden, rejecting every such constitutional claim, or requiring an additional return merely because new expert opinions are offered.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** O’Connor’s Part IV-B, unanimous; independently sufficient original-writ ground.

**Material application:** Herrera supplies a particular-submission insufficiency holding, not one general constitutional innocence burden. The full medical dispute receives consideration; the conviction and circuit refusal alone do not replace examination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal freestanding-innocence threshold, categorical denial of every such right, presumed adequate Georgia remedy or mandatory further return from new expert opinions.

##### Whether A-890 warrants protection after claim-specific consideration is complete

**Holding:** Execution's irreversibility does not alone establish entitlement to a stay. Because the independently considered claims show no sufficiently substantial basis for relief through the available proceedings and no identified unfinished legally required process warrants protection, application A-890 is denied.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part V, unanimous, in the reasoned combined threshold decision.

**Material application:** Bowersox’s substantial-ground assessment remains distinct from Lonchar’s protection of legally required process and McFarland’s counsel/preparation predicates. Neither the petition’s existence nor retained jurisdiction supplies relief by itself.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic lateness penalty, execution date, altered prior stay, exhaustion of all other possible avenues or execution legality determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The pending stay request is denied, 9–0, after independent examination; no unfinished identified required process or sufficiently substantial relief basis remains.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Ex parte McCardle:** Applied to the specified withdrawn appellate route; no conclusion follows that every separate habeas source is repealed.
- **Ex parte Yerger and Ex parte Bollman:** Applied to the retained statutory habeas channel and its constitutional appellate character. They do not make issuance of an original writ automatic.
- **Landgraf and Rivers:** Their controlling provision-specific temporal inquiry remains in force; the application here concerns post-enactment authorization/review proceedings, not all pending habeas litigation.
- **McCleskey v. Zant:** Its recognition of lawful successive/abuse restrictions supports consideration of the new standards; it does not eliminate their separate textual requirements or settle their mandatory reach here.
- **United States v. Klein:** Its jurisdiction/rule-of-decision distinction is preserved; the present channel and standards do not direct a factual result under unchanged law.
- **Victor and Sandoval:** The whole-charge reasonable-likelihood test is applied by the eight Associates, with its existing limits on isolated terminology and automatic cures intact.
- **Sullivan:** Its structural-error rule is preserved; the Court finds no constitutional vitiation of the proof standard and conducts no harmless-error substitute.
- **Lisenba:** Its fundamental-fairness inquiry governs the presented medical-testimony trial claim. **Ake, Ford and Eddings** do not supply the proposed automatic physician-only testimony requirement.
- **Schlup:** Its gateway remains distinct from a substantive innocence claim and the new statutory conditions; the eight Associates find the present showing insufficient under its governing probability standard.
- **Herrera:** Its particular-submission holding is preserved without converting any separate theory into a general Court rule. The present original denial likewise assumes the possible constitutional claim without deciding its universal merits threshold.
- **Bowersox, Lonchar and McFarland:** Their distinct substantial-ground, required-process and counsel/preparation rules retain their actual triggers. None guarantees additional proceedings or a stay from filing alone.

## Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.
Source: [canonical federal-courts volume](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md).

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co., No. 98-470, May 17, 1999: resolves the bounded nonmerits sequencing question by permitting straightforward personal-jurisdiction dismissal before a difficult subject-matter inquiry, subject to the stated state-law and statutory limits; the contested-merits prohibition remains unchanged.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.
- **Church of Scientology v. United States:** Meaningful partial relief remains enough; no effectual relief of that kind is available on these allegations.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.
- **Bennett v. Spear:** Supported pleading-stage causal and remedial links remain sufficient; its ongoing constraint differs from the completed reporting deprivation here.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Applied for the live-controversy requirement and limited corrective authority to vacate without reaching merits.
- **Norton v. Mathews; Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech:** Their already-controlled merits and distinct jurisdictional settings remain intact; neither decides this unresolved EPCRA dispute.
- **Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation:** Its different statutory wording and enforcement scheme do not settle the merits question the Court leaves open.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western:** Its RCRA cause-and-remedy limits remain statute-specific; it supplies no automatic EPCRA current-violation rule.

## Triestman v. United States
Source: [canonical federal-courts volume](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md).

#### Triestman v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** Second Circuit No. 96-2563; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-03-12.

##### The authorization refusal is not subject to ordinary Supreme Court review

**Holding:** Section 2255's successive-motion certification cross-reference incorporates §2244(b)(3)(E)'s bar on ordinary certiorari review of a court of appeals' authorization decision. Because the only order presented is the Second Circuit's refusal to authorize a successive §2255 motion, this attempted review must be dismissed for want of jurisdiction, without affirming that order or adjudicating the underlying claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined by all nine participating Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Only an authorization refusal is presented, not an independent original application or §2241 judgment. Original Supreme Court habeas retains its statutory and Rule 20 conditions; §1651 creates no jurisdiction. Savings-clause eligibility, effective earlier opportunity, retroactivity, defenses, firearm liability and ultimate relief remain undecided.

## Bousley v. United States
Source: [canonical federal-courts volume](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md).

#### Bousley v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-8516.
**Decided:** 1998-05-18.

##### 1. Correct knowledge of the offense is necessary to an intelligent plea

**Holding:** A guilty plea requires real notice of the charge's essential nature. A materially mistaken description of a statutory element can render the plea unintelligent; Bailey's explanation of what the firearm statute prohibits is substantive law, not a new constitutional procedure barred by Teague.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg’s opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct supporters.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule concerns the plea’s own informed validity, not every antecedent claim surrendered by a valid plea or a later disappointed expectation. Competence alone does not establish informed assent.

##### 2. Direct-review default requires cause and prejudice or factual innocence

**Holding:** Failure to challenge plea intelligence on direct review defaults this claim. Neither anticipated defeat nor adverse precedent establishes cause where the legal argument was available; absent cause, the claim may proceed only through a sufficient factual-innocence showing, not a showing of legal error alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II–III are joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct supporters.

**Material application:** Schlup nevertheless permits review where, considering the whole probative record under the correct offense, it is more likely than not that no reasonable, properly instructed juror would convict.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The district court has not determined factual innocence, and the Government does not assert a distinct waiver from failure to raise innocence below. The gateway precedes adjudication of constitutional invalidity and does not itself establish entitlement to relief.

##### 3. The evidence and actual plea bargain define the innocence inquiry

**Holding:** The Government may offer admissible evidence outside the plea record to rebut factual innocence. Where it actually forgave an identified more-serious charge in the plea bargain, the innocence showing must also cover that charge; hypothetical charges, unproved bargains and alternative statutory verbs create no added burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** The complete rule has the five direct supporters of Parts III and IV.A: O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela additionally joins Part III’s beyond-the-plea-record inquiry and IV.B’s use-only application and conditional remedy, but not IV.A’s affirmative burden concerning an actually forgone more-serious charge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This indictment charged use alone; no actually relinquished carrying or other identified more-serious charge is established. Carrying cannot be presumed to preserve this conviction. A successful gateway requires adjudication of plea validity, not automatic acquittal or vacatur.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Seven Justices reverse the Eighth Circuit; the six joining Parts II–III and IV.B require an opportunity to establish factual innocence followed, if successful, by adjudication of the plea-intelligence claim. Stevens would vacate the firearm conviction immediately. The controlling mandate does not vacate that conviction now, affect the drug conviction, or require an oral hearing, release or dismissal.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bailey:** Active employment remains necessary for the charged use offense; possession alone is insufficient.
- **Henderson and Smith:** Applied to real notice of the charge's essential nature.
- **Rivers:** Supplies the substantive effect of an authoritative statutory construction.
- **Teague:** Its new-procedure limitation does not bar this substantive statutory claim.
- **Broce and Brady:** Distinguished from an attack on the plea's informed character; a disappointed expectation and misinformation about an element remain different.
- **Godinez:** Competence does not itself establish informed assent.
- **Reed and Engle:** An available argument's expected failure does not establish cause.
- **Schlup:** Extended to this defaulted plea claim with its demanding factual test intact.
- **Carriger:** Passing the gateway remains distinct from proving constitutional error and entitlement to relief.
- **Hyde:** Its presentence withdrawal setting does not decide this collateral claim.

## College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board
Source: [canonical federal-courts volume](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts.md).

#### College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-149; October Term 1998.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress expressly authorized federal suit and otherwise available remedies

**Holding:** Sections 1122(a)–(b) and 1125(a)(1)(B), (a)(2) unmistakably subject States and their instrumentalities to federal suit and otherwise available legal and equitable remedies for covered Lanham Act violations occurring on or after October 27, 1992. That enacted intent establishes neither constitutional power, State consent, a statutory violation nor entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices.

##### This own-product-advertising theory does not establish the required §5 basis

**Holding:** The alleged loss of customers from Florida Prepaid's misdescription of its own product does not establish a constitutional entitlement to prospective customers or truthful competitive self-advertising, and the statutory tort and economic injury alone do not identify a constitutional deprivation to which this §5 remedy is fitted. Section 5 therefore does not sustain the asserted application on this theory; established business assets, goodwill and legal claims are not categorically excluded from constitutional property.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens disagrees. The eight-Justice proposition is controlling despite the different judgment votes.

##### Continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit

**Holding:** A State's continued operation of an otherwise lawful commercial undertaking does not unequivocally submit it to federal jurisdiction merely because Congress clearly and prospectively subjects that activity to private federal enforcement, even when the undertaking is realistically optional. Congress's legislative intent is distinct from the State's voluntary decision to consent.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, directly joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer disagree.

##### The reversal has no common affirmative amenability rationale

**Authority of the judgment:** Five Justices reverse the federal immunity dismissal. Stone-Zsela relies on Article I and an included-argument view of review scope; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer rely on prospective commercial consent, and Stevens adds §5. These are distinct affirmative grounds, not five joins in any one of them.

**Limits of the judgment:** The Part V judgment binds the parties and permits the federal claim to return for ordinary adjudication. Parts I–III retain their direct majority support. No Marks rule makes the four-Justice consent position controlling: a participating majority expressly rejects that operative consent proposition, and consent and unilateral congressional displacement depend on different legal predicates. Stevens's §5 ground likewise cannot displace the eight-Justice rejection. No common constitutional or review-scope rule is extracted by combining unused alternatives.

The decision neither sustains nor rejects a general Article I abrogation power. It reaches no litigation-waiver question, particular accepted-benefit condition, new officer claim, underlying advertising merits or separate patent issue. A prospective officer action would require a connected officer, ongoing federal violation, proper claim and genuinely prospective relief; Edelman's retrospective-compensation boundary and Coeur d'Alene's actual limits remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Union Gas:** Its private damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion remain effective within their decided scope. No majority here extends or overrules that conclusion, and Brennan's entire plurality does not become a majority rule.
- **Seminole Tribe:** Its clear-authorization distinction is applied. It established no controlling direct-State amenability rule or categorical Article I prohibition and did not overrule Union Gas.
- **Parden, Welch and Hilton:** The residual inference of federal-forum consent from ordinary commercial participation is rejected even under the new explicit enactment. Parden's independently retained substantive FELA coverage and Hilton's specialized state-court enforcement and reliance holding remain intact; no wholesale overruling of those retained propositions occurs.
- **Beers, Great Northern and Atascadero:** Their requirement of State consent supplies the distinction between unmistakable congressional intent and unequivocal State submission.
- **Boerne, South Carolina v. Katzenbach, Katzenbach v. Morgan and City of Rome:** The enforcement/redefinition boundary is applied while retaining real preventive legislation reaching nonviolations and each precedent's constitutional setting. No new numerical or formal legislative-record requirement is imposed.
- **Roth, Paul, Haddle and Sullivan:** Their distinctions between constitutional entitlement, statutory injury and fair adjudication are applied without denying established intangible property or converting every statutory tort into a constitutional deprivation. Logan's protection of a legal claim does not equate the underlying alleged commercial injury with a completed due-process violation.
- **Hans and Fitzpatrick:** Their existing force is unchanged. The separate federal-question/common-law account does not become a Court holding, and the distinct Reconstruction power remains available on its proper predicates.
- **Izumi, Caspari, Madsen, Lebron and X-Citement Video:** Their distinct question-presentation rules remain. There is no majority determination that this Article I argument is fairly included or exceptionally reviewable. Murphy, Kolstad and Olmstead retain their actual scope applications rather than creating an automatic rule for every alternative source of congressional power.
- **Hess, Lane, Blue Fox, Schacht, Cedar Rapids and West:** Their arm-status, remedy-specific consent, removal and accepted-funding distinctions remain confined to their actual settings. They establish neither Florida's commercial consent nor this claim's liability.
- **Ex parte Young, Edelman and Coeur d'Alene:** Their defendant, ongoing-violation and prospective-relief requirements remain; this judgment creates no officer defendant or substitute damages action.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank, June 23, 1999, Part III, resolves the Article I question left open here: Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit, and Article I independently sustains that patent action. Hans supplies no immunity Congress cannot displace in that setting; see Federal Courts — Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Effective June 23, 1999, the federal immunity dismissal is removed in this action, but no common affirmative rationale supplies a general rule permitting other direct-State Lanham suits. The express statutory authorization remains distinct from constitutional power, State consent and entitlement to relief.

The Court adds a controlling rejection of the proposed explicit prospective commercial-participation consent theory and a confined rejection of the asserted own-product-advertising §5 basis. It preserves actual voluntary consent, fitted preventive §5 legislation and Hilton's retained FELA state-court coverage. Union Gas remains precedent at its decided scope; neither a categorical Article I permission nor prohibition emerges here. Lower courts must respect the direct majority propositions and the mandate without treating the four-Justice consent theory, the one-Justice Article I theory or Stevens's separate §5 theory as a unified holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Reversed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The reversal removes only the federal Lanham immunity dismissal. Falsity, materiality, the statutory cause, commercial injury, causation, preserved defenses and legally available relief remain for ordinary adjudication. No damages, injunction, finding of deceit or special evidence proceeding is ordered. The judgment revives neither the abandoned common-law count nor the dismissed, unappealed counterclaim and decides no part of the separate patent action.

## Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

#### Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-531.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States

**Holding:** Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly subject States and their instrumentalities to private patent actions in federal court for covered violations on or after October 28, 1992. Section 296(b) makes the otherwise available patent remedies available on their own statutory terms; that authorization does not itself establish constitutional power, State consent, infringement, or entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by all nine Justices, directly adopts this statutory proposition and its limits.

##### Appropriate respondent alternatives are legally available within their limits

**Holding:** A respondent may defend an unchanged favorable judgment on an appropriately supported alternative that neither enlarges its rights nor reduces the adversary's rights under that judgment. CSB's posture permits consideration of its preserved defenses of the immunity denial, but preservation and briefing alone do not expand the §5 question or establish an alternative's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' joins at this precise level. Their actual decisions on the alternative grounds remain distinct.

##### Section 5 independently sustains the authorized patent action

**Holding:** Section 5 permits Congress to prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional enforcement legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional. Sections 271(h) and 296 validly authorize this patent action on that basis; the plaintiff need not establish a separate constitutional violation to proceed under the Act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas disagree.

##### Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy as an independent ground for sustaining the immunity denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas oppose the extension.

##### Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit

**Holding:** A State's continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes suit does not constitute consent: Florida Prepaid did not consent to federal patent suit by continuing its prepaid-tuition program after §§271(h) and 296 took effect. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available. Whether Florida Prepaid's litigation conduct waived immunity need not be decided, and no waiver finding is made.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by all nine Justices, applies College Savings Part III and states these limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, who did not join College Savings Part III, accept it as controlling. Neither constitutional holding depends on consent.

The Court makes no finding of patent validity, infringement, willfulness, Florida-remedy adequacy, constitutional injury, or entitlement to relief. Section 296(b) retains its own conditions and any properly presented award-specific constitutional limits. Neither constitutional holding nor the judgment preapproves every compensation, injunction, fee, or enhanced award. Kolstad's Title VII punishment rules do not determine patent enhancement.

The appearance, transfer request under §1404(a), and request for affirmative declarations of invalidity, noninfringement, and unenforceability remain distinct litigation-consent contentions. The Court finds no waiver from any of them. Schacht does not settle their effect. No actual-violation exception, willfulness exception, State-remedy exhaustion, new constitutional damages cause, or Just Compensation Clause justification is adopted.

The action is not converted into an officer suit. Young and Coeur d'Alene require a connected officer, ongoing federal violation, proper cause, and genuinely prospective relief; Edelman and Pennhurst preserve retrospective-compensation and state-law boundaries. No Florida claims-bill or takings remedy is adjudged adequate or inadequate, and State Street supplies no invalidity finding about this different patent.

- **Union Gas:** Extends its retained five-vote Commerce-power constitutional conclusion into the Article I enforcement principle applied to the Patent Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause here. Its statutory holding and private third-party damages posture remain intact; its four-Justice complete rationale and absence of a Parden-waiver ground are not enlarged into historical majority holdings.
- **Seminole Tribe; Hans:** Seminole’s separation of explicit State-action authorization from constitutional amenability remains intact. Its set-aside of the State-dismissal direction established no controlling constitutional rationale, categorical Article I prohibition, abrogation-before-officer sequence, or overruling of Union Gas. The Court resolves the reserved power question here and limits Hans: its immunity does not prevent Congress from authorizing this federal-question action within the Article I enforcement principle.

Congress may reach some conduct beyond independently unconstitutional deprivation without replacing the protected right. The holding adds no mandatory findings, evidentiary quantity, geographic restriction, or sunset requirement.

##### Whether an untimely objection may defeat a scheduled exemption lacking a colorable statutory basis

**Holding:** Under 11 U.S.C. §522(l) and the applicable Bankruptcy Rule 4003(b), property claimed as exempt becomes exempt when no party in interest objects within 30 days after the creditors' meeting concludes or a relevant amendment to the exemption list is filed, whichever is later, and the court grants no further time within that period. Uncertainty about value allowed timely inquiry, objection or a request for extension; it did not suspend the deadline until collection. Rule 9006(b)(3) confines enlargement to Rule 4003(b)’s conditions; the August 1, 1991 supplemental-schedule wording does not reopen the expired period.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide §105(a), unpreserved fraud, unscheduled property, differently limited exemption claims, retroactivity, alternative valuation, liens or good faith. Rule 4003(c)’s burden on the objector remains applicable in a properly presented objection proceeding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. Its reversal of the $23,483.75-plus-interest turnover order stands; the scheduled proceeds remain exempt against this untimely theory. No alternative recovery, sanction or new award is adjudicated.

## United States v. Noland, 517 U.S. 535
Source: [canonical bankruptcy volume](../../../state/holdings/bankruptcy.md).

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-323.
**Decided:** May 13, 1996.

##### Administrative penalties after conversion and susceptibility to equitable subordination

**Holding:** Allowed §503(b)(1)(C) postpetition tax penalties retain case-era §507(a)(1) administrative priority after conversion because §348(d) expressly excepts §503(b) claims from its ordinary treatment of post-order, preconversion claims. Priority does not immunize them from lawful §510(c) equitable subordination: §726 makes distribution subject to §510.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 726(a)(4)'s fourth rank applies to covered fines, penalties, forfeitures, and multiple, exemplary, or punitive damages arising before the earlier of the order for relief or appointment of a trustee, and only insofar as they do not compensate actual pecuniary loss. It does not demote every noncompensatory penalty. Section 510(c), after notice and a hearing, permits all-or-part distributional subordination of an allowed claim to another allowed claim, or an allowed interest to another allowed interest, and transfer to the estate of a lien securing the subordinated claim. Both priority and the express exception retain effect; their coexistence establishes no conflicting congressional choices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No §510(a) agreement, §510(b) securities ground, or lien-transfer basis is established here. Susceptibility to equitable subordination does not prove a sufficient ground for its exercise or freeze equitable principles into a closed catalogue.

##### Insufficiency of the lower courts' class-based reasons

**Holding:** Section 510(c) does not authorize subordinating this statutory penalty class merely because the penalties compensate no pecuniary loss and payment reduces recovery for creditors who supplied value. The lower courts' stated reasons are legally inadequate even though they expressly disclaimed automatic subordination of every penalty. Lawful claim-specific equity remains available, but the Court establishes no other sufficient ground on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Noncompensation, insufficient assets, losses to value-giving creditors, and retained priority for tax and interest recur whenever this class competes with compensatory claims in an insolvent estate. Those reasons express the category-wide preference Congress's administrative classification rejects; calling them individualized balancing does not supply a different equitable circumstance. Statutory susceptibility and legislative statements about continued equitable development do not create a replacement ranking. Innocent-creditor and financing concerns cannot alone displace priority or bypass separate financing conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither requires misconduct universally nor establishes an affirmative entitlement to subordination without it. Permissible development is not frozen at 1978; particular inequitable conduct or another recognized claim-specific ground remains addressable when lawfully presented and proved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand, 9–0. The same class reasons cannot preserve subordination under a new label. Any other consideration depends on the record and preservation; no factual reopening, misconduct hearing, lien transfer, distribution schedule, or payment amount is ordered. Penalty allowance and tax-and-interest priority remain unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Ahlers* requires equity within the Code without itself construing §510(c) or prohibiting all equity. *Taylor v. Standard Gas*, *Pepper*, and *Comstock* preserve their misconduct-based applications without establishing a universal prerequisite. *Simonson*'s former statutory penalty prohibition supplies no present ranking. *Boteler* and *Nicholas* preserve postpetition penalties' distinct treatment and compliance function. *Things Remembered* supports giving compatible statutes separate effect; its remand-review ruling and separate equitable-remand theory do not authorize a new distribution rank.

## Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña
Source: [canonical civil-rights volume](../../../state/holdings/civil-rights.md).

#### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Federal Courts — Equal-competition standing and continuing injury (Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing).

##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid. Metro Broadcasting's lower federal tier is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The optional incentive differs from STURAA's expenditure command and a mandatory subcontracting duty. The Court preserves Fullilove's supported fractured judgment, Wygant and Paradise, without a general benign-purpose exemption, unconditional concessions, a finding of Gonzales's certification route, or approval of all future applications. The distinct certification and payment limits below remain; conflicting section 8(d) regulations are unresolved.

**Material application:** The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): overrules its selection of a lower tier for benign federal racial classifications; federal sponsorship supplies no exemption from the common standard.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains recognition of supported congressional remedial action at its supported scope; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): displaces its categorical strict-scrutiny formulation with the common standard while retaining its demand for relevant evidence.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to the actual burdens imposed by racial treatment.
- United States v. Paradise, 480 U.S. 149 (1987): retains attention to flexibility and fit.
- Shaw v. Reno (June 28, 1993): leaves its rejection of the design-only districting claim untouched; the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements also remain unchanged.

##### Vacatur and bounded remand under the common standard

**Holding:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, respecting concessions and supported findings in their actual scope and allowing further proceedings only as needed under ordinary procedural rules. Vacatur neither holds the arrangement unconstitutional nor directs any injunction; interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices support vacatur and remand. The Court's five-Justice coalition adopts the common-standard directions; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Material application:** The lower judgment, which applied the inherited federal framework, cannot settle the preserved challenge under the common standard without examining the arrangement's operation. Gonzales's certification route is unknown, and the overlapping rules' treatment of economic disadvantage remains unresolved; neither establishes an irrebuttable presumption or a lack of congressional evidence. The Tenth Circuit's recorded stipulation that section 502 satisfied Fullilove's evidentiary requirements and the concession that the agency stayed within delegated authority keep their ordinary force in their actual scope. Courts must neither fabricate an unconditional waiver nor treat the change of standard as erasing evidence or supported findings, and a stipulation under the inherited rule does not necessarily resolve every question under the common standard. Further proceedings require identification of a genuinely unresolved material issue and an explanation of why the existing record, findings or concessions do not resolve it. Existing records may suffice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fullilove v. Klutznick: preserves the actual scope of the section 502 evidentiary stipulation without treating it as resolution of every question under the common standard.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC: its federal tier no longer supplies the rule for final judgment; application of the common standard remains necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Tenth Circuit judgment and remand, 9–0, for application of the common standard. The remand directions require the court to identify the actual program and preserved challenge; assess the supported objective and the evidence connecting the measure's beneficiaries, categories and scope to it; examine presumptions, certification routes and review in operation; examine burdens and concrete, practicable alternatives; and examine continued justification. If government cannot establish the required objective, substantial relationship or justification for the burden, the challenged racial treatment fails, and relief addresses the invalid provision or application under ordinary remedial and severability rules. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. No program redesign, injunction, damages or award of a subcontract is ordered.

## Hopwood v. Texas
Source: [canonical civil-rights volume](../../../state/holdings/civil-rights.md).

#### Hopwood v. Texas

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 94-50664; related No. 94-50569 is outside the grant.

**Decided:** November 25, 1996.

##### The established unequal admissions process

**Holding:** The State’s racial admissions classifications must satisfy Adarand’s common substantial-justification standard: the State must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its racial treatment and that objective, and must justify the classification’s coverage and actual burdens. The 1992 law-school process fails because it subjected applicants to unjustified, separate racial decision systems rather than genuinely competitive individual consideration; unequal opportunity to compete is an injury without proof that an applicant would have received a seat.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not find that every index difference independently fails, that enrollment aspirations were fixed reserved seats, that race determined every final seat, or that any plaintiff would have been admitted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Croson's retained evidentiary demands and Wygant require concrete justification, fit and attention to individual burdens for state classifications. Bakke supplies the distinction between separate allocation and competitive consideration. Adarand supplies the governing standard and equal-competition injury.

##### A supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available

**Holding:** A public university’s interest in concrete educational benefits from a genuinely diverse student body can be an important objective on a properly supported record. The university must establish the educational objective, a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its actual racial classification and that objective, and justified burdens; a diversity label, racial balance, or an assumption that racial identity determines viewpoint does not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Kennedy supplies a seventh vote to vacate the categorical prohibition without joining this affirmative rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular holistic program, universal admissions design or later Texas program is approved. The decision requires no racial consideration and leaves federal classifications, other educational levels and employment outside its scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bakke's five express Part V-C joins permitted properly designed competitive consideration and rejected an absolute race-use injunction; Powell's complete diversity rationale was not a prior majority. This decision newly adopts the six-Justice supported-diversity rule. Adarand's common standard governs; Metro Broadcasting's separate federal tier was overruled there.

##### The responsible State and the continuing dismantlement duty

**Holding:** A State that operated a legally segregated university system must eliminate traceable policies that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices; good faith and facial neutrality alone do not discharge that duty, and the State bears the showing of dismantlement. The responsible actor cannot categorically be confined to the law school as an administrative unit, but a remedial racial classification requires a demonstrated connection and fit to a continuing wrong rather than generalized societal discrimination or racial imbalance alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No current Texas remnant or justified preference is found. Inquiry is confined to the lawful admissions counterfactual and properly presented relief in these four individual claims; no general structural suit is reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fordice and Ayers supply the State's continuing dismantlement duty and burden, including traceable interacting policies, continuing effects and practicable removal consistent with sound education. Croson and Wygant require supported wrong and fitted voluntary repair without invariably requiring a fresh formal adjudication. Jenkins preserves violation-linked and causally limited remedies.

**Holding:** Once these applicants have established the unconstitutional admissions process, the school must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that each applicant would not have been admitted under a specified constitutional process to defeat relief for the denied admission and its consequences. Plaintiffs still must establish the existence and amount of compensable loss under an available cause against a proper defendant; failure of the school’s defense yields neither an automatic damages amount nor unconditional admission.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-A, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the individual-remand disposition.

Elliott's uncommunicated offer is not treated as communicated or accepted, and Carvell's tuition testimony establishes no awarded sum. A successful same-decision defense does not erase unequal-process injury.

**Holding:** Deliberately using race to give applicants unequal admissions treatment is intentional racial discrimination for the Title VI issue presented, even when officials act in good faith and intend educational or remedial benefits. **Holding:** Smith v. Wade permits punitive damages in its applicable civil-rights setting for evil motive or intent, or for reckless or callous indifference to federally protected rights; lack of malice alone is not the whole standard. The punitive denial is affirmed on this record’s accepted good-faith finding and unsettled legal setting, without establishing a universal good-purpose defense or deciding the availability of punitive relief under every pleaded statute.

**Holding:** The established unequal process supports declaratory and nominal recognition independently of proof of entitlement to admission, subject to the available cause, proper defendant and immunity rules. Preserve that relief and fee-free lawful reconsideration if still practically available, while remanding the remaining claim-specific questions; no compensation, admission, immunity waiver, or personal-capacity liability is awarded here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Official-capacity pleadings do not become personal-capacity claims. ## Bailey v. United States; Robinson v. United States, 516 U.S. 137

##### Active employment under the use prong of §924(c)(1)

## Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, 512 U.S. 267

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-744, including Maher Terminals under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Maher Terminals is included under Rule 12.2, covering lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims; Greenwich concerns black-lung benefits.

##### The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order

**Holding:** When APA §556(d) governs and no applicable statutory exception provides otherwise, the proponent of a rule or order bears the burden of persuasion, not merely the obligation to produce evidence. - Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): preserves its preponderance standard and uses its explanation of the APA framework; its proof-quantum holding is not recast as an earlier decision squarely settling allocation.
- NLRB v. ##### The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation

**Holding:** Longshore Act §19(d), 33 U.S.C. §919(d), requires APA adjudication notwithstanding other provisions of the chapter, and §23(a), 33 U.S.C. §923(a), does not exempt these claims from §556(d)'s persuasion allocation by relaxing evidentiary and technical procedural rules. Section 923(a) separately requires reception of a deceased employee's declarations concerning the injury under investigation and makes them sufficient to establish the injury when corroborated by other evidence; that preserved rule does not establish Santoro's disputed aggravation and hastened death on the claim presented here, and the vacatur and remand for a lawful preponderance determination are affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the Longshore incorporation holding and apply it to both the lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims in Maher Terminals.

**Material application:** Section 923(a) frees the deputy commissioner or Board from common-law or statutory evidence rules and technical or formal procedure except as provided by the chapter; it permits, rather than commands, a chosen investigative or hearing method best suited to ascertain the parties' rights. Its deceased-declaration reception and corroborated-sufficiency commands are separate. The §20(a) presumption that a claim comes within the Act operates in the absence of substantial evidence to the contrary; the ALJ found it rebutted here, and it is no longer evidence supporting ultimate persuasion. The causal questions concern aggravation and hastened death, not an established finding that the workplace incident originally caused the cancer. Incomplete weighing remains a question for administrative reconsideration and ordinary review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): applies the preponderance quantum in APA adjudication while retaining the distinction between agency factfinding and appellate review.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393 (1983): retains genuine affirmative-defense burdens; it does not authorize transfer of claimant persuasion merely because a benefits scheme is remedial.

**Holding:** The Black Lung Benefits Act's §932(a) incorporates the relevant Longshore adjudicatory provisions subject to its statutory and regulatory qualifications; even assuming it authorizes an appropriate regulatory departure from APA §556(d), §718.3(c)'s statement of a congressional reasonable-doubt policy does not establish that departure. It excludes Longshore Act §§1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 37, 38, 41, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50 and 51. Incorporation is except as otherwise provided in §932(a) or by the Secretary's regulations, and references to the employer refer to fund trustees as the Secretary considers appropriate consistently with 26 U.S.C. §9501(d). Section 718.403 places proof of an alleged fact on the party alleging it except as provided in the subchapter, but its independent effect is not decided. ## Standards and Tests selection: Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

### Concrete personal interests and procedural citizen-suit standing

**Governing question:** Article III standing for a citizen-suit plaintiff alleging a deprivation of procedure, including ESA §11(g) enforcement.

**Current rule:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury; a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant's challenged conduct rather than to independent third parties' own decisions; and a likelihood, not mere speculation, that the requested relief will redress the injury. Authorization for any person to sue does not supply standing for only the public's undifferentiated interest in lawful administration. A procedural right must protect the plaintiff's own threatened concrete interest; when that connection exists, the plaintiff need not prove that the required procedure will certainly alter the ultimate agency decision. Aesthetic, recreational or professional wildlife interests may qualify, but past visits and indefinite return intentions without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure do not establish threatened interference with personal use. At summary judgment the plaintiff must support challenged standing elements with specific facts by affidavit or other evidence, with favorable reasonable inferences available; allegations cannot replace missing evidence.

**Authority by component:**
- Stage-specific evidentiary requirement and existing injury, traceability and redressability elements: Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, “1. Article III elements must be supported at the litigation stage”, June 12, 1992; Scalia Part II, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five).
- Prospective personal-use boundary: Lujan, “2. Indefinite return intentions and general species interests do not establish threatened personal injury here,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part III-A, same five-Justice opinion; Stone separately supports the record-specific conclusion.
- Citizen-suit and procedural-right boundary: Lujan, “3. Citizen-suit authorization does not dispense with a threatened concrete interest,” June 12, 1992; Scalia Part IV, same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, June 12, 1992, for the identified citizen-suit and procedural-right clarification; ordinary Article III elements and evidentiary burdens are retained.

**Exceptions and limits:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document requirement is adopted. The redressability application to absent agencies, binding force of the challenged regulation upon those agencies, effect of particular funding changes and ESA §7(a)(2)'s foreign reach are not controlling holdings.

**Holdings navigation:** Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992) — Standing and personal injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article III power before disputed statutory merits

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

## Standards and Tests selection: Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

### Habeas and §2255 appeals and appellate certificates

**Governing question:** Appeal from district habeas and §2255 orders and the certificate procedures in amended §2253 and Rule 22.

**Current rule:** District final habeas and §2255 orders are reviewable in the appropriate circuit, except that §2253(b) supplies no right of appeal from a final order testing a federal criminal removal warrant or detention pending removal. An applicant’s appeal from a final state-process habeas order or §2255 order requires a certificate of appealability from a circuit justice or judge, issued only upon a substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right and identifying the qualifying issues. Rule 22(a) directs original applications to the appropriate district court; a circuit judge must transfer an application there, and after district denial the applicant may pursue an authorized appeal rather than renew before a circuit judge. For state-process habeas appeals, Rule 22(b) permits a district or circuit judge’s certificate; the district judge must issue it or explain refusal and send the certificate/reasons with the notice and file to the appellate court. No certificate is required for an appeal by a State or its representative. An appellate court’s judicial denial of a certificate of appealability is a case in that court reviewable by certiorari under §1254(1), although it prevents the merits appeal from proceeding. Review or vacatur of a certificate denial does not itself issue a certificate or satisfy its substantial-showing and issue-identification conditions.

An authorized judge’s actually issued, issue-specific certificate admits the identified claim to appeal even when the judge allegedly erred in assessing §2253(c)(2)’s constitutional showing. That alleged assessment error does not itself nullify the existing appellate authorization. The substantial-constitutional-showing condition remains necessary for proper issuance; this rule neither finds a bare rules claim sufficient nor authorizes future certificates without the statutory showing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

### Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

**Governing question:** The constitutional justification federal, state or local government must supply when it changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification.

**Current rule:** Federal, state and local racial classifications are governed by one common substantial-justification standard. Government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. It must justify the measure's coverage, operation and actual burdens, including a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it; a corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro Broadcasting's lower tier for benign federal racial classifications is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. The standard governs a State university's admissions classifications, local contracting preferences and municipal promotion preferences alike; general inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not supply the justification.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective, evidence-supported relationship, coverage, burdens and alternatives; permissible and impermissible objectives; absence of universal prerequisites; application to federal, state and local government; overruling of Metro's lower federal tier and displacement of Croson's categorical formulation: Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña — The common substantial-justification standard, 1995-06-12; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five). O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join the standard.
- State university admissions classifications: Hopwood v. Texas — I. The established unequal admissions process, 1996-11-25; O'Connor, all nine.
- Local contracting classifications and serious consideration of responsive race-neutral alternatives: Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County — The local racial classifications are subject to Adarand's common standard and The BBE and HBE programs fail the governing standard's alternatives requirement, 1998-09-21; O'Connor for the Court, Parts II and III with all eight Associates.
- Municipal promotion preferences and the distinct sex standard: City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association — Local racial and sex classifications retain distinct constitutional standards, 1999-06-18; Kennedy's Part I, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Adarand, 1995-06-12, for the standard and its components; Hopwood (1996-11-25), Engineering Contractors (1998-09-21) and Dallas (1999-06-18) for their applications.

**Exceptions and limits:** The standard is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality, and it supplies no universal exemption for corrective measures. Congress may justify a national remedial category through authority and evidence unavailable to a municipality's independently designed measure; an agency need not reproduce Congress's entire evidentiary case for each project but must remain within its delegation and justify materially different or unsupported implementation. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. Sex-equality doctrine and other source-specific protections are not replaced; Shaw's design-only districting rule and the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements are untouched.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory priorities and claim-specific equitable subordination

### Statutory priorities and claim-specific equitable subordination

**Governing question:** Whether §510(c) permits a court to reduce rank because an allowed penalty competes with compensatory creditors.

**Current rule:** Statutory priority does not immunize a claim from §510(c), and lack of priority does not itself justify subordination. After notice and a hearing, §510(c) permits distributional subordination of all or part of an allowed claim to another allowed claim, or an allowed interest to another allowed interest, under principles of equitable subordination, and transfer of a lien securing a subordinated claim. A legally sufficient, supported claim-specific ground is required; noncompensatory character, estate insufficiency and reduced recovery for innocent or value-giving creditors cannot alone replace Congress’s distribution choices, even when described as individual balancing. This limit applies to both postpetition administrative penalties and prepetition nonpriority penalties.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article I enforcement against States in federal court

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part III with Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Independently sufficient alongside the Section 5 holding.

**Present operation effective:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires a valid enumerated-power obligation, an enforcement measure within that power and unmistakable congressional authorization; enacted clarity alone does not supply power. It does not rest on commercial-participation consent, which College Savings Bank rejects, and decides no actual litigation waiver. Validity, infringement, culpability and each remedy’s statutory and properly presented constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication; no compensation, injunction, fee or enhanced award is preapproved. The action is not converted into an officer suit; Young, Coeur d’Alene, Edelman and Pennhurst retain their boundaries.

**Former-rule note:** Union Gas’s retained five-vote constitutional conclusion supplies the foundation; the rule extends that conclusion without treating every proposition in its plurality rationale as binding. Seminole Tribe separated unmistakable statutory authorization from constitutional amenability and set aside the State-dismissal direction without a controlling constitutional rationale; it established no categorical Article I prohibition, overruling of Union Gas or abrogation-before-officer sequence. Florida Prepaid resolves the constitutional question Seminole left open.

**Related current rules:** “Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement” supplies the independent Section 5 ground for the same patent remedy. “Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers” separately governs the state-court forum. “Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit” remains intact.

**Holdings navigation:** [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action](HOLDINGS.md#article-i-independently-sustains-the-expressly-authorized-action).

#### Holdings

#### I. The judicial certificate denial is reviewable

**Holding and operative rule:** A court of appeals' judicial denial of appellate certification is a case reviewable by certiorari under section 1254(1), whether the certificate was correctly or incorrectly required. That review does not itself issue a certificate or authorize the underlying merits appeal.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Hohn establishes the reviewability of the actual judicial certificate determination while preserving the separate conditions for certification and collateral relief. The Ninth Circuit decided that threshold judicial request; the absence of authorization for the merits appeal does not eliminate the case before it.

Felker bars ordinary review of an actual circuit decision granting or denying authorization for a second or successive district application. This proceeding reviews a certificate denial, not that different statutory determination. Stewart likewise requires attention to the actual judicial action rather than treating every habeas threshold as authorization review. The Court therefore may decide the certificate and classification issues without exercising an unrecognized review exception or awarding the underlying writ.

#### II. The post-enactment appeal requires the amended certificate

**Holding and operative rule:** Amended section 2253(c) governs an applicant's appeal initiated after April 24, 1996, even when the identified pre-AEDPA law governs district adjudication of the earlier habeas application. Slack's April 29, 1998 notice therefore invokes the certificate-of-appealability requirement; the prior CPC label and the State's earlier contrary legal position do not dispense with that required authorization.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas with its author, Ginsburg: five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with applying that regime but joins no Part II rationale. Stevens, Souter and Breyer would retain former CPC treatment.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2253 regulates taking an appeal. The district proceeding and the later effort to enter appellate review are distinct events for the provision governing each. Slack began the renewed district proceeding in 1995 under either supported date but sought appellate review in 1998, after the amendment's enactment.

Lindh's chapter comparison preserves the specified pre-enactment district applications and does not decide every later appellate provision. Carriger's capital extension concerns its identified district-law provisions and does not settle this noncapital certificate question. Felker applies a review restriction to the post-enactment proceeding that restriction regulates; it does not transfer every new merits limitation backward. Hohn expressly reserved certificate transition, so its review holding supplies no already settled answer here.

The contrary whole-pending-case construction gives one commencement date to all later steps. The text's focus on appellate entry warrants the separate treatment adopted here. Applying the new certificate requirement does not apply amended section 2254(d) to Slack's pre-enactment application or adopt a universal claim-by-claim temporal rule for amendments. The State's earlier legal concession cannot authorize an appeal without the certificate the governing statute requires. That conclusion neither makes every subsection jurisdictional nor excuses every possible forfeiture.

The request may be considered under its proper legal character despite the CPC label. Rule 22 permits district or circuit certification and treats a notice without an express certificate request as a request to the circuit judges. A request is not an issued certificate.

#### III. A procedural-dismissal certificate requires both constitutional and procedural showings

**Holding and operative rule:** When a district court dismisses on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, a certificate of appealability requires reasonable debate both about whether the petition states a valid claim of denial of a constitutional right and about whether the procedural ruling was correct. Both are threshold showings; the court may address the more apparent component first, and any certificate must identify the qualifying issue or issues.

**Authority:** Part III has nine direct joins: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens, Souter and Breyer adopt this interpretation conditionally while maintaining that former CPC governs this appeal; their Part III agreement does not enlarge the Part II coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2253(c)(2) demands a substantial showing of the denial of a constitutional right. Barefoot supplies the reasonable-debate threshold, which requires more than a nonfrivolous assertion but not ultimate success. A procedural barrier does not eliminate the underlying constitutional claim from that inquiry. The State's categorical exclusion of procedural-dismissal appeals would insulate an erroneous barrier even when it blocks a substantial constitutional claim.

The opposite procedure-only approach also fails. A debatable procedural question or bare constitutional label alone does not make the required constitutional showing. The two inquiries must each be satisfied without converting certification into final adjudication of the constitutional claim. A plain and correctly applied procedural bar can defeat certification; a court need not decide the constitutional issue unnecessarily when the procedural answer is clear. It may instead begin with the constitutional component when that answer is more apparent. No universal sequence is prescribed.

Where a district court actually rejects the constitutional merits, the corresponding substantial showing asks whether reasonable jurists could debate that assessment or find it wrong. Slack's procedural dismissals are not silently reclassified as merits adjudications. Section 2253(c)(3) separately requires issue identification. Peguero addresses the effect of an actually issued certificate; none exists here. Neither this Court's review nor an unaccompanied notice supplies one. Rule 22's State-appeal exception remains distinct and is not implicated by this applicant's appeal.

#### IV. This exhaustion return remains a first application, including its additional grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** When an initial federal application is dismissed without prejudice solely to permit completion of state exhaustion before any federal merits adjudication, the renewed application is not second or successive merely because it follows that filing or adds grounds. Rule 9(b)'s new-ground abuse branch does not supply the missing second-or-successive predicate in this exhaustion-return setting; the contrary classification here is rejected, while actual independent default, exhaustion, timing and lawful case-management requirements remain.

**Authority:** Part IV of Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, making seven direct votes for this rule, its application and reversal with remand. Scalia and Thomas dissent from this classification and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Rose's complete-exhaustion procedure contemplates return for a first federal merits opportunity. The February 1992 dismissal did not adjudicate Slack's claims and expressly permitted renewal after exhaustion. Rule 9(b) begins with the second-or-successive category. Its repeated-ground branch requires a prior merits determination; its new-ground branch addresses omission constituting abuse within that category. The latter does not erase the opening classification in this setting. Former section 2244(b)'s prior-merits setting reinforces that reading.

The State emphasizes the new-ground branch's separate wording, Sanders's treatment of a nonmerits denial and McCleskey's pleading and excuse framework. Those authorities preserve structured abuse inquiries, but a deficient pleading or completed merits application is not this without-prejudice exhaustion return. The State's adequate abuse pleading and Slack's response cannot supply a statutory or Rule category that does not apply. The additional claims therefore do not alone convert this application into a successive one.

Stewart supplies a confined analogy: its Ford claim had actually been presented, dismissed as premature and preserved. It did not already decide omitted claims or every nonmerits dismissal. Today's holding supplies the exhaustion-return rule. Lonchar requires governing dismissal predicates rather than general disapproval of repeated litigation. Legitimate concerns about serial filings may support lawful pleading rules, exhaustion requirements or properly communicated return conditions on their actual predicates; they do not establish intentional disobedience here.

The holding creates no immunity for actual deliberate withholding, a genuinely applicable abuse rule, state default, prejudicial delay or lawful anti-duplication measures. It confers no unlimited right to amend. The classification error settles the procedural objection under review; the Ninth Circuit must still determine the substantial constitutional showing before issuing an issue-specific certificate. Reversal of its denial does not decide the unreviewed exhaustion grounds, the constitutional claims or entitlement to collateral relief.

**Operative procedural qualifications:** Rule 9(a)'s distinct delay provision permits dismissal where filing delay prejudiced the State's ability to respond, unless reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before that prejudice arose. No such prejudice or diligence finding is made here. Rule 9(b)'s dismissal permission is not an automatic command, and former section 2244(b)'s exception to its prior-merits permission requires both an unadjudicated ground and absence of deliberate withholding or other abuse; that prior-merits predicate is absent here. No free-floating equitable sanction replaces an applicable source's conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal rule covers every nonmerits dismissal or every later claim. Amended section 2244's restrictions retain their own applicability and classification predicates; no authorization determination or exception is decided here. The State's exhaustion alternatives and any actual independent, adequate and properly applied state default remain separate. ### Christensen v. Harris County — 2000-05-01

#### Judgment & Remedy

#### Holdings

#### I. The statute does not make employee requests the exclusive means of using accrued time

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 207(o) does not categorically forbid a public employer from directing paid use of lawfully accrued compensatory time merely because no agreement specifically authorizes compulsory use. The employer remains bound by lawful agreement restrictions, protected employee requests and the statute's accrual, value and overtime requirements; compelled paid use is not permission to confiscate credits or evade overtime.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's opinion is supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, six Justices in all.

**Controlling explanation:** Paragraph (5) protects an employee who requests accrued time: use must be allowed within a reasonable period unless it would unduly disrupt operations. It does not say that a request is the only permissible trigger for use. Paragraph (2)'s agreement requirement governs substituting time for cash overtime, not every later scheduling choice. Moreau preserves its actual agreement routes; neither it nor the statute establishes the terms of this class's agreements.

The accrual cap limits how much time may be accumulated and requires cash for additional overtime at the cap; it does not promise employees the right to reach that cap. The permitted cashout likewise contradicts an absolute right to retain every credit, although cash payment and paid absence remain different transactions. Paid time away from work consumes credits in their defined form: the employee receives the regular rate for hours not worked, which are excluded from hours worked when computing overtime. These statutory reasons suffice without a general presumption that employers control every unregulated workplace question.

The competing consent construction treats the substitute for cash as exclusively employee-controlled. It does not follow from the original agreement requirement or protected request right. An actual lawful term can restrict compulsory use, but none identified here defeats the categorical statutory judgment. That absence does not prove no agreement exists or every employee consented. It supplies no automatic right to new agreement proceedings. The decision leaves constitutional property questions, other labor law and actual wrongful-refusal claims outside this review.

**Operative qualifications:** For the covered state, local and interstate public agencies, a wholly time-based substitute must accrue at least one-and-one-half hours for each overtime hour under the applicable representative agreement or prior individual understanding. For employees outside the applicable representative-agreement route who were hired before April 15, 1986, regular practice on that date itself supplies the agreement; no additional individual agreement is required. Moreau preserves the later-hire individual route where the chosen representative cannot lawfully enter a binding agreement. Unwritten understandings require a record; an express employment condition requires knowing, voluntary acceptance and information about preservation, use and cashout. Notice and failure to object may evidence assent, but coercion or pressure may not substitute for it. Compatible agreements may mix cash and time or set use terms while preserving the statutory premium; inconsistent terms yield to the Act.

The cap is 480 hours for the specified public-safety, emergency-response or seasonal work, and 240 for other covered work. Additional overtime at the applicable cap must be compensated as overtime. Cashout may occur at any time using the regular rate when paid. Termination requires at least the higher of the final regular rate or the average over the last three years. For termination-rate averaging, employment after a break is treated as a new period when the break was intended to be permanent and post-April 14, 1986 credits were cashed out at the initial separation; if the final period is shorter than three years, averaging uses that period. No termination or rate calculation is decided here.

For employee requests, customary schedules, peak demands, emergency staffing, substitutes and compatible agreement terms inform a reasonable period. An employer must reasonably and in good faith anticipate an unreasonable burden on acceptable public service to establish undue disruption; inconvenience alone is insufficient, and the statute does not guarantee the employee's exact chosen date. Section 553.25 forbids using time to evade overtime or coercing acceptance beyond what can realistically and in good faith be made available within a reasonable request period. Its request provisions exclude separately defined other compensatory time, including pre-April 15, 1986 accruals. The separate court-reporter transcript provision is not presented in this Sheriff's Department case. No violation of these distinct protections is found.

#### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding and operative rule:** The supported advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The actual implementing grant permits administration and regulation. It does not make every later advisory response an exercise of authority to bind courts or employers. NationsBank and Smiley involved identified authoritative exercises of delegated responsibility; they do not settle this letter's status merely by identifying its agency. Skidmore preserves its potential persuasive significance rather than discarding it by label. Its supported construction adds a specific-consent restriction not supplied by the statutory request trigger or prior-agreement condition. No supposed change in policy, unproved internal instrument or unprovided Government endorsement supplies the result. Boston & Maine forbids inventing an agency ground. The Court decides this letter's supported advisory status, not the validity of every informal agency interpretation.

#### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding and operative rule:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** Auer concerns a responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. It does not impose a universal rulemaking-format prerequisite on interpretation, but requires actual textual fit. Section 553.23 permits additional consistent terms; section 553.25 protects requests and practical availability. Neither makes employee requests exclusive or requires an agreement covering every scheduling choice. The letter's interpretation therefore adds a condition rather than resolves pertinent ambiguity. The separate inquiry into statutory authority does not replace this own-regulation analysis. No conclusion here forecloses whatever authority the Secretary may possess to adopt a valid new regulation limiting compelled use.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Moreau retains its distinct earlier-hire practice and later-hire legally capable representative rules; it supplies no finding about this class's actual scheduling terms or general bargaining right.
- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's supported advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

#### Law After Decision

Section 207(o) supplies no categorical employee veto over compulsory paid use of valid accrued time. Actual binding agreements and every identified statutory protection retain their force. This advisory letter supplies no controlling statutory restriction and cannot add a condition inconsistent with the regulation. The Court decides no future rule's validity, blanket employer-control principle, confiscation power or constitutional property question.
