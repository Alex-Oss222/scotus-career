#### Fraternal Order of Police v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 97-5304; judgment below, 152 F.3d 998.
**Decided:** 1998-10-07.

##### Equal protection does not require extending the older exemption

**Holding:** Congress may withhold section 925(a)(1)'s governmental-use exception from the domestic-violence-misdemeanor supply and possession provisions while retaining that exception for the felony provision when the distinction rationally closes a practical gap in restrictions on access to weapons. The Fifth Amendment does not require Congress simultaneously to perfect every older exemption, and the challenged exclusion satisfies that rule on the developed official-use claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court.

##### Core-Preservation Review permits this conduct-based disability and official-use exclusion

**Holding:** The qualifying domestic-violence disability is a substantial regulation of particular persons under Wright's Core-Preservation Review and satisfies intermediate scrutiny on the developed official-use challenge. Keeping and bearing remain covered at Stage 1; at Stage 2, preventing armed recurrence is an important objective, the adjudicated violent-conduct predicate and conviction safeguards substantially connect the restriction to that objective, and the defined eligibility and relief limits constrain its burden; the personal right does not compel an official-use exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** *Love* recognizes personal protection, *Hale* distinguishes coverage from regulation, and *Rybar* rejects a setting-based denial of the right. Keeping or bearing does not lose protection merely because it occurs at work. There is, however, no independent fundamental entitlement to an armed public job. *Wright* measures justification by practical burden. This serious disability, confined by its adjudicated conduct predicate and statutory safeguards, is a substantial regulation of particular persons rather than incidental paperwork or a general abolition of ordinary defensive arms. The developed claim concerns access through the governmental channel for armed service; no individual's ordinary home-possession application is presented.

Intermediate scrutiny requires an important objective, a substantial relationship to it, and a restriction that does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate protected activity. Preventing renewed armed domestic violence is important. The force, attempted-force or threatened-deadly-weapon predicate within defined domestic relationships, counsel and jury-where-entitled protections, and statutory conviction-relief qualifications supply the substantial connection and constrain this conduct-based restriction. The classification rests on those limits, not merely on the availability of arms to other people. No particular claimant's access to relief is found.

The contrary argument confuses an individual right with immunity from a justified conduct-based restriction. A service weapon remains accessible to its possessor; official employment does not neutralize the risk established by qualifying conduct. Intermediate scrutiny is satisfied on the presented record, independently of ordinary equality review. This does not authorize disarmament for any misdemeanor, a mere allegation or an unrelated offense. An improperly classified conviction, an unavailable statutory safeguard or a genuinely different individual private-possession record remains open. Wright's prohibition of a general abolition of meaningful home possession remains intact; no public-carry licensing, training or recertification question is presented.

##### Remedy and questions not reached

**Holding:** Because the developed constitutional objections do not invalidate this exclusion, section 928 supplies no basis to extend the governmental-use exception. Reversal returns the case for a consistent judgment and any grounds that are both properly preserved and still undecided; it does not decide their preservation, revive an undeveloped theory, or automatically reinstate every District Court rationale.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the developed official-use controversy, not every privately held weapon of a public employee. No individual conviction-classification or restoration ruling, private-possession application, or unresolved commerce or commandeering ground is decided. Section 928 does not authorize extending an exemption the developed objections do not invalidate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns firearms and ammunition in the governmental channels defined by section 925(a)(1): transportation, shipment, receipt, possession or importation of items imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for the use of the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. That provision separately withholds its exception for sections 922(d)(9), 922(g)(9), and provisions concerning firearms prohibited by section 922(p). It is not an exemption for every privately held weapon of a public employee and is distinct from the machinegun exceptions in section 922(o)(2).

Section 922(d)(9)'s supply prohibition requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction. Section 922(g)(9)'s separate language addresses interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of an item previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Neither provision receives the other's elements by implication. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with a timely renewal application when required, during indictment and until any conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded administrative process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement for an individual in this case.

The qualifying misdemeanor must be under federal or state law and have an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. Counsel or a knowing and intelligent waiver is required. If the person was entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, there must have been a jury trial or a knowing and intelligent waiver by plea or otherwise; no new jury entitlement is created.

A conviction does not count when expunged or set aside, or when the person was pardoned or had civil rights restored if the jurisdiction provides for loss of civil rights for that offense, subject to the statute's express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court does not enlarge that wording or resolve its application to an individual. These are statutory limits on the rule, not newly imposed evidentiary procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Hunt* is applied to this uniform prospective associational claim; its three requirements remain distinct.
- *United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group* is applied for concrete member injury and the distinction between constitutional and prudential requirements; its statutory participation exception is not generalized.
- *Craig v. Boren* and *Barrows v. Jackson* support the directly regulated relationship for the officers' equality claim; no universal third-party standing rule follows.
- *FCC v. Beach Communications* and *Williamson v. Lee Optical* permit fitted incremental regulation; they do not make a generic public-safety assertion conclusive.
- *Heller v. Doe* retains both its actual proof-burden and family-participation holdings; its requirement of a rational relation to the challenged disadvantage is applied here.
- *Romer v. Evans* retains its protection against the unrelated blanket exclusion it addressed; this conduct-defined access restriction is distinguished by its actual regulatory connection.
- *Love* is applied for personal invocation and necessary acquisition; recognition of the holder supplies no unconditional official-use exemption.
- *Hale* is applied for the distinction between coverage and permissible regulation; its machinegun and registration holdings do not decide this different statute's commerce question.
- *Rybar* is applied for the rule that the setting alone does not extinguish personal invocation; its machinegun holding neither decides this disability nor orders automatic remand.
- *Wright* is applied through both stages of Core-Preservation Review and extended to restrictions on particular persons. The qualifying domestic-violence disability is a substantial regulation satisfying intermediate scrutiny on the developed official-use claim. Its prohibition of a general abolition of meaningful home possession and reservations concerning public-carry licensing, training and recertification retain their scope; no automatic remand or universal misdemeanor exception follows.

#### Hiley v. Barrett

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 97-9006; 155 F.3d 1276. No separately established Supreme Court docket.
**Decided:** 1998-10-09.

##### Ordinary equality review permits the domestic-violence category

**Holding:** On the preserved ordinary-review challenge, Congress may restrict firearm access following a qualifying domestic-violence misdemeanor without extending the restriction to every other violent misdemeanor at the same time. The specified violent-conduct and domestic-relationship predicates rationally connect this classification to preventing further violence.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgments without joining the opinion.

##### Different restoration systems do not invalidate this federal selector

**Holding:** Congress may recognize the convicting jurisdiction's expungement, set-aside, pardon and qualified civil-rights-restoration arrangements without guaranteeing identical firearm consequences across States or between offenses that do and do not cause loss of civil rights. The presented facial equality challenge based on those differences fails; the Court need not decide whether rights never lost can count as restored in a particular statutory application.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgments without joining the opinion.

##### The distinct occupational-effects theory lacks a discriminatory-purpose allegation

**Holding:** The greater employment effect of a qualifying-conviction firearm restriction on occupations requiring weapons does not itself establish the asserted occupational discrimination where the rule applies beyond those occupations and the complaint alleges no purpose to disadvantage law-enforcement officers. That uneven-effects theory fails without changing the separate analysis of an express statutory classification.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgments without joining the opinion.

##### Public employment alone supplies no fundamental substantive entitlement

**Holding:** A state-created employment interest and a wish to retain an armed public position do not alone establish a fundamental substantive federal right to that position. The qualifying-conduct firearm restriction rationally serves prevention of armed recurrence, so the preserved legislative employment claim fails.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgments without joining the opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The case decides the preserved civil challenges and no criminal prosecution, notice or mental-state defense. No separate abandoned strict-scrutiny or procedural-due-process claim is revived; the local claims settled with prejudice stay closed. No individual conviction-relief entitlement, never-lost-rights construction or universal firearms disability is established.

For the other questions in Hiley v. Barrett, decided 1998-10-09, Eleventh Circuit No. 97-9006; 155 F.3d 1276. No separately established Supreme Court docket., see Federal Courts, Constitutional Structure.

#### United States v. Wright

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 95-8397; judgment below, 117 F.3d 1265. No separately established Supreme Court docket.
**Decided:** 1998-10-05.

##### Personal invocation does not depend on state-militia affiliation

**Holding:** Wright may invoke the personal arms guarantee without proving membership in, service to, or preparation for a militia actively maintained and trained by a State. Military usefulness neither excludes an arm from the guarantee nor gives an unrestricted exemption from regulation; the actual weapon, conduct and restriction must be considered.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this holding.

##### Core-Preservation Review governs restrictions on the personal arms right

**Holding:** Stage 1 asks whether the law interferes with protected keeping, bearing, acquisition, necessary ammunition or transportation of arms, with constitutional coverage determined from text, history and the Court's precedents. If protected activity is burdened, Stage 2 requires justification corresponding to the severity of the restriction's practical operation, subject to the independent categorical rule that government may not abolish meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home.

**Operative qualifications:** A core deprivation receives strict scrutiny: the government must establish a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and the absence of a comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative. A substantial regulation receives intermediate scrutiny: an important governmental objective, a substantial relationship between the regulation and that objective, and a restriction that does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate the protected activity. An incidental regulation imposing no meaningful interference receives deferential reasonableness review; arbitrary administration, discrimination and cumulative destruction of the right remain unconstitutional. Routine equipment specifications, administrative procedures and minor requirements qualify for that last category only when they do not materially obstruct the right. Actual operation controls: calling an effective prohibition a safety measure or administrative rule cannot reduce its level of review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg as well as its author, Stone-Zsela. These five adopt the entire framework, including each tier, the practical-operation rule and the categorical home boundary. The rule is direct majority law, not a synthesis of separate opinions.

**Material application:** Love identifies the personal holder, incorporates the guarantee and protects acquisition necessary to exercise it. Hale requires attention to the actual weapon and keeps coverage distinct from lawful regulation; military usefulness decides neither immunity nor exclusion. Rybar requires a genuine application instead of disposing of the claim through militia affiliation. Those decisions leave the measure of justification open. The Court now supplies that measure because a remand must identify how protected activity and permissible regulation relate. Text and history inform coverage and regulatory limits without creating a closed catalogue. A real individual guarantee requires protection against practical destruction, while regulation that leaves its exercise meaningfully available calls for a justification fitted to its actual burden. Public safety is important, but its invocation alone does not establish that fit. Conversely, recognizing a personal right does not subject every equipment rule or administrative step to strict scrutiny. Registration and a possession prohibition must be examined separately. The home boundary protects a meaningful core; it does not grant immunity to every weapon, person or use. Necessary ammunition and transportation receive protection as incidents of protected keeping or bearing, not through coverage of every item or transport method. That boundary, the graduated standard and the application to particular facts perform different functions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Prohibiting ordinary functional handguns in the home and effectively eliminating all lawful public carry illustrate core burdens; no particular public-carry law is before the Court. Public-carry licensing, safety instruction, examinations, demonstrated proficiency, refresher training and recertification, including their classification and justification, are expressly reserved. No universal numerical rule, militia exclusion, military-weapon immunity or comprehensive prohibited-arms list is adopted.

##### The machinegun-count affirmance requires both stages in the first instance

**Holding:** When the appellate affirmance of this machinegun conviction materially rests on the invalid affiliation requirement and supplies no adequate independent application of the personal arms guarantee to the actual weapons and restriction, that affirmance must be vacated for the preserved constitutional application. On remand, the lower courts must apply both stages of Core-Preservation Review in the first instance: determine constitutional coverage of the completed automatic weapon under Hale, address the disassembled weapon and its actual statutory branch where material, and then classify and justify any burden on protected activity by its practical operation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II-C, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, controls the direction to apply both stages of Core-Preservation Review. Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer also join the vacatur and bounded remand apart from that framework direction; the judgment is unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Only the appellate affirmance of the one section 922(o) count is vacated. The existing record may suffice; ordinary additional proceedings are permitted only as needed. The Court creates no new statutory element, second machinegun count or severance of the conditional plea, and does not reopen admitted possession. It orders no empirical study, hearing, new trial, acquittal, release or return of arms. The alternative jury-instruction request returns only insofar as it remains procedurally material under the preserved plea after lawful disposition of the arms claim; no particular instruction or trial is ordered. This is not an automatic-remand rule for every arms case.

##### The Ninth Amendment supplies no established independent exemption here

**Holding:** Invoking privacy and natural self-defense under the Ninth Amendment does not itself establish an independent constitutional immunity for this machinegun possession and destructive-device nonregistration. The asserted exemption fails without resolving the separately preserved Second Amendment application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court; all nine Justices join this holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Core-Preservation Review governs restrictions on protected activity. The tier and ultimate validity of this machinegun restriction remain undecided; no closed weapons catalogue or categorical exclusion of dangerous arms is adopted. The applicable statutory exceptions and registration limits remain intact. The Court orders no acquittal, release, return of arms, new trial, automatic hearing, acceptance adjustment or particular sentence; the machinegun count and sentence return on their distinct grounds. A reserved legal challenge does not immunize false factual denials or other conduct inconsistent with actual acceptance.

For the other questions in United States v. Wright, decided 1998-10-05, Eleventh Circuit No. 95-8397; judgment below, 117 F.3d 1265. No separately established Supreme Court docket., see Constitutional Structure, Criminal Procedure.

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-B and III, resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. Part II-A preserves the anti-evasion/material-impairment route; Part III requires material support for either mechanism.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

#### Hiley v. Barrett

**Docket or dockets:** Eleventh Circuit No. 97-9006; 155 F.3d 1276. No separately established Supreme Court docket.
**Decided:** 1998-10-09.

##### The possession restriction supports the distinct prohibited-recipient supply regime

**Holding:** The presented facial commerce challenge to section 922(g)(9) fails because possession by the qualifying recipient class is a fitted anti-evasion and anti-impairment component of the valid federal restriction on supplying firearms and ammunition to that class. The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses permit that inclusion, while section 922(g)'s enacted interstate conditions remain separate requirements rather than an automatic constitutional cure.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgments without joining the opinion.

##### The prohibition governs later firearm conduct, not punishment for the earlier battery

**Holding:** Applying section 922(g)(9) to firearm conduct after its September 30, 1996 effective date does not violate the Ex Post Facto Clause merely because the qualifying conviction occurred earlier. The conviction identifies the regulated class; the statute neither criminalizes completed preenactment firearm possession nor increases the sentence for Hiley's 1995 battery.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgments without joining the opinion.

##### This restriction is not legislative punishment without trial

**Holding:** The general, prospective firearm disability tied to adjudicated qualifying domestic violence is not a bill of attainder under the historical, functional and purpose inquiries. Its fitted prevention of further violence and its serious but incidental employment consequences do not establish legislative occupational punishment or a congressional adjudication of guilt for a future firearm offense.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgments without joining the opinion.

##### The federal rule does not command the sheriff's administration

**Holding:** The challenged possession-and-supply rule does not violate the Tenth Amendment on the asserted staffing theory because it does not command the sheriff to terminate Hiley, legislate, prosecute federal offenses, administer background checks or appropriate funds. Its consequences for lawful staffing choices do not themselves make it a federal direction to administer a regulatory program.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela agrees in the judgments without joining the opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The case decides the preserved civil challenges and no criminal prosecution, notice or mental-state defense. No separate abandoned strict-scrutiny or procedural-due-process claim is revived; the local claims settled with prejudice stay closed. No individual conviction-relief entitlement, never-lost-rights construction or universal firearms disability is established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 922(g)(9) separately covers interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of firearms or ammunition previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Section 922(d)(9)'s distinct sale-or-disposition rule requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction; that mental-state condition is not silently imported as the complete culpability rule of section 922(g)(9). The present civil action decides no prosecution's proof or notice defense.

Under section 921(a)(33), the qualifying offense must be a federal or state misdemeanor with an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. The person must have had counsel or knowingly and intelligently waived counsel. If entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, the person must have received a jury trial or knowingly and intelligently waived it by plea or otherwise; this creates no additional jury entitlement. Hiley's predicate is treated as qualifying in this action, without supplying an unproved different conviction or deciding a challenge to one of these conditions.

An expunged or set-aside conviction, a pardon, or restoration of civil rights where the jurisdiction provides for loss of those rights for the offense can remove the conviction from the definition, subject to the express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court neither enlarges that wording nor decides its application to Hiley. No actually available pardon, administrative relief process or individual relief entitlement is found.

Section 925(a)(1)'s governmental-use exception does not extend to sections 922(d)(9) or 922(g)(9); it concerns its defined governmental channels, not every privately held gun of a public employee. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with timely renewal when required, during indictment and until a conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement here. The Court does not decide any of those individual applications.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hunt** and **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** applied to the uniform prospective representative claim, preserving real member injury and the distinct participation requirement; United Food's WARN exception is not generalized.
- **Love, Hale and Rybar:** applied to reject a collective-only arms premise and distinguish personal invocation from a particular restriction. Love's state-interest/federal-right distinction also governs the employment theory.
- **Wright:** its Core-Preservation Review governs a preserved arms challenge; constitutional coverage precedes justification measured by practical burden. It supplies no decision or automatic remand on Hiley's abandoned claim.
- **Lopez:** applied for the identified interstate mechanism and the boundary against generalized crime costs or jurisdictional words alone.
- **Hale** and **Kirk:** their necessary-inclusion method is extended to the particular prohibited-supply/recipient relationship established here. Their machinegun-stock holdings are not treated as already deciding section 922(g)(9).
- **Fraternal Order of Police (October 7, 1998):** its application of Wright treats the qualifying domestic-violence disability as a substantial regulation satisfying intermediate scrutiny and rejects an official-use exception. That arms rule governs a preserved challenge, which Hiley does not present. Its resolved express classification is distinct from Hiley's uneven occupational-effects theory; its reservation of the different commerce question is resolved here only for the presented facial challenge.
- **Lee Optical** and **Beach:** applied for rational incremental regulation, without eliminating the need for a relevant justification.

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime, or when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

### Core-Preservation Review

**Governing question:** Whether a restriction burdens constitutionally protected arms activity and what justification its practical operation requires.

**Current rule:** Stage 1 asks whether the law interferes with protected keeping, bearing, acquisition, necessary ammunition or transportation of arms, with constitutional coverage determined from text, history and the Court's precedents. If protected activity is burdened, Stage 2 requires justification corresponding to the severity of the restriction's practical operation, subject to the independent categorical rule that government may not abolish meaningful possession of ordinary functional defensive arms in the home.

A core deprivation receives strict scrutiny: the government must establish a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and the absence of a comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative. A substantial regulation receives intermediate scrutiny: an important governmental objective, a substantial relationship between the regulation and that objective, and a restriction that does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate the protected activity. An incidental regulation imposing no meaningful interference receives deferential reasonableness review; arbitrary administration, discrimination and cumulative destruction of the right remain unconstitutional. Routine equipment specifications, administrative procedures and minor requirements qualify for that last category only when they do not materially obstruct the right. Actual operation controls: calling an effective prohibition a safety measure or administrative rule cannot reduce its level of review.

**Exceptions and limits:** The home boundary protects a meaningful core; it does not grant immunity to every weapon, person or use. Necessary ammunition and transportation receive protection as incidents of protected keeping or bearing, not through coverage of every item or transport method. That boundary, the graduated standard and the application to particular facts perform different functions.

**Application to particular persons:** The qualifying domestic-violence disability is a substantial regulation of particular persons under Wright's Core-Preservation Review and satisfies intermediate scrutiny on the developed official-use challenge. Keeping and bearing remain covered at Stage 1; at Stage 2, preventing armed recurrence is an important objective, the adjudicated violent-conduct predicate and conviction safeguards substantially connect the restriction to that objective, and the defined eligibility and relief limits constrain its burden; the personal right does not compel an official-use exception.

*Love* recognizes personal protection, *Hale* distinguishes coverage from regulation, and *Rybar* rejects a setting-based denial of the right. Keeping or bearing does not lose protection merely because it occurs at work. There is, however, no independent fundamental entitlement to an armed public job. *Wright* measures justification by practical burden. This serious disability, confined by its adjudicated conduct predicate and statutory safeguards, is a substantial regulation of particular persons rather than incidental paperwork or a general abolition of ordinary defensive arms. The developed claim concerns access through the governmental channel for armed service; no individual's ordinary home-possession application is presented.

The contrary argument confuses an individual right with immunity from a justified conduct-based restriction. A service weapon remains accessible to its possessor; official employment does not neutralize the risk established by qualifying conduct. Intermediate scrutiny is satisfied on the presented record, independently of ordinary equality review. This does not authorize disarmament for any misdemeanor, a mere allegation or an unrelated offense. An improperly classified conviction, an unavailable statutory safeguard or a genuinely different individual private-possession record remains open. Wright's prohibition of a general abolition of meaningful home possession remains intact; no public-carry licensing, training or recertification question is presented.

**Statutory scope of the official-use application:** The holding concerns firearms and ammunition in the governmental channels defined by section 925(a)(1): transportation, shipment, receipt, possession or importation of items imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for the use of the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. That provision separately withholds its exception for sections 922(d)(9), 922(g)(9), and provisions concerning firearms prohibited by section 922(p). It is not an exemption for every privately held weapon of a public employee and is distinct from the machinegun exceptions in section 922(o)(2).

Section 922(d)(9)'s supply prohibition requires knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has the qualifying conviction. Section 922(g)(9)'s separate language addresses interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of an item previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Neither provision receives the other's elements by implication. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under section 925(b), or persons granted section 925(c) relief, remains. Section 925(b) concerns an importer, manufacturer, dealer or collector indicted for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license, with a timely renewal application when required, during indictment and until any conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety and public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded administrative process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or entitlement for an individual in this case.

The qualifying misdemeanor must be under federal or state law and have an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child with the victim; a person cohabiting or formerly cohabiting as spouse, parent or guardian; or a person similarly situated to a spouse, parent or guardian. Counsel or a knowing and intelligent waiver is required. If the person was entitled to a jury under the jurisdiction's law, there must have been a jury trial or a knowing and intelligent waiver by plea or otherwise; no new jury entitlement is created.

A conviction does not count when expunged or set aside, or when the person was pardoned or had civil rights restored if the jurisdiction provides for loss of civil rights for that offense, subject to the statute's express-firearms-reservation clause. That clause names a pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibiting shipment, transportation, possession or receipt of firearms; it does not separately repeat set-aside. The Court does not enlarge that wording or resolve its application to an individual. These are statutory limits on the rule, not newly imposed evidentiary procedures.

- Stage 1 coverage, all three Stage 2 standards, practical operation and the independent categorical home rule: United States v. Wright, 1998-10-05, Part II-B; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (five direct rationale votes).
- Both-stage remand direction and incidental registration application: Wright, Parts II-C and III; the same five. All nine support the bounded machinegun vacatur and registration affirmance. Stevens's concurrence, Part A, joined by all seven other Associates, independently sustains the tax-administration and registration ground; Stone-Zsela adds no independent tax ground.
- Substantial regulation of qualifying persons, intermediate scrutiny and official-use limits: Fraternal Order of Police v. **Present operation effective:** Wright, 1998-10-05; Fraternal Order of Police, 1998-10-07, for the application to qualifying persons in the defined official-use category.

No universal numerical rule, militia exclusion, military-weapon immunity or comprehensive prohibited-arms list is adopted.

### Personal arms protection, incorporation and necessary acquisition

**Governing question:** Who may invoke the Second Amendment against federal and state action and what acquisition interest it protects.

**Current rule:** The Second Amendment protects an individual’s keeping and bearing of arms within its constitutional scope; formal militia enrollment, current service or preparation for organized military activity is not a prerequisite. Through the Fourteenth Amendment’s Due Process Clause, the same constitutional standards apply against the States. Cruikshank and Presser cease to control only insofar as they bar that application; Presser’s unauthorized armed-organization disposition remains. Acquisition necessary to constitutionally protected keeping falls within that protection, so a State cannot categorically exclude a claim because it obstructed purchase rather than seized an arm. Coverage still depends on the actual weapon and conduct and is distinct from the validity of a restriction. Military usefulness alone neither excludes an arm nor supplies immunity from manufacture, transfer, possession, taxation or registration controls. In particular, personal protection and military utility alone supply no exemption from NFA identification and registration requirements administering lawful making and transfer.

**Authority by component:**

- Personal holder, state incorporation and necessary acquisition: The arms guarantee is personally held and does not require formal militia enrollment; The Fourteenth Amendment applies the personal arms guarantee to the States; Necessary acquisition cannot be excluded merely because the State obstructed purchase, 1995-12-11. O’Connor Parts III-A–C with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (six).
- Military-utility boundary: The right is personally held, and military usefulness supplies no automatic immunity, 1996-02-12; O’Connor Part III-A, all nine.
- Separate NFA registration obligation: Military usefulness supplies no exemption from the separate registration duty, 1996-02-12; O’Connor Part IV, all Associates (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Love, 1995-12-11, and Hale, 1996-02-12, for their respective components.

**Open question:** No comprehensive weapons classification, closed regulatory list or universal defensive-purpose rule is established. Purchase from every seller is not guaranteed; licensing and investigation are not categorically prohibited. State-law illegality alone does not establish infringement. Registration that cannot lawfully be approved remains a different unresolved application.

**Related current rules:** Core-Preservation Review supplies the justification standard after constitutional coverage is established.

**Holdings navigation:** Love v. Pepersack; United States v. Hale, judgment below 978 F.2d 1016.
