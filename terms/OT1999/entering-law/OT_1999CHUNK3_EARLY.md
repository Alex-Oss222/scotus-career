## Standards and Tests selection: Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702

### Scientific expert evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702

**Governing question:** Reliability and fit of scientific, technical and other specialized evidence under original Rules 104(a) and 702.

**Current rule:** Frye's mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite does not govern scientific expert evidence under the federal Rules. Under original Rules 104(a) and 702, the proponent must establish by a preponderance a preliminary foundation of scientifically reliable knowledge fitting the disputed factual question and assisting the factfinder. The judge examines principles, methodology and valid application flexibly. Relevant nonexclusive considerations include testability and testing, peer review and publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance; none is an invariant prerequisite. Acceptance alone does not establish admissibility, and its absence alone does not require exclusion. Admissibility does not determine causation or sufficiency. The judge may examine the reliable inferential connection from the scientific materials to the offered conclusion; credentials or the label weight of evidence cannot replace that connection. No identical-study condition, automatic exclusion of animal or nonsignificant studies, or requirement that each study independently prove the conclusion follows; supported cumulative inference remains possible. Admission and exclusion both receive abuse-of-discretion appellate review, with independent correction of legal error and proper protection for historical findings. An exclusion’s decisive effect on summary judgment does not alter that standard. No later Rule text is adopted. The reliability-and-fit duty applies to scientific, technical and other specialized knowledge under original Rule 702. Experience may supply knowledge, but a professional label does not replace a reliable connection between the method, its actual application and the proposition offered. Daubert’s nonexclusive considerations apply when useful to the particular expertise; a judge need not demand laboratory testing, publication or every consideration in every field. Abuse-of-discretion review governs both the reasonable selection of the screening method and the ultimate evidentiary ruling, while categorical legal error remains independently correctable.

Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- Reliable scientific foundation and fit require flexible preliminary screening: Daubert v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Operational assets and the unitary-business limitation on apportioned income

### Operational assets and the unitary-business limitation on apportioned income

**Governing question:** Whether a State may apportion income from an asset held by a nondomiciliary corporation doing business there.

**Current rule:** The State may apportion income connected to the corporation's unitary business but may not include discrete, unrelated investment value merely because the corporation does business in the State or owns the asset. A unitary relationship between payor and recipient is sufficient but unnecessary when the income-producing asset or capital transaction itself serves an operational function in the taxpayer's unitary business, subject to territorial connection and fair apportionment. A general acquisition strategy and intended later use of sale proceeds cannot replace an identifiable operational function where the enterprises lack functional integration, centralized management and economies of scale. A valid taxable base and a fair allocation formula remain separate requirements; the taxpayer's existing clear-and-cogent burden survives.

**Authority by component:**
- Unitary relationship and operational-function routes: Allied-Signal, Inc. v. Director, Division of Taxation, “Holding 1: The unitary-business limitation and operational-asset route remain governing law”, June 15, 1992; Kennedy, joined by White, Stevens, Scalia and Souter (five).
- Insufficiency of general acquisition strategy and later proceeds use without either connection: Allied-Signal, “Holding 2: The stipulated record requires exclusion of the ASARCO gain,” June 15, 1992; same five Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Allied-Signal, Inc. v. Director, Division of Taxation, June 15, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** No holding makes every state-law category of business income constitutional or reduces the inquiry to investment duration.

**Holdings navigation:** Allied-Signal, Inc., as successor-in-interest to Bendix Corp. v. Director, Division of Taxation, 504 U.S. 768 (1992) — State-tax apportionment and investment income.

## Standards and Tests selection: Notice-date selection of the Tax Court refund lookback

### Notice-date selection of the Tax Court refund lookback

**Governing question:** The payment window under 26 U.S.C. §6512(b)(3)(B) for an ordinary refund claim.

**Current rule:** The hypothetical claim filed on the deficiency-notice date both selects and measures the incorporated §6511(b)(2) lookback. Ordinarily, §6511(a) permits a claim within three years after a return or two years after payment, whichever ends later; without a return, two years after payment applies. A claim within the return-based three-year period reaches payments within three years plus an applicable filing extension; otherwise the payment lookback is two years. The notice-date hypothetical supplies a claim, not a return: a later-filed return cannot retrospectively select the three-year window or bring earlier payments inside it.

**Authority by component:**

- Lundy — notice-date selection and measurement, 1996-01-17; O’Connor, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Lundy, January 17, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** Deficiency jurisdiction remains distinct from refund authority. Section 6512(b)(3)(A)’s postnotice payments, subsection (C)’s qualifying actual prenotice claims, assessment-extension agreements and special statutory periods retain their separate operation. No universal jurisdictional characterization of all tax deadlines follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Commissioner v. Lundy, 516 U.S. 235.

## Standards and Tests selection: Tax-refund filing and payment limits do not permit incapacity tolling

### Tax-refund filing and payment limits do not permit incapacity tolling

**Governing question:** Whether mental incapacity or extraordinary inability to file permits a nonstatutory extension of the applicable §6511 tax-refund limits.

**Current rule:** The integrated filing, allowance and recovery-amount scheme in §6511 rebuts the requested equitable extension for mental incapacity or extraordinary inability to file, even assuming Irwin's rebuttable presumption applies. The separate §6511(b)(2) payment lookback is not enlarged by that extension even if the claim is otherwise timely under §6511(a). Actual filing, the return due date and the separately recoverable amount remain distinct; express statutory adjustments retain their own conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Brockamp — The enacted filing scheme rebuts nonstatutory equitable suspension, 1997-02-18; Breyer Part I for all nine supplies the filing-scheme exclusion
- United States v. Brockamp — The payment lookback independently bars these tax-payment recoveries, 1997-02-18; Breyer Part II for all nine supplies the independent amount-limit exclusion

**Present operation effective:** Brockamp, 1997-02-18.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule concerns tax payments; whether a remittance instead is a deposit requires its own determination. It establishes no universal jurisdictional classification and resolves no separate fraud, inducement, recoupment or constitutional theory.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Brockamp and United States v. Scott (1997-02-18) — The enacted filing scheme rebuts nonstatutory equitable suspension; United States v. Brockamp and United States v. Scott (1997-02-18) — Federal Taxation / Refund limitations and equitable tolling.

## Standards and Tests selection: Civil RICO limitation, new injury and concealed discovery

### Civil RICO limitation, new injury and concealed discovery

**Governing question:** Whether a later predicate or concealment permits recovery of earlier civil RICO losses after the four-year period.

**Current rule:** A later predicate in the same pattern, including an act injuring someone else, does not renew the four-year limitation period for an already actionable injury. A later wrongful act directly causing a genuinely new injury may support recovery for that injury without reviving older damages. Damages genuinely too speculative or incapable of proof earlier may accrue when they occur; continuing calculable loss, insufficient investigation or a later calculation of old harm does not meet that qualification. Fraudulent-concealment tolling requires the plaintiff to show concealment prevented timely discovery despite reasonable diligence. Misleading conduct alone is insufficient where reasonable inquiry would reveal the claim; extraordinary investigation overcoming otherwise successful concealment is not required.

**Authority by component:**
- No stale-injury revival and distinct new-injury/unprovable-damage routes: Klehr, Klehr v. A.O. Smith Corp. — I. A later predicate does not renew a stale injury and Klehr v. A.O. Smith Corp. — II. New injury and previously unprovable damages are different from continuing loss, Breyer Parts I–II, six direct joins, 1997-06-19.
- Diligence for concealment tolling: Klehr, Klehr v. A.O. Smith Corp. — III. Concealment does not eliminate reasonable diligence, Breyer Part III, eight Associates, 1997-06-19.

**Present operation effective:** Klehr, 1997-06-19.

**Open question:** No universal injury, injury-discovery or injury-and-pattern-discovery accrual trigger is selected. Common product, damage type, defendant or pattern does not conclusively make an injury old; the required direct causal relation remains. The diligence holding is not a rule for every equitable-estoppel doctrine.

**Holdings navigation:** Klehr v. A. O. Smith Corp. (1997-06-19) — Business and Commercial Law / Civil RICO limitations and concealment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury

### Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury

**Governing question:** What instructions govern territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury?

**Current rule:** Deliberate or predominant racial consideration in territorial district design, including departures from ordinary districting criteria, does not alone state the design-only equal-protection claim without racial exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, legally inferior civic membership or another independently cognizable constitutional deprivation. This substantive boundary is not heightened pleading, a universal downstream-loss requirement or an additional injury element for independent statutory claims. Other claims retain their own elements; no predominance scrutiny rule or general replacement for Adarand's common standard follows. This remains so when race actually predominates and traditional criteria are subordinated. A cognizable status or participation injury can be complete in government’s own unequal treatment without a second governmental act, monetary loss or later electoral defeat; shared-electorate composition alone does not establish imposed political beliefs, racial ineligibility, inferior civic status or a representative legally responsible only to one race. Absence of a statutory command or prior adjudication requiring a particular district does not itself prohibit otherwise lawful voluntary consideration of racial conditions. Shape is evidence, not a necessary gateway or sufficient proof. Purpose findings receive ordinary clear-error review; their legal consequences remain separate.

## Standards and Tests selection: Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

### Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications

**Governing question:** The constitutional justification federal, state or local government must supply when it changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification.

**Current rule:** Federal, state and local racial classifications are governed by one common substantial-justification standard. Government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. It must justify the measure's coverage, operation and actual burdens, including a justified response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it; a corrective purpose alone does not establish validity. Metro Broadcasting's lower tier for benign federal racial classifications is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained. The standard governs a State university's admissions classifications, local contracting preferences and municipal promotion preferences alike; general inequality, numerical imbalance and good intentions alone do not supply the justification.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective, evidence-supported relationship, coverage, burdens and alternatives; permissible and impermissible objectives; absence of universal prerequisites; application to federal, state and local government; overruling of Metro's lower federal tier and displacement of Croson's categorical formulation: Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña — The common substantial-justification standard, 1995-06-12; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five). O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join the standard.
- State university admissions classifications: Hopwood v. Texas — I. The established unequal admissions process, 1996-11-25; O'Connor, all nine.
- Local contracting classifications and serious consideration of responsive race-neutral alternatives: Engineering Contractors Association of South Florida v. Metropolitan Dade County — The local racial classifications are subject to Adarand's common standard and The BBE and HBE programs fail the governing standard's alternatives requirement, 1998-09-21; O'Connor for the Court, Parts II and III with all eight Associates.
- Municipal promotion preferences and the distinct sex standard: City of Dallas v. Dallas Fire Fighters Association — Local racial and sex classifications retain distinct constitutional standards, 1999-06-18; Kennedy's Part I, all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Adarand, 1995-06-12, for the standard and its components; Hopwood (1996-11-25), Engineering Contractors (1998-09-21) and Dallas (1999-06-18) for their applications.

**Exceptions and limits:** The standard is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality, and it supplies no universal exemption for corrective measures. Congress may justify a national remedial category through authority and evidence unavailable to a municipality's independently designed measure; an agency need not reproduce Congress's entire evidentiary case for each project but must remain within its delegation and justify materially different or unsupported implementation. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. Sex-equality doctrine and other source-specific protections are not replaced; Shaw's design-only districting rule and the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements are untouched.

**Related current rules:** Race-conscious public-university admissions and individual relief; Race-conscious local public-contracting preferences; Sex-conscious public-contracting preferences; Territorial racial design and independently cognizable injury.

## Standards and Tests selection: Appeal-warning omissions and causal loss on §2255 review

### Appeal-warning omissions and causal loss on §2255 review

**Governing question:** Collateral relief for failure to give the appeal advice required by Rule 32.

**Current rule:** The sentencing judge must give the required appeal advice despite independent knowledge. But actual knowledge and an unchallenged voluntary decision not to appeal can establish that the omission caused no lost appeal; the rule violation alone then does not require §2255 relief. Causal deprivation is distinct from probable appellate success: a person actually deprived of an appeal need not prove the lost appeal would have succeeded.

**Authority by component:**

- Mandatory advice, causal loss and no meritorious-appeal substitute: Canonical Decision Record `peguero_v_united_states_merits_1999-03-02.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / A mandatory warning error does not restore an appeal that the omission did not cause the defendant to lose, 1999-03-02; unanimous Part II.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-03-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal clear-and-convincing burden on disputed knowledge, presumption that cooperation waives appeal or procedure for future contested cases is adopted. The scope of a restored appeal is not resolved.

**Holdings navigation:** [Peguero v. United States — A mandatory warning error does not restore an appeal that the omission did not cause the defendant to lose](HOLDINGS.md#a-mandatory-warning-error-does-not-restore-an-appeal-that-the-omission-did-not-cause-the-defendant-to-lose-1).

#### Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, 509 U.S. 579 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-102.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### The enacted Rules displace mandatory general acceptance

**Holding:** Frye’s mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite does not govern admission of scientific expert evidence in federal trials under the Federal Rules of Evidence. Acceptance remains potentially relevant under Rule 702, but its absence does not alone require exclusion and its presence does not dispense with the Rules’ other requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Frye v. United States, 293 F.1013: displaced as a mandatory prerequisite in federal trials; not a Supreme Court Rule 702 holding being overruled.
- Beech Aircraft v. Rainey, 488 U.S. 153: statutory interpretation of enacted Rules applied.
- United States v. Abel, 469 U.S. 45: compatible common-law guidance retained, incompatible prerequisite rejected.
- Bourjaily v. United States, 483 U.S. 171: Rules supersede incompatible common law.

##### Reliable scientific foundation and fit require flexible preliminary screening

**Holding:** Under original Rules 104(a) and 702, the proponent must establish by a preponderance the preliminary foundation for scientifically reliable knowledge that fits the disputed factual question and will assist the factfinder. The judge examines principles, methodology and valid application using relevant nonexclusive considerations—testability and testing, peer review/publication, meaningful error information and operational standards, and acceptance—without making any one consideration an invariant prerequisite; admissibility does not determine ultimate causation or sufficiency.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The original Rules govern. No later Rule 702 text, universal technical-specialty rule, appellate-review standard or substantive causation finding is adopted. Cross-examination supplements screening for admissible evidence; Rules 403, 703, 706 and ordinary sufficiency rules remain available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Frye-based summary judgment and remand for evaluation of material proffers and reconsideration of summary judgment. Further fair foundation submissions may be received; no direction to admit every expert.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bourjaily v. United States, 483 U.S. 171, 175–176: preponderance for preliminary admissibility applied, not a substantive causation burden.
- Rock v. Arkansas, 483 U.S. 44: adversarial testing remains appropriate for admissible evidence; no excuse for unsupported foundation.
- United States v. Downing, 753 F.2d 1224: helpful fit and flexible reliability discussion informs interpretation, not a binding Supreme Court multifactor checklist.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael, No. 97-1709, March 23, 1999: extends original-Rule-702 reliability and fit screening to technical and other specialized knowledge, with case-specific flexible criteria and discretion over the reasonable screening method.

## Authority copied from state/holdings/federal-courts.md

#### General Electric Co. v. Joiner

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-188.

**Decided:** 1997-12-15.

##### Admission and exclusion receive the same abuse-of-discretion review

**Holding:** Appellate courts review a trial court's admission or exclusion of scientific expert testimony for abuse of discretion. Legal error remains independently correctable, historical findings receive their proper protection, and permissible case-specific evidentiary judgments receive deference. The importance of an exclusion to summary judgment supplies no different or asymmetric standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, unanimous.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael, No. 97-1709, March 23, 1999: applies abuse-of-discretion review to both the reasonable reliability-screening method and the final technical-expert evidentiary decision.

##### Reliability includes the inferential connection to the offered proposition

**Holding:** Original Rules 104(a) and 702 permit the judge to examine whether the expert's reasoning reliably connects the scientific material to the conclusion actually offered. Credentials, accepted terminology, or an assertion of a weight-of-evidence method cannot substitute for an adequate connection; screening that connection does not itself decide ultimate causation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, unanimous at the stated legal level.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Under the original Rules 104(a) and 702, the scientific foundation and fit must be established by a preponderance. Neither a study identical to the claimant’s circumstances nor independent proof from each study is required; animal work, nonsignificant associations, competing causes and individually inconclusive materials may support a reliable cumulative inference.

##### The PCB-only exclusion was permissible on this proffer

**Holding:** The described differences between the experimental materials and Joiner's exposure and disease, considered with the identified limitations of the human studies, permitted the District Court to find an insufficiently supported PCB-only inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Stone-Zsela joins reversal without this application; Stevens would vacate for appellate reconsideration.

**Material application:** The experimental dose, subjects and tumor differences and limitations of the human studies permitted exclusion of this PCB-only promotion inference. Admission was not held necessarily forbidden; no finding that PCBs cannot promote lung cancer follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 8–1. The unchallenged furan/dioxin exposure ruling survives, and causation admissibility on that assumed exposure remains open. The full defense summary judgment is not reinstated; no mandatory expert appointment or new hearing follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Daubert's flexible foundation, valid-application and preponderance rules are applied and supplemented by today's appellate-review rule; its distinction between admissibility and ultimate causation remains. Koon, Pierce and Cooter & Gell inform the allocation of legal correction and discretionary application. Ornelas is distinguished by its particular ultimate constitutional inquiry, not narrowed into a universal rule about mixed questions. Frye's mandatory general-acceptance prerequisite remains displaced. Rules 403, 703 and 706 and Rule 56 keep their separate functions. No precedent is overruled.

## Authority copied from state/holdings/federal-courts.md

#### Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1709.
**Decided:** 1999-03-23.

##### Rule 702 requires reliable specialized knowledge, whatever the field

**Holding:** Original Rule 702 requires the trial judge to assess the reliability and fit of scientific, technical and other specialized expert testimony. Experience may supply relevant knowledge, but the expert's professional label does not excuse a reliable connection between the method used and the proposition offered.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part I, unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Daubert's reliability-and-fit duty is extended to the other categories of knowledge expressly covered by original Rule 702; its flexible character remains. Joiner's inferential-connection rule applies to this technical proffer, without converting admissibility into a decision on the underlying claim.

##### The reliability inquiry must fit the proffer and receives abuse-of-discretion review

**Holding:** A trial judge may use Daubert's nonexclusive reliability considerations when they help evaluate the particular expertise, method and application, but need not demand every consideration in every field. #### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1841.
**Decided:** June 12, 1995.

For the other questions in Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Peña, decided June 12, 1995, No. 93-1841. see Federal Courts — Equal-competition standing and continuing injury (Equal competitive opportunity and prospective standing).

##### The common substantial-justification standard

**Holding:** When government changes an individual's competitive opportunity through a racial classification, government must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between the racial treatment and that objective. Government must justify the measure's coverage and the actual burdens it imposes, including its response to concrete, practicable alternatives that would comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. Unsupported assertion, administrative convenience alone, or an inclusive label does not satisfy that burden. Racial hierarchy, stereotypes, hostility, political favoritism, and racial allocation for its own sake are impermissible objectives.

Supported correction of discrimination and exclusion, prevention of their recurrence, and supported efforts to overcome continuing barriers to meaningful market participation are permissible objectives. An adjudicated violation by every implementing office, proof that each beneficiary personally suffered discrimination, and a prior judgment against each private contractor are not universal prerequisites. The government must identify the relevant barrier or condition and explain why the racial measure substantially addresses it. This is constitutional permission within lawful governmental authority, not a constitutional command to adopt preferences or a freestanding power to regulate every private inequality.

This standard governs federal, state and local government alike. A corrective purpose alone does not make a program valid. Metro Broadcasting's lower federal tier is overruled, and Croson's categorical strict-scrutiny formulation is displaced while its demand for relevant evidence is retained.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; these five adopt the complete common standard and its operative qualifications. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The optional incentive differs from STURAA's expenditure command and a mandatory subcontracting duty. The Court preserves Fullilove's supported fractured judgment, Wygant and Paradise, without a general benign-purpose exemption, unconditional concessions, a finding of Gonzales's certification route, or approval of all future applications. The distinct certification and payment limits below remain; conflicting section 8(d) regulations are unresolved.

**Material application:** The compensation clause is an optional incentive, distinct from STURAA's expenditure command or a mandatory subcontracting duty. DBE subcontracts must total more than 10% of the original prime contract; compensation is 10% of approved final DBE subcontract amounts, capped at 1.5% of the original contract for one DBE and 2% for two or more. It compensates the stated assistance and documentation duties and requires certification evidence and a certified executed subcontract copy. Compensation is confined to DBE-performed work; adjustment may apply to non-DBE second-tier work, with full compensation permitted where only insignificant portions are subcontracted and the DBE performs the major portion. No finding is made that every duty was performed or every payment is constitutional.

SBA §8(a) presumes social disadvantage but requires individual proof of economic disadvantage. DOT state certification rebuttably presumes both, and third parties may challenge actual disadvantage. The §8(d) regulations remain in tension: 13 CFR §124.106(b) appears to require individual economic proof while 48 CFR §19.703(a)(2) appears to presume both. Nonminority applicants may establish disadvantage. Other governmental, county or city certification is accepted individually only on the contracting officer's finding of an acceptable and viable certification program. Mandatory §8(a) review, urged but nonmandatory DOT state review, and the absence of an identified separate §8(d) review provision remain distinct. Gonzales's actual route is unknown. The Court does not find irrebuttability, lack of actual disadvantage, or resolve the conflicting §8(d) rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC, 497 U.S. 547 (1990): overrules its selection of a lower tier for benign federal racial classifications; federal sponsorship supplies no exemption from the common standard.
- Fullilove v. Klutznick, 448 U.S. 448 (1980): retains recognition of supported congressional remedial action at its supported scope; no single universal rationale is attributed to the fractured decision.
- City of Richmond v. J. A. Croson Co., 488 U.S. 469 (1989): displaces its categorical strict-scrutiny formulation with the common standard while retaining its demand for relevant evidence.
- Wygant v. Jackson Board of Education, 476 U.S. 267 (1986): retains attention to the actual burdens imposed by racial treatment.
- United States v. Paradise, 480 U.S. 149 (1987): retains attention to flexibility and fit.
- Shaw v. Reno (June 28, 1993): leaves its rejection of the design-only districting claim untouched; the Voting Rights Act's statutory elements also remain unchanged.

##### Vacatur and bounded remand under the common standard

**Holding:** The Tenth Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case is remanded to apply the common standard to the actual program and preserved challenge, respecting concessions and supported findings in their actual scope and allowing further proceedings only as needed under ordinary procedural rules. Vacatur neither holds the arrangement unconstitutional nor directs any injunction; interim or final relief requires its own legal and equitable basis.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices support vacatur and remand. The Court's five-Justice coalition adopts the common-standard directions; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in the judgment on strict-scrutiny grounds and do not join that standard.

**Material application:** The lower judgment, which applied the inherited federal framework, cannot settle the preserved challenge under the common standard without examining the arrangement's operation. Gonzales's certification route is unknown, and the overlapping rules' treatment of economic disadvantage remains unresolved; neither establishes an irrebuttable presumption or a lack of congressional evidence. The Tenth Circuit's recorded stipulation that section 502 satisfied Fullilove's evidentiary requirements and the concession that the agency stayed within delegated authority keep their ordinary force in their actual scope. Courts must neither fabricate an unconditional waiver nor treat the change of standard as erasing evidence or supported findings, and a stipulation under the inherited rule does not necessarily resolve every question under the common standard. Further proceedings require identification of a genuinely unresolved material issue and an explanation of why the existing record, findings or concessions do not resolve it. Existing records may suffice.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fullilove v. Klutznick: preserves the actual scope of the section 502 evidentiary stipulation without treating it as resolution of every question under the common standard.
- Metro Broadcasting, Inc. v. FCC: its federal tier no longer supplies the rule for final judgment; application of the common standard remains necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Tenth Circuit judgment and remand, 9–0, for application of the common standard. The remand directions require the court to identify the actual program and preserved challenge; assess the supported objective and the evidence connecting the measure's beneficiaries, categories and scope to it; examine presumptions, certification routes and review in operation; examine burdens and concrete, practicable alternatives; and examine continued justification. If government cannot establish the required objective, substantial relationship or justification for the burden, the challenged racial treatment fails, and relief addresses the invalid provision or application under ordinary remedial and severability rules. No single disparity ratio, regression method, commissioned study, least-restrictive-means formula, mandatory consultation procedure or permanent judicial monitoring system is constitutionalized. No program redesign, injunction, damages or award of a subcontract is ordered.

#### Peguero v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-9217
**Decided:** 1999-03-02.

##### A mandatory warning error does not restore an appeal that the omission did not cause the defendant to lose

**Holding:** The sentencing judge must give Rule 32's required advice despite a defendant's independent knowledge. But where actual knowledge and an unchallenged voluntary decision not to appeal establish no lost appeal attributable to the omission, that violation alone does not require section 2255 relief; causal loss is distinct from whether an appeal would probably succeed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II has all nine Justices' direct joins.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hohn v. United States:** Applied to distinguish the judicial certificate process and resulting appellate case while preserving statutory issuance conditions; its different temporal reservation is not extended here.
- **Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424; United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780:** Applied to distinguish a consequential collateral injury from formal rule error, without excusing the underlying judicial duty.
- **Rodriquez v. United States, 395 U.S. 327:** Preserved for actual deprivation of an appeal without a likely-success showing; it does not establish causal deprivation on these contrary findings.
- **Bousley v. United States:** Its separation of statutory meaning, constitutional defect and collateral remedy remains; this decision supplies no new constitutional claim or default exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Proper certificate issuance still requires the statutory constitutional showing, but the disputed eligibility assessment does not erase the actual authorization here. Rule 32's judicial warning duty remains mandatory. Section 2255 supplies no automatic restoration for an omission that caused no appeal loss; actual loss and appellate success remain separate inquiries.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Affirmed, 9-0.**

**Supporting:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The sentence remains in force. No appeal is restored, no resentencing is ordered and no new factual hearing is required. The rejected counsel claim remains outside this review.
