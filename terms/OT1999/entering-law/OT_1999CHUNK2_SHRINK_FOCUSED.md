#### Russell v. Burris

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 98-397 and 98-399; companion Citizens for Clean Government v. Russell; judgment below, 146 F.3d 563.
**Decided:** 1999-04-28.

##### Contribution limits require actual anticorruption fit; two candidate categories fail here

**Holding:** Candidate contribution limits must be closely drawn to a sufficiently important interest in preventing corruption or its appearance while preserving meaningful political association. On this record the $300 per-person/per-election ceilings for Governor, Lieutenant Governor, Secretary of State, Treasurer of State, Auditor of State, Attorney General and Commissioner of State Lands, and the $100 ceilings for Arkansas Supreme Court Justice and Court of Appeals Judge, fail that requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, Part I, unanimous.

##### The residual $100 restriction survives the tried categorical challenge

**Holding:** The record does not establish the asserted categorical invalidity of the $100 per-person/per-election ceiling for covered offices outside the seven-office $300 list and the two named appellate judicial offices. This residual class includes other covered judicial offices; rejection of the blanket challenge does not decide a genuinely different office-specific application without its own presented grounds and record.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five Justices).

##### The ordinary-PAC receipt limit survives on this operational record

**Holding:** The $200 annual limit from one person to any one ordinary PAC survives the presented challenge where the tried operational record preserves effective collective advocacy and the restriction is fitted to the actual candidate-conduit channel. It is a per-recipient limit, not one aggregate pool across all committees, and supplies no general approval for materially different recipient functions or demonstrated pooling burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five Justices).

##### The exact small-donor qualification supports a different candidate allowance

**Holding:** Arkansas may permit a qualifying registered small-donor PAC to give $2,500 per candidate per election while ordinary sources face lower ceilings, where that committee receives contributions exclusively from individuals and no more than $25 cumulatively from any individual during a calendar year. The financial distinction is justified by the anticorruption interest and fit of the dispersed-donor structure; it is neither a viewpoint preference nor a rule that any small-donor label suffices.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, Parts II–IV, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five Justices).

**Limits and questions not reached:** The residual $100 class includes covered judicial offices other than the two named appellate offices. No permanent dollar floor, aggregate pool across committees, universal candidate-conduit finding or approval of materially different financing burdens follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm invalidation of the seven named statewide $300 limits and two named appellate-office $100 applications; reverse the residual categorical invalidation and the ordinary-PAC and exact small-donor differential rulings on their stated grounds. No replacement ceiling, refunds, damages or retrospective enforcement direction is ordered; consistent severability treatment remains.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Buckley v. Valeo preserves contribution/expenditure distinctions, sufficient interest and close fit without a permanent dollar floor.
- Day v. Holahan protects actual pooling and requires a supported circumvention fit; the Court distinguishes its burden record rather than overruling it.
- Kentucky Right to Life preserves separate direct-contribution, aggregate-pool and sourcewide-receipt analyses and their actual claim limits.
- California Medical Association v. FEC supports actual candidate-conduit regulation and attention to financing structures; its fractured lowest-protection rationale is not a universal rule.
- Colorado Republican and Clifton preserve genuine independence and require actual candidate connections; a committee label bypasses neither protection.
- Suster preserves judicial impartiality while distinguishing expenditures from contributions and requiring examination of the actual restraint.
- Reform Party and Brower reject resource-parity entitlements while leaving practical prohibitory burdens reviewable.

#### Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. Federal Election Commission, 518 U.S. 604 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-489.
**Decided:** June 26, 1996.

##### Whether the Senate party-expenditure ceiling may reach this independent advertisement

**Holding:** A political party's own campaign advertisement that is genuinely independent of a candidate may not be subjected to the Senate-election expenditure ceiling in 2 U.S.C. §441a(d)(3) merely because its speaker is an official political party. Party identity and a continuing electoral relationship do not, without more, justify applying that ceiling to this independent expression; the adverse enforcement judgment must therefore be vacated.

**Proposition-level authority:** O’Connor’s Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; six direct votes.

**Material application:** The party’s $15,000 pre-nomination advertisement was genuinely independent. Its prior assignment of the whole $103,248 adjusted election allowance explains statutory excess, not constitutional validity. Legitimate corruption and circumvention concerns do not establish an actual candidate connection from party identity alone.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No majority adopts the complete narrower expenditure framework or its prudential reservation rationale (O’Connor, Souter, Breyer), or Kennedy/Scalia/Thomas’s broader coordinated-party-expression protection. No Marks combination is used. Coordinated expression, direct candidate cash, earmarks, republication, sham independence and actual conduits are neither generally immunized nor upheld by implication. No universal bribery-proof, express-advocacy formula, constitutional dollar floor, vagueness or administrative-deference rule.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Adverse enforcement/penalty mandate vacated and remanded, 6–3. The broader Senate-election counterclaim receives no majority constitutional determination; no fixed penalty, damages, refund or compelled new factual trial.

##### Whether party identity supplies the connection required by the alternative contribution theory

**Holding:** For the government's theory that this advertisement is a contribution under §441a(a)(7)(B)(i), party status alone cannot substitute for expenditure made in cooperation, consultation or concert with, or at the request or suggestion of, a candidate, the candidate's authorized political committees or their agents. On the accepted independence premise, that theory cannot preserve the adverse enforcement judgment under §441a(a)(2).

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six-Justice Part I coalition.

**Material application:** A categorical agency characterization is not a factual finding of consultation; competing assertions about novelty and noncontest establish neither forfeiture nor concession. Transfers among national, State, district, local and subordinate same-party committees are exempt from §441a(a)(1) and (2). Candidate-material dissemination, distribution or republication under (a)(7)(B)(ii), and original-source attribution for earmarked/intermediary contributions under (a)(8), have distinct predicates absent here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutionality of a transaction with an actual connection, all interactions with (d)(1)’s notwithstanding clause, presidential allowances and other House limits are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1:** Its distinction between independent expenditures and candidate contributions supplies the present independent-advertisement rule. Its treatment of the party provision's Fifth Amendment challenge did not decide this First Amendment question. The general distinction remains in force.
- **FEC v. National Conservative Political Action Committee, 470 U.S. 480:** Its protection of genuinely independent political advocacy supports the result; it supplies no categorical immunity for actual contribution channels.
- **Day v. Holahan, May 15, 1995:** Its controlling protection of meaningful intermediary association and preservation of realistic conduit regulation inform the required distinction. Its recipient category excluded political-party units, and its responsive-financing judgment had no unified controlling rationale. Neither is converted into an earlier decision of this party-spending question.
- **FEC v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life, Inc., 479 U.S. 238:** Its protection of political expression and its nonprofit setting remain distinct; the Court does not rest today's judgment on a universal express-advocacy formula or extend the nonprofit exception to every entity.

### Candidate contribution limits and anticorruption fit

**Governing question:** What justifies direct candidate-contribution limits and differentiated source allowances.

**Current rule:** Candidate contribution limits must be closely drawn to a sufficiently important interest in preventing corruption or its appearance while preserving meaningful political association. No completed bribe is necessary, and equality of political influence is not an independent justification. Amount and inflation comparisons establish no permanent constitutional boundary; actual campaign scale, fundraising burden and cumulative restrictions matter. Under the adjudicated Arkansas classification, a registered committee receiving contributions exclusively from individuals and no more than $25 cumulatively from any individual during a calendar year may receive a $2,500 per-candidate, per-election contribution allowance while ordinary sources face lower ceilings. The actual dispersed-donor structure and its anticorruption fit, rather than a small-donor label, support the distinction.

**Authority by component:**

- Direct-candidate interest and close fit: Russell v. Burris — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Contribution limits require actual anticorruption fit; two candidate categories fail here”, April 28, 1999; O’Connor Part I, all nine Justices.
- Exact dispersed-donor classification: Russell — Holdings, “The exact small-donor qualification supports a different candidate allowance”, April 28, 1999; O’Connor Part IV with Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Russell, April 28, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Office-specific dollar applications establish no general floor or safe harbor. Every small-donor qualification remains necessary to the adjudicated distinction; no unlimited candidate-giving right, immunity for sham structures or automatic validity of materially different classifications follows. A failed categorical challenge does not resolve a genuinely different office-specific application.

**Related current rules:** Intermediary contribution limits and meaningful association separately governs committee receipt restrictions and actual candidate-conduit fit.

### Intermediary contribution limits and meaningful association

**Governing question:** Fit of limits on contributions received by political committees and political funds.

**Current rule:** Contributions remain distinct from independent expenditures. A broadly restrictive ceiling on contributions received by political committees or funds must preserve meaningful collective political association and fit an identified corruption or circumvention mechanism. Realistic routes around valid candidate limits may be regulated; an insufficiently fitted ceiling cannot be sustained merely by invoking circumvention. The invalidation of the reviewed $100 aggregate annual ceiling establishes no permanent constitutional dollar minimum. Annual duration, actual recipient function, per-recipient versus aggregate operation, amount and demonstrated effect on collective advocacy must be considered together. Other available committees and aggregate political success do not alone cure a burden on the chosen association.

**Authority by component:**

- This $100 intermediary-contribution ceiling lacks sufficient fit: This $100 intermediary-contribution ceiling lacks sufficient fit, 1995-05-15. Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine direct votes, at this stated functional application.

- Russell v. Burris — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “The ordinary-PAC receipt limit survives on this operational record”, 1999-04-28; O’Connor Parts II–IV with Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part III supplies the actual-channel and operational considerations.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-05-15; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** The reviewed Day recipient class and its exclusions remain as originally stated. Russell supplies no general approval of a $200 intermediary receipt ceiling, no permanent dollar floor, no immunity for sham or materially different structures and no unlimited candidate-contribution authority. Genuine independence and actual candidate-conduit status retain their separate requirements.
