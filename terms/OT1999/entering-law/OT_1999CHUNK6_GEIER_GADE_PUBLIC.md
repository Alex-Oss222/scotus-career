### Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, No. 90-1676

Merits decision, June 18, 1992

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 23, 1992. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit. Case reference: [505 U.S. 88](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-505/pdf/USREPORTS-505-88.pdf). In *National Solid Wastes Management Association v. Killian*, 918 F.2d 671 (November 14, 1990), the court of appeals vacated and remanded the District Court's disposition; rehearing was denied January 29, 1991. It rejected unapproved state regulation that directly, clearly, and substantially regulated occupational safety. It expressly condemned Illinois's 4,000-hour experience requirement and left undisturbed the unappealed District Court ruling invalidating the requirement of training within Illinois.

The question is whether §18 of the Occupational Safety and Health Act automatically displaces Illinois's hazardous-waste worker training and licensing standards on an issue covered by a federal standard whenever Illinois lacks an approved state plan, including standards that also protect the public.

OSHA's hazardous-waste standards require initial instruction, supervised experience, certification, and refresher training. Illinois requires licensing, training, examination, and, for equipment operators, 4,000 hours of experience to protect workers and the public. Illinois has no approved §18 plan. The court of appeals' direct-and-substantial overlap rule went beyond the District Court's purpose-based approach. The record contains no completed provision-by-provision review under the rule the Court adopts today. All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court reverses in part and remands, by a vote of 5–4.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Categorical preemption solely from occupational overlap and absence of an approved state plan | Reversed, 5–4; remanded | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, Thomas | White, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy | Examine the challenged provisions under the shared rejection of automatic displacement; preserve independently supported and unappealed rulings; neither wholesale enforcement nor blanket invalidation is authorized. |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Section 18 does not automatically displace compatible supplementation

**Controlling proposition:** Section 18 does not automatically preempt a supplementary state occupational-safety standard merely because a federal standard covers the same issue and the State lacks an approved plan. The categorical overlap rule is reversed; provisions must instead be examined for an actual basis of displacement, without treating either dual public purpose or occupational overlap as conclusive.

**Authority:** Souter’s plurality, joined by Blackmun, Stevens and Thomas, and Stone-Zsela’s concurrence in the judgment independently and expressly support this limited negative proposition and its remand consequence: five of nine Justices. No complete opinion commands five joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that overlap alone does not establish congressional displacement. Rice requires a clear basis before traditional state police authority yields, and English makes the statutory inquiry essential rather than treating all concurrent regulation as conflict. Section 18(a) preserves authority where no federal standard exists; it does not necessarily prohibit every additional standard where one exists. Section 18(b), including its enacted heading, gives the approval process substantial work when a State assumes the federal regulatory role. The approval and withdrawal provisions can operate on that replacement arrangement without establishing a general ban on compatible supplementation. The contrary reading treats an asserted preference for one regulator as if Congress had enacted it. Federal minimum training and additional state training are not incompatible merely because both must be satisfied. The Court likewise gives no immunity to a conflicting law simply because it protects the public. Which challenged provisions have an actual basis for displacement remains for further proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator Corp., 331 U.S. 218 — applied at majority level for requiring a clear basis before displacement of traditional police regulation.
- English v. General Electric Co., 496 U.S. 72 — applied for identifying the actual statutory basis and scope of preemption; no universal prohibition of dual regulation follows.
- Florida Lime & Avocado Growers, Inc. v. Paul, 373 U.S. 132 — preserved; impossibility is an established conflict ground, but four Justices’ proposed exclusive use of that ground is not adopted by five.
- Hines v. Davidowitz, 312 U.S. 52 — not overruled; no majority treats an assumed anti-duplication objective as sufficient to decide this statute. Stone-Zsela preserves demonstrated interference with a clearly enacted operation.
- Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc. (June 1, 1992) — distinguished; its express airline wording does not decide §18.

**Fractured-decision authority:** No Marks extraction is necessary for the expressly shared negative holding: five Justices state it at the same operative scope. Souter’s broader permission whenever simultaneous compliance is physically possible has only four votes. Stone-Zsela’s reservation for demonstrated conflict, a clear ceiling or exclusivity receives no additional formal join and is not a new Court-wide conflict test. The disagreement is not erased by calling one opinion narrower; no broader positive rule is extracted from it.

#### Limits and Current-Law Effect

An approved state plan is not an automatic prerequisite to every compatible supplementary occupational-safety standard merely because a federal standard exists. The Court rejects the categorical rule, while leaving provision-specific review for further proceedings. It does not endorse every compatible state license or decide that any particular provision survives review. No five-Justice opinion adopts a complete replacement test for conflict. Existing conflict principles remain in force; actual statutory ceilings, exclusivity, savings provisions, and proved incompatibility must be respected on their own terms. Section 4(b)(4), 29 U.S.C. §653(b)(4), remains operative: it provides that nothing in the Act supersedes or affects any workers'-compensation law, or enlarges, diminishes, or otherwise affects the common-law or statutory rights, duties, or liabilities of employers and employees under any law with respect to employees' work-related injuries, diseases, or death. This savings clause does not decide the occupational-license preemption question presented here.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Souter, joined by Justices Blackmun, Stevens, and Thomas, would permit concurrent regulation whenever compliance with both sets of requirements is physically possible. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in reversal but preserves displacement based on demonstrated conflict or a clearly enacted federal ceiling or exclusive arrangement. Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices White and Scalia, would affirm because the integrated state-plan scheme excludes unapproved regulation of covered issues. Justice Kennedy would separately affirm on the statute's express structure, while rejecting a broad judicial inference of conflict from duplication.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court review is complete. The Seventh Circuit's judgment is reversed insofar as it establishes automatic displacement from occupational overlap and the absence of an approved plan, and the case is remanded for further proceedings. Its ruling on the 4,000-hour requirement cannot rest solely on that rejected rationale. The unappealed District Court invalidation of training within Illinois remains undisturbed. Preserved alternative grounds, the coverage of individual provisions, and severability remain for lawful proceedings. The Court makes no Commerce Clause merits ruling, authorizes neither wholesale enforcement nor blanket invalidation, and sets no mandate-issuance date. No further Supreme Court matter is retained; no later rulemaking, license, filing, or hearing is established.
