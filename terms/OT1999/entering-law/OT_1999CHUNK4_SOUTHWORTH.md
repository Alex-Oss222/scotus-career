## Standards and Tests selection: Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

### Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

**Governing question:** Viewpoint-neutral access for private religious expression and the distinct establishment inquiry in the forums and benefits actually opened.

**Current rule:** When a public school admits private family-life and child-rearing discussion to an after-hours forum, it cannot exclude an otherwise eligible presentation solely for its religious viewpoint, even assuming a nonpublic forum. Neutral restrictions outside the forum's actual subjects remain possible. Equal access to voluntary, publicly accessible after-hours films without school sponsorship does not establish religion. That establishment holding concerns private speech, not an official devotional program; it supplies no replacement for the inherited establishment framework or universal ruling on worship restrictions. A State may not categorically exclude an otherwise eligible private unattended religious display from a forum actually opened to comparable expression merely because its message is religious or its location adjoins or lies within a capitol. Pinette’s access holding includes the proposal’s offered private-attribution safeguard; it establishes neither adequacy of a later sign nor a comprehensive establishment test. Chabad separately holds that an interior capitol location does not establish religion when actual context makes the expression clearly private. Actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preference or obscured private sponsorship can alter that conclusion; no forum label or unspecified sign decides attribution. In a generally available, privately edited student-publication program, a religious editorial viewpoint cannot exclude an otherwise eligible publication from printing support. Its secular expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent attribution and controlled vendor payments together permit that benefit; neutrality alone does not authorize devotional financing or governmental worship.

In a broad after-hours educational and community forum, an otherwise comparable private meeting cannot be excluded solely for its worship form; exact secular sacramental counterparts are not required. Genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral operational and activity limits remain available. An admitted instructional subject likewise cannot exclude comparable religious instruction merely while permitting religious discussion. Independent private worship in rented school space does not by itself establish religion; actual governmental adoption must be shown rather than inferred from religious identity or location.

## Standards and Tests selection: Protection of objectors' compulsory bar dues before ideological use

### Protection of objectors' compulsory bar dues before ideological use

**Governing question:** What protection an integrated bar must provide after a member makes known continuing opposition to financing nongermane ideological activity.

**Current rule:** A continuing general objection suffices; the bar may not require repeated objections to each announced position or leave the reasonably disputed share available for use until a new objection arrives. It must give adequate advance explanation, protect that share before use through a reasonable advance reduction or adequate interest-bearing escrow, and provide reasonably prompt impartial review. The bar retains the burden to justify chargeability. Estimates and protection of a reasonably sufficient amount may address uncertainty about future activities; later reimbursement and interest do not cure temporary compelled ideological financing. No universal opt-in system is required.

**Authority by component:**

- Continuing objection, advance protection, chargeability burden, and retained alternatives: Gibson v. Florida Bar — Timing and scope of protection, December 4, 1991; Stone's opinion joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Gibson*, December 4, 1991.

**Holdings navigation:** Gibson v. Florida Bar, 502 U.S. 104 (1991) — Compulsory bar dues.

## Standards and Tests selection: Impartiality of balanced bar-dues arbitration panels

### Impartiality of balanced bar-dues arbitration panels

**Governing question:** Whether compulsory bar membership alone disqualifies arbitrators reviewing dues objections.

**Current rule:** Bar membership alone does not establish constitutional bias where the procedure gives the bar and objector one selection each and requires a mutually selected chair or judicial appointment if agreement fails. Actual bias, a direct stake, obstructive delay, and ineffectiveness in operation remain reviewable. The rule rejects a facial membership-only disqualification and does not conclusively approve any particular panel's operation.

**Authority by component:**

- Balanced selection and membership-only objection; retained operational challenges: Gibson v. Florida Bar — Impartial review, December 4, 1991; Stone's opinion joined on this proposition by all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** *Gibson*, December 4, 1991.

**Holdings navigation:** Gibson v. Florida Bar, 502 U.S. 104 (1991) — Compulsory bar dues.

## Standards and Tests selection: Nonconsensual union-fee arbitration and federal review

### Nonconsensual union-fee arbitration and federal review

**Governing question:** Whether objecting nonmembers must exhaust union-provided fee arbitration before suing.

**Current rule:** An employee who did not agree to arbitrate need not exhaust a union-provided impartial arbitration procedure before bringing a compulsory-fee claim in federal court. The union’s duty to offer a prompt impartial challenge procedure does not make that private procedure a compulsory prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- Nonconsenting nonmembers and the distinction between offering and mandating arbitration: Air Line Pilots Association v. Miller, No. 97-428. — controlling Holdings, 1998-05-26.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-05-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Express statutory exhaustion, appropriate administrative exhaustion and an actual arbitration agreement retain their independent operation.

**Holdings navigation:** Air Line Pilots Association v. Miller — Agency-fee arbitration and nonconsenting objectors.

## Standards and Tests selection: Generic promotion assessments in collective agricultural marketing

### Generic promotion assessments in collective agricultural marketing

**Governing question:** Compelled funding of generic product promotion within comprehensive collective agricultural regulation.

**Current rule:** A comprehensive marketing program may charge regulated producers for generic, nonideological product promotion germane to its substantially collective economic functions when messages are not attributed to objecting producers and independent advertising and criticism remain free. In that combination, Central Hudson's restrictions-on-commercial-message framework does not require invalidating the assessment. Common benefit, majority approval, or a claim that funding is nonexpressive regulation alone does not justify it.

**Authority by component:**
- Collective-program permission and conditions: Glickman, Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc. — Germane generic promotion may be funded as part of this collective marketing program, O'Connor with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (six), 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** Glickman, 1997-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not authorize advertising-only programs in otherwise independent markets, ideological advocacy, false statements, viewpoint penalties, personal attribution or interference with independent speech. Compelled funding remains capable of implicating expression; no universal advertising-effectiveness trial or finding of superior advertising is required.

**Holdings navigation:** Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc. (1997-06-25) — Germane generic promotion may be funded as part of this collective marketing program.

## Standards and Tests selection: Lemon test

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement do not cure public payment for demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation. The inquiry concerns the instructional function purchased; religious identity alone does not establish the defect, and genuinely independent private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

**Authority by component:**

- General inquiry and operative qualifications: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function, 1997-06-23. Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.
- Object of the inquiry, evidence role and Holding I limits: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function (controlling explanation and precedent treatment), 1997-06-23; the same five Justices.
- Safeguarded secular instruction: Agostini v. Felton — II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location, 1997-06-23. Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

- Jackson v. Benson — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction”, 1999-03-16; O’Connor Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Jackson v. Benson, March 16, 1999, for integrated provider-cost instructional payments; Agostini’s general inquiry retains its June 23, 1997 effective date.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

**Former-rule note:** Lemon's independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs ceased to govern as separate or fallback tests on June 23, 1997 (Agostini v. Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises (Agostini v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Open question:** Jackson resolves only the demonstrated provider-cost arrangement purchasing integrated devotional instruction; it adopts no universal voucher prohibition or exclusion of religious providers and no constitutional entitlement to subsidy. Other voucher arrangements, monuments, legislative prayer, general accommodation and other aid retain their actual governing rules. Witters and Mueller remain effective for their actual private-benefit relationships.

**Preserved specific holdings:** Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

**Related current rules:** Denominational selection for general governmental authority; Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits. Each states existing specific holdings that continue to govern their established relationships under this test.

**Holdings navigation:** Agostini v. Felton (1997-06-23) — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function; II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location. [Jackson v. Benson — Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction](HOLDINGS.md#parental-routing-does-not-justify-the-demonstrated-purchase-of-integrated-devotional-instruction-1).

#### Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-329.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

##### Religious viewpoint in the private publication program

**Holding:** The University may not exclude an otherwise eligible private student publication from its generally available printing benefit because its editorial viewpoint is religious. The rule applies to this privately edited, independently attributed publication program on its otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms; it does not require funding every religious activity or every subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt the program-specific viewpoint rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Recognition, facilities, distribution and money remain distinct; not every public expenditure creates a speech forum. The publication remains independently edited and attributed under required disclaimers and approved vendor-payment terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: preserves limited-forum definition while forbidding viewpoint discrimination.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches; Widmar v. Vincent: apply private religious-viewpoint equality without assuming that facility access decides every subsidy.
- Regan v. Taxation With Representation: distinguishes definition of a government financing program from discriminatory exclusion within this opened private-publication arrangement.

##### Permissible printing support on the actual program terms

**Holding:** Providing this neutral printing benefit to the otherwise eligible private religious publication does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Its secular student-expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent editorial attribution and controlled vendor payments matter together; neutrality alone is not a universal authorization of devotional financing or governmental sponsorship of worship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas join this concrete program conclusion. Lemon remains governing law; no new universal framework is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry remains; neutral availability alone neither authorizes unrestricted devotional financing nor governmental worship. Compelled-fee challenges, unrestricted religious aid and particular awards are outside review.

The denial concerned religious activity, defined by the Guidelines as primarily promoting or manifesting belief in or about deity or ultimate reality, rather than an established finding of religious-organization ineligibility. The organization rule separately concerns devotion as organizational purpose. Political restrictions separately exclude principal-purpose lobbying and candidate electioneering while permitting incidental or insubstantial advocacy, nonpartisan study/research and ideological views; those permissions are not exceptions to the religious-activity rule. Permitted ability-to-perform membership limits likewise remain distinct. Abandoned free-exercise/state individual-rights claims and unchallenged equal-protection intent findings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills: preserves the actual antecedent-law remand; supplies no religious-aid merits holding here.

## Copied Holdings selection: Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc.

#### Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1184.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

##### Germane generic promotion may be funded as part of this collective marketing program

**Holding:** A comprehensive agricultural marketing program may require regulated producers to finance generic, nonideological product promotion germane to its substantially collective economic functions when the message is not attributed to dissenting producers and they remain free to advertise and criticize independently. On this combination of facts, Central Hudson's framework for restrictions on a speaker's commercial message does not require invalidation of the assessment; common benefit or majority approval alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt this program-specific ground, its qualifications and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The orders already replace substantial independent price, quality, supply and marketing decisions with collective regulation. Promotion is a germane expense of that system; the decision does not find generic advertising superior to individual advertising.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Compelled funding can implicate speech without ideology or attribution. Advertising-only arrangements in otherwise independent markets, ideological or false content, viewpoint penalties, personal attribution and interference with independent expression remain outside the holding; there is no universal commercial-speech or economic-regulation exception.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The reviewed First Amendment judgment is reversed, 6–3, and remanded. Unreviewed or resolved statutory and administrative matters are not reopened, and no collection award is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Abood, Keller and Lehnert supply germane common-function principles, while Gibson preserves safeguards for disputed nongermane dues. Hurley is distinguished because no private expressive product or authorship is compelled. Rosenberger reserved compulsory-fee questions. Central Hudson, 44 Liquormart and Turner retain their speech-restriction rules without requiring an efficacy trial for this germane collective expense.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

### Strout v. Albanese — 2000-02-28

[Record](../records/strout-v-albanese_merits_2000-02-28.md)

#### Event, question and statutory setting

**Strout v. Albanese**; review of 178 F.3d 57 (1st Cir. 1999). Certiorari granted November 1, 1999; argued January 24, 2000; decided February 28, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, affirming the District of Maine's judgment at 13 F. Supp. 2d 112.

The granted questions concern whether excluding otherwise eligible sectarian schools and their students from a generally available tuition-assistance program solely because the schools are religious violates free exercise, and whether establishment principles require, permit or forbid that exclusion when assistance reaches schools through independent parental choice. Three families in Minot, where no secondary school was maintained, sought ordinary St. Dominic's tuition. Their application concerns integrated sectarian instruction, not a separately identified secular service or expression forum. The case was resolved on cross-motions for summary judgment without a genuine dispute of material fact.

Section 2951 retains basic school approval, nonsectarian status in accordance with the First Amendment, incorporation under Maine or United States law, reporting and auditing under sections 2952 and 2953 and requirements adopted under section 2954. Schools with at least 60 percent publicly funded students, measured by the previous year's October and April average enrollment, must participate in statewide assessment. Upon a school unit's request, the private school must release copies of all student records for students transferring from that private school to the requesting unit. The repealed fourth condition is not restored. Section 5204(4) applies when the resident unit neither maintains a secondary school nor contracts for secondary-school privileges. It permits attendance at an approved private school, a public school in an adjoining unit accepting tuition students, or an approved school in another state or country, subject to receiving-school permission. The resident unit must pay tuition up to the legal rate. The Department's acceptance of schools' self-identification without a classification dispute is a lower-court finding about administration, not a statutory command.

#### Maine may refuse the integrated devotional tuition presented here

**Holding and operative rule:** The Religion Clauses permit Maine to refuse public payment for the integrated devotional instructional function presented by this ordinary-tuition application. Independent parental choice and neutral student eligibility do not themselves justify public purchase of demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Jackson requires attention to the instructional function purchased; Agostini applies that inquiry within the governing, unified Lemon test. The parents selected St. Dominic's, and the requested ordinary tuition of approximately $3,850 was below the $4,900 statutory ceiling. That distinguishes the amount formula from Jackson's provider operating-and-debt costs, but does not separate the devotional work in the integrated program for which payment is sought. Maine is not buying an identified interpreter, secular remedial service, or controlled printing benefit. Rosenberger preserves its independently directed publication program; it does not make every publicly financed activity a speech forum. The actual funded function therefore supports refusal of this tuition without prohibiting the families' private educational choice. Neither a school's religious name nor its affiliation alone establishes the character of its work. The Court does not approve a universal religious-provider exclusion, decide all voucher arrangements, or create a constitutional entitlement to every education subsidy.

#### Free exercise receives its own inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Refusing this public purchase of integrated devotional instruction does not, in this application, violate the Free Exercise Clause. The conclusion does not depend on whether attending the school is a central religious tenet and does not authorize religious hostility, exclusion from a neutral private-expression forum, or categorical rejection of genuinely severable secular work solely because its provider is religious.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Lukumi requires scrutiny of religious targeting; Shahar preserves an independent free-exercise inquiry and rejects theological compulsion as a prerequisite to sincere exercise. Calling a program secular cannot by itself answer that inquiry. Here the challenged benefit is public purchase of the ordinary integrated instructional package, and the State may refuse that financed devotional function while the parents remain free to choose it privately. The lower court's observation that no parent described attendance as a central tenet does not defeat the claim, and the Court does not adopt that reasoning. Agostini protects its separately administered secular service despite religious location, and the private forum decisions protect genuinely private religious expression on their actual terms. Those distinct relationships remain intact; this tuition request presents neither one. The Court therefore affirms on the actual financing ground rather than an affiliation-based presumption or an independent-prong Lemon test.

#### The affirmance is limited to the granted claims and this application

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment sustaining the challenged refusal of integrated tuition is affirmed within the granted Establishment and Free Exercise questions. The decision does not reopen the ungranted speech, equal-protection or parental-liberty claims, the unchallenged dismissals of local defendants, or distinct applications for reliably separated secular services.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The cross-motions were decided on an agreed absence of genuine material factual dispute. No remand is required to adjudicate a different benefit application not before the Court or to restructure the school. The statutory payment duty for eligible schools remains subject to its independent approval, receiving-school permission and tuition-rate conditions. Affirmance leaves the challenged denial in place; it awards no tuition, damages or recoupment, orders no transfer or school closure, and imposes no statewide funding cutoff.

#### Precedent treatment

- **Jackson v. Benson:** Applied to the actual integrated instructional function; its provider-cost formula is distinguished in amount, without making capped tuition sufficient to separate devotional work. Its limits preserving private benefits and secular services remain.
- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing unified Lemon inquiry and to the distinction between integrated devotional work and separately controlled secular instruction; no categorical inference follows from religious location.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues only through Agostini's unified inquiry; the former independent purpose, effect and entanglement prongs are not revived.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its controlled printing benefit for independently directed expression remains valid; that relationship does not determine the ordinary tuition purchase here.
- **Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah:** Its neutrality and religious-targeting inquiry is preserved; refusal of the demonstrated devotional purchase is not a license for hostile religious discrimination.
- **Shahar v. Bowers:** Applied to the independent free-exercise inquiry and the irrelevance of theological compulsion to sincere exercise.
- **Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind:** Its actual individual vocational-benefit relationship remains distinct; it supplies no automatic answer for integrated tuition.
- **Mueller v. Allen:** Its general tax-deduction relationship remains distinct and is not displaced by this application.
- **Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District:** Its antecedent-law remand supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding or general tuition entitlement.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its bounded private-expression and worship-access protection remains intact; forum access differs from purchasing integrated instruction.

#### Resulting law and limits

Effective February 28, 2000, Jackson's funded-function rule applies to the integrated ordinary-tuition application presented here despite the tuition cap and parental choice. Agostini's unified Lemon inquiry continues; its former independent prongs do not return. The separate Free Exercise inquiry rejects theological centrality as a prerequisite, but does not require public purchase of this devotional instructional function. Religious identity alone remains insufficient to establish the financing defect. Genuinely independent private benefits, neutral private-expression forums and reliably separated secular services retain their actual protections. The decision settles this application, not every permitted or required tuition arrangement.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's application and emphasizes the distinction between paying for devotional instruction and allowing genuinely private religious expression. Parental designation does not turn a public expenditure into private money. At the same time, an otherwise eligible, genuinely severable secular service cannot be denied payment solely because its provider is religious, and religious expression in a genuinely neutral forum involving no governmental financing, control, selection or attribution must receive equal treatment. Agostini supplies the secular-service distinction; the private-forum decisions protect private expression. These qualifications preserve lawful separate relationships, not a general right to tuition. No such distinct service or forum application is before the Court.

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas.** They would reverse the exclusion as applied and remand for otherwise lawful eligibility and payment proceedings. The actual benefit is ordinary tuition, capped by statute and here below that cap, selected through independent parental choice at a private school. They distinguish that individual educational benefit from Jackson's provider operating-and-debt-cost formula, while maintaining their disagreement with Jackson's attribution analysis. Rosenberger protects genuinely independent private activity within an available benefit; Witters supports the distinction between a private beneficiary's choice and the State's devotional direction; Lukumi requires scrutiny of excluding religious choices from that benefit. Maine's public-substitute characterization does not, for these Justices, overcome the actual independent direction and capped tuition. They would not excuse sham parental choice, governmental devotional direction, or other independent statutory ineligibility, and would order no immediate subsidy or entitlement to every tuition program.

Supreme Court merits review of the granted Religion Clause questions is complete. The First Circuit's judgment remains in force at the stated scope. No new lower-court factfinding, statutory redesign or subsidy proceeding is directed by the affirmance.
