## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory priorities and claim-specific equitable subordination

### Statutory priorities and claim-specific equitable subordination

**Governing question:** Whether §510(c) permits a court to reduce rank because an allowed penalty competes with compensatory creditors.

**Current rule:** Statutory priority does not immunize a claim from §510(c), and lack of priority does not itself justify subordination. After notice and a hearing, §510(c) permits distributional subordination of all or part of an allowed claim to another allowed claim, or an allowed interest to another allowed interest, under principles of equitable subordination, and transfer of a lien securing a subordinated claim. A legally sufficient, supported claim-specific ground is required; noncompensatory character, estate insufficiency and reduced recovery for innocent or value-giving creditors cannot alone replace Congress’s distribution choices, even when described as individual balancing. This limit applies to both postpetition administrative penalties and prepetition nonpriority penalties.

**Authority by component:**

- Noland — Parts I–II, real statutory equity and forbidden class replacement, 1996-05-13; Thomas for all nine.
- CFI — Part II, the same boundary within the general-unsecured class, 1996-06-20; Souter for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** CFI, June 20, 1996, for application to nonpriority penalties; Noland’s administrative-priority rule effective May 13, 1996.

**Controlling construction:** Allowed §503(b)(1)(C) postpetition tax penalties retain case-era §507(a)(1) priority after conversion through §348(d)’s §503(b) exception. Section 726(a)(4)’s fourth-rank category instead requires a fine, penalty, forfeiture or multiple/exemplary/punitive damages arising before the earlier of the relief order or trustee appointment, and only to the extent noncompensatory. Section 726 distribution remains expressly subject to §510.

**Open question:** Misconduct’s universal necessity remains unresolved; no entitlement without misconduct or frozen catalogue of equitable grounds follows. Rejection of a class-based reason neither orders a new misconduct hearing nor establishes a different ground, final payment or tax priority.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Noland, 517 U.S. 535; United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc., 518 U.S. 213 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Actual statutory operation in bankruptcy tax characterization

### Actual statutory operation in bankruptcy tax characterization

**Governing question:** Whether an assessment is a tax entitled to Bankruptcy Code priority.

**Current rule:** Absent an applicable statutory direction adopting an external classification, the exaction’s actual statutory operation controls federal bankruptcy characterization. A tax label and a deterrent purpose are each insufficient alone. The §4971(a) funding-default assessment at issue operates as a noncompensatory penalty because it sanctions failure of an independent pension-funding obligation without satisfying that obligation or replacing pension/PBGC liability; it receives no pre-1994 §507(a)(7)(E) excise-tax priority. The related-penalty route under §507(a)(7)(G) independently requires a qualifying related claim and compensation for actual pecuniary loss. An external provision expressly directing Title 11 tax treatment cannot be disregarded solely because the Bankruptcy Code lacks a reciprocal cross-reference. Section 412(n)(4)(C)’s incorporation of rules similar to ERISA §4068(c), including Title 11 treatment, must be construed with the specified lien conditions and applicable pre-1994 §507(a)(7) requirements before priority is granted. The tax direction concerns an amount with respect to which the specified lien is imposed; the existence and timing of that lien and the effect of the stay remain necessary unresolved predicates in the adjudicated matter.

**Authority by component:**

- CFI — Part I, text-and-operation tax characterization, 1996-06-20; Souter and all participating Justices except Thomas (eight).

- PBGC v. CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “The actual tax-treatment connection must be read with its lien conditions”, 1999-05-13; Souter Parts I and III with Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** PBGC v. CF&I, May 13, 1999, for express external Title 11 incorporation; the separate §4971 assessment rule remains effective June 20, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** Minimum-funding waivers can remove the deficiency underlying either the ordinary or additional assessment; the distinction is not a finding that only the additional assessment is avoidable. Congress may expressly assign priority. This supplies no general Lorber checklist, constitutional punishment classification, disallowance, dischargeability determination or independent ground of equitable subordination.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc., 518 U.S. 213 (1996). [Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation v. CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc. — The actual tax-treatment connection must be read with its lien conditions](HOLDINGS.md#the-actual-tax-treatment-connection-must-be-read-with-its-lien-conditions-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: APA persuasion burdens and statutory departures

### APA persuasion burdens and statutory departures

**Governing question:** Who loses when evidence is genuinely balanced in an adjudication governed by APA §556(d).

**Current rule:** Unless a controlling statute otherwise provides, §556(d)’s burden of proof assigns the burden of persuasion to the proponent of the order; in the proceedings governed here, the proponent must establish the claim by a preponderance and loses at genuine equipoise. Burdens of producing evidence, lawful presumptions and separately allocated defenses remain distinct. The Longshore Act’s incorporation of APA adjudication is not displaced by §23’s evidentiary flexibility or its treatment of corroborated declarations of deceased employees. In the black-lung setting, even assuming statutory authority for an otherwise valid regulatory departure, the invoked remedial-policy provision does not establish an ultimate tie-breaking rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Persuasion allocation: Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries — The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order, 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six participating Justices directly adopt this allocation, its application to both proceedings, and the limited treatment of prior precedent; no aggregation of different grounds is necessary.
- Longshore incorporation despite evidentiary flexibility: Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries — The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation, 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the Longshore incorporation holding and apply it to both the lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims in Maher Terminals.
- No established black-lung departure: Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries — The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA, 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the actual-regulation ground, the express reservation of broader delegated power, and the bounded administrative remedy.

**Present operation effective:** Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, 1994-06-20.

**Exceptions and limits:** Transportation Management’s contrary description of the APA burden is disapproved; its distinct mixed-motive defense allocation is not overruled. The full scope of power to create a black-lung exception is left open.

**Holdings navigation:** The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order; The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation; The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA.

#### United States v. Noland, 517 U.S. 535

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-323.
**Decided:** May 13, 1996.

##### Administrative penalties after conversion and susceptibility to equitable subordination

**Holding:** Allowed §503(b)(1)(C) postpetition tax penalties retain case-era §507(a)(1) administrative priority after conversion because §348(d) expressly excepts §503(b) claims from its ordinary treatment of post-order, preconversion claims. Priority does not immunize them from lawful §510(c) equitable subordination: §726 makes distribution subject to §510.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 726(a)(4)'s fourth rank applies to covered fines, penalties, forfeitures, and multiple, exemplary, or punitive damages arising before the earlier of the order for relief or appointment of a trustee, and only insofar as they do not compensate actual pecuniary loss. It does not demote every noncompensatory penalty. Section 510(c), after notice and a hearing, permits all-or-part distributional subordination of an allowed claim to another allowed claim, or an allowed interest to another allowed interest, and transfer to the estate of a lien securing the subordinated claim. Both priority and the express exception retain effect; their coexistence establishes no conflicting congressional choices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No §510(a) agreement, §510(b) securities ground, or lien-transfer basis is established here. Susceptibility to equitable subordination does not prove a sufficient ground for its exercise or freeze equitable principles into a closed catalogue.

##### Insufficiency of the lower courts' class-based reasons

**Holding:** Section 510(c) does not authorize subordinating this statutory penalty class merely because the penalties compensate no pecuniary loss and payment reduces recovery for creditors who supplied value. The lower courts' stated reasons are legally inadequate even though they expressly disclaimed automatic subordination of every penalty. Lawful claim-specific equity remains available, but the Court establishes no other sufficient ground on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same unanimous Court opinion.

**Material application:** Noncompensation, insufficient assets, losses to value-giving creditors, and retained priority for tax and interest recur whenever this class competes with compensatory claims in an insolvent estate. Those reasons express the category-wide preference Congress's administrative classification rejects; calling them individualized balancing does not supply a different equitable circumstance. Statutory susceptibility and legislative statements about continued equitable development do not create a replacement ranking. Innocent-creditor and financing concerns cannot alone displace priority or bypass separate financing conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither requires misconduct universally nor establishes an affirmative entitlement to subordination without it. Permissible development is not frozen at 1978; particular inequitable conduct or another recognized claim-specific ground remains addressable when lawfully presented and proved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand, 9–0. The same class reasons cannot preserve subordination under a new label. Any other consideration depends on the record and preservation; no factual reopening, misconduct hearing, lien transfer, distribution schedule, or payment amount is ordered. Penalty allowance and tax-and-interest priority remain unchanged.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Ahlers* requires equity within the Code without itself construing §510(c) or prohibiting all equity. *Taylor v. Standard Gas*, *Pepper*, and *Comstock* preserve their misconduct-based applications without establishing a universal prerequisite. *Simonson*'s former statutory penalty prohibition supplies no present ranking. *Boteler* and *Nicholas* preserve postpetition penalties' distinct treatment and compliance function. *Things Remembered* supports giving compatible statutes separate effect; its remand-review ruling and separate equitable-remand theory do not authorize a new distribution rank.

#### United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc., 518 U.S. 213 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-325.
**Decided:** June 20, 1996.

##### Whether the allowed §4971(a) funding-default assessment receives excise-tax priority

**Holding:** The allowed 1989 section 4971(a) assessment is a noncompensatory penalty, not an excise tax entitled to priority under the governing pre-1994 section 507(a)(7)(E). When the Bankruptcy Code does not direct adoption of an external classification, the exaction's actual statutory operation controls its bankruptcy tax characterization; neither a tax label nor a deterrent purpose alone resolves that inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** Payment of the deficiency-measured sanction neither satisfies the independent funding obligation nor replaces pension or PBGC liability. The ten-percent assessment is distinct from §4971(b)’s additional hundred-percent assessment. Minimum-funding waivers under §412(d) and 29 U.S.C. §1083(a) can remove the deficiency and prevent either assessment. Section 507(a)(7)(G) separately requires a penalty related to a qualifying claim and compensation for actual pecuniary loss, absent here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The governing text is pre-1994 §507(a)(7)(E), not later (a)(8). Its excise-tax timing requirements are not separately disputed. The claim remains allowed. No general Lorber checklist, exclusion of regulatory taxes, constitutional punishment rule, dischargeability or proportionality holding follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Denial of priority preserved, 8–1; Thomas would accord tax treatment.

##### Whether recurring creditor-loss reasons suffice for equitable subordination

**Holding:** Section 510(c) does not authorize distributional subordination of this noncompensatory penalty merely because its payment reduces innocent creditors' recoveries. Its equitable authority remains available on a legally sufficient, supported claim-specific ground, but the stated class-based reasons are inadequate even if described as individual balancing; the challenged subordinate ranking must be set aside. Rejection of that equitable ground does not itself confer tax priority; the Part I majority separately resolves priority.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part II, unanimous; Thomas joins this part only.

**Material application:** The assessment’s noncompensatory character, insolvency and reduction of innocent creditors’ recoveries recur across the claim class; calling those reasons individualized balancing does not make them sufficient. Section 510(c), after notice and a hearing, permits distributional subordination of allowed claims to allowed claims and allowed interests to allowed interests. Priority does not immunize a claim from that power and lack of priority does not justify its use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal misconduct prerequisite, frozen catalogue of equitable grounds or entitlement to subordination without misconduct is adopted. Properly presented §§1122(a) and 1129(a)(7) arguments, including the asserted relevance of §726(a)(4), and supported lawful claim-specific equity remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Judgment vacated and remanded. The subordinate ranking cannot stand on the recurring class grounds; Class 12 ordinary-unsecured treatment remains the plan’s fallback absent an independently lawful basis for different treatment. No fixed distribution, new misconduct hearing, lien transfer or restoration of another disallowed claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **City of New York v. Feiring, 313 U.S. 283 (1941):** Applies the federal bankruptcy inquiry into the exaction's legal operation rather than the creating statute's label.
- **United States v. New York, 315 U.S. 510 (1942):** Applies that inquiry to a federal exaction and preserves the distinction between a revenue tax that creates incentives and a sanction for violating an independent duty.
- **United States v. Sotelo, 436 U.S. 268 (1978):** Applies the rule that a penalty label does not change the character of liability for already-withheld taxes; distinguishes this independent funding-default sanction.
- **United States v. Noland, May 13, 1996:** Applies the limit on class-based equitable reprioritization, including recurring reasons described as balancing. Preserves statutory equitable susceptibility even for priority claims. Its distinct administrative classification of postpetition tax penalties is not extended to this prepetition assessment.
- **Pepper v. Litton, 308 U.S. 295 (1939):** Preserves its misconduct-based equitable application without converting misconduct into a necessary condition for every section 510(c) case. No precedent is overruled.

#### Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation v. CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1440; judgment below, 150 F.3d 1293.
**Decided:** 1999-05-13.

##### The actual tax-treatment connection must be read with its lien conditions

**Holding:** A court may not reject §412(n)(4)(C)'s incorporation of rules similar to ERISA §4068(c), including its express Title 11 tax treatment, solely because the Bankruptcy Code does not refer back to the pension provision. It must determine the incorporated treatment's scope and satisfy the specified lien conditions and applicable pre-1994 §507(a)(7) requirements before granting tax priority; the Court grants none here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join the tax-priority remand.

##### Prepetition-service compensation does not become a new administrative debt merely because payment is later due

**Holding:** The additional pension-contribution claim, attributable to prepetition employee services, does not obtain §503(b)(1)(A) administrative priority merely from a later due date, continuing funding duty or later plan termination. The already allowed component for actual postpetition services remains, and Reading's distinct postpetition-liability route without an estate-benefit requirement remains intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, unanimous.

##### The statutory liability measure must be reconciled with bankruptcy allowance

**Holding:** Sections 1362(b)(1)(A) and 1301(a)(18), including the latter's incorporation of PBGC-prescribed assumptions, define the underlying unfunded-benefit liability and cannot be displaced categorically merely because the claim is in bankruptcy. Courts must apply that lawful measure together with §502(b)'s petition-date allowance command, enforce actual statutory limits and conflicts, and consider properly presented validity or application objections; the Court validates no universal methodology and fixes no amount.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join the valuation remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 412(n) concerns covered unpaid required contributions exceeding $1 million and a lien arising on the sixtieth day after the installment due date. The tax instruction concerns an amount with respect to which that lien is imposed and imports rules similar to §4068(c), (d), and (e). The lower finding that the installment was due fewer than sixty days prepetition, and the preserved no-lien/stay alternative, remain unresolved by this Court. No universal estate-benefit prerequisite, exclusion of postpetition statutory wrongs, PBGC methodology, duplicate discounting or universal pension rate is approved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate tax priority and the $124,441,000 valuation for lawful construction and liability/allowance review; affirm denial of additional administrative priority for prepetition-service debt. Preserve the allowed postpetition-service component and Appeal Fund limit. No priority, amount, lien attachment, new rule or hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators retains its operation-based treatment of the separate §4971 assessment and its subordination limits; unpaid contributions with this conditional Title 11 instruction are a different debt.
- United States v. Noland preserves statutory priority and actual exceptions rather than judicial reprioritization.
- Reading Co. v. Brown preserves administrative treatment of qualifying postpetition liability without a measurable estate benefit; the additional debt here remains prepetition in origin.
- Hughes Aircraft and UNUM distinguish obligation, function and remedy; UNUM's state-insurance saving clause is not transplanted into this federal scheme.
- Haggar supplies actual-authority and statutory-limit review, distinct from particular validity and application.
- Associates Commercial Corp. v. Rash follows its particular retained-collateral valuation rule; it supplies no universal pension discount method.
- LaSalle separates the legal allocation of rights from their numerical valuation and imposes no general market test on this claim.
