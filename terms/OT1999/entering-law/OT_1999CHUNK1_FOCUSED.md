### Interstate detainers and trial-period commencement

#### Fex v. Michigan, 507 U.S. 43 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7873.

**Decided:** February 23, 1993.

##### Article III(a): actual receipt starts the trial period

**Holding:** When a prisoner invokes Article III while serving a term of imprisonment and an untried charge in another participating State is the subject of a lodged detainer, the 180-day trial period begins only when the required written notice and request for final disposition have actually reached both the prosecuting officer and the appropriate court in that State. Delivery to the custodian alone does not start it; a necessary or reasonable continuance may be allowed for good cause shown in open court with the prisoner or counsel present, Article VI(a) tolls the period while the prisoner is unable to stand trial as determined by the court having jurisdiction, and Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice when the applicable period is violated.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by White, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies six votes for this rule and its application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Carchman v. Nash, 473 U.S. 716 (1985): applied for the compact’s federal-law character and attention to operative language; its exclusion of probation-violation detainers remains intact.
- Houston v. Lack, 487 U.S. 266 (1988): distinguished because a prisoner’s own appeal-filing deadline differs from commencement of the receiving State’s prosecution period; its mailbox rule remains in force within its setting.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The custodian’s prompt forwarding and Article III(c) notice obligations remain; actual receipt does not excuse official delay. Separate notice remedies, constitutional claims and a continuance first sought after expiration are not resolved. There is no automatic grace period or bad-faith prerequisite.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm: trial began 177 days after both required recipients received the papers, so Article III(a) supplies no dismissal with prejudice here.

### Tactile recognition during a protective frisk

#### Minnesota v. Dickerson, 508 U.S. 366 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2019.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Minnesota v. Dickerson, June 7, 1993, No. 91-2019, see Federal Courts — Collateral consequences of diversion.

##### Authorized touch can supply probable cause without an additional search

**Holding:** An officer lawfully conducting a protective patdown may seize contraband when the authorized touching itself supplies probable cause of its incriminating character and the officer has lawful access to it. The officer may not manipulate or otherwise explore a nonthreatening object beyond the frisk to obtain that probable cause; immediate recognition requires probable cause, not certainty.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Part II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Terry v. Ohio, 392 U.S. 1: the protective purpose limits the scope of the frisk.
- Michigan v. Long, 463 U.S. 1032: seizure of contraband found within an authorized protective search applied.
- Horton v. California, 496 U.S. 128: lawful access and immediately apparent incriminating character extended to lawful touch.
- Arizona v. Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: probable cause may not be manufactured through a separate unauthorized search.
- Soldal v. Cook County, December 8, 1992: independent possessory protection preserved; no enlargement of a frisk follows.

##### Manipulation after excluding a weapon requires suppression here

**Holding:** When the accepted findings establish that the officer identified contraband only by squeezing and manipulating an object after determining that it was not a weapon, the examination exceeds Terry and cannot justify the seizure. That independent ground sustains suppression here despite the state court’s erroneous categorical rejection of tactile recognition.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Parts III and IV; Stone-Zsela, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Probable cause must arise from authorized contact, with lawful access; exploratory touching is not a new exception. The diversion record’s identified legal uses, rather than an invented conviction, support review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm suppression on the accepted finding of manipulation after the weapon concern ended; no further evidentiary opportunity for the State is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Terry, 392 U.S. 1: protective authority ended when the object was known not to be a weapon.
- Hicks, 480 U.S. 321: information obtained by the additional exploration cannot validate it retrospectively.

### Plea-discussion impeachment waivers

#### United States v. Mezzanatto

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1340.
**Decided:** January 18, 1995.

##### A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable

**Holding:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that his statements may be used to impeach his own inconsistent trial testimony; absent an affirmative indication that this agreement was entered unknowingly or involuntarily, the limited waiver is enforceable. Supported challenges based on ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness, or the agreement's scope remain subject to individual review, and inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment only; his different proof requirement is not controlling.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ricketts v. Adamson, 483 U.S. 1 (1987): draws on enforcement of an agreement's actual lawful terms, without importing consequences that were not agreed.
- Town of Newton v. Rumery, 480 U.S. 386 (1987): applies its rejection of automatic invalidity while preserving individual voluntariness and overreaching review; no new universal public-interest evidentiary burden is imposed.
- Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742 (1970): preserves knowing-and-voluntary assent despite the existence of bargaining pressure; pressure alone does not establish coercion.
- Peretz v. United States, 501 U.S. 923 (1991): uses its bounded recognition of consent as an analogy, not a rule that every institutional protection can be waived.
- Brooklyn Savings Bank v. O'Neil, 324 U.S. 697 (1945): distinguishes its prohibition on private defeat of the particular public statutory protection there; the Court does not deny that some protections may be nonwaivable.
- Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993): preserves the distinction between mental capacity and an actually informed, voluntary choice; competence or a signature alone does not dispose of a supported contrary record.
- United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993): applies the distinction between intentional relinquishment and forfeiture, preserving right-specific requirements and the actual preserved objection here.
- United States v. Salerno, 505 U.S. 317 (1992): distinguishes express relinquishment from dispensing with enacted evidentiary conditions or treating independent contradiction as disclosure; its holdings remain unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Then-Rule 410 excludes, against the defendant who made the plea or participated in the discussions in any civil or criminal proceeding, a later-withdrawn guilty plea, a no-contest plea, statements in Rule 11 or comparable state proceedings regarding either such plea, and statements in discussions with the prosecuting attorney that produce no guilty plea or one later withdrawn. Rule 11(e)(6) is substantively parallel. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion to have been introduced and fairness to require contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires the statement to have been made under oath, on the record, and in counsel's presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. This decision validates no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing, or other-person-testimony use and does not independently adjudicate the agreement's separate perjury-use term. Ordinary constitutional and evidentiary restrictions and the actual agreement's scope remain independent. The Court decides no universal rule for every advance waiver or every fundamental institutional protection.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's categorical nonwaiver ruling and resulting new-trial order are reversed, 7–2, for ordinary implementation of the limited waiver. No mandatory validity hearing, factual coercion finding, acquittal, independent retrial direction or sentencing ruling is ordered. If preserved nonconstitutional error is found, the Government must establish whole-record fair assurance that it did not substantially sway the judgment; grave doubt defeats harmlessness. No constitutional error is newly reached, and Olano's forfeited-error burden is not substituted.

### Appellate review of suspicion and probable cause

#### Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690

**Docket or dockets:** 95-5257; Seventh Circuit Nos. 94-3349 and 94-3350
**Decided:** May 28, 1996

##### Which suppression questions remain preserved for appellate review through these conditional pleas?

**Holding:** Review encompasses both ultimate reasonable suspicion for the initial stop and ultimate probable cause for the warrantless search, as preserved in these conditional pleas. Agreement between the parties about a proposed review standard neither determines that legal standard nor expands review to unpreserved discovery or suppression-merits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Rule 11(a)(2) permits a written reservation of specified adverse pretrial determinations with the court's approval and the Government's consent. The selected orders establish preservation of both thresholds here; incompleteness of other documents does not establish litigant default. The Court reviews the allocation of appellate responsibility, not a newly presented database-discovery question or the ultimate suppression outcome.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Success in obtaining the proper review standard does not itself establish a right to withdraw the pleas. The ordinary requirement of prevailing on the reserved issue remains.

##### How must appellate courts review reasonable suspicion and probable cause for a warrantless search?

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently determine the ultimate constitutional sufficiency of reasonable suspicion for a stop and probable cause for a warrantless search under the objective totality of the circumstances. Historical facts, credibility determinations and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences receive clear-error protection, while grounded contextual inferences drawn by resident judges and experienced officers receive genuine due weight without deference to the ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts II-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice majority. Scalia dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** Independent review develops workable constitutional standards and permits comparison of recurring circumstances. The reviewing court must nevertheless respect what occurred, the trial court's credibility role, local context and grounded police experience. Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane support allocating mixed questions by function rather than a single label; First Options preserves separate legal and factual review. Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White supply the substantive totality inquiry but do not decide who finally applies it on appeal. Gates's substantial-basis review of a magistrate's warrant decision concerns a different institutional task. Mere acknowledgment of an officer's inference is insufficient due weight, but experience cannot manufacture an unproved fact or entitle the officer's ultimate conclusion to a presumption of correctness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** There is no numerical weighting formula, intermediate standard, new evidentiary fact or police-correctness presumption. Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain deference for their task-specific discretionary determinations; Salve Regina's treatment of legal review remains. The Court imports neither habeas burdens from Thompson nor a general solution to every mixed question; Markman's reserved appellate issue remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Miller v. Fenton and Thompson v. Keohane supply functional allocation of legal and factual review; Thompson's habeas setting does not transfer its burdens here.
- First Options preserves independent legal review and protection of historical findings.
- Brinegar, Gates, Sokolow and White retain objective totality-of-circumstances substantive standards.
- Gates's warrant-issuance substantial-basis rule is distinguished from independent review of a warrantless search.
- Pierce and Cooter & Gell retain their task-specific deference; Salve Regina supports independent legal review.
- Markman does not decide the reserved appellate-review question by analogy alone.

##### What follows for these two appeals and the district court's suppression findings?

**Holding:** Both appellate judgments must be vacated and reconsidered under this allocation, without automatically ordering suppression or reopening the evidentiary record. Under section 636(b)(1)(B), the district judge independently determines specifically objected-to portions of the magistrate's recommendation and may receive evidence or recommit; that duty does not invariably require a new hearing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part III, joined by the same eight-Justice majority.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court accepted the credited loose-panel finding; rust was rejected, scratches were inconclusive, and actual NADDIS identification was unproved. Independent legal review cannot improve those facts by invoking experience. Raddatz permits the district judge's specified independent determination without an invariably repeated hearing; its concern about rejecting credibility findings is not triggered by the acceptance here. Section 636(b)(1)(A) does not set the ultimate suppression standard or the appellate allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes the circuit's limited-consent premise: consent did not authorize dismantling the panel. It adopts no general consent rule and does not revive waived inevitable discovery. The allocation also governs authorized Government suppression appeals, but section 3731's actual conditions remain: the statutory restriction after jeopardy attaches and before verdict, required certification of no delay and substantial material proof, the thirty-day and diligence requirements, and double-jeopardy limits. No independent Government appeal is authorized here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Seventh Circuit judgments, reported at 16 F.3d 714 and 52 F.3d 328, are vacated and remanded, 8-1. The Court orders reconsideration of both constitutional thresholds, not suppression, plea withdrawal, release, a new evidentiary hearing or a particular ultimate result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Raddatz preserves the distinction between a required independent judicial determination and an invariably new hearing; its reserved credibility-rejection question remains untriggered.

### Retrospective punishment and civil notification

#### W.P. v. Verniero

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 96-5416; the distinct E.B. appeal, No. 96-5132, is outside review.

**Decided:** 1998-03-16.

##### The challenged notification is not punishment on this record

**Holding:** For the class whose offenses preceded October 31, 1994 and who face Tier 2 or Tier 3 notification, New Jersey's civil public-protection purpose remains unrebutted by the clearest proof of punitive purpose or effect in the scheme's supported combined operation. The challenged notification therefore supplies no Ex Post Facto or Double Jeopardy ground for relief; the Court decides no same-offense question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices directly adopt the common civil-classification ground. Scalia and Thomas support rejection on their separate grounds; Stevens dissents on Ex Post Facto punishment and does not reach independent Double Jeopardy identity.

**Material application:** Risk grading, individual exceptions and audience tailoring link disclosure to public protection. Tier 2 reaches qualifying schools, child-care facilities and organizations serving children or supporting women, under the likely-encounter restriction; Tier 3 reaches persons likely to encounter the registrant. The scheme imposes no direct residence, movement, employment or licensing prohibition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Severe disclosure and private consequences are not categorically irrelevant, but do not establish the demanded punitive proof on this record. Registration alone, Tier 1, post-enactment offenses, materially different operation and every later registry are undecided. Same-offense identity is not reached.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ursery, Ward and Mendoza-Martinez supply rebuttable civil classification and combined purpose/effect examination; neither labels nor a single indicator is conclusive.
- Hendricks retains its as-applied punitive-confinement holding and actual-operation method; its outcome is distinguished, not replaced with an approval of that custody.
- Hudson preserves aggregate examination and Halper in its separate personal-monetary field. Halper is not extended into a notification accounting test.
- Kurth Ranch retains its extraordinary-tax punishment holding; its arrest-linked operation differs from the tailored warnings here.
- Blockburger and retained Grady offense-identity questions are not reached. No precedent is overruled.

For this case’s other questions, see Civil Rights (The State must justify both tier and notification plan before ordinary notification; Risk classification requires clear and convincing proof; the plan injunction is retained on a narrower authority basis); Federal Courts (The advance-notice emergency question remains unripe).

### Public-university admissions and racial classifications

#### Hopwood v. Texas

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 94-50664; related No. 94-50569 is outside the grant.

**Decided:** November 25, 1996.

##### The established unequal admissions process

**Holding:** The State’s racial admissions classifications must satisfy Adarand’s common substantial-justification standard: the State must establish an important objective within its lawful authority and a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its racial treatment and that objective, and must justify the classification’s coverage and actual burdens. The 1992 law-school process fails because it subjected applicants to unjustified, separate racial decision systems rather than genuinely competitive individual consideration; unequal opportunity to compete is an injury without proof that an applicant would have received a seat.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not find that every index difference independently fails, that enrollment aspirations were fixed reserved seats, that race determined every final seat, or that any plaintiff would have been admitted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Croson's retained evidentiary demands and Wygant require concrete justification, fit and attention to individual burdens for state classifications. Bakke supplies the distinction between separate allocation and competitive consideration. Adarand supplies the governing standard and equal-competition injury.

##### A supported educational-diversity interest is constitutionally available

**Holding:** A public university’s interest in concrete educational benefits from a genuinely diverse student body can be an important objective on a properly supported record. The university must establish the educational objective, a substantial, evidence-supported relationship between its actual racial classification and that objective, and justified burdens; a diversity label, racial balance, or an assumption that racial identity determines viewpoint does not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Kennedy supplies a seventh vote to vacate the categorical prohibition without joining this affirmative rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No particular holistic program, universal admissions design or later Texas program is approved. The decision requires no racial consideration and leaves federal classifications, other educational levels and employment outside its scope.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bakke's five express Part V-C joins permitted properly designed competitive consideration and rejected an absolute race-use injunction; Powell's complete diversity rationale was not a prior majority. This decision newly adopts the six-Justice supported-diversity rule. Adarand's common standard governs; Metro Broadcasting's separate federal tier was overruled there.

##### The responsible State and the continuing dismantlement duty

**Holding:** A State that operated a legally segregated university system must eliminate traceable policies that continue to foster segregation when their effects can practicably be removed consistently with sound educational practices; good faith and facial neutrality alone do not discharge that duty, and the State bears the showing of dismantlement. The responsible actor cannot categorically be confined to the law school as an administrative unit, but a remedial racial classification requires a demonstrated connection and fit to a continuing wrong rather than generalized societal discrimination or racial imbalance alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No current Texas remnant or justified preference is found. Inquiry is confined to the lawful admissions counterfactual and properly presented relief in these four individual claims; no general structural suit is reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Fordice and Ayers supply the State's continuing dismantlement duty and burden, including traceable interacting policies, continuing effects and practicable removal consistent with sound education. Croson and Wygant require supported wrong and fitted voluntary repair without invariably requiring a fresh formal adjudication. Jenkins preserves violation-linked and causally limited remedies.

##### Individual causation and the same-decision burden

**Holding:** Once these applicants have established the unconstitutional admissions process, the school must prove by a preponderance of the evidence that each applicant would not have been admitted under a specified constitutional process to defeat relief for the denied admission and its consequences. Plaintiffs still must establish the existence and amount of compensable loss under an available cause against a proper defendant; failure of the school’s defense yields neither an automatic damages amount nor unconditional admission.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-A, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the individual-remand disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The lawful counterfactual must permit the uses of race that controlling law permits. Elliott's uncommunicated offer is not treated as communicated or accepted, and Carvell's tuition testimony establishes no awarded sum. A successful same-decision defense does not erase unequal-process injury.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mt. Healthy and Arlington Heights supply the school-borne same-decision defense. Carey separates actual compensable loss from nominal recognition. Bakke's admission award depended on a burden concession absent here.

##### Purposeful treatment and statutory intent

**Holding:** Deliberately using race to give applicants unequal admissions treatment is intentional racial discrimination for the Title VI issue presented, even when officials act in good faith and intend educational or remedial benefits. That conclusion does not establish every element of a statutory remedy, a proper defendant, or a waiver of immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-B, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with the component disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sections 1981 and 1983 and Title VI keep their separate remedial requirements. No independent index disparate-impact claim or claim belonging to the unsuccessful intervenors is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bakke's individual statutory protection is applied without collapsing purpose, justification and available remedies.

##### The punitive request on this record

**Holding:** Smith v. Wade permits punitive damages in its applicable civil-rights setting for evil motive or intent, or for reckless or callous indifference to federally protected rights; lack of malice alone is not the whole standard. The punitive denial is affirmed on this record’s accepted good-faith finding and unsettled legal setting, without establishing a universal good-purpose defense or deciding the availability of punitive relief under every pleaded statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-C, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with denial of punitive damages.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful unequal treatment and punitive culpability are distinct. The decision adopts neither an animus-only standard nor a good-purpose immunity and decides no general punitive entitlement under every pleaded statute.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith v. Wade retains both evil motive or intent and reckless or callous indifference; the denial creates neither an animus-only rule nor benevolent-purpose immunity.

##### Source-specific relief and undecided defenses

**Holding:** The established unequal process supports declaratory and nominal recognition independently of proof of entitlement to admission, subject to the available cause, proper defendant and immunity rules. Preserve that relief and fee-free lawful reconsideration if still practically available, while remanding the remaining claim-specific questions; no compensation, admission, immunity waiver, or personal-capacity liability is awarded here.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-D, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Official-capacity pleadings do not become personal-capacity claims. Claim-specific statutory relief, proper defendants, Eleventh Amendment immunity, waiver, fees and any properly arising personal-immunity question remain for decision. Fee-free reconsideration is not a right to a seat.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Carey preserves nominal recognition without proved compensable harm. Adarand's competition injury does not establish entitlement to any particular remedy.

##### Prospective relief and the bounded remand

**Holding:** The refusal to issue a broad prospective injunction remains intact, and directions purporting to govern unexamined future programs beyond resolution of these 1992 claims are vacated. Any later request for prospective relief requires its own sufficiently concrete future exposure, supported record and equitable basis; neither the discontinued procedure nor the existence of retrospective claims alone supplies those requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The continued retrospective claims establish no future exposure. The Court decides no replacement program and enters no broad prospective injunction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Adarand preserves the distinction between past competitive injury and actual renewed exposure needed for prospective relief.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The 1992 process invalidity stands unanimously. The diversity prohibition is vacated 7-2 and the law-school-only remedial restriction 8-1, with the proposition-level coalitions stated above. Unanimously preserve the individual-remedy remand, purposeful-treatment disposition and punitive denial; preserve refusal of a broad injunction and vacate directions purporting to govern unexamined future programs. No compensatory sum or admission is awarded.

### Government use of medical information

#### Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 95-1559; no separate Supreme Court merits docket supplied.
**Decided:** 1997-05-05.

##### This confined benefits-administration use does not establish the asserted constitutional violation

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding a constitutional interest against unjustified governmental acquisition and internal use of intimate medical information, this record does not establish its violation through unsolicited receipt followed by the limited, benefits-related administrator and physician inquiry and cessation of identified reporting. That conclusion accepts the supported deliberate follow-up, unnecessary identifiers and deficient safeguards; it does not require every datum to be indispensable, and it authorizes no general publication or use detached from the benefits function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer; five direct joins to the assumed-right application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public dissemination is not declared indispensable to every possible future claim; broader or unrelated internal use remains distinct. Hodge and Uhlrig do not supply an immunity judgment here, and Chandler's bodily-search standard is not an informational due-process test. The Court decides no independent damages sufficiency, preserved Rule 59 issue, private-employer duty, municipal-attribution issue, or unappealed failure-to-train claim. Confidential retention, lawful disclosure and different administrative uses retain their own questions. The Court neither recognizes nor rejects the general medical-information right, adopts the lower court's seven-factor formula, requires universal indispensability, nor treats accepting benefits as waiver.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the direction of judgment for SEPTA and Pierce on this confined constitutional application; no majority restores the $125,000 award or orders a remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Whalen permits legitimate identified-drug administration while reserving abuses; Sheets retains intentional public-dissemination protection and favorable-evidence review without automatic extension to internal use. Hodge and Uhlrig supply no immunity judgment; Chandler's bodily-search rule is not an informational due-process test.

### Process before community notification

#### W.P. v. Verniero

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 96-5416; the distinct E.B. appeal, No. 96-5132, is outside review.

**Decided:** 1998-03-16.

##### The State must justify both tier and notification plan before ordinary notification

**Holding:** In the ordinary circumstances presented, Tier 2 or Tier 3 notification may not proceed without a meaningful advance opportunity to contest both the risk classification and the proposed notification plan, with the prosecutor bearing persuasion for each. The protected interest rests on New Jersey's substantive restriction requiring disclosure to serve a legitimate and substantial interest with minimized invasion, rather than on reputation alone or a promise of procedures.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices directly adopt this protected-interest, hearing and State-persuasion ground. Stevens joins conditionally because civil notification remains available under the Court's judgment. Stone-Zsela supports the disposition on his stated procedural grounds without joining this distinct state-entitlement explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Review must allow challenges to factual accuracy, calculation, atypical cases and excessive plans. Credible supported likely reoffense requires high-risk treatment regardless of score; a physical condition minimizing risk, including advanced age or debilitating illness, requires low-risk treatment regardless of score. Individual review does not reopen a facial challenge to the Scale or its weights or establish a general federal informational-privacy right.

##### Risk classification requires clear and convincing proof; the plan injunction is retained on a narrower authority basis

**Holding:** Before the ordinary Tier 2 or Tier 3 notification presented here, the State must establish the factual basis and risk classification by clear and convincing evidence. Preponderance alone is constitutionally insufficient for that classification in this setting; proof beyond a reasonable doubt and certainty of future conduct are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct supporters. Stevens's join operates because the Court permits civil notification. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would hold State preponderance sufficient.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This risk-classification burden concerns ordinary Tier 2/3 notification for the class whose offenses preceded October 31, 1994. It creates no universal heightened standard for civil predictions and adopts none of the concurrence’s additional safeguards as a general majority rule.

##### Required proof for the notification plan

**Authority of the judgment:** The injunction's requirement of clear and convincing State proof for the notification plan is also affirmed, 5–4, by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Only Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join Part III-B's plan-specific error-allocation reasoning. Stone-Zsela supports retention of that injunction component without adopting a plan-specific ground. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would retain State persuasion but permit preponderance. The injunction binds this litigation; no five-Justice plan-quantum rationale or broader general rule is manufactured from the judgment. There is no logical-subset combination of a reasonless judgment agreement and the four-Justice explanation under Marks.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The affirmed injunction requires clear and convincing State persuasion for the plan in this litigation; only the separate advance-review and State-persuasion propositions have direct majority authority. No broader plan-quantum rule follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sandin's substantive-constraint principle informs the protected state interest without transplanting a prison threshold into all civil settings. Paul v. Davis's reputation-alone boundary remains intact.
- Mathews supplies the context-specific process comparison. Addington and Santosky support attention to serious and irreversible error without creating a universal civil proof quantum.
- Sahhar preserves the distinct statutory initial-commitment and discharge burdens; neither is mechanically imported.

For this case’s other questions, see Criminal Procedure (The challenged notification is not punishment on this record); Federal Courts (The advance-notice emergency question remains unripe).

### Standing and personal injury

#### Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1424.
**Decided:** June 12, 1992.

##### What evidence of Article III injury, causation and redressability is required at summary judgment

**Holding:** A plaintiff invoking federal jurisdiction must establish a concrete and particularized, actual or imminent injury, a causal connection fairly traceable to the defendant rather than independent third parties, and a likelihood that the requested relief will redress the injury. At summary judgment, the plaintiff must supply specific facts by affidavit or other evidence on the challenged elements; favorable reasonable inferences remain available, but allegations cannot substitute for the missing evidence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the elements and litigation-stage evidentiary requirements. Stone concurs in the judgment without joining this section.

**Material application:** Earlier survival of the pleading stage does not establish the specific facts needed at summary judgment. The Court resolves this action on insufficient threatened personal injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The general redressability element controls. Its application to absent funding agencies and uncertain foreign-project responses appears only in the three-Justice Part III-B and is not controlling. Favorable reasonable inferences remain available; the decision does not replace Rule 56 or the separate APA programmatic-review holding of National Wildlife Federation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The merits declaration, mandatory rulemaking relief and dependent §1540(g)(4) fee award are vacated, and the action is remanded for jurisdictional dismissal. Six Justices support dismissal for lack of standing; seven support reversal, with Stevens relying on a noncontrolling statutory ground. No independent fee-entitlement rule or substitute award is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Warth v. Seldin, 422 U.S. 490 (1975): applied for the plaintiff's personal stake and burden to establish standing; its statutory-rights discussion remains subject to concrete injury.
- Allen v. Wright, 468 U.S. 737 (1984): applied for the constitutional elements and the limits on generalized judicial supervision.
- Simon v. Eastern Kentucky Welfare Rights Organization, 426 U.S. 26 (1976): applied for fair traceability and likely redress; no new majority application concerning these absent agencies follows.

##### Whether past visits, indefinite return intentions and general wildlife interests establish imminent personal injury

**Holding:** Aesthetic, recreational and professional interests in observing wildlife can support standing, but these plaintiffs must show threatened interference with their own concrete use. Prior visits and an indefinite intention to return, without concrete plans or another supported basis for imminent renewed exposure, do not establish standing for these prospective claims; an ecosystem, animal or professional connection that supplies only general interest in distant species does not cure that defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, controls with five votes. Stone's separate concurrence supplies a sixth vote for the record-specific personal-injury conclusion without a formal opinion join.

**Material application:** The affidavits describe visits to Egyptian and Sri Lankan project areas but no sufficiently definite return undertaking or continuing activity showing imminent exposure. General ecosystem, animal and professional connections supply no missing evidence of these members' own threatened use.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchased-ticket or particular-document prerequisite is imposed. Actual use of an affected ecosystem and demonstrated injury transmitted across geographic distance remain undecided. Aesthetic, recreational and professional harm remains cognizable when it affects the claimant's own concrete activity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Jurisdictional dismissal leaves the statute's geographic reach unresolved and does not preclude a later action supported by sufficient standing facts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hunt v. Washington State Apple Advertising Commission, 432 U.S. 333 (1977): applied to require an injured member for this associational claim; no other associational requirement is changed.

**Holding:** ESA §11(g)'s authorization for any person to sue does not give Article III standing to a plaintiff asserting only the public's common interest in lawful administration. A procedural right may protect a plaintiff's threatened concrete interest without requiring proof that the procedure will certainly change the ultimate agency decision, but the procedural label cannot replace that personal interest; these respondents have not shown the required connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five Justices adopt the citizen-suit and concrete-interest rule. Stone does not join this section.

**Material application:** Section 11(g), 16 U.S.C. §1540(g), permits enforcement but identifies no concrete injury to these members independent of the unsupported future use. The public's common interest in lawful administration cannot supply the missing personal connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may recognize concrete factual injuries previously inadequate in law; the outer reach of that power is not settled. Procedural protections tied to a threatened concrete interest remain cognizable without certainty of a changed ultimate decision. Neither §7(a)(2)'s geographic reach nor the binding effect of a revised rule on absent agencies is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The action is dismissed for want of Article III standing; no new consultation rule or cancellation of any foreign project is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fairchild v. Hughes, 258 U.S. 126 (1922): applied to reject a general right to compel lawful administration as a sufficient personal injury.

- United States v. Richardson, 418 U.S. 166 (1974), and Schlesinger v. Reservists Committee to Stop the War, 418 U.S. 208 (1974): applied to generally shared governmental grievances without distinctive concrete harm.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5418.

**Holding:** The congressionally sanctioned Interstate Agreement on Detainers is federal law, and a state prisoner's claim that custody violates its trial-period provisions presents a federal statutory question within 28 U.S.C. §2254(a) as in force at decision. - Fex v. Michigan, February 23, 1993: its application of the compact as federal law remains intact; its Article III actual-receipt trigger does not replace Article IV's arrival trigger or decide collateral entitlement.
- Stone v. Powell, 428 U.S. 465 (1976): its restriction concerning the Fourth Amendment exclusionary remedy is not extended to decide this statutory claim.
- Withrow v. Williams, April 21, 1993: its refusal to extend Powell merely from a fair state opportunity to a different safeguard is preserved; its Miranda holding does not itself decide the distinct statutory-relief threshold here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts II and III of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly join the standard and this record's denial. The stronger-demand discussion in Part IV has three votes and supplies no additional Court holding; no combination of separate opinions is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than decides the Article IV(c) breach and does not adopt automatic motion-pendency tolling, find a valid continuance, or resolve the competing statutory-merits positions. It does not impose a required oral formula or article-number citation, find complete silence or knowing waiver, or decide that an adequately presented demand necessarily produces habeas relief. No independent Sixth Amendment claim is presented. Barker and Doggett retain their complete constitutional inquiry, including Doggett's qualified treatment of presumed defense impairment; neither a 120-day overrun alone nor absence of separately proved prejudice alone answers that inquiry. The transfer-hearing and habitual-offender issues are outside the question decided. No categorical bar to every IAD claim, affirmative entitlement in an aggravated case, or general rule governing every statutory habeas claim is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hill v. United States, 368 U.S. 424 (1962): its ordinary nonconstitutional-error threshold is extended from §2255 to the presented §2254 claim; its distinction between formal omission and fundamental defect remains operative.
- United States v. Timmreck, 441 U.S. 780 (1979): its collateral distinction is applied to this timing claim without treating every statutory entitlement as a technicality or deciding every aggravated case.
- Davis v. United States, 417 U.S. 333 (1974): its statutory no-offense holding remains available; this procedural timing claim does not establish that Reed's conduct lay outside the criminal prohibition.

### State immunity and IGRA officer suits

#### Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-12.
**Decided:** March 27, 1996.

##### IGRA's binding good-faith adjudication is judicial despite later statutory steps

**Holding:** Adjudication of IGRA's federal good-faith negotiation dispute is a judicial function when its judgment conclusively determines legal relations and activates constrained statutory consequences. Later mediation and executive action, and the absence of a guaranteed compact, do not make that judgment advisory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Stevens's serious doubt and Stone-Zsela's nonjoin supply no additional support.

Brimson supports judicial assistance to administration within its adversarial, conclusive, enforceable-order setting, without creating IGRA contempt power. Ferreira's revisable commissioner process is distinguished. Gordon's jurisdictional disposition supplies no delivered reasoned majority opinion requiring execution in every judicial case; Hayburn produced no Supreme Court merits decision, and its reported circuit communications remain historical materials. Wisconsin's partial-redress rule concerns the judicial capacity to afford meaningful relief. Morgan Stanley and Plaut: Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement of qualifying completed private securities limitations dismissals under §27A(b)'s objectively defined procedure while courts independently determine eligibility and adjudicate the claims. **Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. No additional general clear-indication theory commands that coalition.

**Material application:** The Governor's representatives negotiated, supplying connection to the alleged violation without establishing that he alone can bind Florida to every term. Mandatory good faith differs from discretion over compact terms, and nonagreement alone does not prove bad faith.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A qualifying request by the Tribe with jurisdiction over the Indian lands triggers §2710(d)(3)(A)'s good-faith duty; suit under (d)(7)(A)(i) may begin only after 180 days. The effective-compact enforcement action under (A)(ii) and Secretary action under (A)(iii) remain separate. Tribal evidence of no compact and failure to respond or respond in good faith precedes the State's good-faith burden under (B)(ii).

An actual adverse finding triggers an order to State and Tribe to conclude a compact within the first sixty days. The court may consider public interest, safety, criminality, financial integrity and adverse economic effects on existing gaming, and shall treat direct-tax demands on the Tribe or Indian lands as evidence of bad faith. Failure to agree during that period requires last-best proposals to a court-appointed mediator, who selects the proposal best comporting with IGRA, other applicable federal law and the findings and order. Submission to both sides begins a second sixty-day period for State consent; consent produces an agreed compact. Nonconsent triggers notice to the Secretary, who shall prescribe procedures in consultation with the Tribe consistently with the selected proposal, IGRA and relevant state law. The findings/order criterion expressly appears in selection under (iv), not verbatim in the Secretary clause (vii).

State nonparticipation does not automatically select the Tribe's proposal. Failure to agree or participate during the ordered period produces statutory progression, not added contempt or an order compelling agreement; every separate obstruction sanction is not decided. Section (d)(4) creates no new taxing authority but preserves agreed regulatory-cost assessments under (d)(3)(C)(iii), separately from direct-tax demands' evidentiary effect. Absence of other taxing authority does not justify refusing negotiation.

Class III gaming still requires approved tribal legislation, a State permitting such gaming for some person, organization or entity, and an effective compact subject to applicable remedial provisions; licensing and regulation remain distinct. Under (d)(8), disapproval may rest on IGRA, other federal law not relating to jurisdiction over gaming on Indian lands, or federal trust obligations. Forty-five-day inaction means approval only insofar as consistent with IGRA, and Federal Register publication remains necessary. Section 2721 severability supplies neither missing predicates nor an alternative program.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical Governor dismissal, 5–4, without finding bad faith or granting a compact, mediator, default proposal, Secretary procedures, damages, game-scope declaration or license. The separate September 22, 1993 merits judgment and stayed appeal No. 93-5256 receive their own lawful treatment and are not vacated. The asserted Secretary substitute remedy, independent administrative authority, complete state-law compacting power, Tenth Amendment defense and mediator-appointments question remain unadjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Young reaches this connected officer and ongoing federal duty without a universal rule for detailed statutes. Edelman's retrospective-compensation boundary remains; prospective costs alone do not establish retrospective relief. Pennhurst is not triggered merely because the federal obligation incorporates state gaming law. Hafer's personal-capacity damages rule does not supply this action. Puerto Rico Aqueduct supplies appealability, not immunity. New York and Voting Rights Coalition do not decide the reserved IGRA constitutional defenses; X-Citement Video preserves appropriate respondent defenses without compelling their resolution here.

**Authority of the judgment:** Florida's immunity-dismissal direction is set aside and remanded by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, 5–4. Stone-Zsela and Stevens would sustain direct-State amenability; Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate without deciding it. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm. No controlling State-amenability rationale exists under Marks: avoidance is not a logical subset of the affirmative constitutional grounds, and three unused conditional alternatives are not actual abrogation joins.

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes an action naming the State through its State-party action, evidentiary burden, State-and-Tribe order and successive State-directed steps. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered State references alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Six actual positions across the separate writings: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The other three's conditional acceptance is not counted. This agreement concerns statutory intent alone.

**Current force:** The clear-statement proposition controls; no majority sustains or rejects Florida's constitutional amenability or requires a particular avoidance sequence.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank, June 23, 1999, Part III, resolves the Article I question left open here: Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit. It extends Union Gas's retained constitutional conclusion and does not decide IGRA's direct-State action; see Federal Courts — Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State-removal judgment establishes neither jurisdiction nor its absence; lawful jurisdiction is necessary before any State merits adjudication. The State claim is not terminated by immunity, and no abandonment, mootness or indefinite stay is presumed. No express or constructive waiver, reciprocal tribal/state consent or delegated United States immunity exemption establishes this suit. The United States appears only as amicus.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Union Gas is not overruled: its damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion retain their decided force without making every plurality sentence binding. Hans remains effective; separate common-law theories do not become law. Fitzpatrick's distinct Fourteenth Amendment authority is unchanged.

#### United States v. Irvine, 511 U.S. 224

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1546.

**Decided:** 1994-04-20.

**Holding:** Subject to the gift-tax chapter's limitations, §§2501(a)(1) and 2511(a) reach a later direct or indirect gratuitous transfer of a valuable property interest, including this delayed disclaimer directing part of a remainder to the disclaimant's children. - Jewett v. Commissioner, 455 U.S. 305 (1982): applied to the indirect transfer through a delayed disclaimer and the absence of a state-law right to federal tax immunity.
- Morgan v. Commissioner, 309 U.S. 78 (1940), and United States v. Pelzer, 312 U.S. 399 (1941): applied to distinguish state property interests from their federal tax treatment unless the federal provision makes state law controlling.

**Independent alternative holding:** If Treasury Regulation §25.2511-1(c)(2) applies to this pre-1977 interest, its disclaimer exception requires an unequivocal refusal effective under local law, within a reasonable time after knowledge of the transfer creating the interest and before acceptance; a partial refusal must be complete and unqualified in light of the facts and local law. **Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Souter's conditional reasonable-time portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices adopt this independently sufficient conditional ground and its explanation. - Jewett v. Commissioner: applied conditionally to the knowledge measure and the gift-tax/estate-tax relationship; its rule is not limited to the date an interest becomes possessory.

**Holding:** Revenue Act of 1932 §501(b) excludes transfers made on or before June 6, 1932; it does not exempt a later gratuitous transfer merely because the transferred interest was created before that date. The relevant taxable transfer here occurred through the 1979 disclaimer, so the statutory prospective limitation does not bar the tax; no ruling requires a pre-enactment disclaimer or approves retroactive Treasury lawmaking generally.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's prospective-application portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this answer to the independent statutory defense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Jacobs, 306 U.S. 363 (1939): applied to distinguish a tax on a later transfer from retroactive taxation of an earlier event.

- Untermyer v. Anderson, 276 U.S. 440 (1928): distinguished; this case taxes the later disposition and does not adjudicate a retroactive tax on the 1917 transfer.

##### What causal relation civil RICO requires between a violation and business or property injury

**Holding:** A plaintiff seeking damages under 18 U.S.C. §1964(c) must establish both factual causation and proximate cause, including some direct relation between the injury asserted and the alleged §1962 violation. Injury that merely follows harm to another person is insufficient on the causal chain presented here; directness is a contextual requirement informed by attribution, overlapping recovery and the availability of directly injured plaintiffs, not a mechanical rule resolving every third-party injury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this rule. Scalia agrees that proximate cause is required but joins no part of the Court's opinion; his distinct reasoning is not added to the opinion's support.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No purchaser-seller requirement is decided: Souter, Stone, Blackmun, Kennedy and Thomas join Part IV’s reservation. No special racketeering injury, antitrust-injury requirement, universal third-party-injury bar, or general zone-of-interests rule is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed on both asserted SIPC claims and remanded with directions to enter judgment for Holmes on the derivative customer RICO claim and the asserted independent §78eee(d) administration-advance claim. Trustees’ distinct claims remain, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery only under lawful liquidation priorities; no recovery or distribution is predetermined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Associated General Contractors of California, Inc. v. Carpenters, 459 U.S. 519 (1983) — its account of common-law causal limits in the borrowed Clayton Act language is extended to §1964(c); its antitrust-specific injury requirement is not imported.
- Agency Holding Corp. v. Malley-Duff & Associates, Inc., 483 U.S. 143 (1987) — relied on for the close statutory relationship between civil RICO and the Clayton Act; its limitations holding remains unchanged.
- Sedima, S.P.R.L. v. Imrex Co., 473 U.S. 479 (1985) — preserved: no special racketeering injury or prior conviction is required; the enacted causal limitation still must be satisfied.
- Cannon v. University of Chicago, 441 U.S. 677 (1979) — applied for Congress's presumed awareness of the settled interpretation of borrowed language; no new implied action is recognized.

##### Whether SIPC may recover for the nonpurchasing customers’ derivative losses

**Holding:** Even assuming SIPC may be subrogated to the nonpurchasing customers' rights against Holmes, it cannot recover under §1964(c) on the asserted stock-manipulation theory because their unpaid claims depend on the broker-dealers' prior losses and intervening insolvency. The trustees' distinct claims remain undisturbed, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery according to the priority SIPA lawfully gives its claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III-A, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the application and its limits. The validity and source of the asserted common-law subrogation are assumed, not established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Customer subrogation and responsibility for coconspirators’ conduct are assumed without decision. Purchasing-customer claims, separately grounded parking claims, directly aimed injury to SIPC, fraud merits, actual causation and damages remain unadjudicated; the unappealed Rule 10b-5 judgment is not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed on both asserted SIPC claims and remanded with directions to enter judgment for Holmes on the derivative customer RICO claim and the asserted independent §78eee(d) administration-advance claim. Trustees’ distinct claims remain, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery only under lawful liquidation priorities; no recovery or distribution is predetermined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Associated General Contractors, 459 U.S. 519 — applied to the derivative creditor-loss chain, attribution difficulties, overlapping recovery and available directly injured plaintiffs.
- Blue Shield of Virginia v. McCready, 457 U.S. 465 (1982) — relied on for causal limits and avoidance of overlapping damages; its recognition of a directly injured claimant's action is not displaced.
- Hawaii v. Standard Oil Co. of California, 405 U.S. 251 (1972) — applied for the concern about duplicate recovery; no state parens patriae issue is decided here.
- Southern Pacific Co. v. Darnell-Taenzer Lumber Co., 245 U.S. 531 (1918) — applied as a general direct-injury principle, without converting its first-step formulation into an exceptionless rule.

##### Whether §78eee(d) authorizes SIPC’s own administration-advance damages claim

**Holding:** Section 78eee(d) of SIPA gives SIPC party-in-interest status, a right to be heard and deemed intervention in liquidation matters; it does not itself create an independent right to recover administration advances as damages from Holmes. Neither that participation provision alone nor its combination with §1964(c) supplies a missing substantive recovery right or dispenses with RICO's own conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part III-B, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this independently sufficient statutory ground for rejecting SIPC's own §78eee(d) claim. This is distinct from the customer-subrogation causation holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide every other-law claim or the unbriefed theory of subrogation to trustees for expense advances. The statute’s participation rights and administration-advance duties remain; a separate RICO-causation ground for SIPC’s own advances is not a controlling alternative holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit’s judgment is reversed on both asserted SIPC claims and remanded with directions to enter judgment for Holmes on the derivative customer RICO claim and the asserted independent §78eee(d) administration-advance claim. Trustees’ distinct claims remain, and SIPC may share in any estate recovery only under lawful liquidation priorities; no recovery or distribution is predetermined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

No earlier decision is independently construed, extended or overruled by this provision-specific holding. Its participation/recovery distinction is consistent with the existing action/remedy distinction; it does not announce a general rule foreclosing implied or subrogated actions under other law.

### Fourteenth Amendment enforcement power

#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For RFRA's State and local application and the prospective effect on earlier statutory applications, see City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074, decided 1997-06-25, First Amendment — Religious freedom and congressional enforcement.

##### I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right

**Holding:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. Its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury it seeks to prevent or remedy; the enforcement power does not permit Congress to replace the judicially enforceable content of the substantive right with a different constitutional standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress need not prove a violation in every jurisdiction, make formal findings, use a prescribed quantity or form of evidence, require individualized proof for every prohibited act, or enact geographic limits or sunsets. Such design features may inform substantive fit but are not universal conditions. Independent Fifteenth Amendment power is not further resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** South Carolina v. Katzenbach and Katzenbach v. Morgan retain broad preventive enforcement tied to the constitutional injury, not authority to redefine the right. City of Rome remains intact in its Fifteenth Amendment setting.
