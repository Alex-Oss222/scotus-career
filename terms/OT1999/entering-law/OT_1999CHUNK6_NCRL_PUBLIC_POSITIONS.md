### Akins v. FEC, D.C. Circuit No. 94-5088

Merits decision, June 25, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

On review under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) of the en banc D.C. Circuit judgment of December 6, 1996, amended January 3, 1997. Merits heard and decided June 25, 1997. The questions concern voter informational standing, review of the Commission's complaint dismissal under FECA, the major-purpose limitation on political-committee status, and the membership and transaction predicates for disclosure.

Six politically active voters challenged dismissal of their complaint that AIPAC had to register and disclose as a political committee. The Commission treated a major electoral purpose as necessary despite its assessment of likely campaign payments above the statutory threshold. A separate corporate-prohibition enforcement decision rested on uncertainty about membership communications. The district court sustained the major-purpose construction; the en banc court rejected it and recognized standing. No final finding that all alleged payments are covered expenditures is made here.

All nine Justices participated in the merits hearing and decision.

#### Judgment

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Article III and availability of statutory legal-error review | Affirmed, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | The statutory merits may be considered; the two dissenters would vacate and require jurisdictional dismissal. |
| Rejection of the categorical major-purpose exemption across otherwise covered candidate financing | Affirmed with the stated limits, 5–4 | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Stone-Zsela would reserve the question; Kennedy supports rejection for candidate cash and genuine conduits but would vacate the broader instruction concerning the association's own coordinated expression; Scalia and Thomas do not reach merits. |
| Separate membership and transaction predicates on remand | Lawful reconsideration required, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Thomas | No committee designation, disclosure award, compelled prosecution or penalty; the five-Justice construction governs the remand. |

The en banc judgment stands at the bounded scope stated here. The case returns through the statutory review channel for the Commission's consideration of the actual coverage questions. The Court does not disturb the separate corporate-prohibition nonenforcement choice merely by correcting the political-committee dismissal's legal premise.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The continuing denial of particular electoral information is a concrete injury

**Holding and operative rule:** Voters denied particular campaign-financing information allegedly required by FECA, which they seek for their own electoral participation, have a concrete informational injury even when many voters share it. Here the Commission's legal ground blocks a statutory process capable of supplying information still sought; the possibility of a later lawful discretionary choice does not defeat traceability or redressability.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's Court opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Public Citizen recognizes concrete denial of useful information without requiring that the injury be numerically exclusive. Lujan still requires the plaintiffs' own concrete interest, causation and likely redress, supported as the litigation stage requires. These voters identify electoral use of particular information that remains withheld. They assert more than opposition to AIPAC's policies or a general demand that government obey the law. The contrary argument treats any elector's interest in campaign oversight as generalized. That characterization overlooks the specific information and practical use alleged here; complaint status alone would not suffice. Bennett requires examination of the actual remedial chain despite retained agency choice. Correction of the legal premise actually used can restore the disclosure process, although the Court guarantees neither prosecution nor ultimate classification. Steel Co. involved reports already supplied and a completed deprivation that the requested remedies did not redress. Its result does not govern information still sought here. Bennett does not establish the facts of this case, and standing does not decide whether every requested item is legally owed.

##### II. FECA permits review of this dismissal's legal premise

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 437g(a)(8) permits an aggrieved complainant to obtain judicial review of a complaint dismissal within sixty days and permits correction of a legally erroneous coverage premise. A court may declare the dismissal contrary to law and may direct conformity within thirty days; if the Commission fails to conform, the complainant may bring an action to remedy the violation involved in the original complaint.

**Authority:** Part II has the same seven-Justice coalition as Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress expressly provided this channel, unlike a claim based only on a general wish to supervise executive enforcement. Brown Group distinguishes an enacted enforcement route from the separate Article III requirement; Bennett requires use of the channel that actually authorizes the claim. The political-committee dismissal rests on a legal construction. Lawful enforcement priorities do not insulate that construction from the review Congress provided. Conversely, review does not compel prosecution, disclosure or a penalty. The distinct 120-day inaction route is not invoked. The Commission's close-membership explanation for its separate corporate-prohibition decision cannot become an unexercised replacement rationale for this dismissal. Legi-Tech preserves actual lawful Commission decisionmaking and statutory prerequisites; it supplies neither an automatic remedy nor a generic review bar. Genuine statutory ambiguities and remaining lawful discretion remain for appropriate agency consideration. The Commission's reading of constitutional decisions is not conclusive merely because it administers FECA.

##### III. Major purpose is not a categorical exemption for otherwise covered candidate financing

**Holding and operative rule:** Buckley and MCFL do not establish an organization-wide major-purpose exemption from political-committee treatment for genuinely covered candidate financing under §431(4)(A). That provision reaches a committee, club, association, or other group of persons receiving contributions aggregating in excess of $1,000 in a calendar year or making expenditures aggregating in excess of $1,000 in a calendar year, subject to the actual definitions, exclusions and constitutional limits; it does not literally make every group that makes a contribution a political committee.

**Authority:** Part III, including III-A for actual candidate cash transfers and genuine conduits and III-B for other genuinely covered candidate-connected financing, is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct votes with Souter. Kennedy supplies a sixth vote only for III-A. Stone-Zsela reserves both subparts. No combination of separate rationales is necessary to make the five-Justice rule controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Buckley's narrowing protects independent electoral discussion from being swept indiscriminately into regulation, while distinguishing resources supplied to candidates. MCFL's independent-spending setting likewise does not give every predominantly issue-oriented organization immunity for its covered candidate financing. The strongest contrary argument concerns the organizational and associational burdens of political-committee status beyond a single transaction. Those concerns require the actual statutory predicates and preserve supported challenges to particular duties; they do not justify a categorical exemption merely because lobbying occupies most of a budget. A candidate payment may fall within §431(9)'s expenditure definition, but its purpose, amount and exclusions must be established. Colorado Republican requires the real statutory candidate connection: cooperation, consultation, concert, request or suggestion where that contribution theory is invoked. Ideological alignment, lobbying, policy cooperation or partisan identity alone is insufficient. Genuine independent expression remains protected. The Court neither classifies AIPAC conclusively nor sustains every organizational or donor-disclosure burden on every application. The Commission must examine actual transactions after the relevant exclusions, rather than stop at its categorical major-purpose premise.

##### IV. Membership exclusions and their distinct reporting proviso govern the remand

**Holding and operative rule:** The Commission must determine membership, covered transactions, candidate connection, calendar-year thresholds and applicable exclusions before imposing political-committee duties. Section 431(9)(B)(iii)'s membership-communication exclusion retains its separate reporting proviso; §441b(b)(2)'s exclusions and the §431 cross-references retain their different terms.

**Authority:** Part IV has the same seven-Justice coalition as Parts I and II. The statutory construction in Part III governs the Court's remand notwithstanding Stone-Zsela's and Kennedy's narrower preferences.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 431(9)(B)(iii) excludes communications by a membership organization or corporation to its members, stockholders, or executive or administrative personnel if it is not organized primarily to influence nomination or election to federal office. The proviso requires a membership organization, including a labor organization, or a corporation to report costs directly attributable to communications expressly advocating election or defeat of a clearly identified candidate when those costs exceed $2,000 per election, except a communication primarily devoted to other subjects. Reporting follows §434(a)(4)(A)(i) and, for a general election, §434(a)(4)(A)(ii). This is neither the $1,000 calendar-year committee threshold nor an exception transplanted into §441b. Section 441b(b)(2) separately excludes corporate communications on any subject to stockholders and executive or administrative personnel and their families, and labor-organization communications to members and their families. Its nonpartisan registration and get-out-the-vote exclusion covers the same respective recipients. Its separate-fund exclusion covers establishment, administration and solicitation of contributions to a separate segregated fund used for political purposes by a corporation, labor organization, membership organization, cooperative or corporation without capital stock. The loan exception concerns a national or State bank's ordinary-course loan made under applicable banking law and regulations. Sections 431(8)(B)(vi) and (9)(B)(v) cross-reference activity allowed by §441b without importing the $2,000 proviso. The agency must apply each provision to the actual record. No final membership finding, coordination finding, threshold calculation, later membership rule, donor list, liability or penalty is supplied by this decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Public Citizen supplies the informational-access analogy; a concrete injury does not become abstract because widely shared.
- Lujan supplies personal injury, causation, redressability and stage-appropriate support; statutory complaint permission alone remains insufficient.
- Brown Group preserves the distinction between statutory enforcement authorization and Article III.
- Bennett supports a real remedial opportunity despite retained lawful agency choice; its particular agency-compliance facts are not attributed to this case.
- Steel Co. requires the disputed Article III inquiry before these contested merits and is distinguished because its reports were already current and its asserted injury completed.
- Buckley is applied to distinguish candidate resources from independent expression; its major-purpose narrowing is not extended to categorically exempt otherwise covered candidate financing.
- MCFL retains its independent-expression protection and does not establish the categorical exemption claimed here.
- Colorado Republican retains genuine independent-expression protection and requires an actual candidate connection; neither organizational identity nor policy agreement supplies coordination.
- Legi-Tech preserves lawful agency reconsideration and statutory predicates without creating a general immunity from legal-error review.

#### Law After Decision

FECA complainants with the concrete continuing informational injury established here may obtain review of the Commission's actual legal ground through §437g(a)(8). A predominantly lobbying purpose does not alone exempt genuinely covered candidate financing from the political-committee inquiry. Membership, actual transactions and distinct statutory exceptions remain essential, and genuine independent expression remains protected. No automatic committee status, prosecution, disclosure award or penalty follows. The Court overrules no precedent.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stone-Zsela concurs in Parts I, II and IV and dissents from the major-purpose disposition. The unresolved membership-communications exclusion may dispose of some or all of the controversy. He would affirm standing and reviewability, vacate the rest of the en banc judgment, and have the Commission decide membership first. He therefore reserves whether Buckley and MCFL's major-purpose limitation reaches qualifying contributions or coordinated transactions, while preserving Colorado Republican's actual candidate-connection rule and lawful agency discretion.

Kennedy concurs in Parts I, II, III-A and IV and dissents from the broader merits instruction. Actual candidate cash and genuine conduits differ from an association's own expression even when cooperation occurs. His Colorado Republican position preserves that distinction. He would reject a major-purpose shield for the former, but require identification of the actual transactions and application of membership exclusions before resolving the constitutional treatment of the latter. He neither endorses the Commission's blanket exemption nor assumes independence merely from AIPAC's lobbying purpose.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissents. Lujan requires a concrete stake personal to the litigants, and statutory permission to complain does not supply it. In their view, the supported interest in learning about AIPAC's electoral influence remains the public's undifferentiated interest in informed oversight. Public Citizen involved denied direct access; it does not eliminate the personal-stake inquiry when plaintiffs seek regulatory classification and future third-party reports. They would vacate the merits judgment and remand for jurisdictional dismissal. They do not hold every shared information injury abstract, prescribe an affidavit requirement, treat agency discretion as invariably fatal to redressability, or decide the major-purpose question.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the D.C. Circuit and the §437g(a)(8) review channel for lawful Commission consideration under the Court's construction. The Commission must address membership and actual covered transactions, preserving genuine independent expression and permissible discretion. The Court itself directs no registration, report, penalty or prosecution and determines no final statutory violation.

### Federal Election Commission v. Maine Right to Life Committee, Inc., No. 96-1818

Plenary merits decision on submission, September 14, 1998

#### Chronology and Posture

Review of the First Circuit's October 18, 1996 affirmance, 98 F.3d 1, of the District of Maine's judgment and reconsideration ruling, 914 F. Supp. 8. No exact oral-argument date is established.

The question is whether 11 C.F.R. §100.22(b)'s context-sensitive definition of express advocacy exceeds 2 U.S.C. §441b as construed in Buckley v. Valeo and FEC v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life (MCFL), and whether the association had to request an advisory opinion before bringing this pre-enforcement challenge. MRLC publishes recurring newsletters, candidate surveys and issue advocacy, and accepts some corporate money. The District Court found actual cessation of speech after the rule took effect October 5, 1995, with no disputed material adjudicative facts. It invalidated paragraph (b) on statutory grounds, reserved standalone vagueness, dismissed the Attorney General, and denied further unnecessary relief.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Present controversy and optional advisory route | Permit the legal challenge without compulsory resort to §437f, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer |  | Threshold conclusion sustained. |
| Statutory invalidation of §100.22(b) | Vacate and remand, 5–4 | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Unresolved vagueness and properly presented applications return under the restrictive construction. |

The four dissenters would affirm the statutory declaration and reserve standalone vagueness. Subsection (a), lawful contribution and membership rules, the Attorney General's dismissal and denial of unrelated additional relief remain undisturbed. No publication is adjudged prohibited, no enforcement is ordered, and no substitute rule or new injunction is imposed.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer in full | Threshold ruling and vacatur/remand | Parts I–III |
| Same opinion, partial joins | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas | Agree with threshold ruling; oppose merits vacatur | Part I only, on the stated statutory-design and actual-controversy ground |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Thomas | Would affirm statutory invalidity | Express-terms boundary and bounded declaratory remedy; joins Court Part I |

The merits construction has five direct votes. No combined or narrower-rationale analysis is needed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. This live challenge does not require an advisory opinion first

**Holding and operative rule:** An association whose recurring issue communications are subject to an effective FEC regulation and whose speech has actually ceased because of its credible application may seek pre-enforcement review of the rule's legal reach. Section 437f's permissive advisory-opinion procedure is not a compulsory exhaustion requirement for this challenge and cannot furnish an agency determination invalidating the governing rule.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine join the statutory-design and actual-controversy ground; no broader exhaustion-balancing proposition is adopted.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that MRLC need not seek advance official permission for each prospective communication before presenting this facial legal claim. The trial findings establish ongoing speech activity and actual cessation after the regulation became effective, rather than a hypothetical speaker's objection to a dormant rule. McCarthy v. Madigan makes the enacted administrative scheme central; FECA makes this advisory route available without making it mandatory. The remedy sought is a legal decision about the regulation itself, which an advisory opinion cannot invalidate. The association supplies a sufficient controversy for the relief sought, so the Court need not decide Corbett's independent recipient-standing theory. Nor does this conclusion disturb dismissal of the Attorney General, against whom no supported enforcement threat was shown. A materially different administrative scheme or an entirely hypothetical future communication is not decided.

**Precedent treatment:** McCarthy's statutory-primacy principle applies; its broader discretionary balancing is unnecessary here. Its later prison-specific statutory modification does not impose exhaustion under FECA.

##### 2. The rule's cumulative restrictions preserve the statutory express-advocacy boundary

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 2 U.S.C. §441b, 11 C.F.R. §100.22(b) does not exceed the statute merely because it permits limited context rather than an exclusive list of electoral slogans. Its coverage requires a communication considered as a whole, only limited reference to external events, an unmistakable and unambiguous electoral portion suggestive of only one meaning, and no reasonable difference over whether it encourages election or defeat of a clearly identified candidate rather than another kind of action; a reasonable nonelectoral interpretation excludes coverage.

**Authority:** Parts II–III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court are joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from the statutory disposition and do not join this construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Buckley v. Valeo and FEC v. Massachusetts Citizens for Life require express electoral advocacy without prescribing a single verbal formula. The lower court itself recognized that MCFL included a marginally less direct explicit appeal; the disagreement is whether paragraph (b)'s contextual route necessarily crosses the narrowed statute's boundary. Its cumulative conditions need not do so. Election proximity, expected electoral effect, policy agreement, hostility toward a candidate, or an agency's preferred reading cannot replace the required unambiguous electoral direction. Limited context may inform the communication's meaning, but cannot displace a reasonable call to issue debate or some other action. The contrary concern that unchanged issue speech will be reclassified whenever an election approaches is answered at this statutory level by enforcing every restriction together, not by deferring to the Commission. A court must independently test the communication against them. This interpretation rejects categorical statutory invalidity; it neither decides the unresolved constitutional vagueness claim nor holds the rule valid in every application. It finds no MRLC publication unlawful and does not order enforcement.

**Precedent treatment:** Buckley and MCFL retain their narrowing construction and protection of genuine issue discussion; the rule is permissible only within that boundary. Akins v. FEC's insistence on actual statutory conditions and independent judicial resolution is applied, without enlarging its distinct coverage holdings.

##### 3. The unresolved constitutional challenge returns for adjudication

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical declaration that §100.22(b) exceeds statutory authority is vacated, and the case is remanded for the unresolved vagueness challenge and any properly presented application issues under the restrictive construction stated above. Subsection (a), separate contribution rules, distinct membership exclusions, and nonprofit protection based on actual characteristics are not invalidated or generally approved by this disposition.

**Authority:** Part III has the same five-Justice merits coalition. The four dissenters would leave the bounded statutory declaration intact instead.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court removes the statutory premise of the judgment below, not the separate questions that premise made unnecessary to decide. A statutory construction alone cannot establish constitutional clarity in every application. No injunction against an otherwise unsupported enforcement threat, replacement rule, or judicial supervision of rulemaking follows. Day v. Holahan and MCFL require attention to an organization's actual political resources and financing; the acceptance of some corporate contributions does not establish MRLC's ultimate category, and no such determination is needed here. Lawful contribution enforcement and the Attorney General's dismissal remain separate. The remand permits the unresolved claims to be adjudicated without treating today's decision as a directive to prosecute.

**Precedent treatment:** Day and MCFL retain their fact-dependent nonprofit protection. Austin v. Michigan Chamber of Commerce remains within its surviving corporate-treasury scope; nothing here approves every corporate restriction.

#### Precedent Treatment

- McCarthy: applies statutory primacy to the permissive FECA advisory process; no broad exhaustion exception is created.
- Buckley: retains protection of issue discussion through the express-electoral-advocacy construction; no exclusive vocabulary is required.
- MCFL: retains its express-advocacy construction and distinct nonprofit protection based on actual characteristics.
- Akins: applies actual statutory predicates and independent judicial review without extending its membership or political-committee holdings.
- Day: preserves actual nonprofit protection despite some corporate funding; no finding about MRLC's full status is made.
- Austin: neither overruled nor enlarged into general approval of every corporate restriction.

#### Law After Decision

Paragraph (b) is no longer categorically invalid on the statutory ground adopted below. Every cumulative condition remains binding: limited context cannot replace an unmistakable electoral direction or defeat a reasonable nonelectoral meaning. The Court leaves constitutional vagueness and concrete applications open. The decision changes no distinct membership exclusion, contribution predicate or nonprofit-status rule, and decides no separate voter-guide regulation.

#### Separate Writings

Kennedy, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Thomas, concurs in Part I and dissents from Parts II–III and the vacatur. He would affirm the declaration that paragraph (b) exceeds the statute while leaving standalone vagueness undecided. Buckley and MCFL protect an ascertainable express-terms boundary before publication: the speaker must supply the electoral direction, rather than having the regulator infer it from election proximity and external events. The Commission's treatment of character and qualification discussion permits precisely the shifting classification that the narrowing construction prevents. The no-reasonable-alternative formula does not cure that enlargement of delegated authority.

The dissent does not demand one canonical slogan. An explicit command, including a functional phrase unambiguously instructing a vote for identified candidates, remains different from policy persuasion with an electoral implication. Ordinary context may identify the candidate or explain express words; it may not supply the absent electoral command. Corporate funding cannot itself transform issue speech into a contribution or express exhortation. The dissent preserves subsection (a), actual candidate contributions, genuinely coordinated expenses and corporate-treasury regulation within lawful categories. It proposes no replacement regulation, general ban on context-sensitive interpretation, corporate exemption, or additional injunction.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The vacatur authorizes no automatic enforcement or finding against MRLC. Ordinary mandate procedure applies; no special deadline or new factfinding requirement is imposed.
