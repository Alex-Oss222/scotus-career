## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.
- Corrective purpose and distinction from judicial interpretation: Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach.

**Exceptions and limits:** An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. Landgraf concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Related current rules:** Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments supplies Morgan Stanley's and Plaut's separate Article III rule for §27A(b).

## Standards and Tests selection: APA persuasion burdens and statutory departures

### APA persuasion burdens and statutory departures

**Governing question:** Who loses when evidence is genuinely balanced in an adjudication governed by APA §556(d).

**Current rule:** Unless a controlling statute otherwise provides, §556(d)’s burden of proof assigns the burden of persuasion to the proponent of the order; in the proceedings governed here, the proponent must establish the claim by a preponderance and loses at genuine equipoise. Burdens of producing evidence, lawful presumptions and separately allocated defenses remain distinct. The Longshore Act’s incorporation of APA adjudication is not displaced by §23’s evidentiary flexibility or its treatment of corroborated declarations of deceased employees. In the black-lung setting, even assuming statutory authority for an otherwise valid regulatory departure, the invoked remedial-policy provision does not establish an ultimate tie-breaking rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Persuasion allocation: Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries — The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order, 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six participating Justices directly adopt this allocation, its application to both proceedings, and the limited treatment of prior precedent; no aggregation of different grounds is necessary.
- Longshore incorporation despite evidentiary flexibility: Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries — The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation, 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the Longshore incorporation holding and apply it to both the lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims in Maher Terminals.
- No established black-lung departure: Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries — The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA, 1994-06-20. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the actual-regulation ground, the express reservation of broader delegated power, and the bounded administrative remedy.

**Present operation effective:** Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, 1994-06-20.

**Exceptions and limits:** Transportation Management’s contrary description of the APA burden is disapproved; its distinct mixed-motive defense allocation is not overruled. The full scope of power to create a black-lung exception is left open.

**Holdings navigation:** The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order; The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation; The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA.

## Standards and Tests selection: Controlling weight for a responsible agency's fair regulatory interpretation

### Controlling weight for a responsible agency's fair regulatory interpretation

**Governing question:** Judicial treatment of a responsible agency's interpretation of its own regulation, including a construction presented in an amicus brief.

**Current rule:** The responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Courts must assess the reading against the operative text, lawful allocation of interpretive responsibility and supported agency treatment. An amicus presentation neither disqualifies the construction nor establishes its correctness. Interpretive authority does not permit amendment of contrary text, unsupported litigation rationalization, automatic authority for an agency lawyer or deference to unproved employer-specific factual assertions.

**Authority by component:**

- Auer v. Robbins — The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds, 1997-02-19; Kennedy Part III, joined by all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela's replacement proposal is noncontrolling

**Present operation effective:** Auer, 1997-02-19, for the amicus and factual-assertion boundaries.

**Related current rules:** Authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary preserves its separate statutory and constitutional limits. Medicare educational costs shifted from a related institution remains the distinct substantive reimbursement rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Auer v. Robbins (1997-02-19) — The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds.

## Standards and Tests selection: Whole-record agency review under the announced substantive standard

### Whole-record agency review under the announced substantive standard

**Governing question:** Substantial-evidence review where the agency allegedly applied a more demanding standard than the one it announced.

**Current rule:** Under NLRA §§10(e)–(f) and APA §706, the agency finding must have substantial whole-record support under the actual governing substantive standard. The agency cannot silently turn reasonable doubt into near-proof of the contrary fact, or convert recurring evidentiary outcomes into a different substantive or proof rule. Review includes evidence fairly detracting from the finding while preserving the agency’s choice between fairly conflicting views. The agency’s §10(c) preponderance burden remains distinct from appellate substantial-evidence review.

**Authority by component:**

- An announced doubt standard cannot silently become minority proof: Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB, 1998-01-26; Scalia Parts III–IV with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** Lawful expressly adopted presumptions and evidentiary rules remain possible subject to statutory authority and ordinary review. This rule creates no de novo factfinding authority, mandatory treatment of every unverified report, or finding of systematic agency manipulation.

**Holdings navigation:** Allentown Mack Sales & Service, Inc. v. NLRB — Incumbent-union polling and agency evidentiary standards.

### Civil statutory temporal reach

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Federal Courts — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences.

##### Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct

**Holding:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive liability does not attach to discriminatory conduct completed before November 21, 1991, because it adds legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. Its Title VII compensation supplements, rather than replaces, §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by that provision; punitive damages have the distinct statutory predicates of malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude government, government-agency and political-subdivision respondents. No such damages award or predicate finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bennett and Bowen: applied to the new monetary burden attached to completed conduct.
- Bradley: distinguished; the collateral fee award did not create this new compensation or punishment for the underlying private conduct.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992): distinguished; appropriate relief in an implied Title IX action does not enlarge Title VII’s earlier express remedial scheme.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished; its express substitution of law governing identified pending disputes does not supply the temporal direction missing here.

##### The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim

**Holding:** Under §102(c), if the complaining party seeks compensatory or punitive damages available under that section, any party may demand a jury trial, and the court must not inform the jury of the statutory monetary limits. Because §102 damages are unavailable for this preenactment conduct, its conditional jury provision supplies no independent right to a new trial; this conclusion does not prohibit applying a genuinely procedural change to an appropriate future procedural act in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser: applied to the enacted condition linking the jury option to §102 damages.
- Bradley: distinguished; current-law treatment of collateral matters does not erase an express condition on a new remedy or automatically reopen a completed trial.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

### Agency interpretation, policy change and antecedent transactions

#### Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., 517 U.S. 735

**Docket or dockets:** 95-860
**Decided:** June 3, 1996

##### Does interpretive change or a rule's development during litigation categorically defeat deference?

**Holding:** A lawful, adequately explained interpretation within delegated statutory ambiguity does not lose eligibility for deference merely because it changes or addresses an old statute during litigation. Unexplained reversal or disregard of legitimate reliance may be arbitrary and capricious; the holding independently rejects the categorical inconsistency objection without approving every change.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-A, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice controlling majority. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment and Parts I, II, IV and VI but does not join Parts III-A, III-B or V. Parts III-A and III-B are independently sufficient answers to the asserted prior-policy barrier; they are not combined into a narrower rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Good Samaritan permits reasoned change within lawful authority while requiring statutory fit, adequate explanation and attention to legitimate reliance. Neither statutory age nor litigation impetus supplies a categorical exclusion. The OCC formally proposed its interpretation on March 3, 1995, received 112 comment letters, and explained its response and grounds in the final rule published February 9, 1996. That process differs from a position invented by litigation counsel. The Court relies on the agency's stated clarification of uncertainty, not a judicially invented economic or reliance finding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Those procedural facts do not certify compliance with every rulemaking statute. Permission to change an interpretation does not itself authorize retrospective alteration of completed conduct's legal consequences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Good Samaritan permits explained interpretive change while retaining authority, statutory-fit and reliance limits. Bowen continues to reject automatic weight for unsupported litigation rationales.

##### Did the earlier letters establish clear official guidance contrary to this rule?

**Holding:** Independently, the identified 1964 and 1988 letters did not establish clear, settled official guidance contrary to the current interpretation; their existence does not itself make the formal rule an unexplained reversal of a binding prior position. This is a second independently sufficient answer to the claimed prior-policy obstacle.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-B, joined by the same seven other Associate Justices; eight-Justice majority. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The 1964 informal automobile-financing statement listed late charges as noninterest items, while the 1988 deputy-counsel response used a home-state-definition approach. Their authority and context differ, and they conflict on whether federal or state law defines the category. They therefore do not establish the clear prior interpretation asserted. The formal rule's explanation that it resolves uncertainty supplies its own reason for a federal definition. Good Samaritan requires attention to agency reasons without treating every informal formulation as binding; Bowen's rejection of unsupported litigation positions does not erase the separately published rule's own explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This conclusion concerns these documents' legal force, not a factual finding about any borrower's actual reliance or a general rule that informal agency materials never matter.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Good Samaritan's reason-giving requirement remains; Bowen's bar against unsupported litigation rationales is distinguished from the independently explained formal interpretation.

##### May a court use the current authoritative interpretation for these antecedent transactions?

**Holding:** In determining statutory meaning for antecedent transactions that lacked clear prior agency guidance, a court may use the current authoritative interpretation without backdating the rule's effective date. The materially different case of a later interpretation displacing clear earlier guidance remains reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part V, joined by the same seven other Associate Justices; eight-Justice majority. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The issue is what section 85 means for these transactions. Part III-B establishes that the cited letters supplied no clear contrary guidance; Part III-A's general allowance for reasoned change would not alone establish retrospective permissibility. Bowen bars unauthorized retroactive legislative rulemaking but does not require a court to disregard current authoritative statutory interpretation in these circumstances. An objection based on displacement of a clear prior interpretation could have substance and is not decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The April 1, 1996 effective date remains unchanged. The rule has no special transition or retroactivity clause; no settled judgment is reopened, no actual-reliance finding is made, and no waiver or preservation ruling resolves the objection. The Court gives no universal permission to alter completed conduct's legal consequences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bowen's prohibition of unauthorized retroactive legislative rulemaking remains; qualified judicial use of current statutory interpretation without clear prior guidance is distinguished.

##### Does merits deference resolve independent section 43 procedure objections?

**Holding:** Deference to the substantive statutory interpretation does not excuse an established procedural violation or adjudicate every independent rule-validity challenge. Section 43's coverage, advance procedure, final-publication duty and differently scoped exceptions remain operative, without a finding of applicability, compliance, violation, preservation or an exception here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part VI, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling qualification.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 43 covers agency opinion letters or interpretive rules, issued on request or on the agency's initiative, concluding that federal law preempts application to a national bank of state community-reinvestment, consumer-protection, fair-lending or intrastate-branch law, and the specified section 36(f)(1)(A)(ii) determinations. Subsection (a) requires advance Federal Register notice describing the issues and each state law, at least thirty days for written comment, and consideration of comments received. Subsection (b) separately requires publication of the specified final action.

Subsection (c)(1) exempts the entire section where essentially identical preemption issues were previously resolved by courts or addressed in an agency opinion or interpretive rule, or where a response concerns a request with no significant legal basis for a preemption determination. Subsection (c)(2) exempts the entire section for materials prepared for judicial proceedings, submission to Congress or a Member, or intragovernmental use. Subsection (c)(3) exempts only advance subsection (a) procedure: it requires a written necessity determination to avoid a serious and imminent threat to a national bank's safety and soundness, or concerns an opinion or rule connected to acquisition of banks in statutory default or danger of default, or an acquisition receiving FDIC assistance under section 1823(c). Final publication remains required under that exception.

Institutional default requires official custodial appointment. Danger of default requires the specified agency advice or determination of inability to meet demands or obligations, or depletion of substantially all capital, with no reasonable prospect of recovery without federal assistance. Borrower delinquency supplies none of these institutional conditions. Published proposals and received comments do not prove every statutory requirement; interpretive form, later use in litigation, section 93a authority and lack of clear prior guidance prove neither exemption nor compliance. Distinguishing substantive classification from its preemptive consequence does not categorically remove section 43.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independently dispositive procedural defect is established; missing documentary proof creates no presumption for either party or alternative disposition. The Court does not decide every paragraph of the rule or every potential procedural challenge.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California judgment is affirmed, 9-0, without agency or evidentiary remand, a special transition rule or newly ordered agency action. The confined section 85 classification and state-law displacement appear under Business and Commercial Law, National-bank interest classification and interstate permission.

### Administrative burdens of persuasion

#### Director, Office of Workers’ Compensation Programs v. Greenwich Collieries, 512 U.S. 267

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-744, including Maher Terminals under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Maher Terminals is included under Rule 12.2, covering lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims; Greenwich concerns black-lung benefits.

##### The APA assigns persuasion to the proponent of an order

**Holding:** When APA §556(d) governs and no applicable statutory exception provides otherwise, the proponent of a rule or order bears the burden of persuasion, not merely the obligation to produce evidence. With the preponderance standard governing these benefits proceedings, a claimant cannot prevail on a necessary element left in genuine evidentiary equipoise after complete weighing and application of operative statutory presumptions and lawful defense burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six participating Justices directly adopt this allocation, its application to both proceedings, and the limited treatment of prior precedent; no aggregation of different grounds is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): preserves its preponderance standard and uses its explanation of the APA framework; its proof-quantum holding is not recast as an earlier decision squarely settling allocation.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393, 404 n.7 (1983): disapproves the production-only construction of APA §7(c), now §556(d), to the extent inconsistent with the persuasion allocation; preserves the Wright Line holding requiring the General Counsel to prove unlawful motivation and permitting an employer same-decision affirmative defense.

##### The Longshore Act's evidentiary flexibility does not displace the APA allocation

**Holding:** Longshore Act §19(d), 33 U.S.C. §919(d), requires APA adjudication notwithstanding other provisions of the chapter, and §23(a), 33 U.S.C. §923(a), does not exempt these claims from §556(d)'s persuasion allocation by relaxing evidentiary and technical procedural rules. Section 923(a) separately requires reception of a deceased employee's declarations concerning the injury under investigation and makes them sufficient to establish the injury when corroborated by other evidence; that preserved rule does not establish Santoro's disputed aggravation and hastened death on the claim presented here, and the vacatur and remand for a lawful preponderance determination are affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six adopt the Longshore incorporation holding and apply it to both the lifetime-disability and death-benefit claims in Maher Terminals.

**Material application:** Section 923(a) frees the deputy commissioner or Board from common-law or statutory evidence rules and technical or formal procedure except as provided by the chapter; it permits, rather than commands, a chosen investigative or hearing method best suited to ascertain the parties' rights. Its deceased-declaration reception and corroborated-sufficiency commands are separate. The §20(a) presumption that a claim comes within the Act operates in the absence of substantial evidence to the contrary; the ALJ found it rebutted here, and it is no longer evidence supporting ultimate persuasion. The causal questions concern aggravation and hastened death, not an established finding that the workplace incident originally caused the cancer. Incomplete weighing remains a question for administrative reconsideration and ordinary review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): applies the preponderance quantum in APA adjudication while retaining the distinction between agency factfinding and appellate review.
- NLRB v. Transportation Management Corp., 462 U.S. 393 (1983): retains genuine affirmative-defense burdens; it does not authorize transfer of claimant persuasion merely because a benefits scheme is remedial.

##### The invoked black-lung regulation does not establish a departure from the APA

**Holding:** The Black Lung Benefits Act's §932(a) incorporates the relevant Longshore adjudicatory provisions subject to its statutory and regulatory qualifications; even assuming it authorizes an appropriate regulatory departure from APA §556(d), §718.3(c)'s statement of a congressional reasonable-doubt policy does not establish that departure. The APA persuasion rule therefore governs the disputed pneumoconiosis and total-disability determinations here, and the Greenwich vacatur and remand are affirmed without deciding the full scope of §932(a)'s regulatory power or adopting an independent ground under §718.403 or Mullins.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices adopt the actual-regulation ground, the express reservation of broader delegated power, and the bounded administrative remedy.

**Material application:** Section 932(a) applies after December 31, 1973 during periods when the State's compensation law is not on the Secretary's §931(b) list, to coal-mine operators respecting death or total disability due to pneumoconiosis arising from mine employment, or entitlements under §921(c)(5). It excludes Longshore Act §§1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 37, 38, 41, 43, 44, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50 and 51. Incorporation is except as otherwise provided in §932(a) or by the Secretary's regulations, and references to the employer refer to fund trustees as the Secretary considers appropriate consistently with 26 U.S.C. §9501(d). Additional provisions the Secretary prescribes in the Federal Register to provide payment to entitled persons may not be inconsistent with the provisions specifically excluded. The cross-reference to §28(h)(1) of the 1984 Longshore amendments excludes that Act's §7 medical-services amendments from black-lung claims. None of those excluded-provision, fund-substitution or medical-services matters is adjudicated here.

Section 718.3(c) expresses the policy of giving claimants the benefit of all reasonable doubt about total or partial disability or death due to pneumoconiosis; it does not expressly prescribe an ultimate equipoise award. Section 718.403 places proof of an alleged fact on the party alleging it except as provided in the subchapter, but its independent effect is not decided. Ondecko's more than ten years of coal-mine employment bears on whether established pneumoconiosis arose from that employment; it does not supply the separate disease and total-disability findings disputed here. An ALJ must not stop weighing at an apparent tie or rename a tie a slight preponderance.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court determines no final entitlement, medical fact, benefit amount or award of relief to either employer. It preserves statutory exceptions and presumptions according to their own triggers and effects, does not settle the full regulatory-exception power under §932(a), and does not decide the independent §718.403/Mullins ground. There is no separate alternative holding and no fractured rationale requiring a Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mullins Coal Co. v. Director, OWCP, 484 U.S. 135 (1987): preserves its requirement of actual weighing under its interim-presumption regulation and its reservation of the APA question; the Court does not adopt the Third Circuit's separate use of Mullins and §718.403 as an independent bar.
- Steadman v. SEC, 450 U.S. 91 (1981): preserves the preponderance standard while the incorporated APA allocation determines the consequence of genuine equipoise.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Third Circuit judgments are affirmed: the Greenwich award and Maher lifetime-disability and death-benefit orders remain vacated for administrative reconsideration. The ALJs must complete weighing under the persuasion rule, operative presumptions and lawful defense burdens. No final medical finding, unconditional denial or restoration of benefits, employer entitlement or dollar amount is ordered.

### FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp. — 2000-03-21

[Record](../records/fda-v-brown-and-williamson_merits_2000-03-21.md)

#### Event and question

FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp., No. 98-1152. Argued December 1, 1999; decided March 21, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, 153 F.3d 155, decided August 14, 1998.

The question is whether the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act authorizes the FDA's asserted drug-and-device regulation of cigarettes and smokeless tobacco as customarily marketed. The agency classified nicotine as a drug and its delivery components as devices, treated the products as combinations, and issued its August 28, 1996 youth-sale, distribution and advertising rule under device authorities. It relied on pharmacological effects, consumer use, manufacturer knowledge and deliberate nicotine-delivery design. The regulated parties did not dispute the agency's factual findings for this appeal; they disputed their statutory consequences.

The FDA acknowledged serious health risks but maintained that abrupt removal could cause greater harm to addicted adults through withdrawal and unsafe substitutes, while youth restrictions could prevent addiction. The courts below disagreed about whether that approach could fit the statute. No new epidemiological finding is made here.

The District Court sustained general authority and device treatment but rejected advertising authority, staying most restrictions while leaving age verification and the under-eighteen sales prohibition in place. Certified interlocutory and injunction appeals brought the case to the Fourth Circuit. That court invalidated the entire August 28 rule for lack of statutory authority and vacated the District Court's separate advertising construction without approving or disapproving it.

**Common authority:** The Chief Justice's opinion, joined throughout by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, directly controls each holding and its stated qualifications.

#### The 1997 Act preserves the authority question and specifies the earlier tobacco regime

**Holding and operative rule:** Public Law 105-115, section 422, leaves the question of tobacco authority unchanged and requires any such authority to be exercised under the FDCA as it stood on November 20, 1997. The Act's new mission and regulatory procedures therefore supply neither a new tobacco delegation nor a means to avoid a restriction of that earlier regime.

**Controlling explanation:** The two commands perform different work. Reserving whether authority exists favors neither party's construction; specifying the earlier Act identifies the rules under which any authority operates. Section 501's general effective date, ninety days after November 21, 1997, is subject to other express provisions and excludes sections 111, 121, 125 and 307. That general provision does not override section 422.

Section 406's mission is mandatory in its own field, with its stated product and consultation qualifications. It is not part of the earlier tobacco regime. Nor may the later classification-default, de novo, postmarket, least-burdensome or added clinical-evidence procedures be used here. The earlier provisions retain their own qualified scientific-evidence and reclassification routes; excluding later additions does not erase that existing flexibility or establish a new numerical minimum of clinical studies. The authority question must be decided under the actual statutory system, not by importing only the later provisions favorable to one side.

#### The asserted customary-tobacco regime exceeds the delegated authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Read with its operative safety and marketing requirements and Congress's distinct tobacco enactments, the FDCA does not authorize the asserted drug/device regime for tobacco as customarily marketed. The FDA cannot obtain that power by construing broad coverage terms to include a continuing-market accommodation that the connected statutory requirements do not permit; the challenged rule is invalid on that ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court assumes for this inquiry the force of the agency's intended-use evidence. Section 321's structure-and-function category is not confined to therapeutic advertising, and nicotine's chemical action does not itself answer the classification of a delivery component. But those definitions operate within the statute's duties, not apart from them.

Section 353(g)'s assignment of primary responsibility by a combination's mode of action does not exempt it from otherwise applicable drug obligations. Misbranding, applicable new-drug approval, device safety and conditional cessation duties remain independent of section 360j(e)'s restriction power. Graduated controls permit real choices; they do not confer a general power to dispense with those duties. The agency's accepted danger findings and its proposal to retain customary tobacco through restrictions expose the conflict. A preference for the least harmful available policy supplies no universal exception to the enacted commands.

Congress's cigarette-labeling, advertising, smokeless-tobacco, reporting and youth-access enactments reinforce this reading at their actual scopes. They are not individually express exemptions from all health law. Their combined treatment of customary tobacco, read with the FDCA's operating requirements, does not support the asserted regime. Failed bills, agency disclaimers and congressional silence alone would not decide the question.

The dissent would separate coverage from correction of an unlawful method. Here the claimed customary-tobacco authority itself presupposes an accommodation the connected scheme does not supply. MCI requires an operative statutory obligation to retain its essential force, while Haggar and Chevron require a genuine delegation before deference. Sweet Home and Iowa Utilities Board likewise distinguish broad delegated authority from independent limits. This conclusion turns on the particular enacted scheme, not economic magnitude or a universal major-questions test. Good Samaritan permits reasoned interpretive change within the statute; a changed view alone is not the defect.

#### Operative qualifications and questions left open

The following distinctions limit the statutory reasoning; they are not new enforcement findings:

- **Directions and warnings:** Section 352(f)(1) requires an exemption by regulation when adequate directions are unnecessary for public-health protection. That directions exemption does not waive section 352(f)(2)'s separate necessary warnings concerning pathological conditions, childhood use, unsafe dosage, method or duration. Section 352(j) concerns danger under labeled dosage, manner, frequency or duration; existing tobacco warnings do not automatically establish FDCA compliance.
- **Classification and approval:** Device classes depend on their statutory control and information criteria, including the qualified low-risk route; risk alone does not compel an immediate judicial ban. The earlier scientific-evidence and reclassification procedures remain. No particular classification, new-drug application, grandfather status or approval is adjudicated. Section 321(p)'s pre-June 25, 1938 exception requires the drug's prior-Act status and unchanged labeling representations; the Court does not find those conditions satisfied or unsatisfied here.
- **Restricted devices:** Section 360j(e) permits practitioner-authorization or other sale, distribution or use conditions upon the required determination that harmful potential or necessary collateral measures prevent reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness otherwise. Training or facility limits require their own safe-use determination; lack of specified board eligibility or certification alone cannot exclude a user. Required label statements remain. None of those permissions is a general exemption from another command.
- **Ban proceedings:** Section 360f permits rulemaking on findings of substantial deception or unreasonable and substantial risk. When the Secretary has determined that labeling can correct the problem and provided written notice specifying the change and time allowed, failure to make that change is separately required. Immediate effect additionally requires unreasonable, direct and substantial danger and advance manufacturer notice, followed by prompt notice, an informal-hearing opportunity and expeditious affirmance, modification or revocation.
- **Cessation and recall:** Section 360h(e)(1) requires immediate cessation of distribution and notification and cessation-of-use instructions to health professionals and user facilities after a reasonable-probability finding that a human-use device would cause serious adverse health consequences or death. An informal-hearing opportunity must be provided within ten days of the order, and inadequate grounds require vacatur. Recall requires a further determination, timetable and progress reports; it cannot reach devices held by individuals, and the greater-risk exception concerns recall from user facilities. Those recall limits do not make the initial cessation command elective. Individual risk-notice duties remain. The Court makes none of the required enforcement determinations itself.
- **Other enactments and State law:** The cigarette statutes' labeling and advertising limits and the smokeless-tobacco statute's distinct package/advertisement rules retain their own scopes, including the latter's outdoor-billboard exception. Section 360k requires a relevant federal device requirement and a different or additional State duty in the specified subject relationship. Its exemption procedure requires an application, regulation, notice and oral-hearing opportunity; greater stringency is one route, compelling local conditions without violating federal requirements another. Medtronic therefore prevents treating coverage alone as universal displacement of State youth-access laws. No particular preemption or exemption is decided.

The Court decides no advertising merits, constitutional objection, therapeutic-claim product, biological-license question, particular new-drug refusal ground, international coordination requirement or later classification procedure. It does not pronounce every risky device unmarketable, require therapeutic claims for every drug, create a general prohibition on agency change, or require Congress to use particular words in every delegation. Congress may confer tobacco authority expressly.

#### Precedent treatment

- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.:** Applies statutory construction before deference; agency reasonableness cannot supply a delegation absent from the enacted scheme.
- **United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.:** Preserves the distinction between genuine delegated ambiguity, regulatory validity and application; formal rulemaking alone does not establish authority.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.:** Applies its requirement that a bounded agency power preserve an operative statutory obligation. It creates no universal magnitude-based exception to delegation.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon:** Preserves broad statutory coverage within actual delegated language and concrete limits; it does not authorize disregard of a connected command.
- **AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board:** Preserves separate analysis of general implementing power, particular statutory constraints and unresolved applications.
- **Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala:** Preserves considered interpretive change within the statute; the FDA's former position is not an automatic legal bar.
- **National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp.:** Preserves recognition of the agency's actual discernible ground without supplying missing findings or a judicial replacement policy.
- **Adams Fruit Co. v. Barrett:** Preserves the requirement of an actual congressional delegation; an agency cannot create its own authority.
- **United States v. Bacto-Unidisk:** Its broad protective reading of the FDCA remains, subject to the entire enacted scheme rather than isolated definitions.
- **United States v. Rutherford:** Preserves statutory safety and effectiveness requirements against a judicially created exemption; it is not a present tobacco ban or an adjudication of an application.
- **Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr:** Preserves the actual federal/state counterpart and subject conditions for device preemption; federal coverage does not automatically eliminate every State rule.
- **United States v. Locke:** Its particular national-vessel and local-waterway allocation remains distinct; overlap alone supplies no general preemption rule here.

#### Resulting law and limits

Effective March 21, 2000, the FDA lacks the asserted FDCA authority for the August 28, 1996 drug/device regime governing tobacco as customarily marketed. The rule remains invalid. This is a tobacco-specific construction of the connected statutory scheme, not a universal major-questions doctrine, categorical bar on agency reinterpretation, or automatic preemption rule. Section 422 preserves the authority question and the earlier statutory version for exercise of any tobacco power. Therapeutic-claim products, particular advertising and constitutional questions, and independently authorized regulatory subjects remain outside the holding; no new ban or recall follows.

### Christensen v. Harris County — 2000-05-01

[Record](../records/christensen-v-harris-county_merits_2000-05-01.md)

#### Judgment & Remedy

The Fifth Circuit's final judgment for the County and all other defendants is affirmed, 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Opposing: Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, who would set aside that judgment and remand for actual agreement coverage, terms and validity under a consent requirement.

The employees' categorical declaration and dependent $21,360 fee award remain displaced. The Court orders no agreement-specific proceedings, negotiations, trial, damages or new fee award. The policy remains subject to express statutory protections, binding agreements and other applicable law. Abandoned wrongful-refusal and retaliation claims are not revived.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter and Thomas throughout; Scalia in Parts I and III | Part I: statutory use and remedy, six. Part II: advisory-letter statutory weight, five. Part III: regulatory textual fit, six. |
| Opinion concurring in part and in the judgment | Scalia | No other Justice | Joins Parts I and III, withholds Part II |
| Concurrence | Souter | No other Justice | Joins the Court throughout; preserves future-regulation question |
| Dissent | Stevens | Ginsburg, Breyer | Supports consent-based agreement proceedings |
| Additional dissent | Breyer | Ginsburg | Explains independently persuasive and conditionally authoritative agency grounds |

#### Holdings

#### I. The statute does not make employee requests the exclusive means of using accrued time

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 207(o) does not categorically forbid a public employer from directing paid use of lawfully accrued compensatory time merely because no agreement specifically authorizes compulsory use. The employer remains bound by lawful agreement restrictions, protected employee requests and the statute's accrual, value and overtime requirements; compelled paid use is not permission to confiscate credits or evade overtime.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Kennedy's opinion is supported by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, six Justices in all.

**Controlling explanation:** Paragraph (5) protects an employee who requests accrued time: use must be allowed within a reasonable period unless it would unduly disrupt operations. It does not say that a request is the only permissible trigger for use. Paragraph (2)'s agreement requirement governs substituting time for cash overtime, not every later scheduling choice. Moreau preserves its actual agreement routes; neither it nor the statute establishes the terms of this class's agreements.

The accrual cap limits how much time may be accumulated and requires cash for additional overtime at the cap; it does not promise employees the right to reach that cap. The permitted cashout likewise contradicts an absolute right to retain every credit, although cash payment and paid absence remain different transactions. Paid time away from work consumes credits in their defined form: the employee receives the regular rate for hours not worked, which are excluded from hours worked when computing overtime. These statutory reasons suffice without a general presumption that employers control every unregulated workplace question.

The competing consent construction treats the substitute for cash as exclusively employee-controlled. It does not follow from the original agreement requirement or protected request right. An actual lawful term can restrict compulsory use, but none identified here defeats the categorical statutory judgment. That absence does not prove no agreement exists or every employee consented. It supplies no automatic right to new agreement proceedings. The decision leaves constitutional property questions, other labor law and actual wrongful-refusal claims outside this review.

**Operative qualifications:** For the covered state, local and interstate public agencies, a wholly time-based substitute must accrue at least one-and-one-half hours for each overtime hour under the applicable representative agreement or prior individual understanding. For employees outside the applicable representative-agreement route who were hired before April 15, 1986, regular practice on that date itself supplies the agreement; no additional individual agreement is required. Moreau preserves the later-hire individual route where the chosen representative cannot lawfully enter a binding agreement. Unwritten understandings require a record; an express employment condition requires knowing, voluntary acceptance and information about preservation, use and cashout. Notice and failure to object may evidence assent, but coercion or pressure may not substitute for it. Compatible agreements may mix cash and time or set use terms while preserving the statutory premium; inconsistent terms yield to the Act.

The cap is 480 hours for the specified public-safety, emergency-response or seasonal work, and 240 for other covered work. Additional overtime at the applicable cap must be compensated as overtime. Cashout may occur at any time using the regular rate when paid. Termination requires at least the higher of the final regular rate or the average over the last three years. For termination-rate averaging, employment after a break is treated as a new period when the break was intended to be permanent and post-April 14, 1986 credits were cashed out at the initial separation; if the final period is shorter than three years, averaging uses that period. No termination or rate calculation is decided here.

For employee requests, customary schedules, peak demands, emergency staffing, substitutes and compatible agreement terms inform a reasonable period. An employer must reasonably and in good faith anticipate an unreasonable burden on acceptable public service to establish undue disruption; inconvenience alone is insufficient, and the statute does not guarantee the employee's exact chosen date. Section 553.25 forbids using time to evade overtime or coercing acceptance beyond what can realistically and in good faith be made available within a reasonable request period. Its request provisions exclude separately defined other compensatory time, including pre-April 15, 1986 accruals. The separate court-reporter transcript provision is not presented in this Sheriff's Department case. No violation of these distinct protections is found.

#### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding and operative rule:** The supported advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The actual implementing grant permits administration and regulation. It does not make every later advisory response an exercise of authority to bind courts or employers. NationsBank and Smiley involved identified authoritative exercises of delegated responsibility; they do not settle this letter's status merely by identifying its agency. Skidmore preserves its potential persuasive significance rather than discarding it by label. Its supported construction adds a specific-consent restriction not supplied by the statutory request trigger or prior-agreement condition. No supposed change in policy, unproved internal instrument or unprovided Government endorsement supplies the result. Boston & Maine forbids inventing an agency ground. The Court decides this letter's supported advisory status, not the validity of every informal agency interpretation.

#### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding and operative rule:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** Auer concerns a responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. It does not impose a universal rulemaking-format prerequisite on interpretation, but requires actual textual fit. Section 553.23 permits additional consistent terms; section 553.25 protects requests and practical availability. Neither makes employee requests exclusive or requires an agreement covering every scheduling choice. The letter's interpretation therefore adds a condition rather than resolves pertinent ambiguity. The separate inquiry into statutory authority does not replace this own-regulation analysis. No conclusion here forecloses whatever authority the Secretary may possess to adopt a valid new regulation limiting compelled use.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Moreau retains its distinct earlier-hire practice and later-hire legally capable representative rules; it supplies no finding about this class's actual scheduling terms or general bargaining right.
- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's supported advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

#### Law After Decision

Section 207(o) supplies no categorical employee veto over compulsory paid use of valid accrued time. Actual binding agreements and every identified statutory protection retain their force. This advisory letter supplies no controlling statutory restriction and cannot add a condition inconsistent with the regulation. The Court decides no future rule's validity, blanket employer-control principle, confiscation power or constitutional property question.
