## Standards and Tests selection: RFRA review of prospective enforcement

### RFRA review of prospective enforcement

**Governing question:** Original RFRA within its surviving valid field, including threatened enforcement of preexisting laws and commercial religious exercise.

**Current rule:** Within a valid field of RFRA operation, the claimant must establish a substantial burden on religious exercise. The government must then produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The inquiry applies to threatened future implementation of a preexisting law. Commercial participation does not forfeit its protection, and labeling a law antidiscrimination does not itself justify its application. Direct transactional and dignitary injury and legally material alternatives or exemptions may be considered; availability of alternative housing is not an automatic answer to direct injury.

RFRA also governs later federal enactments unless Congress expressly excludes application by reference to RFRA; FACE contains no such exclusion.

RFRA's universal State and local command cannot rest on Fourteenth Amendment §5: its substitution of the substantial-burden/compelling-interest/least-restrictive-means standard for Smith across the field lacks the required congruent and proportional enforcement relationship. Prospective State and local RFRA claims relying only on that power cannot proceed after June 25, 1997. Completed judgments under Church of Scientology and Swanner retain finality; their independent grounds and statutory reasoning survive within a valid field of operation. Federal RFRA, including its later-federal-enactment rule, remains outside this power holding.

**Authority by component:**

Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: eight direct rationale votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that RFRA governs, but does not join the opinion or its disposition of these adverse determinations.
- RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws: RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws, 1995-03-27. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five direct rationale and judgment votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer dissent from the vacatur. Their agreement that RFRA governs does not turn their distinct application into Court law.

- Subsequent federal enactments and separate prospective-enforcement inquiry: RFRA requires further adjudication of the prospective-enforcement claim, 1995-11-13; O’Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five). The claimant’s substantial-burden threshold and government production/persuasion sequence remain unchanged; the full own-minor FACE remedy exclusion must be considered as enacted, without presuming it is comparable to an outsider blockade or that an equivalent state remedy exists.
- State/local §5 invalidity and preservation of completed judgments: City of Boerne, City of Boerne v. Flores — II. RFRA's universal State and local command exceeds that enforcement power and City of Boerne v. Flores — III. The reserved power question limits future statutory applications without reopening completed judgments, Kennedy's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** City of Boerne, 1997-06-25, limits State/local operation; the earlier claimant-specific statutory mechanics survive within a valid field.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary standing and appropriate-relief limits remain. RFRA protects religious belief and preserves the Establishment Clause; this statutory inquiry does not replace the constitutional free-exercise or establishment standards. A remand under it establishes neither an exemption nor failure of the government’s eventual showing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article III power before disputed statutory merits

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground”, 1999-05-17; Scalia, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

**Holdings navigation:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (1997-06-02) — Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question. [Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#a-court-may-choose-a-straightforward-nonmerits-jurisdictional-ground-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

### Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review

**Governing question:** When an issue outside the stated certiorari question may be considered.

**Current rule:** Rule 14.1(a) creates a strong prudential presumption against deciding questions neither stated in the petition nor fairly included, subject to exceptional circumstances. A distinct intervention denial is not fairly included merely because reversing it would permit reaching a settlement-vacatur question; discussion in the petition’s body and merits briefing does not alone suffice. A consistently asserted Teague defense may be subsidiary where it limits application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief challenged by the petition. Geographic tailoring is fairly included in a challenge to exclusion from designated speech zones. An exceptional extension requires its own supported basis; adversarial briefing, preservation, logical priority or usefulness alone does not enlarge the questions. The duty to notice jurisdictional defects remains separate. A respondent may defend the existing favorable judgment on a record-supported alternative rejected below when doing so neither enlarges its own rights nor reduces the adversary’s rights under that judgment. The Court may also elect to consider a deliberately disavowed legal argument supporting the same preserved claim when it falls within the question presented and can be resolved on the established record without a new factual theory or additional findings. That discretion imposes no duty to consider abandoned grounds and does not erase limits on distinct claims, relief, factual stipulations or missing facts.

#### Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1062.
**Decided:** December 5, 1994.

##### RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication

**Holding:** RFRA applies to the threatened future implementation of Clearwater's preexisting ordinance: the Church must establish a substantial burden on its religious exercise, after which Clearwater must produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The adverse prospective determinations concerning the identifying disclosures designated as section 100.03(1)(a), (b), (c), and (j), and the written-refund-policy requirement in section 100.05(1)(g), must therefore be vacated for that inquiry, with ordinary Article III standing and appropriate-relief limits preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Current-force modification:** City of Boerne v. Flores, decided June 25, 1997, prevents this prospective statutory application from governing future State or local RFRA claims resting only on Congress's Fourteenth Amendment §5 power. The completed judgment remains final; independent grounds and the statutory reasoning retain force within a valid field of operation. Boerne neither reopens the judgment nor decides federal RFRA or separate preserved claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994): applies the distinction between future enforcement and new legal consequences for completed conduct, consistently with RFRA's express temporal direction; neither Landgraf's general framework nor its distinct relevant-activity rationale is enlarged.
- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993): preserves its objective religious-targeting, substantial secular-underinclusion, and strict-scrutiny holdings; the statutory remand neither supplies a purpose finding nor converts the enactment-history discussion into a controlling test.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet, 512 U.S. 687 (1994): preserves the distinct denominational-allocation and unequal particular-grant rules; accommodation does not cure an establishment violation, and RFRA does not decide that constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final substantial-burden or justification finding is made for the surviving identifying or written-policy requirements. The Court does not decide RFRA's constitutionality, retrospective monetary liability, a general exemption from civil fraud law, the disputed sectarian purpose, the precise Florida severability boundary, the unreviewed general anti-fraud clause, or unpreserved hardship-discretion and procedural objections. Section 7 leaves the Establishment Clause unaffected; section 6(c) supplies no authority to burden religious belief.

Section 100.05(1)(g) requires written refund terms and conditions when a refund representation is made, but does not prohibit a statement made in good faith at that time. It prescribes no substantive refund policy and does not incorporate §100.05(1)(f)'s separate written-request and willful sixty-day nonpayment conditions. No promise to offer refunds is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The adverse prospective identifying-disclosure and written-refund-policy determinations are vacated for RFRA adjudication. Existing relief favorable to the Church and the purpose/severability remand remain undisturbed, without adoption of the lower constitutional rationales or renewed enforcement of provisions set aside below. The written-policy inquiry retains the contemporaneous good-faith proviso.

**Later-authority backlinks:** City of Boerne v. Flores, June 25, 1997, resolves the reserved §5 power question and limits future State and local applications at that scope.

#### Swanner v. Anchorage Equal Rights Commission

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-124.
**Decided:** March 27, 1995.

##### RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws

**Holding:** For prospective enforcement of the marital-status housing prohibitions against Swanner, Swanner must establish a substantial burden on his religious exercise; if he does, the government must produce evidence and persuade that applying the burden to him furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The adverse federal prospective-enforcement determination is vacated for that inquiry because commercial participation and a general prohibition of discrimination do not by themselves complete it; no exemption or ultimate statutory justification is decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: five Justices.

**Current-force modification:** City of Boerne v. Flores, decided June 25, 1997, prevents this prospective statutory application from governing future State or local RFRA claims resting only on Congress's Fourteenth Amendment §5 power. The completed judgment remains final; independent grounds and the statutory reasoning retain force within a valid field of operation. The reserved federal constitutional claim and the distinct state-law and due-process grounds are not extinguished; no retrospective reopening or recovery is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater, December 5, 1994: applies its prospective, claimant-specific RFRA inquiry and distinct burdens to the present housing enforcement; its reservations concerning RFRA's constitutionality and retrospective monetary liability remain.
- United States v. Lee, 455 U.S. 252: preserves its recognition that exemptions can impose religious limitations on third parties; commercial activity is not treated as categorical forfeiture under RFRA.
- Roberts v. United States Jaycees, 468 U.S. 609: recognizes the weight of protected access and injury from exclusion; it does not automatically decide justification under every antidiscrimination law.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872: its constitutional neutral-law rule does not replace the enacted RFRA inquiry; its constitutional holding remains unchanged.
- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, June 11, 1993: preserves objective neutrality, comparable secular harm, and strict-scrutiny rules without deciding the constitutional claim here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final substantial-burden or justification finding is made. The Anchorage ordinance's distinct individual-home exception applies when the renter would share common living areas with the owner, lessor, manager, agent, or other person; no finding places Swanner within it, and its legal comparability cannot be assumed either way. The Court neither invalidates the housing provisions nor ranks all marital-status protections. It does not decide religious teaching or advocacy, religious-mission housing, intimate owner-occupied arrangements, RFRA's constitutionality, or retrospective monetary liability. Alaska statutory construction, the state constitutional ruling, and separate notice/delegation due-process rulings are outside reconsideration. The federal constitutional exemption claim is reserved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The adverse federal prospective-enforcement determination is vacated and remanded for RFRA's separate burden and justification inquiries. No final exemption or constitutional free-exercise ruling is made. Alaska statutory, state-constitutional and separate notice/delegation rulings are not reconsidered; no retrospective monetary or penalty relief is adjudicated.

**Later-authority backlinks:** City of Boerne v. Flores, June 25, 1997, resolves the reserved §5 power question and limits future State and local applications at that scope.

#### American Life League v. Reno

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 94-1869; no separate Supreme Court merits docket established; judgment below, 47 F.3d 642.
**Decided:** November 13, 1995.

##### Facial speech challenge to the defined reproductive-service access restrictions

**Holding:** FACE's challenged service-related force, threat, physical-obstruction and intentional-property-damage provisions regulate access-related conduct without selecting a protected message. When the conduct is expressive, the defined harm, intent and alternative service-purpose requirements satisfy intermediate scrutiny because they further substantial access and safety interests unrelated to suppression, burden no substantially greater speech than necessary and leave usable nonobstructive communication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 248(a)(1) requires force, threat of force or physical obstruction; intentional injury, intimidation, interference or attempt; and conduct because a person obtains or provides reproductive services, or intended to intimidate that person, another person or a class from doing so. Section (a)(3)'s reviewed branch separately reaches intentional facility-property damage or destruction, or attempt, because the facility provides those services. Interference restricts movement; intimidation means reasonable apprehension of bodily harm to oneself or another; obstruction makes ingress or egress impassable or passage to or from the facility unreasonably difficult or hazardous. Services include medical, surgical, counseling and referral services concerning reproduction, pregnancy or termination, including carrying pregnancy to term.

**Limits and questions not reached:** These qualifications govern all FACE holdings in this entry: a minor's parent or legal guardian is subject to no §248 penalty or civil remedy, including private damages and private or governmental injunctions, for described activity directed exclusively at that minor; no FACE remedy survives for that excepted conduct. Other law is not preempted, but no equivalent state remedy, general parental immunity or constitutional veto is presumed. The inclusive facility definition covers reproductive-service establishments and their building or structure, adding neither grounds nor worship premises. Separate passage language creates no interior-only limit, property-line rule or outdoor immunity; the worship branches remain unchallenged. Section 248(d)(1) preserves protected expression including peaceful picketing and demonstrations; (d)(2) separately creates no new remedy for interference with protected speech or religion outside a facility regardless of viewpoint and limits no existing remedy. Nonexclusive remedies, no preemption of state/local remedies, and preservation of state/local reproductive-service regulation retain their distinct terms. No numerical buffer, invitation rule, compulsory silence, discriminatory-enforcement ruling or resolution of materially different mixed-motive applications follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the facial speech challenge. The other constitutional and categorical damages dismissals also stand; only RFRA receives the separate remand below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mitchell permits relevant motive accompanying proscribable conduct without ending scrutiny of expressive obstruction. O'Brien and Ward supply intermediate review; Turner requires actual classification and fit, not benign purpose alone. Madsen's more demanding injunction rule and distances are not transplanted. Hurley's private-expression protection remains; no expressive composition is compelled here.

##### Substantial overbreadth of the defined access restrictions

**Holding:** FACE's intentional-act and service-purpose requirements, defined access or bodily-harm predicates and textually available protection for First Amendment expression do not reach substantial protected expression relative to the law's legitimate sweep on this facial challenge. Political offense, persuasion and abstract belief alone are not violations; protected expression cannot itself become punishable merely because it proves relevant motive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-A, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Relevant statements retain ordinary safeguards. True threats remain distinct from political hyperbole; no universal threat mental-state rule or particular uncharged-statement liability is decided. Applications actually punishing protected advocacy remain challengeable.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** R.A.V.'s controlling substantial-overbreadth rule applies; its separate content-selection opinion remains noncontrolling. Mitchell preserves relevant motive evidence without punishment of abstract belief and distinguishes speculative evidentiary chilling.

##### Facial vagueness of the defined conduct

**Holding:** The movement, passage and reasonable-apprehension-of-bodily-harm definitions, together with intentional conduct and required service-related purpose, provide ascertainable boundaries sufficient to defeat the presented facial vagueness claim. Offense, disagreement, resentment or persuasion alone does not satisfy them.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Particular enforcement and threatening-statement questions remain; no actual criminal liability is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cameron supports intelligibility of physical-obstruction restrictions in context. R.A.V.'s vagueness reservation survives; overbreadth is not substituted for this inquiry.

##### Categorical First Amendment challenge to elected statutory damages

**Holding:** Outside the parent/legal-guardian exception for activity directed exclusively at the actor's own minor, an otherwise eligible claimant may elect §248(c)(1)(B)'s $5,000 per violation before final judgment in lieu of actual compensatory damages. The First Amendment does not categorically require violation-based statutory damages to equal proved actual loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part IV-A; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela joins rejection of the categorical challenge without a general statutory-damages rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The election is permissive and replaces actual compensatory damages, not every other remedy. For §248(a)(1), the private civil claimant must be involved in providing or seeking to provide, or obtaining or seeking to obtain, services in a reproductive-health facility. No amount-specific reasonableness, entitlement, violation count, particular award, punitive damages or criminal sentence is decided. No private elected damages or other FACE relief survives the own-minor exception.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Claiborne forbids damages for protected persuasion and others' unlawful acts without attribution; it does not categorically prohibit a legislatively selected substitute for actual compensatory loss from an attributable violation.

##### Personal attribution of civil liability

**Holding:** FACE liability requires the defendant's legally attributable violation. Shared belief, membership, independent advocacy or another person's unlawful act alone is insufficient; protected persuasion that reduces demand cannot itself be the unlawful injury supporting an award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part IV-B, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court determines no particular organizational responsibility, conspiracy, actual concert or individual award. An injunction's actual-concert rule does not supply damages liability predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Claiborne preserves responsibility for unlawful conduct and protection for association and persuasion. Madsen's responsible-party and actual-concert restrictions remain injunction rules, not a new damages cause.

##### Facial religious targeting and the own-minor exemption

**Holding:** The challenged text establishes neither religious targeting nor a substantial exemption for comparable secular blockades: its predicates concern service-related acts and access, not religious identity. The parent/guardian exclusion of all penalties and civil remedies for activity directed exclusively at that person's own minor does not itself establish secular outsiders' permission to obstruct others' access.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part V-A; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the constitutional free-exercise disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Labor and environmental viewpoints receive no general statutory exemption; conduct with a different object may fail the enacted predicates. The full exclusion, including preventive injunctions, is considered, not misdescribed as a lower-penalty rule. No universal family-status exception to comparability, discriminatory-enforcement approval or finding that a plaintiff qualifies in another encounter follows. Its separate RFRA significance remains below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lukumi's objective text, operation, exemption and comparable-secular-harm inquiry applies. Religious prominence among opponents proves no targeting by itself; secular purpose language cannot save actual discrimination. Its noncontrolling legislative-purpose discussion is not substituted for the governing rule.

##### Broader constitutional exemption for the pleaded physical obstruction

**Holding:** Under Smith's neutral-law rule as bounded by Lukumi, the asserted religious obligation to engage in the covered physical obstruction supplies no constitutional exemption on the pleaded frame after the presented targeting and comparable-secular-exemption challenges fail. This does not decide RFRA or every materially different application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part V-B, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct votes. O'Connor and Souter reserve the broader question; Stone-Zsela contributes no rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The exclusively-own-minor exception is not a general secular blockade entitlement. Constitutional protection from discriminatory enforcement survives; RFRA's constitutionality is not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith remains the constitutional neutral-law rule with Lukumi's targeting and general-applicability limits; Clearwater and Swanner require a separate statutory inquiry.

##### Person-and-application adjudication under RFRA

**Holding:** The RFRA prospective-enforcement dismissal must be vacated. The district court must identify each live claimant and threatened application, determine a genuinely disputed substantial burden, and, if established, require the Government to produce evidence and persuade that applying the burden to that person furthers a compelling interest by the least restrictive means, considering concrete alternatives and legally comparable exceptions.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's RFRA opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from this remand.

**Material application:** General access, safety and health interests and successful intermediate speech scrutiny do not complete RFRA's application-specific showing. Reliable medical access and bodily safety carry compelling weight, and an accommodation may fail because it preserves the access injury; nonetheless, the complete parent/own-minor exclusion, even of preventive FACE injunctions, defeats an assumption of universal enforcement and requires actual comparison. Neither comparability with outsider blockades nor an equivalent state remedy is presumed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Religious burden is assumed for review of dismissal, not found; sincerity and doctrinal necessity are not adjudicated. Claimants bear substantial burden, then Government bears production and persuasion on justification; claimants have no universal duty to prove alternatives. No mandatory hearing whenever religion is asserted, entitlement to blockade or redirect patients, final exemption or invalidation follows. RFRA applies to later federal law absent express exclusion by reference to RFRA; FACE contains none. Article III standing, appropriate relief, belief protection and the Establishment Clause remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand only the RFRA prospective-enforcement claim. It may fail on the actual pleadings and a sufficient showing or at the substantial-burden threshold. No interim injunction follows automatically, and no new hearing or deadline is prescribed. The constitutional and categorical damages dismissals remain affirmed, each 9–0 in judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Clearwater and Swanner preserve the actual-person/application inquiry and burden sequence. Smith does not replace RFRA. Lee and Roberts retain the significance of direct injury without creating a categorical statutory exception.

The commerce and federalism holding is under Constitutional Structure, American Life League, November 13, 1995, Fourth Circuit No. 94-1869.

#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For Congress's congruent-and-proportional enforcement authority and its limits, see City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074, decided 1997-06-25, Constitutional Structure — Fourteenth Amendment enforcement power.

##### II. RFRA's universal State and local command exceeds that enforcement power

**Holding:** RFRA's general application to State and local laws cannot be sustained under §5 because it substitutes its substantial-burden, compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means standard across the field for the constitutional boundary retained in Smith, without the required enforcement relationship. The Fifth Circuit's contrary power ruling is reversed, and the RFRA claim against the City must be dismissed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not reconsider Smith or decide the permit denial's neutrality, motive, substantial burden or justification. Federal RFRA, including its rule concerning later federal enactments, and other possible congressional powers remain outside the disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's power ruling is reversed and remanded, 6–3, for dismissal of the RFRA claim against the City. Separately pleaded and preserved constitutional and state-law claims retain their own analysis; no permit, exemption, damages or new hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith remains the rule for genuinely neutral and generally applicable laws; Lukumi preserves objective-targeting and substantial-secular-underinclusion protections. Neither determines the City's actual conduct here.

##### III. The reserved power question limits future statutory applications without reopening completed judgments

**Holding:** Church of Scientology and Swanner did not decide the constitutional power question and therefore do not establish §5 authority for RFRA's universal State and local application. Their prospective statutory applications cannot govern future State and local RFRA claims resting only on that power after this decision, but their completed judgments retain finality and their independent grounds and statutory reasoning retain force within a valid field of operation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No blanket reopening, retrospective recovery or reconsideration of independent constitutional or state-law grounds follows. Particular transactional or remedial reliance claims require their own lawful source.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Church of Scientology and Swanner are limited prospectively in the invalid §5-based State and local field, preserving completed judgments, independent grounds and statutory reasoning where validly operative. Agostini retains Lemon as the governing Establishment Clause test and updates it into one inquiry with three routes: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives the attribution-and-function inquiry. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within that inquiry, not freestanding prongs or an alternative test. Its overruling of Aguilar's categorical onsite rule and displacement of Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale concern a different Establishment Clause setting and supply no RFRA power ruling. Plyler's prospective-relief rule does not authorize redefinition of substantive rights.

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co., No. 98-470, May 17, 1999: resolves the bounded nonmerits sequencing question by permitting straightforward personal-jurisdiction dismissal before a difficult subject-matter inquiry, subject to the stated state-law and statutory limits; the contested-merits prohibition remains unchanged.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.

- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.

# Adams — public reading copy

## Event

Adams v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue, No. 99-798. Merits decision, March 6, 2000. Certiorari granted December 6, 1999; argument or submission February 7, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, 170 F.3d 173, No. 98-7200, affirming the Tax Court, 110 T.C. 137. The Third Circuit decided the appeal March 4, 1999, and denied rehearing June 10, 1999.

The question granted is whether RFRA requires a religious accommodation from ordinary federal income-tax collection or associated additions when payment conflicts with the taxpayer's religious convictions. Review encompasses collection and the actual failure-to-file and estimated-tax additions under sections 6651 and 6654; it does not separately grant the Code or constitutional claims. The deficiencies and additions concern 1988, 1989, 1992, 1993 and 1994.

Adams, a Quaker employed as a Peace Field Secretary, sincerely objects to supporting war through taxation. She proposed full payment into a nonmilitary fund or for nonmilitary use, offered to consider other accommodations with her religious community, and sought nonliability until accommodation. Those proposals are not findings of feasibility, revenue equivalence or governmental acceptance. She conceded a compelling interest in collecting taxes, while contesting the means used. The lower court relied on Lee's uniform-tax rationale to dispense with further justification under RFRA. Browne concerns different taxpayers and a distinct collection proposal and is not consolidated with this case.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at merits argument or submission and at decision. All nine participate in every judgment component.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the Third Circuit's judgment insofar as it sustains the challenged collection and the identified additions against RFRA and remands for further proceedings consistent with the opinion. Each RFRA component is decided 5–4. The independent Code and constitutional determinations remain undisturbed at their own scope.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, supplies five direct votes for each holding below at its stated level of generality, including its application, remedy and limits.

### RFRA requires an application-specific determination of the collection defense

**Holding and operative rule:** For the federal RFRA defense presented here, Adams must establish a substantial burden on religious exercise; the Government must then produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to her furthers a compelling interest by the least restrictive means. The rejection of her collection defense must be vacated because the conceded compelling collection interest and the general need for uniform taxation do not themselves complete that application-specific inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** Clearwater establishes the claimant's threshold and the Government's distinct burdens; Swanner preserves those requirements against a categorical reliance on the importance of the regulated system. American Life League applies the same inquiry to federal enforcement and rejects a universal requirement that the claimant first prove an alternative works. Boerne's state-and-local power holding leaves that federal statutory question outside its disposition. The Court assumes substantial burden for this review without conclusively deciding it or equating sincerity with the statutory threshold.

Lee identifies the powerful interest in compulsory participation and the religious restrictions an exemption can impose on others. Hernandez reinforces the importance of general revenues and the limits of comparing exceptions. Those decisions weigh heavily against the requested spending restriction, but they do not create a tax exclusion from RFRA. Here the lower court treated the conceded interest as resolving the means inquiry and expressly required no further production. Adams's conditional full-payment proposal is not an established equivalent to unrestricted general revenue; neither its feasibility nor its failure may be presumed from that description alone. The Government may show that the requested restriction is an unacceptable legal or operational substitute. The Court holds only that the adverse determination must result from RFRA's actual inquiry. Adequate existing facts and legal reasons may suffice; neither a new empirical study nor an evidentiary hearing follows merely from asserting religious exercise. No exemption, compelled negotiation, or appropriation change is ordered.

### The failure-to-file addition requires its own RFRA justification

**Holding and operative rule:** RFRA separately governs the challenged section 6651(a)(1) failure-to-file addition. Neither a lawful underlying tax obligation nor failure to qualify for the Code exception alone establishes the Government's justification under RFRA, so the adverse RFRA determination concerning this addition is vacated for consideration of its actual filing-enforcement function and the relief sought.

**Controlling explanation:** Boyle explains why prompt returns matter to ascertainment and collection and distinguishes the ordinary filing duty from genuinely impaired ability to comply. Those points give the Government substantial grounds for defending a filing addition. Section 6651(a)(1)'s own exception requires both reasonable cause and absence of willful neglect; sincere religious objection does not itself establish objective inability or satisfaction of that exception. The Court does not reopen the lower court's independent Code conclusion.

Clearwater and American Life League nevertheless require the separate statutory justification once substantial burden is established. The need to collect the principal tax cannot simply stand in for an explanation of this additional burden. The lower courts must consider the filing function, the actual dispensation sought and any legally material alternative or comparable exception, without assuming that any exception is comparable or that a proposed alternative is workable. The Court does not find that deliberate nonfiling qualifies for a waiver, that a secular comparator exists, or that this enforcement mechanism ultimately fails RFRA. The existing record and legal reasons may sustain it after the proper inquiry; no new hearing is mandatory.

### The estimated-tax addition requires a distinct inquiry into timely installments

**Holding and operative rule:** RFRA separately governs the challenged section 6654 estimated-tax addition, whose justification must address timely installment revenue and the actual accommodation sought. The rejection of that RFRA defense is vacated; neither the principal tax obligation nor the independent failure to obtain a statutory waiver supplies a conclusive answer.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 6654's relationship to the amount and duration of underpayment gives the Government a substantial justification tied to timely revenue. Lee's compulsory-contribution principle supports consideration of that function. But Clearwater and American Life League do not permit a general uniformity premise to resolve every enforcement burden without its application to the person and means at issue.

The statutory qualifications remain distinct. Section 6654(e)(3)(A) bars the addition to the extent the Secretary determines that casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstances would make its imposition against equity and good conscience. It is not a general reasonable-cause exception. Section 6654(e)(3)(B), not claimed applicable here, requires retirement after age 62 or becoming disabled in the taxable year at issue or its predecessor, together with underpayment due to reasonable cause and not willful neglect. Neither provision is section 6651's exception. Their existence does not establish comparable treatment or Adams's eligibility.

The lower court must apply RFRA to the addition's timing function and the proposed relief without presuming an alternative feasible or an exception comparable. Conscience alone supplies no automatic waiver, and the Court makes no calculation or finding about an excessive charge. A sufficient legal showing on the existing facts may support enforcement; remand does not predetermine the Government's failure or command further evidence where no material factual issue requires it.

### Limits and questions not reached

The Court decides the granted RFRA defenses only. Federal RFRA's constitutional validity was not contested below and remains undecided. The independent Code and Free Exercise determinations are outside the grant and remain undisturbed without adoption as Supreme Court holdings. The Court makes no final substantial-burden finding, tax calculation, finding of selective enforcement or objective incapacity, ruling on an unidentified additional sanction, or finding that any proposed accommodation is operationally feasible. It judges neither the truth of Adams's beliefs nor the wisdom of government expenditure. No general rule allows taxpayers to direct public spending, and no general rule forecloses all tax accommodations.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater, December 5, 1994:** Its claimant-specific RFRA threshold and Government production-and-persuasion requirements apply to these federal tax defenses; the statutory burden sequence is unchanged.
- **Swanner v. Anchorage Equal Rights Commission, March 27, 1995:** Its rejection of categorical justification from a regulatory label applies to the asserted tax-uniformity shortcut; its statutory reasoning remains operative within RFRA's valid field.
- **American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995:** Its federal-enforcement inquiry, consideration of concrete alternatives and legally comparable exceptions, and rejection of a universal claimant duty to prove alternatives are applied. Its later-federal-enactment rule is unchanged.
- **City of Boerne v. Flores, June 25, 1997:** Its limitation on RFRA's state-and-local application under Fourteenth Amendment section 5 does not decide federal RFRA's validity or displace the statutory reasoning used here.
- **United States v. Lee, 455 U.S. 252 (1982):** Its uniform-tax and third-party-burden reasoning remains substantial guidance for the justification inquiry. Its pre-RFRA result does not dispense with RFRA's enacted application-specific requirements.
- **Hernandez v. Commissioner, 490 U.S. 680 (1989):** Its general-revenue analysis and caution against treating every exception as comparable remain relevant; they establish no tax-wide exclusion from RFRA and no factual comparator here.
- **United States v. Boyle, 469 U.S. 241 (1985):** Its filing-duty, reasonable-cause and genuine-inability distinctions inform the section 6651 analysis without replacing RFRA or establishing that Adams satisfies a Code exception.
- **Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872 (1990):** Its constitutional neutral-law rule remains unchanged and does not replace the distinct federal statutory test.

## Law After Decision

Effective March 6, 2000, the existing claimant-specific RFRA framework governs the presented income-tax collection defense and the two identified enforcement additions separately. Uniform participation and timely revenue remain weighty justification grounds, but the collection label and the rejection of a Code waiver cannot themselves dispose of RFRA. The Government retains production and persuasion after the claimant's substantial-burden showing; neither a new claimant feasibility burden nor a universal evidentiary-hearing duty is created. Existing statutory waiver requirements, constitutional doctrine and the reserved validity of federal RFRA remain unchanged. The decision awards further adjudication of these RFRA defenses, not an accommodation or control over common revenues.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens, dissenting, joined by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer.** They would affirm each RFRA determination. They accept the claimant's substantial-burden threshold and the Government's justification burden, and reject the lower court's categorical suggestion that the means inquiry answers itself. Their disagreement concerns whether the existing facts and law already supply the answer. Lee and Hernandez show why full payment subject to a protected restriction on public use differs legally from the unconditional contribution the common-revenue system requires. Adams seeks nonmilitary use and nonliability until accommodation, rather than an established substitute preserving the same legal character of payment. That stated condition, without an assumed administrative cost or accounting impossibility, suffices for these Justices to sustain collection under RFRA.

They separately sustain the filing addition because Boyle identifies the need for timely returns to ascertain and collect liability, and conscience alone does not establish the genuine inability that Boyle reserves. They sustain the estimated-tax addition because its relationship to the amount and period of withheld installments gives it a distinct timely-revenue function. They do not infer that tax liability validates every sanction, merge section 6654's unusual-circumstance qualification with section 6651's reasonable-cause exception, or foreclose a defense satisfying an actual statutory exception. RFRA continues to govern other tax applications; they find no need to remand these particular proposals and burdens.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting.** He would affirm collection and both challenged additions at the scope of the conscientious-objection theory. He assumes substantial burden and places compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means justification on the Government. Applying Lee through that burden, he identifies the interest as collecting this taxpayer's assessed share through a system in which no individual directs or vetoes the uses of common funds. An exemption shifts the shortfall to others; an equivalent payment restricted to approved purposes transfers part of Congress's allocation authority to the objector. It does not preserve the same common-revenue arrangement. Payment accompanied by protest may leave the religious burden intact; RFRA can nevertheless permit a burden that the Government justifies.

He treats collection and enforcement as distinct. Forgiving the challenged ordinary additions solely because the noncompliance was deliberate for religious reasons would leave an option to postpone payment that other taxpayers finance, without securing the same timely contribution. He does not hold every additional charge necessary. Erroneous amounts, penalties for protected advocacy and independently established statutory defenses retain their own analysis. Nor does he reject every tax accommodation: a proposed means preserving full, timely payment without shifting costs must be assessed on its actual operation. He judges neither religious truth nor spending policy and adopts no categorical taxation exception to RFRA.

## Procedure After Action

The Supreme Court's merits review of the granted RFRA question is complete. The case returns to the Third Circuit for further proceedings consistent with the opinion on collection and each identified addition. Any genuinely disputed substantial-burden threshold and, if it is met, the Government's application-specific justification remain to be resolved under ordinary procedures. Adequate existing facts and legal reasons may support enforcement without a new hearing. The independent ungranted rulings remain undisturbed at their own scope. No present tax cancellation, suspension, refund, collection injunction, nonmilitary account, spending direction or new deadline follows from the mandate.
