## Standards and Tests selection: Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration

### Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration

**Governing question:** Whether a later law may withdraw mandatory discretionary-parole reconsideration for an earlier offender by doubling hearing intervals or permitting individualized postponement for up to three years.

**Current rule:** The Ex Post Facto Clause forbids applying to a pre-enactment offender a later statute that removes every alternate mandatory parole-reconsideration opportunity by doubling the post-denial interval from twelve to twenty-four months, where ordinary parole depends on consideration and no retained ordinary means of obtaining intervening review is established. That binding loss of a meaningful release opportunity makes punishment more onerous despite unchanged initial eligibility, maximum sentence and ultimate release discretion; the prisoner need not prove the omitted hearing would have produced release. Relief restores the consideration opportunity, not an entitlement to parole. An individualized initial prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that prediction do not preserve later mandatory opportunities to present rehabilitation, changed circumstances or a materially mistaken forecast. Where the actual three-year deferral removes meaningful annual consideration while parole is legally available and no ordinary substitute is established, it increases punishment. The prisoner must show the earlier entitlement, retrospective actual withdrawal and a concrete connection to a legally available avenue to earlier release; inconvenience alone is insufficient. Once shown, the State must substantiate a substitute it invokes through its legal basis, practical availability and timely capacity to provide that consideration. The prisoner retains the ultimate burden of proving an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

**Authority by component:**

- Mandatory annual-to-biennial withdrawal, proof and relief boundary: Cavanaugh v. Roller — The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record, 1993-11-30. Part III of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; six Justices adopt the rule, its application to Roller, and the prospective remedy. Ginsburg's conditional agreement in her separate opinion adds no vote to this controlling coalition.

- The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here: The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here, 1995-04-25. Part II of O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the same rule, application and relief.
- The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes: The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes, 1995-04-25. Part III of O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg; five Justices expressly adopt this bounded evidentiary direction and its application. Recognition of a source limitation in the dissent is not an additional join.

**Present operation effective:** Morales, April 25, 1995, for the bounded individualized-deferral application and claimant/substitute substantiation rule; Cavanaugh retains its original scope.

**Open question:** Materially different individualized arrangements and demonstrated interim review remain unresolved. No automatic ban on every frequency reduction, universal probability formula, numerical response deadline or general presumption shifting ultimate constitutional proof is adopted. Changed suitability or voting rules, parole abolition and extraordinary clemency are not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record; The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here; The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes.

## Standards and Tests selection: Visibility of protected nonobscene cabaret expression

### Visibility of protected nonobscene cabaret expression

**Governing question:** Whether a city may prohibit protected cabaret performances or their images solely because they can be seen from outside.

**Current rule:** A city may not suppress protected nonobscene adult-cabaret performances or images solely because listed anatomy is visible outside. Limiting the prohibition to cabaret entertainment does not make its communicative effect a secondary effect or establish obscenity, minor-obscenity, captive-home intrusion or actual-conduct conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- Ino Ino Holding 4: eight-Justice protected-image rule: Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue — controlling Holdings, 1998-09-28.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-09-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Authoritative narrowing or a nonobscene-work exception in one separate ordinance provision does not supply a missing condition in another. Independent lawful public-nudity, obscenity, trespass and neutral-sign restrictions retain their own operation.

**Holdings navigation:** Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue — Independent state licensing protection and federal review; Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue — Fee eligibility and limits on defensive fee shifting; Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue — Cabaret operational rules, narrowed overbreadth and visible images.

## Standards and Tests selection: Supplemental jurisdiction over related state administrative review

### Supplemental jurisdiction over related state administrative review

**Governing question:** Federal power and retention of state administrative-review claims accompanying substantial federal claims.

**Current rule:** Substantial federal claims on a complaint’s face may supply §1331 original jurisdiction and §1441(a) removal. Section 1367(a) then covers related state administrative-review claims forming part of the same Article III case, notwithstanding confinement to an administrative record. The governing state review standards remain intact. Power does not compel retention: §1367(c) permits nonretention for novel or complex state law, substantial state predominance, dismissal of all original claims, or exceptional circumstances with other compelling reasons. Economy, convenience, fairness and comity inform lawful discretion without replacing those conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- Mixed-complaint jurisdiction; power distinct from retention: City of Chicago v. International College of Surgeons, 1997-12-15; Thomas Part I with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** 1997-12-15.

**Exceptions and limits:** Established abstention retains its own predicates and remedy limits. Burford surrender requires timely adequate state review and either difficult state law of transcendent public importance or disruption of coherent policy on a matter of substantial public concern; independent legal damages cannot simply be relinquished on that ground, although a justified stay may be available. A state-administrative-only action and a diversity route for it remain unresolved.

**Related current rules:** Burford abstention and independent legal-damages claims states the separate remedy boundary.

**Holdings navigation:** City of Chicago v. International College of Surgeons — Removal and supplemental administrative-review jurisdiction.

## Standards and Tests selection: Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

### Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

**Governing question:** When an asserted statutory obligation creates an individual right enforceable through §1983.

**Current rule:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding obligation and defining the individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than vaguely or amorphously. Text, statutory setting and the asserted entitlement govern; a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish a right. An identified federal right is presumptively enforceable under §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Right creation and remedial foreclosure remain separate inquiries. Title IX’s individual right against sex discrimination in a covered educational program is not categorically excluded from §1983 enforcement by its administrative funding process or established implied action. The claimant must prove the actual statutory violation, including defendant coverage and recipient-responsibility conditions; the enforcement vehicle enlarges no duty. Title IX also does not displace a §1983 Equal Protection claim independently secured by the Constitution merely because the same school harassment supplies both theories; purposeful constitutional discrimination, state action, defendant-specific or municipal responsibility, causation, remedies and actual preclusion remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Blessing v. Freestone — A §1983 statutory claim must identify an individual right, 1997-04-21; unanimous Opinion of the Court, holding 1, supplies the provision-specific right inquiry
- Blessing v. Freestone — General federal oversight does not categorically displace §1983, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 3 supplies presumptive enforcement and congressional foreclosure
- Blessing v. Freestone — State-plan placement neither defeats nor creates a right, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 4 supplies the §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10 construction
- Blessing v. Freestone — Any remedy must follow the particular proved right and violation, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 6 supplies the right-and-violation limit on relief

- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “1. Independent constitutional claims remain available”, 1999-06-03; Thomas, all nine Justices. Supplies independent constitutional enforcement.
- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “2. The identified statutory right is not categorically foreclosed under section 1983”, 1999-06-03; Thomas with Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Supplies statutory Title IX enforcement without duty expansion.

**Present operation effective:** Bruneau, June 3, 1999, for Title IX’s statutory and independent constitutional enforcement relationships; earlier individual-right and actual-foreclosure components retain their dates.

**Controlling construction:** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 forbid denying enforceability solely because a Social Security Act provision appears in state-plan requirements; they neither create every asserted right nor disturb the preserved result that §671(a)(15) is not privately enforceable. Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure, adequate staffing and satisfactory computerized administration create no individual entitlement to general program adequacy. Its audits, corrective plans and funding sanctions alone do not foreclose every otherwise existing individual claim.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Identify each actual provision, conduct, remedy, operative version and effective date; determine the particular right, any congressional foreclosure, proper defendant, breach, causation and lawful relief. A general request for programwide relief does not erase a concrete allegation concerning a particular duty. No superseded or not-yet-effective duty may silently support an injunction.

**Open question:** Whether an actually asserted distinct Title IV-D distribution provision creates a particular right remains unresolved. The decision authorizes no undifferentiated substantial-compliance decree, blanket dismissal or prescribed staffing/computer redesign.

**Related current rules:** Private enforcement of Adoption Assistance Act reasonable-efforts and reporting clauses retains its provision-specific outcomes and unresolved §671(a)(16) question.

**Holdings navigation:** Blessing v. Freestone (1997-04-21) — Civil Rights / Federal statutory rights and child-support enforcement. [Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District — 1. Independent constitutional claims remain available](HOLDINGS.md#1-independent-constitutional-claims-remain-available-1); [Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District — 2. The identified statutory right is not categorically foreclosed under section 1983](HOLDINGS.md#2-the-identified-statutory-right-is-not-categorically-foreclosed-under-section-1983).

## Standards and Tests selection: CAPTA investigation commands and individual enforcement

### CAPTA investigation commands and individual enforcement

**Governing question:** Private enforcement of conduct-time 42 U.S.C. §5106a(b)(2).

**Current rule:** A participating jurisdiction must provide for prompt initiation of investigation upon a report of known or suspected child abuse or neglect. That CAPTA command does not itself confer the asserted individual federal right enforceable through §1983.

**Authority by component:**

- Doe v. District of Columbia — CAPTA's report-triggered investigation command, 1997-06-02; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five direct joins

**Present operation effective:** Doe v. District of Columbia, 1997-06-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** The binding statutory obligation survives rejection of this private action. Other CAPTA provisions and funding conditions require their own analysis; local duties and remedies are not extinguished.

**Holdings navigation:** Doe v. District of Columbia (1997-06-02) — Civil Rights / Child-welfare statutory enforcement.

## Standards and Tests selection: Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX

### Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX

**Governing question:** Sex-based teacher harassment and compensatory responsibility of the funding recipient.

**Current rule:** Sexual subject matter or offense alone does not show discrimination because of sex. Same-sex harassment can constitute sex discrimination; the claim requires sex-based causation, unwelcome treatment, sufficient educational harm, recipient responsibility and causal injury. Participation or later continuation does not conclusively establish earlier welcome. The environment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to be objectively abusive and actually experienced as such, assessed in the educational context without mandatory grade-loss, psychiatric-injury or factor requirements. One sufficient route to recipient damages is actual notice to an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial risk and deliberate, unreasonable disregard of that known danger causing injury or continuation. This actual-corrective-official-notice route is not exclusive. A separate sufficient attribution route exists when school-conferred authority actually and materially enables the teacher’s intentional discrimination and the recipient’s own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes that discrimination to continue. This route does not itself establish discrimination, causal loss, damages or defeat of an independent defense.

**Authority by component:**

- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — I. The sex of the harasser does not exclude a Title IX claim, 1997-05-27; Stone-Zsela Part I, all nine
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — II. Participation does not establish welcome, and educational harm is contextual, 1997-05-27; the same nine
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — III. Recipient compensation requires its own culpable response, 1997-05-27; Stone-Zsela Part IIA with O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins independent of the remand coalition
- Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. — The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex, 1997-02-24; Ginsburg for all nine supplies this independently sufficient statutory element

- Gebser I: five direct votes rejecting exclusivity; II: the narrower authority-plus-recipient-fault attribution component under Marks and Cipollone: Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, No. 96-1866. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-22.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-22, for the new component; earlier component authorities retain their stated dates.

**Exceptions and limits:** Sexual behavior alone does not prove the elements; an opposite-sex comparator is not invariably necessary. Employment, ordinary teacher access or a technical policy breach alone does not establish recipient responsibility. A reasonable response does not become unlawful merely because harm continues. Gebser’s four broader agency adherents do not formally join Stone-Zsela’s recipient-fault reasoning: their materially-aided-authority rationale necessarily covers every case satisfying his narrower conjunction, supplying the component-specific five-Justice ground. Neither Stone-Zsela’s complete proposed framework nor his disputed-evidence application has five joins.

**Holdings navigation:** Kinman v. Omaha Public School District (1997-05-27) — Civil Rights / Teacher harassment and institutional responsibility; Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. (1997-02-24) — The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex. Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, 524 U.S. 274 — Title IX recipient responsibility for teacher harassment.

**Material development:** Gebser, 1998-06-22, removes Kinman’s exclusivity for actual notice to a corrective official while retaining that route and the independent substantive discrimination requirements. Its judgment does not find knowledge, willful blindness, material aid, causation or liability.

## Standards and Tests selection: Recipient responsibility for peer harassment under Title IX

### Recipient responsibility for peer harassment under Title IX

**Governing question:** When a funding recipient’s response to student peer harassment supports compensatory liability.

**Current rule:** A sufficient route to recipient damages exists when an official empowered to correct harassment or its substantial danger has actual notice, the recipient substantially controls the harasser and the educational context, and deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that danger causally contributes to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. Harassment must be because of sex and sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive and impair educational access or benefit in context. Notice, culpability, control, discrimination and causal injury remain independent requirements; peer attendance does not impute the peer’s acts to the recipient.

**Authority by component:**

- Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A school’s own culpable response can support damages for controlled peer harassment”, 1999-05-24; Ginsburg Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Breyer. Part I supplies the sufficient actual-notice, control and recipient-own-wrong route.
- Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Educational deprivation follows the contextual severe-or-pervasive standard”, 1999-05-24; Ginsburg Part II with Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Breyer. Five direct joins supply the disjunctive harm threshold; Stone-Zsela does not join that component.

**Present operation effective:** Davis, May 24, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Generic constructive notice, a harasser’s knowledge, ordinary negligence, policy breach and an unsuccessful reasonable response do not establish this route. No mandatory grade loss, psychiatric harm, physical exclusion, total loss of schooling or one-incident rule is adopted. Courts prescribe no preferred punishment; legal limits on discipline inform control and response.

**Related current rules:** Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX retains its distinct school-delegated-authority route; that route is not imputed to peers. Receipt of federal assistance remains separately necessary.

**Holdings navigation:** [Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education — A school's own culpable response can support damages for controlled peer harassment](HOLDINGS.md#a-schools-own-culpable-response-can-support-damages-for-controlled-peer-harassment-1); [Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education — Educational deprivation follows the contextual severe-or-pervasive standard](HOLDINGS.md#educational-deprivation-follows-the-contextual-severe-or-pervasive-standard).

## Standards and Tests selection: Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

### Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

**Governing question:** Whether mootness from voluntary settlement warrants vacating a judgment lawfully brought for review.

**Current rule:** Subsequent mootness prevents a merits decision but leaves authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of the judgment and proceeding. Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not establish entitlement to vacatur: the movant must supply a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances can warrant relief, but a settlement term requiring vacatur is not itself such a circumstance. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not command vacatur whenever review becomes moot.

**Authority by component:**

- Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment: Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment, 1994-11-08. Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.
- Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur: Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur, 1994-11-08. Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Present operation effective:** 1994-11-08; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** No heightened proof quantum or exhaustive list of exceptional circumstances is adopted. This settlement rule does not turn loss of review through circumstances outside the parties’ control into automatic vacatur.

**Holdings navigation:** Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment; Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur.

#### California Department of Corrections v. Morales

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1462.
**Decided:** April 25, 1995.

##### The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here

**Holding:** Applying the 1981 California hearing-deferral provision to Morales’s 1980 offense violates the Ex Post Facto Clause because the actual three-year deferral withdraws offense-date mandatory annual consideration during a period when parole is legally available, without an established ordinary substitute for the meaningful intervening opportunities. The separately reasoned prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that initial prediction do not preserve consideration of later rehabilitation, changed circumstances, or a materially mistaken forecast; relief restores consideration, not release.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: applies its opportunity rule to this reserved individualized setting; its original holding and rejection of proof of the omitted hearing’s outcome remain in force.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): applies protection of a legally available avenue to reduced custody and the distinction between guaranteed benefits and discretionary alternatives; it supplies no right to parole.
- Miller v. Florida, 482 U.S. 423 (1987): applies scrutiny of binding adverse operation despite unchanged outer limits and a possibly identical result.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990): preserves the punishment boundary; no free-standing rule against every retrospective procedural disadvantage is adopted.
- In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985): accepts its separate-reasons construction, permitted factual overlap and initial review; its state-court federal characterization does not displace Cavanaugh or establish ordinary later reopening.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1981 amendment retains annual review as the default. It permits a next hearing no later than three years after denial only for a prisoner convicted, in the same or different proceedings, of more than one offense involving the taking of a life, when the Board finds that parole cannot reasonably be expected at a hearing during the intervening years and states its bases. Jackson requires reasons separately directed to postponement, permits their supporting facts to overlap the present-unsuitability reasons, and establishes review of the initial choice. It establishes no generally available later reopening procedure. The rule concerns Morales's actual maximum deferral, not a proved cohort-wide practice; his eligibility arose during the postponed interval.

##### The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes

**Holding:** A prisoner challenging retrospective withdrawal of a parole-consideration opportunity must show the earlier legal entitlement, its retrospective actual withdrawal, and a concrete connection to a legally available avenue to earlier release; inconvenience alone is insufficient, and proof that the omitted hearing would actually have produced parole is unnecessary. After that showing, the State must substantiate any replacement mechanism it invokes by identifying its legal basis and establishing its practical availability and timely capacity to provide the relevant consideration; the prisoner retains the ultimate burden of establishing an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: preserves meaningful opportunity loss and the absence of an actual-release prerequisite; this decision expressly clarifies substantiation of an asserted replacement.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): applies its refusal to assume discretionary alternatives compensate for guaranteed benefits; it did not itself prescribe this parole evidentiary direction.
- In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985): confines review and conceivable advancement to what the opinion establishes; initial review does not become later reconsideration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns Morales’s demonstrated meaningful loss under the challenged 1981 provision. It does not invalidate every individualized postponement, require an interim hearing in every possible individualized scheme, or constitutionalize every scheduling detail. Maximum deferral is permission, not a statutory command or a proved near-uniform cohort practice. The Court neither requires wholly different facts for the two decisions nor treats the initial forecast as unreviewable.

No universal probability threshold, general presumption transferring constitutional proof to the State, numerical response deadline, mandatory filing procedure, suitability finding, release date, damages award, or right to favorable parole is adopted. Adequacy of a demonstrated materially different arrangement, later amendments and their transition or mootness effects are not decided. The false-information and sentence claims are not reopened. Unresolved credit and matrix calculations establish neither release entitlement nor legal impossibility of earlier release.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's hearing-frequency judgment is affirmed, 5–4, preserving its remand for offense-date annual consideration. Lawful suitability standards and release discretion remain; no parole, release date or damages are ordered. Rejected false-information and sentence claims remain undisturbed.

## Verbatim Holdings selection from state/holdings/first-amendment.md

#### Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC, 512 U.S. 622

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-44.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### Cable editorial expression receives ordinary First Amendment protection

**Holding:** Cable operators' editorial selection and cable programmers' expression are protected speech, and compulsory carriage burdens those interests. Cable's physical transmission capacity and market power do not justify importing broadcasting's spectrum-scarcity standard or reducing review of this expressive burden to rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts II-A and II-B as relevant to this proposition, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stevens recognizes protected cable expression but does not join these complete portions and is not counted for their formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Leathers v. Medlock, 499 U.S. 439: applies its recognition of cable speech and press; its approval of a broad content-neutral tax does not resolve compulsory carriage.
- City of Los Angeles v. Preferred Communications, Inc., 476 U.S. 488: applies its protection of editorial selection and attention to cable's characteristics; it had left the precise standard open.
- Red Lion Broadcasting Co. v. FCC, 395 U.S. 367: confines its scarcity rationale to the broadcasting setting; it supplies no diminished cable standard here.

##### Content classification depends on what the law selects and why

**Holding:** A law burdening protected expression because of its content ordinarily must be necessary and narrowly drawn to serve a compelling interest; a burden justified without reference to content is subject to the appropriate intermediate review. A benign purpose or absence of hostility does not neutralize a content-based operation, while a distinction among speakers or media does not by itself establish content discrimination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stevens does not join that portion; his agreement with the case-specific classification below is distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991: applies its actual content-based escrow holding; neutral collection remedies remain outside that holding.
- Arkansas Writers' Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S. 221: applies its scrutiny of differential burdens dependent on a publication's content.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: applies justification without reference to content as the classification inquiry.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: applies its rejection of benign-purpose neutrality for an unexplained content distinction; its commercial-speech fit holding remains bounded.
- Leathers v. Medlock: applies its distinction between permissible neutral differential treatment and censorial or content-based burdens.

##### The general full-power carriage requirements are content neutral

**Holding:** The general commercial and noncommercial full-power must-carry obligations regulate cable's distribution bottleneck and preservation of independently available broadcast service without selecting the stations' individual messages; intermediate scrutiny therefore governs those obligations. This classification does not decide the distinct low-power local-programming criteria, the market-adjustment provision, or a materially different content-based administration, and creates no general right to compel private editorial publication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts II-C and II-D, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg reject this application.

**Material application:** The classification addresses the general full-power core of §§4–5, whose commercial and noncommercial eligibility, capacity, duplication, signal-quality and channel-position provisions remain distinct. It does not adjudicate the separately reserved low-power programming criteria or market adjustments, authorize an editorial-balance mandate for another medium, or confer both must-carry and retransmission-consent entitlement at once.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo, 418 U.S. 241: distinguishes compelled reply and interference with newspaper editorial judgment; its protection is not displaced.
- Pacific Gas & Electric Co. v. Public Utilities Commission, 475 U.S. 1: distinguishes its fractured compelled-access reasoning concerning speech-responsive access and attribution; no unanimous categorical rule is attributed to it.
- Leathers v. Medlock: applies its allowance for justified neutral distinctions between media; the cable-specific burden still requires constitutional scrutiny.
- Red Lion Broadcasting Co. v. FCC: supplies no basis for the classification; broadcast scarcity is not extended.

##### Intermediate scrutiny requires advancement and a proportionate speech burden

**Holding:** A qualifying content-neutral carriage requirement must further an important or substantial governmental interest unrelated to suppressing expression and must not burden substantially more speech than necessary to further that interest. The Government need not establish that the law is the least restrictive conceivable alternative, but the actual expressive burden and relevant alternatives bear on whether its fit is sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. This common standard does not establish common agreement about whether the existing record satisfies it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. O'Brien, 391 U.S. 367: applies the important-interest, nonsuppression and incidental-burden requirements to this content-neutral carriage burden.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism: applies its requirement against substantially excessive burdens while preserving its rejection of least-restrictive-means review.

##### Preserving broadcast availability, diverse sources and fair competition are substantial interests

**Holding:** Preserving free local broadcast service, maintaining diverse sources of information and promoting fair competition in television distribution are important content-neutral interests supporting consideration of the general full-power carriage scheme under intermediate scrutiny. Their importance does not itself prove threatened harm, advancement, adequate fit or the scheme's final constitutionality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. The separate opinions disputing the neutrality of the Act's localism and programming preferences do not join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Leathers v. Medlock: applies the distinction between a medium's economic characteristics and selection of protected ideas.
- United States v. O'Brien and Ward v. Rock Against Racism: apply the requirement of a substantial nonsuppressive interest without treating that element as proof of advancement or tailoring.

##### Predictive judgments receive substantial deference and remain subject to review

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** In reviewing Congress's predictive judgments relevant to the carriage scheme, a court must give substantial deference while independently assuring that Congress drew reasonable inferences from substantial evidence. Congress need not compile the formal evidentiary record required of a court or agency; this general principle neither decides whether the evidence here suffices nor joins the four-Justice application requiring additional proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B, supported by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun and Souter, together with Stevens's express agreement in his concurrence at this limited level of generality: five Justices. ##### What a claimant must identify before invoking §1983 for a statutory violation

**Holding:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit the claimant, creating a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement for judicial enforcement. **Holding:** Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure creates no personal right to systemwide compliance. **Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

#### Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, 524 U.S. 274

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1866.

**Decided:** June 22, 1998.

##### Actual notice to a corrective official is not the exclusive attribution route

**Holding:** A funding recipient's responsibility for a teacher's intentional discrimination cannot invariably be defeated solely because no official empowered to correct the harassment had actual notice. Kinman is limited insofar as it made that particular notice route exclusive; employment, ordinary teacher access or a technical regulatory breach, standing alone, still does not establish recipient liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five directly adopt this limited negative proposition. They do not adopt a common complete damages framework.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Kinman v. Omaha Public School District:** Limited insofar as actual notice to a corrective official was the exclusive recipient-attribution threshold. Its actual-notice, deliberate and unreasonable disregard and causal-injury route remains available, together with the other Title IX elements. Its rejection of automatic teacher-status attribution and a technical policy breach as sufficient by themselves remains. No earlier sole-Justice awareness inference becomes Court law merely through this decision.
- **Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools:** Preserved as allowing appropriate compensation for proved intentional Title IX discrimination, subject to recipient responsibility, causation and loss. The present attribution holding does not establish a damages award or unintended-effects liability.
- **Cannon v. University of Chicago:** The established private action remains intact; no new regulatory cause of action is recognized.
- **Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association:** Its distinction between a directly shared negative proposition and unadopted complete affirmative frameworks supports keeping the exclusivity holding separate from the limited Marks analysis.

##### Authority materially enabling discrimination, combined with the recipient's causal fault, supplies a narrower attribution route

**Holding:** A recipient may be held responsible when school-conferred authority materially enables a teacher's intentional discrimination and the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes that discrimination to continue. This is a sufficient route for attributing the discrimination beyond the actual-corrective-official-notice route; it establishes neither the underlying discrimination nor causal loss, damages or the defeat of a supported defense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has no other formal joins. Its stated attribution component supplies the narrower controlling ground under Marks v. United States and Cipollone's component-specific application. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer independently support attribution whenever actual delegated educational power materially aids intentional harassment, without Stone-Zsela's additional recipient-fault requirement. School-conferred authority must actually and materially enable the wrong; title or opportunity alone is insufficient under either ground. Every case meeting the narrower conjunction therefore meets that independently sufficient broader attribution route. Those judgment-supporting positions total five, without converting the four Justices into joins in Stone-Zsela's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The authority, recipient-fault and causal-continuation conditions are conjunctive. The alternative involving reporting failure requires willful blindness and deliberate indifference, not constructive notice or negligence alone. The Court does not adopt ordinary agency liability as a complete general rule, the four-Justice negligence route or every Title VII attribution principle. Discrimination, institutional fault, causation, compensable injury, damages and defenses remain unresolved. Official-policy cases, prospective enforcement, individual liability and state-law remedies are outside this holding. The separate regulatory duties to adopt and publish prompt, equitable grievance procedures under 34 C.F.R. §106.8(b) and give nondiscrimination notice under §106.9(a) are not converted into strict private damages liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Marks v. United States:** Applied to the particular sufficient attribution ground, without treating every reason or remedy proposed by a judgment supporter as controlling.
- **Cipollone v. Liggett Group:** Its component-specific logical-subset inquiry supports the limited attribution holding despite the absence of whole-opinion joins; separate proof, defenses and damages questions remain distinct.
- **Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson, Andrade and Faragher:** Their employment-attribution principles are not transferred wholesale to Title IX. Faragher's unresolved effective-protection/nonuse defense is not resolved here.
- **Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman:** Its funding-notice principle remains; neither it nor Title IX's administrative enforcement sequence becomes a literal exhaustion requirement for this private action.

##### The appealed Title IX summary judgment is reversed for ordinary further proceedings

**Authority of the judgment:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this formal reversal and ordinary remand. Stone-Zsela alone adopts Part III's application of his recipient-fault reasoning to the disputed warning and reporting evidence; the other four support further proceedings on their separately stated grounds. Their judgment agreement is not a five-Justice finding of actual knowledge, deliberate disregard, willful blindness, negligence, material aid, causation or liability. The Court orders neither compensation nor a compulsory trial. The unappealed constitutional, personal and state-law claims are not reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's judgment is reversed only insofar as it affirms summary judgment for the district on the appealed Title IX claim, and the case is remanded. The lower courts must determine whether school-conferred authority materially enabled the discrimination and whether the recipient's own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure caused it to continue, while preserving the actual-notice route and the remaining elements, ordinary procedures and defenses.

#### Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-843.
**Decided:** 1999-05-24.

##### A school's own culpable response can support damages for controlled peer harassment

**Holding:** A Title IX funding recipient may be liable for its own response to peer harassment because of sex when an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial danger has actual notice, the recipient substantially controls both the harasser and the relevant educational context, and deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that danger causally contributes to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. A peer's attendance does not impute the peer's acts to the recipient; knowledge, culpability, control, sex-based discrimination and causal loss are independent requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six Justices).

##### Educational deprivation follows the contextual severe-or-pervasive standard

**Holding:** The harassment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive and impair educational access or benefit in its full context. Sexually charged conduct or offense alone does not establish discrimination because of sex, the required educational harm or the independent institutional elements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (five Justices). Stone-Zsela does not join the severe-or-pervasive threshold.

##### The pleaded recipient claim warrants ordinary further proceedings

**Holding:** Allegations of repeated school-setting harassment, reports including to the principal, continued conduct, deliberately inadequate protection and resulting educational interference state a claim for examination under the sufficient recipient-fault route. The categorical dismissal must be reversed, but an absent element, reasonable response, inadequate control or notice, missing causation or a supported defense may defeat recovery on the developed record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six Justices).

**Limits and questions not reached:** Notice, culpability, control, sex-based discrimination, educational deprivation and causal compensable injury are independent. Ordinary negligence, policy breach, peer attendance or unsuccessful reasonable correction alone do not establish liability. No general agency/defense code or Stone-Zsela reporting-failure proposal becomes controlling; teacher-attribution routes remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical dismissal and remand for ordinary proceedings under the sufficient recipient-own-wrong route. No compulsory trial, damages, particular discipline, individual-capacity recovery or reopening of unrelated claims is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools is applied to available compensation for proved intentional discrimination, with recipient responsibility, causation and loss still distinct.
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District is extended at its sufficient actual-notice/culpable-disregard route to substantially controlled peer conduct. Its severe-or-pervasive contextual educational-harm standard remains; the peer extension is stated expressly.
- Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District retains the limited teacher-authority-plus-recipient-fault conjunction and its rejection of exclusivity in teacher cases. Neither delegated teacher power nor that complete route is imputed to peers.
- NCAA v. Smith preserves the distinct federal-assistance recipient requirement; economic connection or control alone does not establish receipt.

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.
