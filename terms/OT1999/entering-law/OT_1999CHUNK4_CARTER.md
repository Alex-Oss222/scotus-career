## Standards and Tests selection: Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation

### Municipal responsibility and the underlying federal deprivation

**Governing question:** The distinction between an underlying federal deprivation and municipal responsibility, including final policymaking for the challenged function.

**Current rule:** A municipal employee who proves deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and the required municipal responsibility need not prove an additional, independent abuse-of-governmental-power element. The federal deprivation and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries. Employment alone establishes neither municipal liability nor a substantive right; *Canton*'s training analysis supplies attribution and causation, not the underlying constitutional guarantee. For a final-policymaker theory, the court identifies through state and local law, including custom or usage with legal force, who speaks finally for the particular entity on the challenged subject. Final operational discretion does not alone answer that question; labels, election, territory, financing or lack of commission control is individually insufficient. An Alabama sheriff's independent local office supplies County final law-enforcement authority in the criminal-investigation function addressed in McMillian, even without commission delegation or review. An attributable policy or final decision must still cause the proved federal injury.

**Authority by component:**

- Rejection of the additional element and separation of deprivation from attribution: Collins v. City of Harker Heights — No extra governmental-abuse element under §1983, February 26, 1992; Part A of Stevens's opinion, joined by all nine Justices.

- McMillian v. Monroe County — I. Final authority and the government represented are distinct inquiries, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg, all nine
- McMillian v. Monroe County — II. The sheriff's independent local office supplies County law-enforcement authority, 1997-06-02; Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five
- McMillian v. Monroe County — III. The County claim returns for the remaining liability elements, 1997-06-02; the same five

**Present operation effective:** McMillian, 1997-06-02, for final-policymaker identity and the specified Alabama function; Collins retains the separate underlying-deprivation rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992) — Municipal liability and public employment. McMillian v. Monroe County (1997-06-02) — Civil Rights / Municipal liability and final policymaking.



**Exceptions and limits:** Other sheriff functions, other States' arrangements, individual liability and State liability or immunity require separate analysis. Institutional identity does not prove the truth of misconduct or make every official act municipal policy. Bryan County's pending reargument supplies no hiring, fault or causation change.

## Standards and Tests selection: Municipal civil-rights pleading under the Federal Rules

### Municipal civil-rights pleading under the Federal Rules

**Governing question:** What instructions govern municipal civil-rights pleading under the Federal Rules?

**Current rule:** Courts may not impose a judge-created heightened factual-particularity requirement on municipal §1983 complaints beyond the then-operative Federal Rules. Rule 8 governs; Rule 9(b)'s fraud-or-mistake categories do not add municipal liability. Monell and Canton retain their substantive attribution and causation requirements. Rule 11, discovery control and Rule 56 remain distinct. No individual-officer pleading standard is decided.

**Authority by component:**

- The Federal Rules supply the pleading standard for municipal § 1983 claims: Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit — The Federal Rules supply the pleading standard for municipal § 1983 claims, 1993-03-03. Stone's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; unanimous controlling authority.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-03-03; Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements

### Prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements

**Governing question:** What instructions govern prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements?

**Current rule:** Ordinary investigative work to identify a perpetrator before probable cause has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for alleged fabrication; later courtroom use does not retrospectively transform it into advocacy. Genuine evaluation and preparation in an advocate's role remain protected; neither all pre-indictment nor all post-indictment acts share one classification. Statements to the press announcing an indictment also lack absolute prosecutorial immunity merely because they concern prosecution or cause injury through subsequent proceedings. Qualified immunity, an actionable constitutional deprivation and other liability predicates remain separate.

Preparing and filing charging papers or a warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions and presenting the State's legal case receive absolute immunity when they are advocacy functions. Bad purpose or alleged false evidence does not remove that protection; the Court adopts no general dishonesty exception. A prosecutor's distinct personal oath supplying facts to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant is instead a complaining-witness evidentiary function without absolute prosecutorial immunity. That classification does not require proof of falsity and leaves ordinary courtroom testimony immunity intact. Denial of this immunity does not establish liability: constitutional deprivation, culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury and available personal defenses remain separate. A judge's warrant does not categorically sever causation where material deception procured it, while a truthful corrected account may still establish probable cause.

**Authority by component:**

- Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity: Buckley v. Fitzsimmons — Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity, 1993-06-24. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- A press conference does not carry an advocate’s absolute immunity: Buckley v. Fitzsimmons — A press conference does not carry an advocate’s absolute immunity, 1993-06-24. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- Protected advocacy and personal warrant certification: Fletcher v. Kalina, Fletcher v. Kalina — I. Protected advocacy and a distinct evidentiary act (eight Associates for the complete advocacy rule), Fletcher v. Kalina — II. Personal factual certification supporting an arrest warrant (all nine), and Fletcher v. Kalina — III. The immunity ruling permits further proceedings, not a liability judgment (eight Associates for the source-specific causation and ordinary qualified-immunity qualifications), 1997-06-16.

**Present operation effective:** Fletcher v. Kalina, 1997-06-16; Buckley's investigation and press components retain their original operation.

**Holdings navigation:** Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259 (1993). Fletcher v. Kalina (1997-06-16) — Protected prosecutorial advocacy remains distinct from personal evidentiary acts; Fletcher v. Kalina (1997-06-16) — Civil Rights / Prosecutorial investigation and press statements.

## Standards and Tests selection: State immunity of bistate compact entities

### State immunity of bistate compact entities

**Governing question:** When a bistate compact entity shares its constituent States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity.

**Current rule:** Exercise of governmental power alone does not confer the States’ immunity. The entity’s actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must give good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred. Exposure of the state treasuries is highly probative but not categorically indispensable; a separate, self-supporting enterprise responsible for its own obligations cannot claim immunity merely from its public functions.

**Authority by component:**

- PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity: PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity, 1994-11-14. Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Six of nine participants directly support this entity-specific rule and its application to both employee actions. O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas dissent; no fragmented-rationale analysis is needed.

**Present operation effective:** 1994-11-14; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This compact-entity inquiry does not establish a universal sole-treasury test, decide abrogation or consent, or equate immunity with governmental status for constitutional-rights purposes.

**Holdings navigation:** PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Collateral indemnification of an otherwise immune state arm

### Collateral indemnification of an otherwise immune state arm

**Governing question:** Whether a third-party promise to reimburse an otherwise immune state instrumentality eliminates its immunity.

**Current rule:** A third party's contractual reimbursement of litigation expense or an adverse judgment does not by itself remove Eleventh Amendment immunity when the state instrumentality remains defendant and primary legal obligor. Antecedent arm status still depends on the complete legal, financial and institutional relationship with the State; collateral reimbursement neither replaces that inquiry nor makes structural financing irrelevant.

**Authority by component:**

- Regents of the University of California v. Doe — Collateral indemnification does not alter an assumed state arm's immunity, 1997-02-19; Stone-Zsela for all nine supplies the distinct collateral-indemnity rule

**Present operation effective:** Regents v. Doe, 1997-02-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule assumes otherwise established arm status; it creates no sole-treasury test, general funding immunity, waiver or abrogation rule.

**Related current rules:** State immunity of bistate compact entities supplies its own antecedent structural inquiry.

**Holdings navigation:** Regents of the University of California v. Doe (1997-02-19) — Collateral indemnification does not alter an assumed state arm's immunity.

## Copied Holdings selection: McMillian v. Monroe County

#### McMillian v. Monroe County

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-542.
**Decided:** June 2, 1997.

##### Final policymaking requires identification of the particular entity and function

**Holding:** Under Monell and Jett, the court identifies through state and local law, including custom or usage with legal force, the official who speaks with final policymaking authority for the particular governmental entity concerning the challenged action. Final operational discretion does not alone identify that entity; office labels, election, territory, funding and absence of commission control are each insufficient standing alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final operational discretion and the identity of the represented government are separate; County government need not consist solely of its commission.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Monell requires the institution's own policy. Pembaur preserves a single authorized final decision without treating four-Justice Part II-B as the whole Court's rationale. Praprotnik's plurality preserves legally effective custom, genuine delegation and ratification; Jett Part IV supplies the majority identification rule. Collins and Kinman separate deprivation and institutional attribution. Swint decided appellate jurisdiction; the later permitted §1292(b) route lawfully presents the merits.

##### The Alabama sheriff supplies County policy in this criminal-investigation function

**Holding:** An Alabama sheriff represents the County when setting and executing the law-enforcement policy involved in these criminal-investigation acts. The independently constituted final local office supplies that County authority without a delegation from, or power of review in, the county commission; the holding does not classify every sheriff function or every act as County policy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Material application:** The locally elected, independently final law-enforcement office, County salary and necessary equipment obligations together establish the local institution. State executive placement, immunity classifications, duties to prosecutors and courts, and §36-22-5 written-investigation directions do not displace that autonomous local authority here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No actual State instruction is assumed, and financing is not treated as investigative control. Other functions, including jails, other States, State damages liability and immunity require separate analysis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hereford and Parker v. Amerson inform Alabama structure but do not decide federal attribution. No all-functions rule follows from separate analogies to Parker v. Williams or Hess.

##### Reinstatement leaves violation, attributable policy and moving-force causation to proof

**Holding:** The County-attribution dismissal is reversed and the claim based on the sheriff's final law-enforcement policymaking is reinstated for further proceedings. McMillian must still establish a federal violation and an attributable County policy or final decision that was the moving force behind his injury; the sheriff's institutional status establishes neither the allegations' truth nor liability for every act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the County-attribution dismissal and reinstate the policy claim without a liability finding, damages or compulsory trial. Personal claims and immunity and unreviewed evidentiary rulings remain in their own posture.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Collins retains the separate deprivation and institutional inquiries; Kinman's single-final-decision route makes the claim legally possible without guaranteeing recovery.

## Copied Holdings selection: Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259 (1993)

#### Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7849.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity

**Holding:** A prosecutor who performs ordinary investigative work to obtain evidence identifying a perpetrator before probable cause has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for alleged fabrication in that function; later courtroom use does not transform the earlier work into advocacy. Genuine evaluation and preparation in an advocate’s role remain protected, and neither all pre-indictment acts nor all post-indictment acts receive a categorical classification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Imbler v. Pachtman, 424 U.S. 409: protected advocacy and genuine preparation preserved; ordinary investigation distinguished.
- Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478: function-specific limits applied to investigation.
- Forrester v. White, 484 U.S. 219, and Antoine, June 7, 1993: function and claimant’s burden control; importance to litigation is insufficient.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Fletcher v. Kalina, June 16, 1997, Ninth Circuit No. 95-36129: the functional distinction extends to a prosecutor’s personal arrest-warrant certification while preserving protected advocacy.

##### A press conference does not carry an advocate’s absolute immunity

**Holding:** A prosecutor’s statements to the press announcing an indictment have no absolute prosecutorial immunity merely because they concern a pending prosecution or cause injury through later judicial proceedings. The ordinary qualified-immunity inquiry and the requirement of an actionable constitutional deprivation remain separate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. Genuine advocacy preparation and unreviewed in-court presentation immunity remain; paid or coerced witness allegations require initial treatment below. Qualified immunity, constitutional injury, causation and damages are unresolved; defamation alone is not held actionable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand: no absolute immunity for the pleaded preliminary fabrication (6–3) or press statements (9–0). No liability or witness-immunity change.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Imbler, 424 U.S. 409, and Burns, 500 U.S. 478: press activity lacks the protected advocacy function.
- Tower v. Glover, 467 U.S. 914: policy alone cannot create a new §1983 absolute immunity.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800, and Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: separate qualified-immunity law left unchanged.

## Copied Holdings selection: Fletcher v. Kalina

#### Fletcher v. Kalina

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit No. 95-36129; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 16, 1997.

##### Protected prosecutorial advocacy remains distinct from personal evidentiary acts

**Holding:** A prosecutor has absolute immunity under §1983 for preparing and filing an information or warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions, and presenting the State's legal case when performing an advocate's function. An allegation of bad purpose or false evidence does not remove that protection from the advocacy function, but the protection does not extend to a distinct personal evidentiary certification merely because the same prosecutor performs both acts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins protection of legitimate advocacy and separation of the personal evidentiary act, but not refusal of an additional dishonesty exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The function, rather than a categorical pre-charge or post-charge boundary, determines immunity. No general dishonesty exception to otherwise protected advocacy is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Imbler preserves charging and courtroom advocacy; Burns separates legal presentation from other acts. Buckley preserves genuine preparation while excluding ordinary investigation; Antoine requires historical and functional justification rather than importance to adjudication.

##### A prosecutor personally certifying arrest-warrant facts acts as a complaining witness

**Holding:** A prosecutor who personally swears to factual assertions offered to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant performs the evidentiary function of a complaining witness and has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for that certification. This classification depends on the personal evidentiary undertaking, not on proof of falsity, and does not withdraw immunity from ordinary courtroom witness testimony.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Kalina personally guaranteed the facts offered to obtain Fletcher's arrest warrant; that evidentiary undertaking is separate from preparing and presenting the charging papers.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither actual falsity nor the availability of another truthful affiant determines the functional classification. Ordinary courtroom testimony immunity remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the denial of absolute immunity for the personal certification and remand, while preserving immunity for protected legal preparation, filing and presentation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Malley supplies the warrant-affiant comparison; official prosecutorial title does not immunize the same evidentiary function. Buckley and Antoine extend their functional distinction to the personal certification, without changing their other boundaries.

##### The function-specific immunity denial leaves constitutional liability and personal defenses unresolved

**Holding:** Denial of absolute immunity for the personal oath leaves Fletcher to establish an actionable constitutional deprivation, the required culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury; Kalina retains qualified immunity and other available personal defenses. The existing qualified-immunity framework governs the remand, using the law and information relevant to the conduct at issue, without a new defense-burden rule or a finding that any element or defense has been established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, adopted in full by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the unresolved-liability and bounded-remand propositions and accepts the existing qualified-immunity framework, without joining the source-specific causation and notice explanations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A judge's warrant does not categorically break causation when material deception procured it, but a corrected truthful account may still establish probable cause. Neither question is resolved here. Disputed historical facts follow ordinary procedural rules; legal immunity is for the judge. No damages judgment or mandatory trial follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hafer preserves personal-capacity liability only on proof of the officer’s own violation and preserves personal defenses. Harlow and Anderson retain objective, particularized clearly-established-law protection. Elder requires consideration of all relevant precedent; Lanier rejects categorical source and factual-twin restrictions, without supplying retrospective notice.

## Copied Holdings selection: Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21 (1991)

#### Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-681.  
**Decided:** November 5, 1991.

##### Whether a state officer sued individually is a section 1983 person for acts undertaken through official authority

**Holding:** A state officer sued in an individual capacity is a person under 42 U.S.C. §1983 even when the challenged act was undertaken through official authority, and state sovereign immunity does not independently bar a judgment payable by that officer personally. The plaintiff must still prove the officer's own violation under color of law, and the officer retains applicable personal immunities.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, and Souter; all eight participants.

**Material application:** The Court of Appeals construed the damages claims as personal claims against Hafer. Her authority to discharge employees supplies the state-law connection without converting the claims into claims against Pennsylvania.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional violations, qualified immunity, damages, and entitlement to reinstatement remain unresolved; no immunity standard changes.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Third Circuit is affirmed. The individual-capacity claims may proceed; no damages or reinstatement are awarded by this decision.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kentucky v. Graham supplies the distinction between litigation capacities. Will v. Michigan Department of State Police is clarified and preserved for States and official-capacity damages defendants. Scheuer v. Rhodes confirms possible personal liability of executive officers; Monroe v. Pape supplies the under-color-of-law rule for misuse of official power. Ex parte Young's prospective-relief channel is unchanged.

## Copied Holdings selection: Plakas v. Drinski

#### Plakas v. Drinski

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1824.
**Decided:** January 9, 1995.

##### The complete supported circumstances establish reasonable defensive force

**Holding:** Deadly force is objectively reasonable on this summary-judgment record because, after an assault, an explicit lethal threat and efforts to obtain surrender, an armed person advanced at close range with a raised poker while the officer's retreat became blocked, and the identified evidence supplies no supported contrary material account. Graham requires the complete contemporaneous circumstances and Garner's serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning safeguards; the Fourth Amendment does not require the least dangerous conceivable tactic, and this decision neither excludes every earlier police act nor decides a developed claim of unjustified police conduct creating the danger.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Graham v. Connor, 490 U.S. 386 (1989): applies objective reasonableness under the complete contemporaneous circumstances from the reasonable officer's on-scene perspective, with allowance for tense and rapidly developing events; no subjective-malice or hindsight test is substituted.
- Tennessee v. Garner, 471 U.S. 1 (1985): preserves the prohibition on deadly force against a nondangerous fleeing person and the serious-threat, necessity and feasible-warning conditions; this supported immediate armed attack differs from nondangerous escape.
- Hunter v. Bryant, 502 U.S. 224 (1991): preserves the supported nonmovant account and complete contemporaneous record at summary judgment; this case neither selects immunity nor uses Hunter as notice predating its decision.
- Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510 (1994): preserves consideration of relevant law on a preserved immunity issue, ordinary record limits and conduct-date notice; no immunity disposition or mandatory merits-first sequence is added.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The earlier-beating theory was not pleaded and lacks the asserted causal showing; the alleged invitation to strike was not developed as immediately preceding or causing the charge. Gilmere's different provocation theory is neither approved nor disapproved. The Court does not decide a case in which the threat ended before the shot or a supported safe alternative makes the force unreasonable. The merits ground supplies no general immunity for tactical decisions.

##### The county equipment-and-training theory fails on its own required elements

**Holding:** A municipality's §1983 liability requires a cognizable federal deprivation attributable to municipal policy or training, the applicable culpability and a causal connection; officer personal immunity is not municipal immunity, and reasonable final force alone does not answer every independent municipal-created-danger theory. The county is entitled to summary judgment here because the actual equipment-and-training contention establishes neither a constitutional equipment-list duty nor a developed independent deprivation with the required causal and culpability showing; a purely derivative force theory also lacks a predicate when the force itself is reasonable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the county judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): preserves municipal policy/custom responsibility and causation for a federal deprivation; employment or vicarious responsibility alone is insufficient.
- City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378 (1989): applies the required failure-to-train culpability and causal connection; it does not create a substantive right to every device or training method.
- Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992): applies the separation between deprivation and municipal attribution; its voluntary-employment holding does not decide every affirmative municipal injury.
- City of Los Angeles v. Heller, 475 U.S. 796 (1986): applies to a derivative theory resting solely on constitutionally reasonable force; it does not supply a universal bar to a distinct municipal wrong.
- Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993): preserves ordinary municipal pleading and distinct Rule 56 proof; it neither relaxes Monell/Canton elements nor supplies personal immunity to a municipality.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary judgment for Drinski and Newton County is affirmed on the presented force and municipal claims. No trial, damages or personal-immunity ruling is ordered.

## Copied Holdings selection: Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., 513 U.S. 30

#### Hess v. Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp., 513 U.S. 30

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1197.
**Decided:** November 14, 1994.

##### PATH’s actual compact and financial structure does not confer the States’ immunity

**Holding:** A bistate compact entity does not acquire the States’ Eleventh Amendment immunity merely by exercising governmental power: its actual legal, financial and institutional relationship must supply good reason to conclude that the States structured it as their arm and Congress concurred, with state-treasury exposure highly probative but not categorically indispensable. PATH is a distinct, financially self-supporting enterprise responsible for its obligations; these employee suits impose no judgment obligation on either State and do not treat either State itself as a defendant, so PATH lacks that immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Lake Country Estates, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency*, 440 U.S. 391 (1979), applies its entity-specific compact inquiry: exercising state power does not itself make a bistate corporation the States, and the compact’s actual structure matters.
- *Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), preserves the distinction between a separate public entity and an arm of a State; a governmental function alone supplies no immunity.
- *Port Authority Trans-Hudson Corp. v. Feeney*, 495 U.S. 299 (1990), is distinguished as deciding federal-forum consent while assuming, rather than deciding, PATH’s arm status. Its consent ruling is not an earlier holding that PATH is immune.
- *Hilton v. South Carolina Public Railways Commission*, 502 U.S. 197 (1991), preserves state-court FELA coverage without constructive waiver of federal-forum immunity; no abrogation or waiver rule is enlarged here.
- *Puerto Rico Aqueduct & Sewer Authority v. Metcalf & Eddy, Inc.*, 506 U.S. 139 (1993), preserves immediate review of a qualifying immunity denial without establishing that a particular entity is an immune arm. Its appealability holding does not decide PATH’s status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not declare every compact entity nonimmune, make a debt disclaimer alone dispositive, decide a materially different entity’s practical dependence, abolish state sovereign immunity or hold that FELA abrogates federal-forum immunity. Negligence, causation, damages, the Boiler Inspection Act merits, every independently applicable nonimmunity limitations defense and hypothetical consent exceptions remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both the Hess and Walsh immunity dismissals are reversed and remanded. The Eleventh Amendment bar is removed; ordinary nonimmunity defenses and the separate actions' merits remain undecided.

## Copied Holdings selection: Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993)

#### Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1657.

**Decided:** March 3, 1993.

##### The Federal Rules supply the pleading standard for municipal § 1983 claims

**Holding:** A federal court may not impose a judge-created heightened factual-particularity requirement on a § 1983 complaint against a municipality beyond the then-operative Federal Rules. Rule 8 governs the complaint, Rule 9(b)'s fraud-or-mistake categories do not create a municipal-liability category, and Monell and Canton retain their separate substantive requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices; unanimous controlling authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S. 41 (1957): applied for fair-notice pleading; no subsequent plausibility rule is imported.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): preserved; municipal policy/custom and causation remain necessary, while freedom from employer-based liability supplies no immunity from suit.
- Owen v. City of Independence, 445 U.S. 622 (1980): applied to reject municipal qualified immunity and the pleading argument built on it.
- City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378 (1989): preserved as substantive failure-to-train attribution law, not authority for greater pleading particularity.
- Elliott v. Perez, 751 F.2d 1472 (5th Cir.1985), and Palmer v. City of San Antonio, 810 F.2d 514 (5th Cir.1987): rejected insofar as their rule demands heightened particularity for municipal § 1983 complaints; individual-immunity pleading remains unresolved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary Rule 8 and Rule 11 duties, discovery control and Rule 56 remain. Monell/Canton attribution and the underlying constitutional wrong are not relaxed. Individual-officer immunity pleading, collateral estoppel and unreviewed alternative summary-judgment grounds remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit and remand under the ordinary pleading rules; no municipal liability finding or new trial is ordered.

## Copied Holdings selection: Regents of the University of California v. Doe

#### Regents of the University of California v. Doe

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1694.
**Decided:** February 19, 1997.

##### Collateral indemnification does not alter an assumed state arm's immunity

**Holding:** A third party's contractual promise to reimburse litigation expense or an adverse judgment does not by itself divest an otherwise immune state instrumentality of Eleventh Amendment immunity. The instrumentality remains the defendant and primary legal obligor; collateral reimbursement does not replace the complete inquiry into its legal, financial and institutional relationship with the State or make structural financing irrelevant to antecedent arm status.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), unanimous.
