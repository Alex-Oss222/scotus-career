#### Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7358.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error

**Holding:** Using Brecht’s silence after Miranda warnings to impeach his trial explanation violates Doyle’s due-process protection; permitted impeachment with prewarning silence is analytically separate. This violation is trial error subject to harmless-error review, not a merely nonconstitutional rule or a structural defect requiring automatic reversal.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Doyle, 426 U.S. 610 — applied: warning-induced silence receives due-process protection.
- Jenkins, 447 U.S. 231, and Fletcher, 455 U.S. 603 — distinguished: prewarning impeachment remains permissible in the circumstances presented.
- Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 — applied: this evidentiary trial error permits harmlessness analysis; structural defects remain distinct.

##### Chapman governs this preserved Doyle claim on habeas

**Holding:** For this preserved Doyle trial error properly before the federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict. Collateral posture alone does not replace that burden with Kotteakos; the reviewing court must apply Chapman to the whole record, with relief conditional on failure of the State’s showing rather than automatic from the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II–III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman, 386 U.S. 18 — applied on federal habeas to this preserved Doyle claim with its burden unchanged.
- Kotteakos, 328 U.S. 750 — not extended to replace Chapman here; its existing nonconstitutional-error operation remains.
- Rose, 478 U.S. 570, and Yates, 500 U.S. 391 — continuity supported; prior assumed use is not misdescribed as a square holding on this distinction.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and Richmond — unchanged: actual correction of capital weighing errors remains distinct.
- Sawyer and Herrera — not extended into a universal innocence requirement for review of preserved trial error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Chapman ruling covers this preserved Doyle claim properly on habeas, not every constitutional collateral claim. Exhaustion, preservation, other habeas gates, structural-error doctrine and capital weighing-error correction remain distinct. The Court does not decide harmlessness or impose an innocence prerequisite.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand for whole-record Chapman review with the State’s burden. Relief follows only if that showing fails; no automatic writ, release, acquittal or present retrial deadline.

**Later-authority backlinks:** O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: extends Chapman review to the preserved personal-intent claim arising from the combined instructions and prosecutorial argument; the State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden if a violation is found. The Doyle holding remains unchanged, and no general rule for all preserved constitutional trial errors is adopted. See Criminal Procedure — Constitutional trial error on habeas. Calderon v. Coleman, No. 98-437, December 14, 1998: extends State-borne Chapman review to the defined, properly available capital-selection commutation-instruction claim; the earlier holding remains unchanged and no universal constitutional-habeas extension is adopted.

#### Sullivan v. Louisiana, 508 U.S. 275 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-5129.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in Sullivan v. Louisiana, June 1, 1993, No. 92-5129, see Federal Courts — Finality of a completed conviction ruling.

##### A charge vitiating the reasonable-doubt standard leaves no valid verdict for harmless review

**Holding:** When an unconstitutional reasonable-doubt charge vitiates the jury’s governing standard for all factual findings, the defendant has received no constitutionally valid jury verdict of guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. That structural defect requires reversal without harmless-error assessment; an appellate court’s hypothetical verdict cannot substitute for the required jury determination, and this rule does not classify every instructional mistake as structural.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Cage defect is conceded. Different instructional errors, evidentiary sufficiency and collateral-review gates remain unresolved; the distinct penalty-phase relief is not reviewed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the conviction-sustaining judgment and remand for proceedings permitting a new trial under a valid reasonable-doubt charge. No acquittal; prior penalty-phase relief survives and the original death sentence is not reinstated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship, 397 U.S. 358: proof-beyond-reasonable-doubt requirement applied.
- Duncan v. Louisiana, 391 U.S. 145: jury’s constitutional role applied.
- Cage v. Louisiana, 498 U.S. 39: conceded defect taken as the predicate, not relitigated.
- Chapman v. California, 386 U.S. 18: actual-verdict inquiry preserved; no substitute hypothetical verdict permitted.
- Rose v. Clark, 478 U.S. 570: separable presumption error distinguished.
- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: structural-defect category applied.
- Estelle v. McGuire, December 4, 1991: contested-charge framework preserved; no reopening of the conceded defect.
- Brecht v. Abrahamson, April 21, 1993: preserved-Doyle habeas rule unchanged; not a Kotteakos constitutional-habeas rule.

#### Stansbury v. California, 511 U.S. 318

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-5770.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

##### Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned

**Holding:** Miranda custody exists upon formal arrest or restraint on freedom of movement of the degree associated with formal arrest, assessed from all objective circumstances as a reasonable person in the interviewee’s position would understand them; an officer’s uncommunicated view that the person is or is not a suspect, and the interviewee’s undisclosed subjective belief, have no independent role. An officer’s words or conduct conveying suspicion matter only to the extent they affect that objective understanding: communicated suspicion is not automatically custody, and undisclosed beliefs bearing on a witness’s credibility do not thereby become independent custody factors.

**Proposition-level authority:** The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule and reversal with remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): applied to the custodial-interrogation trigger; no new exception to warnings is created.
- Oregon v. Mathiason, 429 U.S. 492 (1977), and California v. Beheler, 463 U.S. 1121 (1983): applied for the arrest-equivalent restraint standard; station questioning and suspect status alone remain insufficient.
- Berkemer v. McCarty, 468 U.S. 420 (1984): applied to exclude uncommunicated intentions from the objective custody inquiry.
- Beckwith v. United States, 425 U.S. 341 (1976): applied to distinguish investigative focus from custody.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual custody, independent coercion, admissibility, harmlessness and capital punishment are not decided. An undisclosed belief bearing on witness credibility does not become an independent custody factor. Withrow and Powell's different review and remedy rules remain unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California Supreme Court judgment is reversed and remanded to reconsider custody without the private investigative-focus criterion. No suppression, new trial, acquittal, release or resentencing is ordered; ultimate custody and any properly reached consequences remain for the state court.

#### Davis v. United States, 512 U.S. 452

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1949.

**Decided:** 1994-06-24.

##### A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification

**Holding:** After a valid waiver, words that a reasonable officer would understand in context as possibly requesting counsel for the present interrogation require a pause in substantive questioning and limited, neutral clarification; a passing unrelated reference to a lawyer or speculation about an unexpressed wish does not. Officers may resume after a clarified voluntary choice to proceed, but must maintain the pause while a reasonable possible present request remains unresolved, and may not demand justification for counsel, promise benefits for continuing, discourage counsel, predict counsel's advice, or repeatedly pressure the suspect under the guise of clarification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt the mandatory clarification rule, its trigger and its limits; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline this part.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miranda v. Arizona, 384 U.S. 436 (1966): develops its protection of an effective custodial counsel choice through the stated postwaiver clarification rule.
- Edwards v. Arizona, 451 U.S. 477 (1981): preserves cessation upon actual invocation; clarification addresses the antecedent genuinely uncertain choice.
- Smith v. Illinois, 469 U.S. 91 (1984): preserves its prohibition on using later answers to defeat an initially clear invocation and resolves the distinct ambiguity question it left open.
- Connecticut v. Barrett, 479 U.S. 523 (1987): applies its attention to the scope of the suspect's communicated choice, without requiring legal terminology.
- McNeil v. Wisconsin, 501 U.S. 171 (1991): preserves the need for an actual expression concerning custodial counsel and does not treat a different judicial-proceeding request as such an expression.

##### The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful

**Holding:** Following Davis's valid initial waiver and genuinely uncertain first reference to counsel, the agents lawfully paused offense questioning, neutrally clarified that he was not requesting counsel, and resumed questioning; their later brief reminder did not supply a new full warning or new written waiver. Admission of the challenged November 4 statements therefore supplies no ground to reverse the military judgment on this invocation theory, without using later answers to defeat an initially clear request.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this actual-clarification application and affirmance; the four Justices who reject a duty accept the permissibility of the clarification actually used.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connecticut v. Barrett: applies respect for the scope of the actual communicated choice to the credited clarification and disclaimer.
- Smith v. Illinois: distinguishes genuine clarification of uncertainty from impermissible later-answer erosion of a clear invocation.

##### A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers

**Holding:** A clear request for custodial counsel stops police-initiated substantive interrogation unless counsel is present or the suspect initiates further communication and validly waives the protection under the governing rules; no prescribed legal formula is required. An initially clear request cannot be rendered ambiguous by later answers elicited in violation of that protection, and Davis's later clear request was honored by ending the interview.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this preservation and application of the clear-invocation rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Edwards v. Arizona: applies and preserves cessation, counsel-presence and suspect-initiation/valid-waiver requirements.
- Smith v. Illinois: applies and preserves the ban on undoing a clear initial invocation through later answers elicited in violation of counsel protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides postwaiver invocation in the Miranda/Edwards framework accepted by the parties and applied through the military rules. It does not independently resolve direct Fifth Amendment operation in military proceedings, Sixth Amendment attachment, prewaiver conduct, initial-warning adequacy, invalid initial waiver or the separate October 20 interview and evidence dispute. Section 3501 was not the Government's merits ground and supplies no alternative holding on construction, applicability or constitutionality. Withrow's collateral and coercion rules and Stansbury's objective custody inquiry remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court of Military Appeals is affirmed unanimously on the postwaiver invocation issue. No exclusion, new trial or remand follows; the separate October 20 dispute and any §3501 ground remain undecided.

#### United States v. Alvarez-Sanchez, 511 U.S. 350

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1812.

**Decided:** 1994-05-02.

##### The relevant presentment duty precedes the measurement of delay

**Holding:** In a federal or District of Columbia prosecution, detention solely on state charges does not itself start §3501(c)’s federal-presentment period; the period concerns delay in performing the duty to bring the person before an officer empowered to commit defendants for federal or District of Columbia offenses. Any law-enforcement officer can hold a person on a basis or arrangement engaging that duty, but suspicion of a federal offense, ordinary interagency notification or a state label alone does not decide it; the Court does not make formal federal charges or arrest indispensable in every possible arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion for the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the same bounded rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- McNabb v. United States, 318 U.S. 332 (1943), and Mallory v. United States, 354 U.S. 449 (1957): used to identify the federal-presentment setting; their complete relationship with §3501 is not decided.
- Anderson v. United States, 318 U.S. 350 (1943): preserved as the relevant improper-working-arrangement authority; the effect of §3501 on its rule and the full range of qualifying arrangements remain open.
- Stansbury v. California, decided April 26, 1994: distinguished; objective Miranda custody does not itself establish the separate federal-presentment duty.

##### The limited statutory protection does not decide all confession admissibility

**Holding:** Section 3501(c) prevents exclusion solely for delay in presentment when the trial judge finds the confession voluntary, its weight is left to the jury, and it was made within six hours of the relevant arrest or detention; the six-hour limit does not apply when the judge finds the longer delay reasonable considering the means of transportation and distance to the nearest available qualifying magistrate or other committing officer. The Court does not decide the treatment of confessions obtained more than six hours after relevant federal arrest outside that proviso, the general relationship among subsections (a), (b) and (c), or §3501’s entire relationship to Miranda.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this limited statutory explanation. Ginsburg’s concurrence, joined by Blackmun, emphasizes the reservation without changing either Justice’s full join or supplying a separate controlling answer.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993): preserved in its distinction between Miranda and independent coercion; no new collateral-review rule is announced.
- Gerstein v. Pugh, 420 U.S. 103 (1975), and County of Riverside v. McLaughlin, 500 U.S. 44 (1991): their constitutional rules remain unchanged; the unpreserved alternative is not adjudicated here.
- Powell v. Nevada, decided March 30, 1994: preserved with its separate timing and remedy questions; it does not excuse the missing presentation of this alternative below.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reported district-court no-collusion finding is procedural posture, not an adopted correctness finding. Section 3501's effect on Anderson, the full range of improper arrangements, post-six-hour confessions outside the proviso, and the statute's general relationship to Miranda remain unresolved. A waiver does not settle independent coercion.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit is reversed and remanded on the state-detention aggregation error. No admission or suppression of the confession, reinstatement of conviction, retrial, acquittal or release is directed. The Gerstein/McLaughlin alternative, unpresented below, is not adjudicated or affirmatively reopened.

#### Beecham v. United States and Jones v. United States, 511 U.S. 368

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-445; Jones included under Rule 12.2.

**Decided:** 1994-05-16.

##### The law of the convicting jurisdiction governs the conviction exclusion

**Holding:** Section 921(a)(20)'s direction to use the law of the jurisdiction where the proceedings occurred governs the related exclusion for expungement, set-aside, pardon, or restoration of civil rights, so state restoration alone does not remove a federal conviction as a §922(g) predicate. The enacted exclusion remains subject to its qualification that a conviction is not excluded if the pardon, expungement, or restoration of civil rights expressly provides that the person may not ship, transport, possess, or receive firearms; the Court decides which sovereign's law governs, not whether a particular federal restorative mechanism exists or has been satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), unanimous; all nine Justices join the complete rule and both affirmances.

**Material application:** State restoration alone does not remove either federal predicate. Jones's two restored West Virginia convictions remain excluded; the decision does not revive them.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court creates no federal restoration process and does not decide a particular federal pardon, the availability or requirements of §925(c) relief, the civil-rights bundle, automatic versus affirmative restoration, or whether federal habeas qualifies as a set-aside. It enters no guilt finding or independent ruling on the linked false-statement count's elements, materiality or intent.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both Fourth Circuit judgments are affirmed, 9–0 each. Jones's indictment-reinstatement and remand disposition remains operative; Beecham receives no new sentencing or restoration order.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453 (1991): applied to reserve lenity for unresolved penal ambiguity after interpretation; competing proposed readings alone do not trigger it.
- United States v. Granderson, decided March 22, 1994: its contextual interpretive approach and residual role for lenity remain intact; its distinct sentencing ambiguity does not establish ambiguity here.
- Jarecki v. G. D. Searle & Co., 367 U.S. 303 (1961): its neighboring-terms principle supports, without mechanically compelling, the shared sovereign reference.
- Dickerson v. New Banner Institute, Inc., 460 U.S. 103 (1983): the intervening statutory choice-of-law text governs; its former federal-law definition is not used to bypass the enacted provision.

The contrary separate-clause reasoning of Geyler and Edwards is rejected on sovereign selection. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

#### United States v. Gaudin

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-514.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### The jury decides application of the materiality element

**Holding:** When materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine it beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge states statutory meaning and the governing legal standard; labeling its application a mixed question does not remove an element from the jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materiality concerns a statement's natural tendency or capacity to influence the relevant governmental decision. The Government conceded that it was an element and did not contest the lower court's error classifications. Clause-specific materiality requirements, universal structural-error treatment and exact unverified preservation history remain undecided. Statutory interpretation and threshold evidentiary sufficiency remain judicial functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship: applies proof beyond a reasonable doubt to facts establishing guilt.
- Sinclair v. United States: overruled to the extent it assigns a criminal pertinency element to the judge; unrelated propositions are not displaced.
- Kungys v. United States: distinguishes civil denaturalization and its appellate allocation.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana: preserves the actual existing constitutional jury/burden holding; this case creates no universal omitted-element error classification.
- Hubbard v. United States: preserves §1001 entity coverage at its actual scope; supplies no authority to remove HUD offense elements from a jury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the reviewed false-statement convictions. Lawful retrial submitting every element, including materiality, to the jury remains available; no acquittal, disposition of the separate equity-skimming conviction or sentence calculation follows.

#### Thompson v. Keohane, 516 U.S. 99

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-6615.
**Decided:** November 29, 1995.

##### Independent determination of ultimate custody and statutory protection of historical facts

**Holding:** Whether established interrogation circumstances would cause a reasonable person to understand freedom of movement as restrained to the degree of formal arrest is a mixed constitutional question for independent federal habeas review. The governing §2254(d) factual presumption does not attach to that ultimate custody conclusion; historical events and supported credibility findings remain subject to the statute's conditions and exceptions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct votes. Thomas dissents.

**Current-force modification:** The original statutory factual-presumption and independent-legal-review analysis remains controlling for proceedings governed by former §2254. AEDPA §104 replaces that scheme: new §2254(e)(1) presumes a state factual determination correct and places on the applicant the burden of rebuttal by clear and convincing evidence. New §2254(d) separately forbids granting relief on a claim adjudicated on the merits in state court unless the decision was contrary to or unreasonably applied clearly established federal law determined by this Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the state-court evidence. These new conditions are not the former eight exceptions; enactment supplies no judicial construction of their terms and does not reclassify the underlying constitutional question as a historical fact. The April 24 source admission does not decide this amendment's temporal application to a particular pending proceeding; the original adjudication is unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The historical-fact presumption requires a competent state court's factual determination after a merits hearing involving the applicant and State or its officer or agent, with a written finding, opinion or other reliable adequate written indicia. It yields when the applicant establishes, it otherwise appears or the respondent admits: unresolved factual-dispute merits; an inadequate full-and-fair factfinding procedure; inadequate development of material facts; lack of subject-matter or applicant jurisdiction; unconstitutional denial of appointed counsel to an indigent; absence of a full, fair and adequate hearing; or other denial of due process. The eighth exception requires production of the pertinent state record as the statute provides and lack of fair support for the finding in the record as a whole. At a federal evidentiary hearing, after due proof of the state determination, the applicant must prove error by convincing evidence unless one of the first seven circumstances is shown, appears or is admitted, or the federal court finds the eighth. No exception is established here; a paper ruling alone establishes none.

**Material application:** The federal courts incorrectly presumed the ultimate noncustody conclusion correct. Voluntary arrival, accusations, duration, setting, communicated freedom to leave and actual departure require consideration together; undisclosed intentions or unsupported later-surveillance allegations do not become communicated custody circumstances.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand, 8–1, for independent ultimate custody review. No finding of custody, suppression, harmlessness, coercion, retroactivity, entitlement to a writ or need for a new evidentiary hearing follows; no abandoned default revives.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Miller v. Fenton's functional law/fact inquiry applies; Berkemer and Stansbury preserve the objective arrest-equivalence standard. Patton and Witt retain their distinct factual classifications and do not make every legal-standard inference factual. Wright supplies no general reasonable-state-decision deference rule. Withrow permits Miranda habeas review subject to ordinary limits and separate presentation of coercion claims.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Antiterrorism and Effective Death Penalty Act of 1996, Pub. L. 104-132, Title I, enacted April 24, 1996: §104, the former §2254(d) custody-review setting and new §2254(d), (e)(1). This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

#### Johnson v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-203.
**Decided:** 1997-05-12.

##### The jury-element violation is reviewed as forfeited error

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the §1623 perjury offense and must be determined by the jury beyond a reasonable doubt; the judge's determination instead was constitutional error. Johnson's failure to object presents forfeiture under Rule 52(b), not an established intentional waiver extinguishing that error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, unanimous in Parts I and IV.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gaudin supplies the jury obligation for actual materiality elements and Griffith applies it on pending direct review. Wells's different §1014 construction does not eliminate §1623 materiality; Olano separates forfeiture from intentional waiver.

##### Plainness is measured at appellate consideration in this intervening-law setting

**Holding:** When the law at trial settled an issue against the defendant but controlling law changes before direct review ends, the error is plain if it is clear at appellate consideration. This resolves that intervening-law setting without deciding every case in which the law was unsettled at trial or dispensing with Rule 52(b)'s other requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Scalia assumes plainness without joining.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano's reservation about error first clarified on appeal is resolved for settled contrary trial law changed before direct review ends, without resolving initially unsettled law.

##### The independent discretionary condition does not warrant correction here

**Holding:** Even assuming an effect on substantial rights, this forfeited materiality error does not warrant correction under Rule 52(b) because, in the established circumstances, it did not seriously affect the fairness, integrity or public reputation of the proceedings. That conclusion rests on the clear materiality of the source-of-funds account in the particular investigation and the intervening change from settled trial law; it is neither a general overwhelming-evidence exception nor a substitute jury verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, unanimous in Parts I and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial-rights impact is assumed; structural classification and ordinary third-step prejudice remain open. There is no innocence requirement or need for a second institutional wrong, no substitute element finding, and no universal overwhelming-evidence exception. Disputed materiality, less decisive proof or trial-strategy effects remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the conviction-sustaining judgment, 9–0, without a new trial, acquittal or sentence calculation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano's fourth condition independently governs correction after forfeiture. Gaudin's classification reservation, Sullivan's vitiated-reasonable-doubt rule and Roy's distinct preserved personal-intent habeas inquiry remain intact.

#### Almendarez-Torres v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6839.

**Decided:** 1998-03-24.

##### Section 1326(b)(2) requires an aggravated-offense allegation before its higher maximum may apply

**Holding:** The prior aggravated-felony conviction specified in §1326(b)(2) is an element of the aggravated unlawful-reentry offense. It must be alleged in the federal indictment and, unless validly admitted as an element of the charged offense, proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt; an admission to the fact of a prior conviction does not cure its omission from this indictment, so the existing conviction permits no sentence above §1326(a)'s two-year maximum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. These five expressly adopt the statutory rule, its ordinary element consequences and the remedy. Four explain the construction through constitutional avoidance; Stone-Zsela gives a definitive constitutional ground separately. No majority adopts a general constitutional rule governing all facts that increase a statutory maximum.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is statutory. No majority decides a general constitutional rule for maximum-increasing facts or a constitutional prior-conviction exception. The admitted convictions remain valid; neither subsidiary proof questions nor facts that leave the maximum unchanged are decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The enhanced sentence is vacated and the judgment reversed and remanded for sentencing within the two-year maximum on this indictment and plea. The plea is not withdrawn and no acquittal follows. The availability of a properly charged further proceeding is reserved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hamling v. United States:** Applied to require an indictment alleging each element of the aggravated offense.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applied after statutory classification; its jury-and-proof rule for actual elements remains controlling without deciding all sentencing classifications.
- **McMillan v. Pennsylvania:** Distinguished because its mandatory minimum left the statutory maximum unchanged; its limited holding remains intact.
- **United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra:** Preserved for ordinary sentencing within punishment authorized by the conviction; it does not resolve this higher-maximum classification.
- **Custis v. United States:** Distinguished as a collateral-review decision; the existing limits on attacking prior convictions remain intact.
- **Old Chief v. United States:** Applied to the distinction between element status and the form of adequate prior-conviction proof; no broader compulsory-stipulation rule is created.

#### Jones v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-6203.
**Decided:** 1999-03-24.

##### Serious bodily injury and death define aggravated carjacking offenses

**Holding:** The version of 18 U.S.C. § 2119 governing the December 1992 offense creates a basic carjacking offense and aggravated offenses when serious bodily injury or death results. Serious bodily injury is an element of the grade carrying a twenty-five-year maximum, and death is an element of the grade carrying a maximum of life imprisonment; the basic offense carries a fifteen-year maximum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six Justices). Separate constitutional theories do not supply an additional Court holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Almendarez-Torres is applied as a statutory element-and-charging analogue, not as a general constitutional maximum-fact holding. Gaudin supplies the significance of identifying an actual element. Monge's bounded double-jeopardy classification is preserved, including its reservation of independent charging and jury questions. Holloway's attention to enacted offense conditions is consistent with this reading, but its construction of later statutory language is not imported into the earlier offense.

##### The existing indictment and verdict authorize only the basic carjacking range

**Holding:** The aggravated injury or death element must be alleged in the federal indictment; absent a valid admission as an element of the charged offense or a valid waiver of jury determination, the Government must submit it to the jury and prove it beyond a reasonable doubt. An ordinary factual admission does not itself cure an indictment that omits the aggravated element. Because injury was neither charged nor established by Jones's verdict, the judge's later injury finding cannot authorize the aggravated range; the aggravated sentence must be vacated, and sentencing on the existing carjacking indictment and verdict may not exceed the fifteen-year basic maximum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six Justices). Separate constitutional theories do not supply an additional Court holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hamling and Gaudin are applied to the aggravated statutory elements. Almendarez-Torres supplies the distinction between admitting a fact and properly charging the aggravated offense. Witte and Watts retain their ordinary within-authority sentencing operation and provide no basis for this increased statutory range. Monge supplies no independent remedy here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court's ground is statutory construction and the protections of the resulting elements. It does not decide a general constitutional rule for all maximum-increasing facts, bare prior-conviction status, mandatory minima within an unchanged maximum, every Guidelines classification or capital sentencing. McMillan's unchanged-maximum holding and Walton's capital rule are not overruled. The death grade is part of the statute's construction, not a finding that Jones caused death.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The sufficient ground is construction of the original carjacking statute and ordinary protections of its aggravated elements. No general constitutional maximum-fact rule, prior-conviction exception, unchanged-maximum minimum rule, universal Guidelines classification or capital-sentencing rule is decided; McMillan and Walton are not overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the aggravated sentence and remand. Sentencing on the existing indictment and verdict may not exceed the fifteen-year basic maximum. No particular sentence, aggregate calculation, acquittal or authorization of a new aggravated prosecution is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Almendarez-Torres supplies the statutory aggravated-element and charging analogue; its result remains distinct from a universal constitutional rule. Hamling requires a federal indictment stating the offense's elements, and Gaudin requires the jury's determination beyond a reasonable doubt. Monge retains its noncapital serious-prior double-jeopardy classification and its express reservation of independent charging and jury claims; no renewed-proof remedy is drawn from it here. Witte and Watts retain their within-authority sentencing permission and do not authorize punishment beyond the charged conviction's statutory range. Holloway concerns later statutory language and does not amend the law governing this offense. McMillan and Walton are not overruled, and Custis's limits on collateral attack remain undisturbed.

#### Jones v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-9361; federal capital sentencing.
**Decided:** 1999-06-21.

##### Actual sentencing deadlock requires a lawful nondeath sentence

**Holding:** When completed penalty deliberations end without a unanimous recommendation, §3594's otherwise clause requires the judge to impose a lawful nondeath sentence. For this death-resulting kidnapping the alternative is life without release. Section 3593(b)(2)(C) does not authorize another attempt to obtain death solely because the jury deadlocked.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I-A has eight direct votes from all Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition without joining that rationale.

##### There is no universal deadlock script, but a false sentencing choice must be corrected here

**Holding:** Neither the Eighth Amendment nor a supervisory rule requires a fixed deadlock-consequence instruction in every capital case. In this case, however, the preserved request for an accurate account of the lawful consequence required correction of the charge's materially misleading below-life alternative; refusing that correction requires a new penalty proceeding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-B's rejection of a universal script is unanimous. Part II's case-specific corrective duty and remedy are directly adopted by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The Court does not adopt every constitutional or plain-error reason stated in the separate concurrence.

##### Independent aggravator validity and cure are not resolved for the Court

**Limits and questions not reached:** The instruction disposition removes this death sentence. No majority resolves whether factors 3(B) and 3(C) were independently invalid or harmless: O’Connor, Kennedy and Thomas find contextual validity; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg find the specific formulations defective; Scalia, Stone-Zsela and Breyer reserve validity. O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would find narrowly assumed drafting error harmless; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg reject that cure, while Stone-Zsela and Breyer reserve it.

**Authority of the divided positions:** These accounts identify the limits of the Court's decision, not additional factor holdings. No Marks synthesis supplies a validity or cure rule from the divided positions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Simmons v. South Carolina: preserves the effective opportunity to understand the only lawful nondeath alternative in its stated setting; supplies no universal deadlock script.
- Shannon v. United States: distinguishes an ordinary nonsentencing guilt jury; its allocation rule does not excuse an affirmatively misleading capital choice.
- Olano and Johnson: retain separate error, plainness, substantial-rights and discretionary conditions. The four-Justice concurrence's alternative application changes no Court-wide rule and does not decide Johnson's reserved initially unsettled-law setting.
- Boyde, Mills and McKoy: preserve contextual examination of the charge and individual mitigation; no verdict-form typography alone decides the case.
- Tuilaepa/Proctor and Payne: retain understandable relevant individualized selection and permissible impact evidence within their limits; no factor-validity majority arises here.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa, Clemons and Chapman: preserve the distinction between valid eligibility, authorized reweighing and actual harmless-error correction. No new general cure or appellate-resentencing authority is adopted.
- Neder: retains its qualified actual-verdict inquiry; the divided factor applications supply no new universal capital harmlessness rule.
- Strickler: preserves distinct initial Brady materiality for guilt and selection; its burden and record do not decide this preserved instruction issue.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual federal sentencing deadlock invokes §3594's lawful nondeath command, with no replacement jury solely to seek death after that deadlock. There is no universal deadlock script. The specific misleading statutory choice in this charge, identified by the preserved corrective request, requires a new penalty proceeding. The conviction remains intact. The separate constitutional, plain-error, factor-validity and factor-cure applications retain only their stated voting support; existing capital-selection and review rules otherwise continue unchanged.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sentence vacated and remanded, 5–4. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, who would affirm.

If the Government pursues death, the new penalty proceeding must accurately state the lawful alternatives and employ lawful selection procedures. A lawful nondeath disposition remains available. The Court orders no release, automatic life sentence, new guilt trial or execution timetable. A new hearing after reversible sentencing error is distinct from convening another jury merely because the first deadlocked.

#### Neder v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1985.
**Decided:** 1999-06-10.

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

##### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Morissette v. United States:** Applies the borrowed-term principle to fraud's established materiality requirement; statutory silence alone does not discard that content.
- **Durland v. United States:** Preserves coverage of fraudulent future promises while distinguishing that breadth from elimination of materiality or importation of every completed-tort requirement.
- **Kungys v. United States:** Uses its natural-tendency or capacity formulation for the financial deceptions presented here; its civil allocation and burden do not govern the criminal jury question.
- **United States v. Wells:** Preserves the different §1014 construction without an added objective-materiality element; it neither removes materiality from fraud statutes nor automatically cures their instructional error through a purpose-to-influence finding.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury determination of each actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt; harmlessness review does not authorize withdrawal of that element at trial.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applies the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to establish that the preserved error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **California v. Roy:** Extends its qualified actual-verdict method to the materiality errors presented here; necessary equivalence is sufficient but not exclusive, and its instructional-focus limitation remains essential.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies attention to the actual charge, findings, and evidence considered under the instructions; proof sufficient for a different verdict cannot replace contribution analysis.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes removal of a particular element from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings; its structural-error rule remains intact.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Distinguishes its discretionary plain-error disposition after forfeiture; it neither supplies a waiver here nor reduces Chapman's burden.
- **Jones v. United States:** Preserves its protection for charged statutory elements without treating it as a classification or harmlessness decision for this error.
- **Mitchell v. United States:** Applies its distinction between an error's actual contribution and sufficient other evidence without transferring its separate privilege holding.
- **Richardson v. United States:** Leaves intact its reservation of availability and application of harmless-error review for the distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Effective June 10, 1999, materiality must be submitted for the charged mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud schemes; §7206(1)'s express materiality element and §1014's different Wells rule retain their respective force. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. The tax verdicts meet the standard on their specific charge and record; the affected fraud verdicts require initial application below.

The six-Justice tax ground is noncontribution to the actual verdict, not a rule that the jury necessarily decided every omitted materiality question. Stevens's supplementary view and Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive equivalent-findings method do not control. The Chief accepts the governing broader method but disagrees with its application and the further fraud inquiry. The decision establishes no universal omitted-element classification, generic tax shortcut, new no-contest waiver, or automatic effect on a differently worded offense. Richardson's separate harmlessness reservation remains open.

**Operative remedy or transition:**

A conviction failing the Government's governing showing must be set aside; retrial under correct instructions remains permissible. No insufficiency acquittal, automatic reinstatement of every conviction, release, restitution amount, or sentence calculation is ordered. Separate §1014, conspiracy, and racketeering claims remain subject to their own availability, actual dependencies, and proper proceedings; acquittals are not reopened.

#### Withrow v. Williams, 507 U.S. 680 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1030.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### A fair state opportunity does not itself bar Miranda habeas review

**Holding:** Powell’s prudential restriction on Fourth Amendment exclusionary claims does not extend to a state prisoner’s claim that the conviction rests on statements obtained in violation of Miranda. A full and fair state opportunity therefore does not itself foreclose that claim; ordinary habeas requirements, merits analysis and the governing harmless-error inquiry remain necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, I–II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Powell, 428 U.S. 465 — distinguished and not extended; its actual prudential scope remains.
- Miranda, 384 U.S. 436 — preserved on habeas for a conviction resting on improperly obtained statements.
- Kimmelman, 477 U.S. 365; Jackson, 443 U.S. 307; Rose v. Mitchell, 443 U.S. 545 — applied as limits on extending Powell to distinct trial protections.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104, and Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: independent voluntariness review remains available on its own predicates.

##### An unpleaded, unconsented coercion theory requires fair litigation

**Holding:** A habeas court may not grant relief on the independent postwarning involuntariness theory presented here when it was neither pleaded nor tried by express or implied consent and the State lacked an opportunity to present relevant evidence. Litigation of Miranda custody and warning issues does not by itself supply consent; any later presentation remains subject to ordinary pleading, exhaustion, retroactivity and other applicable requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Connelly, 479 U.S. 157 — preserved: actual police coercion is necessary to due-process involuntariness; absent warnings alone do not establish it.
- Miller v. Fenton, 474 U.S. 104 — preserved: voluntariness requires its own legal determination on the proper record.
- Teague, 489 U.S. 288 — reserved here; no new exception or merits resolution of the retroactivity objection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Miranda and due-process involuntariness are independent inquiries. Rule 15(b) consent is absent on this record; custody/warning litigation alone does not provide it. Coercion, exhaustion and Teague questions remain for any lawfully presented claim. No automatic writ, universal prophylactic-safeguard review rule or reopening of Powell-barred search claims follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm availability of Miranda review; reverse the independent postwarning voluntariness ruling for lack of pleading, consent and fair opportunity to litigate; remand for proper merits and remedial analysis.

#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For RFRA's State and local application and the prospective effect on earlier statutory applications, see City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074, decided 1997-06-25, First Amendment — Religious freedom and congressional enforcement.

##### I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right

**Holding:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. Its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury it seeks to prevent or remedy; the enforcement power does not permit Congress to replace the judicially enforceable content of the substantive right with a different constitutional standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress need not prove a violation in every jurisdiction, make formal findings, use a prescribed quantity or form of evidence, require individualized proof for every prohibited act, or enact geographic limits or sunsets. Such design features may inform substantive fit but are not universal conditions. Independent Fifteenth Amendment power is not further resolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** South Carolina v. Katzenbach and Katzenbach v. Morgan retain broad preventive enforcement tied to the constitutional injury, not authority to redefine the right. City of Rome remains intact in its Fifteenth Amendment setting.

## Standards and Tests selection: Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody

### Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody

**Governing question:** Which circumstances determine custody for Miranda warnings.

**Current rule:** Custody turns on the objective circumstances confronting the person questioned and whether a reasonable person in that position would understand freedom of movement to be restrained to the degree associated with formal arrest. An officer’s uncommunicated view of the person as suspect or witness does not enter the calculation. Beliefs conveyed by words or conduct may affect the objective setting to the extent they would affect that reasonable understanding. Communicating suspicion does not itself establish custody, and an officer’s private intent cannot substitute for the objective inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- Objective arrest-equivalent restraint and communicated beliefs: Stansbury v. California — Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned, 1994-04-26. The per curiam opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule and reversal with remand.

**Present operation effective:** Stansbury v. California, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Custody turns on the circumstances communicated to the person questioned.

## Standards and Tests selection: Clarifying a possible present request for interrogation counsel

### Clarifying a possible present request for interrogation counsel

**Governing question:** What police must do after a valid waiver when a suspect may be presently asking for counsel.

**Current rule:** After a valid waiver, a statement that a reasonable officer would understand in context as a possible present request for counsel requires a pause in substantive questioning and neutral clarification. Unexpressed wishes and unrelated references to a lawyer do not trigger that duty. While the request remains unresolved, substantive interrogation may not resume; clarification may not discourage counsel through pressure, promises or repeated persuasion. A clear request requires cessation under Edwards, and later answers cannot retroactively erase it. Further interrogation then requires counsel or the suspect’s own initiation followed by a valid waiver.

**Authority by component:**

- Possible-request trigger and lawful neutral clarification: Davis v. United States — I. A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification; II. The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful, 1994-06-24. Part I of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens and Ginsburg. Five Justices directly adopt the mandatory clarification rule, its trigger and its limits; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas decline this part. Part II of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this actual-clarification application and affirmance; the four Justices who reject a duty accept the permissibility of the clarification actually used.
- Clear invocation and prohibition on retrospective erosion: Davis v. United States — III. A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers, 1994-06-24. Part III of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine directly join this preservation and application of the clear-invocation rule.

**Present operation effective:** Davis v. United States, 1994-06-24.

**Open question:** Prewaiver statements, an independent military Fifth Amendment claim and §3501 are outside this holding.

**Holdings navigation:** A reasonable possible present request requires neutral clarification; The actual clarification and ensuing statements were lawful; A clear request requires cessation and cannot be undone by later answers.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent Miranda custody review under the former habeas statute

### Independent Miranda custody review under the former habeas statute

**Governing question:** Classification of ultimate Miranda custody and historical facts in proceedings governed by former §2254(d).

**Current rule:** Whether established interrogation circumstances would make a reasonable person understand freedom of movement as restrained to the degree associated with formal arrest is a mixed constitutional question requiring independent federal habeas review. Former §2254(d)’s factual presumption does not attach to that ultimate conclusion. Historical events and supported credibility findings retain statutory protection. A competent state court’s factual determination after a merits hearing involving the applicant and State or its officer or agent, evidenced by written findings, opinion or other reliable adequate written indicia, is presumed correct subject to the statutory exceptions. Those exceptions are an unresolved factual dispute; factfinding procedure lacking a full and fair hearing; inadequately developed material facts; absence of jurisdiction over subject matter or applicant; unconstitutional denial of counsel to an indigent applicant; denial of a full, fair and adequate hearing; other denial of due process; or a pertinent produced state record, as a whole, failing fairly to support the determination. At a federal evidentiary hearing, after due proof of the state determination, the applicant must establish error by convincing evidence unless one of the first seven circumstances is established, otherwise appears or is admitted, or the federal court finds the eighth. A ruling on papers does not itself establish an exception or require a fresh hearing.

**Authority by component:**

- Ultimate classification: Ultimate Miranda custody requires independent federal review; factual conditions and exceptions: The statutory protection of historical factfinding remains unchanged, 1995-11-29. O’Connor joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Thompson, 1995-11-29.

**Effective transition:** This entry states the former §2254(d) regime. AEDPA separately enacted §§2254(d) and (e) on April 24, 1996. Lindh preserves former law for the covered noncapital applications filed before April 24, 1996; see “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” and Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application (1997-06-23). Separately directed provisions remain distinct; Thompson does not displace a genuinely applicable new statutory restriction.

**Related current rules:** “Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody” supplies the substantive custody inquiry; “State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254” supplies the distinct amended text.

**Holdings navigation:** Thompson v. Keohane, 516 U.S. 99. Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application.

## Standards and Tests selection: Jury determination of criminal materiality

### Jury determination of criminal materiality

**Governing question:** Allocation of materiality when it is an element of the charged offense.

**Current rule:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to decide materiality beyond a reasonable doubt when materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense. The judge states the statutory meaning and legal standard; describing its application as a mixed question cannot remove an element from the jury. Preserved materiality withdrawals and omissions in the financial-deception and tax instructions addressed in Neder are trial errors subject to the Government’s Chapman burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient but not exclusive: supported whole-record inferences may establish noncontribution only after examination of actual instructions, evidence they directed to the jury, required findings and material record conflicts. Strong, uncontested or overwhelming evidence alone is no automatic substitute where the omission prevents a reliable connection to the actual verdict. Review is count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts.

**Authority by component:**

- The jury decides application of the materiality element: The jury decides application of the materiality element, 1995-06-19. Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

- Neder v. United States — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “III. Qualified review of the actual verdict”, 1999-06-10; Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Seven direct joins supply classification and qualified inference; Part V supplies the bounded count-specific remand requirement.

**Present operation effective:** Neder, June 10, 1999, for the identified materiality errors and qualified actual-verdict harmlessness; Gaudin’s jury-allocation rule remains effective June 19, 1995.

**Open question:** No universal omitted-element or constructive-amendment classification, no generic tax harmlessness shortcut and no no-contest waiver follows. Sullivan’s vitiated reasonable-doubt rule remains structural; Richardson’s distinct predicate-nonunanimity harmlessness reservation remains unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: Aggravated unlawful-reentry elements and authorized maximum

### Aggravated unlawful-reentry elements and authorized maximum

**Governing question:** When does 8 U.S.C. §1326(b)(2) authorize its higher unlawful-reentry maximum?

**Current rule:** The prior aggravated-felony conviction is an element of the aggravated offense and must appear in the federal indictment. **Authority by component:**

## Standards and Tests selection: Renewed proof of noncapital recidivist status

### Renewed proof of noncapital recidivist status

**Governing question:** Whether evidentiary insufficiency bars a second effort to prove a noncapital recidivist sentencing allegation.

**Current rule:** Double Jeopardy does not bar renewed proof of the serious character of a prior conviction in the noncapital recidivist-status determination addressed in Monge. The personal-conduct qualification classifies the prior conviction for punishment of the present offense rather than adjudicating a new offense or a capital acquittal-like determination. Trial safeguards and an increased punishment ceiling alone do not make that determination an offense acquittal.

**Authority by component:**

- Parts I–II: bounded status classification and lawful renewed proof: Monge v. California, No. 97-6146. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** The first proof is not thereby declared sufficient or excused; renewed proceedings must satisfy applicable law. No general rule for every sentencing fact, capital determination or separate offense follows.
