## Standards and Tests selection: Voluntary public employment and substantive workplace safety duties

### Voluntary public employment and substantive workplace safety duties

**Governing question:** Whether a voluntary public-employment relationship creates a general substantive-due-process duty to provide workplace safety.

**Current rule:** Voluntary municipal employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care, and substantive due process imposes no independent general obligation to provide such employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks. A state-law safety duty, even assuming it creates a protected liberty interest, does not convert its breach alone into a substantive constitutional wrong. The underlying federal right and constitutional arbitrariness remain distinct from state-law entitlement and municipal attribution.

**Authority by component:**

- No employment-based general safety duty or custody: Collins v. City of Harker Heights — Voluntary public employment creates no general constitutional safety duty, February 26, 1992; Part B of Stevens's unanimous opinion.
- State entitlement does not alone establish arbitrary deprivation: Collins — These pleaded omissions do not establish constitutional arbitrariness, February 26, 1992; Part C joined by White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas with Stevens. Stone's distinct concurrence is not part of this component's authority.

**Present operation effective:** *Collins*, February 26, 1992.

**Open question:** Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, procedural due process, and a properly alleged affirmative-danger claim are not resolved. *Collins* creates neither a general affirmative-danger test nor a categorical rejection of one.

**Holdings navigation:** Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992) — Municipal liability and public employment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Police restraint and involuntary transfer into known danger

### Police restraint and involuntary transfer into known danger

**Governing question:** When an officer’s own restraint and forced transfer create a due-process duty to respond to danger.

**Current rule:** An officer who actually restrains a person’s ability to protect herself and personally directs or carries out her involuntary transfer into a serious danger the officer actually appreciates may not deliberately and unreasonably disregard that danger when the officer has authority and a real opportunity to respond. Injury must be attributable to that defendant’s conduct.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 1: direct-restraint due-process rule: City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-01.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-01.

**Exceptions and limits:** Negligence, constructive or pooled knowledge, genuinely voluntary departure, and a reasonable protective response that fails are insufficient. This is no general duty to protect everyone from private danger.

**Holdings navigation:** City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence — Knowing dangerous restraint and arbitrary selective enforcement.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. ### Affirmative danger and conduct-time qualified immunity

#### Pinder v. Johnson

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-2125; no Supreme Court docket stated in the Record
**Decided:** May 21, 1996

##### Do protective assurances or custody of a private assailant establish a custodial duty to protect the threatened family?

**Holding:** Official knowledge of danger, promises of protection, and custody of the private assailant do not establish a custodial special relationship with a mother or her children. An alleged affirmative deprivation of otherwise available private protection remains analytically distinct from an omission of public protection; absence of custody of the threatened persons does not itself resolve that different claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale. Part I's separate appellate holding appears under Federal Courts, Qualified-immunity appeals and presentation.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a protection duty founded merely on official knowledge and assurances, but its discussion of increased vulnerability does not fully resolve this alleged affirmative course of conduct. The substantive-due-process inquiry concerns the official's own deprivation and the culpability attached to that deprivation, not simply the making of a statement. Daniels excludes mere negligence; the Court does not decide its reserved questions about recklessness or gross negligence. Pinder alleged that Johnson's assurance induced her to return to work rather than provide or arrange private protection. That favorable account must remain distinct from a general right to police protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court establishes no general constitutional duty to detain an assailant, warn a victim, or fulfill every protective promise. It does not hold that custody is indispensable to every affirmative-danger claim, or decide the constitutional merits of the assumed affirmative-deprivation theory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- DeShaney rejects knowledge-and-promise protection duties without deciding this entire affirmative-increased-vulnerability theory.
- Daniels excludes merely negligent deprivation while leaving its reserved culpability questions unresolved here.

##### Was Johnson's alleged conduct sufficiently established as unconstitutional by March 10, 1989 to defeat qualified immunity?

**Holding:** Qualified immunity turns on whether all relevant law existing when the officer acted made the particular conduct's unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable officer, without demanding a factual twin. On the favorable allegations here, law existing March 10, 1989 did not sufficiently establish that this noncoercive undertaking, release of the assailant, and uncorrected exposure constituted Johnson's own substantive-due-process deprivation; he therefore receives individual immunity on the reviewed claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II's notice standard joined by all nine Justices; Part III's application and affirmance joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, a five-Justice majority. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from the immunity disposition; their substantive theory is not controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not identical prior facts, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant law. DeShaney had rejected an omission-based duty while leaving the distinct affirmative theory incomplete. Bowers discussed active official wrongdoing but rejected release-based liability; Archie discussed obstruction of private aid in a materially different culpability setting and reached an adverse outcome; Jensen's discussion of a distinct danger did not preserve a knowledge-and-promise special relationship after DeShaney. Those sources did not sufficiently identify the assumed noncoercive interference here as the officer's own deprivation. A judge's disagreement, the number of judges on each side, or an officer's subjective belief in legality does not independently establish immunity. Later cases cannot retroactively supply the missing conduct-time notice.

**Material application:** The Court assumes that the assurance caused the mother to return to work and forgo alternative protection and that the remaining favorable allegations are true. It does not award immunity because actual reliance was absent, choose disputed facts against Pinder, or reject constitutional liability on the merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is notice-specific and claim-specific. Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets do not supply retroactive notice; Love establishes no mandatory merits-first sequence. Neither broader affirmative-danger liability nor the ultimate culpability and causation merits is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's reviewed individual-immunity judgment for Johnson is affirmed, 5-4. No new constitutional merits judgment or damages award is entered. Official-capacity and municipal claims, equal protection, section 1985, state wrongful-death and survival claims, and other excluded matters remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Anderson supplies the objective, particularized notice inquiry without an exact-facts requirement.
- Elder requires review of all relevant contemporaneous authority rather than an isolated citation or subjective belief.
- DeShaney supplies the rejection of a general protective duty but does not itself resolve the assumed affirmative-deprivation merits.
- Bowers, Archie and Jensen are considered as conduct-time circuit-law notice sources; their distinct facts, outcomes and culpability limits are preserved, and they are not converted into Supreme Court holdings.
- Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets cannot establish notice for conduct preceding those decisions; Love does not impose mandatory sequencing.

#### Uhlrig v. Harder

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 94-3190.
**Decided:** February 18, 1997.

##### Conduct-time law did not sufficiently identify these administrators' acts as their own constitutional deprivation

**Holding:** Individual qualified immunity applies unless all relevant law existing when each official acted made the particular conduct's federal unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable official; no identical prior case is required. Even accepting the estate's supported favorable knowledge and causal account, law at these administrators' 1991 acts, no later than February 23, 1992, did not sufficiently establish that the challenged hospital restructuring, followed by clinical placement and the reported warnings and training, constituted each administrator's own substantive-due-process deprivation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight votes. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different, noncontrolling ground.

**Material application:** The favorable summary-judgment account includes knowing closure of a protective unit despite serious risk to an identified staff group. It does not merge each administrator's conduct with clinical placement decisions; warnings and professional procedures bear on the legal analogy, not findings of reasonable care, no awareness or no causation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The affirmative-danger merits, culpability standard and Tenth Circuit five-part test remain open. Neither circuit disagreement nor the absence of identical facts alone establishes immunity. Collins, Elder and Pinder cannot supply notice before their dates. Official-capacity, survivor-personal and state-tort claims are outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Unanimous affirmance ends the reviewed individual-capacity damages claim without further factfinding or trial; only the eight-Justice immunity rationale controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow and Anderson supply objective, particularized notice without requiring a factual twin. Elder requires all contemporaneous authority and no mandatory merits-first sequence. Pinder separates affirmative deprivation from nonprotection but does not dictate the result for different conduct dates. Collins, DeShaney and Daniels retain their bounded workplace, protection-duty and negligence holdings; the considered circuit danger decisions do not become Supreme Court rules.

### Knowing dangerous restraint and arbitrary selective enforcement

#### City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence

**Docket or dockets:** Sixth Circuit Nos. 96-5993 and 96-5996; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** 1998-06-01.

##### 1. Knowing, involuntary transfer into danger states a bounded due-process claim

**Holding:** An officer who actually restrains a person's ability to protect herself and personally directs or carries out her involuntary transfer into a serious danger the officer actually appreciates may not deliberately and unreasonably disregard that danger when the officer has authority and a real opportunity to respond. The claim requires injury attributable to that defendant's conduct; negligence, constructive knowledge, another officer's knowledge, assistance in a genuinely voluntary departure or a reasonable protective response that fails does not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion for all nine Justices controls this direct-restraint application. It adopts no broader free-standing conscience framework or general prison-to-roadside rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The favorable knowing-coercion account remains disputed. Each officer’s knowledge, authority, opportunity, causal acts and reasonable response require individual proof; obviousness permits but does not compel awareness. No general protection, investigation or arrest duty, broad conscience framework or noncustodial danger rule is adopted.

##### 2. The favorable knowing-coercion account cannot be dismissed categorically on qualified-immunity grounds

**Holding:** Qualified immunity does not categorically bar Black's claim on the favorable allegation that an officer knowingly used actual police restraint to compel her transfer into an appreciated serious danger. The relevant clarity is that of February 18–19, 1994, assessed for each officer's own information, authority and acts; the ruling leaves supported factual and legal defenses available at their proper stage.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls this separate conduct-time immunity holding; it neither finds every defendant liable nor substitutes a new defense for Harlow and Anderson.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The law must make each officer’s own conduct unlawful at the February 18–19, 1994 notice cutoff. An exact factual twin is unnecessary, but later Elder, Farmer, Pinder, Doe, Lanier, Lewis and DeCarlo cannot create notice. Facts and supported defenses remain open at their proper stage, with no mandatory merits-first sequence.

##### 3. Probable cause does not defeat the pleaded arbitrary-selection claim

**Holding:** Probable cause to arrest does not defeat an equal-protection claim that officers deliberately selected an otherwise chargeable driver solely to impose arbitrary group hostility while deliberately leaving an actually comparable driver unenforced on that basis. The claimant must establish discriminatory purpose and effect, relevant comparison, each defendant's participation and attributable injury; a supported legitimate enforcement distinction defeats the asserted arbitrary comparison.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls this narrow selective-enforcement rule, without an orientation-based scrutiny tier.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Perceived orientation supplies the alleged arbitrary selection; no heightened scrutiny tier is adopted. Probable cause, epithets, nonarrest, later acquittal or another person’s characterization alone is insufficient. No duty to arrest every offender, prosecutor-discovery threshold, universal burden sequence or resolution of every personal-animosity claim follows.

##### 4. The narrow arbitrary-selection prohibition predates this encounter

**Holding:** The existing prohibition against purposeful arbitrary group-based enforcement permits Stemler's narrow claim to proceed despite qualified immunity, subject to proof of the actual comparator, arbitrary purpose, effect and each officer's conduct. The Court relies on law existing before February 18–19, 1994, not later recognition of a new protected classification or retroactive notice from Romer or Whren.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls this distinct immunity application and its proof limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Yick Wo, Oyler, Wayte and Moreno supply the pre-event prohibition; Romer and Whren do not create retrospective notice. Individual comparison, purpose, effect and responsibility remain to be proved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both individual reinstatements are affirmed unanimously for ordinary further proceedings, not final liability or guaranteed trial. Black’s and Stemler’s distinct claims and defendants remain separate. Favorable municipal and official-capacity judgments and the other excluded claims are undisturbed. Existing state judgments retain their proper effect; no later reversal or automatic revival is assumed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County:** Applied to actual restraint rather than a general failure to protect; noncustodial state-created danger remains unaddressed.
- **Youngberg v. Romeo:** Applied for the protection principle arising from actual custody, without mechanically transferring every institutional standard.
- **Daniels v. Williams:** Applied to exclude negligence; the separate actual-awareness requirement is preserved by the custody authorities and this direct-restraint application.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights, February 26, 1992:** Its distinction between voluntary exposure and actual compulsion is applied; its separate municipal-attribution inquiry remains intact.
- **Pinder v. Johnson, May 21, 1996:** Distinguished because assurances and a noncoercive undertaking did not restrain the claimant there; its immunity result and reservation of distinct affirmative deprivation remain.
- **Farmer v. Brennan, June 6, 1994:** Its actual-awareness, permissible-inference and reasonable-response limits inform the present analysis; it supplies no earlier notice or wholesale roadside Eighth Amendment framework.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District, October 16, 1995:** Its personal knowledge, authority, opportunity and causal-role limits inform the analysis without becoming a mechanically transplanted checklist.
- **County of Sacramento v. Lewis, May 26, 1998:** Distinguished from alleged knowing roadside restraint and transfer; its rapid-pursuit harmful-purpose requirement is not extended to every vehicle-related injury.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald:** Applied to preserve personal qualified immunity and the conduct-time inquiry.
- **Anderson v. Creighton:** Applied to require particularized apparent unlawfulness without an identical factual precedent.
- **Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994:** Its all-relevant-law method is preserved; its date postdates the encounter and supplies no earlier notice.
- **United States v. Lanier, March 31, 1997:** Its rejection of a factual-twin prerequisite informs present review without retrospective notice or substitution of an unpresented Fourth Amendment claim.
- **DeCarlo v. Fry, May 28, 1998:** Applied to preserve personal role, actual information and the proper procedural stage; its administrative immunity result does not decide this coercive-transfer claim.
- **Meador v. Cabinet for Human Resources:** Its pre-event custodial deliberate-indifference application supports notice at the bounded restraint level.
- **Heflin v. Stewart County:** Its pre-event custodial protection rule supports the same particularized notice inquiry.
- **Walton v. Southfield:** Distinguished because the absence of police custody mattered to its immunity result.
- **Nishiyama v. Dickson County:** Considered for intentional unreasonable exposure to known danger; its gross-negligence language and disputed noncustodial reach are not adopted.
- **Yick Wo v. Hopkins:** Applied to arbitrary discriminatory enforcement despite formally lawful authority.
- **Oyler v. Boles:** Applied while preserving discretion, the insufficiency of mere selectivity and the need for an unjustifiable discriminatory basis.
- **Wayte v. United States:** Applied to require discriminatory purpose and effect.
- **United States Department of Agriculture v. Moreno:** Applied to exclude bare group hostility as a legitimate enforcement purpose.
- **Bowers v. Hardwick:** Distinguished as addressing a particular substantive conduct question, not authorization for arbitrary unrelated DUI enforcement.
- **Whren v. United States, June 10, 1996:** Applied to preserve independent equality despite objective probable cause; it supplies no 1994 notice.
- **Romer v. Evans, May 20, 1996:** Applied at ordinary review's bare-hostility boundary, without a new scrutiny tier or retrospective notice.
- **United States v. Armstrong, May 13, 1996:** Its prosecutor-discovery threshold is not extended to this police-enforcement merits claim.
- **Crawford-El v. Britton, June 16, 1997:** Ordinary proof and lawful case management remain; no heightened evidentiary hurdle or universal burden sequence is imposed.

### Substantive due process in rapid police pursuits

#### County of Sacramento v. Lewis

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1337.
**Decided:** 1998-05-26.

##### What culpability the supported rapid pursuit requires for substantive-due-process liability

**Holding:** In the rapidly evolving high-speed pursuit presented here, where officers must make instant choices amid competing enforcement and safety obligations, reckless or deliberately indifferent risk-taking alone does not establish a substantive-due-process violation. A purpose to cause harm unrelated to a legitimate law-enforcement objective is required; because this plaintiff-favorable record does not establish that purpose, the reviewed federal individual claim fails.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for the complete rule and application. Neither the two distinct constitutional votes nor Stevens's immunity vote supplies additional support for that rule. No combination of separate rationales is needed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual opportunity for deliberation matters. The decision establishes no universal intent requirement, seconds cutoff, automatic indifference whenever pursuit could end, permission to manufacture an emergency or broad noncustodial immunity. Purposeful collisions, terrorizing conduct, contrived encounters and materially more deliberative circumstances require their own analysis. The constitutional standard is grounded in precedent and the setting, not personal judicial reaction; no mandatory merits-first sequence is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** All nine reverse the reviewed individual ruling, but only six adopt the controlling constitutional rationale. The individual federal claim terminates; municipal judgments and unappealed state-law dispositions remain outside review. No damages, trial or new evidentiary hearing follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Rochin v. California, 342 U.S. 165:** Applied as the executive-abuse boundary, with the required culpability identified through the actual emergency setting.
- **Daniels v. Williams, 474 U.S. 327:** Its negligence exclusion remains; the Court resolves the previously open intermediate-culpability question only for this rapid pursuit.
- **Whitley v. Albers, 475 U.S. 312:** Its competing-obligation analysis informs the rule, but its prison-security holding is not treated as already deciding civilian pursuit liability.
- **Farmer v. Brennan, June 6, 1994:** Distinguished by the custodial duty and actual opportunity for response. Its awareness, reasonable-response and permissible-inference rules remain intact.
- **Doe v. Taylor Independent School District, October 16, 1995:** Distinguished because knowing supervisory acquiescence in deliberate abuse requires authority, opportunity and causal participation not supplied merely by continuing this pursuit.
- **Collins v. City of Harker Heights, February 26, 1992:** Its bounded workplace-omission holdings do not automatically resolve affirmative police conduct or merge the underlying right with municipal attribution.
- **Pinder v. Johnson, May 21, 1996:** Its distinction between nonprotection and affirmative deprivation remains; it did not decide this culpability question.
- **Uhlrig v. Harder, February 18, 1997:** Its conduct-time immunity disposition is preserved without importing the lower court's unadopted merits test or a mandatory merits-first sequence.
- **United States v. Lanier, March 31, 1997:** Its rejection of categorical immunity for noncustodial official wrongdoing remains. A governing specific constitutional provision must be used where it covers a claim; that principle does not itself dispose of every due-process claim.
- **Albright v. Oliver, January 24, 1994:** Its fractured judgment supplies no controlling general displacement rationale for this case.
- **Washington v. Glucksberg, June 26, 1997:** Its assistance-in-suicide holding remains limited; no exclusive most-specific-history methodology governing all liberties is established or displaced here.

### Municipal liability and public employment

#### Collins v. City of Harker Heights, 503 U.S. 115 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1279.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether §1983 adds a freestanding governmental-power-abuse element for municipal employees

**Holding:** A municipal employee who establishes deprivation of a federal right under color of state law and municipal responsibility need not also prove an independent, freestanding abuse-of-governmental-power element. The existence of the constitutional wrong and its attribution to municipal policy or custom remain separate required inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part A of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous statutory holding.

**Material application:** The Fifth Circuit’s additional statutory requirement cannot sustain dismissal merely because the city acted as an employer. Its rejection does not establish the underlying federal right or final municipal attribution; attribution is assumed only to resolve the constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Employment alone does not establish municipal liability, and deficient training does not create a substantive constitutional right.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The extra statutory element is rejected, but dismissal of the federal claim is affirmed on the separate constitutional grounds below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Monell v. Department of Social Services*, 436 U.S. 658, supplies municipal policy and causation requirements distinct from employment alone. *Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378, is clarified as an attribution doctrine, not a source of the underlying substantive right. *Oklahoma City v. Tuttle*, 471 U.S. 808, preserves the distinction between constitutional wrong and municipal responsibility.

##### Whether voluntary municipal employment or these pleaded safety omissions establish a substantive-due-process deprivation

**Holding:** The substantive Due Process Clause does not impose an independent general obligation on a municipality to provide its voluntary employees a workplace free from unreasonable risks of harm. Accepting public employment does not itself create the custody or deprivation of liberty that supports affirmative constitutional duties of care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part B of Stevens’s opinion, joined by the other eight Justices; unanimous duty limitation.

**Material application:** The complaint alleges known hazards and deliberate failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers. Those allegations are accepted at dismissal; they do not make voluntary employment a custodial restraint.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not excuse violation of other applicable federal rights. Actual compulsion, deliberately inflicted bodily injury, a different properly pleaded affirmative-danger claim and procedural due process are not decided. State safety and tort remedies are not adjudicated, and knowing allegations are not reduced to negligence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal of the federal substantive-due-process claim. No federal compensation or supervision order is entered; independently available state-law remedies retain their own merits and procedural requirements.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services*, 489 U.S. 189, supplies the absence of a general protective duty while leaving distinct custody and affirmative-deprivation questions. *Youngberg v. Romeo*, 457 U.S. 307, is distinguished because voluntary employment is not involuntary custody. *Daniels v. Williams*, 474 U.S. 327, preserves the distinction between constitutional due process and ordinary tort law without redescribing these allegations as negligence. *Bishop v. Wood*, 426 U.S. 341, rejects a general constitutional guarantee against employment mistakes.

**Independent alternative holding:** The pleaded municipal failures to train, warn and equip sewer workers against known hazards do not constitute arbitrary or conscience-shocking deprivation under substantive due process in this voluntary-employment setting. Even assuming the Texas safety statute creates the asserted duties and a protected liberty interest, breach alone does not establish that distinct substantive constitutional wrong.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part C of Stevens’s opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices control this alternative rejection. Stone’s agreement in the judgment does not join Part C.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** No deliberate bodily injury or direction knowingly sending Collins into a dangerous sewer is alleged. The public-program rationale recognizes competing social, political and economic choices as a legal presumption; it is not a finding that the city acted responsibly or that its budget justified these omissions. The alternative independently supports affirmance without deciding state remedies or a different affirmative-injury claim. *Rochin v. California*, 342 U.S. 165, is distinguished from these protective omissions. *Bishop*, 426 U.S. 341, and *Daniels*, 474 U.S. 327, preserve the distinction between state employment or tort duties and substantive constitutional arbitrariness. *Harrah Independent School District v. Martin*, 440 U.S. 194, distinguishes an assumed state-created interest from arbitrary deprivation.

### Qualified immunity

#### Hunter v. Bryant, 502 U.S. 224 (1991)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1440.
**Decided:** December 16, 1991.

##### Who decides qualified immunity from an arrest-without-probable-cause claim and what record governs at summary judgment

**Holding:** Qualified immunity from a claim of arrest without probable cause is a legal question for the judge under ordinary summary-judgment constraints. The court must determine whether a reasonable officer could believe the arrest lawful on the supported nonmovant version of the complete contemporaneous record; choosing the “more reasonable” interpretation is not the test, and a generalized security concern alone does not establish a reasonable belief that the offense's elements were satisfied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; these five Justices supply the controlling majority of the eight participants.

**Material application:** The agents could consider assassination references, a reported gesture, oral statements, the confusing interview, and refusal to elaborate. They also had the letter's repeated attribution of the supposed plot to an outside group, bearing on whether Bryant communicated a serious threat under §871 or reported an imagined danger. The Ninth Circuit's comparison of the relative reasonableness of interpretations and its general assignment of legal immunity to a jury were erroneous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds neither guilt, innocence, bad faith, nor a constitutional violation. It neither directs immunity nor orders an automatic trial. The separate warrant ruling remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment is vacated and the case remanded. The remand court must specify any supported dispute over material historical facts and decide immunity promptly under the correct standard.

**Material judgment:** Vacatur and remand are supported by Stone, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter, 6–2. White would direct immunity; Stevens would affirm, and Scalia would reverse and direct immunity. These differing remedial positions do not enlarge the five-Justice controlling rationale.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow, Anderson v. Creighton, and Malley supply protection for reasonable mistakes and early judicial resolution when supported facts permit it. Watts supplies the contextual distinction between a true threat and protected expression. Mitchell, Beck, and Liberty Lobby are applied and preserved. No qualified-immunity rule is revised.

#### Aponte Matos v. Toledo Dávila

**Docket or dockets:** First Circuit No. 97-1645; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-02-26.

##### A material fabrication dispute cannot be resolved by relying on the warrant it produced

**Holding:** An officer’s personally responsible knowing or reckless falsehood material to probable cause cannot obtain qualified immunity merely from the warrant that deception procured. Competent conflicting sworn accounts of the essential observation require ordinary factual resolution; negligence, a failed search or ulterior purpose alone does not establish this claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), joined by the seven other Associates, controls the complete knowing-or-reckless rule. Stone-Zsela also joins the deliberate-falsehood application, without the complete culpability and retained-immunity rationale.

**Material application:** Competent sworn accounts contradict the affiant’s claimed essential observation; another officer repeats the affiant’s account rather than supplying independent observation. The resulting credibility dispute cannot be resolved against the plaintiffs at summary judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Fabrication, culpability, materiality, personal responsibility, causation and injury must still be proved. An empty search does not establish a lie, and denial of summary judgment supplies neither liability nor an automatic entitlement to discovery or trial.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmance preserves reversal of summary judgment for Laboy on this claim and its ordinary factual remand.

##### Each defendant must be connected to the asserted constitutional wrong

**Holding:** Participation in a later search or supervisory rank alone does not establish responsibility for an affiant’s fabrication. Summary judgment stands where the supported record supplies no knowledge, assistance, agreement or affirmative causal link connecting the other officers or Puerto Rico supervisors to the alleged falsehood.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous on personal responsibility and this application.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The monthly warrant expectation and remote criticism of the affiant do not establish encouragement, acquiescence or deliberate indifference to this deception. Federal supervisor Ilario’s disposition rests instead on an untimely appeal.

##### The timely execution claims fail on their own record and conduct-date grounds

**Holding:** Qualified immunity on the timely search-scope, announcement and force components is sustained under the law and information relevant to December 6, 1993, without deciding that every challenged act was constitutional. Neither a Supreme-Court-only notice rule nor a physical-injury prerequisite governs.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), joined by the seven other Associates, applying the retained Harlow/Anderson defense. Stone-Zsela agrees in disposition without this complete rationale.

**Material application:** No supported search beyond places capable of concealing the weapons was identified; uncompelled questions about money alone did not establish clearly unlawful expansion. Announcement immunity rests on reasonable uncertainty in the pre-December 6, 1993 legal setting. The active weapons-search security setting, including the daughter’s attempted passage through the blockade, supports the force-immunity disposition; absence of physical contact alone does not.

**Limits and questions not reached:** All relevant existing law matters, and an obvious violation needs no factual twin. Later Wilson and Richards cannot establish retrospective notice. Ultimate constitutionality of the unannounced entry remains open; no general permission to threaten a person follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Franks and Malley are applied to personally responsible, culpable material affidavit falsehoods; judicial issuance is not categorical protection.
- Clanton supplies the same bounded material-deception rule and preserves corrected truthful probable cause and all liability predicates.

### Doe v. Rosenberg — 2000-04-24

[Record](../records/doe-v-rosenberg_merits_2000-04-24.md)

#### Judgment & Remedy

Affirmed, 9–0. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The federal summary judgment stands for both Rosenberg's referral and hospital-security transport and the hospital physicians' certification and confinement. The dismissal of pendent claims under section 1367(c)(3) remains without a decision of their merits. The ungranted discovery ruling is undisturbed. No damages, new confinement, release, mandatory discovery or hearing is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Kennedy delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight other Justices. There is no separate writing.

#### Holdings

#### Regulation and permission do not alone make this private confinement the State's act

**Holding and operative rule:** Private physicians' and a private hospital's independently exercised commitment decisions are not attributable to the State merely because a statute authorizes confinement, the institution is extensively regulated, and judicial review is available. Attribution requires a sufficient governmental connection to the particular challenged act, such as coercion, significant encouragement, joint participation or delegation of a traditionally exclusive governmental function; none is established for either identified decision here.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Justice Kennedy's opinion and both applications of this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Sullivan requires responsibility for the particular private choice, not treatment of an entire regulated system as governmental. Blum and Rendell-Baker likewise distinguish regulation from responsibility for the act; Lugar keeps legal authorization distinct from sufficient governmental attribution. The serious loss of liberty does not eliminate that separate element.

Rosenberg directed hospital security to transport Doe; the record identifies no state officer executing that choice. The hospital's private physicians separately certified confinement. The licensing and described contractual relationship establish no governmental command to confine this patient. Section 9.27(a) permits specified admission upon an application and separate certificates by two examining physicians; subsection (d) requires consideration of alternatives. Those safeguards limit permission rather than direct this particular choice. Section 9.01's definitions and the state courts' dangerousness construction are distinct; dangerousness is not inserted into subsection (a)'s express text or found proved here. Later judicial release does not retroactively make initial private decisions public ones.

The contrary public-function argument cannot be answered merely by calling confinement medical. But the supported history includes private confinement, and this record establishes neither an exclusively governmental undertaking nor joint public participation. West concerns a State's entrusted custodial duty, while Edmonson concerns actual governmental jury machinery. Their predicates are absent here and their rules remain available when proved.

The Court decides no general immunity for private hospitals, insignificance of transport, validity of Doe's confinement or underlying constitutional merits. An actual public command, joint decision, selective official enforcement or governmental duty or adjudicative delegation would require its own application. Unused restraints, other emergency-admission provisions and hypothetical contract terms supply no separate holding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Sullivan is applied to responsibility for the particular private decision; its payment setting is not treated as automatic resolution of confinement.
- Blum and Rendell-Baker are applied for the distinction between regulation and governmental responsibility for a challenged act.
- Lugar retains separate legal-authorization and governmental-attribution requirements; state-created permission alone does not satisfy both.
- West is distinguished because no entrusted governmental custodial duty is established here; professional discretion does not negate attribution when that duty is actually delegated.
- Edmonson is distinguished because this record lacks the governmental machinery and participation present in its jury-function setting.
- Carter retains deprivation, attribution, culpability and causation as separate requirements; its pleading decision supplies no new discovery entitlement in this summary-judgment case.

#### Law After Decision

This private referral, transport and commitment remain outside constitutional state-action attribution on the established record. The decision creates no blanket private-confinement exemption and changes no underlying dangerousness, constitutional-merits or state-tort rule. Proved governmental direction, joint action or exclusive-function delegation remains subject to its ordinary predicates.
