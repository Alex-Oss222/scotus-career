#### Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail; Rapone v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, 502 U.S. 367 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-954 and 90-1004.
**Decided:** January 15, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Rufo and Rapone are consolidated petitions presenting the same modification request; both judgments are vacated.

##### What change justifies modification of an institutional consent decree under Rule 60(b)(5)

**Holding:** Under Rule 60(b)(5), the party seeking to modify an institutional consent decree must prove a significant change in fact or law that makes continued enforcement inequitable; an unforeseeable catastrophe is not required. Actual anticipation and acceptance of the relevant risk impose a heavy burden to justify relief, and dissatisfaction, a new administration, or the availability of a cheaper constitutional alternative alone does not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five of eight participating Justices support the exact proposition. O'Connor concurs in the judgment without joining this rationale; Thomas did not participate.

**Current-force modification:** The Rule 60(b)(5) holding survives, but PLRA §802 supplies independent routes for federal-law civil prison-conditions relief, excluding habeas challenges to the fact or duration of confinement and expressly including prospective relief granted before enactment. Under §3626(b)(1), relief is terminable on a party's or intervener's motion two years after grant or approval, one year after denial of termination under that paragraph, or, for an order issued on or before enactment, two years after enactment. Section 3626(b)(2) entitles a defendant or intervener to immediate termination of prospective relief granted without findings that it is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the federal violation and the least intrusive necessary means. The original §3626(b)(3) prevents termination when record-based written findings establish that relief remains necessary to correct a current or ongoing federal violation, is no broader than necessary, and is narrowly drawn and least intrusive. This current-or-ongoing formulation is preserved as enacted. Under original §3626(e)(2), relief subject to a pending motion is automatically stayed from day 30 for a (b)(1) or (b)(2) motion, or day 180 for a motion under other law, until final ruling. Admission establishes no motion, missing finding, termination or elapsed stay period; relief entered by a state court solely on state-law claims is outside §3626's limitations. The former equitable rule cannot alone foreclose these independent statutory avenues.

**Material application:** Officials reaffirmed the single-occupancy obligation in 1985 after the original 1979 decree. The centrality of that term and acceptance of known risks matter, but the lower court must assess inequity without treating unforeseeability as an absolute condition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Population growth, expense, and the need for the proposed design are asserted grounds, not Supreme Court findings satisfying the burden. Modification under Rule 60(b)(5) concerns the continuing prospective operation of an existing institutional decree following a demonstrated significant change in fact or law. It does not establish a legislative power to compel reinstatement of a completed private damages or limitations judgment, nor does it decide whether a separate valid statutory remedy may provide collateral relief from such a judgment.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both First Circuit judgments are vacated and the cases remanded for the district court to determine whether proven changed circumstances make continued enforcement inequitable and whether the proposed modification responds suitably to those changes. The existing decree remains operative until lawfully modified.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Swift & Co.*, 286 U.S. 106 — its disposition is preserved; its demanding factual formulation is confined to its setting rather than imposed universally.
- *System Federation No. 91 v. Wright*, 364 U.S. 642 — applied to preserve equitable power to adjust continuing obligations to changed conditions.
- *United States v. United Shoe Machinery Corp.*, 391 U.S. 244 — applied to connect modification to the continuing decree's purpose and operation.
- *Board of Education v. Dowell*, 498 U.S. 237 — reinforces contextual treatment of institutional equitable relief; no school-desegregation termination rule is imported.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §802, independent §3626(b)(1)–(2) termination, (b)(3) limitation, (d) state-law exception, original (e)(2) stay and §802(b)(1) enactment transition. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

##### What fit and protections a justified consent-decree modification must preserve

**Holding:** A justified modification must be suitably tailored to the proven change and preserve lawful protection of the decree's remaining purposes; the court may not simply rewrite a consent decree to the constitutional minimum. Bell's clarification of constitutional limits does not by itself eliminate a voluntarily accepted single-cell obligation, and financial pressure cannot authorize unconstitutional confinement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; five of eight participating Justices support the exact proposition. O'Connor concurs in the judgment without joining this rationale; Thomas did not participate.

**Current-force modification:** Rufo's equitable tailoring rule remains controlling where its premise is otherwise lawful. For covered prison-conditions relief, PLRA §802 additionally requires findings before entry or approval of a consent decree that the prospective relief is narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the particular plaintiff's federal violation and the least intrusive necessary means, with substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting officials to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violating state or local law, additionally requires that federal law permit that contrary relief, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation and that no other remedy correct the violation. The original current-or-ongoing-violation limitation on statutory termination appears in the preceding question. A consensual obligation exceeding the constitutional minimum does not by itself defeat these statutory conditions. Private settlements not judicially enforceable beyond reinstatement of the settled proceeding remain distinct, and their breach may be pursued through available state-law remedies; relief entered by a state court solely under state law retains §3626(d)'s exception. No decree is dissolved by this source admission.

**Material application:** The district court may consider practical administration and cost in selecting lawful relief, while exercising its own equitable judgment; these considerations cannot replace proof of a qualifying change. Bell does not dissolve the officials' independently accepted single-cell promise.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court approves no double-cell design or number of cells and makes no determination that the proposed arrangement is constitutional or that remaining safeguards are adequate.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Local No. 93, International Association of Firefighters v. Cleveland*, 478 U.S. 501 — consent obligations may exceed what constitutional liability alone would require.
- *Bell v. Wolfish*, 441 U.S. 520 — its constitutional treatment of double-celling does not erase a consensual single-cell requirement.
- *System Federation No. 91 v. Wright*, 364 U.S. 642 — preserves adjustment for changed circumstances while maintaining judicial control of lawful relief.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §802, consent-decree limits, private-settlement distinction and surviving equitable rule. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the Establishment Clause grounds in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see First Amendment — Official school prayer.

##### Whether continued exposure at a forthcoming graduation preserves a live claim for prospective relief

**Holding:** A student who remains in the same public-school system and faces its challenged official prayer policy at her forthcoming high-school graduation has a live claim for prospective protection although her earlier graduation is over. Deborah Weisman's continued exposure permits review and preservation of the injunction without deciding her father's taxpayer standing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six Justices adopt the threshold proposition.

**Current force:** Remains controlling as stated for the student's continuing prospective exposure.

**Material application:** Deborah remains enrolled at Classical High School, and the stipulated record makes clergy-led prayer at her later graduation likely. Her relationship to that forthcoming policy application is concrete; formal optional attendance does not remove it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no taxpayer-standing rule and creates no general advisory-jurisdiction exception for completed school controversies that might recur.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Preserve review and the injunction against the challenged official practice. The injunction's substantive scope follows the First Amendment holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, supply the substantive school-prayer interest without supplying a new taxpayer-standing rule.

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Civil Rights — Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct; Civil Rights — The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim.

##### Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences

**Holding:** When Congress has prescribed a civil statute’s temporal reach, courts apply that direction subject to constitutional limits; absent sufficient direction, they ask whether applying the provision would impair rights possessed when a party acted, increase liability for past conduct, or impose new duties concerning completed transactions. If it would, the provision does not govern those events without clear congressional intent; a pending case or reference to earlier facts alone does not establish such an effect, and prospective relief, jurisdiction, procedure and collateral matters require examination of what the particular provision actually changes rather than an automatic reopening of completed acts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser Aluminum & Chemical Corp. v. Bonjorno, 494 U.S. 827 (1990): applied to make statutory temporal direction the starting inquiry.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204 (1988): applied to the presumption against retrospective legal burdens; no automatic civil constitutional prohibition is inferred.
- Bennett v. New Jersey, 470 U.S. 632 (1985): applied to protect substantive obligations fixed under earlier law.
- Bradley v. School Board of Richmond, 416 U.S. 696 (1974): retained in its collateral litigation-fee setting and reconciled with the new-consequence inquiry, not overruled.
- Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, 509 U.S. 86 (1993): distinguished as judicial retroactivity; its equal-application rule remains unchanged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

#### Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-780.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

For the other questions in Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette, decided June 29, 1995, No. 94-780. see First Amendment — Private religious displays in public forums (Ohio’s categorical Establishment Clause defense cannot sustain this exclusion).

##### The expired permit dispute remains reviewable

**Holding:** A dispute over a short seasonal permit remains live when its duration prevents full review and there is a reasonable expectation that the same claimant will again face the challenged restriction. These applicants meet both requirements; recurrence affecting other speakers alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone, Part I, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: nine votes for this threshold holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A broader prayer alone does not establish renewed exposure. Reviewability neither revives the expired permit nor extends its relief to every future application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Weinstein v. Bradford: applies both short duration and same-claimant recurrence; the exception does not extend to every recurring public issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit at the original scope of the Count One merits judgment: the December 21–24, 1993 permit period remains expired. No new permit, extension, sign condition, removal order, damages or remand is entered. Unappealed rally, damages and other matters remain outside review.

#### Plyler v. Moore

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 96-6884; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

For the Article III validity of the conditional termination rule, see Plyler v. Moore, Fourth Circuit No. 96-6884, decided 1997-06-23, Constitutional Structure — Congressional amendment of law governing prospective relief.

##### I. The original PLRA supplies an independent, conditional termination rule

**Holding:** For covered federal prison-conditions prospective relief, including relief entered before April 26, 1996, original §3626(b)(2) entitles a defendant or intervener to immediate termination when the relief was approved without findings that it was narrowly drawn, extended no further than necessary to correct the federal-right violation and was the least intrusive necessary means. Under original §3626(b)(3), relief must nevertheless continue upon written, record-based findings that it remains necessary to correct a current **or** ongoing federal violation, is no broader than necessary, and is narrowly drawn and the least intrusive means; existing substantive findings may satisfy the statute without ritual wording.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the following rules and qualifications.

**Material application:** Section 802 expressly reaches existing prospective relief. The State must establish the termination basis and the court must independently apply (b)(3); missing ritual phrases, age of the decree or relief exceeding constitutional minima alone does not establish dispensability.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is (b)(2) termination, not the waiting-period route under (b)(1) or automatic stay under (e). The legal appeal establishes neither a preserved failure to consider qualifying findings nor a supported violation requiring a new hearing; no statewide compliance finding or accrued monetary liability is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lewis supplies the complete original findings and preservation conditions. Rufo remains the equitable modification rule, but its significant-change threshold is no additional prerequisite to this independent statutory route.

**Current-force modification:** From November 26, 1997, including pending cases, Pub. L. 105-119, §123(a)(2), (b), 111 Stat. 2470–2471, requires a current and ongoing federal-right violation under §3626(b)(3), instead of the original current-or-ongoing condition. The independent (b)(2) route and the substantive written-findings, need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness requirements remain. The announced Holding continues to state the original-text rule; the amendment does not rewrite that adjudication.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Pub. L. 105-119, §123(a)(2), (b), effective 1997-11-26, changes the preservation condition for pending cases. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse, First Circuit Nos. 97-1261, 97-1262, 97-1263 and 97-1334, decided 1998-01-15, applies the amended condition without deciding a case where the changed conjunction excludes an otherwise supported violation.

##### III. Negotiated decree protections are not automatically independent federal rights

**Holding:** Incorporating agreed protections into a federal consent decree does not make every protection exceeding underlying federal law the independent federal right whose violation prevents termination under §3626(b)(2). Continuing prospective enforcement remains subject to the statute, while relief necessary to correct an actual federal violation must be preserved under the full original (b)(3) conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the following rules and qualifications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Relief exceeding a constitutional minimum may still be necessary to correct an actual federal violation. Separate contractual or state-law claims and independently accrued rights are not adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rufo permits negotiated obligations beyond constitutional minima without insulating their future enforcement from valid legislation. Lewis permits necessary corrective measures; Joseph A.'s performance-condition ruling, made without a PLRA prison-conditions predicate, does not displace this statutory route.

**Current-force modification:** The distinction between negotiated decree terms and independent federal rights remains controlling. For the preservation condition incorporated in this proposition, Pub. L. 105-119, §123(a)(2), (b), effective November 26, 1997 and applicable to pending cases, substitutes current and ongoing for current or ongoing; the other preservation requirements remain. The full statutory transition appears in this case’s independent-termination question above.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Pub. L. 105-119, §123(a)(2), (b), effective 1997-11-26, changes the incorporated preservation condition; Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse, decided 1998-01-15, applies the amended condition and preserves the decree/right distinction.

##### IV. The presented due-process claim does not bar termination

**Holding:** Beneficiaries of this mutable prospective decree have no vested entitlement to perpetual federal enforcement of its unchanged terms despite valid later law. On the presented legal challenge, the judicial motion process, opportunity to oppose termination and appellate review satisfy due process; no additional evidentiary remand follows from an unestablished possibility that a statutory preservation condition was overlooked.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt the following rules and qualifications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No unrecorded hearing is presumed or actual denial of an opportunity to present a preserved factual claim adjudicated. A demonstrated failure to apply (b)(3) would be distinct, and state sovereignty would not excuse it. General equal protection, accrued or contractual rights, other PLRA provisions and particular current prison conditions remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Termination of prospective federal enforcement is affirmed unanimously against the presented legal objections. No new hearing, automatic stay, prisoner-release order, accrued-damages cancellation or extinguishment of independent rights follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lewis's meaningful-access and original current-or-ongoing protections remain operative.

#### Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse

**Docket or dockets:** First Circuit Nos. 97-1261, 97-1262, 97-1263 and 97-1334; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1998-01-15.

For the Article III validity of the prospective-law change, see Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse, First Circuit Nos. 97-1261, 97-1262, 97-1263 and 97-1334, decided January 15, 1998, Constitutional Structure — Congressional amendment of law governing prospective relief.

##### The independent statutory termination route and amended preservation condition govern

**Holding:** Section 3626(b)(2) permits termination of covered prospective prison-conditions relief approved without the substantive need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings. The State must establish that predicate. Existing actual findings can suffice without ritual statutory wording, and Rufo's separate equitable significant-change threshold is not an additional prerequisite. Under §3626(b)(3), as amended effective November 26, 1997 and applicable to pending cases, preservation requires written, record-based findings of necessity to correct a current and ongoing federal-right violation, with relief no broader than necessary, narrowly drawn and least intrusive. Consent decrees and pretrial-detention conditions are covered. Negotiated terms are not automatically independent federal rights, but necessary corrective relief may exceed the minimum conduct rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, unanimous.

##### The supported findings assessment permits termination without a speculative new inquiry

**Holding:** The reported judicial evaluation establishes no qualifying findings or supported federal violation requiring preservation of this relief. No violation meeting the broader original condition but excluded by the amended conjunction is identified. The narrower text therefore creates no outcome-determinative difference on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent judicial review and a proper opportunity to offer relevant evidence remain required. Evaluated reports and existing findings support termination here; no qualifying violation under the broader original condition is identified. No permanent compliance finding or new hearing follows from speculative deterioration.


##### Vested-right and procedural due process do not preserve unchanged future enforcement

**Holding:** Beneficiaries possess no perpetual entitlement to federal enforcement of the mutable decree's unchanged prospective terms against valid later remedial law. The reported motion, opposition and review support rejection of the presented procedural objection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Past lawful effects and independently accrued claims are preserved. Calling continuing judicial obligations contractual does not exempt them from the governing termination conditions.

##### The assumed independent contract-impairment objection fails at its presented scope

**Holding:** Assuming a contract and substantial impairment solely to decide the objection, the federal-nonparty rationality framework permits this restriction on continuing federal supervision. The United States does not become a contracting party merely because a federal court approved the decree or Congress enacted legislation benefiting States.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part IV has all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejecting the objection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither a separate contract’s existence, survival, breach or enforceability nor accrued contractual liability is decided.

##### The presented equality challenge fails without diminishing pretrial rights or meaningful access

**Holding:** Ordinary rational review permits the statute's restriction on continuing prison-decree supervision because it reasonably relates the actual supervisory disadvantage to legitimate institutional responsibility and resource concerns while retaining enforceable federal-right protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Pretrial protection under Bell and meaningful court access remain; nonsuspect status alone does not prove validity, and no blanket prohibition on supported threatened-injury relief is adopted.

##### Termination ends prospective judicial operation, not past adjudication

**Holding:** The statutory direction to terminate, together with the inclusion of consent decrees in prospective relief, requires ending the decree's continuing judicial obligations themselves. It does not declare the original judgment erroneous, erase completed determinations or payments, or retrospectively vacate lawful past effects.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine join the prospective/historical distinction; Kennedy's Part V ordinary-affirmance mandate has all eight Associates.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The separate Rule 60(b) theory was set aside conditionally upon obtaining termination; its merits remain unaddressed once that condition is satisfied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The First Circuit judgment is affirmed, including termination of the decree’s future operation without retrospective annulment. No new merits inquiry, hearing, release, automatic stay or deadline is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Plyler supplies the independent statutory route, judicial preservation inquiry and bounded constitutional operation under its original text; the present amendment is applied without rewriting that holding. Lewis retains the statute's scope, transition, findings and meaningful-access limits. Rufo retains its independent significant-change and tailored-modification rule, not an extra statutory prerequisite. Morgan Stanley and Plaut retain their limited completed-dismissal reinstatement holdings; Robertson, Klein and Wheeling Bridge preserve the changed-law/adjudication distinction. Bell preserves pretrial protection. Beach, Heller and Romer supply ordinary review within their actual limits, and Atchison supplies the assumed federal-nonparty contract analysis. No precedent is overruled.

#### Young v. Harper

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1598.
**Decided:** March 18, 1997.

##### Whether an expressly accepted late rehearing filing makes the subsequent certiorari petition timely

**Holding:** When the appellate court grants leave for a late rehearing filing, treats it as timely and keeps the proceeding open until disposition, denial of that accepted rehearing supplies the relevant finality point for certiorari. The petition filed within ninety days of denial on this sequence is timely.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part I, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees with the timely-review resolution without adopting its reasons.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An unauthorized late paper, an en banc suggestion not treated as rehearing, the mandate date by itself, or an order solely extending the Supreme Court filing period does not establish the same result. No statutory period is waived.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The operative Rule 13.3 and FRAP 40 permit this accepted-rehearing sequence. Bowman distinguishes permitted and entertained rehearing from unauthorized submission; Missouri v. Jenkins, 495 U.S. 33 (1990), respects actual rehearing treatment within the statutory limit.

For the conditional-community-liberty, revocation-process and remedy holdings in Young v. Harper, No. 95-1598, decided March 18, 1997, see Criminal Procedure, “Conditional community release and revocation.”

#### Metropolitan Life Insurance Co. v. Robertson-Ceco Corp.

**Docket or dockets:** Second Circuit No. 95-7370; companion petition Nos. 96-306 and 96-472.
**Decided:** 1997-04-21.

##### A reasonable multiyear inquiry governs the contacts record

**Holding:** Whether a corporation's forum business is continuous and systematic may be assessed over a reasonable period preceding commencement, rather than exclusively during the filing year; a ceased earlier activity is relevant to that course but does not by itself establish present amenability. The 1987–1993 discovery period was reasonable on this record, and the discovery limit remains undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part I: unanimous for the reasonable-period rule; eight Justices join the discovery-limit rationale, with Stone-Zsela agreeing only in that subcomponent's disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No mandatory seven-year period or new evidentiary hearing is prescribed; the former office alone does not establish present jurisdiction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** International Shoe supplies quality and nature; Perkins and Helicopteros distinguish substantial continuing operations from insufficient recurring contacts without a mandatory filing-year snapshot.

##### The credited aggregate contacts satisfy the existing general-jurisdiction standard

**Holding:** At the prima-facie stage presented here, Robertson's sustained selling, deliberate solicitation, supported distribution relationships, repeated company visits, and earlier staffed office, considered together, constitute continuous and systematic forum business sufficient for general jurisdiction. This conclusion credits the plaintiff's factual averments after discovery; it neither converts them into final findings nor makes sales, registration, an independent dealer, a former office, or any percentage independently sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No numerical threshold, automatic dealer attribution, registration-consent holding or incorporation-or-headquarters-only rule is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Perkins and Helicopteros govern the aggregate course. Quill's related use-tax due-process holding supplies no all-purpose-jurisdiction rule; its distinct Commerce Clause holding is unchanged.

##### General jurisdiction retains an independent constitutional fairness inquiry

**Holding:** Sufficient continuous and systematic contacts do not end the due-process inquiry: a defendant may still establish that exercising general jurisdiction would be constitutionally unreasonable by making a compelling showing grounded in the actual burden and relevant plaintiff, forum, and judicial-system interests. The absence of a connection between the claim and forum may inform that inquiry, but ordinary unrelatedness cannot alone do decisive work because general jurisdiction exists to reach some unrelated claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter; six direct joins. Scalia reserves the invariably separate inquiry; Ginsburg and Breyer do not reach it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Serious domestic unfairness can qualify; the inquiry is not limited to foreign defendants. Ordinary venue convenience remains distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** International Shoe and Burger King supply fair play and the compelling-case burden. Asahi's independent-fairness holding applies to general jurisdiction; its stream-of-commerce plurality is not made controlling.

##### The offered fairness objection is insufficient on this record

**Holding:** The foreign location of the claim-generating conduct, proof and witnesses, the parties' nonresidence, and the slight domestic defense burden shown here, considered together, do not make jurisdiction over this deliberately maintained commercial relationship constitutionally unfair. The Vermont dismissal cannot rest on that reasonableness determination; preserved venue and transfer questions remain for their own rules.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part III-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; seven direct joins. Scalia assumes the full independent inquiry applies.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The jurisdictional dismissal is reversed and remanded by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; Stone-Zsela would vacate instead. The prima-facie conclusion leaves ordinary factual, service, limitations, venue, transfer and merits questions open, requires no new hearing on already resolved matters, and does not disturb the other defendant's separate judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Burger King's demanding unfairness burden governs. Asahi's severe international burdens distinguish its result without limiting fairness protection to foreign defendants. Keeton keeps choice of law and a favorable limitations period separate from personal jurisdiction.

#### Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 94-60631; 88 F.3d 274 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

For the official-school-worship grounds and limits on the interim injunction, see *Ingebretsen*, March 19, 1997, No. 94-60631 (First Amendment — Official school worship and private student expression).

##### Whether imminent personal school-event exposure supports prospective standing

**Holding:** Students concretely and imminently facing an official school-event mechanism may challenge that threatened personal exposure before it occurs, where the injury is causally connected to the mechanism and prospectively redressable. Standing does not establish that the threatened conduct is unlawful.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The enrolled students face impending events under a mandatory permission mechanism administered by officials, supported by the hearing record. Lack of a completed local policy does not make that prospect remote; parents may represent their children's interests through the proper representative route.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general taxpayer standing or standing for an abstract objector elsewhere is recognized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Lujan* supplies concrete injury, causation, and redressability; standing is not derived from abstract opposition to a statute.

##### Whether the student applicants established inadequate representation under Rule 24(a)(2)

**Holding:** A timely applicant with a protectable interest that litigation may impair is entitled to intervene when existing representation may be inadequate. Shared desired results and the State's official identity do not conclusively prove adequacy, but the Attorney General actually represented these applicants' private-expression interests and no relevant omitted interest was identified; denial stands without reaching unnecessary alternative elements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part V-A, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela dissents from this component.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Amicus participation cannot substitute for an otherwise established intervention right. Materially changed interests may support a proper later application, without an advance entitlement or a required new adequacy hearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Trbovich* supplies the minimal burden of possible inadequacy and recognizes potentially different governmental and private interests; the inquiry remains individualized.

##### Whether a common question compels permissive intervention

**Holding:** Rule 24(b)'s common-question condition permits intervention but does not require it. The court retains discretion considering undue delay or prejudice, and its duplication-and-delay assessment justifies denial in this narrow statutory proceeding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part V-B, joined by all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees with the disposition only.

**Material application:** Available amicus submissions support the conclusion that excluding duplicative party participation caused no demonstrated prejudice; they do not defeat intervention as of right.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court creates no novelty-of-argument requirement, unreviewable discretion, or universal plain-error prerequisite.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### III. The Court's present change supplies the legal change required for prospective relief

**Holding:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join the present-change rule and its distinction between this continuing decree and a completed judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Denial of Rule 60(b)(5) relief is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with its governing legal premises reviewed as questions of law. Rufo requires a significant change making continued enforcement inequitable and a modification fitted to that change. The legal change here is the Court's ruling today. It is not the expectation that the Court might reconsider Aguilar, an increase in the expense of compliance, or a supposed earlier overruling. Kiryas Joel expressly preserved Aguilar and Ball. Zobrest decided antecedent statutory and regulatory questions without deciding establishment. Rosenberger's actual printing benefit and Pinette and Chabad's private-expression holdings did not authorize this public teaching program. Rodriguez de Quijas required the lower courts to apply the directly governing rule.

The objection that Rule 60 review cannot become an untimely rehearing of the original judgment does not bar this relief. The motion addresses the decree's continuing prospective operation, and the preserved question presents the constitutional rule supporting that operation. The Court changes that rule expressly; it does not hold that the district court should have anticipated the change. Requiring a new motion solely to invoke today's decision would preserve a now-rejected prohibition without a remaining legal reason.

Rufo's limits remain. Dissatisfaction, a cheaper lawful alternative and anticipated doctrinal change are insufficient. Actual anticipation and acceptance of a risk can impose a heavy burden, but mere foreseeability is not an absolute bar and an unforeseeable catastrophe is unnecessary. A clarification of constitutional permission does not automatically erase a voluntarily accepted stricter consent obligation. This injunction enforces an adjudicated constitutional prohibition; the record establishes no negotiated promise independently requiring that prohibition. The Court neither reopens completed claims nor finds an independently sufficient factual change in costs.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail is applied to the present legal change and retained in its significant-change, accepted-risk and tailored-relief requirements. Rodriguez de Quijas v. Shearson/American Express, Inc. is applied to preserve lower-court obedience to direct precedent. Browder v. Director, Department of Corrections does not foreclose adjustment of continuing relief on the basis of the legal change actually adopted here; its limit on using a Rule 60 appeal to reopen the underlying completed judgment remains.

##### IV. Relief must remove the rejected prohibition while preserving independently lawful obligations

**Holding:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. Any remaining obligation must have an identified lawful basis, and any further proceedings must concern a specified material issue under the surviving law rather than require proof that future misconduct is impossible.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of the Court's opinion; the same five Justices directly join this tailored relief and its public-control limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rufo connects modification to the proven change while preserving the lawful purposes of continuing relief. The change today eliminates the categorical site-and-monitoring ground. The district court therefore cannot simply reinstate that same ground, presume that public service and religious instruction are inseparable, or treat ordinary secular supervision as itself forbidden. It must remove the prohibition to that extent. Supported questions about actual religious instruction, diversion, religious control of public employees or another independent legal prohibition remain subject to ordinary procedure and the governing law; hypothetical misconduct does not justify preserving Aguilar's rejected inference.

No additional negotiated obligation is presumed. If an independent statutory, contractual or decree obligation is established, constitutional permission does not by itself extinguish it. Existing lawful services may continue while the mandate is implemented promptly. Children currently receiving those services should continue to receive them during implementation, with existing statutory duties preserved and no new service entitlement created. Any implementation period must address identified tasks and circumstances; the Court sets no number of days, and administrative delay cannot indefinitely preserve the rejected categorical prohibition. The district court modifies the decree under the mandate. Until it acts, the City must obey the operative injunction.

The holding permits the described program; it does not command onsite delivery, create an unconditional funding entitlement, approve a replacement program, award damages or repayment, cancel contracts, or excuse past disobedience. It makes no finding that every present implementation satisfies the required secular controls.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Rufo's suitable-tailoring requirement controls implementation and preserves any independently lawful obligation. Its consent-decree rule is not converted into a presumption that this adjudicated injunction contains a voluntary stricter undertaking.

For the Lemon test and the onsite secular-service rule in Holdings I–II, see First Amendment, “Public instruction on religious-school premises,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

#### Barclays Bank PLC v. Franchise Tax Board of California, 512 U.S. 298

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 92-1384 and 92-1839 (Colgate-Palmolive included within this entry).

**Decided:** 1994-06-20.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** No. 92-1384 concerns Barclays’ foreign-parent group; No. 92-1839 concerns Colgate-Palmolive’s domestic-parent group. Their different judgment coalitions are preserved below.

##### Ordinary constitutional tax requirements and the apportionment challenge — Part I-A

**Holding:** California may attribute the fair California share of an accepted unitary business's worldwide income through the equally weighted property, payroll and sales formula only if the assessment has substantial nexus, fair apportionment, nondiscrimination and a fair relationship to state services; an accepted unitary base does not excuse a defect in any other requirement. These taxpayers establish no failure of those requirements, and Colgate's comparison with separate accounting does not supply the clear-and-cogent constitutional showing of income out of all appropriate proportion to California activity or a grossly distorted result.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion, Part I-A, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: nine Justices adopt the rule and its bounded application. Agreement on this component does not decide Barclays' separate foreign-commerce objections.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Under California Revenue and Taxation Code §25137, when ordinary allocation and apportionment do not fairly represent the extent of California business activity, the taxpayer may petition for, or the Board may require, a reasonable alternative for all or part of that activity: separate accounting; exclusion of one or more factors; inclusion of additional factors fairly representing California activity; or another method producing equitable allocation and apportionment. This is no unconditional taxpayer election. The failure-of-predicate ruling does not decide a categorical refusal to consider separate accounting, and this Court does not independently alter the state statutory proof standard.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Complete Auto Transit, Inc. v. Brady, 430 U.S. 274 (1977): applies all four independent state-tax requirements; foreign-commerce scrutiny remains additional.
- Container Corp. of America v. Franchise Tax Board, 463 U.S. 159 (1983): applies its treatment of unitary value, three-factor apportionment and the clear-and-cogent constitutional burden; separate accounting is not the constitutional benchmark.
- Allied-Signal, Inc. v. Director, Division of Taxation, June 15, 1992: preserves the connected-income boundary and the distinction between a lawful base and fair apportionment; no new unitary finding is made.
- Kraft General Foods, Inc. v. Iowa Department of Revenue and Finance, June 18, 1992: preserves independent foreign-income nondiscrimination review; its location-based dividend distinction is absent here.
- Associated Industries of Missouri v. Lohman, May 23, 1994: applies the relevant-burden comparison, without importing its sales/use-tax mechanism into income apportionment.

##### The presented reporting-burden challenge fails under the actual accommodations — Part I-B

**Holding:** A foreign-based unitary group does not establish unconstitutional discrimination merely by estimating the expense of literal worldwide reporting when binding rules require consideration of effort and expense, permit reasonable approximations in appropriate cases, and the record shows accommodations without an identified unreasonable rejection of a reasonable approximation. The conclusion concerns this administration and record, leaving demonstrated discriminatory administration or arbitrary information demands open; the Board must consider effort and expense but need not accept every proposed approximation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion, Part I-B, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: nine direct joins to this reporting-burden application.

**Material application:** Regulation §25137-6(e)(1) requires consideration of effort and expense and permits reasonable approximations in appropriate cases, including when ordinary business records cannot supply necessary information. Accommodations were used and no reasonable approximation was shown to have been unreasonably rejected. Full-group literal-compliance estimates and BBI-only historical preparation costs are different measures, not interchangeable proof of burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Complete Auto Transit, Inc. v. Brady, 430 U.S. 274 (1977): applies its nondiscrimination requirement to the actual reporting burden.
- Kraft General Foods, Inc. v. Iowa Department of Revenue and Finance, June 18, 1992: preserves the need for an independent foreign/domestic comparison despite accepted unitary status.

**Holding:** Due process permits reasonable approximations in complex multijurisdictional tax allocation when intelligible legal constraints and meaningful review limit administrative discretion; California's reasonableness standard, mandatory consideration of effort and expense, clarification procedures and judicial review satisfy that requirement on Barclays' challenge. The absence of an identified rejected approximation does not establish a universal injury or exhaustion prerequisite: genuinely standardless authority, arbitrary application or excessive attribution remains challengeable under the governing requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Kennedy's opinion, Part II, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: nine direct joins. ##### Container governs the domestic-parent multiple-taxation claim — Part III-A

**Holding:** Container's domestic-parent rule remains controlling for Colgate's accepted unitary group: a mismatch between worldwide formula apportionment and foreign separate accounting does not itself establish a forbidden international-multiple-taxation burden. #### Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., 511 U.S. 658

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-609.

**Decided:** 1994-05-23.

##### Bounded statutory reinstatement of completed securities limitations dismissals

**Holding:** Article III permits §27A(b)'s expressly retrospective requirement to reinstate a private §10(b) action commenced on or before June 19, 1991, dismissed as time barred after June 19, 1991, and timely under the jurisdiction's law, including retroactivity principles, as that law existed on June 19, 1991, upon a motion filed within sixty days after the provision's December 19, 1991 enactment. The federal court must independently determine whether those cumulative conditions are satisfied and, if they are, must reinstate the action without a discretionary balancing test, retaining independent responsibility for the claims, defenses and remedies; this bounded entitlement to adjudication neither directs a merits judgment nor establishes an unrestricted power to reopen completed federal judgments.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. These five direct joins control the rule, explanation, qualifications and remedy; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** Pacific Mutual's January 31, 1992 motion met the sixty-day deadline; that fact is not a finding of fraud or entitlement to a merits judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, March 25, 1992: extends its distinction between changing governing law and prescribing adjudication to this bounded reinstatement procedure; resolves the final-judgment question left open there only within the present limits.
- United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128 (1872): applies its protection of independent adjudication; a new eligibility rule requiring renewed adjudication does not supply a legislative finding or dictate securities liability.
- McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102 (1898): preserves protection of completed judicial determinations while distinguishing this new statutory entitlement to litigate claims dismissed on limitations grounds from legislative contradiction of adjudicated facts or liability.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421 (1856): distinguishes a change in the continuing operation of prospective relief; its setting does not itself resolve reinstatement of this completed dismissal.
- United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371 (1980): distinguishes waiver of the Government's own defense; retained judicial adjudication is relevant, but no governmental waiver of a private defendant's defense is assumed here.
- Lampf, Pleva, Lipkind, Prupis & Petigrow v. Gilbertson, 501 U.S. 350 (1991): identifies the intervening limitations rule to which Congress responded; §27A(b) supplies the bounded statutory remedy without deciding the underlying fraud claims.
- James B. Beam Distilling Co. v. Georgia, 501 U.S. 529 (1991), and Harper v. Virginia Department of Taxation, June 18, 1993: retain the distinction between judicial retroactivity in still-open litigation and final judgments; the present entitlement arises from an express statute, not judicial retroactivity alone.
- Landgraf v. USI Film Products and Rivers v. Roadway Express, April 26, 1994: preserve the separation between express legislative temporal direction and constitutional limits; no ordinary presumption against retroactivity defeats §27A(b)'s explicit reach.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may not declare a completed judicial factual finding erroneous and compel the contrary finding, dictate a merits judgment, or disguise an individualized award or assessment of liability as a procedural reopening statute. The Court announces no independent due-process holding and does not decide §27A(a), general severability, fraud, liability, damages or entitlement to judgment on the securities merits. Pending litigation, prospective relief and independently authorized judicial reopening remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's judgment sustaining the reinstatement procedure is affirmed. Proceedings may continue under §27A(b). The federal court must determine the statutory prerequisites and reinstate the action when they are proved; it has no discretion to deny reinstatement through a balancing test. The securities claims, defenses and remedies remain for ordinary judicial adjudication. No fraud finding, liability judgment or damages award is entered.

#### Plaut v. Spendthrift Farm, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1121.
**Decided:** April 18, 1995.

##### Section 27A(b) permits mandatory reinstatement of this completed limitations dismissal

**Holding:** Under Morgan Stanley, Article III permits Congress to require reinstatement under §27A(b) of a qualifying private §10(b) action even though its limitations dismissal became final without appeal before the provision's December 19, 1991 enactment: the action must have commenced on or before June 19, 1991, been dismissed as time barred after that date, and been timely under the jurisdiction's law, including retroactivity principles, as it existed June 19, 1991, with a reinstatement motion filed within sixty days after enactment. **Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Those five direct rationale joins control every proposition, qualification, and remedy stated here. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas dissent; no combination of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** A narrowly defined, exclusively retrospective class does not by itself turn this procedure into a legislative judgment: the statute covers every action meeting its legal criteria and selects no parties for legislatively determined liability. The statutory conditions are satisfied, including the timely February 11, 1992 motion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Morgan Stanley & Co. v. Pacific Mutual Life Insurance Co., May 23, 1994: applies its five-Justice Article III holding sustaining §27A(b)'s bounded mandatory reinstatement procedure to this qualifying completed private limitations dismissal; preserves independent eligibility and merits adjudication and all constitutional limits.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, March 25, 1992: applies its distinction between genuine legislative change and prescribed judicial adjudication; Morgan Stanley supplies the completed-dismissal rule that Robertson did not decide.
- United States v. Klein, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 128: applies its protection of the independent judicial function; a class-defined entitlement to renewed adjudication does not dictate findings or securities liability.
- McCullough v. Virginia, 172 U.S. 102: preserves protection of completed judicial determinations while distinguishing the statutory entitlement to litigate a limitations-dismissed claim from a legislative declaration of facts or liability contrary to an adjudication.
- James B. Beam Distilling Co. v. Georgia, 501 U.S. 529: preserves finality against reopening by later judicial doctrine alone; the entitlement here comes from an express statute whose Article III validity Morgan Stanley establishes.
- Pennsylvania v. Wheeling & Belmont Bridge Co., 59 U.S. (18 How.) 421: distinguishes the continuing operation of prospective equitable relief; that setting does not itself decide this completed limitations dismissal.
- United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians, 448 U.S. 371: distinguishes the Government's waiver of its own defense; no governmental waiver of a private defendant's defense supplies the holding here.

The Court decides no general power to set aside completed judgments adjudicating the underlying merits, no constitutionality of every retrospective statute, and no independent due-process question; applicable due-process protections and other constitutional restrictions remain. Section 27A(a), other applications of subsection (b), general severability, federal waiver, continuing prospective relief, and independently authorized judicial reopening are not decided. #### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.
**Decided:** June 24, 1992.

For the live-controversy holding in *Lee v. Weisman*, June 24, 1992, No. 90-1014, see Federal Courts — Prospective equitable relief.

##### Whether officials may organize clergy-led worship at public secondary-school graduation

**Holding:** Public secondary-school officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request invocations and benedictions, guide the prayers' religious content and incorporate that worship into the official graduation program. Neither nonsectarian language nor formally optional attendance cures that sponsorship; no separate showing of punishment or quantified social pressure is required to invalidate this practice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Blackmun's opinion, joined by Stone, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices adopt this independently sufficient sponsorship ground. Kennedy does not join Part II.

**Current force:** Remains controlling for the identified school-organized graduation worship, independently of a separate coercion showing.

**Material application:** The principal selected the rabbi, requested prayer, supplied civic-prayer guidance and placed the exercise in the program. A desire for solemnity does not authorize the State to undertake religious presentation; the record does not establish that officials wrote or edited a complete prayer script.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not prohibit genuinely private prayer or private forum speech, replace the general Establishment Clause framework, or resolve adult legislative prayer, funding, denominational aid or universal accommodation questions. Lemon's purpose and entanglement prongs are not separately decided; broader views in Souter's concurrence are not additional Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit and preserve the prospective injunction against officials' authorizing or encouraging the challenged clergy-led graduation prayer practice. The injunction reaches the official program, not private prayer or genuinely private forum speech.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Engel v. Vitale, 370 U.S. 421, and School District of Abington Township v. Schempp, 374 U.S. 203, are applied: nonsectarian language, voluntary attendance and permission to abstain do not cure official school worship. Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, is applied through its prohibition on a primary effect advancing religion, without a replacement test. Marsh v. Chambers, 463 U.S. 783, is distinguished as legislative prayer in a different setting; Board of Education of Westside Community Schools v. Mergens, 496 U.S. 226, protects private religious equal-access activity rather than official sponsorship.

**Independent alternative holding:** School-directed clergy prayer at a public secondary-school graduation independently violates the Establishment Clause when school control and the ceremony's importance place objecting students under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the occasion. Formally optional attendance and the opportunity to stand or remain silent do not remove that pressure here; mere offense alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's concurrence in part and in the judgment, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five Justices expressly adopt this independently sufficient ground. It controls through direct majority support, not Marks. Stone does not join this writing.

**Current force of alternative holding:** Remains independently controlling for the stated school-directed secondary-school graduation practice and substantial participation pressure.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** The absence of a disciplinary penalty does not eliminate the injury; a student need not protest publicly or miss the singular educational ceremony to avoid apparent participation. The ground does not equate disagreement or ordinary courtesy with coercion and leaves mature adult audiences and other accommodations open. It independently sustains the same limited prospective injunction. Engel and Schempp apply to this indirect religious pressure; Marsh is distinguished by the adult legislative setting and different freedom to leave. Lynch v. Donnelly, 465 U.S. 668, supplies the minimum prohibition on coerced religious support or participation without extending its display holding. County of Allegheny v. ACLU, 492 U.S. 573, is not reopened as to displays or every accommodation dispute.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method.

#### Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 94-60631; 88 F.3d 274 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

For personal standing and both intervention rulings, see *Ingebretsen*, March 19, 1997, No. 94-60631 (Federal Courts — Standing and intervention in school-religion litigation).

##### Whether student delivery or a disclaimer insulates officially organized school worship

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause forbids a school's organization, adoption, direction, or religious supervision of worship within its own event. Student initiation or delivery and a statutory disclaimer do not alone make that activity private, and official sponsorship can violate the Clause without a separate quantified-pressure showing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Breyer; six Justices directly adopt the sponsorship ground.

**Material application:** Section 1(2)'s permission for student-initiated, nonsectarian, nonproselytizing prayer at compulsory and noncompulsory events cannot be administered through official devotional organization or religious supervision. Section 1(4)'s disclaimer cannot erase actual official conduct. Section 2's prohibition on prescribing prayer's form or content is a legal command, not a finding that officials always obey or violate it.

**Independent alternative holding:** School direction of religious exercise also violates the Clause when compulsory attendance or comparable practical pressures create actual pressure to participate. Calling participation voluntary or using a student speaker does not eliminate that inquiry; offense at another student's private viewpoint is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III, joined by Ginsburg, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer; six direct votes independently support this ground, without a *Marks* synthesis.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** *Lee* supplies the separate sponsorship and school-pressure rules. Pressure requires no quantified measurement or legal penalty, but is not presumed from hearing private religious speech or attending any voluntary gathering. The hearing-supported threat justifies bounded interim protection, not final invalidation of every statutory application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pinette* and *Chabad of Southern Ohio* require contextual attribution; a disclaimer or forum label is not conclusive. *Rosenberger* preserves neutral aid to independently edited private speech. *Lamb's Chapel*, *Good News Club*, and *Brown v. Polk County* preserve independent expression without making every school event a private forum or importing employment burdens.

##### Whether the preliminary injunction may exempt graduation categorically or suppress independently lawful expression

**Holding:** Interim relief must address the supported unlawful official mechanism while preserving genuinely private religious expression and independently lawful provisions. Graduation has no automatic student-initiation or student-delivery exception to the official-attribution and pressure inquiries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV's private-expression and lawful-provision limit has all nine joins. Continued restraint, removal of the graduation exemption, and remand are adopted by Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer; Scalia and Thomas would also vacate the retained restraint.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the supported restraint, vacate the graduation safe harbor and overbroad reach, and remand for a severed, provision- and event-sensitive interim order. Give effect separately to §1(3)'s other voluntary expression, §2's voluntary-prayer provision and prohibition on prescribing content, and §1(5)'s severability direction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new forum or wider access entitlement is created by judicial deletion of the religious filters. State-law construction and precise decree terms remain below; no final damages claim or universal statutory invalidity is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Lee* forecloses the lower-court *Jones II* criteria as a categorical graduation safe harbor while leaving genuine private graduation speech protected.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

#### Tanford v. Brand

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-3053; no separate Supreme Court docket stated.

**Decided:** 1997-12-22.

##### The live controversy concerns the University's own devotional program

**Holding:** The continuing policy and Urbanski's concrete expectation of attending his 1999 graduation support prospective review. The University selects a religious leader and incorporates actual invocation and benediction prayers into its official ceremony; independent composition of the words does not make the exercises private equal-access speech.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Continuing policy and Urbanski’s supported expected graduation exposure establish the live claim; ideological objection alone or treating other plaintiffs as perpetual students would not.

##### Official university commencement worship violates the Establishment Clause on this record

**Holding:** A public university violates the Establishment Clause when it incorporates officially arranged religious prayer into its commencement exercises, as the University did by selecting a religious leader to conduct actual invocation and benediction prayers as components of its official commencement. The violation does not depend on whether attendance is compulsory, whether the prayer is sectarian, or whether the ceremony also serves secular purposes. This governmental organization of worship falls under the specific-prohibition route of Lemon as Agostini modified it; mature attendance, meaningful freedom to abstain, brevity, nonsectarian language and a sincere ceremonial purpose do not cure it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Parts II–III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The specific official-worship prohibition resolves this practice under Lemon as modified by Agostini; no independent primary-effect prong, separate coercion showing, sham-purpose, denominational-exclusion or excessive-entanglement finding is required. No other adult ceremonial arrangement is decided.

##### Relief must address the supported official practice and preserve private expression

**Holding:** On remand, the lower courts shall determine appropriate prospective relief against the University's official selection and inclusion of these prayers, subject to the plaintiffs' continuing standing and the ordinary requirements for an injunction. Independently chosen private religious expression, neutral access to a genuine forum and nondevotional ceremonial remarks remain distinct; the judgment requires no new forum and no suppression of otherwise lawful viewpoints.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Parts II–III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Any warranted decree must preserve independent prayer and lawful private expression. Private graduate remarks, nondevotional historical acknowledgment and a moment of silence are not prohibited by this holding, but the Court does not approve unpresented arrangements or require a new forum.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 6–3, for prospective relief limited to the supported official prayer practice, subject to continuing standing and ordinary injunction requirements. No damages, fee ruling or automatic hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Lee and Ingebretsen's official-worship prohibition is applied to the actual university commencement program; their separate substantial-pressure rules remain unchanged and need not be applied to find a violation here. Engel and Schempp retain their official-worship protections despite nonsectarian wording and permission to abstain. Lemon remains the governing test as Agostini modified it: the specific-prohibition route resolves this governmental organization of worship, and primary religious effect is evidence rather than an independent prong. Agostini's onsite secular-service holding does not authorize governmental worship. Marsh is distinguished by its legislative context, and Sherman by its civic exercise. Pinette, Rosenberger, Good News and Mergens preserve genuinely private religious expression; their neutral-access holdings do not authorize governmental worship. No precedent is overruled.

#### Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10

**Docket or dockets:** Second Circuit No. 96-9633; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** 1998-05-11.

##### 1. Private worship is not government worship merely because it uses school space

**Holding:** Permitting this outside congregation to use school space for its own Sunday worship does not by itself establish religion. The district cannot justify this exclusion as constitutionally required, or rely on an identification of the school with the church, without facts establishing genuine governmental adoption rather than private access.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I is joined by all eight other Justices. It answers the establishment question without determining the separate scope of the speech forum.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The congregation, rather than school officials, would arrange the services. Genuine official adoption, control or preferential operation requires its own inquiry. Agostini’s modified Lemon framework applies; the ruling neither opens every public building nor immunizes every religious use.

##### 2. An admitted instructional subject cannot exclude its religious perspective

**Holding:** Once this forum admits instruction on a subject, the district may not exclude an otherwise eligible speaker because the instruction presents a religious perspective. Permitting religious discussion does not cure a ban on religious instruction where comparable secular ethical and character teaching is admitted.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices support the general rule in Part II. Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer also join its application and the instruction reversal; Stone-Zsela does not join that disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The instruction holding extends only to subjects actually admitted for comparable secular instruction. It does not itself classify every integrated worship service as an admitted use.

##### 3. This broad private community forum cannot exclude the requested use solely for its devotional form

**Holding:** In this actual after-hours educational and community forum, the district may not disqualify the church's otherwise comparable private Sunday meeting solely because its expression takes the form of worship. The district retains reasonable, viewpoint-neutral operational and genuine activity limits, but it cannot define comparable admitted uses simply by removing their religious expression.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Parts III and IV are joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, a five-Justice controlling majority. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg oppose this application; no narrower rationale is assembled from their votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual broad after-hours forum, rather than a universal public-facility entitlement, controls. Exact secular counterparts to a sacrament are unnecessary. Reasonable neutral scheduling, safety, capacity, supervision and genuine activity boundaries survive. No continuous rental, preferential control, public devotional financing, compulsory disclaimer or permanent forum opening follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Worship exclusion is reversed by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, 5–4; the instruction disposition is reversed by all eight Associates, 8–1. The case returns for nondiscriminatory consideration and appropriate relief within those questions. No final permit, fixed injunction or damages is ordered; ungranted free-exercise, equality and RFRA issues remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Agostini:** The governing modified Lemon inquiry is applied without reinstating independent former prongs or replacing it with a coercion-only rule.
- **Lamb's Chapel; Good News/Good Sports; Church on the Rock:** Their instruction and viewpoint holdings apply; their standalone-worship reservations are answered only for the present comparable use.
- **Rosenberger:** Viewpoint neutrality within the actual program supplies the speech principle; its printing-benefit setting is not converted into a general funding obligation.
- **Pinette and Chabad:** Preserve bounded private access and contextual attribution; location and religious identity alone are insufficient.
- **Tanford and Chaudhuri:** Official organization of devotional prayer remains prohibited and distinct from independent rental use.
- **Perry Education and Cornelius:** Preserve reasonable, viewpoint-neutral genuine subject and activity limits; consistency alone does not validate an exclusion.
- **Widmar:** Supports the distinction between private religious exercise in a forum and a governmental religious program.
- **Perry v. Los Angeles Police Department:** Supplies no traditional-public-forum classification or transplanted tailoring standard for this school.

#### Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-757.

**Decided:** 1994-04-26.

For the other questions in Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 1994-04-26, No. 92-757, see Federal Courts — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences.

##### Section 102 does not add its new damages to this preenactment conduct

**Holding:** Section 102’s new compensatory and punitive liability does not attach to discriminatory conduct completed before November 21, 1991, because it adds legal consequences without sufficient congressional direction for that reach. Its Title VII compensation supplements, rather than replaces, §706(g) relief and excludes backpay, interest on backpay and other relief authorized by that provision; punitive damages have the distinct statutory predicates of malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude government, government-agency and political-subdivision respondents. No such damages award or predicate finding is made here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s §102 application, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas independently reach the same unavailability of new damages through their concurrence. Blackmun disagrees about compensation and reserves punitive damages.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Bennett and Bowen: applied to the new monetary burden attached to completed conduct.
- Bradley: distinguished; the collateral fee award did not create this new compensation or punishment for the underlying private conduct.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992): distinguished; appropriate relief in an implied Title IX action does not enlarge Title VII’s earlier express remedial scheme.
- Robertson v. Seattle Audubon Society, 503 U.S. 429 (1992): distinguished; its express substitution of law governing identified pending disputes does not supply the temporal direction missing here.

##### The jury election depends on an available statutory damages claim

**Holding:** Under §102(c), if the complaining party seeks compensatory or punitive damages available under that section, any party may demand a jury trial, and the court must not inform the jury of the statutory monetary limits. Because §102 damages are unavailable for this preenactment conduct, its conditional jury provision supplies no independent right to a new trial; this conclusion does not prohibit applying a genuinely procedural change to an appropriate future procedural act in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s jury portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the controlling five-Justice explanation. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reach the same conditional conclusion in their concurrence; Blackmun would permit a jury election because he would make compensation available.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kaiser: applied to the enacted condition linking the jury option to §102 damages.
- Bradley: distinguished; current-law treatment of collateral matters does not erase an express condition on a new remedy or automatically reopen a completed trial.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is provision-specific, not a declaration that the whole 1991 Act is inapplicable in pending cases. Section 402(a)'s enactment-date clause, §109(c)'s later-conduct limitation for extraterritorial amendments, and §402(b)'s exception for disparate-impact complaints filed before March 1, 1975 with initial decisions after October 30, 1983 remain distinct. Judicial retroactivity under Harper does not control legislative reach. An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. The present case concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is affirmed, 8–1. No new damages trial, award or remand is ordered, and the existing equitable-remedy judgment and unreviewed constructive-discharge findings remain intact.

#### Washington v. Glucksberg

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-110.

**Decided:** June 26, 1997.

##### Whether competent terminally ill adults have the asserted fundamental right to willing physician assistance in suicide

**Holding:** The Due Process Clause does not establish a fundamental right for a competent, terminally ill adult to obtain a willing physician's intentional assistance in suicide through a lethal prescription. The asserted class-wide entitlement therefore receives no heightened protection; a materially different, more particularized claim is not foreclosed merely by this disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five direct rationale votes. The four other Justices concur only in the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exclusive most-specific-history methodology governs all liberties. Forced treatment, interference with valid refusal or directives, denial of lawful pain care, punishment of good-faith palliation merely for foreseeable death and discriminatory administration are not decided. The reservation of a materially different particularized claim recognizes no new entitlement or pending action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cruzan's informed-consent foundation, assumed competent-refusal premise and protection of evidence concerning an incompetent patient remain intact; intentional lethal assistance is not their necessary extension. Casey retains individualized viability, life-and-health protection and its entire six-step framework. Mazurek retains complete purpose and residual-interference review; neither abortion decision supplies a universal medical-liberty test.

##### Whether Washington's prohibition rationally serves legitimate protective interests

**Holding:** Because the asserted assistance entitlement is not fundamental, Washington's prohibition is valid against this substantive-due-process challenge if it is rationally related to legitimate state interests. It meets that standard by protecting life, preventing suicide, guarding vulnerable people against error and pressure, maintaining medical responsibility and containing the risks of a legalized lethal practice; the Ninth Circuit's class-wide invalidation cannot stand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five direct rationale votes. The four other Justices concur only in the judgment.

**Material application:** Risk of error, pressure, abuse of vulnerable patients and extension of legalized lethal practice supplies the rational connection. Neither a finding that every request is involuntary nor proof that prohibition is the best policy is necessary; the existence of proposed safeguards does not compel their adoption.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lawful palliation is not a finding that all suffering is actually relieved. No incapacity or misconduct of a named party is found. The ruling does not constitutionally forbid legislative authorization and regulation of assisted death.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit, 9–0, and remand with directions to enter judgment for Washington on the asserted due-process entitlement. No patient-specific hearing or prescription authority is ordered.

#### County of Sacramento v. Lewis

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1337.
**Decided:** 1998-05-26.

##### What culpability the supported rapid pursuit requires for substantive-due-process liability

**Holding:** In the rapidly evolving high-speed pursuit presented here, where officers must make instant choices amid competing enforcement and safety obligations, reckless or deliberately indifferent risk-taking alone does not establish a substantive-due-process violation. A purpose to cause harm unrelated to a legitimate law-enforcement objective is required; because this plaintiff-favorable record does not establish that purpose, the reviewed federal individual claim fails.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for the complete rule and application. Purposeful collisions, terrorizing conduct, contrived encounters and materially more deliberative circumstances require their own analysis. The constitutional standard is grounded in precedent and the setting, not personal judicial reaction; no mandatory merits-first sequence is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** All nine reverse the reviewed individual ruling, but only six adopt the controlling constitutional rationale. ## Standards and Tests selection: Prison-conditions prospective relief

### Prison-conditions prospective relief

**Governing question:** The §3626 scope, necessity findings and preliminary-relief conditions.

**Current rule:** In a civil proceeding arising under federal law concerning conditions of confinement or officials' effects on confined persons' lives, prospective relief must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the particular plaintiffs' federal-right violation and be the least intrusive necessary means; the court must make those findings and give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice-system effects. Relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority or otherwise violate that law also requires that federal law require the state-law violation, necessity to correct the federal violation and absence of any other corrective relief. The statute confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable remedial limits.

Otherwise authorized preliminary injunctive relief must be narrow, no broader than necessary for the identified harm and least intrusive, with substantial safety/system weight and respect for the distinct state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. An existing permanent decree does not automatically become a preliminary order.

**Authority by component:**

- Enacted conditions, scope and all-relief transition: PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §802, original 18 U.S.C. §3626(a)(1)–(2), (c)–(d), (g) and §802(b)(1), April 26, 1996.
- Existing-decree application and substantive findings: Lewis v. Casey — Holdings IV–VI, Holding IV, June 24, 1996; Kennedy Part IV for all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela joins only the statutory disposition. 
- Bounded constitutional operation of termination, not a change to the present entry’s remedial conditions: Plyler v. Moore, Plyler v. Moore — II. The prospective-relief rule preserves judicial adjudication, June 23, 1997; see the termination entry’s component authority.

- Federal-law requirement replaces federal permission; effective on enactment and in pending cases: Prison Litigation Reform Act amendments — §123(a)(1)(A) and (b), amending §3626(a)(1)(B)(i), 1997-11-26.

**Present operation effective:** Pub. L. 105-119, §123, 1997-11-26, for the federal-requirement condition; the other statutory conditions and Lewis qualifications retain their original dates.

**Controlling construction:** Prospective relief is all relief other than compensatory monetary damages. A §3626 civil prison-conditions action excludes habeas proceedings challenging the fact or duration of confinement. A prison includes federal, state and local juvenile or adult criminal detention facilities. Consent decrees include relief based wholly or partly on consent or acquiescence and must comply with subsection (a). Private settlements whose only federal judicial enforcement is reinstatement of the settled proceeding are distinct; otherwise available state breach remedies survive. Section 3626 does not govern relief a state court enters solely on state-law claims.

**Effective transition:** Amended §3626 applies to prospective relief originally granted or approved before, on or after April 26, 1996. Existing findings may meet the necessary predicates at their actual scope; ritual wording, modest cost or reasonableness alone neither establishes nor defeats compliance. Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays states Plyler’s June 23, 1997 resolution of the presented Article III and due-process objections to original §3626(b)(2), with the full §3626(b)(3) preservation condition. Rouse, Benjamin and Gavin apply the amended conditions within the bounds stated in Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays; no automatic-stay constitutional holding follows.

**Related current rules:** Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays states the separate actual-motion triggers; Prisoner-release orders and prison crowding supplies additional release-order requirements.

**Holdings navigation:** Lewis v. Casey, 518 U.S. 343 (1996). Plyler v. Moore (1997-06-23) — Federal Courts / Prison access and prospective relief.

**Former-rule note:** Before November 26, 1997, §3626(a)(1)(B)(i) required federal permission rather than a federal requirement for the departure from state law. Section 123 applies the revised term from enactment, including pending cases.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays

### Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays

**Governing question:** When existing prospective prison relief becomes terminable or stayed under §3626.

**Current rule:** On a party’s or intervener’s motion, prospective prison relief is terminable two years after grant or approval, one year after denial of a termination motion under §3626(b)(1), or, for an order on or before April 26, 1996, two years after that date. Under §3626(b)(2), a defendant or intervener is entitled to immediate termination when the movant establishes that relief was entered without substantive need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings, subject to (b)(3)’s preservation condition. Actual sufficient findings require no ritual language, and Rufo’s separate significant-change threshold is not an additional condition. Under amended (b)(3), relief shall not terminate if the court makes written, record-based findings that it is necessary to correct a current and ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn and is the least intrusive necessary means. Earlier agreed or otherwise lawful judicial modification and termination remain possible.

The court independently determines the current violation and the necessity and scope of every retained duty. Consent decrees are covered; negotiated obligations do not automatically become independent rights, while a necessary corrective remedy may exceed the minimum substantive conduct rule. Material current proof needed for this adjudication must receive a meaningful opportunity for presentation; neither speculation that more evidence might help nor the fact of a termination motion requires a new hearing. Unsupported prospective duties must end, but termination erases neither completed determinations nor payments or other lawful historical effects. It ends the judicial obligations themselves, rather than merely shifting enforcement of identical obligations to another court. An independent State-law agreement or accrued claim requires its own legal basis.

## Standards and Tests selection: Present legal change and prospective relief from an adjudicated injunction

### Present legal change and prospective relief from an adjudicated injunction

**Governing question:** Rule 60(b)(5) relief when this Court expressly removes a continuing adjudicated injunction's constitutional premise.

**Current rule:** When this Court expressly removes the constitutional premise supporting a continuing adjudicated injunction, that present change is a significant change in law under Rule 60(b)(5), and continued categorical enforcement on the displaced premise is inequitable. The Court may apply its ruling in the pending review of denied prospective relief without requiring a second motion merely to invoke the rule it has just announced; lower courts remain bound by directly controlling precedent until this Court changes it.

**Present operation effective:** Agostini v. Felton, June 23, 1997.

**Recurring remedy:** On remand the district court must remove the injunction's prohibition of the described secular Title I service insofar as that prohibition rests on religious location, presumed inculcation from location or ordinary supervision of public personnel; if no independently supported legal basis sustains the onsite prohibition, it must be dissolved. ## Standards and Tests selection: Official graduation worship and independent religious pressure

### Official graduation worship and independent religious pressure

**Governing question:** Officially arranged graduation worship and independently coercive school prayer.

**Current rule:** Officials violate the Establishment Clause by selecting clergy, requesting invocations and benedictions, guiding their religious content and incorporating the worship into the official ceremony. Nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure that sponsorship; no separate punishment or quantified-pressure showing is required for this ground. Independently, school-directed clergy prayer is forbidden when the school's control and the occasion's importance place objecting adolescents under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the ceremony. Optional attendance and standing or remaining silent do not necessarily remove that pressure; mere offense is insufficient. The official-sponsorship prohibition also applies when a school organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event but uses student initiation or delivery or a statutory disclaimer. Those features alone do not make the worship private, and the sponsorship ground remains independent of quantified pressure. The official-worship prohibition also reaches a public university’s selection of a religious leader and incorporation of actual invocation and benediction prayers into commencement. Mature attendance, meaningful freedom to abstain, brevity, nonsectarian language and a sincere secular ceremonial purpose do not cure that official worship; independent clergy composition does not make it private forum speech. Separately, actual pressure to participate in school-directed worship in compulsory or comparably pressured settings violates the Establishment Clause; offense at a genuinely private viewpoint alone does not suffice.

## Standards and Tests selection: Revocation of existing parole-like community liberty

### Revocation of existing parole-like community liberty

**Governing question:** Termination of a prisoner's existing release to ordinary community life under parole-like conditions.

**Current rule:** When a State releases a prisoner to live at home, work and conduct ordinary community life under parole-like supervision, ending that existing conditional liberty requires Morrissey's preliminary and final protections. Retained legal custody, an overcrowding purpose, the preparole label or a separate formal-parole decision does not eliminate the interest. A constitutionally adequate determination must precede final revocation; a completed final hearing need not invariably precede initial arrest or detention.

**Authority by component:**

- Young v. Harper — Part II — Existing parole-like community liberty requires protection, 1997-03-18; Part II joined by all nine supplies the actual-liberty trigger
- Young v. Harper — Part III — Morrissey’s ordinary stages and qualifications govern, 1997-03-18; Part III joined by all nine supplies the process and its distinct exceptions

**Present operation effective:** Young v. Harper, 1997-03-18.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** When the participant is arrested and detained pending revocation, a reasonably prompt inquiry at or reasonably near the place of the alleged violation or arrest determines probable cause or reasonable ground for detention for a release-condition violation. A person not directly involved in the case decides; that person need not be a judge and may be a different parole officer. The participant receives notice of the inquiry, its purpose and the alleged violations, may appear and speak, and may present relevant documents and witnesses. On request he may question an adverse informant in his presence. If the officer determines that identifying the informant would expose that person to harm, confrontation and cross-examination of that informant may be withheld. The officer summarizes the proceedings and states the reasons and evidence supporting the determination; formal judicial findings are unnecessary. Supported probable cause permits continued detention and institutional return pending final decision.

The State must offer a final revocation hearing, if desired, within a reasonable time after custody and before effective final revocation. It must resolve contested relevant facts and whether the circumstances warrant revocation, allowing denial and mitigation. Minimum protections are written notice of the claimed violations or asserted grounds; disclosure of adverse evidence; an opportunity to be heard personally and present witnesses and documents; confrontation and cross-examination unless the officer specifically finds good cause to withhold them; a neutral and detached decisionmaker, whose members need not be judges or lawyers; and a written statement of the evidence relied on and reasons.

Informal evidence, including letters and affidavits, may be considered. Issues already determined adversely in another forum, including a relevant criminal conviction, need not be relitigated. Morrissey’s discussion of two months establishes no universal deadline or safe harbor. Preliminary informant risk and final-stage specific good cause are distinct exceptions, each permitting rather than requiring withholding upon the necessary finding. The separate counsel question remains undecided.

**Exceptions and limits:** Initial parole eligibility, complete state-program entitlement and independently lawful custody grounds remain distinct. The decision supplies no new evidentiary burden or automatic-release consequence.
