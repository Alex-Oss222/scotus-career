## Standards and Tests selection: Former drug-possession probation revocation and the imprisonment range

### Former drug-possession probation revocation and the imprisonment range

**Governing question:** How former §3565(a)’s mandatory drug-possession proviso measures replacement imprisonment in the ordinary zero-to-six-month Guidelines setting.

**Current rule:** For controlled-substance possession triggering the former proviso, with an initially available zero-to-six-month Guidelines imprisonment range and no established lawful departure, revocation requires imprisonment of at least one-third of that range’s six-month maximum and no more than that maximum. Probation months are not equivalent prison months. After ordinary interpretation, genuine residual uncertainty independently defeats the larger probation-to-prison conversion; disagreement alone is not ambiguity and lenity is no universal lowest-number rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded range construction: United States v. Granderson — The former proviso requires two to six months’ imprisonment on this record, 1994-03-22. Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; six Justices support this complete construction, independently of Scalia's and Kennedy's judgment concurrences.
- Independent residual-lenity rejection of larger punishment: United States v. Granderson — Residual penal uncertainty defeats the larger probation-to-prison conversion, 1994-03-22. Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; the same six Justices adopt this independently sufficient reason to reject the Government's twenty-month construction.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Granderson, 1994-03-22.

**Open question:** Materially different initial ranges and downward-departure situations require their own lawful calculation. No denominator for those situations, supervised-release rule or later statutory amendment is supplied.

**Holdings navigation:** The former proviso requires two to six months’ imprisonment on this record.

## Standards and Tests selection: Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration

### Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration

**Governing question:** Whether a later law may withdraw mandatory discretionary-parole reconsideration for an earlier offender by doubling hearing intervals or permitting individualized postponement for up to three years.

**Current rule:** The Ex Post Facto Clause forbids applying to a pre-enactment offender a later statute that removes every alternate mandatory parole-reconsideration opportunity by doubling the post-denial interval from twelve to twenty-four months, where ordinary parole depends on consideration and no retained ordinary means of obtaining intervening review is established. That binding loss of a meaningful release opportunity makes punishment more onerous despite unchanged initial eligibility, maximum sentence and ultimate release discretion; the prisoner need not prove the omitted hearing would have produced release. Relief restores the consideration opportunity, not an entitlement to parole. An individualized initial prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that prediction do not preserve later mandatory opportunities to present rehabilitation, changed circumstances or a materially mistaken forecast. Where the actual three-year deferral removes meaningful annual consideration while parole is legally available and no ordinary substitute is established, it increases punishment. The prisoner must show the earlier entitlement, retrospective actual withdrawal and a concrete connection to a legally available avenue to earlier release; inconvenience alone is insufficient. Once shown, the State must substantiate a substitute it invokes through its legal basis, practical availability and timely capacity to provide that consideration. The prisoner retains the ultimate burden of proving an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

**Authority by component:**

- Mandatory annual-to-biennial withdrawal, proof and relief boundary: Cavanaugh v. Roller — The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record, 1993-11-30. Part III of O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter; six Justices adopt the rule, its application to Roller, and the prospective remedy. Ginsburg's conditional agreement in her separate opinion adds no vote to this controlling coalition.

- The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here: The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here, 1995-04-25. Part II of O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the same rule, application and relief.
- The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes: The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes, 1995-04-25. Part III of O’Connor’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg; five Justices expressly adopt this bounded evidentiary direction and its application. Recognition of a source limitation in the dissent is not an additional join.

**Present operation effective:** Morales, April 25, 1995, for the bounded individualized-deferral application and claimant/substitute substantiation rule; Cavanaugh retains its original scope.

**Open question:** Materially different individualized arrangements and demonstrated interim review remain unresolved. No automatic ban on every frequency reduction, universal probability formula, numerical response deadline or general presumption shifting ultimate constitutional proof is adopted. Changed suitability or voting rules, parole abolition and extraordinary clemency are not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** The binding loss of annual reconsideration increases punishment on this record; The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here; The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

### Statutory temporal reach and new consequences for completed conduct

**Governing question:** How courts decide whether new legislation governs events preceding enactment.

**Current rule:** First determine whether Congress prescribed the statute’s temporal reach. If it did, that direction governs subject to independent constitutional limits. Otherwise ask whether applying the provision would attach new legal consequences to completed events by impairing existing rights, increasing liability for past conduct or imposing new duties concerning completed transactions. Such operation requires clear congressional intent. Pending litigation alone does not answer the question. Procedural, jurisdictional and prospective-relief labels are not substitutes for examining the provision’s actual operation. Legislative correction of a judicial construction, or a purpose to restore an earlier understanding, does not by itself establish retrospective reach; this legislative inquiry differs from the temporal operation of judicial decisions.

**Authority by component:**

- Sequential temporal inquiry: Landgraf v. USI Film Products — Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s general-framework portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this organizing rule. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur on a distinct relevant-activity approach, not as additional joins at this level of generality.
- Corrective purpose and distinction from judicial interpretation: Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc — Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach, 1994-04-26. Stevens’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls by five votes at this explanatory level. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept the judicial/legislative distinction through their separate textual method, not through a presumed full join.

**Present operation effective:** Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc, 1994-04-26.

**Holdings navigation:** Determine statutory temporal reach before applying the presumption against new retrospective consequences; Corrective legislative purpose does not itself give an amendment retrospective reach.

**Exceptions and limits:** An express congressional direction concerning the temporal operation of a statute resolves the threshold statutory question, subject to applicable constitutional limits. Such a direction does not alone determine whether Congress may constitutionally require reconsideration of an already completed Article III judgment. Landgraf concerns statutory consequences for earlier conduct in litigation properly before the courts and does not decide that separate final-judgment issue.

**Related current rules:** Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments supplies Morgan Stanley's and Plaut's separate Article III rule for §27A(b).

### Probation revocation and lenity

#### United States v. Granderson, 511 U.S. 39

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1662.

**Decided:** 1994-03-22.

##### The former proviso requires two to six months’ imprisonment on this record

**Holding:** When controlled-substance possession triggers former §3565(a)'s mandatory probation-revocation proviso on an ordinary record with an initially available zero-to-six-month Guidelines imprisonment range and no established lawful departure, the replacement imprisonment term must be at least one-third of the range's six-month maximum and no more than that maximum: two to six months here. The sixty months of probation actually imposed do not become sixty equivalent prison months; materially different original ranges and downward-departure situations are not resolved by this calculation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; six Justices support this complete construction, independently of Scalia's and Kennedy's judgment concurrences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. R. L. C., 503 U.S. 291 (1992): its recognition of a lawful Guidelines ceiling informs the distinct revocation context; its juvenile minimum and interpretive divisions are not transplanted.
- Williams v. United States, 503 U.S. 193 (1992): preserved for actual lawful departure grounds; no departure is invented to sustain twenty months.
- Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36 (1993): preserved; commentary cannot override a federal statute or resolve the proviso by contrary text.

**Independent alternative holding:** After examination of the former proviso's text, sentencing structure and companion enactment, any remaining genuine uncertainty about the Government's proposed conversion of sixty probation months into a twenty-month prison minimum must be resolved against that greater punishment. This residual-lenity ground independently rejects the Government's construction; it neither makes every competing reading ambiguous nor supplies a universal lowest-number rule for different sentencing records.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; the same six Justices adopt this independently sufficient reason to reject the Government's twenty-month construction.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336 (1971): applied for fair warning and clear legislative authorization of punishment.
- Bifulco v. United States, 447 U.S. 381 (1980): applied to genuine uncertainty about the authorized penal consequence.
- Chapman v. United States, 500 U.S. 453 (1991): preserved; interpretive disagreement alone does not activate lenity.
- Ratzlaf v. United States, January 11, 1994: its residual-lenity principle is applied to this distinct statutory ambiguity, without importing its offense-specific knowledge requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materially different original ranges and probation following a substantial downward departure remain unresolved, including the denominator and any distinct lenity question. An actual lawful departure is necessary; no later statutory amendment or probation-to-prison exchange rate is applied.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit is affirmed, 8–1. Its vacatur of twenty months' imprisonment and release order remain operative, and revocation remains intact. No new custodial sentencing, renewed probation, substitute fine or separate supervised-release change is ordered; the unrevoked fine's payment status is undecided.

### Conditional community release and revocation

#### Young v. Harper

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1598.
**Decided:** March 18, 1997.

##### Whether preparole community freedom is protected existing liberty

**Holding:** A prisoner released to live at home, work and participate in ordinary community life under parole-like conditions possesses conditional liberty protected by Morrissey. Legal custody, the preparole label, an overcrowding purpose or denial of formal parole does not dispense with revocation process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Parts II–IV, unanimous.

**Material application:** The August 1991 procedure cannot by itself establish a condition communicated at the October 1990 release or validate the March 1991 telephone return order. Silence in the earlier procedure does not establish all release conditions, prove compliance or make release irrevocable.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Five months is not a constitutional minimum. Formal parole entitlement, short furloughs, work details, ordinary institutional transfers and the later procedure’s prospective validity are not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Morrissey protects freedom already conferred; Greenholtz concerns initial release and Meachum ordinary internal transfer. Sandin preserves direct constitutional liberty alongside its sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement route; no exhaustive program-entitlement finding is required.

##### Which preliminary and final safeguards govern withdrawal of that liberty

**Holding:** Revocation of this community liberty requires Morrissey’s preliminary and final protections, with their distinct qualifications. Initial arrest and detention need not await a completed final hearing, but final revocation must rest on constitutionally adequate process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part III, unanimous.

**Material application:** For arrest and detention pending revocation, a reasonably prompt inquiry at or reasonably near the alleged violation or arrest determines probable cause or reasonable ground to detain for a release-condition violation. The decisionmaker must be uninvolved, but may be a different parole officer rather than a judge. Notice must identify the inquiry, its purpose and alleged violations; the participant may appear, speak, present relevant documents and witnesses and, on request, question adverse informants in person. An officer’s finding that identification would expose an informant to harm permits withholding confrontation of that informant. A summary must state the evidence and reasons without formal judicial findings; supported probable cause permits detention and institutional return pending final decision.

The State must offer a final hearing if desired, within a reasonable time after custody and before effective final revocation, to resolve disputed relevant facts and whether circumstances justify revocation, including denial and mitigation. Minimum safeguards are written notice of violations or grounds, disclosure of adverse evidence, a personal opportunity to present witnesses and documents, confrontation and cross-examination unless specifically withheld for good cause, a neutral and detached decisionmaker, and written evidence and reasons. The decisionmaker need not be a judge or lawyer. Informal letters and affidavits may be considered; matters already decided adversely in another forum, including a relevant conviction, need not be retried.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Preliminary informant-danger and final-hearing good-cause exceptions are separate permissions, not commands to withhold confrontation. No exception, waiver, dispositive admission, prior determination or supported probable-cause decision is established here. Morrissey’s two-month discussion establishes no universal deadline or safe harbor; no new evidentiary burden, automatic-release rule or separate counsel holding is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Morrissey’s flexible administrative safeguards govern in full, without converting revocation into a criminal trial or replacing it with a telephone instruction.

##### What relief follows from the failure to provide revocation process

**Holding:** The Tenth Circuit correctly reversed denial of habeas relief because this termination required Morrissey process. Oklahoma may still establish a legally available revocation ground through adequate procedures or rely on an independent lawful basis for custody.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Breyer), Part IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The procedural violation proves neither sentence expiration, formal-parole entitlement nor compliance with every possible condition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirmance returns the case for proceedings under the protected-liberty and process holdings; it orders no unconditional release, release date or new state deadline. Permissible interim detention remains subject to the preliminary and final safeguards.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lynce supplies no unproved exhaustion defect or waiver; Gonzales’s concurrency construction supplies no authority for summary revocation.

For the eight-Justice accepted-rehearing holding in Young v. Harper, No. 95-1598, decided March 18, 1997, see Federal Courts, “Accepted rehearing and certiorari finality.”

### Garner v. Jones — 2000-03-28

[Record](../records/garner-v-jones_merits_2000-03-28.md)

#### Event and question

Garner v. Jones, No. 99-137. Merits decision, March 28, 2000. Argued January 11, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, reviewing Jones v. Garner, 164 F.3d 589, which reversed summary judgment for the parole officials and remanded. The question is whether Georgia may apply Jones's eight-year reconsideration deferral retrospectively in place of his offense-date three-year opportunity, given the asserted expedited-review mechanism.

Jones received life sentences for murders committed in 1974 and, after escape, 1982. The offense-date rule supplied initial consideration after seven years and reconsideration after denial at three-year intervals. The later rule permits intervals no longer than eight years. Following consideration in 1989, 1992 and 1995, the Board denied parole in 1995 and set reconsideration for August 2003, citing the nature and circumstances of the offenses and multiple offenses. Those facts establish neither suitability for release nor legal impossibility of earlier parole.

The Board's 1996 policy permits a deferral up to eight years when parole cannot reasonably be expected in the intervening years. Eligible life-sentence cases, including cases involving parole later revoked, may receive expedited review when changed circumstances or new information warrant earlier consideration. Members must indicate the interval when voting to deny parole. A custodian's affidavit describes full individual review, an individualized prediction and an existing expedited procedure. The regulation separately requires reasons for denial while protecting confidential sources and potentially discouraging diagnostic opinions.

**Authority:** Both holdings are supplied by Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Seven directly join this application of controlling Morales. Stevens and Ginsburg agree with the governing opportunity protection but do not join the vacatur disposition or its justification.

#### Jones has shown the meaningful reconsideration opportunity withdrawn

**Holding:** Jones's actual eight-year deferral retrospectively removed the offense-date three-year occasion for consideration while parole remained legally available, satisfying Morales's initial showing of a meaningful lost opportunity. He need not prove that the omitted occasion would have produced release; the original prediction of continued unsuitability does not itself preserve consideration of later rehabilitation, changed circumstances or a materially mistaken prediction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Morales governs this withdrawal. That decision requires an earlier legal entitlement, its actual retrospective removal, and a concrete connection to legally available earlier release; the prisoner retains ultimate constitutional proof. Cavanaugh protects the meaningful consideration opportunity, not a promise of favorable parole. Weaver explains why a legally available avenue to reduced custody can matter despite discretion over the outcome, and Miller rejects unchanged outer sentencing limits as a complete answer. Collins confines the inquiry to punishment rather than every procedural disadvantage. Jones's multiple murders, escape and repeated adverse assessments bear on suitability, but do not establish that earlier release was legally impossible. The Court does not decide suitability, impose an actual-release probability requirement or invalidate every longer interval. It applies Morales to the established individual withdrawal and turns separately to the asserted substitute.

#### The asserted expedited procedure requires assessment under the actual substitute rule

**Holding:** A written discretionary policy and sworn description of an existing expedited-review procedure must be assessed for legal basis, practical availability and timely capacity to provide the consideration withdrawn; neither the discretionary wording alone nor an assertion that the procedure exists decides its adequacy. Because the appellate court rejected the arrangement on a broader categorical premise and the district court applied a different Morales premise, the judgment must be vacated for the proper inquiry under ordinary procedure.

**Controlling explanation:** Morales requires the State to substantiate a replacement it invokes without shifting the prisoner's ultimate burden. Georgia supplies a policy and actual affidavit evidence, which materially differ from an advocate's hypothetical power to advance review. Courts must consider that evidence and supported practice. But an assertion that expedited review exists does not itself establish an ordinarily usable route and timely consideration as a matter of law. Conversely, the policy's permission to advance review does not establish that such a route is impossible simply because it is discretionary or lacks an invariably enforceable hearing demand. The Court rejects both shortcuts. The remand preserves Jones's established initial showing and does not replace it with a requirement to prove likely release. It guarantees no opportunity to cure every evidentiary failure, orders no administrative study or automatic discovery, and prescribes no filing form or response deadline. If an adequate substitute is not established, the relief restores timely consideration under lawful rules while leaving the Board's lawful suitability judgment intact.

#### Precedent treatment

- California Department of Corrections v. Morales governs the actual opportunity and substitute inquiries. Its original authority, burden allocation and consideration-only remedy remain; this decision addresses the materially different Georgia arrangement it left open.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller retains protection against the binding loss of a meaningful avenue to earlier release without proof of the omitted hearing's result.
- Weaver v. Graham supplies the distinction between a legally available opportunity and a guaranteed favorable outcome; theoretical discretion does not establish a compensating substitute.
- Miller v. Florida supplies scrutiny of binding adverse operation despite unchanged sentence ceilings or a potentially identical outcome.
- Collins v. Youngblood preserves the punishment boundary and supplies no categorical exemption for parole procedure.

#### Resulting law and limits

Morales continues to govern retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole consideration. Actual discretionary practices may establish a materially adequate replacement, but their practical availability and timely capacity require support. The Court establishes neither a general significant-risk substitute for that inquiry nor a rule invalidating every discretionary or longer reconsideration arrangement.

#### Source and record limits

[Jones v. Garner, 164 F.3d 589](https://static.case.law/f3d/164/html/0589-01.html) supplies the lower judgment, record history and policy account. The custodian's affidavit and Board instruments appear in the [record and briefs](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0323). The affidavit establishes an assertion of an existing procedure, not an adjudicated finding that the procedure is ordinarily available and timely. No request by Jones for expedited review, rejection of such a request, new rehabilitation finding or statewide operational rate is established. The policy permits expedited review; it does not guarantee a hearing on every request or a response within a fixed period.
