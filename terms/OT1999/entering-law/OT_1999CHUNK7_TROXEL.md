### Severe parental-contact restrictions

#### A. St. P. C. v. B. C.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-7810; judgment below, 643 So. 2d 743 (La. 1994).
**Decided:** November 20, 1995.

##### Proof required before La. Rev. Stat. 9:364(D)'s complete-contact cutoff

**Holding:** Clear and convincing evidence of a parent's sexual abuse of his or her child or children is required before §9:364(D)'s severe restriction may begin. Bare preponderance cannot trigger mandatory loss of all visitation and contact until an adversarial hearing establishes both successful completion of a sexual-abuser program and that supervised visitation serves the children's best interests; the statutory treatment requires evaluation and psychotherapy specifically designed for family-violence perpetrators and conducted by licensed mental-health professionals.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes.

**Material application:** The earlier bare-preponderance finding, entered without notice of these additional consequences, cannot supply the necessary predicate for this complete, potentially indefinite exclusion followed only by conditional supervised contact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides neither abuse nor nonabuse and requires no clear-and-convincing finding for every investigation, emergency intervention, temporary separation or lesser supported restriction. Those measures remain governed by their own requirements. No universal visitation proof rule, periodic-review schedule, restoration procedure, treatment-admission condition or fee ruling is created.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the state judgment on the federal proof-burden ground, 5–4, for lawful state implementation, including any available conforming construction. No contact or treatment is ordered. The independent state separation-of-powers ruling against mandatory greater weight for the abused child's qualifying therapist remains undisturbed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mathews governs the actual loss and error risks; Santosky's error-allocation method extends to this severe statutory consequence while its termination holding survives. Foucha and Heller inform the analysis by analogy without transferring their distinct confinement rules.

### Child-custody implementation and personal security

#### Baby Richard, by his guardian ad litem, v. Kirchner (In re Petition of Kirchner)

**Docket or dockets:** Illinois Supreme Court No. 78101; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 21, 1996.

##### Whether a child has an independent liberty interest in the manner of compulsory custody transfer

**Holding:** When the State abruptly severs an established caregiving relationship to implement an otherwise lawful custody transfer, the child has an independent liberty interest against serious, avoidable injury from the manner of that compulsory implementation. That interest permits a claim directed to concrete transition harm and practicable means of avoiding it; it confers no right to retain an invalid adoption, remain indefinitely with nonparents or defeat a fit father's custody entitlement through a best-interests comparison.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Part I, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices.

**Material application:** The interest concerns personal security during affirmative state enforcement. Years of caregiving identify the relationship but neither prove serious injury nor establish permanent custody rights. Neither state action nor lawful custody entitlement alone resolves an actual implementation injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary grief, a preferred-home comparison and unsupported delay do not establish the protected injury. The adoption’s invalidation and the fit father’s lawful custody entitlement remain fixed. This §1257 review is not barred by Ankenbrandt’s limited diversity exception.

##### What opportunity and relief the supported implementation claim requires

**Holding:** Before compulsory implementation in these circumstances, the child's representative must have a meaningful, prompt opportunity to present concrete evidence of serious transition harm and practicable measures to avoid it, and the father must have an opportunity to respond; the state court must consider that issue under Mathews and give a reasoned implementation decision. Procedures and any protective measures must fit the actual showing and preserve the father's entitlement: unsupported requests may be rejected, immediate implementation may follow adequate consideration, and no automatic evidentiary hearing, fixed delay or renewed custody contest is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Material application:** Mathews applies after identification of the bounded liberty. Competent evidence must concern actual circumstances; the father may contest it. Expedition protects both his entitlement and the child’s need for a settled home. A supported brief transition may be permissible when necessary to avoid the serious harm shown; it is not mandatory.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic full trial, clinician, recurring professional approval, visit count, special proof burden, fixed delay or permanent nonparent contact is required. Independently lawful emergency protection remains possible; no emergency or actual injury is found.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated in part and remanded, 5–4. The categorical federal rejection and inconsistent forthwith requirement are removed only to permit meaningful, prompt consideration before compulsory transfer. The state court may reject an unsupported proffer or order immediate transfer after adequate consideration; no fixed Supreme Court stay or residence period.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Smith v. Organization of Foster Families for Equality & Reform, 431 U.S. 816:** Preserves its assumed-interest analysis and natural-family qualification. Its recognition of caregiving ties informs this new implementation rule but establishes no right to defeat the fit father.
- **Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S. 584:** Applies the principle that the child has an independent liberty interest and that process fits the actual decision; distinguishes its institutional-admission interest and procedures.
- **Stanley v. Illinois, 405 U.S. 645, and Lehr v. Robertson, 463 U.S. 248:** Preserve parental relationship and responsibility protections; the implementation inquiry cannot reopen Kirchner's lawful entitlement.
- **Santosky v. Kramer, 455 U.S. 745:** Preserves its termination and error-allocation principles without importing its proof burden or establishing a right to retain this adoption.
- **Michael H. v. Gerald D., 491 U.S. 110:** Preserves its reservation concerning a child's independent filial claim; its rejection of two legal fathers does not resolve these implementation safeguards.
- **Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319:** Applies only after the bounded liberty interest is identified; requires consideration of that interest, error risk and procedural value, and governmental burdens.
- **Reno v. Flores, March 23, 1993:** Distinguishes the sustained temporary immigration-custody scheme and its actual review avenues. Its facial holdings establish neither this transition rule nor categorical immunity from individual implementation claims.
- **A. St. P. C. v. B. C., November 20, 1995:** Uses its attention to the specific deprivation while preserving its distinct clear-and-convincing rule for Louisiana's complete-contact cutoff.
- **Foucha v. Louisiana, May 18, 1992, and Heller v. Doe, June 24, 1993:** Preserve their separate confinement and family-participation rules; neither provides a child-transition proof burden or hearing schedule.
- **DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services, 489 U.S. 189, and Collins v. Harker Heights, February 26, 1992:** Preserve the absence of a general protective-services or safe-environment guarantee and distinguish the present challenge to affirmative compelled implementation.
- **Pinder v. Johnson, May 21, 1996:** Preserves the distinction between private nonprotection and attributable affirmative deprivation and its reserved constitutional merits. Its immunity judgment does not decide this prospective implementation claim.
- **Ankenbrandt v. Richards, June 15, 1992:** Preserves its limited exception to diversity jurisdiction; it does not bar section 1257 review of this decided federal claim.

### Parental liberty and compelled school presentations

#### Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** First Circuit No. 95-1275.
**Decided:** February 24, 1997.

##### The pleaded compulsory exposure does not establish the asserted parental-liberty deprivation

**Holding:** Parental liberty protects choice among lawful educational paths and parents' own instruction; it does not give each parent authority to prescribe every public-school lesson reasonably related to a genuine pedagogical objective. Accepting the alleged compulsory exposure and aftermath, this complaint establishes no displacement of family custody, educational choice or parental teaching that supports the requested substantive federal veto; the ruling does not approve pretextual curricular action or decide independent state-law permission rights.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Required attendance is accepted rather than treated as voluntary. Public-school enrollment does not waive parental teaching. Breach of the written permission policy may implicate independent law but does not alone create this substantive federal veto.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meyer and Pierce protect educational choice and parental instruction. Curtis's optional-program holding expressly reserved compulsory instruction, so these allegations receive independent analysis. Settle requires genuine pedagogy and does not validate a pretextual educational label.

##### These allegations establish neither the claimed student privacy deprivation nor extreme official abuse

**Holding:** Compulsory exposure to the alleged presentation and subsequent coarse imitation does not, on this complaint, establish the students' asserted decisional-privacy or bodily-integrity deprivation or extreme abuse of official power. The conclusion rests on the actual allegations, including the absence of compelled personal performance, affirmation, intimate disclosure, physical contact or treatment; it is no categorical rule that words, a single event or compulsory educational programming can never violate the Constitution.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Material application:** The complaint and favorable inferences include compulsory attendance and weeks of coarse imitation; neither student is alleged to have performed a skit, affirmed a message, disclosed intimate information, engaged in sexual or religious conduct or undergone bodily intrusion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision establishes no rule that speech, a single event or compelled educational programming can never violate protected interests. Compelled profession, personal participation, bodily intrusion, establishment, targeting and independently proved discrimination remain distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Curtis and Settle retain their qualifications and provide no general school immunity or answer to different personal coercion.

##### The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex

**Holding:** Title IX requires discrimination, exclusion or a hostile educational condition imposed because of sex; sexual subject matter and offense alone do not establish that relationship. The complaint connects the alleged later hostility to disagreement with the presentation's message rather than the plaintiffs' sex, so this element's absence requires dismissal without deciding severity or pervasiveness, a comprehensive recipient-liability rule, or the treatment of materially different sex-based targeting.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A mixed-sex audience is not a categorical defense. Personal targeting, physical contact, psychological injury, academic loss and multiple episodes are not new indispensable elements. Severity/pervasiveness, recipient attribution, deliberate indifference and different sex-specific humiliation remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The three granted federal dismissals are affirmed without discovery, amendment, trial, damages or remand. Distinct procedural-due-process, Free Exercise, RFRA, prospective-declaratory and state-law dispositions are undisturbed and receive no new controlling merits rationale.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harris and DeAngelis inform contextual analysis without added injury thresholds but do not transplant an entire employment-liability formula into Title IX. Franklin v. Gwinnett County preserves damages for proved intentional discrimination without supplying the missing because-of-sex element.

#### Elizabeth G. Curtis v. School Committee of Falmouth

**Docket or dockets:** Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court review; historical petition No. 95-617.
**Decided:** January 21, 1997.

**Holding:** The described school condom-availability program does not violate parental liberty or familial privacy merely because parents cannot require the school to exclude their children or notify them of participation. **Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), unanimous.

High-school recipients may obtain them from the nurse or purchase them at restroom machines; counseling is optional. Program entry is voluntary, and the stated terms require neither affirmation nor conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Summary judgment stands on the granted constitutional claims. The asserted parental deprivation supplies no constitutional exclusion or notice requirement. No injunction, new evidence proceeding or prescribed procedure follows; voluntary accommodation and independent state consent or disclosure law remain open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Meyer, Pierce, Yoder and Prince protect parental direction without requiring this school-enforced veto. A. St. P. ## Canonical source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md ? Washington v. Glucksberg

#### Washington v. Glucksberg

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-110.

**Decided:** June 26, 1997.

##### Whether competent terminally ill adults have the asserted fundamental right to willing physician assistance in suicide

**Holding:** The Due Process Clause does not establish a fundamental right for a competent, terminally ill adult to obtain a willing physician's intentional assistance in suicide through a lethal prescription. The asserted class-wide entitlement therefore receives no heightened protection; a materially different, more particularized claim is not foreclosed merely by this disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five direct rationale votes. The four other Justices concur only in the judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No exclusive most-specific-history methodology governs all liberties. Forced treatment, interference with valid refusal or directives, denial of lawful pain care, punishment of good-faith palliation merely for foreseeable death and discriminatory administration are not decided. The reservation of a materially different particularized claim recognizes no new entitlement or pending action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cruzan's informed-consent foundation, assumed competent-refusal premise and protection of evidence concerning an incompetent patient remain intact; intentional lethal assistance is not their necessary extension. Casey retains individualized viability, life-and-health protection and its entire six-step framework. Mazurek retains complete purpose and residual-interference review; neither abortion decision supplies a universal medical-liberty test.

##### Whether Washington's prohibition rationally serves legitimate protective interests

**Holding:** Because the asserted assistance entitlement is not fundamental, Washington's prohibition is valid against this substantive-due-process challenge if it is rationally related to legitimate state interests. It meets that standard by protecting life, preventing suicide, guarding vulnerable people against error and pressure, maintaining medical responsibility and containing the risks of a legalized lethal practice; the Ninth Circuit's class-wide invalidation cannot stand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five direct rationale votes. The four other Justices concur only in the judgment.

**Material application:** Risk of error, pressure, abuse of vulnerable patients and extension of legalized lethal practice supplies the rational connection. Neither a finding that every request is involuntary nor proof that prohibition is the best policy is necessary; the existence of proposed safeguards does not compel their adoption.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lawful palliation is not a finding that all suffering is actually relieved. No incapacity or misconduct of a named party is found. The ruling does not constitutionally forbid legislative authorization and regulation of assisted death.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit, 9–0, and remand with directions to enter judgment for Washington on the asserted due-process entitlement. No patient-specific hearing or prescription authority is ordered.

### Treatment refusal, suicide assistance and class-wide constitutional claims

**Governing question:** Substantive due process and equal protection of intentional physician assistance in suicide for competent terminally ill adults.

**Current rule:** The Due Process Clause does not establish the asserted class-wide fundamental right to a willing physician's intentional assistance in suicide through a lethal prescription. A prohibition therefore receives rational-basis review on that claim, and may rationally protect life, prevent suicide, guard vulnerable people against error and pressure, preserve medical responsibility and contain risks of a legalized lethal practice. Equal protection permits a rational distinction between competent refusal of unwanted treatment and intentional suicide assistance based on bodily consent, legal causation, intent and professional responsibility. Death as a shared ultimate result and the practical option available to life-support-dependent patients do not compel permission for lethal assistance. Good-faith palliation may foresee a shorter life without adopting death as its purpose; labels or a patient's wish alone do not settle purpose or causation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Protection against serious avoidable harm in compulsory custody transitions

### Protection against serious avoidable harm in compulsory custody transitions

**Governing question:** The manner of state-compelled severance of an established caregiving relationship to implement otherwise lawful custody entitlement.

**Current rule:** The child has an independent liberty interest against serious, avoidable injury attributable to the manner of compulsory implementation. Before implementation, the child's representative must receive a meaningful, prompt opportunity to present concrete evidence of that transition harm and practicable means of avoiding it; the entitled parent must be able to respond, and the court must consider the issue under Mathews and give a reasoned implementation decision. Mathews requires attention to the identified private interest, error risk and value of further procedures, and governmental interests and burdens. Procedures and protective measures must fit the actual showing, proceed promptly and preserve lawful parental custody entitlement.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded child interest: I. The child's own interest limits the manner of compulsory implementation; fitted process and relief: II. The required opportunity and relief must fit the actual transition claim, 1996-06-21; Stone-Zsela Parts I–II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Baby Richard v. Kirchner, 1996-06-21.

**Exceptions and limits:** Caregiving duration does not itself prove serious injury. No right to preserve an invalid adoption, defeat a fit parent through a best-interests comparison, or remain indefinitely with nonparents follows. Unsupported requests may be rejected and adequate consideration may support immediate implementation. There is no automatic evidentiary hearing, fixed delay, appointed-clinician requirement, prescribed proof burden or renewed custody contest. Independently lawful emergency protection remains available.
