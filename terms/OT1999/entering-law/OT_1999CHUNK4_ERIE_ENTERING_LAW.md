## Standards and Tests selection: Visibility of protected nonobscene cabaret expression

### Visibility of protected nonobscene cabaret expression

**Governing question:** Whether a city may prohibit protected cabaret performances or their images solely because they can be seen from outside.

**Current rule:** A city may not suppress protected nonobscene adult-cabaret performances or images solely because listed anatomy is visible outside. Limiting the prohibition to cabaret entertainment does not make its communicative effect a secondary effect or establish obscenity, minor-obscenity, captive-home intrusion or actual-conduct conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- Ino Ino Holding 4: eight-Justice protected-image rule: Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue — controlling Holdings, 1998-09-28.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-09-28.

**Exceptions and limits:** Authoritative narrowing or a nonobscene-work exception in one separate ordinance provision does not supply a missing condition in another. Independent lawful public-nudity, obscenity, trespass and neutral-sign restrictions retain their own operation.

**Holdings navigation:** Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue — Independent state licensing protection and federal review; Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue — Fee eligibility and limits on defensive fee shifting; Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue — Cabaret operational rules, narrowed overbreadth and visible images.

## Standards and Tests selection: Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

### Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

**Governing question:** Whether an individual former employee's challenge to an employment rule remains live after departure.

**Current rule:** A prospective individual challenge becomes moot after voluntary departure from covered employment when no intent to return, realistic recurrence or surviving claim is established. A general prayer cannot preserve the controversy by producing nominal damages against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom §1983 does not make a damages person. Consent to payment cannot create the missing cause of action, and certification of an unsettled state-law question cannot supply missing Article III jurisdiction.

**Authority by component:**

- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — A continuing personal controversy is absent, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the continuing-controversy and unavailable-damages limits
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — Certification cannot restore the controversy, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the certification boundary

**Present operation effective:** Arizonans for Official English, 1997-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is no categorical express-pleading requirement for nominal damages and does not decide a legally available personal-capacity damages claim. Limited authority to correct lower-court jurisdictional error is distinct from merits authority; judgment vacatur remains equitable under the existing rule. No automatic initiative-sponsor standing or mandatory certification rule is established.

**Holdings navigation:** Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona (1997-03-03) — Federal Courts / Mootness, corrective jurisdiction and equitable vacatur.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

### Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

**Governing question:** Whether mootness from voluntary settlement warrants vacating a judgment lawfully brought for review.

**Current rule:** Subsequent mootness prevents a merits decision but leaves authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of the judgment and proceeding. Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not establish entitlement to vacatur: the movant must supply a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances can warrant relief, but a settlement term requiring vacatur is not itself such a circumstance. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not command vacatur whenever review becomes moot.

**Authority by component:**

- Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment: Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment, 1994-11-08. Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.
- Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur: Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur, 1994-11-08. Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Present operation effective:** 1994-11-08; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** No heightened proof quantum or exhaustive list of exceptional circumstances is adopted. This settlement rule does not turn loss of review through circumstances outside the parties’ control into automatic vacatur.

**Holdings navigation:** Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment; Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur.

#### Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue

**Docket or dockets:** 97-858; Washington Nos. 62941-2, 62939-1 and 62940-4.

**Decided:** 1998-09-28.

##### The supported operational rules regulate conduct while preserving expression

**Holding:** These actual spacing, stage, lighting and hours requirements survive federal review because they serve substantial contact-prevention and detection interests unrelated to suppressing the message, are sufficiently fitted to those interests and preserve meaningful expressive performance. Their exact customer, measurement, dimensional and time limits control; neither commercial operation nor erotic content removes the expression from First Amendment protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Kennedy's Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct rationale votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Bellevue City Code §5.08.070(A)(6), as authoritatively construed, requires four feet between a nonstage performer and that performer's customer, measured between bodies rather than outstretched arms; it does not require separation from every person in the room. Section (A)(1) permits exposure of the female breast below the top of the areola, or the pubic region, anus, buttocks, vulva or genitals only on a stage at least eighteen inches above the immediate floor and at least eight feet from the nearest public patron. Section (D)(2) requires sufficient, equally distributed lighting throughout public areas so objects remain plainly visible, with thirty lux measured horizontally thirty inches above the floor on ten-foot centers, not at every possible point. Section (E) closes the cabaret to public operation from 2 a.m. to 10 a.m., leaving sixteen daily hours.

**Material application:** United States v. O'Brien requires governmental authority, an important or substantial interest, a purpose unrelated to expressive suppression, and an incidental burden no greater than essential to that interest. Ward v. Rock Against Racism requires genuine fit and sufficient remaining channels without a universal least-restrictive-means test. Barnes v. Glen Theatre recognizes the expressive interest through distinct opinions; no single Barnes rationale authorizes dispensing with scrutiny of every cabaret-specific restriction. City of Renton v. Playtime Theatres permits attention to actual secondary effects, not hostility to the communication itself.

The supported contact incidents and visibility difficulties justify the actual body-to-body four-foot rule and elevated stage eight feet from patrons. A six-foot stage separation allowed a tall patron to reach a dancer. The specified lighting measurements constrain enforcement discretion while aiding observation. The hours rule addresses the late-night movement of patrons from closing bars and retains sixteen daily hours of performance. These provisions regulate the physical setting; the Court rejects the lower court's rational-basis-only account of stage dancing.

The record does not establish that these bounded rules eliminate practical expression. A business has no guaranteed profit margin, but proof that regulation destroys meaningful channels would matter; inevitable closure is not the exclusive measure of speech injury. The rejected forecast cannot be assessed as though the law required eight or nine feet between table-dance bodies. Madsen and Schenck require attention to each burden but address injunctions; their heightened remedial standard is not silently imposed on all legislation. No categorical immunity, morality-only justification or national distance safe harbor follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- O'Brien supplies the operative expressive-conduct requirements.
- Ward supplies fitted manner regulation without universal least restrictive means.
- Barnes preserves expression and its actual divided rationales; it is not converted into categorical cabaret immunity or no-scrutiny treatment.
- Renton applies noncommunicative secondary-effects analysis without immunizing censorship.
- Madsen v. Women's Health Center preserves its provision-specific speech analysis and distinct injunction standard.
- Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York preserves context-specific fit without making all distances unconstitutional or valid.

##### The narrowed definition and conduct provisions survive substantial-overbreadth review

**Holding:** The presented overbreadth challenge fails as to the adult-entertainment definition and dance-distance provisions when their authoritative state constructions and nonobscene-work exceptions are applied. Section .070(A)(5) is sustained only as confined to obscene conduct through the state's construction of (F); that construction supplies no missing exception in (B)(2).

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Kennedy's Part III; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on this component.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The adult-entertainment definition, §5.08.010(B), covers performances with specified anatomical exposure, predominant emphasis on sexual activities, or intended sexual stimulation conducted regularly or as a substantial part of the premises' activity. The regular-or-substantial condition belongs to the third branch, not all three. Section .070(F) preserves nonobscene plays, operas, musicals and other dramatic works; nonobscene classes, seminars and lectures held for serious scientific or educational purposes; and nonobscene exhibitions, performances, expressions and dances. Its stated exception concerns the sexual conduct described in RCW 7.48A.010(2)(b)(ii)–(iii): masturbation, oral-genital acts, bestiality, excretory functions, lewd genital exhibition, and violent or destructive sexual acts including mutilation, dismemberment, rape or torture. The Washington court construes the interaction with (A)(5) to prohibit only obscene conduct, not those subjects irrespective of obscenity. The incorporated obscenity criteria require prurient appeal under contemporary community standards, the specified patently offensive depiction or description, and absence of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value when the work and its context are considered as a whole.

**Material application:** Broadrick v. Oklahoma requires real and substantial overbreadth compared with a conduct regulation's legitimate sweep; New York v. Ferber preserves the protected-expression inquiry. The definition's hypothetical occasional or revue applications do not establish substantial overbreadth on the presented record of the live activity studied. The four-foot rule applies to the performer's customer rather than every member of the public. Section (F) protects the three stated nonobscene categories, and the Washington court reads its sexual-conduct exclusion with the statutory obscenity criteria rather than as an exception for all nonobscene depictions of the listed acts. That controlling state construction defeats the asserted nonobscene reach of (A)(5). The Court does not strike the defined live-entertainment category merely because every conceivable venue has not been separately studied. It equally does not endorse a categorical rule that every adult-entertainment definition is valid. The independent outdoor prohibition has a different actual scope and receives its own analysis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Broadrick applies substantial overbreadth to these conduct provisions at their construed scope.
- Ferber preserves examination of protected expression; it supplies no general adult-cabaret exemption.

##### Visibility alone cannot justify the protected-image prohibition

**Holding:** Section .070(B)(2) is invalid insofar as it prohibits protected nonobscene adult-cabaret performances or their images from being visible outside solely because the listed anatomy appears. Restricting the subject to cabaret entertainment does not convert its communicative effect into a secondary effect or supply missing obscenity, minor-obscenity, captive-home intrusion or actual-conduct conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). Kennedy's Part IV, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section .070(B)(2) separately prohibits a performance, photograph, drawing, sketch or other pictorial representation displaying breasts below the top of the areola, pubic hair, buttocks, genitals or anus from being visible outside the cabaret. Its state construction limits it to adult-cabaret performances and their images; it does not add an obscenity or material-obscene-as-to-minors condition. The (A)(5) construction is not transferred to (B)(2).

**Material application:** Erznoznik v. City of Jacksonville protects public displays from broad suppression merely because unwilling viewers can see them. Madsen preserves that principle even in its distinct injunction setting: distress at a visible image is not itself a sufficient ground for banning it. The City invokes children and other passersby, but (B)(2) contains no material-obscene-as-to-minors definition and no captive-home predicate. A picture of protected performance is representation, not the sexual contact addressed by the operational rules. Limiting the ban to cabaret performances does not cure that defect. The state construction of (A)(5) cannot be silently inserted into a different provision. Independent lawful public-nudity, obscenity, trespass and neutral-sign restrictions retain their own operation. State courts must determine severability and conforming relief; the Court writes no replacement display code and authorizes no violation of independently valid rules.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Erznoznik applies protection against broadly suppressing images visible to unwilling passersby.
- Madsen preserves its observable-images principle without exporting the entire injunction test.
- Renton is distinguished: the prohibited visibility is the communication itself, not an independently demonstrated secondary harm.

Related questions in Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue, 1998-09-28, dockets 97-858; Washington Nos. 62941-2, 62939-1 and 62940-4, appear under Federal Courts — Independent state licensing protection and federal review; Civil Rights — Fee eligibility and limits on defensive fee shifting.

#### Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement, 505 U.S. 123 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-538.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether the County's demonstration-fee mechanism is facially invalid for unbounded discretion or audience-hostility pricing

**Holding:** A licensing rule directed at protected demonstrations is facially invalid when neither its text, authoritative construction, nor established administration supplies narrow, objective, definite standards controlling whether and how much officials charge. A challenger need not prove discriminatory administration of its own application, and a maximum fee does not supply the missing standards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela separately concurs in the judgment on compatible grounds and does not formally join that opinion.

**Material application:** The administrator may select whether to charge, which time and support costs to include and whether to discount them without a binding method or explanation requirement. A $1,000 cap does not constrain those choices, and the finding that the particular $100 assessment reflected administrative work does not cure the facial defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Different fees alone do not prove invalidity. Authoritative narrowing construction and established administration may supply constraints; presumed benign discretion does not. Concrete indigency, fixed neutral fees, particular content-independent costs and municipal funding duties remain undecided; separate City and School Board claims are outside this review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm facial relief against enforcement of the County fee mechanism. The County may enact lawful neutral coordination and sufficiently bounded cost rules. No damages, refund amount, fee schedule or mandatory municipal spending formula is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): retained and distinguished; its authoritative nondiscrimination construction supports flexible lawful fees, not unbounded discretion.
- Shuttlesworth v. Birmingham, 394 U.S. 147 (1969): applied; expressive licensing requires narrow, objective, definite standards.
- Niemotko v. Maryland, 340 U.S. 268 (1951): applied; officials licensing protected expression must act under definite standards.
- City of Lakewood v. Plain Dealer Publishing Co., 486 U.S. 750 (1988): applied; the risk inherent in unbounded expressive licensing supports facial review despite one unobjectionable application.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applied as to authoritative constructions and actual administration; a binding narrowing construction matters, a presumed benign discretion does not.

**Independent alternative holding:** A permit fee that varies with anticipated hostile reaction to a demonstration is content based and violates the First Amendment when justified by recovering the costs of protecting speakers from that reaction. Neither calling those expenses a secondary effect nor imposing a small numerical ceiling makes the charge constitutional.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: an independently sufficient five-Justice holding. Stone-Zsela's separate concurrence agrees with the rule without a formal join.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** Audience-hostility pricing independently sustains the same facial protection against enforcement. It does not find the actual $100 assessment retaliatory, fix police expenditures or forbid genuinely content-independent administrative charges. The following precedent treatment applies to this alternative ground:

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applied; the justification must be independent of the message.
- Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988): applied at its supported content-classification level; listeners' hostile responses are not neutral secondary effects.
- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): distinguished and retained; coordination costs do not include a message-dependent hostility premium.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991 simulated decision: consistent application of the prohibition on unjustified content-based financial burdens; its separate remedial limits are unchanged.
- Terminiello v. Chicago, 337 U.S. 1 (1949): applied in its protection against suppressing speech because of hostile response; no new police-protection entitlement is inferred.

##### Whether the First Amendment imposes a universal nominal-fee ceiling or makes a small fee necessarily valid

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not make a demonstration fee invalid merely because it exceeds a nominal amount, or valid merely because it is small. Cox's allowance for lawful, nondiscriminatory regulatory cost recovery survives; a fee's lawful purpose, content neutrality, and bounded administration govern the defects presented here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, independently controls both rejections of numerical shortcuts. White's dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, and Stone's separate concurrence also reject a universal nominal ceiling; no judgment-only position is converted into a full-opinion join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No permissible dollar amount or comprehensive schedule of recoverable costs is fixed. A fee exceeding a nominal amount must still have a lawful purpose, neutral criteria and bounded administration.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court rejects the court of appeals' amount-only reasoning while affirming the facial relief on the independently sufficient discretion and hostility grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): retained; flexible, nondiscriminatory regulatory fees are not limited to nominal amounts.
- Murdock v. Pennsylvania, 319 U.S. 105 (1943): distinguished; its nominal-fee reference does not establish an amount-only rule or displace Cox.

#### Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-974.
**Decided:** March 3, 1997.

##### Whether this former employee retains a live challenge to the employment rule

**Holding:** An individual prospective employment-rule challenge becomes moot when the employee voluntarily leaves covered employment without an intention to return, realistic recurrence or a surviving claim. A general relief request cannot manufacture nominal damages against a State or official-capacity defendant that is not a damages person under §1983.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous; all nine Justices join every holding.

**Material application:** No class or replacement claimant remained, and the dismissed individual-capacity claims were not preserved on appeal. State nonpersonhood under §1983 is a statutory bar distinct from waivable Eleventh Amendment immunity; a proposed consensual payment cannot supply the missing cause of action. The Attorney General’s constitutional participation under §2403(b) did not overturn the denial of State party status.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general requirement to plead nominal damages by name is established. A legally available individual-capacity claim presents a different question. The Court does not fix the precise April 1990 date when mootness attached.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Preiser requires a controversy throughout review. Will and Hafer distinguish the unavailable State or official-capacity damages claim from genuine individual-capacity liability.

##### Whether limited corrective jurisdiction permits vacatur without deciding sponsor standing or constitutional merits

**Holding:** The Court may correct a lower federal court’s jurisdictional error without exercising constitutional merits jurisdiction. On the equities here, that authority requires vacating the appellate judgment and directing vacatur of the District Court declaration and dismissal for mootness, without resolving ultimate initiative-sponsor standing.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The prevailing plaintiff ended the covered employment during review efforts; this was not settlement or abandonment by a reviewing loser. The later appellate merits adjudication and absence of a lawful damages residue justify clearing both unreviewable constitutional judgments.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 2106 does not independently create merits jurisdiction. Intervention, an appeal notice or political commitment does not confer automatic sponsor standing. The original declaration is not declared void when entered, and the Court resolves neither every intervention ruling nor Article XXVIII’s validity.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate both merits judgments and dismiss the action as moot.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bender and Corrick supply limited correction of jurisdictional error. Lujan and Brown Group retain constitutional injury and lawful representation requirements. Bancorp requires equitable rather than automatic vacatur; Munsingwear and Anderson support judgment clearing within that inquiry.

##### Whether state-law certification can revive a moot federal action

**Holding:** Certification of unsettled state law can assist a live federal controversy but cannot cure the absence of Article III jurisdiction. No certification is ordered in this moot case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The meaning of Article XXVIII and any constitutional challenge in future live litigation remain open. No mandatory certification requirement is adopted.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Federal review leaves independent Washington protection intact

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court may review the adverse, determined federal questions under §1257 while leaving the independently sufficient state constitutional manager-license invalidation intact. The collateral fee accounting does not reopen the underlying federal merits or authorize this Court to correct Washington's own constitutional rule.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Michigan v. Long preserves an adequate independent state ground. The manager-license ruling expressly rests on Washington's greater protection against prior restraints and supports relief without a federal determination. The Court neither restores that fourteen-day licensing gap nor decides that federal law independently invalidates or permits every such interval. FW/PBS, Inc. v. City of Dallas retains its separate federal procedural requirements. Other adverse federal rulings remain reviewable; a state-law discussion does not insulate them. The state court has resolved those merits, leaving collateral fee accounting rather than the unresolved underlying merits presented in Jefferson v. City of Tarrant. Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith concerned coextensive state reasoning and supplies no power to revise this independent state protection. State privacy, postjudgment evidence and amendment rulings are not independently reviewed as federal claims.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Long applies the adequate-independent-ground boundary.
- FW/PBS retains its federal licensing rules without deciding the independently protected manager interval here.
- Jefferson is distinguished because underlying merits, rather than merely collateral fees, remained unresolved there.
- Florida v. Smith preserves federal review where state reasoning is coextensive; that premise is absent from the manager-license ruling.

##### 2. The supported operational rules regulate conduct while preserving expression

**Holding and operative rule:** These actual spacing, stage, lighting and hours requirements survive federal review because they serve substantial contact-prevention and detection interests unrelated to suppressing the message, are sufficiently fitted to those interests and preserve meaningful expressive performance. Their exact customer, measurement, dimensional and time limits control; neither commercial operation nor erotic content removes the expression from First Amendment protection.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. O'Brien requires governmental authority, an important or substantial interest, a purpose unrelated to expressive suppression, and an incidental burden no greater than essential to that interest. Ward v. Rock Against Racism requires genuine fit and sufficient remaining channels without a universal least-restrictive-means test. Barnes v. Glen Theatre recognizes the expressive interest through distinct opinions; no single Barnes rationale authorizes dispensing with scrutiny of every cabaret-specific restriction. City of Renton v. Playtime Theatres permits attention to actual secondary effects, not hostility to the communication itself.

The supported contact incidents and visibility difficulties justify the actual body-to-body four-foot rule and elevated stage eight feet from patrons. A six-foot stage separation allowed a tall patron to reach a dancer. The specified lighting measurements constrain enforcement discretion while aiding observation. The hours rule addresses the late-night movement of patrons from closing bars and retains sixteen daily hours of performance. These provisions regulate the physical setting; the Court rejects the lower court's rational-basis-only account of stage dancing.

The record does not establish that these bounded rules eliminate practical expression. A business has no guaranteed profit margin, but proof that regulation destroys meaningful channels would matter; inevitable closure is not the exclusive measure of speech injury. The rejected forecast cannot be assessed as though the law required eight or nine feet between table-dance bodies. Madsen and Schenck require attention to each burden but address injunctions; their heightened remedial standard is not silently imposed on all legislation. No categorical immunity, morality-only justification or national distance safe harbor follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- O'Brien supplies the operative expressive-conduct requirements.
- Ward supplies fitted manner regulation without universal least restrictive means.
- Barnes preserves expression and its actual divided rationales; it is not converted into categorical cabaret immunity or no-scrutiny treatment.
- Renton applies noncommunicative secondary-effects analysis without immunizing censorship.
- Madsen v. Women's Health Center preserves its provision-specific speech analysis and distinct injunction standard.
- Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York preserves context-specific fit without making all distances unconstitutional or valid.

##### 3. The narrowed definition and conduct provisions survive substantial-overbreadth review

**Holding and operative rule:** The presented overbreadth challenge fails as to the adult-entertainment definition and dance-distance provisions when their authoritative state constructions and nonobscene-work exceptions are applied. Section .070(A)(5) is sustained only as confined to obscene conduct through the state's construction of (F); that construction supplies no missing exception in (B)(2).

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on this component.

**Controlling explanation:** Broadrick v. Oklahoma requires real and substantial overbreadth compared with a conduct regulation's legitimate sweep; New York v. Ferber preserves the protected-expression inquiry. The definition's hypothetical occasional or revue applications do not establish substantial overbreadth on the presented record of the live activity studied. The four-foot rule applies to the performer's customer rather than every member of the public. Section (F) protects the three stated nonobscene categories, and the Washington court reads its sexual-conduct exclusion with the statutory obscenity criteria rather than as an exception for all nonobscene depictions of the listed acts. That controlling state construction defeats the asserted nonobscene reach of (A)(5). The Court does not strike the defined live-entertainment category merely because every conceivable venue has not been separately studied. It equally does not endorse a categorical rule that every adult-entertainment definition is valid. The independent outdoor prohibition has a different actual scope and receives its own analysis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Broadrick applies substantial overbreadth to these conduct provisions at their construed scope.
- Ferber preserves examination of protected expression; it supplies no general adult-cabaret exemption.

##### 4. Visibility alone cannot justify the protected-image prohibition

**Holding and operative rule:** Section .070(B)(2) is invalid insofar as it prohibits protected nonobscene adult-cabaret performances or their images from being visible outside solely because the listed anatomy appears. Restricting the subject to cabaret entertainment does not convert its communicative effect into a secondary effect or supply missing obscenity, minor-obscenity, captive-home intrusion or actual-conduct conditions.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part IV, joined by all seven other Associates; eight direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Controlling explanation:** Erznoznik v. City of Jacksonville protects public displays from broad suppression merely because unwilling viewers can see them. Madsen preserves that principle even in its distinct injunction setting: distress at a visible image is not itself a sufficient ground for banning it. The City invokes children and other passersby, but (B)(2) contains no material-obscene-as-to-minors definition and no captive-home predicate. A picture of protected performance is representation, not the sexual contact addressed by the operational rules. Limiting the ban to cabaret performances does not cure that defect. The state construction of (A)(5) cannot be silently inserted into a different provision. Independent lawful public-nudity, obscenity, trespass and neutral-sign restrictions retain their own operation. State courts must determine severability and conforming relief; the Court writes no replacement display code and authorizes no violation of independently valid rules.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Erznoznik applies protection against broadly suppressing images visible to unwilling passersby.
- Madsen preserves its observable-images principle without exporting the entire injunction test.
- Renton is distinguished: the prohibited visibility is the communication itself, not an independently demonstrated secondary harm.

##### 5. Plaintiffs' fee eligibility must be considered under the actual federal result

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical denial of plaintiffs' §1988 fees must be vacated for consideration of their actual federal success and the distinct eligibility and reasonable-amount inquiries. State-law success alone does not automatically establish entitlement, but it also does not categorically defeat fees where an available substantial, fee-eligible federal claim supports an award.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part V.A; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins this rule whatever federal success the Court's judgment supplies.

**Controlling explanation:** Farrar v. Hobby distinguishes enforceable success from the reasonable amount of fees; it does not guarantee a substantial award. National Private Truck Council, Inc. v. Oklahoma Tax Commission preserves Maher v. Gagne's qualified rule that an available substantial, fee-eligible federal claim may support fees without an express federal merits decision. The Washington court's categorical statement cannot replace that inquiry, and the present reversal of the protected-image prohibition supplies actual federal success that must be considered. Hensley v. Eckerhart requires attention to actual results and unrelated unsuccessful work. No fee total is fixed, no nominal damages are awarded, and not every unsuccessful challenge becomes compensable. The manager-license remedy remains state law; eligibility is not created solely because expressive activity was involved.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Farrar preserves eligibility separately from reasonable amount, including a permissible reduction to zero on the appropriate assessment.
- National Private Truck Council preserves the need for an available fee-eligible federal claim and rejects a categorical state-success exclusion.
- Maher preserves possible fees through an available substantial, fee-eligible federal claim without an express federal merits decision; state-law success alone neither establishes entitlement nor bars the award.
- Hensley governs actual success and unrelated unsuccessful work in determining a reasonable amount.

##### 6. State equity cannot evade federal limits on defense-fee shifting

**Holding and operative rule:** A municipality cannot recover the expense of defending a substantial, nonfrivolous §1983 request merely because interim restraint was dissolved, by describing those defense fees as state equitable damages. The City's award is vacated to that extent for separation of federal defense expenses from any independently permissible state-claim expenses or actual procedural misconduct.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part V.B; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; nine direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Christiansburg Garment Co. v. EEOC and Hughes v. Rowe protect nonfrivolous civil-rights claims from automatic prevailing-defendant defense fees. A rule designed to deter plaintiffs from seeking preliminary protection cannot circumvent that limit by changing the award's label. The mere decision to request interim relief is not misconduct. The Court does not erase every state equitable basis, decide an unpresented bond-enforcement claim, or treat the failure to post the disputed bond as itself resolving the federal issue. The state court must identify the legal source and attributable work. Its refusal to award the City's later appellate defense fees remains distinct from the remanded dissolution accounting. No fixed award, federal fee entitlement for the City or automatic recovery of every defense expense follows.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Christiansburg applies its limitation on prevailing-defendant fees without making loss of interim relief sufficient.
- Hughes carries that protection into §1983 litigation; state-law labels do not evade it.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Michigan v. Long preserves independent state constitutional protection; FW/PBS retains its distinct federal licensing safeguards.
- Jefferson v. City of Tarrant is distinguished because unresolved underlying merits differ from this collateral fee accounting. Libertarian Party of Florida v. Smith's coextensive state reasoning does not authorize review of this independent Washington ground.
- United States v. O'Brien and Ward v. Rock Against Racism supply fitted expressive-conduct and manner scrutiny without a universal least-restrictive-means rule.
- Barnes v. Glen Theatre retains its distinct rationales; cabaret-specific expression does not lose protection through a general-conduct label. City of Renton v. Playtime Theatres permits regulation of actual noncommunicative effects, not offense at images.

#### Law After Decision

The supported operational restrictions remain enforceable at their actual customer, distance, stage, light-measurement and hours terms. The Court applies ordinary expressive-conduct and manner scrutiny; no categorical exemption for adult-cabaret regulation, guaranteed profit margin or exclusive inevitable-closure threshold results. The narrowed overbreadth judgment remains, while (B)(2)'s suppression of protected nonobscene expression cannot survive merely because visibility is outside the club. State courts retain responsibility for severability and lawful conforming relief.

Independent Washington manager-license protection remains state law. Federal fee eligibility is distinct from amount: neither state success nor dissolution of interim relief automatically determines the two fee disputes. No general state equitable remedy is abolished, and no bond-enforcement question is decided. Existing precedents retain the qualifications stated in the holdings; none is overruled.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Washington Supreme Court judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part, and vacated in part. On remand the state courts must conform outdoor-display relief to the protected-expression holding and determine severability; reassess plaintiffs' §1988 eligibility and reasonable fees; and separate the City's permissible state-law expenses or actual-misconduct expenses from impermissible defense-fee shifting for the substantial, nonfrivolous federal request.
