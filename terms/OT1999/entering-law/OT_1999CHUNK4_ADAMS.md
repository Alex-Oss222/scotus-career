## Standards and Tests selection: RFRA review of prospective enforcement

### RFRA review of prospective enforcement

**Governing question:** Original RFRA within its surviving valid field, including threatened enforcement of preexisting laws and commercial religious exercise.

**Current rule:** Within a valid field of RFRA operation, the claimant must establish a substantial burden on religious exercise. The government must then produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The inquiry applies to threatened future implementation of a preexisting law. Commercial participation does not forfeit its protection, and labeling a law antidiscrimination does not itself justify its application. Direct transactional and dignitary injury and legally material alternatives or exemptions may be considered; availability of alternative housing is not an automatic answer to direct injury.

RFRA also governs later federal enactments unless Congress expressly excludes application by reference to RFRA; FACE contains no such exclusion.

RFRA's universal State and local command cannot rest on Fourteenth Amendment §5: its substitution of the substantial-burden/compelling-interest/least-restrictive-means standard for Smith across the field lacks the required congruent and proportional enforcement relationship. Prospective State and local RFRA claims relying only on that power cannot proceed after June 25, 1997. Completed judgments under Church of Scientology and Swanner retain finality; their independent grounds and statutory reasoning survive within a valid field of operation. Federal RFRA, including its later-federal-enactment rule, remains outside this power holding.

**Authority by component:**

- RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication: RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication, 1994-12-05. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: eight direct rationale votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that RFRA governs, but does not join the opinion or its disposition of these adverse determinations.
- RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws: RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws, 1995-03-27. Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five direct rationale and judgment votes. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer dissent from the vacatur. Their agreement that RFRA governs does not turn their distinct application into Court law.

- Subsequent federal enactments and separate prospective-enforcement inquiry: RFRA requires further adjudication of the prospective-enforcement claim, 1995-11-13; O’Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas (five). The claimant’s substantial-burden threshold and government production/persuasion sequence remain unchanged; the full own-minor FACE remedy exclusion must be considered as enacted, without presuming it is comparable to an outsider blockade or that an equivalent state remedy exists.
- State/local §5 invalidity and preservation of completed judgments: City of Boerne, City of Boerne v. Flores — II. RFRA's universal State and local command exceeds that enforcement power and City of Boerne v. Flores — III. The reserved power question limits future statutory applications without reopening completed judgments, Kennedy's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** City of Boerne, 1997-06-25, limits State/local operation; the earlier claimant-specific statutory mechanics survive within a valid field.

**Current wording authority:** American Life League, 1995-11-13, preserves the later-enactment condition and applies the existing person-specific inquiry; it changes no burden sequence.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary standing and appropriate-relief limits remain. RFRA protects religious belief and preserves the Establishment Clause; this statutory inquiry does not replace the constitutional free-exercise or establishment standards. A remand under it establishes neither an exemption nor failure of the government’s eventual showing.

**Holdings navigation:** RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication; RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws; American Life League v. Reno. City of Boerne v. Flores (1997-06-25) — II. RFRA's universal State and local command exceeds that enforcement power; City of Boerne v. Flores (1997-06-25) — III. The reserved power question limits future statutory applications without reopening completed judgments.

## Copied Holdings selection ? Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater

#### Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1062.
**Decided:** December 5, 1994.

##### RFRA governs future enforcement and requires application-specific adjudication

**Holding:** RFRA applies to the threatened future implementation of Clearwater's preexisting ordinance: the Church must establish a substantial burden on its religious exercise, after which Clearwater must produce evidence and persuade that applying that burden to this person furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The adverse prospective determinations concerning the identifying disclosures designated as section 100.03(1)(a), (b), (c), and (j), and the written-refund-policy requirement in section 100.05(1)(g), must therefore be vacated for that inquiry, with ordinary Article III standing and appropriate-relief limits preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), eight Justices; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Current-force modification:** City of Boerne v. Flores, decided June 25, 1997, prevents this prospective statutory application from governing future State or local RFRA claims resting only on Congress's Fourteenth Amendment §5 power. The completed judgment remains final; independent grounds and the statutory reasoning retain force within a valid field of operation. Boerne neither reopens the judgment nor decides federal RFRA or separate preserved claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994): applies the distinction between future enforcement and new legal consequences for completed conduct, consistently with RFRA's express temporal direction; neither Landgraf's general framework nor its distinct relevant-activity rationale is enlarged.
- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, 508 U.S. 520 (1993): preserves its objective religious-targeting, substantial secular-underinclusion, and strict-scrutiny holdings; the statutory remand neither supplies a purpose finding nor converts the enactment-history discussion into a controlling test.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel Village School District v. Grumet, 512 U.S. 687 (1994): preserves the distinct denominational-allocation and unequal particular-grant rules; accommodation does not cure an establishment violation, and RFRA does not decide that constitutional question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final substantial-burden or justification finding is made for the surviving identifying or written-policy requirements. The Court does not decide RFRA's constitutionality, retrospective monetary liability, a general exemption from civil fraud law, the disputed sectarian purpose, the precise Florida severability boundary, the unreviewed general anti-fraud clause, or unpreserved hardship-discretion and procedural objections. Section 7 leaves the Establishment Clause unaffected; section 6(c) supplies no authority to burden religious belief.

Section 100.05(1)(g) requires written refund terms and conditions when a refund representation is made, but does not prohibit a statement made in good faith at that time. It prescribes no substantive refund policy and does not incorporate §100.05(1)(f)'s separate written-request and willful sixty-day nonpayment conditions. No promise to offer refunds is required.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The adverse prospective identifying-disclosure and written-refund-policy determinations are vacated for RFRA adjudication. Existing relief favorable to the Church and the purpose/severability remand remain undisturbed, without adoption of the lower constitutional rationales or renewed enforcement of provisions set aside below. The written-policy inquiry retains the contemporaneous good-faith proviso.

**Later-authority backlinks:** City of Boerne v. Flores, June 25, 1997, resolves the reserved §5 power question and limits future State and local applications at that scope.

## Copied Holdings selection ? Swanner v. Anchorage Equal Rights Commission

#### Swanner v. Anchorage Equal Rights Commission

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-124.
**Decided:** March 27, 1995.

##### RFRA requires a distinct inquiry into this application of the housing laws

**Holding:** For prospective enforcement of the marital-status housing prohibitions against Swanner, Swanner must establish a substantial burden on his religious exercise; if he does, the government must produce evidence and persuade that applying the burden to him furthers a compelling governmental interest by the least restrictive means. The adverse federal prospective-enforcement determination is vacated for that inquiry because commercial participation and a general prohibition of discrimination do not by themselves complete it; no exemption or ultimate statutory justification is decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas: five Justices.

**Current-force modification:** City of Boerne v. Flores, decided June 25, 1997, prevents this prospective statutory application from governing future State or local RFRA claims resting only on Congress's Fourteenth Amendment §5 power. The completed judgment remains final; independent grounds and the statutory reasoning retain force within a valid field of operation. The reserved federal constitutional claim and the distinct state-law and due-process grounds are not extinguished; no retrospective reopening or recovery is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Church of Scientology Flag Service Organization, Inc. v. City of Clearwater, December 5, 1994: applies its prospective, claimant-specific RFRA inquiry and distinct burdens to the present housing enforcement; its reservations concerning RFRA's constitutionality and retrospective monetary liability remain.
- United States v. Lee, 455 U.S. 252: preserves its recognition that exemptions can impose religious limitations on third parties; commercial activity is not treated as categorical forfeiture under RFRA.
- Roberts v. United States Jaycees, 468 U.S. 609: recognizes the weight of protected access and injury from exclusion; it does not automatically decide justification under every antidiscrimination law.
- Employment Division v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872: its constitutional neutral-law rule does not replace the enacted RFRA inquiry; its constitutional holding remains unchanged.
- Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah, June 11, 1993: preserves objective neutrality, comparable secular harm, and strict-scrutiny rules without deciding the constitutional claim here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final substantial-burden or justification finding is made. The Anchorage ordinance's distinct individual-home exception applies when the renter would share common living areas with the owner, lessor, manager, agent, or other person; no finding places Swanner within it, and its legal comparability cannot be assumed either way. The Court neither invalidates the housing provisions nor ranks all marital-status protections. It does not decide religious teaching or advocacy, religious-mission housing, intimate owner-occupied arrangements, RFRA's constitutionality, or retrospective monetary liability. Alaska statutory construction, the state constitutional ruling, and separate notice/delegation due-process rulings are outside reconsideration. The federal constitutional exemption claim is reserved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The adverse federal prospective-enforcement determination is vacated and remanded for RFRA's separate burden and justification inquiries. No final exemption or constitutional free-exercise ruling is made. Alaska statutory, state-constitutional and separate notice/delegation rulings are not reconsidered; no retrospective monetary or penalty relief is adjudicated.

**Later-authority backlinks:** City of Boerne v. Flores, June 25, 1997, resolves the reserved §5 power question and limits future State and local applications at that scope.

## Copied Holdings selection — American Life League RFRA holding

##### Person-and-application adjudication under RFRA

**Holding:** The RFRA prospective-enforcement dismissal must be vacated. The district court must identify each live claimant and threatened application, determine a genuinely disputed substantial burden, and, if established, require the Government to produce evidence and persuade that applying the burden to that person furthers a compelling interest by the least restrictive means, considering concrete alternatives and legally comparable exceptions.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's RFRA opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from this remand.

**Material application:** General access, safety and health interests and successful intermediate speech scrutiny do not complete RFRA's application-specific showing. Reliable medical access and bodily safety carry compelling weight, and an accommodation may fail because it preserves the access injury; nonetheless, the complete parent/own-minor exclusion, even of preventive FACE injunctions, defeats an assumption of universal enforcement and requires actual comparison. Neither comparability with outsider blockades nor an equivalent state remedy is presumed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Religious burden is assumed for review of dismissal, not found; sincerity and doctrinal necessity are not adjudicated. Claimants bear substantial burden, then Government bears production and persuasion on justification; claimants have no universal duty to prove alternatives. No mandatory hearing whenever religion is asserted, entitlement to blockade or redirect patients, final exemption or invalidation follows. RFRA applies to later federal law absent express exclusion by reference to RFRA; FACE contains none. Article III standing, appropriate relief, belief protection and the Establishment Clause remain distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Remand only the RFRA prospective-enforcement claim. It may fail on the actual pleadings and a sufficient showing or at the substantial-burden threshold. No interim injunction follows automatically, and no new hearing or deadline is prescribed. The constitutional and categorical damages dismissals remain affirmed, each 9–0 in judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Clearwater and Swanner preserve the actual-person/application inquiry and burden sequence. Smith does not replace RFRA. Lee and Roberts retain the significance of direct injury without creating a categorical statutory exception.

The commerce and federalism holding is under Constitutional Structure, American Life League, November 13, 1995, Fourth Circuit No. 94-1869.

## Copied Holdings selection ? City of Boerne v. Flores

#### City of Boerne v. Flores

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-2074.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

For Congress's congruent-and-proportional enforcement authority and its limits, see City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074, decided 1997-06-25, Constitutional Structure — Fourteenth Amendment enforcement power.

##### II. RFRA's universal State and local command exceeds that enforcement power

**Holding:** RFRA's general application to State and local laws cannot be sustained under §5 because it substitutes its substantial-burden, compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means standard across the field for the constitutional boundary retained in Smith, without the required enforcement relationship. The Fifth Circuit's contrary power ruling is reversed, and the RFRA claim against the City must be dismissed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not reconsider Smith or decide the permit denial's neutrality, motive, substantial burden or justification. Federal RFRA, including its rule concerning later federal enactments, and other possible congressional powers remain outside the disposition.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's power ruling is reversed and remanded, 6–3, for dismissal of the RFRA claim against the City. Separately pleaded and preserved constitutional and state-law claims retain their own analysis; no permit, exemption, damages or new hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Smith remains the rule for genuinely neutral and generally applicable laws; Lukumi preserves objective-targeting and substantial-secular-underinclusion protections. Neither determines the City's actual conduct here.

##### III. The reserved power question limits future statutory applications without reopening completed judgments

**Holding:** Church of Scientology and Swanner did not decide the constitutional power question and therefore do not establish §5 authority for RFRA's universal State and local application. Their prospective statutory applications cannot govern future State and local RFRA claims resting only on that power after this decision, but their completed judgments retain finality and their independent grounds and statutory reasoning retain force within a valid field of operation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No blanket reopening, retrospective recovery or reconsideration of independent constitutional or state-law grounds follows. Particular transactional or remedial reliance claims require their own lawful source.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Church of Scientology and Swanner are limited prospectively in the invalid §5-based State and local field, preserving completed judgments, independent grounds and statutory reasoning where validly operative. Agostini retains Lemon as the governing Establishment Clause test and updates it into one inquiry with three routes: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives the attribution-and-function inquiry. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within that inquiry, not freestanding prongs or an alternative test. Its overruling of Aguilar's categorical onsite rule and displacement of Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale concern a different Establishment Clause setting and supply no RFRA power ruling. Plyler's prospective-relief rule does not authorize redefinition of substantive rights.

### Strout v. Albanese — 2000-02-28

[Record](../records/strout-v-albanese_merits_2000-02-28.md)

#### Event, question and statutory setting

**Strout v. Albanese**; review of 178 F.3d 57 (1st Cir. 1999). Certiorari granted November 1, 1999; argued January 24, 2000; decided February 28, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, affirming the District of Maine's judgment at 13 F. Supp. 2d 112.

The granted questions concern whether excluding otherwise eligible sectarian schools and their students from a generally available tuition-assistance program solely because the schools are religious violates free exercise, and whether establishment principles require, permit or forbid that exclusion when assistance reaches schools through independent parental choice. Three families in Minot, where no secondary school was maintained, sought ordinary St. Dominic's tuition. Their application concerns integrated sectarian instruction, not a separately identified secular service or expression forum. The case was resolved on cross-motions for summary judgment without a genuine dispute of material fact.

Section 2951 retains basic school approval, nonsectarian status in accordance with the First Amendment, incorporation under Maine or United States law, reporting and auditing under sections 2952 and 2953 and requirements adopted under section 2954. Schools with at least 60 percent publicly funded students, measured by the previous year's October and April average enrollment, must participate in statewide assessment. Upon a school unit's request, the private school must release copies of all student records for students transferring from that private school to the requesting unit. The repealed fourth condition is not restored. Section 5204(4) applies when the resident unit neither maintains a secondary school nor contracts for secondary-school privileges. It permits attendance at an approved private school, a public school in an adjoining unit accepting tuition students, or an approved school in another state or country, subject to receiving-school permission. The resident unit must pay tuition up to the legal rate. The Department's acceptance of schools' self-identification without a classification dispute is a lower-court finding about administration, not a statutory command.

#### Maine may refuse the integrated devotional tuition presented here

**Holding and operative rule:** The Religion Clauses permit Maine to refuse public payment for the integrated devotional instructional function presented by this ordinary-tuition application. Independent parental choice and neutral student eligibility do not themselves justify public purchase of demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Jackson requires attention to the instructional function purchased; Agostini applies that inquiry within the governing, unified Lemon test. The parents selected St. Dominic's, and the requested ordinary tuition of approximately $3,850 was below the $4,900 statutory ceiling. That distinguishes the amount formula from Jackson's provider operating-and-debt costs, but does not separate the devotional work in the integrated program for which payment is sought. Maine is not buying an identified interpreter, secular remedial service, or controlled printing benefit. Rosenberger preserves its independently directed publication program; it does not make every publicly financed activity a speech forum. The actual funded function therefore supports refusal of this tuition without prohibiting the families' private educational choice. Neither a school's religious name nor its affiliation alone establishes the character of its work. The Court does not approve a universal religious-provider exclusion, decide all voucher arrangements, or create a constitutional entitlement to every education subsidy.

#### Free exercise receives its own inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Refusing this public purchase of integrated devotional instruction does not, in this application, violate the Free Exercise Clause. The conclusion does not depend on whether attending the school is a central religious tenet and does not authorize religious hostility, exclusion from a neutral private-expression forum, or categorical rejection of genuinely severable secular work solely because its provider is religious.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Lukumi requires scrutiny of religious targeting; Shahar preserves an independent free-exercise inquiry and rejects theological compulsion as a prerequisite to sincere exercise. Calling a program secular cannot by itself answer that inquiry. Here the challenged benefit is public purchase of the ordinary integrated instructional package, and the State may refuse that financed devotional function while the parents remain free to choose it privately. The lower court's observation that no parent described attendance as a central tenet does not defeat the claim, and the Court does not adopt that reasoning. Agostini protects its separately administered secular service despite religious location, and the private forum decisions protect genuinely private religious expression on their actual terms. Those distinct relationships remain intact; this tuition request presents neither one. The Court therefore affirms on the actual financing ground rather than an affiliation-based presumption or an independent-prong Lemon test.

#### The affirmance is limited to the granted claims and this application

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment sustaining the challenged refusal of integrated tuition is affirmed within the granted Establishment and Free Exercise questions. The decision does not reopen the ungranted speech, equal-protection or parental-liberty claims, the unchallenged dismissals of local defendants, or distinct applications for reliably separated secular services.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The cross-motions were decided on an agreed absence of genuine material factual dispute. No remand is required to adjudicate a different benefit application not before the Court or to restructure the school. The statutory payment duty for eligible schools remains subject to its independent approval, receiving-school permission and tuition-rate conditions. Affirmance leaves the challenged denial in place; it awards no tuition, damages or recoupment, orders no transfer or school closure, and imposes no statewide funding cutoff.

#### Precedent treatment

- **Jackson v. Benson:** Applied to the actual integrated instructional function; its provider-cost formula is distinguished in amount, without making capped tuition sufficient to separate devotional work. Its limits preserving private benefits and secular services remain.
- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing unified Lemon inquiry and to the distinction between integrated devotional work and separately controlled secular instruction; no categorical inference follows from religious location.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues only through Agostini's unified inquiry; the former independent purpose, effect and entanglement prongs are not revived.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its controlled printing benefit for independently directed expression remains valid; that relationship does not determine the ordinary tuition purchase here.
- **Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah:** Its neutrality and religious-targeting inquiry is preserved; refusal of the demonstrated devotional purchase is not a license for hostile religious discrimination.
- **Shahar v. Bowers:** Applied to the independent free-exercise inquiry and the irrelevance of theological compulsion to sincere exercise.
- **Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind:** Its actual individual vocational-benefit relationship remains distinct; it supplies no automatic answer for integrated tuition.
- **Mueller v. Allen:** Its general tax-deduction relationship remains distinct and is not displaced by this application.
- **Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District:** Its antecedent-law remand supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding or general tuition entitlement.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its bounded private-expression and worship-access protection remains intact; forum access differs from purchasing integrated instruction.

#### Resulting law and limits

Effective February 28, 2000, Jackson's funded-function rule applies to the integrated ordinary-tuition application presented here despite the tuition cap and parental choice. Agostini's unified Lemon inquiry continues; its former independent prongs do not return. The separate Free Exercise inquiry rejects theological centrality as a prerequisite, but does not require public purchase of this devotional instructional function. Religious identity alone remains insufficient to establish the financing defect. Genuinely independent private benefits, neutral private-expression forums and reliably separated secular services retain their actual protections. The decision settles this application, not every permitted or required tuition arrangement.

The [First Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/178/f3d/57/strout-v-albanese) supplies the judgment under review; the petition reproduces both lower opinions and the historical statutory appendix. The stated grant, argument and decision dates are the case's governing merits calendar. No finding about the devotional content of every course or every religious provider is made. The Department's self-identification practice is described as a record finding, not enacted statutory text.

### Strout v. Albanese — 2000-02-28

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's application and emphasizes the distinction between paying for devotional instruction and allowing genuinely private religious expression. Parental designation does not turn a public expenditure into private money. At the same time, an otherwise eligible, genuinely severable secular service cannot be denied payment solely because its provider is religious, and religious expression in a genuinely neutral forum involving no governmental financing, control, selection or attribution must receive equal treatment. Agostini supplies the secular-service distinction; the private-forum decisions protect private expression. These qualifications preserve lawful separate relationships, not a general right to tuition. No such distinct service or forum application is before the Court.

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas.** They would reverse the exclusion as applied and remand for otherwise lawful eligibility and payment proceedings. The actual benefit is ordinary tuition, capped by statute and here below that cap, selected through independent parental choice at a private school. They distinguish that individual educational benefit from Jackson's provider operating-and-debt-cost formula, while maintaining their disagreement with Jackson's attribution analysis. Rosenberger protects genuinely independent private activity within an available benefit; Witters supports the distinction between a private beneficiary's choice and the State's devotional direction; Lukumi requires scrutiny of excluding religious choices from that benefit. Maine's public-substitute characterization does not, for these Justices, overcome the actual independent direction and capped tuition. They would not excuse sham parental choice, governmental devotional direction, or other independent statutory ineligibility, and would order no immediate subsidy or entitlement to every tuition program.

Supreme Court merits review of the granted Religion Clause questions is complete. The First Circuit's judgment remains in force at the stated scope. No new lower-court factfinding, statutory redesign or subsidy proceeding is directed by the affirmance.

### Baral v. United States — 2000-02-22

[Record](../records/baral-v-united-states_merits_2000-02-22.md)

#### Event, question and statutory setting

Baral v. United States, No. 98-1667. Merits decision; February 22, 2000. Argued January 18, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, 172 F.3d 918. Question: When are wage withholding and estimated income-tax remittances deemed paid for the refund amount lookback, and does later return filing or assessment change those dates?

#### Withholding and estimated income tax have their statutory payment dates

**Holding and operative rule:** For sections 6511 and 6512, income tax actually deducted and withheld under chapter 24 is deemed paid by the income recipient on the fifteenth day of the fourth month following the taxable year for which it is allowable as a section 31 credit; estimated income tax is deemed paid on the last day prescribed for filing that year's return without regard to filing extensions. A later return or assessment does not postpone either date.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices, supplies nine direct votes for each proposition; no fractured rationale is involved.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 6513(b)(1) expressly governs withholding and section 6513(b)(2) governs estimated income tax. Those provisions leave no role for assessment to create a different payment date. Section 6151 requires payment without assessment or notice and demand; sections 31 and 6315 likewise treat withholding and estimates as income-tax payments or credits, rather than independent taxes converted only upon assessment. Rosenman concerned an estate-tax remittance held in a suspense account as a deposit; that distinct arrangement does not override the specified income-tax rules here. The $4,104 withheld during 1988 and the $1,100 estimate remitted in January 1989 are consequently deemed paid April 15, 1989. The Court decides no rule for remittances outside section 6513.

#### A timely claim remains subject to the separate recoverable-amount ceiling

**Holding and operative rule:** For a claim within section 6511(a)'s return-based period, section 6511(b)(2)(A) limits a credit or refund to tax paid during the three years immediately preceding the claim plus any extension for filing the return. Compliance with the claim-filing period does not bring payments outside that window within the recoverable amount.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined in full by all eight Associate Justices, supplies nine direct votes for each proposition; no fractured rationale is involved.

**Controlling explanation:** Lundy separates the claim that selects a window from the payments the window reaches; Brockamp gives the amount ceiling independent force. This case involves an actual return and a claim whose section 6511(a) timeliness is undisputed, so it presents no need to decide that question or use Lundy's Tax Court hypothetical. Baral filed the return and credit claim June 1, 1993. Adding his four-month filing extension to the three-year lookback produces a window beginning February 1, 1990. Both deemed payments preceded it. The extension enlarges that window but does not move the estimated-tax date fixed without extensions by section 6513(b)(2). No part of the requested $1,175 credit is recoverable through this route. The denial rests on amount, not late filing of the claim; no incapacity, fraud, inducement, special-period or universal jurisdictional question is decided.

#### Precedent treatment

- Rosenman v. United States: distinguished as a suspense-account estate-tax deposit; its deposit rule survives for its proper setting.
- Commissioner v. Lundy: applied for the distinction between claim timing and payment lookback; its notice-date hypothetical does not govern this actual claim.
- United States v. Brockamp: applied for the independent recovery-amount ceiling; its reservation of genuine deposits is preserved.

#### Resulting law and limits

Sections 6513(b)(1) and (2) fix the payment dates of these withholding and estimated-income-tax remittances before application of the separate section 6511 amount window. A later return or assessment cannot restart those dates. Claim timeliness and recoverable amount remain separate; other remittance categories retain their own classification questions.
