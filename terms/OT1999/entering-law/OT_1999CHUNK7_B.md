## Standards and Tests selection: Jury determination of criminal materiality

### Jury determination of criminal materiality

**Governing question:** Allocation of materiality when it is an element of the charged offense.

**Current rule:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to decide materiality beyond a reasonable doubt when materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense. The judge states the statutory meaning and legal standard; describing its application as a mixed question cannot remove an element from the jury. Preserved materiality withdrawals and omissions in the financial-deception and tax instructions addressed in Neder are trial errors subject to the Government’s Chapman burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient but not exclusive: supported whole-record inferences may establish noncontribution only after examination of actual instructions, evidence they directed to the jury, required findings and material record conflicts. Strong, uncontested or overwhelming evidence alone is no automatic substitute where the omission prevents a reliable connection to the actual verdict. Review is count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts.

**Authority by component:**

- The jury decides application of the materiality element: The jury decides application of the materiality element, 1995-06-19. Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

- Neder v. United States — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements”, 1999-06-10; Thomas Part II, all nine Justices. Supplies application of jury allocation and preserved-error burden.
- Neder v. United States — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “III. Qualified review of the actual verdict”, 1999-06-10; Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Seven direct joins supply classification and qualified inference; Part V supplies the bounded count-specific remand requirement.

**Present operation effective:** Neder, June 10, 1999, for the identified materiality errors and qualified actual-verdict harmlessness; Gaudin’s jury-allocation rule remains effective June 19, 1995.

**Open question:** No universal omitted-element or constructive-amendment classification, no generic tax harmlessness shortcut and no no-contest waiver follows. Sullivan’s vitiated reasonable-doubt rule remains structural; Richardson’s distinct predicate-nonunanimity harmlessness reservation remains unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: Aggravated unlawful-reentry elements and authorized maximum

### Aggravated unlawful-reentry elements and authorized maximum

**Governing question:** When does 8 U.S.C. §1326(b)(2) authorize its higher unlawful-reentry maximum?

**Current rule:** The prior aggravated-felony conviction is an element of the aggravated offense and must appear in the federal indictment. Unless validly admitted as an element of the charged offense, it must be proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt. A factual admission to a prior conviction does not cure omission of that element from the indictment; a conviction charged only under subsection (a) authorizes no sentence above its two-year maximum.

**Authority by component:**

- Statutory element construction and ordinary indictment/jury consequences; five direct joins: Almendarez-Torres v. United States, No. 96-6839 — Section 1326(b)(2) requires an aggravated-offense allegation before its higher maximum may apply, 1998-03-24.

**Present operation effective:** Almendarez-Torres v. United States, 1998-03-24.

**Exceptions and limits:** No majority adopts a general constitutional rule covering every fact that increases a maximum. Findings selecting punishment within existing conviction-based authority remain a distinct inquiry.

**Holdings navigation:** Almendarez-Torres v. United States — Aggravated reentry and the charged offense.

## Standards and Tests selection: Original armed carjacking and cumulative firearm punishment

### Original armed carjacking and cumulative firearm punishment

**Governing question:** Fault and punishment under the offense-date versions of §§2119 and 924(c).

**Current rule:** Original §2119 requires knowing armed participation in taking or attempting to take the covered vehicle from another’s person or presence by force and violence or intimidation. Aiding and abetting requires intentional assistance, not presence, association or awareness alone. The Government must prove the enacted vehicle-history nexus; no profit motive, interstate-resale purpose, knowledge of federal law or later intent-to-kill-or-seriously-injure element is added. Offense-date §924(c) expressly authorizes an additional consecutive term even though firearm possession is part of the original carjacking definition, but use or carrying during and in relation to the qualifying predicate must independently be established. The original §2119 creates aggravated offenses when serious bodily injury or death results: injury is an element of the twenty-five-year grade, death an element of the life-imprisonment grade, and the basic offense has a fifteen-year maximum. The aggravated element must appear in the federal indictment and, absent a valid admission as an element of the charged offense or valid jury waiver, must be submitted to the jury and proved beyond a reasonable doubt. An ordinary factual admission cannot repair its omission from the indictment. Judicial relevant-conduct findings may select punishment within the conviction’s authority but cannot supply the missing aggravated charge or verdict.

The Chief Justice’s additional conclusions about knowledge of interstate history and permanent deprivation appear only in his separate concurrence and have no additional joins.

## Standards and Tests selection: APA limits on exhaustion of superior-agency appeals

### APA limits on exhaustion of superior-agency appeals

**Governing question:** What instructions govern APA limits on exhaustion of superior-agency appeals?

**Current rule:** In an APA action, an otherwise final agency decision is reviewable without an optional superior-agency appeal unless a statute expressly requires exhaustion or an agency rule both requires the appeal and makes the action inoperative pending it. Courts cannot add exhaustion beyond those alternatives. APA finality, reviewability and adequate-remedy conditions remain; express statutory exhaustion and non-APA judicial exhaustion retain their own operation.

**Authority by component:**

- Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review: Darby v. Cisneros — Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review, 1993-06-21. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, Section 704 controls exhaustion of superior-agency review portion; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct proposition-level majority; no Marks aggregation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Judicial exhaustion and damages-only federal prison medical-care claims

### Judicial exhaustion and damages-only federal prison medical-care claims

**Governing question:** Whether exhaustion of an administrative process must precede an existing federal constitutional damages action when Congress has imposed no exhaustion command.

**Current rule:** An express congressional exhaustion requirement governs according to its terms. In the non-APA judicial-exhaustion setting addressed here, absent such a command the court exercises sound discretion consistent with the statutory scheme, weighing prompt judicial access against the actual interests in agency authority, expertise, correction of error, useful fact development, and judicial efficiency. Under the law governing McCarthy, neither the general BOP delegation nor the then-effective §1997e imposed an exhaustion requirement on the existing *Carlson* federal medical-care damages channel. For claims governed by that former statutory baseline, a federal prisoner seeking only damages for a completed medical-care wrong need not exhaust the general BOP procedure considered in *McCarthy*: it cannot award compensation; its initial 15-day filing period and successive 20- and 30-day appeal periods, even with valid-reason extensions, risk forfeiture; and investigation and written responses without a hearing or formal evidentiary findings provide insufficient adjudicative benefit to outweigh access to court. Speculative FTCA conversion or settlement does not supply an available monetary remedy in that process.

**Authority by component:**

- Statutory primacy, judicial discretion, and actual congressional scope: McCarthy v. Madigan — Statutory design governs the exhaustion inquiry, March 4, 1992.
- Damages-only BOP exception and its operative procedural premises: McCarthy — This damages-only claim need not pass through the general BOP process, March 4, 1992. Blackmun's opinion joined by Stone, White, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter controls both components; Scalia and Thomas join only the result on their no-compensation ground.

**Present operation effective:** McCarthy, March 4, 1992, within its surviving scope; PLRA, April 26, 1996, changes the covered statutory premise.

**Open question:** Prospective relief, a procedure able to award the requested compensation, and materially different administrative adjudication are not decided. The rule supplies no exception to a congressional command and no extension of *Bivens* to a new setting.

**Holdings navigation:** McCarthy v. Madigan, 503 U.S. 140 (1992) — Administrative exhaustion.

**Related current rules:** APA limits on exhaustion of superior-agency appeals states Darby's separate §704 restriction; Prior administrative exhaustion of an FTCA action states McNeil's express statutory prerequisite. Those decisions do not change McCarthy's former-law damages-only BOP holding; the later express PLRA command operates at its own scope.

**Effective transition:** PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §803(d), April 26, 1996, replaces §1997e with a command to exhaust available remedies before a covered confined-prisoner conditions action under §1983 or other federal law. See Statutory exhaustion and screening of prisoner conditions actions. McCarthy's absence-of-command premise no longer supplies a blanket exemption from that text; disputed pending-case operation is not resolved by admission. The general judicial-exhaustion rule survives outside a controlling statutory command.

## Standards and Tests selection: Independent foreign prosecution and compelled testimony

### Independent foreign prosecution and compelled testimony

**Governing question:** Whether fear of independent foreign prosecution supplies a Self-Incrimination Clause privilege.

**Current rule:** A real and substantial fear of prosecution by an independent foreign sovereign, without more and absent asserted domestic criminal exposure, does not permit refusal to give compelled testimony under the Self-Incrimination Clause. The inquiry concerns threatened criminal use, not resident-alien status or the civil label of the proceeding.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 1: common seven-Justice sufficient rule: United States v. Balsys, No. 97-873. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Sufficiently coordinated prosecution potentially attributable to the United States remains unresolved; general liaison or evidence sharing alone establishes no such attribution. No cooperation exception, rigid extension test or mandatory discovery procedure is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Balsys — Self-incrimination and independent foreign prosecution.

## Standards and Tests selection: Voting Rights Act §5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority

### Voting Rights Act §5 coverage of reallocations of governing authority

**Governing question:** Whether a transfer of duties among government officials changes a voting practice subject to preclearance.

**Current rule:** Section 5 requires a direct relation to voting; a redistribution of governing authority does not qualify merely because it diminishes an elected official's influence over a function. Changes in voting procedure, candidacy, electorate, or method of office selection supply familiar but nonexhaustive covered categories. Reallocation of road funds within an elected commission and transfer of road operations to an appointed engineer while the elected commission retains substantial appointment and budget authority do not, on those premises, require preclearance. The Attorney General's interpretation receives substantial consideration within the statutory boundary, but a significant-transfer/different-constituencies formulation cannot substitute for a connection to voting. Postelection timing alone creates no exemption, and coverage remains separate from discriminatory purpose or effect.

**Authority by component:**

- Voting/governing boundary and retained-office scope: Presley v. Etowah County Commission — The two resolutions do not change a voting practice, January 27, 1992.
- Limit on administrative and different-constituencies constructions: Presley — Administrative interpretation does not eliminate the voting boundary, January 27, 1992. Kennedy's opinion joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Thomas controls both.

**Present operation effective:** *Presley*, January 27, 1992.

**Open question:** Whether an extreme transfer effectively replacing an elective office with an appointive office requires preclearance is unresolved. The decision does not dispose of independent §2, Title VI, or constitutional claims.

**Holdings navigation:** Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission, 502 U.S. 491 (1992) — Voting Rights Act preclearance.

## Standards and Tests selection: Section 2 challenges to governing-body size

### Section 2 challenges to governing-body size

**Governing question:** What comparison permits a vote-dilution claim directed at the size of a local governing body.

**Current rule:** A local governing body’s size is within Voting Rights Act §2 coverage. A vote-dilution challenge must supply a reasonable comparison with independent legal and practical roots, not a number chosen solely to produce desired racial winners. No uniquely ideal size is required. Accepting the comparison does not establish liability: the claimant must still satisfy the applicable Gingles predicates and the statutory totality inquiry into unequal electoral opportunity. A remedy for an established violation must address the violation while considering genuine governmental interests; the decision supplies no automatic judicial entitlement to a particular number of offices.

**Authority by component:**

Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices directly adopt this threshold rule. O'Connor joins this part while opposing statutory liability; Kennedy reserves the affirmative coverage question, and Scalia and Thomas reject coverage of dilution.
- Five-Justice comparison, liability and remedy requirements: Holder v. Hall — 2. A reasonable comparison must have legal and practical roots independent of a desired racial tally; 3. The accepted comparison does not replace the required proof of unequal opportunity; 4. The existing remand must provide a lawful remedy for the established violation, 1994-06-30. Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices directly adopt the complete comparison rule and its application. O'Connor and Kennedy reject that comparison; Thomas and Scalia reject dilution coverage on a different, broader ground. Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; the five-Justice judgment coalition adopts these remedial limits and the affirmance of the Eleventh Circuit's existing remedy remand.

## Standards and Tests selection: Section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims

### Section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims

**Governing question:** What instructions govern section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims?

**Current rule:** A §2 single-member fragmentation claim requires the district-capacity condition described below, political cohesion and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat the group’s preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate. These are necessary, not alone sufficient; totality remains. Distinct groups' numbers cannot replace missing cohesion or bloc-voting proof. Coalition cognizability and the population denominator remain open. A State need not prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts; challengers initially must show unequal opportunity caused by the arrangement under the totality, while a federal court may order such districts only as necessary to remedy an established violation. Independent constitutional limits remain. Assuming an influence-only claim exists, majority bloc voting remains necessary; influence-only cognizability remains unresolved. The necessary voting conditions do not require maximization of majority-minority districts. Unequal electoral opportunity under the totality does not require discriminatory purpose. Relevant proportionality compares the group’s share of the relevant population with the share of districts in which it has an effective opportunity to elect preferred candidates; its weight depends on the other evidence. It is neither a conclusive safe harbor nor a burden-shifting affirmative defense, and disproportionality alone does not establish liability. Nominal district labels and officeholders’ race do not replace demonstrated opportunity. No proportional-election entitlement, racial floor or ceiling, universal geographic scale or population denominator follows. Opportunity elsewhere cannot excuse proved local packing, fragmentation or another electoral barrier. For one protected group, the first condition may also be met through rigorous proof that a lawful, geographically compact proposed district would give the cohesive group dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates. Speculation, isolated favorable elections, near-majority arithmetic and mere influence do not suffice. Plaintiffs must prove actual voting behavior and cohesion within that proposed district and the relevant majority-bloc defeat and causal connection in the challenged arrangement; success in one setting neither negates nor establishes defeat in another. Both the retained district-majority route and this functional route remain subject to lawful population, geography, special-circumstance qualifications, independent totality and constitutional limits.

**Authority by component:**

Scalia's statutory opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; all nine adopt the stated single-member extension and record application. No fractured-opinion or Marks inference is required.
- H1. Section 2 does not make a State prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts: Voinovich v. Quilter — H1. Section 2 does not make a State prove remedial necessity before choosing majority-minority districts, 1993-03-02. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Part II.B, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the complete proposition; it directly controls the rejection of the lower court's statutory prohibition and burden allocation.
- H2. The assumed influence-dilution claim fails for lack of the required majority bloc voting: Voinovich v. Quilter — H2. The assumed influence-dilution claim fails for lack of the required majority bloc voting, 1993-03-02. O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, Parts II.A and II.C, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. ## Standards and Tests selection: Section 2 opportunity and remedies in judicial elections

### Section 2 opportunity and remedies in judicial elections

**Governing question:** Voting Rights Act §2 claims against at-large judicial elections and proposed judicial-election remedies.

**Current rule:** Section 2 applies to judicial elections. Plaintiffs must prove the applicable Gingles conditions and unequal opportunity under the totality for minority voters to elect their actual preferred candidates; no requirement that the preferred candidate be Black or that voters or lawmakers acted from racial motives is added. Evaluate the challenged electoral units and the evidence in context, including preference, incumbency, appointments, partisanship, qualifications and success. A comparison or remedy must have independent legal and practical roots and fit the governmental task; genuine jurisdiction–electorate linkage, qualified-candidate pool and judicial-administration concerns receive concrete weight but supply no categorical remedial immunity. Race awareness in territorial remedial design alone does not establish unconstitutional partiality or add a constitutional-injury element to §2.

**Authority by component:**

- Judicial-election opportunity, grounded comparison and bounded relief: 1. The opportunity determination must use the statutory results inquiry, 2. Judicial structure warrants concrete consideration, not categorical remedial immunity and 3. Reconsideration preserves findings and the plaintiffs' burden, 1995-12-18; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer (five), applying Chisom, Houston Lawyers, Gingles, Holder and De Grandy at their preserved scopes.

**Present operation effective:** SCLC v. Sessions, 1995-12-18, for the judicial-election remedial boundary; existing §2 proof mechanics remain unchanged.

**Related current rules:** “Section 2 fragmentation, state districting choice and influence claims” supplies the applicable district-majority, cohesion, bloc-voting, special-circumstance and totality requirements; “Section 2 challenges to governing-body size” supplies the independent-root comparison and violation-fitted remedy requirements.

**Exceptions and limits:** Necessary voting conditions alone do not establish liability. No universal evidentiary exclusion, new statistical formula, unique ideal arrangement, maximization duty, proportional-results entitlement or entitlement to a particular judicial structure follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Southern Christian Leadership Conference of Alabama v. Sessions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Selective racial restructuring of access to lawful remedies

### Selective racial restructuring of access to lawful remedies

**Governing question:** A State’s relocation of authority over otherwise lawful racial remedies to a more remote political process.

**Current rule:** A supported claim that a State selectively moves authority over otherwise lawful racial remedies to a more remote political process requires examination of the resulting unequal political burden; calling the foreclosed measures preferences does not answer that claim. Interim protection must correspond to the demonstrated threatened loss of access to otherwise available lawful racial remedies and leave independently lawful provisions operative to the extent severable under state law. This does not require a preference or validate an unconstitutional program. The racial political-process rule does not decide a sex-based theory: an actual sex classification requires the important-objective and substantial-relationship inquiry without presuming that every statewide withdrawal of policy creates such a classification.

Preliminary relief must correspond to the supported injury, 1998-11-10; five direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-11-10.

**Exceptions and limits:** Title VII’s permission for defined voluntary affirmative action does not alone preempt state withdrawal of that policy or guarantee subordinate entities authority to adopt it. Actual conflict with a federal duty or binding remedy remains subject to federal supremacy and applicable savings provisions. Authority: the same Record, Holding III, all nine Justices.

## Standards and Tests selection: ERISA equitable categories and nonfiduciary compensation

### ERISA equitable categories and nonfiduciary compensation

**Governing question:** Available equitable categories and individual redress under ERISA §502(a)(3).

**Current rule:** Section 502(a)(3)'s appropriate equitable relief means traditionally equitable categories, not compensatory damages merely because equity could award them in trust litigation. Compensation from an admitted nonfiduciary for plan losses is unavailable under that provision. The rule does not prohibit every equitable monetary remedy or decide Mertens’s expressly disclaimed antecedent nonfiduciary cause-of-action question; restitution is not reopened. Sections 409 and 502(a)(2)’s plan-centered remedy does not categorically bar §502(a)(3)’s appropriate individual equity for an actual §404 violation. Adequate relief under another ERISA provision normally makes further equity unnecessary and inappropriate, without an absolute prohibition governing every alternative pleading. Relief must redress an actual ERISA or plan obligation; veil piercing alone supplies no freestanding violation.

**Authority by component:**

- The reviewed compensatory award is not appropriate equitable relief: Mertens v. Hewitt Associates — The reviewed compensatory award is not appropriate equitable relief, 1993-06-01. Scalia’s Opinion of the Court; Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.
- Varity — Part III, individual equity and qualified adequacy principle, 1996-03-19; Souter, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (five); Stone-Zsela’s stricter condition is noncontrolling.
- Peacock — ERISA enforcement requires an independently violated statutory or plan obligation, 1996-02-21; Thomas, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).

**Present operation effective:** Varity, March 19, 1996, resolving the categorical individual-fiduciary-redress question; Mertens’s equitable-category limit survives.

**Holdings navigation:** Mertens v. Hewitt Associates, 508 U.S. 248 (1993); Varity Corp. v. Howe, 516 U.S. 489; Peacock v. Thomas, 516 U.S. 349.

**Exceptions and limits:** Restoration supplies existing membership rights, not immutable benefits. The controlling rule does not classify every substituted monetary item as equitable or adopt all trust-law compensation. An adequate benefits remedy is not established merely because that statutory route exists in the abstract.

## Standards and Tests selection: Fiduciary benefit communications and deliberate disloyal deception

### Fiduciary benefit communications and deliberate disloyal deception

**Governing question:** When an employer’s benefit communication is ERISA administration and breaches loyalty.

**Current rule:** Fiduciary responsibility under §3(21)(A) follows the particular exercise of discretionary plan administration, including benefit information communicated in that capacity. Authority over administration, the occasion and linked benefit content may establish that function; general employer status, forecasting or participant perception alone does not. An administrator acting in that capacity violates §404(a)(1) by knowingly and significantly misleading participants about benefit security to induce surrender of existing plan interests for the employer’s benefit. Lawful amendment or termination power does not authorize that deliberate disloyal deception.

**Authority by component:**

- Varity — Parts I–II, functional administration and loyalty breach, 1996-03-19; Souter, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer (six).

**Present operation effective:** Varity, March 19, 1996.

**Open question:** No general duty to volunteer information, answer every inquiry, disclose every forecast or avoid negligent misstatement is decided; not every corporate prediction is fiduciary administration. The distinct retirees’ assignment determination establishes no new substantive assignment rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Varity Corp. v. Howe, 516 U.S. 489.

## Canonical Holdings selections: criminal-procedure

#### United States v. Gaudin

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-514.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### The jury decides application of the materiality element

**Holding:** When materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine it beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge states statutory meaning and the governing legal standard; labeling its application a mixed question does not remove an element from the jury.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Materiality concerns a statement's natural tendency or capacity to influence the relevant governmental decision. The Government conceded that it was an element and did not contest the lower court's error classifications. Clause-specific materiality requirements, universal structural-error treatment and exact unverified preservation history remain undecided. Statutory interpretation and threshold evidentiary sufficiency remain judicial functions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- In re Winship: applies proof beyond a reasonable doubt to facts establishing guilt.
- Sinclair v. United States: overruled to the extent it assigns a criminal pertinency element to the judge; unrelated propositions are not displaced.
- Kungys v. United States: distinguishes civil denaturalization and its appellate allocation.
- Sullivan v. Louisiana: preserves the actual existing constitutional jury/burden holding; this case creates no universal omitted-element error classification.
- Hubbard v. United States: preserves §1001 entity coverage at its actual scope; supplies no authority to remove HUD offense elements from a jury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Ninth Circuit's reversal of the reviewed false-statement convictions. Lawful retrial submitting every element, including materiality, to the jury remains available; no acquittal, disposition of the separate equity-skimming conviction or sentence calculation follows.

#### Almendarez-Torres v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6839.

**Decided:** 1998-03-24.

##### Section 1326(b)(2) requires an aggravated-offense allegation before its higher maximum may apply

**Holding:** The prior aggravated-felony conviction specified in §1326(b)(2) is an element of the aggravated unlawful-reentry offense. It must be alleged in the federal indictment and, unless validly admitted as an element of the charged offense, proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt; an admission to the fact of a prior conviction does not cure its omission from this indictment, so the existing conviction permits no sentence above §1326(a)'s two-year maximum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. These five expressly adopt the statutory rule, its ordinary element consequences and the remedy. Four explain the construction through constitutional avoidance; Stone-Zsela gives a definitive constitutional ground separately. No majority adopts a general constitutional rule governing all facts that increase a statutory maximum.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is statutory. No majority decides a general constitutional rule for maximum-increasing facts or a constitutional prior-conviction exception. The admitted convictions remain valid; neither subsidiary proof questions nor facts that leave the maximum unchanged are decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The enhanced sentence is vacated and the judgment reversed and remanded for sentencing within the two-year maximum on this indictment and plea. The plea is not withdrawn and no acquittal follows. The availability of a properly charged further proceeding is reserved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hamling v. United States:** Applied to require an indictment alleging each element of the aggravated offense.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applied after statutory classification; its jury-and-proof rule for actual elements remains controlling without deciding all sentencing classifications.
- **McMillan v. Pennsylvania:** Distinguished because its mandatory minimum left the statutory maximum unchanged; its limited holding remains intact.
- **United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra:** Preserved for ordinary sentencing within punishment authorized by the conviction; it does not resolve this higher-maximum classification.
- **Custis v. United States:** Distinguished as a collateral-review decision; the existing limits on attacking prior convictions remain intact.
- **Old Chief v. United States:** Applied to the distinction between element status and the form of adequate prior-conviction proof; no broader compulsory-stipulation rule is created.

#### United States v. Balsys

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-873.
**Decided:** 1998-06-25.

##### Independent foreign prosecution risk alone does not supply the privilege

**Holding:** A real and substantial fear of prosecution by an independent foreign sovereign, without more and without asserted domestic criminal exposure, does not permit a witness to refuse compelled testimony under the Self-Incrimination Clause. The rule concerns the scope of the threatened criminal use, not a resident alien's constitutional status or the civil label of the proceeding.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). The common sufficient part has Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. These seven directly adopt the rule and application; no fractured-decision aggregation is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A civil inquiry can still threaten protected domestic criminal use; pretrial invocation is not categorically premature. The established foreign dangers concern Lithuania and Israel, not Germany. No wartime guilt, deportability, universal limitations ruling, right to give false answers or treaty/transfer objection is decided; independent domestic-use and immunity objections remain.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Murphy's domestic intersovereign protection and Kastigar's effective use/derivative-use immunity remain intact; Zicarelli's reserved question is answered at the independent-foreign-risk scope; Verdugo is used only for the specific constitutional distinction, not an alien-status exclusion.

##### The present cooperation record does not establish an attributable prosecution

**Holding:** OSI's general liaison mandate, a Lithuania assistance memorandum and past evidence sharing with Israel do not on this record establish that this subpoena serves a foreign prosecution attributable to the United States. A materially different, sufficiently coordinated prosecution is not decided; no cooperation exception, mandatory discovery inquiry or rigid test is adopted.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Kennedy). All seven Justices joining the common sufficient part accept the bounded record application. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter additionally join the affirmative general reservation that sufficiently coordinated prosecution might implicate American constitutional restraints; the other two express only ordinary nondecision. The more detailed possible-extension discussion has only four supporters and is noncontrolling.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The four-Justice policy illustrations supply no controlling proxy-prosecution test. The decision commands neither discovery of a hypothetical arrangement nor unconditional disclosure, contempt, conviction, deportation or immunity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Murphy's protection against domestic evasion remains; no new extension to a cooperative foreign prosecution is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit judgment is reversed. The case returns for lawful subpoena-enforcement proceedings consistent with the independent-foreign-risk holding and preservation of distinct objections. No new collateral proceeding or remedy is ordered.

#### Jones v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-6203.
**Decided:** 1999-03-24.

##### Serious bodily injury and death define aggravated carjacking offenses

**Holding:** The version of 18 U.S.C. § 2119 governing the December 1992 offense creates a basic carjacking offense and aggravated offenses when serious bodily injury or death results. Serious bodily injury is an element of the grade carrying a twenty-five-year maximum, and death is an element of the grade carrying a maximum of life imprisonment; the basic offense carries a fifteen-year maximum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six Justices). Separate constitutional theories do not supply an additional Court holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Almendarez-Torres is applied as a statutory element-and-charging analogue, not as a general constitutional maximum-fact holding. Gaudin supplies the significance of identifying an actual element. Monge's bounded double-jeopardy classification is preserved, including its reservation of independent charging and jury questions. Holloway's attention to enacted offense conditions is consistent with this reading, but its construction of later statutory language is not imported into the earlier offense.

##### The existing indictment and verdict authorize only the basic carjacking range

**Holding:** The aggravated injury or death element must be alleged in the federal indictment; absent a valid admission as an element of the charged offense or a valid waiver of jury determination, the Government must submit it to the jury and prove it beyond a reasonable doubt. An ordinary factual admission does not itself cure an indictment that omits the aggravated element. Because injury was neither charged nor established by Jones's verdict, the judge's later injury finding cannot authorize the aggravated range; the aggravated sentence must be vacated, and sentencing on the existing carjacking indictment and verdict may not exceed the fifteen-year basic maximum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six Justices). Separate constitutional theories do not supply an additional Court holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hamling and Gaudin are applied to the aggravated statutory elements. Almendarez-Torres supplies the distinction between admitting a fact and properly charging the aggravated offense. Witte and Watts retain their ordinary within-authority sentencing operation and provide no basis for this increased statutory range. Monge supplies no independent remedy here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court's ground is statutory construction and the protections of the resulting elements. It does not decide a general constitutional rule for all maximum-increasing facts, bare prior-conviction status, mandatory minima within an unchanged maximum, every Guidelines classification or capital sentencing. McMillan's unchanged-maximum holding and Walton's capital rule are not overruled. The death grade is part of the statute's construction, not a finding that Jones caused death.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The sufficient ground is construction of the original carjacking statute and ordinary protections of its aggravated elements. No general constitutional maximum-fact rule, prior-conviction exception, unchanged-maximum minimum rule, universal Guidelines classification or capital-sentencing rule is decided; McMillan and Walton are not overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the aggravated sentence and remand. Sentencing on the existing indictment and verdict may not exceed the fifteen-year basic maximum. No particular sentence, aggregate calculation, acquittal or authorization of a new aggravated prosecution is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Almendarez-Torres supplies the statutory aggravated-element and charging analogue; its result remains distinct from a universal constitutional rule. Hamling requires a federal indictment stating the offense's elements, and Gaudin requires the jury's determination beyond a reasonable doubt. Monge retains its noncapital serious-prior double-jeopardy classification and its express reservation of independent charging and jury claims; no renewed-proof remedy is drawn from it here. Witte and Watts retain their within-authority sentencing permission and do not authorize punishment beyond the charged conviction's statutory range. Holloway concerns later statutory language and does not amend the law governing this offense. McMillan and Walton are not overruled, and Custis's limits on collateral attack remain undisturbed.

#### Presley v. Etowah County Commission; Mack v. Russell County Commission, 502 U.S. 491 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 90-711 and 90-712.
**Decided:** January 27, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Presley concerns the Common Fund Resolution in No. 90-711; Mack concerns the Unit System in No. 90-712. One opinion supplies the same controlling grounds for both appealed noncoverage judgments.

##### Whether the Common Fund Resolution and Unit System require §5 preclearance

**Holding:** Section 5 does not require preclearance of these reallocations of governing authority because they do not alter voting procedures, candidacy, the electorate, or the method of selecting the relevant offices and have no direct relation to voting. This ruling does not resolve whether an extreme transfer could effectively replace an elective office with an appointive one, nor does it exempt a change merely because it occurs after an election.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this proposition in both appeals.

**Material application:** The Common Fund Resolution replaces individual district road-fund authority with collective commission control. The Unit System places road operations under an appointed engineer while retaining substantial authority, including appointment and budget powers, in the elected commission. Neither changes the relevant selection mechanism or electorate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The listed electoral categories are illustrative, not exhaustive. Effective replacement of an elective office with an appointive office remains unresolved. The Court decides no racial-purpose or discriminatory-effect question, §2 claim, Title VI claim, or constitutional claim; the unappealed Road Supervision ruling is undisturbed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both appealed noncoverage judgments are affirmed. Neither challenged resolution requires §5 preclearance or is enjoined under §5 on these grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Allen v. State Board of Elections*, 393 U.S. 544 — broad coverage of changes affecting electoral choice remains; elective-to-appointive selection is distinguished from these retained elected offices.
- *Perkins v. Matthews*, 400 U.S. 379 — changes to the electorate and voting arrangements remain covered; coverage remains distinct from discrimination merits.
- *Dougherty County Board of Education v. White*, 439 U.S. 32 — candidacy restrictions directly related to electoral choice remain covered.
- *McCain v. Lybrand*, 465 U.S. 236, and *City of Lockhart v. United States*, 460 U.S. 125 — changes in electoral arrangements remain within §5; neither decides ordinary internal governing duties.
- *NAACP v. Hampton County Election Commission*, 470 U.S. 166 — practical changes to election timing or procedure require proper review; postelection timing alone supplies no exemption here.

##### Whether the Attorney General's interpretation permits coverage without the statutory connection to voting

**Holding:** The Attorney General's interpretation of §5 is entitled to substantial consideration but cannot extend preclearance to governing changes that lack the statutory connection to voting. The proposed significant-transfer/different-constituencies test cannot substitute for that connection in these appeals.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas; five Justices support this proposition in both appeals.

**Material application:** The rejected test concerns significant transfers of authority between officials responsible to substantially different constituencies. That formulation can reach governing responsibilities without identifying any change in voting and therefore cannot substitute for the required connection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The distinction between §5 coverage and review of discriminatory effect remains intact. Administrative expertise retains substantial weight within the statutory voting boundary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Board of Commissioners of Sheffield*, 435 U.S. 110 — administrative interpretation retains substantial weight within the statutory voting boundary.
- *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.*, 467 U.S. 837 — preserved; deference does not authorize exceeding the statute's scope.
- *Beer v. United States*, 425 U.S. 130 — coverage and discriminatory-effect review remain separate inquiries.
- *South Carolina v. Katzenbach*, 383 U.S. 301 — §5's remedial function is preserved; it does not by itself establish coverage of every governing change.

#### Holder v. Hall

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2012.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

##### The county's governing-body size is within Section 2's coverage

**Holding:** The number of members of this elected county governing authority is a standard, practice or procedure within Section 2, 42 U.S.C. §1973; its combination of legislative and executive functions and its present single-member form do not categorically exempt it from the statute. Coverage alone establishes neither a usable measure of dilution nor unequal electoral opportunity, and it does not prescribe a remedy.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices directly adopt this threshold rule. O'Connor joins this part while opposing statutory liability; Kennedy reserves the affirmative coverage question, and Scalia and Thomas reject coverage of dilution.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chisom v. Roemer, 501 U.S. 380 (1991): applies its rejection of artificial exclusions from Section 2's electoral coverage; the decision does not itself settle the body-size comparison.
- Houston Lawyers' Association v. Attorney General of Texas, 501 U.S. 419 (1991): follows its separation of coverage, legitimate state interests, liability and remedy; extends no automatic right to additional offices.
- Presley v. Etowah County Commission, January 27, 1992: preserves its Section 5 voting/governing boundary and its reservation of independent Section 2 claims; it supplies no Section 2 size benchmark.
- City of Rome v. United States, 446 U.S. 156 (1980): distinguishes Section 5 review of changes against an existing practice from the proof required for this Section 2 results claim.

##### A reasonable comparison must have legal and practical roots independent of a desired racial tally

**Holding:** A Section 2 challenge to this sole-commissioner structure may use a reasonable, workable alternative grounded in the actual legal and institutional setting, suitable to the governmental task, and justified independently of the desired number of minority-preferred winners; a uniquely ideal size is not required. The county-specific authorization of five district-elected commissioners plus an at-large chair, the existing school-board districts and the compact potential majority satisfy that comparison requirement here, without making population share, a possible seat, or any proposed enlargement sufficient by itself.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices directly adopt the complete comparison rule and its application. O'Connor and Kennedy reject that comparison; Thomas and Scalia reject dilution coverage on a different, broader ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles, 478 U.S. 30 (1986): extends its comparison inquiry to this locally grounded sole-commissioner challenge while retaining its causal predicates and totality inquiry.
- Houston Lawyers' Association, 501 U.S. 419: applies meaningful consideration of the State's institutional interests; those interests are neither ignored nor converted into categorical structural immunity.
- Growe v. Emison, February 23, 1993: preserves the State's districting responsibility and causal proof requirements; it neither decided nor barred this size comparison.

##### The accepted comparison does not replace the required proof of unequal opportunity

**Holding:** This size-dilution claim requires a sufficiently large and geographically compact minority to form a district majority, political cohesion, and majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat its preferred candidate, absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate, together with unequal opportunity established under the totality of circumstances; the comparison alone is insufficient and discriminatory purpose is not required. The extent of protected-class electoral success may be considered but creates no right to election in proportion to population, and the preserved appellate findings establish statutory liability here without reopening cohesion or bloc voting outside the limited review question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices directly adopt the stated application and preservation of the existing proof requirements. There is no separate holding that the Gingles predicates alone establish liability or shift the ultimate statutory burden.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Gingles, 478 U.S. 30: follows the causal prerequisites and independently necessary totality inquiry; no universal statistical method or proportional representation rule is adopted.
- Growe, February 23, 1993: preserves the need for local cohesion and bloc-voting proof, including the stated special-circumstances qualification; coalition cognizability, influence claims and the population denominator remain unresolved.
- Voinovich v. Quilter, March 2, 1993: follows the distinction between unequal-opportunity proof and preference for a district form; no change to its initial burden rule or its influence-claim reservations.
- Shaw v. Reno, June 28, 1993: preserves the independent elements of Section 2; its rejection of the design-only constitutional theory adds no constitutional-injury prerequisite to this statutory claim.

##### The existing remand must provide a lawful remedy for the established violation

**Holding:** Because Section 2 liability is affirmed, the existing remand for formulation of a workable remedy continues, with genuine county-government interests and the responsible state institutions receiving consideration and relief confined to curing the established violation. The local authorization and district pattern inform that inquiry but compel neither their exact adoption, a particular number of officers, a racial quota nor any candidate's victory; no new liability trial is ordered and the constitutional alternative need not be decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; the five-Justice judgment coalition adopts these remedial limits and the affirmance of the Eleventh Circuit's existing remedy remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Houston Lawyers' Association, 501 U.S. 419: follows its instruction to consider genuine state structural interests in fitting relief to a proved violation.
- Growe, February 23, 1993: preserves state responsibility and the lawful federal remedial role; its case-specific dismissal direction is not a universal bar to retaining remedial proceedings.
- Voinovich, March 2, 1993: applies the distinction between state districting choice and a federal remedy justified by an established violation; no mandate to maximize districts follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit's Section 2 liability judgment and existing remand for formulation of a lawful remedy are affirmed, 5–4. Relief must cure the established violation with attention to genuine county-government interests and responsible state institutions. No new liability trial, mandatory board size, map, racial quota or electoral winner is ordered; the constitutional alternative remains unnecessary.

#### Valdespino v. Alamo Heights Independent School District

**Docket or dockets:** lower docket 98-50227.
**Decided:** 1999-06-17.

##### The first condition includes a proved functional ability-to-elect route

**Holding:** For a claim by one protected group, the first Gingles condition may be met either through the retained district-majority route or through rigorous proof that a lawful, geographically compact proposed district would provide the cohesive group dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its candidates of choice. Speculation, an isolated favorable election, near-majority population arithmetic or mere influence does not suffice; the relevant majority-bloc showing, causal connection and independent totality remain necessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I is joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices adopt this express modification and its limits.

##### The population figures do not resolve functional opportunity

**Holding:** The accepted 47.9% and 48.3% Hispanic citizen voting-age shares neither prove nor foreclose dependable ability to elect. The judgment resting exclusively on their being below fifty percent must be vacated for application of the changed rule, with all remaining statutory conditions and totality to be established before relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five Justices join Stone-Zsela's Part I and this application and remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The proposed district must remain lawful in population and geography; the 8.1% underpopulation is not approved. No mathematical crossover formula, racial floor, proportional-election right, distinct-minority-group combination or influence-only entitlement is adopted. Supplementation under ordinary procedure is neither automatically required nor forbidden; the excluded survey is not automatically admitted.

##### The independent demographic and disclosure rulings stand

**Holding:** The credited proof of concrete post-census housing, occupancy and growth changes supports the present demographic findings under clear-error review. Excluding the survey disclosed on the eve of trial for surprise under the applicable disclosure rule was within the trial court's discretion; changing the capacity rule does not itself make either independent ruling erroneous.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices adopt this rule and explanation. Scalia and Thomas do not join it unconditionally.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Thornburg v. Gingles: expressly modifies numerical exclusivity in the first condition for the defined single-group functional route; preserves compactness, cohesion, relevant majority-bloc proof, causation and independent totality.
- Growe v. Emison: modifies the same exclusivity while preserving its causal framework and the distinct-group reservation.
- Voinovich v. Quilter: preserves the claimant's burden, relevant bloc-voting requirement and distinction between lawful state districting choice and a court-compelled remedy; the present rule resolves only the defined functional-capacity question.
- Johnson v. De Grandy: applies the rejection of maximization and conclusive ratios; preserves independent totality and actual opportunity without attributing today's change to that earlier decision.
- Brooks v. Barnes: applies its insistence that proof address the actual electoral barrier; its nomination holding did not already decide this district-formation question.
- Department of Commerce v. United States House of Representatives / Clinton v. Glavin: preserves the national enumeration ruling; it neither freezes local demographics at the census date nor validates every projection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A single protected group below a numerical majority may prove the first condition through dependable actual ability to elect in a lawful compact district. A majority-population route remains available, but neither route establishes every other condition or liability. Cohesion, relevant bloc-voting causation with applicable special circumstances, totality, independent constitutional limits and ordinary evidence procedures retain their force. Mere influence and speculative near-majority calculations do not create a right to districting. The present record establishes no functional success or completed violation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Partial vacatur and remand are 5–4. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Opposing: O'Connor and Kennedy would affirm under a numerical citizen-voting-age majority requirement; Scalia and Thomas would affirm on statutory noncoverage grounds.

The demographic and disclosure rulings are sustained by seven direct votes: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia and Thomas express only conditional agreement if the dilution framework is applied. Those positions do not supply direct joins in the evidence holding or the numerical rule.

Vacatur concerns the categorical sub-fifty bar. The lower courts must assess the functional route on the preserved admissible record and any supplementation independently permitted under ordinary procedure. They must then consider the remaining statutory conditions and totality if necessary. No liability, district, election system, new trial or automatic survey admission follows. The claim may still fail.

#### Mertens v. Hewitt Associates, 508 U.S. 248 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1671.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

##### The reviewed compensatory award is not appropriate equitable relief

**Holding:** Section 502(a)(3)’s appropriate equitable relief encompasses traditionally equitable categories of relief, not compensatory damages merely because a court of equity could award them in trust litigation. The requested monetary compensation from this admitted nonfiduciary for the plan’s losses is therefore unavailable under that provision; the Court does not bar every monetary equitable remedy or decide the antecedent, expressly disclaimed cause-of-action question.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court; Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). Direct majority support for this exact proposition; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The expressly disclaimed antecedent cause of action is reserved, not rejected as jurisdictional. Other equitable monetary remedies are not categorically barred; restitution, unappealed claims and the separate malpractice disposition remain untouched.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm rejection of the reviewed compensatory claim under §502(a)(3); no liability finding or award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Curtis v. Loether, 415 U.S. 189: traditional legal compensation distinction applied.
- Chauffeurs, Teamsters & Helpers v. Terry, 494 U.S. 558: remedy classification applied; trust forum alone does not decide the statutory category.
- Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. v. Bruch, 489 U.S. 101: trust-law guidance preserved subject to express statutory limits.
- Clews v. Jamieson, 182 U.S. 461: equity’s monetary powers acknowledged but distinguished from the statutory category.
- Massachusetts Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Russell, 473 U.S. 134: enforcement-structure caution applied; its reserved question is now resolved only within this holding.
- United States v. Burke, May 26, 1992: its fractured judgment supplies no controlling general remedial-range test.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February 26, 1992: appropriate-remedy presumption distinguished because Congress expressly limited this provision to equitable relief.

#### Varity Corp. v. Howe, 516 U.S. 489

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1471.
**Decided:** March 19, 1996.

##### The integrated benefit-security presentation was fiduciary administration

**Holding:** ERISA §3(21)(A) attaches fiduciary responsibility to the particular exercise of discretionary plan administration, including plan information communicated in that capacity. Varity exercised that function when it used administrative authority, benefit comparisons and linked assurances of benefit security to induce the class's transfer; employer status, corporate forecasting or participant perception alone would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Material application:** Current-benefit descriptions and deliberately linked security assurances formed one administrative presentation. Warnings that future conditions depended on MCC's success and that changes might occur did not displace the unchallenged trial findings that the combined presentation was knowingly misleading.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Every employment statement or corporate-solvency prediction is not thereby fiduciary. Corporate restructuring and plan-amendment powers remain distinct from administration.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** John Hancock's particular-function inquiry is applied while keeping status, breach and remedy separate. Curtiss-Wright preserves sponsor amendment authority but distinguishes retained power from authorized exercise; it creates no immunity for this administrative act.

##### Deliberate disloyal deception violates §404

**Holding:** An administrator acting as a fiduciary violates §404(a)(1)'s loyalty duty by knowingly and significantly misleading participants about benefit security to induce surrender of existing plan interests for the employer's benefit. Varity's found deception and the class's reliance meet that rule despite reserved lawful amendment or termination powers.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by the same six Justices as Part I.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This intentional-deception holding decides no general duty to volunteer information, answer every inquiry, disclose every forecast or avoid negligent misstatement. It supplies no substantive rule for the ten retirees' different assignment claim and no permanent-benefits promise.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Firestone's trust-law guidance explains loyalty within ERISA's statutory function, without replacing that threshold. Curtiss-Wright's amendment rule supplies no permission to deceive participants into surrendering present membership.

##### Section 502(a)(3) permits appropriate individual equitable redress for fiduciary breach

**Holding:** Sections 409 and 502(a)(2) do not impose a categorical plan-only bar on §502(a)(3)'s express authorization of appropriate individual equitable relief for a §404 violation. Relief must be traditionally equitable; where another ERISA provision adequately remedies the injury, further equity normally is unnecessary and inappropriate, without an absolute rule governing every alternative pleading.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Stone-Zsela's stricter adequate-remedy condition supports the class's judgment but is not the Court's rule.

**Material application:** The class sought restoration after fiduciary deception procured surrender of membership. Rejection of its lifetime-benefits contract theory established neither an adequate §502(a)(1)(B) remedy for that injury nor an automatic bar to restoration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not classify each monetary item as equitable, including replacement-insurance premiums or all unpaid benefits. It neither revives the rejected $7.6 million and $712,332 compensatory awards nor permits general compensation through trust law. Section 510 and estoppel remain unadopted alternatives.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The clarified restoration decree is affirmed. Reinstatement confers only the rights of the M-F plan as it now exists. ### Rice v. Cayetano — 2000-02-23

[Record](../records/rice-v-cayetano_merits_2000-02-23.md)

#### Event, question and statutory setting

Rice v. Cayetano, No. 98-818. Merits decision; February 23, 2000. Argued October 6, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 146 F.3d 1075, affirming summary judgment for the Governor of Hawaii. Question: Whether Hawaii may limit voting for trustees of the Office of Hawaiian Affairs to otherwise qualified voters who meet the statutory Hawaiian ancestry definition, consistently with the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendments.

#### Racial ancestry cannot define the electorate for public state office

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may not restrict the electorate for public state office by racial ancestry on the ground that the office serves a particular beneficiary community. Hawaii's ancestry qualification for voting in the public OHA trustee election violates the Fifteenth Amendment and may not be enforced against otherwise qualified voters.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy. Those five directly adopt this rule and its explanation. Souter and Breyer support reversal on a distinct ground stated separately; their judgment votes do not enlarge the Court opinion's coalition.

**Controlling explanation:** The qualification determines who may cast a ballot for state officials. The broader statutory Hawaiian class comprises descendants of the aboriginal peoples who exercised sovereignty and subsisted in the islands in 1778 and thereafter continued to reside in Hawaii; it differs from the native-Hawaiian definition requiring at least one-half ancestry. The voting line uses ancestry as a racial classification here. Incomplete correspondence with present racial identity and the absence of hostility do not remove that operation. Gomillion establishes that a State's power over electoral arrangements remains subject to the Fifteenth Amendment's prohibition of racial disfranchisement.

Even assuming substantial congressional authority concerning native Hawaiians and delegation to the State, Morton v. Mancari does not authorize this restriction. Its political and self-government setting differs from an election of public state officials. OHA administers state laws and public responsibilities; serving beneficiaries does not make its trustees a separate tribe's sovereign government. Salyer and Ball concern permissible departures from equal vote weight in special-purpose districts, not permission to exclude voters by race. Shaw and Miller distinguish territorial design from independent exclusion; Rice's denied ballot presents the latter. Adarand and Hopwood's equal-protection treatment of supported classifications cannot displace this independent voting prohibition. Rayphand's confined territorial accommodation supplies no exception for this State's electorate. The qualification therefore fails without deciding the Fourteenth Amendment claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tribal membership rule, internal election of a genuinely sovereign tribal government, general federal recognition power, underlying benefit eligibility, validity of OHA's programs or trusts, trustee candidate qualification or separate sovereignty ballot. The judgment establishes no blood-quantum rule and invalidates no prior trustee act.

#### Precedent treatment

- Gomillion v. Lightfoot: applied; state control of electoral structure remains limited by the prohibition of racial disfranchisement.
- Morton v. Mancari: distinguished; its political and self-government rationale remains valid but does not authorize this ancestry restriction in a public state election.
- Salyer Land Co. v. Tulare Lake Basin Water Storage District: distinguished; its special-purpose vote-weight holding creates no racial-franchise exception.
- Ball v. James: distinguished on the same vote-weight versus racial-exclusion distinction; its proper special-purpose field remains.
- Shaw v. Reno: its design-only boundary is preserved and distinguished from Rice's personal exclusion from the electorate.
- Miller and consolidated cases: their separation of territorial predominance from independently cognizable unequal participation is preserved.
- Adarand Constructors v. Peña: its substantial-justification rule in equal protection remains; it does not displace the Fifteenth Amendment's separate prohibition.
- Hopwood v. Texas: its application of the equal-protection standard remains distinct from eligibility to vote in a public election.
- Rayphand v. Sablan / Torres v. Sablan: its confined territorial accommodation is distinguished; it supplies no exception for this State's ancestry restriction.

#### Resulting law and limits

Hawaii must permit otherwise qualified voters to participate in the public OHA trustee election without the prohibited ancestry qualification. The controlling five-Justice state-office rule is distinct from Souter's and Breyer's actual trust-and-electorate ground. No fractured-decision aggregation is necessary or adopted. Political and indigenous self-government classifications retain their own questions; this judgment settles no benefit eligibility or recognition issue.

#### Source and record limits

[Statutory arrangements and proceedings, 528 U.S. 495](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-528/pdf/USREPORTS-528-495.pdf). The broader Hawaiian electorate and narrower native-Hawaiian beneficiary definition are distinct; no general federal-recognition determination is supplied by the factual record.

### Village of Willowbrook v. Olech — 2000-02-23

[Record](../records/village-of-willowbrook-v-olech_merits_2000-02-23.md)

#### Event, question and statutory setting

Village of Willowbrook v. Olech, No. 98-1288. Merits decision; February 23, 2000. Argued January 10, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 160 F.3d 386, which reversed dismissal under Rule 12(b)(6). Question: Whether intentional differential treatment of an individual from materially similarly situated persons, without a rational basis, may state an equal-protection claim without membership in a separately identified class or an independent showing of subjective ill will.

#### Intentional irrational treatment of an individual may violate equal protection

**Holding and operative rule:** An individual states an equal-protection claim by alleging intentional differential treatment from materially similarly situated persons without a rational basis for the distinction; membership in a separately identified group is unnecessary. Subjective ill will may be evidence of arbitrariness or pretext, but is not an independent element of this objectively irrational intentional-treatment claim.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight directly adopt this rule and the pleading application. Breyer concurs in the judgment on the additional allegation of vindictive action and does not join the objective rule's independent sufficiency.

**Controlling explanation:** Sioux City Bridge and Allegheny Pittsburgh protect persons against deliberate unequal administration; equal protection does not cease because the challenged selection is individual. Beach preserves reasonably conceivable legitimate grounds and the challenger's burden to negate them. Heller requires a rational relation to the actual disadvantage, while Romer confirms that this inquiry need not turn on a finding about every actor's subjective motive. These principles permit the stated claim without making every service dispute a constitutional case. The difference must be intentional, the comparators materially alike in relation to the demand, and the distinction without a rational basis. Negligence, ordinary mistake, disagreement with a discretionary judgment, materially different circumstances or an objectively rational distinction does not suffice. Officials need not have contemporaneously recorded their rationale, and the Court creates no heightened scrutiny or reversed proof burden.

The complaint alleges that the Village intentionally demanded a 33-foot easement for water service while requiring only 15 feet from materially similar owners, and that the additional demand was irrational and wholly arbitrary. Those allegations suffice independently of the separate spite theory. The allegation of reckless disregard is an alternative; it is not held sufficient to replace intent. The Village's acceptance of 15 feet about three months later proves neither original irrationality nor liability. Ordinary pleading rules govern, without a new requirement to plead evidence or prove every conceivable factual issue in the complaint.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the alternative subjective-ill-will theory or find intent, material similarity, irrationality or damages. The class-of-one description identifies an individual claim; it does not assert that only one owner was affected. The holding creates no constitutional negligence action, general administrative tort or separate substantive-due-process entitlement.

#### Precedent treatment

- Sioux City Bridge Co. v. Dakota County: applied for equal protection of an individual against intentional unequal administration, without requiring a separately defined group.
- Allegheny Pittsburgh Coal Co. v. County Commission: applied for deliberate disparate treatment of similarly situated persons; its principle is not limited to a showing of subjective spite.
- FCC v. Beach Communications: its reasonably conceivable legitimate-ground rule, challenger burden and rejection of compulsory formal findings remain.
- Heller v. Doe: applied for the rational relationship to the actual disadvantage; relevant material differences and ordinary government latitude remain distinct from irrational selection.
- Romer v. Evans: applied for objective evaluation of the imposed disadvantage without a required finding about every actor's subjective hostility; no heightened tier is added.

#### Resulting law and limits

The ordinary rational-basis requirement reaches intentional individualized unequal treatment satisfying the stated comparator and irrationality elements. Subjective ill will is not an additional element of that claim. Existing deference to reasonably conceivable legitimate distinctions remains, and pleading sufficiency establishes neither the elements as facts nor liability or relief.

#### Source and record limits

[Allegations and proceedings, 528 U.S. 562](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-528/pdf/USREPORTS-528-562.pdf). The complaint includes other affected owners; the allegations and alternative intent descriptions remain distinct from findings.

### Shalala v. Illinois Council on Long Term Care, Inc. — 2000-02-29

[Record](../records/shalala-v-illinois-council_merits_2000-02-29.md)

#### Event, question and statutory setting

**Shalala v. Illinois Council on Long Term Care, Inc.**, No. 98-1109. Argued November 8, 1999; decided February 29, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 143 F.3d 1072, which reversed the jurisdictional dismissal of an association of approximately 200 Illinois nursing homes' action.

The question is whether the incorporated Medicare review channel bars the otherwise proper federal-question action when the claims properly before the Court have no safe, practical route to meaningful judicial review without catastrophic exposure. The complaint challenges regulatory requirements and administrative procedures; this decision concerns access to judicial review, not the regulations' validity.

Section 1395cc(h)(1) gives an institution or agency dissatisfied with a determination that it is not a provider of services, or with a determination described in subsection (b)(2), reasonable notice and a hearing to the same extent as §405(b), followed by §405(g) review of the final decision after hearing. References to the Commissioner of Social Security and the Social Security Administration become references to the Secretary and the Department of Health and Human Services in applying those provisions and §405(l). Paragraph (h)(2) withholds entitlement to separate notice and hearing opportunities under both §1320a–7 and §1395cc when the determinations rest on the same underlying facts and issues; it does not itself erase judicial review. No such overlapping determination is established here. Section 405(g)'s final-decision/hearing requirement, sixty-day period after mailing of notice or authorized extension, and substantial-evidence rule and power to affirm, modify or reverse with or without rehearing remain. The ordinary venue is the district of residence or principal place of business, with the District of Columbia alternative when neither lies in a judicial district. Its separately conditioned remands also remain: a pre-answer motion by the Secretary for good cause, or additional material new evidence with good cause for its earlier omission. This judgment neither applies those review procedures to a individual provider determination not before the Court nor converts them into an unconditional immediate-access guarantee.

#### Particular Medicare determinations ordinarily use the prescribed review route

**Holding and operative rule:** A particular Medicare reimbursement or reopening claim within the statutory scheme ordinarily must use the review route Congress provided; describing the claim as procedural does not by itself place it outside that route. Your Home's rule for a discretionary reopening request after an original review opportunity remains effective at that scope.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices; all nine support this common baseline. They do not all join Part II's construction of the broader channel or its practical-foreclosure exception.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1395ii incorporates designated Social Security provisions into Medicare to the same extent as applicable, with the specified institutional substitutions. Section 405(h) separately makes post-hearing findings and decisions binding on the parties, restricts review to the provided routes, and bars actions under §§1331 and 1346 to recover on claims arising under the incorporated statutory scheme. Your Home applied that structure to an effort to reopen particular reimbursement determinations after an available initial appeal; relabeling that claim did not create federal-question jurisdiction. This common proposition does not itself settle the distinct general regulatory challenge here. The APA supplies no independent jurisdiction, and the Court identifies neither a new clear duty supporting mandamus nor a hypothetical alternative administrative route.

#### Channeling cannot eliminate the practical possibility of meaningful review

**Holding and operative rule:** General procedural or constitutional attacks on Medicare regulations are not categorically exempt from the incorporated review channel merely because they challenge regulations rather than individual payments. But that channel does not displace otherwise proper §1331 jurisdiction where, as applied to the claims properly presented, it offers no safe and practical path to meaningful judicial review and obtaining a reviewable vehicle requires catastrophic exposure; ordinary cost, delay, exhaustion or individual hardship despite a realistically available route is insufficient.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court is joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, giving five direct joins to this construction and its confined application. Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas agree with the judgment on their different statutory ground and do not join this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Michigan Academy preserves judicial review against statutory construction that would eliminate it altogether. Thunder Basin requires attention to the actual route and injury, rather than an assumption that eventual review is always meaningful; its Mine Act result depended on review that could realistically be obtained. Your Home likewise involved an original opportunity to contest particular reimbursement determinations. Here the prescribed route is practically unavailable for the claims before the Court: obtaining a reviewable vehicle requires catastrophic exposure rather than ordinary expense, delay or exhaustion. That is a practical denial of review, not ordinary hardship in pursuing an available channel. The enacted arising-under restriction still performs its ordinary channeling work; the Court construes its incorporation consistently with the statutory provision for meaningful judicial review rather than invalidating it. The Secretary's asserted interest in orderly administration cannot supply a route that is absent on this record. No Mine Act temporary-relief mechanism is imported into Medicare, no general immediate-review entitlement follows, and the statutory distinction between particular determinations and general rules alone does not decide this Court's rationale.

#### Jurisdictional affirmance preserves the separate limits on claims and relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The Seventh Circuit's jurisdictional judgment is affirmed for the claims properly before the Court, leaving regulatory merits and independent conditions for relief to the lower courts. The unchallenged determination that the APA notice-and-comment claim is ripe remains; vagueness remains unripe, Medicaid remains outside this review, and the other claims retain the required further ripeness inquiry.

**Authority:** Part I's scope and disposition portions of Stone-Zsela's Court opinion have all nine Justices' direct support. The judgment is unanimous despite the different sufficient jurisdictional grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** A forum to test legality is not a finding that the regulations are invalid. The Council's complaint includes statutory, procedural due-process and notice-and-comment objections, but the jurisdictional question does not erase each claim's own limits. The Secretary did not seek review of the APA ripeness determination, and the practical-access premise neither ripens vagueness nor brings Medicaid into the case. The lower courts must proceed within the surviving scope, considering the remaining independent questions. The judgment awards no sanctions, enjoins no regulation, guarantees no ultimate relief and establishes no additional provider-specific violation.

#### Precedent treatment

- **Bowen v. Michigan Academy of Family Physicians:** Applied by the controlling opinion to preserve review against total practical foreclosure. The separate regulatory-challenge construction advanced by four Justices is not the Court's holding.
- **Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich:** Applied for its requirement of meaningful review through the actual statutory route. Its Mine Act channel and bounded temporary-relief provisions are not transferred to Medicare.
- **Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala:** Its particular-reimbursement/reopening rule, arising-under treatment and lack of independent APA jurisdiction remain effective; an available original review opportunity distinguishes it from the foreclosure here.
- **Califano v. Sanders:** Its rule that the APA supplies no independent grant of subject-matter jurisdiction remains applicable; this decision rests on otherwise proper §1331 jurisdiction and the construed statutory bar.

#### Resulting law and limits

Effective February 29, 2000, the Medicare channel ordinarily governs arising-under regulatory as well as particular-payment claims, but cannot displace otherwise proper federal-question review when the actual statutory route practically forecloses meaningful review through the catastrophic exposure established here. Mere expense, delay, ordinary exhaustion, or hardship while meaningful review remains realistically available does not suffice. Your Home's particular-reopening rule remains. Five Justices directly adopt the controlling construction; the four-Justice alternative for general regulatory challenges creates no broader controlling exception. The two rationales are not combined into a new rule: the Court's foreclosure ground can cover claims outside the regulatory category, while the concurrence's regulatory ground can operate without catastrophe. The judgment decides neither regulatory validity nor an independent requirement for relief.

#### Source and record limits

The [official report and statutory appendix](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-529/pdf/USREPORTS-529-1.pdf) supply the public procedural history and statutory setting. The [1999 United States Code, §1395cc](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1999-title42/pdf/USCODE-1999-title42-chap7-subchapXVIII-partD-sec1395cc.pdf) supplies both provider-review paragraphs, including the duplicate-hearing qualification. The practical absence of a safe review route is the case's governing factual premise; descriptions of a minor-penalty route do not establish an available path for the claims adjudicated here. The decision makes no finding of a particular facility's regulatory violation and resolves no Medicaid or unripe vagueness claim.

### United States v. Morrison / Brzonkala v. Morrison — 2000-05-15

[Record](../records/united-states-v-morrison-brzonkala-v-morrison_merits_2000-05-15.md)

#### Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed in both consolidated dockets, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Fourth Circuit's constitutional dismissal of the presented §13981 private-assault claim is affirmed. The Court rejects both asserted sources of power for that claim. No new merits or damages proceeding is ordered, and matters outside the grant remain undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas | Affirms the constitutional dismissal in Nos. 99-5 and 99-29 | Complete Commerce and Section 5 grounds, statutory scope and remedy limits |
| Dissent | Breyer | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | Would reverse the constitutional dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of the statutory claim | Rational substantial-class-effects Commerce proposal; expressly reserves ultimate Section 5 validity |

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, supplies five direct votes for both constitutional holdings and their stated limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent on Commerce grounds and reserve the Section 5 alternative; their reservation is not a vote sustaining that alternative.

##### Commerce authority for the enacted private-assault action

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress's findings of the serious economic consequences of gender-motivated violence do not supply the constitutionally adequate interstate connection needed to sustain §13981's presented private-assault action. The enacted class is not directed at an actual interstate market, shown reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of an identified valid interstate regulatory regime, or supported as a substantial interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably internalize; downstream economic magnitude cannot replace that missing connection.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires the Court to identify the class Congress regulated and an eligible interstate mechanism before measuring aggregate effects. The class here is gender-motivated crimes of violence. Congress documented serious consequences for work, travel, consumption, treatment and productivity. The Court does not dispute their gravity or empirical force. But the statute covers the assault whether or not its object is commercial service or market participation. American Life League and Bird concern interference directed at actual medical services; Robinson concerns operating business assets under its enacted commerce element; Condon concerns an identified information market. Those connections do not extend to every assault with later economic costs.

Hale, Kirk, Hiley and Gillespie permit fitted inclusion needed to protect valid interstate supply restrictions. This civil action has no demonstrated comparable role. A broad legislative package does not by itself make every included remedy necessary to an interstate regime. The Court may not invent the missing anti-evasion relationship.

Home Builders supplies a genuine noneconomic route: materially supported, substantial cross-border effects that States cannot reasonably internalize, with a reasonable fit between the enacted prohibition and that spillover. Nationwide prevalence and the desirability of a national remedy do not establish those distinct conditions. The supported findings here do not identify the required cross-border mechanism and state inability. The claim that violence is a national economic barrier does not change the class or supply that missing mechanism.

The Court therefore rejects the four dissenters' proposed substitution of rational aggregate class effects for Lopez's prerequisite. It adopts no categorical bar on noneconomic conduct or traditional state subjects, universal jurisdictional-element requirement, individual border-crossing rule, numerical threshold or findings checklist. No concrete eligible mechanism awaits an unresolved application below. Hinds's separate statutory property-use inquiry cannot insert a commerce element into §13981, and the Court orders no new opportunity to invent a constitutional connection.

##### Section 5 and the relationship to discriminatory state protection

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 5 does not sustain the presented nationwide action against a private assailant for gender-motivated violence when its defendant, triggering private wrong and relief lack a sufficient remedial relationship to the unconstitutional state denial invoked to support it. A private assailant's gender animus does not itself establish the State's constitutional denial, and Congress's account of discriminatory state justice does not, without that fit, convert liability for the private assault into enforcement of equal state protection.

**Controlling explanation:** The Civil Rights Cases and Harris (1883) distinguish prohibited state conduct from unsupported private violence. State action includes official administration, judicial action and customs carrying legal force; it is not limited to a discriminatory statute. Those decisions do not categorically forbid every corrective remedy involving a private defendant or require Congress to await an individual completed violation.

Boerne requires congruence and proportionality between the constitutional injury and the preventive or remedial means. Florida Prepaid permits coverage beyond independently provable violations when the remedy is fitted to the States' own property deprivations and the actual structure of redress. College Savings preserves the need for a real constitutional injury, rather than a statutory tort alone. Thus neither individual proof of a state violation in every case nor a universal geographic limitation, sunset or findings quota is required.

The strongest defense is that a federal civil avenue can substitute for biased state protection, while requiring each victim first to prove discrimination could defeat prevention. Congress's documented state-justice concerns, the serious violence, animus requirement and civil relief all matter. The defect is not that Congress identified no state wrong. It is that liability and relief follow the private assault whether the State denies equal protection or faithfully provides it. The assailant's animus does not identify discriminatory official administration. The action neither replaces a specified constitutionally defective procedure nor defines a supported class of official practices to which its coverage and relief are fitted.

That gap distinguishes Florida Prepaid's preventive remedy; nationwide breadth alone is not the defect. The Court decides the private-assault action presented, not every hypothetical application to a person acting under color of state law, an unpresented severability issue or a new state-action claim. Saenz supplies no official intervention in this complaint and no rule resolving congressional prophylaxis. Its direct-claim predicates are not imported as universal conditions for preventive legislation.

##### Statutory scope, remedies and reservations

The action concerns a crime of violence committed because of or on the basis of gender and due at least in part to gender animus. The referenced violence includes acts or a series constituting a felony against the person, or a felony against property presenting a serious risk of physical injury, within the State or federal offenses described in 18 U.S.C. §16; it also includes otherwise qualifying conduct excluded only because of the relationship between actor and victim. The definition does not require occurrence in federal maritime, territorial or prison jurisdiction. Gender motivation must be shown by a preponderance of the evidence; random violence unrelated to gender is excluded. No prior criminal complaint, prosecution or conviction is required.

Section 13981(c) includes a person acting under color of state statute, ordinance, regulation, custom or usage and provides compensatory and punitive damages, injunctions, declarations and other appropriate relief. Federal and state courts share jurisdiction. Neither §13981(c) nor 28 U.S.C. §1367, by reason of this claim, confers federal jurisdiction over state-law claims seeking a divorce, alimony, equitable distribution of marital property or child-custody decree. None of these distinct provisions supplies an omitted interstate-commerce or unconstitutional-state-denial element.

The Court affirms the constitutional dismissal of the private-assault claim in both consolidated dockets. It makes no finding that the alleged assault, gender animus, causation or liability has been established and awards no damages. It neither rewrites the action by inserting a commerce or state-failure element nor decides a severability theory absent from the case. The separate Title IX university litigation, state remedies, other VAWA provisions and hypothetical color-of-law applications remain outside this judgment. No remand for new legislative proof, particular hearing or additional pleading is ordered.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **United States v. Lopez (April 26, 1995):** Applied its enacted-class, eligible-connection and fit sequence and its rejection of downstream magnitude as a substitute for connection. The Court declines the dissent's proposed revision; the rule remains controlling.
- **American Life League v. Reno; United States v. Bird:** Distinguished direct interference with an actual interstate medical-service market from the enacted class of private assault. Their service-market holdings remain effective.
- **United States v. Robinson; Reno v. Condon:** Distinguished operating business assets with an enacted commerce element and the identified interstate information market. Neither makes a victim's eventual spending or a crime's costs a sufficient connection.
- **United States v. Hale; United States v. Kirk; Hiley v. Barrett; United States v. Gillespie:** Preserved their fitted anti-evasion or material-impairment relationships to valid interstate supply regimes. The Court finds no analogous identified relationship for this action; domestic violence alone was not their constitutional mechanism.
- **National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt:** Applied its independent substantial-cross-border-externality route and evidentiary/fit requirements. The route remains available for noneconomic conduct; national prevalence is not its substitute, and its particular existing-record remand created no general right to another evidentiary chance.
- **United States v. Hinds:** Distinguished its statutory property-use inquiry from the constitutional mechanism and text of §13981. It supplies no judicially inserted element here.
- **The Civil Rights Cases, 109 U.S. 3 (1883); United States v. Harris, 106 U.S. 629 (1883):** Applied the boundary between state constitutional denial and unsupported private wrong, retaining their recognition of official administration and appropriately adapted corrective legislation. Neither is read as a categorical private-defendant bar. The 1883 Harris is distinct from the later Commerce case bearing that name.
- **City of Boerne v. Flores:** Applied its congruence-and-proportionality requirement and prohibition on replacing the substantive right while retaining preventive coverage beyond individually proven violations. No geographic, temporal or findings-count prerequisite is added.
- **Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank:** Distinguished its upheld preventive patent-property remedy, tied to the States' deprivations, exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative redress. That holding remains intact.
- **College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board:** Applied the requirement of an actual constitutional injury rather than a statutory tort alone.
- **Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents:** Its reservation of Section 5 supplies no decision of this question.
- **Saenz v. Heldenfels Brothers, Inc. (May 9, 2000):** Distinguished an official's own affirmative removal of protection from private harm not prevented by the State. It supplies neither such an act in this complaint nor a holding on Congress's preventive enforcement power.

#### Law After Decision

Effective May 15, 2000, the presented §13981 private-assault action cannot proceed under either asserted source of congressional power. The decision applies Lopez and the developed market, necessary-regime and externality routes without adopting a categorical economic/noneconomic division. The connection-before-magnitude prerequisite survives the dissent's proposal to revise it.

Under Section 5, the action's particular defendant, trigger and relief lack the required relationship to the state equality violation invoked. Boerne's preventive latitude and the fitted remedy upheld in Florida Prepaid remain governing law; the Court adds no universal private-defendant, individual-violation, geographic or findings-count prohibition. Other constitutional applications, other VAWA provisions, Title IX and state remedies are not invalidated by this judgment.

### Rice v. Cayetano — 2000-02-23

[Record](../records/rice-v-cayetano_merits_2000-02-23.md)

**Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Souter, concurring in the judgment.** They agree that this actual election cannot escape the Fifteenth Amendment but do not join the Court's sufficient state-office rationale. Their ground is the failure of the asserted political-beneficiary analogy on the actual statutory arrangement. The Admission Act trust serves public education, native-Hawaiian betterment, widespread farm and home ownership, public improvements and lands for public use. OHA administers a dedicated twenty-percent revenue share for native-Hawaiian betterment, as well as state and federal appropriations and donations supporting the broader Hawaiian class. Those real trust and beneficiary features cannot be ignored. But the State's broad descent-only electorate, its public functions and its mixed obligations do not sufficiently establish membership in the political beneficiary community invoked to justify exclusion. Morton v. Mancari does not validate that state-created definition merely because it uses descent. The difference between the two ancestry definitions alone is not decisive. They prescribe no blood quantum, reject no genuine indigenous membership rule and reserve a differently constituted federal or political relationship. Their ground supports the same bounded reversal and remand.

**Justice Stevens, dissenting; Justice Ginsburg joins the federal-relationship ground only.** Stevens would affirm because Congress's special responsibility toward indigenous peoples, implemented through Hawaii's trustee arrangements, makes the qualification political and fiduciary in this context. Morton v. Mancari's recognition of indigenous participation in a governing agency does not depend on transplanting a formal-recognition prerequisite into every analogous relationship. The entrusted resources, beneficiary connection and cultural continuity support the elected trustees' relationship to the community they serve. He accepts the prohibition of actual racial disfranchisement but rejects treating this arrangement as ordinary racial control of public government. In his additional ancestry-and-context discussion, which Ginsburg does not join, Stevens distinguishes inherited community and beneficiary ties here from exclusion designed to perpetuate racial political dominance. Benevolent purpose alone would not excuse a racial franchise; the asserted political relationship does the legal work.

**Justice Ginsburg, dissenting.** She shares Stevens's federal-indigenous relationship ground, without joining every additional ancestry, inherited-property or historical-purpose rationale. In her view, Congress's special responsibility and its delegation to Hawaii adequately support the beneficiary-linked electorate under Morton v. Mancari. She would sustain this arrangement under both the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendments; the federal relationship is the justification, not a general exception for well-intentioned racial voting restrictions.

No further Supreme Court matter or supplied cross-case dependency is created. The public election rule does not alter a benefit or tribal-election rule by implication.

### Village of Willowbrook v. Olech — 2000-02-23

[Record](../records/village-of-willowbrook-v-olech_merits_2000-02-23.md)

**Justice Breyer, concurring in the judgment.** He agrees that an individual can invoke equal protection and that this complaint should proceed. It additionally alleges vindictive action following the owners' success in earlier unrelated litigation. That circumstance permits affirmance without deciding whether the objective disparity allegations alone suffice throughout ordinary discretionary administration. His concern is that ordinary municipal errors or disputed discretionary choices could otherwise become constitutional litigation. Allegheny Pittsburgh supports the individual-equality principle, but he reserves the objective rule's reach without an additional limiting circumstance and does not join the conclusion that subjective ill will is unnecessary for the claim stated here. He does not hold that hostility is indispensable in every equal-protection case. The spite allegation remains unproved, and his concurrence supplies no finding of liability or damages.

No new Supreme Court matter is created. The ordinary rational-basis standard, deference and burden remain; the decision adds its stated individualized-treatment and pleading operation without deciding any religious-program, benefit-eligibility or distinct heightened-scrutiny question.

### Shalala v. Illinois Council on Long Term Care, Inc. — 2000-02-29

[Record](../records/shalala-v-illinois-council_merits_2000-02-29.md)

**Justice Thomas, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Justices Stevens and Kennedy, and by Justice Scalia except for the additional presumption favoring preenforcement review.** They join the Court's particular-determination baseline and claim-specific disposition, but would resolve the general regulatory challenge through Michigan Academy's construction of §1395ii. On that view, incorporation distinguishes an attack on a particular Medicare determination from a general challenge to the Secretary's instructions and regulations; the latter may proceed under §1331 subject to ordinary ripeness. Your Home concerns a particular reimbursement decision and does not erase that distinction. Catastrophic exposure reinforces the reason to permit access here, but is unnecessary to their statutory ground. Thomas, Stevens and Kennedy additionally rely on the presumption favoring judicial review, including preenforcement review when statutory ambiguity remains. They retain ordinary ripeness and do not demand immediate review of every Medicare question. Scalia withholds this additional rationale. These four do not adopt the Court's general channeling proposition or its practical-foreclosure construction merely because each produces affirmance here.

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins Thomas's full concurrence and the Court's common baseline and disposition. His additional explanation distinguishes an action to recover benefits from this provider association's challenge to governing regulations. The text's recovery language and the provider/beneficiary distinction support the Michigan Academy construction; the general statutory and procedural objections are not converted into a claim for an individual benefit payment. The opinion preserves the particular-payment rules and the independent ripeness limits, and decides no regulatory merits.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins Thomas's statutory construction but not its additional preenforcement-review presumption. Even though he questions Michigan Academy's original interpretation, its settled statutory construction supplies the sufficient ground here. Preenforcement review is a background rule that reasonable statutory implication may displace, distinct from the stronger presumption against elimination of all judicial review. His earlier constitutional concern in Thunder Basin remains unnecessary to decide because the available statutory ground resolves this case; he does not vote to invalidate the Medicare channel.

Supreme Court review of the jurisdictional question is complete. The Seventh Circuit's reversal of the jurisdictional dismissal stands, and the action proceeds below within the surviving claims and ripeness limits. The district court must decide properly presented remaining questions through ordinary proceedings; the Court orders no administrative waiver or guaranteed merits remedy.

### United States v. Morrison / Brzonkala v. Morrison — 2000-05-15

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.** The dissent would uphold the action under the Commerce Clause and reverse the power-based dismissal. Congress could rationally find that the regulated class of gender-motivated violence substantially obstructs participation in interstate labor, travel, education and service markets, and choose reasonable means against the conduct creating that obstruction. The inquiry would retain judicial review, substantiality, a rational class-wide relationship and reasonable means; it would not make a congressional label, national importance or any conceivable economic consequence conclusive.

This is a proposed change to Lopez's connection-before-magnitude prerequisite, not an application that leaves that prerequisite intact. The dissenters maintain the broader class-effects view expressed in their Lopez dissent. In their judgment, requiring an independently eligible mechanism before considering the supported class-wide effects excludes a reasonable congressional response to this demonstrated economic barrier. They would displace that restriction to the extent it forecloses the rational substantial-class-effects ground adopted here. Their proposal would preserve market, necessary-regime and externality holdings as sufficient grounds without making them exclusive prerequisites to this broader approach.

Their joins in Condon, Gillespie and other market or supply cases do not withdraw the broader position merely because those cases had an additional sufficient connection. Ginsburg and Breyer's Home Builders joins in Parts II–IV operated under controlling Part I while expressly preserving their disagreement with its restriction. The dissent does not relabel national prevalence as proof of a noninternalizable externality, invent an anti-evasion necessity, or assume new legislative facts. It rejects the Court's treatment of connection and magnitude on the supported record.

The dissent reserves Section 5 because Commerce authority would independently require reversal. Congress's account of discriminatory state protection and the preventive latitude recognized by Boerne and Florida Prepaid make substitution for biased state remedies a substantial argument. That observation is not a holding that §13981 satisfies Section 5, nor a claim that every private defendant is excluded or every claimant must prove a completed state violation. No additional legislative-process test is adopted. Reversal would return the statutory claim to ordinary adjudication, without establishing assault, animus, causation or damages and without guaranteeing a particular hearing, trial or award.
