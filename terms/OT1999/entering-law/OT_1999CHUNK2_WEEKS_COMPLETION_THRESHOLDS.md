## Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md; selected heading: Gray v. Netherland, 518 U.S. 152

#### Gray v. Netherland, 518 U.S. 152

**Docket or dockets:** 95-6510
**Decided:** June 20, 1996

##### Did reopening the due-process claim also reopen the independently dismissed Brady ground?

**Holding:** The partial amendment reopening the identified Sorrell due-process claim while expressly preserving other dismissals did not reopen the independent Brady ground. That dismissal stays outside general availability or merits reconsideration, while related evidence remains usable to prove the distinct live preparation injury and its effect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, all eight Associate Justices; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment. Dismissal left undisturbed, 9–0.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shared evidence does not merge claims or satisfy separate procedural predicates. Reported uncontested state-habeas factual availability does not resolve trial counsel's disputed knowledge. No new suppression, materiality, appellate-abandonment or comprehensive state-default finding is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kyles's collective lawful-use rule applies to available Brady claims and does not reopen dismissals. Picard, Anderson and Vasquez require claim-specific comparison; Coleman preserves established independent and adequate state claim defaults.

##### Is meaningful opportunity to answer this capital evidence a rule dictated at finality?

**Holding:** The established opportunity to deny or explain material capital-sentencing information is denied when supported State assurances and a materially different last-minute presentation deprive the defendant of a real opportunity to investigate and answer. This supported loss of protected function, rather than a fixed disclosure interval, invokes law dictated before October 5, 1987 finality and is not a new rule barred by Teague.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer; six. O'Connor's join does not extend to the final violation, effect or remedy.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal notice period, automatic exclusion or relief for mere surprise is created. No Teague exception is invoked or abolished. Separate affirmative-deception formulations still require their own proper presentation and legal predicates.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gardner supplies the prefinality opportunity to respond; eventual public disclosure is not necessarily effective response. Weatherford's no-general-discovery rule remains. Graham supplies functional comparison, not new law inserted at finality; Teague and Caspari retain the dictated-at-finality inquiry and both contemporary exceptions for substantive restrictions and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness.

##### Are presentation, denial of opportunity and prejudicial effect established here?

**Holding:** The reopened claim fairly encompasses the preparation injury, and accepted existing findings establish denial of the protected opportunity and prejudice in selecting death. Neither absence of a formal continuance request nor surviving vileness eligibility defeats relief on this record; no newly recast deception or dismissed Brady claim must first succeed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five.

**Material application:** State Claim IX, the state appeal and federal Ground A presented delayed disclosure, impaired preparation and unfairness. Accepted evidence supplements that same injury. The prosecutor's statements-only assurance, reasonable reliance and last-minute photographs, police and medical-examiner evidence impaired investigation, impeachment, objections and response. Actual preparation objections and exclusion requests were made; the absent formal continuance motion establishes no feasible cure or adequate bar. The findings establish substantial probability of a different sentence, substantial injurious influence, and inability to find beyond reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal collateral error-effect standard is selected because relief follows under both competing approaches. No structural-error category, automatic exclusion of all uncharged-crime evidence, other-perpetrator finding or universal exhaustion of related theories is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Picard and Anderson require claim substance, not identical language; Vasquez permits supplementation without fundamental alteration while retaining its deliberate-withholding reservation. Keeney retains Townsend's fifth hearing circumstance for inadequately developed material facts of a properly presented claim, subject to Fay's knowing, considered choice by the prisoner to bypass state process. Ordinary counsel negligence or counsel's choice without prisoner participation is not automatically bypass. Established deliberate bypass permits refusal of the otherwise required hearing rather than requiring denial; Coleman remains distinct. Tuggle separates lawful eligibility from actual sentence selection. Brecht's preserved-Doyle and O'Neal's personal-intent holdings retain their limits; Chapman and Kotteakos supply the two satisfied competing effect inquiries without a general extension.

##### How must conditional sentencing relief be implemented?

**Holding:** The established violation and prejudicial effect require reversal of the adverse due-process disposition and restoration and implementation of conditional death-sentence relief. Before an operative writ, any genuinely applicable, unadjudicated statutory condition material to relief must be resolved; once satisfied or inapplicable, Virginia must provide a constitutionally adequate new penalty proceeding within a reasonable prospective District Court period or a lawful noncapital disposition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five. Reversed in part and remanded, 5–4, on this claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The established constitutional violation and effect are not returned for unrestricted reconsideration. Actually applicable amended §2254(d), state-fact-presumption or chapter 154 conditions require temporal and predicate analysis; §107(c)'s pending-case clause neither establishes Virginia qualification nor resolves chapter 153 transition. No express exhaustion waiver, statutory exception or satisfaction is presumed. The completed 1994 hearing differs from a new hearing; none is authorized or exempted categorically from §2254(e)(2). The reservation concerns actual legal conditions, not hypothetical impediments.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The old 180-day clock is neither reinstated nor declared expired. No guilt retrial, acquittal, fixed replacement sentence, release from independent lawful custody, new Supreme Court stay or execution authorization follows. Independent Brady, Smallwood, investigator-assistance, guilt-sufficiency and ungranted vileness-instruction rulings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Graham and Simmons preserve sentence-only relief and unaffected guilt. Landgraf requires provision-specific temporal analysis; Keeney does not erase applicable later statutory restrictions. No general AEDPA temporal holding is made.

## Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md; selected heading: Lambrix v. Singletary

#### Lambrix v. Singletary

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-5658.
**Decided:** 1997-05-12.

##### The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense

**Holding:** Procedural default and Teague nonretroactivity are distinct, nonjurisdictional limits, with no inflexible rule requiring one always to precede the other. In this case the Court decides the accepted Teague issue while reserving the disputed state-default predicates; that choice neither excuses default nor decides the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The State did not wholesale waive default. No jurisdictional defect is bypassed and neither universal default-first nor Teague-first sequencing is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Stringer permits old-rule adjudication before unresolved default without prescribing sequence; Schiro preserves nonjurisdictional nonretroactivity and Caspari distinguishes presentation from subsidiary-defense preservation.

##### This claim invokes an old guidance rule at November 24, 1986 finality

**Holding:** For Lambrix's judgment final on November 24, 1986, the claim that inadequate aggravator guidance in Florida's legally consequential capital jury recommendation can infect the final death sentence invokes an already dictated rule. Teague therefore does not bar this claim as new merely because the judge separately must exercise independent judgment; the ruling establishes no actual instructional defect, harmful influence or failed cure on this record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins. O'Connor joins once Teague is reached despite disagreeing with sequence; Stone-Zsela takes no position on this question.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Judicial independence remains an additional protection, not proof that the influential jury recommendation disappears. No actual defect, harmful transmission or failed cure is found, no new Teague exception is adopted, and current comparison cases are not treated as available at 1986 finality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Godfrey's prefinality meaningful-guidance duty, applied through Teague/Caspari's precise comparison and Stringer's functional analysis, governs. Espinosa's influential-recommendation rule remains distinct from this finality question. Spaziano and Baldwin preserve judicial sentencing without automatic cure; Zant's nonweighing and Barclay's state-law invalidity settings do not authorize uncured unconstitutional influence.

##### Vacatur returns the preserved default defense for resolution

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded for resolution of the preserved, unresolved state procedural-default defense and any applicable exception. If the state ground is adequate, independent, preserved and unexcused, relief must be denied on that ground; this common remand direction does not itself decide Teague, a constitutional violation or sentencing relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct joins at this common-remand scope.

**Material application:** Lambrix requested a limiting instruction at trial; the asserted state bar concerns failure to pursue the issue on direct appeal. State successive treatment of appellate-ineffectiveness claims and alternative merits rejection establish no federal cause finding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Coleman and Ford require the actual independent, adequate, preserved and unexcused state ground; Sawyer's clear-and-convincing death-eligibility gateway remains distinct from ordinary cause and prejudice and mere selection error.

##### The old-rule determination binds the subsequent proceedings

**Holding:** The Eleventh Circuit's disposition of the Espinosa claim is vacated, and the case is remanded with the new-rule ground conclusively rejected. The lower court must first resolve the preserved, unresolved procedural-default defense; if the claim is barred and unexcused it must deny relief on that ground, while an available claim proceeds to actual merits, effect and lawful correction subject to genuinely applicable unresolved statutory conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III-B, joined by O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes for the complete binding mandate. Stone-Zsela joins common vacatur but not this old-rule instruction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Teague cannot again bar this claim as new. If default permits review, decide actual defect, transmitted effect and lawful cure. An established violation requires genuine authorized reweighing or Chapman harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt addressing the recommendation's influence; valid aggravators, proportionality review or an independent-judge label alone do not suffice. If no lawful cure is available and relief is otherwise warranted, a constitutionally adequate new sentencing proceeding is required, but none is now ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Chapter 153 temporal reach, amended §2254(d), chapter 154 qualification and other genuinely applicable statutory conditions remain unresolved. No present writ, hearing, sentence vacatur, guilt retrial, release, stay or execution authorization issues; unrelated claims are untouched.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Espinosa and Stringer supply actual-cure requirements; Chapman supplies the State's harmlessness burden. Gray preserves genuinely applicable unresolved statutory gates before an operative writ.

## Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md; selected heading: Caspari v. Bohlen, 510 U.S. 383

#### Caspari v. Bohlen, 510 U.S. 383

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1500.

**Decided:** 1994-02-23.

##### The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here

**Holding:** In this habeas case, Missouri's consistently asserted Teague defense is fairly included as a subsidiary limit on applying the very constitutional rule and granting the writ challenged by the petition, although the stated question names the double-jeopardy issue rather than Teague. The Court therefore considers that nonjurisdictional defense; preservation, disclosure in the petition's body or logical priority alone does not make every independent omitted question reviewable.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Yee v. City of Escondido: applied to distinguish subsidiary legal arguments from distinct questions; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate requirements.
- Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.: preserved; an omitted independent question does not become reviewable merely through notice, logical priority or ordinary economy. This confined same-rule habeas limitation is distinguished from its separate intervention determination.
- Schiro v. Farley: distinguished on presentation; the defense there first appeared at merits briefing after earlier omissions. Its discretionary treatment of a nonjurisdictional defense remains intact.

##### The requested extension was a new rule at finality

**Holding:** Bohlen's conviction and sentence became final on January 2, 1986, and the rule forbidding renewed proof of persistent-offender status in this noncapital proceeding was not dictated by the precedent then in force. Federal habeas relief therefore depends on a recognized Teague exception; the Court does not decide whether the proposed double-jeopardy protection should govern a case eligible for a decision on the constitutional merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt the finality-specific new-rule determination, not a prospective rule permitting every noncapital sentencing rehearing.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applied to the date at which the state judgment became final and the distinction between dictated applications and new rules.
- Graham v. Collins: applied in its controlling form; a dictated application does not become new merely because the facts differ or courts disagree.
- Gilmore v. Taylor: applied to preserve the possibility of a genuinely new noncapital extension after substantive comparison.
- Bullington v. Missouri and Arizona v. Rumsey: preserved for acquittal-like capital adjudications; their extension to this noncapital status proceeding was not dictated at finality, and their ultimate reach is not decided.
- United States v. DiFrancesco: applied as prefinality support for distinguishing sentencing determinations from offense acquittals.
- Griffith v. Kentucky: used for the finality convention; direct review was exhausted and the certiorari period expired on January 2, with January 1 a legal holiday.
- United States v. Dixon: its retention of Grady remains in force but does not answer whether this sentencing-status proceeding has the necessary jeopardy character.

##### Neither Teague exception permits this writ

**Holding:** The proposed rule falls within neither the exception for rules excluding conduct or a class of persons or punishments from the State's substantive power, nor the exception for watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Its protection against renewed litigation of offender status does not remove the robberies or these authorized sentences from lawful punishment and does not supply a watershed accuracy procedure; the habeas grant on this new rule must therefore be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices apply both exceptions as they exist at this decision's date. Neither exception is eliminated or prospectively narrowed out of existence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane: applies both contemporary exceptions after finding a new rule; neither is abolished.
- Penry v. Lynaugh: applies the substantive exception's protection against punishment beyond the State's constitutional power; this claim does not satisfy it.
- Sawyer v. Smith: applies the distinction between an important procedural protection and the exceptional accuracy-and-fairness requirement.
- Bullington v. Missouri: preserved; refusing retroactive extension does not overrule its established capital-acquittal protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Both contemporary Teague exceptions remain. The State's consistently presented defense is subsidiary here; preservation, mention in the petition body and logical priority do not automatically make an omitted independent question reviewable. The ultimate constitutional reach of double jeopardy to the noncapital status hearing is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eighth Circuit's conditional habeas grant on this claim is reversed, 8–1, under Teague. No new State proof opportunity, replacement sentence or release deadline is prescribed, and unrelated properly preserved claims are not disturbed.

## Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md; selected heading: Trest v. Cain

#### Trest v. Cain

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-7901; review of Trest v. Whitley, Fifth Circuit No. 94-40515.
**Decided:** December 9, 1997.

##### Whether an appellate court must invoke an omitted state procedural-default defense

**Holding:** An appellate court is not obliged to raise state procedural default that the State has not asserted; default does not deprive the federal court of subject-matter jurisdiction. This absence of duty decides neither whether a court has discretion to raise the defense nor when such action would be proper, including in this case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The Fifth Circuit treated predicted Mississippi untimeliness as requiring exclusion and relied on Sones without party discussion. Affirmance on a discretionary ground the court did not give would replace its actual rationale and assume undeveloped predicates. Comity and federalism do not supply an independent command to invoke the defense.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Omission establishes neither intentional waiver nor automatic forfeiture. Actual adequate independent default, cause and prejudice, miscarriage of justice, plea validity and a route to challenge expired predicate convictions remain distinct and unresolved. Under Lindh, the covered chapter 153 amendments do not govern this noncapital application filed September 30, 1991; amended §2254(b)(3)'s exhaustion-waiver rule supplies no default rule here, while separately directed provisions retain their scope. Any lawful later consideration requires attention to the actual ground and an appropriate response opportunity, without a universal constitutional additional-briefing rule for every unargued issue.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit judgment is vacated for further proceedings free of the supposed mandatory duty. The Court grants no writ, invalidates no conviction, reduces no sentence and requires no automatic evidentiary hearing or predetermined default outcome.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lambrix establishes default's nonjurisdictional character and rejects inflexible sequencing; its preserved-defense remand does not require revival of an omitted defense. Granberry's treatment of omitted exhaustion supplies an analogy against compulsion, not a resolution of discretionary default initiative. Coleman retains its adequate-independent-ground predicates, supported anticipated-default inquiry and cause/prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice exceptions; its stay action establishes neither jurisdictional classification nor a new default. Schlup's innocence gateway remains distinct. Lindh supplies the temporal limit. Gray creates no general automatic forfeiture rule for omitted default defenses.

#### O'Sullivan v. Boerckel

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-2048; Seventh Circuit judgment, 135 F.3d 1194.
**Decided:** 1999-06-07.

##### I. An available ordinary discretionary petition is part of one complete round

**Holding:** Under the pre-AEDPA law governing this application, a prisoner must fairly present each federal claim through one complete round of the State's established ordinary appellate process, including a petition for discretionary review to its highest court when that petition is an ordinary available step. Former section 2254(b) preserves exceptions for absence of a state corrective process or circumstances making that process ineffective to protect the applicant's rights; actual state-law unavailability must be respected, and the rule does not require every extraordinary remedy or repetitive collateral presentation after a complete ordinary round.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, commands six direct votes.

##### II. Expiration of the omitted opportunity produces default, with existing gateways preserved

**Holding:** When a prisoner omits a claim from an otherwise required ordinary state-review step and the opportunity to use that step has expired, the claim is procedurally defaulted rather than awaiting exhaustion through a still-available remedy. Boerckel's three omitted claims fall within that rule, subject to recognized cause-and-prejudice and actual-innocence gateways at their existing scope; further proceedings must respect the actual prior gateway rulings and the governing pre-AEDPA law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the opinion of the Court; the same six-Justice coalition directly adopts the classification and remand.

##### III. The effect of an express state opt-out retaining occasional review remains open

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether a State may expressly identify an occasionally available discretionary procedure as outside its standard review process and state that prisoners need not invoke it before federal habeas. No such Illinois directive is established here. The decision neither requires a State to eliminate every possibility of occasional discretionary relief nor conclusively defines what an effective express opt-out must contain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. It is a five-Justice reservation, not an adopted rule resolving the hypothetical procedure's availability. Stone-Zsela does not join this reservation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lindh supplies pre-AEDPA chapter 153 for this application; the decision does not import amended exhaustion or evidentiary provisions merely because it is delivered after AEDPA.
- Picard's fair-presentation requirement is applied to each claim through the ordinary state-review round.
- Castille is distinguished: first presentation through a discretionary mechanism differs from omission at the next ordinary stage after proper presentation below. Its proper-presentation rule remains intact.
- Coleman supplies the distinction between an available unexhausted remedy and procedural default after an otherwise required route closes; it does not independently identify Illinois's required sequence.
- Lonchar preserves source-governed habeas procedure and does not itself decide which Illinois steps constitute exhaustion.
- Gray preserves separate treatment of distinct claims and defaults; it does not convert this exhaustion ruling into a decision of the constitutional merits.
- Lambrix preserves default as an independent procedural issue with its recognized gateway requirements; it does not convert this exhaustion ruling into a decision of the constitutional merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Illinois's ordinary petition for leave to appeal must include each federal claim sought to be preserved through a complete ordinary review round. Selective acceptance alone does not excuse omission. Expiration of that required opportunity produces default, subject to the existing gateways and actual procedural posture. Actual state-law unavailability and the former statute's absent-process and ineffective-process exceptions remain distinct from mere discretion. The effect of an express opt-out retaining occasional review is unresolved. No precedent is overruled, no universal duty to pursue extraordinary remedies is adopted and no new innocence standard is imposed.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 6–3. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Opposing: Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. The three omitted claims are procedurally defaulted because their ordinary Illinois review opportunity has expired, subject to the governing exceptions. Any properly preserved review of the existing innocence-gateway ruling must use pre-AEDPA law and consider the District Court's independent alternative ground. The Court awards no writ, new trial, release or new evidentiary hearing and decides no underlying federal claim.

### Exhaustion and merits denial under amended §2254

**Governing question:** State-remedy exhaustion and express waiver in §2254(b).

**Current rule:** Relief for a person in custody under a state judgment may not be granted unless state remedies have been exhausted, no available state corrective process exists, or circumstances render that process ineffective to protect the applicant’s rights. The court may deny on the merits despite nonexhaustion; it is not required to do so. A State is neither deemed to waive exhaustion nor estopped from relying on it unless its counsel expressly waives it.

**Authority by component:**

- Exhaustion alternatives, permissive merits denial and express counsel waiver: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §104(1), 28 U.S.C. §2254(b), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

**Present operation effective:** AEDPA enactment, April 24, 1996; Lindh, June 23, 1997, for the chapter 153 amendments at issue in preenactment noncapital §2254 applications. The separate §2255 and capital questions are not thereby decided.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

### Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review

**Governing question:** The order of two nonjurisdictional habeas defenses.

**Current rule:** Teague nonretroactivity and state procedural default are distinct nonjurisdictional limits. No inflexible priority requires either always to precede the other; an accepted Teague question may be resolved while disputed default predicates remain reserved. This does not excuse default, permit bypass of an established jurisdictional defect or decide constitutional merits. An appellate court has no mandatory duty to raise an omitted state procedural-default defense; the defense is not a subject-matter-jurisdiction defect.

**Authority by component:**

- Lambrix v. Singletary — I. The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense, 1997-05-12; Stevens with Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven

- An appellate court has no mandatory duty to raise an omitted state procedural-default defense: Trest v. Cain, 1997-12-09; Stevens for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Trest, 1997-12-09, for the no-duty rule; Lambrix, 1997-05-12, retains the sequencing rule.

**Exceptions and limits:** The specific old-rule conclusion for the influential capital recommendation at November 24, 1986 finality is an application, not a universal exception to Teague. Preserved adequate, independent and unexcused state grounds remain enforceable.

**Holdings navigation:** Lambrix v. Singletary (1997-05-12) — The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense. Trest v. Cain — Habeas procedural default and appellate initiative.

**Open question:** Trest leaves judicial authority to raise an omitted default, the proper exercise of any discretion, intentional waiver or forfeiture, actual default and applicable exceptions unresolved. Any lawful later consideration must address the actual ground with an appropriate opportunity to respond; no universal extra-briefing requirement is imposed.

### Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity

**Governing question:** Whether an omitted Teague defense must be entertained when first raised in Supreme Court merits briefing.

**Current rule:** Teague nonretroactivity is not jurisdictional. When the State omitted it below and in its certiorari opposition and first asserted it in merits briefing, the Court may decline consideration. The permission is discretionary, not a mandatory waiver rule. A consistently preserved subsidiary defense limiting the very constitutional rule and habeas relief under review presents the distinct fair-inclusion setting addressed in Caspari; preservation and fair inclusion remain separate.

- Preserved subsidiary-defense distinction: Caspari v. Bohlen — The preserved nonretroactivity defense is fairly included here, 1994-02-23. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices. Stevens disagrees with fair inclusion and would decline the defense under Rule 14.1(a).

**Present operation effective:** Schiro v. Farley, 1994-01-19, for discretionary treatment of the late defense. Caspari, 1994-02-23, supplies the distinct preserved-defense comparison, not a later change to that discretion.

**Related current rules:** “Presented questions, subsidiary arguments and exceptional Supreme Court review” supplies the question-presentation boundary. Neither decision changes Teague’s new-rule inquiry or either contemporary exception.

### One complete ordinary state-review round under former §2254

**Governing question:** What state review a prisoner must complete under the pre-AEDPA exhaustion law.

**Current rule:** Each federal claim must be fairly presented through one complete round of the State’s established ordinary appellate process, including discretionary review in its highest court when that petition is an ordinary available step. Selective acceptance or unlikely success does not make the filing unavailable. Actual state-law unavailability and the former statute’s absent-process and ineffective-process exceptions remain. Omission of a required step whose opportunity has expired produces procedural default, subject to the existing cause-and-prejudice and actual-innocence gateways, rather than an unexhausted still-available remedy.

**Authority by component:**

- O’Sullivan v. Boerckel — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “I. An available ordinary discretionary petition is part of one complete round”, 1999-06-07; Souter Parts I–II with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Part II supplies expiration/default classification; Part III’s five-Justice reservation excludes Stone-Zsela.

**Present operation effective:** O’Sullivan, June 7, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Every extraordinary remedy, repetitive collateral presentation after a complete round and a petition to the Supreme Court are not required by this rule. Former chapter 153 governs the source application; amended statutory provisions retain their own temporal scope.

**Open question:** The effect of an express State opt-out retaining occasional discretionary review remains unresolved; no universal formula for such an opt-out is adopted.
