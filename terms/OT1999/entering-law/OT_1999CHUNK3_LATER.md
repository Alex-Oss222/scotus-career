## Standards and Tests selection: Rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications

### Rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications

**Governing question:** What instructions govern rational-basis review of ordinary economic classifications?

**Current rule:** For an economic classification involving neither a suspect class nor a fundamental right under the question reviewed, a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis suffices and the challenger must negate it. Formal legislative findings are unnecessary. Conceivable differences in bargaining arrangements, regulatory costs or competition can justify a line without proof that every regulated entity shares a monopoly. Moreno and Cleburne retain their prohibition of irrational or impermissibly motivated classifications; this rule supplies no resolution of a distinct heightened-scrutiny or First Amendment claim.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

### Equal protection of commitment proof and family participation

**Governing question:** What instructions govern equal protection of commitment proof and family participation?

**Current rule:** Kentucky's clear-and-convincing burden for adult intellectual-disability commitment, compared with reasonable-doubt proof for mental illness, fails rational-basis review because the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not justify the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Lawful equalization and severability must be addressed; Addington's federal minimum is unchanged and no national reasonable-doubt requirement or automatic release follows. Separately, continuing family knowledge rationally supports guardians' and immediate relatives' party participation for lifelong developmental disability. Such participation is not categorically unfair where the adult has counsel and effective opportunity to contest evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives cannot replace statutory grounds, control decision or suppress the adult's position. Heightened scrutiny remains undecided.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

### Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

**Governing question:** Viewpoint-neutral access for private religious expression and the distinct establishment inquiry in the forums and benefits actually opened.

**Current rule:** When a public school admits private family-life and child-rearing discussion to an after-hours forum, it cannot exclude an otherwise eligible presentation solely for its religious viewpoint, even assuming a nonpublic forum. Neutral restrictions outside the forum's actual subjects remain possible. Equal access to voluntary, publicly accessible after-hours films without school sponsorship does not establish religion. That establishment holding concerns private speech, not an official devotional program; it supplies no replacement for the inherited establishment framework or universal ruling on worship restrictions. A State may not categorically exclude an otherwise eligible private unattended religious display from a forum actually opened to comparable expression merely because its message is religious or its location adjoins or lies within a capitol. Pinette’s access holding includes the proposal’s offered private-attribution safeguard; it establishes neither adequacy of a later sign nor a comprehensive establishment test. Chabad separately holds that an interior capitol location does not establish religion when actual context makes the expression clearly private. Actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preference or obscured private sponsorship can alter that conclusion; no forum label or unspecified sign decides attribution. In a generally available, privately edited student-publication program, a religious editorial viewpoint cannot exclude an otherwise eligible publication from printing support. Its secular expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent attribution and controlled vendor payments together permit that benefit; neutrality alone does not authorize devotional financing or governmental worship.

In a broad after-hours educational and community forum, an otherwise comparable private meeting cannot be excluded solely for its worship form; exact secular sacramental counterparts are not required. Genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral operational and activity limits remain available. An admitted instructional subject likewise cannot exclude comparable religious instruction merely while permitting religious discussion. Independent private worship in rented school space does not by itself establish religion; actual governmental adoption must be shown rather than inferred from religious identity or location.

## Standards and Tests selection: Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

### Religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny

**Governing question:** What instructions govern religious targeting, comparable secular conduct and strict scrutiny?

**Current rule:** A law is nonneutral when its text, definitions, exemptions and operation identify religious conduct for distinctive burdens even without naming the religion; adverse impact or the words ritual or sacrifice alone do not establish that trigger. Substantially leaving comparable secular conduct unregulated despite its threat to the asserted interests defeats general applicability at least to that extent. Nonneutral or nongeneral restrictions require compelling interests and narrow tailoring. Appreciable comparable harm left unregulated despite feasible controls defeats the selective compelling-interest showing, and available neutral narrower controls and underinclusion can independently defeat tailoring. Smith is not replaced by a general exemption regime; all boundaries of general applicability and exemption from genuinely general anti-cruelty laws remain open. The enactment-history plurality supplies no additional controlling test. A public-employment restriction alleged to burden sincere private religious exercise must receive its own neutrality/general-applicability inquiry rather than be absorbed into an employee-speech balance. Religious impact or sincerity alone does not establish targeting; lack of theological compulsion does not defeat sincere exercise.

## Standards and Tests selection: Parental educational choice and genuine public-school instruction

### Parental educational choice and genuine public-school instruction

**Governing question:** Whether parental liberty permits a substantive federal veto over particular public-school lessons.

**Current rule:** Parental liberty protects choosing lawful educational paths and parents' own instruction; it does not authorize each parent to prescribe every public-school lesson reasonably related to a genuine pedagogical objective. The inquiry must examine the actual alleged interference rather than treating compulsory attendance as either automatic constitutional injury or categorical curricular immunity.

**Authority by component:**

- Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. — The pleaded compulsory exposure does not establish the asserted parental-liberty deprivation, 1997-02-24; Ginsburg for all nine supplies the curricular boundary while preserving Meyer, Pierce, Curtis and Settle

**Present operation effective:** Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, 1997-02-24.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not approve pretextual curricular action or resolve independent state-law permission rights. Materially different compelled personal performance, affirmation, intimate disclosure, physical contact or treatment remain outside the adjudicated application.

**Holdings navigation:** Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. (1997-02-24) — The pleaded compulsory exposure does not establish the asserted parental-liberty deprivation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Mine Act channeling and meaningful judicial review

### Mine Act channeling and meaningful judicial review

**Governing question:** Whether anticipatory Mine Act representation-and-posting disputes may bypass the Commission and appellate review.

**Current rule:** Where Congress’s intent is fairly discernible from the Mine Act’s language, structure and purpose and the claims can receive meaningful review, an anticipatory challenge to representation and posting requirements must proceed through the independent Commission and then the court of appeals. Connection to enforcement, relevant agency expertise and actual review availability inform that scheme-specific inquiry, not three mandatory elements for every administrative statute. Review adequacy must reflect actual injury and access; collection only after review, expedition and bounded temporary relief supported the conclusion on the absence of demonstrated irreversible compliance injury. Delayed payment alone does not establish adequate review.

**Authority by component:**

- Statutory channel and inquiry form: Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich — This Mine Act dispute must follow the statutory review channel, 1994-01-19. Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, statutory-channel portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants adopt this proposition at the stated level of generality.
- Meaningful-review qualification and bounded interim routes: Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich — The available route does not impose an intolerable choice on this record, 1994-01-19. Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, meaningful-review and due-process portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven participants adopt this ground. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on a distinct constitutional formulation and are not counted as joining this explanation.

**Present operation effective:** Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 1994-01-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** Commission temporary relief under §815(b)(2) requires a hearing opportunity for each party, substantial likelihood of favorable findings and no adverse health-and-safety effect; relief is forbidden for §814(a) or (f) citations. Appellate relief under §816(a)(2) requires notice and hearing opportunity, substantial likelihood of success and protection of miners’ health and safety; it excludes §817(a) imminent-danger withdrawal orders and §814(a) or (f) citations. Section 816(c) makes review nonstaying. No stay of every posting citation, immunity from penalties or exclusion of every collateral constitutional claim is established.

## Standards and Tests selection: Review of Medicare reimbursement reopening refusals

### Review of Medicare reimbursement reopening refusals

**Governing question:** Which statutory routes permit review of an intermediary’s refusal to reopen a final Medicare reimbursement determination.

**Current rule:** A discretionary refusal to reopen is not a new final determination of total program reimbursement under §1395oo(a)(1)(A)(i). Judicial review of the Board’s final jurisdictional ruling under §1395oo(f)(1) permits deciding that boundary; it does not authorize Board review of the refusal or direct judicial calculation. A request for reopening to obtain additional Medicare payment arises under that scheme; describing it as procedural does not avoid incorporated §405(h)’s bar on the asserted §1331 route. ## Standards and Tests selection: Occupational-safety supplementation without an approved state plan

### Occupational-safety supplementation without an approved state plan

**Governing question:** Whether OSH Act §18 automatically preempts a supplementary state occupational-safety standard on an issue covered by a federal standard when the State lacks an approved plan.

**Current rule:** Occupational overlap and lack of an approved state plan do not alone establish preemption. The provisions require examination for an actual basis of displacement; neither overlap nor a dual worker-and-public-safety purpose is conclusive. This is a limited negative holding: it supplies no complete replacement test for conflict, no universal permission whenever simultaneous compliance is physically possible and no approval of every compatible state license. Surviving pre-divergence conflict principles, including Hines and Florida Lime, remain operative on their own terms.

**Authority by component:**
- Rejection of automatic displacement and provision-specific inquiry: Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, “1. Section 18 does not automatically displace compatible supplementation”, June 18, 1992; expressly shared proposition of Souter, Blackmun, Stevens, Thomas and Stone-Zsela (five). Souter's complete opinion has only four joins; no broader Marks rule is extracted.

**Present operation effective:** Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, June 18, 1992.

**Unresolved interaction:** Morales construes a different express airline clause and supplies no general anti-duplication objective for §18. Gade does not replace the independent force of an actual statutory ceiling, exclusive arrangement, savings provision or proved incompatibility; the competing writings do not establish a unified new positive conflict test.

**Holdings navigation:** Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, 505 U.S. 88 (1992) — Federal preemption.

## Standards and Tests selection: Supported categorical exclusion of polygraph credibility evidence

### Supported categorical exclusion of polygraph credibility evidence

**Governing question:** When does military Rule 707 permissibly restrict defense evidence?

**Current rule:** The right to present a defense allows evidentiary restrictions justified by legitimate trial interests and proportionate to their purposes. Supported serious disagreement about polygraph reliability permits Rule 707’s categorical exclusion of a credibility proffer when the accused remains able to present the underlying facts and personal account. The Constitution does not require an individualized admissibility hearing merely because the Government administered the examination. Rule 707(a) covers results, examiner opinions and references to offering, taking or failing to take an examination; subsection (b) preserves examination statements only when otherwise admissible.

**Authority by component:**

- Reliability-based categorical restriction and actual Rule 707 exception: United States v. Scheffer, No. 96-1133 — Supported reliability concerns justify this categorical polygraph rule against the presented constitutional challenge, 1998-03-31.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Scheffer, 1998-03-31.

**Exceptions and limits:** Permission to testify does not invariably establish a complete defense. No constitutional accuracy percentage, finding that every polygraph is unreliable, or Article 36(a) statutory-validity ruling follows; the three-Justice credibility-role and collateral-litigation rationales do not control.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Scheffer — Reliability restrictions on defense evidence.

## Standards and Tests selection: Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

### Preserved protected-silence and personal-intent errors on federal habeas review

**Governing question:** Harmlessness for the protected-silence, personal-intent and commutation-misinformation categories properly available on federal habeas.

**Current rule:** Use of post-Miranda silence to impeach a trial explanation violates Doyle and constitutes constitutional trial error rather than a nonconstitutional rule or structural defect. Permitted prewarning impeachment remains distinct. For preserved Doyle error properly before a federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that it did not contribute to the verdict, considering the whole record. Collateral posture alone does not substitute Kotteakos; relief depends on failure of the State's Chapman showing and is not automatic. Other habeas gates and structural-error rules remain independent; this does not prescribe every collateral-review procedure. The same State-borne Chapman inquiry governs a preserved constitutional trial violation in which the combined jury instructions and prosecutor’s statement permit conviction without proof of the defendant’s own intent required by the offense. Kotteakos’s substantial-and-injurious-effect and grave-doubt inquiry does not govern that category. If an available violation is established and the State fails to prove that it did not contribute to the verdict, conditional relief must allow a reasonable retrial period for the affected conviction; identifying the wrong review standard alone does not establish a violation or command a writ. The personal-intent category also covers omission of the defendant’s required aiding purpose without an accompanying prosecutorial statement. Such omission is trial error, not automatically structural error. Necessary jury findings embracing the omitted purpose are sufficient but not the exclusive route to harmlessness: supported whole-record inferences may show no contribution to the actual verdict. The reviewing court must ask whether the instructions led the jury to consider the evidence supporting that inference. If the omission prevented reliable consideration of the personal-purpose question, strong evidence alone is insufficient. Knowledge plus assistance, general mental capacity, or evidence sufficient for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction cannot substitute for the required actual-verdict inquiry.

The State-borne Chapman rule also governs a properly available constitutional trial error in a commutation instruction affecting a capital jury’s choice between death and life without parole: the State must show beyond a reasonable doubt from the whole penalty record that the error did not contribute to the actual death sentence. Consider the complete charge, mitigation, prosecution theory and supported jury requests, rather than what a hypothetical properly instructed jury could have imposed. A corrective direction or strong aggravation is not independently conclusive. Boyde’s error-identification inquiry does not itself establish the effect required for relief. The actual reasoning must perform that causal inquiry under the governing burden, but no prescribed words, citation or separate heading is indispensable. An available violation failing the State’s showing requires conditional sentence relief allowing a reasonable opportunity for a lawful new penalty proceeding; a method error alone does not establish entitlement to a writ.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

**Authority by component:**
- Application-date construction and preserved exceptions: Lindh, Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

- Pending-capital extension for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions: Carriger v. Stewart — Holding 1. Former-law treatment of the pending capital application, 1998-04-16.

**Present operation effective:** Carriger v. Stewart, 1998-04-16, for the specified capital-application extension; Lindh’s noncapital rule remains effective from 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Carriger does not decide §2254(e)(2)’s temporal application or require an evidentiary hearing.  The rule creates no constitutional entitlement to preserve every filing-date judicial doctrine and decides no capital State qualification, exhaustion, merits or ultimate entitlement to relief.

#### FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc., 508 U.S. 307 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-603.
**Decided:** June 1, 1993.

For the other question-level holdings in FCC v. Beach Communications, Inc., June 1, 1993, No. 92-603, see Federal Courts — Legislative reenactment and mootness.

##### Common-owner bargaining and regulatory costs sustain the economic line

**Holding:** For an economic classification involving neither a suspect class nor a fundamental right under the question reviewed, a reasonably conceivable legitimate basis suffices; the challenger must negate such a basis, and formal legislative findings are unnecessary. Congress could rationally distinguish these commonly owned, controlled or managed dwellings from separately owned properties because bargaining arrangements and the relationship between regulatory cost and consumer protection differ; Moreno and Cleburne continue to forbid irrational or impermissibly motivated classifications.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas’s Parts I and II-A; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States Railroad Retirement Board v. Fritz, 449 U.S. 166: conceivable legitimate grounds and imperfect economic fit applied.
- Sullivan v. Stroop, 496 U.S. 478: ordinary review of social and economic classifications applied.
- Nordlinger v. Hahn, June 18, 1992: the rational-basis holding applied without converting its particular tax grounds into universal immunity from review.
- Department of Agriculture v. Moreno, 413 U.S. 528: illegitimate-purpose limit preserved.
- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: irrational-prejudice limit preserved.

##### Competitive entry independently supports the ownership distinction

**Independent alternative holding:** Congress could independently distinguish separately owned buildings because an incumbent provider can add nearby buildings at a lower marginal cost than a new entrant can incur to install its own reception facilities, creating a plausible first-mover advantage. This conceivable competition concern is sufficient under ordinary rational-basis review without proof that every regulated system possesses monopoly power.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Thomas’s Part II-B; White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (7 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The regulatory-cost and competition grounds are independently sufficient. No First Amendment or heightened-scrutiny question is decided, and no actual monopoly is found.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the equal-protection invalidation; the separate questions not decided may receive further proceedings.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Fritz, 449 U.S. 166: a second conceivable legitimate economic basis independently sustains the line.
- Nordlinger, June 18, 1992: multiple sufficient rational grounds and tolerance for imperfect fit applied within ordinary review.

## Authority copied from state/holdings/civil-rights.md

#### Heller v. Doe, 509 U.S. 312 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-351.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### The lower overall proof protection lacks a rational relation to the disadvantage imposed

**Holding:** Kentucky’s clear-and-convincing burden for involuntary commitment of adults with intellectual disability, compared with proof beyond a reasonable doubt for mental illness, violates equal protection on the preserved rational-basis challenge: the offered diagnostic, prediction and treatment differences do not rationally justify assigning this class the greater risk of mistaken confinement. Addington’s federal minimum remains unchanged; the remedy must address lawful equalization and severability rather than impose a national reasonable-doubt standard or order every class member released.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- City of Cleburne v. Cleburne Living Center, 473 U.S. 432: rational relationship to the actual disadvantage required; no new scrutiny tier adopted.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418: the clear-and-convincing minimum survives; unequal safeguards remain separately reviewable.
- FCC v. Beach Communications, June 1, 1993: no formal-findings or empirical-certainty requirement imposed; its specific economic justification does not decide this classification.
- Foucha, May 18, 1992: current lawful confinement predicates preserved; noncontrolling equal-protection reasoning is not promoted to law.

##### Family-party participation has a distinct rational basis

**Holding:** Kentucky may distinguish the two commitment proceedings by allowing guardians and immediate family members to participate as parties for adults with a lifelong developmental disability. Continuing family knowledge rationally supports that participation; the availability of testimony or a less extensive form of participation does not by itself defeat ordinary rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cleburne, 473 U.S. 432: relevant differences may support a particular procedural distinction.
- Parham v. J.R., 442 U.S. 584: family information may be useful; adult confinement is not delegated to relatives.
- Schweiker v. Wilson, 450 U.S. 221: ordinary review does not require the least restrictive legislative alternative.

##### Party participation does not itself deprive the adult of fair adjudication

**Holding:** Allowing relatives or guardians to participate as parties does not categorically violate procedural due process where the adult has counsel and an effective opportunity to contest adverse evidence before an independent decisionmaker. Relatives may neither supply a substitute for the statutory commitment grounds nor control the decision or suppress the adult’s position.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No new scrutiny tier or national reasonable-doubt requirement is adopted; heightened scrutiny and Kentucky’s repealed voluntary-admission rule remain outside review. Party status is sustained categorically, not every hearing’s fairness.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm invalidation of unequal proof burdens and remand for severability and lawful equalization; reverse categorical invalidation of family-party participation subject to counsel, effective contest and independent adjudication. No blanket release.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: private interest, error risk and safeguards, and government interests applied separately from equality review.
- Parham, 442 U.S. 584: informational participation distinguished from final decisional control.

#### Romer v. Evans, 517 U.S. 620

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1039.
**Decided:** May 20, 1996.

##### Withdrawal and constitutional foreclosure of ordinary protection for an identified class

**Holding:** Colorado Amendment 2's combined withdrawal of existing orientation-based nondiscrimination protection and foreclosure of comparable protection through ordinary state and local government violates equal protection under ordinary rational-basis review. Its established class-specific disability lacks a rational relationship to the asserted legitimate interests, without reliance on suspect status, political-process strict scrutiny, elimination of generally applicable protection, or a finding about every voter's motive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The authoritative state construction covers legislative, executive, and judicial channels of the State, agencies, schools, and local government, including public and private settings; it is not merely a quota prohibition or local preemption leaving the legislature free to act. Uniformity, regulatory restraint, finite resources, religious and intimate association, parental teaching, and conduct-based morality do not explain this blanket ineligibility across public and impersonal commercial life. Ordinary review permits supported predictions, imperfect categories, and conceivable grounds without formal findings or empirical certainty; a narrower alternative alone is insufficient. The defect concerns the relationship to the actual disadvantage, rather than an independent hostility finding or mandatory least-restrictive-means inquiry.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes, without deciding, that generally applicable criminal, tort, contract, and public-service protections remain. It creates no suspect or quasi-suspect tier, general duty to enact nondiscrimination laws, nationwide code, quota entitlement, universal access to every lawmaking level, or presumption invalidating every group-affecting repeal, referendum, or constitutional policy reservation. Particular religious, intimate-association, and expressive defenses retain their own predicates; no municipal exemption is approved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Beach* preserves conceivable legitimate grounds and reasonable generalizations together with the *Moreno/Cleburne* limits on bare harm and irrational prejudice. *Heller*'s controlling proof-burden decision requires a relationship to the actual disadvantage; its separate family-participation holding preserves relevant generalizations and rejects invalidity based on narrower alternatives alone. *Hurley* protects concrete private expression without making ordinary service categorically expressive association; *Swanner* and *American Life League* retain their particular religious-liberty inquiries. *Bowers* remains effective on its specified-conduct question but does not justify this broader disability. *Adarand* adds no orientation tier, and *Klinger*'s treatment-specific comparison does not import *Armstrong*'s charging-discovery threshold into an express classification.

##### Resolution of the preserved alternative and complete injunctive relief

**Holding:** The fully presented rational-basis alternative permits affirmance without adoption of the lower court's strict-scrutiny rationale. The defect permeates Amendment 2's inseparable common operation, warranting the complete existing injunction without a new merits remand or federal rewriting of state law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same seven-Justice Court opinion.

**Material application:** State severability law treats orientation, conduct, practices, and relationships as descriptions of the same class; deletion of a description leaves the same disability. No independently lawful remainder is established. Failure under strict scrutiny is not itself rational-basis failure, and no unconditional State concession supplies the result. The ability to seek another constitutional amendment does not cure the unequal barrier to ordinary protection.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm, 7–2; the permanent injunction remains effective in full. No enactment, damages, factual inquiry, severance proceeding, or new hearing is ordered. Unappealed suspect-status treatment is not reopened, and independently preserved First Amendment claims are neither decided nor presumed abandoned.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hunter* and *Seattle* retain their racial political-restructuring settings without a new fundamental-right extension. *Valtierra*, *Gordon*, and *Crawford* prevent treating every referendum, neutral voting rule, or policy revision as exclusion. *Dalton* requires relief matched to the violation and preservation of independently lawful operation; no general facial formula, *Miller* abortion method, or *44 Liquormart* empirical burden replaces this analysis.

#### Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-329.
**Decided:** June 29, 1995.

##### Religious viewpoint in the private publication program

**Holding:** The University may not exclude an otherwise eligible private student publication from its generally available printing benefit because its editorial viewpoint is religious. The rule applies to this privately edited, independently attributed publication program on its otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms; it does not require funding every religious activity or every subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas; five Justices adopt the program-specific viewpoint rule.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Recognition, facilities, distribution and money remain distinct; not every public expenditure creates a speech forum. The publication remains independently edited and attributed under required disclaimers and approved vendor-payment terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators’ Association: preserves limited-forum definition while forbidding viewpoint discrimination.
- Lamb’s Chapel v. Center Moriches; Widmar v. Vincent: apply private religious-viewpoint equality without assuming that facility access decides every subsidy.
- Regan v. Taxation With Representation: distinguishes definition of a government financing program from discriminatory exclusion within this opened private-publication arrangement.

##### Permissible printing support on the actual program terms

**Holding:** Providing this neutral printing benefit to the otherwise eligible private religious publication does not itself violate the Establishment Clause. Its secular student-expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent editorial attribution and controlled vendor payments matter together; neutrality alone is not a universal authorization of devotional financing or governmental sponsorship of worship.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas join this concrete program conclusion. Lemon remains governing law; no new universal framework is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The actual purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry remains; neutral availability alone neither authorizes unrestricted devotional financing nor governmental worship. Compelled-fee challenges, unrestricted religious aid and particular awards are outside review.

The denial concerned religious activity, defined by the Guidelines as primarily promoting or manifesting belief in or about deity or ultimate reality, rather than an established finding of religious-organization ineligibility. The organization rule separately concerns devotion as organizational purpose. Political restrictions separately exclude principal-purpose lobbying and candidate electioneering while permitting incidental or insubstantial advocacy, nonpartisan study/research and ideological views; those permissions are not exceptions to the religious-activity rule. Permitted ability-to-perform membership limits likewise remain distinct. Abandoned free-exercise/state individual-rights claims and unchallenged equal-protection intent findings are not reopened.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Lemon v. Kurtzman: retains the existing purpose, effect and entanglement inquiry; no universal replacement is adopted.
- Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind; Mueller v. Allen: apply neutral-benefit and private-direction distinctions within the actual program, not neutrality alone.
- Board of Education of Kiryas Joel v. Grumet: distinguishes denominational governmental authority and retains religious-neutrality limits.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills: preserves the actual antecedent-law remand; supplies no religious-aid merits holding here.
- Lee v. Weisman: preserves limits on governmental religious participation and coercion; this private printing benefit compels no student worship.
- Church of Scientology v. Clearwater: distinguishes RFRA burdens and exemptions from constitutional financing authority.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for removal of the religious-viewpoint exclusion under otherwise lawful eligibility, payment and administrative terms. Examine any properly available independent Virginia funding defense consistently with the federal viewpoint holding, without declaring applicability, validity or revival of forfeited defenses. No full payment, damages, immunity, fee or compelled-fee refund is determined.

**Current-force modification:** Effective June 23, 1997, Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test as modified in Agostini v. Felton, Holding I. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform a single inquiry with three routes: controlling specific prohibition or distinct doctrine, established permissible relationship, and residual attribution and function. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms. It concerns financing, not merely facility access; it neither decided Agostini's public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553, June 23, 1997, Holdings I–II; First Amendment — Public instruction on religious-school premises.

## Authority copied from state/holdings/first-amendment.md

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.

#### Your Home Visiting Nurse Services, Inc. v. Shalala

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1489.
**Decided:** 1999-02-23.

##### I. Refusal to reopen is not a new appealable reimbursement determination

**Holding:** A fiscal intermediary's discretionary refusal to reopen a final reimbursement determination is not a new final determination as to the amount of total program reimbursement under 42 U.S.C. §1395oo(a)(1)(A)(i). Judicial review of the Board's final jurisdictional ruling under §1395oo(f)(1) permits deciding that question; it does not give the Board jurisdiction over the refusal or authorize direct judicial calculation of reimbursement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I-A of Kennedy's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. Part I-B's additional regulatory construction and deference ground is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela does not join that ground. Each stated rationale commands a majority at that scope.

##### Reopening jurisdiction and review of an actual revised reimbursement determination

**Holding:** Section 405.1885(c) gives reopening jurisdiction exclusively to the body that last decided the matter; §405.1889 separately recognizes review when actual reopening produces a revision in the reimbursement amount. The Secretary's fair, considered reading consistent with those provisions receives regulatory deference, while the regulation's mandatory reopening commands remain intact and an agency's jurisdictional label is not conclusive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I-B, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join this additional regulatory ground.

##### II. The claim cannot proceed directly under federal-question jurisdiction

**Holding:** Incorporated §405(h) bars the asserted 28 U.S.C. §1331 route because this request seeks reopening for additional Medicare reimbursement and derives its standing and substantive basis from the Medicare scheme. Describing the requested order as procedural does not remove that claim from the statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, commands eight votes. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on this component. This same eight-Justice coalition controls Holdings III and IV.

##### III. The APA supplies no independent jurisdiction

**Holding:** The Administrative Procedure Act does not independently confer subject-matter jurisdiction over this refusal to reopen. Its review standards do not supply jurisdiction missing from the asserted statutory routes.

##### IV. No clear duty to reopen these determinations supports mandamus

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that incorporated §405(h) does not universally preclude mandamus under 28 U.S.C. §1361, mandamus fails because no clear nondiscretionary duty to reopen these final determinations is established. The denial rests on the absence of that duty, not on failure to exhaust the available avenues concerning the refusal.

##### V. The affirmance decides the presented routes and preserves their limits

**Holding:** The Sixth Circuit's judgment is affirmed without a reimbursement award, reopening order, Board merits hearing or further remand. Timely initial appeals, actual reopening followed by a new determination, genuinely presented regulatory procedural violations and independently authorized constitutional channels retain their own requirements and are not decided here.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Part V's stated disposition and reservations. The conclusions about the independent routes remain controlled by the distinct coalitions stated above.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Califano v. Sanders* is followed: the APA supplies no independent subject-matter jurisdiction. Its treatment of genuine constitutional claims is not displaced.
- *Heckler v. Ringer* supplies the Medicare arising-under analysis and mandamus's clear-duty requirement. Its reservation about universal §405(h) preclusion of mandamus remains open.
- *Bowen v. Michigan Academy of Family Physicians* is distinguished: its review presumption does not create a new route around the original provider-appeal scheme.
- *Auer v. Robbins* and *Thomas Jefferson University v. Shalala* support the eight-Justice regulatory rationale within their text-consistency and considered-interpretation limits; neither makes agency labels conclusive.
- *Shalala v. Guernsey Memorial Hospital* preserves the distinction between a lawful manual explanation and an amendment of binding law.
- *Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala* is applied to keep corrective adjustments within authorized reimbursement methods; it supplies no general duty to reopen final cost reports.
- *Regions Hospital v. Shalala* is distinguished and preserved: correcting a historical input for future payments does not reopen a closed annual payment or impose a reciprocal reopening duty here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A bare discretionary reopening refusal does not renew the original Board appeal. The courts may review the Board's jurisdictional ruling without acquiring authority to review the underlying refusal or calculate reimbursement through that channel. These asserted federal-question, independent-APA and mandamus routes fail separately. Actual revisions after reopening, mandatory HCFA-notification and fraud-or-similar-fault triggers, properly presented procedural violations and independently authorized constitutional claims retain their distinct terms and channels; the decision promises no automatic remedy for them. The original reimbursement remains undisturbed without a decision approving its substantive amount.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

All nine support denial of the Board, federal-question, independent-APA and mandamus routes presented. The Court orders no reimbursement, reopened report, Board merits hearing or additional remand. Agreement with that disposition does not establish agreement with every supporting rationale.

## Authority copied from state/holdings/administrative-law.md

#### Thunder Basin Coal Co. v. Reich, 510 U.S. 200

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-896.

**Decided:** 1994-01-19.

##### This Mine Act dispute must follow the statutory review channel

**Holding:** The Mine Act precludes original district-court review of this anticipatory challenge to miners' representation and posting requirements because Congress's intent to channel the dispute through the independent Commission and then a court of appeals is fairly discernible from the Act's language, structure, and purpose, and these claims can receive meaningful review there. The claim's relation to the statutory enforcement process, relevant agency expertise, and actual availability of judicial review inform that inquiry; they are not three newly mandatory elements governing every administrative statute.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, statutory-channel portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg; all nine participants adopt this proposition at the stated level of generality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Block v. Community Nutrition Institute, 467 U.S. 340 (1984): applied to discern congressional review allocation from the statutory scheme.
- Abbott Laboratories v. Gardner, 387 U.S. 136 (1967): distinguished because that scheme preserved supplemental judicial review; the presumption favoring review does not require every forum.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): distinguished because its collateral systemic challenge lacked meaningful review through individual proceedings; that protection remains.
- Board of Governors v. MCorp Financial, Inc., December 3, 1991: its scheme-specific channel rule supports the analysis, without extending its express bar to the Mine Act by assumption.
- Darby v. Cisneros, June 21, 1993: preserved; limits on judicially added APA exhaustion do not displace Congress's allocation of original review.

##### The available route does not impose an intolerable choice on this record

**Holding:** The operator has not shown that compliance with these representation and posting requirements, or their contest through the Mine Act's review process, imposes a constitutionally intolerable obstacle to meaningful judicial review. This conclusion depends on the actual absence of demonstrated irreversible injury, review before contested penalties become payable, expedited proceedings, and the statute's bounded opportunities for temporary relief; it creates neither immunity from lawful penalties nor a rule that delayed payment alone always makes review adequate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's Opinion of the Court, meaningful-review and due-process portion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Ginsburg; seven participants adopt this ground. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on a distinct constitutional formulation and are not counted as joining this explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Temporary relief is discretionary, not automatic. Under §815(b)(2), Commission relief from covered orders requires a hearing with an opportunity for every party to be heard, a substantial likelihood of favorable Commission findings, and no adverse effect on miners' health and safety; relief is forbidden for citations under §814(a) or (f). Section 816(a)(2) permits appellate temporary relief only after notice and an opportunity to be heard, a substantial likelihood of success, and protection of miners' health and safety; it excludes orders concerning §817(a) imminent-danger withdrawals and §814(a) or (f) citations. Section 816(c) makes clear that commencing review does not itself stay an order. Thus this decision promises no stay of a posting citation and no suspension of every accruing penalty. The separate availability of contest, expedition, review before collection, and the unproved irreparable-compliance injury supports the present result.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908): distinguished on practical access; its protection against a penalty structure effectively foreclosing judicial review remains intact.
- McNary v. Haitian Refugee Center, Inc., 498 U.S. 479 (1991): its requirement of meaningful practical review is preserved and satisfied on this record.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final agency action, cause of action, ripeness and exhaustion are unnecessary to the preclusion disposition. Commission inability finally to decide a constitutional question does not eliminate appellate review. The representation, posting and labor-law merits remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Tenth Circuit's jurisdictional judgment is affirmed; the district-court injunction remains unavailable. The representation/posting challenge must use the authorized Commission and appellate process. No labor-law merits determination, penalty assessment, guaranteed stay or new administrative outcome is ordered.

#### Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, 505 U.S. 88 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1676.
**Decided:** June 18, 1992.

##### Whether occupational overlap and absence of an approved state plan automatically preempt supplementary state standards under OSH Act §18

**Holding:** Section 18 does not automatically preempt a supplementary state occupational-safety standard merely because a federal standard covers the same issue and the State lacks an approved plan. The categorical overlap rule is reversed; provisions must instead be examined for an actual basis of displacement, without treating either dual public purpose or occupational overlap as conclusive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s plurality, joined by Blackmun, Stevens and Thomas, and Stone-Zsela’s concurrence in the judgment independently and expressly support this limited negative proposition and its remand consequence: five of nine Justices. No complete opinion commands five joins.

**Material application:** Section 18(a) preserves authority where no federal standard exists. Section 18(b), including its enacted heading, and the approval and withdrawal provisions can govern a State assuming the federal regulatory role without establishing a universal prohibition on compatible supplementation. Federal minimum training and additional state training do not become incompatible solely through overlap.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No complete opinion commands a majority. Souter, Blackmun, Stevens and Thomas alone would permit concurrent standards whenever simultaneous compliance is physically possible; Stone alone preserves a separate account of demonstrated conflict, a clear ceiling or exclusivity. Neither positive account is adopted as a new Court-wide test, and no broader rule is extracted under Marks. The shared negative proposition does not endorse every compatible license, find any challenged provision valid, eliminate existing conflict principles, or decide the remaining Commerce Clause and severability questions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit judgment is reversed only insofar as it establishes automatic occupational-overlap/no-plan displacement and remanded for provision-specific review. Its 4,000-hour ruling cannot rest solely on that rationale. The unappealed invalidation of training within Illinois and independently supported rulings remain undisturbed. The Court authorizes neither wholesale enforcement nor blanket invalidation.

**Material judgment:** Reversed in part and remanded, 5–4: Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Thomas support reversal; White, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy oppose it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Rice v. Santa Fe Elevator Corp., 331 U.S. 218 — applied at majority level for requiring a clear basis before displacement of traditional police regulation.
- English v. General Electric Co., 496 U.S. 72 — applied for identifying the actual statutory basis and scope of preemption; no universal prohibition of dual regulation follows.
- Florida Lime & Avocado Growers, Inc. v. Paul, 373 U.S. 132 — preserved; impossibility is an established conflict ground, but four Justices’ proposed exclusive use of that ground is not adopted by five.
- Hines v. Davidowitz, 312 U.S. 52 — not overruled; no majority treats an assumed anti-duplication objective as sufficient to decide this statute. Stone preserves demonstrated interference with a clearly enacted operation.
- Morales v. Trans World Airlines, Inc. (June 1, 1992) — distinguished; its express airline wording does not decide §18.

#### Freightliner Corp. v. Myrick

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-286.
**Decided:** April 18, 1995.

##### No in-effect federal standard supplies the express-preemption predicate here

**Holding:** Section 1392(d)'s express restriction requires an in-effect federal motor-vehicle safety standard governing the same aspect of performance as the asserted state duty. The asserted antilock-equipment defect claims are not expressly displaced on this record because that federal predicate is absent; the Court does not decide whether common-law duties themselves are safety standards within that provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), eight Justices; Scalia concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- CSX Transportation, Inc. v. Easterwood, April 21, 1993: applies its attention to the aspect of performance actually covered, without importing the separate railroad speed holding or treating every braking rule as coverage of antilock omission.
- Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., June 24, 1992: applies clause- and duty-specific analysis; its particular cigarette holdings do not answer all Safety Act questions.
- Paccar, Inc. v. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, 573 F.2d 632: treats its judicial invalidation as the regulatory-history predicate, not as a new federal right to avoid common-law liability.

##### Judicial invalidation alone establishes no actual federal conflict

**Holding:** An express preemption clause does not categorically foreclose inquiry into an independently established actual conflict with operative federal law or a demonstrated federal objective. Here neither impossibility nor interference with such an objective is established by judicial invalidation and the absence of an operative antilock requirement, so the asserted conflict does not bar these common-law claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Ginsburg), eight Justices; Scalia concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cipollone v. Liggett Group, Inc., June 24, 1992: rejects the lower court's universal inference that any express provision exhausts every possible actual-conflict inquiry; its enacted-clause holdings remain intact.
- Gade v. National Solid Wastes Management Association, June 18, 1992: preserves the shared rule against automatic displacement from overlap; no physical-impossibility-only theory or broader separate theory is made Court law.
- Paccar, Inc. v. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, 573 F.2d 632: distinguishes inadequate support for a mandatory standard from an affirmative policy protecting noninstallation against state duties.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1397(k)'s compliance saving clause is not held to immunize every common-law action from an actual conflict. The Court does not decide whether tort duties are standards under §1392(d), every effect of the saving clause when a standard exists, or a federal field-wide ceiling. It does not find all air-brake regulation absent, invent an agency policy from an unproduced revision, or decide defect, accident causation, consortium entitlement, wrongful-death recovery, or damages.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The return of the Myrick and Lindsey common-law claims for further proceedings is affirmed. The abandoned Georgia RICO contention, Fruehauf dismissal and stipulated predecessor treatment are undisturbed. Ordinary liability proof and defenses remain.

#### Brecht v. Abrahamson, 507 U.S. 619 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7358.

**Decided:** April 21, 1993.

##### The protected-silence error remains constitutional trial error

**Holding:** Using Brecht’s silence after Miranda warnings to impeach his trial explanation violates Doyle’s due-process protection; permitted impeachment with prewarning silence is analytically separate. This violation is trial error subject to harmless-error review, not a merely nonconstitutional rule or a structural defect requiring automatic reversal.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Doyle, 426 U.S. 610 — applied: warning-induced silence receives due-process protection.
- Jenkins, 447 U.S. 231, and Fletcher, 455 U.S. 603 — distinguished: prewarning impeachment remains permissible in the circumstances presented.
- Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279 — applied: this evidentiary trial error permits harmlessness analysis; structural defects remain distinct.

##### Chapman governs this preserved Doyle claim on habeas

**Holding:** For this preserved Doyle trial error properly before the federal habeas court, the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the constitutional error did not contribute to the verdict. Collateral posture alone does not replace that burden with Kotteakos; the reviewing court must apply Chapman to the whole record, with relief conditional on failure of the State’s showing rather than automatic from the violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court, II–III; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chapman, 386 U.S. 18 — applied on federal habeas to this preserved Doyle claim with its burden unchanged.
- Kotteakos, 328 U.S. 750 — not extended to replace Chapman here; its existing nonconstitutional-error operation remains.
- Rose, 478 U.S. 570, and Yates, 500 U.S. 391 — continuity supported; prior assumed use is not misdescribed as a square holding on this distinction.
- Stringer, Sochor, Espinosa and Richmond — unchanged: actual correction of capital weighing errors remains distinct.
- Sawyer and Herrera — not extended into a universal innocence requirement for review of preserved trial error.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Chapman ruling covers this preserved Doyle claim properly on habeas, not every constitutional collateral claim. Exhaustion, preservation, other habeas gates, structural-error doctrine and capital weighing-error correction remain distinct. The Court does not decide harmlessness or impose an innocence prerequisite.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the Seventh Circuit judgment and remand for whole-record Chapman review with the State’s burden. Relief follows only if that showing fails; no automatic writ, release, acquittal or present retrial deadline.

**Later-authority backlinks:** O’Neal v. McAninch, February 21, 1995: extends Chapman review to the preserved personal-intent claim arising from the combined instructions and prosecutorial argument; the State bears the beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden if a violation is found. The Doyle holding remains unchanged, and no general rule for all preserved constitutional trial errors is adopted. See Criminal Procedure — Constitutional trial error on habeas. Calderon v. Coleman, No. 98-437, December 14, 1998: extends State-borne Chapman review to the defined, properly available capital-selection commutation-instruction claim; the earlier holding remains unchanged and no universal constitutional-habeas extension is adopted.

#### United States v. Scheffer

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1133.

**Decided:** 1998-03-31.

##### Supported reliability concerns justify this categorical polygraph rule against the presented constitutional challenge

**Holding:** The constitutional right to present a defense permits evidentiary restrictions justified by legitimate trial interests and proportionate to their purposes. Serious, supported concerns about polygraph reliability justify Rule 707's categorical exclusion in this setting, where the accused can present his own factual account and underlying evidence; the Constitution does not require individualized admissibility proceedings for this credibility proffer.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. The credibility-role and collateral-litigation rationales are separate, three-Justice grounds and do not become holdings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision establishes constitutional permission for this restriction, without adjudicating Article 36(a) statutory validity, the accuracy of any particular polygraph, or a general jury monopoly over credibility expertise. Testimony alone does not invariably establish a complete defense.

Rule 707(a) excludes polygraph results, examiner opinions, and references to offering, failing to take or taking an examination. Rule 707(b) preserves statements made during an examination when they are otherwise admissible; it does not make every examination statement admissible.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The contrary constitutional judgment is reversed and remanded, 8–1, Stevens opposing. The decision orders neither automatic reinstatement of all convictions and sentence nor admission, an individualized hearing, acquittal or a new trial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Chambers v. Mississippi:** Preserved for meaningful presentation of a defense subject to justified evidentiary limits.
- **Rock v. Arkansas:** Distinguished at the sufficient reliability justification and nature of this credibility proffer; no absolute scientific-evidence entitlement is inferred.
- **Montana v. Egelhoff:** Applied at its protection for rebuttal of retained elements and express reservation for justified reliability rules; the substantive element is not redefined here.
- **Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals:** Preserved as construction of the original general Federal Rules, with flexible reliability-and-fit screening; its architecture is not imposed as a constitutional requirement.
- **General Electric Co. v. Joiner:** Preserved for inferential fit and abuse-of-discretion review under those Rules; it does not invalidate Rule 707's separate categorical scheme.
