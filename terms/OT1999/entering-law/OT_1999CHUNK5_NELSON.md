#### Peacock v. Thomas, 516 U.S. 349

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1453.

**Decided:** February 21, 1996.

##### Whether the later collection action has original ERISA jurisdiction

**Holding:** A later action imposing the corporate judgment on Peacock alleges no underlying ERISA or plan violation supporting the asserted federal jurisdiction. Veil piercing is a means of imposing liability, not an independent statutory violation; §502(a)(3) supplies relief for the obligations it identifies, not equitable relief at large.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices. Stone-Zsela does not join this analysis.

**Material application:** Later control and collection obstruction do not erase the original nonfiduciary adjudication or establish fiduciary responsibility during the earlier breaches. An unrestricted compensation demand does not become an authorized remedy merely by being called equitable.

**Limits and questions not reached:** ERISA's possible permission for veil piercing in an appropriately founded action is assumed without decision. No general ban on equitable monetary remedies or rule treating every unsuccessful federal merits theory as jurisdictionally defective follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Mertens's bounded equitable-category holding and antecedent-action reservation remain. O'Melveny and Travelers supply no general federal collection rule, and the lower court's categorical federal-preemption rationale is not approved.

##### Whether ancillary enforcement jurisdiction permits a new full-debt action

**Holding:** Authority to identify, preserve and execute against a judgment debtor's assets does not authorize a later independent action imposing its entire debt on another person without an independent jurisdictional basis. Prior party status does not itself make that person a judgment debtor.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** The later conduct differs materially from the original pension violations and requires new facts, duties and defenses. Rule 69 supplies procedure, not original jurisdiction; the original 1987 action predates §1367's December 1, 1990 boundary, and the later case supplies no original claim to which supplemental jurisdiction can attach.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Contempt, asset discovery, ordinary attachment and garnishment, fraudulent-transfer process directed to debtor property and independently founded actions remain available. No particular asset restoration, general alter-ego prohibition, personal-jurisdiction or service holding, governing South Carolina enforcement rule, federal displacement or later-conduct preclusion determination is made. The preserved service objection is neither rejected nor waived; subject-matter jurisdiction alone supplies no nationwide service.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for dismissal of the later action and removal of dependent personal liability, interest, fees and expenses, including the appellate recalculation instruction. The vacated $205,182.01 combined assessment is not reinstated; the original $187,628.93 Tru-Tech judgment remains intact. Otherwise available state-law rights and independently founded actions are not extinguished.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Kokkonen's independent-authority requirement and distinct decree-effectuation branch apply; Beecher limits creation of new obligations. Riggs and Labette County are distinguished because they enforced existing official duties rather than shifting the judgment debt.

### Ancillary enforcement and a new judgment debtor

**Governing question:** Whether an existing federal judgment supplies jurisdiction over a later independent action imposing its debt on another person.

**Current rule:** The court's power to identify, preserve and execute against its judgment debtor's assets does not confer jurisdiction over a later independent action imposing that debtor's full obligation on another person. Such an action needs an independent jurisdictional basis; factual connection, remedial need, Rule 69 and a supplemental-jurisdiction claim lacking an original federal anchor cannot supply it. The decree-effectuation branch of ancillary jurisdiction remains distinct from factual interdependence and from creation of a new obligation.

**Authority by component:**

- New-debtor limit and preserved enforcement: Peacock — Ancillary jurisdiction does not support this new full-debt action; The jurisdictional disposition preserves ordinary enforcement and leaves unresolved merits questions, February 21, 1996; Thomas for Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer and himself (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Peacock, February 21, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary contempt, asset discovery, attachment, garnishment and fraudulent-transfer process directed to debtor property remain available on their own lawful predicates, as does an independently founded action. There is no general bar on every alter-ego proceeding or every proceeding involving a nondebtor, and ancillary subject-matter jurisdiction does not itself authorize nationwide service.

**Related current rules:** Jurisdiction to enforce a settlement after dismissal states Kokkonen's distinct order-incorporation and retained-jurisdiction mechanics.

**Holdings navigation:** Peacock v. Thomas, 516 U.S. 349.

### Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

**Governing question:** The due-process prerequisite to claim or issue preclusion against a nonparty.

**Current rule:** Identical interests, vigorous advocacy, resources, public importance and fiscal reliance alone do not authorize binding absent persons' own legal rights. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or representative litigation, or another recognized relationship or special remedial arrangement consistent with due process. Constitutionally inadequate representation is not independently cured by notice, opportunities to object or opt out; notice alone imposes no general duty on strangers to intervene. Issue preclusion, like claim preclusion, requires a lawful basis to bind the person. Genuine representative public-right adjudication and use of precedent in a later case remain distinct from extinguishing a stranger's personal claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Representation independent of notice and exclusion rights: Matsushita — Constitutional representation and unresolved review questions limit the remand, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part IV-A; eight Associates reserve complete collateral-review scope in IV-B.
- Nonparty binding and direct personal tax claims, including the issue-preclusion alternative: Richards — Holdings II–IV, June 10, 1996; Stone-Zsela for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Richards, June 10, 1996; Matsushita supplies the retained representation component.

**Open question:** The Court assumes without deciding whether adequate representation may cure absent notice in some representative proceedings. It neither requires individual notice in every such action nor constitutionalizes every federal class-action requirement. The broader adequacy of officials' representation of constituents, ordinary state-law-error review and Matsushita's collateral-review boundary are not resolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367; Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793.

- **Two Pesos, Inc. v. Taco Cabana, Inc., June 26, 1992:** Its inherent-distinctiveness route for qualifying nonfunctional restaurant presentation remains effective. Its express reservation of other product-design categories permits the distinct acquired-source requirement now adopted; the accepted restaurant findings are not retried.
- **Qualitex Co. v. Jacobson Products Co., March 28, 1995:** Its separation of eligible subject matter from acquired source significance is applied to product design. Its nonfunctional-color rule remains intact and supplies no blanket inherent-distinctiveness route for every aesthetic feature.
- **Inwood Laboratories, Inc. v. Ives Laboratories, Inc., 456 U.S. 844 (1982):** Its definition of secondary meaning supplies the source-versus-product inquiry, while its essential-use-or-purpose and cost-or-quality functionality boundary remains independent. Its distinct contributory-infringement holding is unchanged.
- **Weisgram v. Marley Co., February 22, 2000:** Its protection of an actual fair opportunity and genuinely supported new-trial grounds is preserved. It supplies neither an unasserted acquired-meaning theory nor an automatic right to reopen proof in this case.
