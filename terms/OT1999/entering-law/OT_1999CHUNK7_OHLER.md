## Standards and Tests selection: Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

### Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

**Governing question:** What instructions govern forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)?

**Current rule:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of forfeited error only if there is error, it is plain, and it affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, unlike failure timely to assert it. Plain means clear or obvious under current law. The defendant ordinarily must show outcome-affecting prejudice. Even then correction is discretionary and should occur only if the error seriously affects fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings; actual innocence is unnecessary, but substantial-rights impact alone is insufficient. Presence of alternates instructed not to participate supplies neither prejudice nor a presumption without a showing of participation or chilling effect. Initially unsettled trial law, special outcome-independent categories, justified prejudice presumptions and hearing authority remain unresolved. Johnson assumes substantial-rights injury without deciding whether withholding materiality is structural or harmless; its fourth-condition application supplies no general guilt-based exception or innocence prerequisite. When settled law permitted the trial action but intervening controlling law makes it erroneous during direct review, plainness is measured at appellate consideration. A forfeited omission of a genuine jury element proceeds under the distinct Rule 52(b) conditions rather than automatic reversal.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prior-conviction status admissions in Rule 403 balancing

### Prior-conviction status admissions in Rule 403 balancing

**Governing question:** Whether the name or nature of a prior conviction should be admitted when an adequate status admission is available.

**Current rule:** Rule 403 requires consideration of an actually available, unconditional admission that legally establishes the complete prior-conviction status element. If status is the evidence’s only legitimate purpose and the conviction’s name or nature presents unfair propensity danger substantially outweighing its incremental legitimate probative value, exclude that name or nature. General prosecutorial preference for its own proof cannot replace this comparison. The conviction remains relevant and the status element must still be proved and submitted under adequate instructions.

**Authority by component:**

- Old Chief v. United States — Old Chief v. United States — I. An adequate status admission must enter the Rule 403 comparison, January 7, 1997; Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Old Chief, January 7, 1997.

**Exceptions and limits:** This bounded substitution does not eliminate ordinary narrative evidence or evidence serving a distinct permissible purpose. Preserved error remains subject to the independent Rule 52(a)/Kotteakos whole-record inquiry; a new trial is not automatic.

**Holdings navigation:** Old Chief v. United States (1997-01-07) — An adequate status admission must enter the Rule 403 comparison.

#### United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1306.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

##### Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority

**Holding:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of a forfeited legal error only if it is plain and affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, while mere failure timely to assert a right does not. Plain means clear or obvious under current law, and the defendant ordinarily must show prejudice affecting the outcome; errors first clarified on appeal, special outcome-independent categories and justified prejudice presumptions are reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458: applies intentional relinquishment; right-specific waiver requirements remain.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies limited plain-error authority and ordinary prejudice requirement.
- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: preserves structural/trial-error distinction without deciding every Rule 52(b) category.
- Brecht, April 21, 1993: distinguished; preserved Doyle habeas review does not control forfeited Rule 24(c) error.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Johnson v. United States, May 12, 1997, resolves the intervening-law plainness reservation where settled contrary trial law changes before direct review ends: clarity at appellate consideration suffices. Initially unsettled law, structural classification and ordinary prejudice remain reserved.

##### Correction remains discretionary

**Holding:** Even when plain forfeited error affects substantial rights, correction under Rule 52(b) is permitted, not automatic, and should occur only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Actual innocence is not a necessary condition, but satisfaction of substantial rights alone does not satisfy this additional inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Atkinson, 297 U.S. 157: applies the independent fairness, integrity and public-reputation standard.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies discretionary, circumscribed correction.
- Sawyer, June 22, 1992: distinguished; capital actual-innocence gateway is not a universal limit on appellate correction.

##### Instructed alternates do not establish prejudice by presence alone

**Holding:** For these alternates, instructed not to participate, mere presence during deliberations neither establishes prejudice nor warrants a presumption of prejudice under Rule 52(b). Without a specific showing of participation or a chilling effect, respondents have not shown an effect on substantial rights; the per se new-trial ruling must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 24(c) waivability, errors first clarified on appeal, outcome-independent categories, appropriate prejudice presumptions, the preserved-objection burden, and Rule 606(b) hearing limits remain undecided. Because substantial rights were not shown, discretionary correction of a prejudicial intrusion is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the alternate-presence new-trial ruling and remand for remaining appellate issues. Gray counts V–VII and Olano counts VI and VIII, reversed for insufficient evidence, remain untouched and are not authorized for retrial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Parker v. Gladden, 385 U.S. 363, and Remmer v. United States, 347 U.S. 227: apply the focus on prejudicial influence, preserving appropriate presumptions in other circumstances.
- Smith v. Phillips, 455 U.S. 209: applies rejection of automatic retrial for every compromising contact.
- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200: applies the ordinary instruction-following presumption, not an irrebuttable cure.
- Zafiro, January 25, 1993: consistent record-specific instruction analysis; no blanket rule that instructions cure intrusions.

#### Old Chief v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-6556.

**Decided:** January 7, 1997.

##### An adequate status admission must enter the Rule 403 comparison

**Holding:** When an actually available, unconditional and legally adequate admission establishes the complete prior-conviction status element, that element is the Government's sole legitimate purpose for the evidence, and the name or nature of the conviction presents unfair propensity danger substantially outweighing its incremental legitimate probative value, Rule 403 requires exclusion of that name or nature. On this record, admitting the similar-assault judgment after rejecting the offered substitute on general prosecutorial-choice grounds was an abuse of discretion; the Ninth Circuit's no-error judgment is reversed.

The admission must establish a conviction qualifying under §922(g)(1) and §921(a)(20). The latter excludes Federal or State antitrust, unfair-trade, restraint-of-trade and similar business-regulation offenses, and State offenses classified as misdemeanors and punishable by two years or less. Conviction status follows the law of the jurisdiction of the proceedings. Expunged or set-aside convictions, pardoned convictions and convictions for which civil rights have been restored do not count, subject to the statute's qualification when the pardon, expungement or restoration expressly prohibits shipping, transporting, possessing or receiving firearms. No such exclusion is found applicable here; the offered instruction requires appropriate consensual clarification rather than an assumption that its original wording was flawless.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A distinct lawful evidentiary purpose requires its own showing and balance. Partial or conditional admissions need not be accepted; an adequate sanitized judgment may suffice. The preserved judgment-record objection was not forfeited by the separate credibility-instruction history. Legal adequacy of the status admission requires consensual clarification; the status element remains for adequate evidence and jury instructions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Huddleston's safeguards are applied to comparative status proof. Gaudin's requirement that the element be established remains intact; no general compulsory-stipulation doctrine is adopted.

##### Harmlessness remains a separate whole-record inquiry

**Holding:** The established preserved Rule 403 error is subject to Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos: the Government must establish, from the whole record, fair assurance that the error did not substantially sway the judgment, and grave doubt defeats harmlessness. The case is remanded for that inquiry and lawful consequences for affected convictions; a new trial follows only if error requiring relief is established, without a present finding that every count was affected.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer; five Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the error ruling and reversal but opposes leaving harmlessness open.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The error is nonconstitutional, so Chapman and Roy do not replace Kotteakos; Olano's ordinary defendant burden concerns forfeited error. No present harmlessness, acquittal, automatic retrial or count-specific outcome is found.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Williamson's preserved nonconstitutional-error rule is applied unchanged; Kotteakos supplies the substantial-influence inquiry, Olano is distinguished at preservation, and Chapman and Roy remain confined to the constitutional errors they govern.
