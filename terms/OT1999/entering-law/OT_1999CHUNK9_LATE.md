## Standards and Tests selection: Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

### Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

**Governing question:** How a court tailors a content-neutral injunction regulating speech-related conduct.

**Current rule:** A content-neutral injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to serve the significant governmental interest supporting the remedy. This inquiry is more demanding than the ordinary content-neutral legislative time, place and manner rule, without becoming a universal least-restrictive-means test. Each restriction needs a remedial connection to the established interest and conduct. Public access, medical quiet and residential privacy may support relief, but neither those interests nor prior misconduct justifies territorial restrictions or advance-permission conditions broader than necessary. Removing an invalid restriction also removes its operative predicate; it cannot be restored by relabeling the same conduct under another clause. Within an otherwise valid fixed access zone, an exception permitting nonthreatening initial counseling by up to two people per approached person or group may end when the recipient objectively communicates, verbally or nonverbally, that counseling is unwanted. The counselor must end that close encounter and leave the fixed zone, while retaining lawful advocacy outside it; this supplies neither universal silence nor a mobile personal radius.

## Standards and Tests selection: Lemon test

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement do not cure public payment for demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation. The inquiry concerns the instructional function purchased; religious identity alone does not establish the defect, and genuinely independent private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

**Authority by component:**

- General inquiry and operative qualifications: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function, 1997-06-23. Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.
- Object of the inquiry, evidence role and Holding I limits: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function (controlling explanation and precedent treatment), 1997-06-23; the same five Justices.
- Safeguarded secular instruction: Agostini v. Felton — II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location, 1997-06-23. Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

- Jackson v. Benson — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction”, 1999-03-16; O’Connor Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Jackson v. Benson, March 16, 1999, for integrated provider-cost instructional payments; Agostini’s general inquiry retains its June 23, 1997 effective date.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

**Former-rule note:** Lemon's independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs ceased to govern as separate or fallback tests on June 23, 1997 (Agostini v. Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises (Agostini v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Open question:** Jackson resolves only the demonstrated provider-cost arrangement purchasing integrated devotional instruction; it adopts no universal voucher prohibition or exclusion of religious providers and no constitutional entitlement to subsidy. Other voucher arrangements, monuments, legislative prayer, general accommodation and other aid retain their actual governing rules. Witters and Mueller remain effective for their actual private-benefit relationships.

**Preserved specific holdings:** Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding.

**Related current rules:** Denominational selection for general governmental authority; Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits. Each states existing specific holdings that continue to govern their established relationships under this test.

**Holdings navigation:** Agostini v. Felton (1997-06-23) — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function; II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location. [Jackson v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

### Private religious viewpoints in public forums and printing benefits

**Governing question:** Viewpoint-neutral access for private religious expression and the distinct establishment inquiry in the forums and benefits actually opened.

**Current rule:** When a public school admits private family-life and child-rearing discussion to an after-hours forum, it cannot exclude an otherwise eligible presentation solely for its religious viewpoint, even assuming a nonpublic forum. Neutral restrictions outside the forum's actual subjects remain possible. Equal access to voluntary, publicly accessible after-hours films without school sponsorship does not establish religion. That establishment holding concerns private speech, not an official devotional program; it supplies no replacement for the inherited establishment framework or universal ruling on worship restrictions. A State may not categorically exclude an otherwise eligible private unattended religious display from a forum actually opened to comparable expression merely because its message is religious or its location adjoins or lies within a capitol. Pinette’s access holding includes the proposal’s offered private-attribution safeguard; it establishes neither adequacy of a later sign nor a comprehensive establishment test. Chabad separately holds that an interior capitol location does not establish religion when actual context makes the expression clearly private. Actual selection, financing, control, adoption, preference or obscured private sponsorship can alter that conclusion; no forum label or unspecified sign decides attribution. In a generally available, privately edited student-publication program, a religious editorial viewpoint cannot exclude an otherwise eligible publication from printing support. Its secular expression purpose, broad neutral availability, independent attribution and controlled vendor payments together permit that benefit; neutrality alone does not authorize devotional financing or governmental worship.

In a broad after-hours educational and community forum, an otherwise comparable private meeting cannot be excluded solely for its worship form; exact secular sacramental counterparts are not required. Genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral operational and activity limits remain available. An admitted instructional subject likewise cannot exclude comparable religious instruction merely while permitting religious discussion. Independent private worship in rented school space does not by itself establish religion; actual governmental adoption must be shown rather than inferred from religious identity or location.

## Standards and Tests selection: Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

### Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

**Governing question:** Whether a legal obligation regulating previability abortion impermissibly interferes with the patient's protected decision or access to timely, clinically appropriate care.

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

**Authority by component:**

- Protected previability choice, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health protection: Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, “The framework retaining protected choice”, June 29, 1992; Stone's opinion joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter.
- All six steps, including the independent obstacle bar, residual severe-interference scrutiny, meaningful lesser-burden substantiation, proof allocation, clinical safeguards and relief: *Casey*, “The standard governing previability regulation after this decision”; Stone's opinion joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter. These five direct joins supply the whole rule; no *Marks* synthesis is needed.
- Integrated displacement of the independent trimester timetable: *Casey*, “The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation”; the same five-Justice rationale. The other four votes against continuing the timetable do not join the replacement framework.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Burdens and proof:** Challengers establish the duty, population, causation and triggering interference. The State then bears the applicable justification and tailoring or fit burden. This does not alter ordinary proof of disputed historical facts or the enacted burden for a separate statutory defense. No State substantiation burden arises merely because a law concerns abortion.

**Exceptions and limits:** Gestational age may support a particular medically justified safety requirement; crossing a trimester boundary does not itself change the standard. The rule creates no general professional immunity, funding entitlement, constitutional fetal-personhood rule, conscience ruling, general right to a particular drug, or universal numerical facial-review formula. It does not decide every postviability restriction.

**Material development:** The independent trimester timing restrictions are displaced only with adoption of this complete substitute. Protected choice, viability and necessary life-and-health care survive. Absence of a substantial obstacle no longer ends the inquiry into demonstrated meaningful interference.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent.

## Standards and Tests selection: Medical emergency and the separate disclosure-health defense

### Medical emergency and the separate disclosure-health defense

**Governing question:** How Pennsylvania's emergency, emergency-explanation and harmful-information provisions operate independently.

**Current rule:** Section 3203 defines emergency through a physician's good-faith clinical judgment that immediate abortion is needed to avert death or that delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. It is sustained under the prospective construction that compliance cannot create a significant threat to life or health, permitting treatment of preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes at onset rather than after catastrophic deterioration. Under §3205(b), the physician must explain the medical indications before emergency abortion only if possible; explanation is not advance permission or a reason to delay necessary care. Separately, §3205(c) protects a physician from liability for omitting §3205(a) information upon proof by a preponderance of a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. Actual occurrence of the feared injury is unnecessary. That defense neither supplies a waiting-period waiver nor replaces emergency treatment protection.

**Authority by component:**

- Emergency construction: Casey, “Medical emergency and the protective construction of §3203”, June 29, 1992.
- Conditional prior explanation: *Casey*, “Emergency explanation under §3205(b).”
- Separate defense and enacted proof burden: *Casey*, “The distinct information-omission defense in §3205(c).” All three are unanimous direct holdings.

**Present operation effective:** *Casey*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** The significant-threat interpretation is the Court's adoption of the Third Circuit's prospective construction, not extra statutory language or a binding Pennsylvania Supreme Court construction. No separate emergency-vagueness holding or decision about every clinical risk or postviability restriction follows. The statutory defense's burden remains distinct from the constitutional proof allocation.

**Holdings navigation:** Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion emergency care; Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992) — Abortion informed consent.

#### Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-749.
**Decided:** June 19, 1995.

##### Private parade composition and compelled inclusion

**Holding:** Applying a public-accommodations law to compel private parade organizers to include an identified contingent conveying a message they do not choose violates the First Amendment. A parade remains expression despite broad admissions, multiple themes and the absence of a single precise message; the rule does not exempt ordinary nonexpressive goods or services from nondiscrimination law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the as-applied private-expression holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Public-street use under a neutral permit does not convert this private parade into a governmental forum. The dismissed and unappealed state-action/public-forum theory against the City is not revived. Independent review of intertwined expression characterizations retains credibility deference. Message misattribution in other settings remains open; no general antidiscrimination exemption or premise that ordinary customer identity is itself a message is adopted.

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.




#### Jackson v. Benson

**Decided:** 1999-03-16.

##### Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction

**Holding:** Wisconsin may not justify public payment for the demonstrated devotional instructional function merely by neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement of its provider-cost payment. ##### The corrective judgment must preserve the actual limits of the federal conflict

### Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### The framework retaining protected choice

**Holding:** Before viability, the patient retains the right to decide whether to continue a pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons; the State may not prohibit that protected choice, give another person effective control over it, or prevent timely care necessary for life or health. After viability the State may prohibit abortion only with the required life-and-health protection, while competent practice, genuinely informed consent, safety and lawful administration remain regulable within these boundaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Viability is individualized and medically supported; no fixed week or conclusive calendar presumption is adopted. Necessary care includes relevant physical and mental health and cannot await imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable treatment, not official approval of the patient's reasons. The holding does not decide constitutional fetal personhood, general public funding, conscience claims, every postviability restriction or admission of a particular drug.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The retained liberty governs the separate provision-specific dispositions. Its six supporters do not all join the complete framework, which has five supporters.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe v. Wade — Retains the protected previability decision, viability boundary and required postviability life-and-health safeguard; this holding does not itself decide the separate treatment of the trimester timetable.
- Griswold v. Connecticut — Applies protection of reproductive decisionmaking as a foundation of the retained liberty.
- Eisenstadt v. Baird — Applies the individual character of reproductive choice; marital status does not supply official control over the decision.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Applies the principle that substantial restrictions on access can burden the protected decision itself, with no general rule about every law implicating sexual freedom.
- Doe v. Bolton — Retains individualized clinical judgment and meaningful physical-and-mental-health protection; no general professional immunity follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Applies the prohibition on State-conferred spousal control and preserves the distinction between voluntary informed consent and interference with protected choice.
- Bellotti v. Baird, Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota, and Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health — Preserve their particular parental-involvement and independent-authorization safeguards; they do not supply an adult spouse's veto or a universal cure for every added parental burden.

##### The standard governing previability regulation after this decision

**Holding:** The Court adopts a complete six-step standard governing previability abortion regulation. Substantial obstacles and violations of the protected baseline are independently invalid. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires the State to substantiate its justification and fit and answer identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. The complete standard replaces the independent trimester timetable in the same holding.

The adopted inquiry has the following complete operative scope:

1. Identify the enacted duty, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances, separating actual commands from assumed administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care independently violates the protected baseline and cannot be justified later in the inquiry. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration makes intervention unavoidable. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe, suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons or a legislative definition declaring all noncompliant care unsafe.
2. Identify actual function and purpose. Safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration and financial accountability may supply permissible functions. Truthful persuasion and a preference for childbirth are permissible; moral disapproval, punishment or deterrence through blocked access are not independent justifications. Obstructive purpose established objectively from text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the duty; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish it. Medical and deliberative justifications require support addressing their actual functions.
3. Challengers must establish a concrete, nontrivial additional interference attributable to the duty, including demonstrated expense, appointments, delay, provider-capacity loss, clinical risk, coercion or disclosure consequences. Compare the system with and without that obligation, or the identified challenged package, holding other law and circumstances constant. Consider cumulative operation without double counting. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding injury, retain that group throughout, and use evidence-supported subgroups where appropriate. Do not average burdens over all pregnancies or define the group only as successful claimants. Poverty, distance, work, transportation and abuse matter through their causal connection to the State's added condition; exceptions, bypasses and providers count only if usable. No universal monetary, distance or time cutoff applies. Reliable evidence and reasonable predictions may establish prospective interference without waiting for lost access. Meaningful interference triggers review but does not itself establish invalidity.
4. Apply the protections in sequence. A violation of the baseline, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle ends the inquiry in invalidity. An obstacle seriously obstructs practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care in the affected population; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and successful compliance by some does not establish access for others. No compelling interest rescues an established obstacle. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; identify the severe intrusion and why the first inquiry did not resolve it. The protections may overlap, but an obstacle cannot be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical decisionmaking, health or protected confidentiality may require exacting justification even where practical access remains usable. For remaining meaningful lesser interference, the State must substantiate its permissible justification and the fit of the additional duty. If challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it, including any inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequence. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice: no particular legislative finding, new study in every case, scientific certainty, numerical benefit calculation or disproof of every hypothetical alternative is required. A court may not substitute its preferred policy once justification and fit are established. Below meaningful interference, the heightened access inquiry ends, but prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary legal requirements remain. Challengers bear duty, population, causation and trigger; the State bears the applicable justification, tailoring or fit burden. Ordinary proof rules for historical facts and separate statutory defenses do not change.
5. Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication. Assess medical reasons against competent evidence, accepted standards, individual circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement has serious evidentiary weight but no association holds a veto; neither a bare clinical assertion nor unsupported official preference is conclusive. Necessary care must remain timely, without an unnecessary committee veto or documentation used as prior permission. Duties must be definite and applicable culpability requirements respected; reasonable later professional disagreement alone does not establish every element of an offense. Fraud, malpractice and misconduct remain enforceable. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material. Concise factual assistance notices need not entail ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accurate content does not itself justify burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Analyze useful confidential records separately from public disclosure and enforcement of invalid conditions.
6. Identify the particular violation and tailor relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under severability law and address dependent certifications, reports and sanctions expressly. Another patient's ability to comply does not defeat an individual claim. Broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation causes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it; voluntary compliance in some cases alone does not justify compulsion. Otherwise limit relief to proved applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not dispense with causation, scope or justification analysis. Cumulative-burden relief addresses the challenged contribution, not all existing regulation. Replacement of the timetable neither dissolves injunctions nor authorizes every challenged duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a direct five-Justice rule; no Marks synthesis or addition of Kennedy's separate approach is required. An independent numerical facial-review threshold is not adopted. The spousal-notice holding supplies its distinct provision-specific affected-group analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The complete framework replaces the independent trimester timetable effective June 29, 1992. Existing injunctions must be conformed to the actual component judgments, not automatically dissolved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe and Doe: preserve the protected decision and clinical safeguards while replacing the independent timetable.
- Carey and Akron: support protection against serious and unjustified interference; the general lesser-burden substantiation rule is expressly adopted here.
- Marks: no aggregation of dissenting rationales is necessary because five Justices directly join the complete rule.
- Ohio v. Akron: its facial-review formulation is qualified at the scope expressly stated in Step 6 and the spousal-notice holding.

##### Stare decisis and the constitutional foundation

**Holding:** Roe's protected reproductive liberty, viability boundary and necessary life-and-health protection are retained because renewed disagreement with the constitutional judgment supplies no sufficient justification for withdrawing that settled protection. The Court may distinguish or alter a separable regulatory rule for a demonstrated legal reason, but such treatment does not silently overrule the liberty that the rule implements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional foundation, each regulatory proposition and each coalition remain distinct. Changed membership, continuing disagreement or criticism of one regulatory implementation does not silently withdraw the protected liberty. No wholesale overruling of Akron or Thornburgh follows; their actual treatment appears with the affected duties.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Arizona v. Rumsey — Applies the requirement of a special justification beyond a belief that a constitutional precedent was wrongly decided.
- Roe v. Wade — Reaffirms its protected-choice foundation, viability boundary and necessary life-and-health safeguard; regulatory implementation receives separate treatment.
- Griswold v. Connecticut and Eisenstadt v. Baird — Their protection of individual reproductive choice remains a doctrinal foundation for retaining Roe.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Retains the connection between protected reproductive choice and meaningful access to its exercise; its existence undercuts a claim that Roe's foundation has been doctrinally displaced.
- Doe v. Bolton and Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Their clinical-judgment, consent and third-party-control rules demonstrate separable applications of the protected liberty and remain subject to the specific holdings stated here.
- Akron v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health and Thornburgh v. American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists — No wholesale overruling follows from this stare-decisis holding; each actual qualification, retained protection or changed rule is identified in the provision-specific holdings.

##### The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation

**Holding:** The independent trimester timetable is displaced together with adoption of the complete six-step replacement. Five Justices adopt that integrated holding. All nine reject continuation of the former timetable in result, but the other four do not join the replacement standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author. The separate result of rejecting the former timetable has nine supporters; only these five adopt its integrated replacement.

**Material application:** The former first-trimester reservation of decision and effectuation to patient and physician and the later health-regulation timing rule are displaced. The viability boundary and necessary-care protection remain. Gestational age may substantiate a particular safety duty when evidence links it to medical risk, but crossing a trimester boundary does not independently change the previability standard.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The integrated replacement takes effect with the complete framework; existing injunctions remain subject to ordinary conforming relief. Statistical use of trimester categories remains distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe: independent trimester timing restrictions displaced; protected choice, individualized viability and necessary life-and-health care retained.
- Doe: individualized clinical judgment and relevant physical and psychological health remain protected.
- Webster: its criticism of rigid timing supports reconsideration of implementation, without treating its fractured opinions as an earlier complete overruling.




### Abortion emergency care

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### Medical emergency and the protective construction of §3203

**Holding:** Section 3203 survives this facial challenge under the prospective construction that compliance with the Act cannot create a significant threat to the patient's life or health. Its good-faith clinical standard permits prompt treatment of preeclampsia, inevitable abortion and prematurely ruptured membranes at onset, without waiting for catastrophic deterioration; the ruling does not adjudicate every other postviability restriction or every degree of clinical risk.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 3203 defines medical emergency through the physician's good-faith clinical judgment: a condition necessitating immediate abortion to avert death, or one in which delay creates serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The significant-threat interpretation and application to the three obstetric conditions are the Third Circuit's construction, not additional statutory words or a binding Pennsylvania Supreme Court interpretation. Section 3205(c)'s separate disclosure defense is not an emergency-delay exception. A materially different authoritative construction or enforcement remains challengeable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Facial relief against the emergency definition is denied under the prospective protective construction. No separate vagueness judgment is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Webster v. Reproductive Health Services: applies its attention to a medical rule's workable, prudent construction; does not adopt every part of its fractured abortion analysis.
- Roe v. Wade and Doe v. Bolton: their retained necessary-care protection is stated separately at its own proposition-level coalition; this unanimous statutory construction does not create unanimous adherence to their entire reasoning.

##### Emergency explanation under §3205(b)

**Holding:** Section 3205(b) requires an explanation of the medical indications supporting emergency abortion before the procedure only if possible. It does not condition necessary immediate care on completing an explanation or obtaining advance permission, and it survives the facial challenge on that limited operation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** When medical emergency compels an abortion, the physician shall inform the woman, prior to abortion if possible, of the medical indications supporting the judgment that abortion is necessary to avert death or substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. The statutory text appears in the appendix at 505 U.S. 903–904.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The explanation requirement survives with its express if-possible qualification; it creates no advance-permission or treatment-delay condition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: applies the permission for appropriate informed-consent administration while keeping interference with the treatment decision distinct.

##### The distinct information-omission defense in §3205(c)

**Holding:** A physician is protected from liability for failing to furnish §3205(a)'s information if the physician proves by a preponderance of the evidence a reasonable belief that furnishing it would have a severely adverse effect on the patient's physical or mental health. This statutory disclosure defense does not supply a general waiting-period waiver, authorize delay of necessary emergency treatment or replace §3203's emergency protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas: 9 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The paragraph identified by the District Court and Third Circuit as §3205(c) protects a physician from liability for failure to furnish subsection (a)'s information when the physician proves, by a preponderance of the evidence, a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect the patient's physical or mental health. The defense concerns the physician's reasonable belief, not proof that feared injury actually occurred. Its enacted preponderance burden does not displace the constitutional justification allocation. It does not excuse every unfavorable reaction or inconvenience.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The enacted disclosure defense remains available without becoming a general waiting waiver or a substitute for prompt emergency treatment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth: ordinary informed-consent administration remains permissible subject to the actual statutory and constitutional limits; no general proof rule is derived from it.




#### Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1712.
**Decided:** February 13, 1995.

For the other questions in this decision, see Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements (The access claims return for a supported assessment and prompt interim consideration).

##### The complete Casey standard governs the access and facial challenge

**Holding:** Casey's complete six-step standard governs the challenged previability access duties, without Salerno's independent no-valid-applications barrier or an independent numerical facial-review threshold. Courts must identify the duty and protected baseline, function and purpose, attributable interference and the affected class, the applicable substantive protection, clinical and communication safeguards, and relief corresponding to the proved violation; finding no substantial obstacle does not end review of remaining severe or meaningful interference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six Justices.

**Material application:** Telephone communication by the authorized physician or agent remains relevant; no statutory additional personal visit is found. The physician retains responsibility for the gestational determination, while an agent may obtain history and convey that determination. The patient's written certification and its receipt by the physician or agent precede the abortion, not necessarily the visit by twenty-four hours. Notice that materials are available does not require accepting, reading or endorsing them; copies must be furnished if chosen. Assistance may be available, but payment is not guaranteed. A support obligation is not a promise of collection. Statutory freedom to comment or dissociate concerns the printed materials; the lower court additionally permits comment on assistance and paternal-support statements.

Casey's qualified information rulings cannot be imported without their limits. Pennsylvania separately protects omission of information upon proof by a preponderance of a reasonable belief that furnishing it would severely adversely affect physical or mental health; the feared injury need not occur, and that defense does not waive the waiting period. Pennsylvania also permits, but does not require, omission of paternal-support information in a rape pregnancy, including spousal rape, without a police-report condition. North Dakota's supplied text contains neither provision. Their absence requires attention to supported and preserved compulsory-information applications; it neither inserts these exceptions into North Dakota law nor establishes automatic invalidity. No independent new speech claim or parental-involvement challenge is adjudicated.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992): applies the complete six-step rule, its proof allocation, broad necessary-care safeguards and tailored-relief qualification unchanged; Pennsylvania's particular interval result is not an automatic North Dakota disposition.
- United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987): does not supply an independent all-applications barrier overriding Casey in this abortion-access challenge; its operation outside this setting is not decided.
- Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health, 497 U.S. 502 (1990): its rejection of speculative additional duties remains relevant, while its older facial formulation remains subject to Casey's actual qualification; a hypothetical second visit is not treated as enacted law.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The complete operative standard is retained without change; see Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, June 29, 1992, Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent, the previability-regulation holding. Baseline violations, obstructive purpose and substantial obstacles independently invalidate a duty; remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring, and remaining meaningful interference requires substantiated justification and fit and an answer to identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. No universal numerical threshold, mandatory new study or cheapest-policy requirement is added. Causation, the stable affected population, usable exceptions, clinical and communication safeguards, and relief matched to proved violations remain necessary. Pennsylvania's particular interval result supplies no automatic North Dakota disposition.

##### The abortion definition excludes an unintended diagnostic termination

**Holding:** Section 14-02.1-02(1), defining abortion as termination of human pregnancy with an intention other than producing a live birth or removing a dead embryo or fetus, identifies a purposeful pregnancy termination. Diagnostic amniocentesis followed by an unintended pregnancy loss is not brought within that definition merely because the diagnostic purpose is neither of the two stated exclusions; this reading defeats the preserved facial notice and enforcement objection without a borrowed culpability default.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is not immunity for diagnostic treatment, negligence or other misconduct; it does not decide a completed prosecution, civil award, general knowledge-of-illegality requirement or actual-knowledge minimum for every statutory element. It does not resolve the emergency definition or every sanction attached to consent duties.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994): applies its contextual definition analysis without transplanting its federal scienter construction or treating fault as a universal cure.
- Kolender v. Lawson, 461 U.S. 352 (1983): applies the separate notice and minimum-enforcement-guidance requirements; distinguishes an undefined official compliance demand from this bounded medical act and purpose.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): preserves its requirement to examine the particular trigger and fault separately; the present ground rests on the category's own words rather than fault addressed to a different element.

##### The specified emergency terms supply a prospective clinical criterion

**Holding:** The preserved facial vagueness challenge to section 14-02.1-02(7) fails because its specified outcomes and timing, assessed through the physician's prospective best clinical judgment, give notice and enforcement guidance: the pregnancy must require immediate abortion to avert death, or a twenty-four-hour delay must create grave peril of immediate and irreversible loss of major bodily function. Gravity, immediacy and major bodily function call for a professional forecast, not certainty, a numerical prognosis or waiting until the threatened harm occurs; this holding does not adjudicate the distinct claim that the exception's substantive breadth fails to protect particular necessary care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Clarity is not a ruling that this restricted emergency category satisfies every Casey life-and-health requirement. Casey's broader necessary-care protection, including individualized physical and mental health and care before catastrophic deterioration, remains law. The emergency undue-burden claim was not pressed below and is not decided here. The federal appellate significant-threat construction is not enacted text or an authoritative North Dakota Supreme Court construction; it does not license deleting immediate, irreversible or major from the statute. The present notice ground stands independently of that broader construction and of any presumed default fault rule.

Section 14-02.1-03(1)'s emergency exception excuses that subsection's ordinary requirements, but requires an explanation of the medical indications before the abortion if possible and written certification of those indications. The conditional explanation is no advance-permission requirement, and certification supplies no authority to delay necessary treatment. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish all elements of a criminal offense or civil damages; disagreement may remain relevant under actual applicable law. Fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct are not immunized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Vuitch, 402 U.S. 62 (1971): applies the intelligibility of professional judgment at the stated scope; its D.C. statute's life-or-health construction and prosecution burden do not become North Dakota law.
- Doe v. Bolton, 410 U.S. 179 (1973): applies its recognition of clinical judgment without importing Georgia's broader statutory construction or creating professional immunity.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): distinguishes its conflicting viability and treatment duties, while retaining independent scrutiny of the actual trigger and culpability for error.
- Kolender v. Lawson, 461 U.S. 352 (1983): applies minimum enforcement guidelines and distinguishes undefined official satisfaction from the stated clinical criteria; no universal all-applications bar is adopted.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992): preserves broad necessary-care safeguards and conditional emergency explanation; its Pennsylvania construction was not a separate emergency-vagueness holding and does not determine this distinct claim.

##### A doubtful default-fault rationale does not invalidate the general penalty by itself

**Holding:** Section 12.1-02-02 alone does not establish default willfulness for the silent misdemeanor clause of section 14-02.1-11, and neither that default nor the latter section's separately expressed willful rule-or-regulation infraction supplies the criminal or civil elements of every chapter violation. Rejection of that additional rationale does not defeat the independently sufficient definition holdings or establish facial invalidity of the general penalty merely because its first sentence lacks an express culpability term; actual element-specific fault and liability remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The misdemeanor clause covers a chapter violation for which no other penalty is specifically prescribed. The distinct infraction clause concerns a willful violation of a promulgated rule or regulation. The latter's express term does not amend the former. The independent civil remedy provides $10,000 punitive damages plus treble actual damages for an abortion without the required informed consent, and $5,000 punitive damages plus treble actual damages for an attempted abortion without compliance. The Court enters no liability determination or award under either provision and creates no civil scienter defense. No separate facial judgment on an unpresented civil-damages theory is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Village of Hoffman Estates v. Flipside, 455 U.S. 489 (1982): applies contextual notice, enforcement and scienter principles, with greater clarity required for criminal sanctions and protected conduct; its commercial all-applications formulation does not govern without its premise.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): preserves the separation of an intelligible clinical standard from fault concerning an erroneous assessment; no particular universal fault level is supplied.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994): preserves its context-specific distinction between definition and fault; no universal knowledge element or scienter cure is inferred.
- City of Dickinson v. Mueller, 261 N.W.2d 787 (N.D. 1977), and State v. North Dakota Education Association, 262 N.W.2d 731 (N.D. 1978): apply their title limitation when assessing the asserted default; no abortion-specific state construction is attributed to either case.
- State v. Goetz, 312 N.W.2d 1 (N.D. 1981): distinguishes definition of an expressly enacted willfulness term from importing default fault; its securities-law instruction and knowledge-of-illegality discussion do not settle these elements.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Access review is vacated and remanded, 6–3; the abortion-definition, emergency-definition and associated limited general-penalty rejections are each affirmed, 8–1. The District Court must promptly consider interim protection under the ordinary standard and current record. No dissolved injunction is automatically restored, no final injunction or hearing is ordered, and no emergency-access claim unpressed below or new parental or speech claim is decided.




#### Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich

**Decided:** 1998-04-20.

##### The complete Casey framework governs the facial and access claims

**Holding:** Casey’s complete six-step standard, as applied in Fargo, governs the challenged previability regulation. **Proposition-level authority:** Part I; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the governing standard, including its proof allocation and qualifications:

**Limits and questions not reached:** The complete governing framework is the six-step rule in Casey, as applied in Fargo: identify the duty and protected baseline; identify actual function and purpose; establish attributable interference and a stable affected population; apply the ordered substantive protections; protect clinical judgment and accurate communication; identify the violation and tailor relief. ##### An act-defined method and professional clinical criteria are not categorically unintelligible

**Holding:** Ohio’s method definition and specified good-faith, reasonable-medical-judgment criteria cannot be invalidated on the categorical ground that statutory and medical procedure labels overlap or that every subsidiary clinical judgment lacks an express culpability term. **Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful skull suction is the defined act, with suction curettage and suction aspiration expressly excluded; knowingly performing or attempting it supplies the separate prohibition. Overlap with some D&E does not itself establish vagueness, ban every D&E or prove substantive validity. Criminal and civil fault remain distinct; no universal Ohio default fault level, professional immunity or knowledge-of-illegality rule is supplied.

##### Postviability prohibition must preserve the presented necessary mental-health care

**Holding:** A State may prohibit abortion after viability, but it must permit abortion necessary in competent individualized medical judgment to avert grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. The patient need not await catastrophe; transient distress, unsupported assertion or mere preference does not establish the protected necessity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III.A; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct joins. Part III.B’s particular construction and relief directions receive the same joins except Stone-Zsela’s: six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Part III.B requires an Ohio-law-supported protective construction of §§2919.17(A), 2919.16(F)/(J) and genuinely dependent duties if available; otherwise the established unconstitutional exclusion remains unenforceable. Courts may neither invent an unlimited exception nor assume all protective constructions impossible. Other postviability duties are not automatically invalidated.

##### The method prohibition requires application to actual conduct, care and alternatives

**Holding:** The final method-ban disposition must be vacated where categorical notice reasoning or the displaced facial formula substituted for Casey’s substantive inquiry. On remand, the lower court must assess actual affected applications, attributable interference, usable alternative care and the distinct statutory defenses before deciding invalidity and matching relief; previability and postviability applications remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV; Souter, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The physician’s prima facie showing that every available alternative poses greater health risk triggers the prosecutor’s burden to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that at least one available alternative would not. The civil affirmative defense remains separate and receives no imported criminal burden shift. Alternatives must be usable for the patient and circumstances; no new evidence hearing is automatic.

##### Relief must follow Ohio severability law and the component judgments

**Holding:** Independently operable provisions and applications must receive Ohio’s state-law severability analysis; invalidity of one command does not itself eliminate the Act. The lower court must conform relief to the affirmed necessary-care protection, determine the unresolved applications and dependent duties separately, and promptly decide interim protection under ordinary equitable standards without automatic blanket enforcement or permanent blanket invalidation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V.A’s ordinary severability principles receive all eight Associates’ direct joins. Part V.B’s full remaining mandate receives Souter, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part III.B separately supplies the six-Justice health-linked construction directions. Stone’s decision to leave precise relief and severability below does not add a join in these particular analyses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ohio §1.50, Geiger and Maurer require independent operation, fidelity to legislative intent and no judicial insertion or fundamental disruption. Viability testing begins at week twenty-two subject to its emergency exception; the week-twenty-four presumption is rebuttable and is no fixed constitutional boundary. Certifications, facility requirements, method selection, second-physician presence and dependent sanctions require their own treatment; no whole-Act validation or invalidation follows.

Under §2919.17(A), purposeful performance, inducement or attempted performance or inducement of an abortion on a viable fetus is prohibited unless a physician, acting in good faith and using reasonable medical judgment, determines either that abortion is necessary to prevent maternal death or a serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function, or that the fetus is not viable after the assessment required by §2919.18(A). The latter provision requires, beginning with the twenty-second week, a good-faith determination of nonviability using reasonable medical judgment, after examining the patient and performing or arranging the viability tests a reasonable physician would use. Its medical-emergency exception excuses that testing obligation. The separate twenty-four-week viability presumption is rebuttable.

Section 2919.16(F) defines the emergency through the physician’s good-faith, reasonable medical assessment of a pregnancy complication: immediate abortion must be needed to prevent the patient’s death or to avoid a serious risk, created by delay, of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. Section 2919.16(J) separately defines that serious-risk category by a medically diagnosed condition complicating the pregnancy that directly or indirectly causes such impairment. Its nonexclusive examples are preeclampsia, inevitable abortion, prematurely ruptured membranes, diabetes and multiple sclerosis. These enacted categories remain distinct from the constitutional protection and the method-prohibition defenses addressed below.

The five postviability duties are the attending physician’s written necessity certification; a second physician’s written certification after reviewing records and tests; a facility with access to neonatal services; a survival-favoring method unless it creates significantly greater maternal death risk or serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function; and a second physician present to care for the fetus. Their emergency exemption permits noncompliance when its terms are satisfied; it does not require omission. The method-choice exception is distinct from the alternative-method affirmative defense.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical notice/scienter ground is vacated 8–1, all Associates supporting. Necessary-care protection is affirmed 7–2, Scalia and Thomas opposing. The method and remaining bounded mandate are remanded 5–4 by Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Protection must conform to the health holding; unresolved-method interim relief requires prompt ordinary equitable consideration without automatic prior dissolution or permanent blanket affirmance.

Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992):** Applied without changing its complete six-step rule, proof allocation, individualized viability, physical-and-mental-health baseline or tailored-relief requirement. Pennsylvania’s emergency-delay construction and distinct information-omission defense do not resolve Ohio’s separate commands.
- **Fargo Women’s Health Organization v. Schafer, February 13, 1995:** Applied to preserve complete access review, separate intelligibility from actual fault and substantive necessary-care protection, and require supported application and ordinary interim-relief consideration. No categorical express-scienter requirement is derived from it.
- **United States v. Salerno, 481 U.S. 739 (1987):** Its all-applications formulation does not independently bar the Casey challenge; its operation outside this setting remains unaddressed.
- **Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health, 497 U.S. 502 (1990):** Its facial-review formulation remains subject to Casey’s tailored-relief qualification; it supplies no independent numerical or no-valid-applications threshold here.
- **United States v. Vuitch, 402 U.S. 62 (1971):** Applied for the intelligibility of professional clinical judgment, without importing the D.C. statute’s construction or prosecution burden into Ohio law.

### Strout v. Albanese — 2000-02-28

[Record](../records/strout-v-albanese_merits_2000-02-28.md)

#### Event, question and statutory setting

**Strout v. Albanese**; review of 178 F.3d 57 (1st Cir. 1999). Certiorari granted November 1, 1999; argued January 24, 2000; decided February 28, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, affirming the District of Maine's judgment at 13 F. Supp. 2d 112.

The granted questions concern whether excluding otherwise eligible sectarian schools and their students from a generally available tuition-assistance program solely because the schools are religious violates free exercise, and whether establishment principles require, permit or forbid that exclusion when assistance reaches schools through independent parental choice. Three families in Minot, where no secondary school was maintained, sought ordinary St. Dominic's tuition. Their application concerns integrated sectarian instruction, not a separately identified secular service or expression forum. The case was resolved on cross-motions for summary judgment without a genuine dispute of material fact.

Section 2951 retains basic school approval, nonsectarian status in accordance with the First Amendment, incorporation under Maine or United States law, reporting and auditing under sections 2952 and 2953 and requirements adopted under section 2954. Schools with at least 60 percent publicly funded students, measured by the previous year's October and April average enrollment, must participate in statewide assessment. Upon a school unit's request, the private school must release copies of all student records for students transferring from that private school to the requesting unit. The repealed fourth condition is not restored. Section 5204(4) applies when the resident unit neither maintains a secondary school nor contracts for secondary-school privileges. It permits attendance at an approved private school, a public school in an adjoining unit accepting tuition students, or an approved school in another state or country, subject to receiving-school permission. The resident unit must pay tuition up to the legal rate. The Department's acceptance of schools' self-identification without a classification dispute is a lower-court finding about administration, not a statutory command.

#### Maine may refuse the integrated devotional tuition presented here

**Holding and operative rule:** The Religion Clauses permit Maine to refuse public payment for the integrated devotional instructional function presented by this ordinary-tuition application. Independent parental choice and neutral student eligibility do not themselves justify public purchase of demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Jackson requires attention to the instructional function purchased; Agostini applies that inquiry within the governing, unified Lemon test. The parents selected St. Dominic's, and the requested ordinary tuition of approximately $3,850 was below the $4,900 statutory ceiling. That distinguishes the amount formula from Jackson's provider operating-and-debt costs, but does not separate the devotional work in the integrated program for which payment is sought. Maine is not buying an identified interpreter, secular remedial service, or controlled printing benefit. Rosenberger preserves its independently directed publication program; it does not make every publicly financed activity a speech forum. The actual funded function therefore supports refusal of this tuition without prohibiting the families' private educational choice. Neither a school's religious name nor its affiliation alone establishes the character of its work. The Court does not approve a universal religious-provider exclusion, decide all voucher arrangements, or create a constitutional entitlement to every education subsidy.

#### Free exercise receives its own inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Refusing this public purchase of integrated devotional instruction does not, in this application, violate the Free Exercise Clause. The conclusion does not depend on whether attending the school is a central religious tenet and does not authorize religious hostility, exclusion from a neutral private-expression forum, or categorical rejection of genuinely severable secular work solely because its provider is religious.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Lukumi requires scrutiny of religious targeting; Shahar preserves an independent free-exercise inquiry and rejects theological compulsion as a prerequisite to sincere exercise. Calling a program secular cannot by itself answer that inquiry. Here the challenged benefit is public purchase of the ordinary integrated instructional package, and the State may refuse that financed devotional function while the parents remain free to choose it privately. The lower court's observation that no parent described attendance as a central tenet does not defeat the claim, and the Court does not adopt that reasoning. Agostini protects its separately administered secular service despite religious location, and the private forum decisions protect genuinely private religious expression on their actual terms. Those distinct relationships remain intact; this tuition request presents neither one. The Court therefore affirms on the actual financing ground rather than an affiliation-based presumption or an independent-prong Lemon test.

#### The affirmance is limited to the granted claims and this application

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment sustaining the challenged refusal of integrated tuition is affirmed within the granted Establishment and Free Exercise questions. The decision does not reopen the ungranted speech, equal-protection or parental-liberty claims, the unchallenged dismissals of local defendants, or distinct applications for reliably separated secular services.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The cross-motions were decided on an agreed absence of genuine material factual dispute. No remand is required to adjudicate a different benefit application not before the Court or to restructure the school. The statutory payment duty for eligible schools remains subject to its independent approval, receiving-school permission and tuition-rate conditions. Affirmance leaves the challenged denial in place; it awards no tuition, damages or recoupment, orders no transfer or school closure, and imposes no statewide funding cutoff.

#### Precedent treatment

- **Jackson v. Benson:** Applied to the actual integrated instructional function; its provider-cost formula is distinguished in amount, without making capped tuition sufficient to separate devotional work. Its limits preserving private benefits and secular services remain.
- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing unified Lemon inquiry and to the distinction between integrated devotional work and separately controlled secular instruction; no categorical inference follows from religious location.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues only through Agostini's unified inquiry; the former independent purpose, effect and entanglement prongs are not revived.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its controlled printing benefit for independently directed expression remains valid; that relationship does not determine the ordinary tuition purchase here.
- **Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah:** Its neutrality and religious-targeting inquiry is preserved; refusal of the demonstrated devotional purchase is not a license for hostile religious discrimination.
- **Shahar v. Bowers:** Applied to the independent free-exercise inquiry and the irrelevance of theological compulsion to sincere exercise.
- **Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind:** Its actual individual vocational-benefit relationship remains distinct; it supplies no automatic answer for integrated tuition.
- **Mueller v. Allen:** Its general tax-deduction relationship remains distinct and is not displaced by this application.
- **Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District:** Its antecedent-law remand supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding or general tuition entitlement.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its bounded private-expression and worship-access protection remains intact; forum access differs from purchasing integrated instruction.

#### Resulting law and limits

Effective February 28, 2000, Jackson's funded-function rule applies to the integrated ordinary-tuition application presented here despite the tuition cap and parental choice. Agostini's unified Lemon inquiry continues; its former independent prongs do not return. The separate Free Exercise inquiry rejects theological centrality as a prerequisite, but does not require public purchase of this devotional instructional function. Religious identity alone remains insufficient to establish the financing defect. Genuinely independent private benefits, neutral private-expression forums and reliably separated secular services retain their actual protections. The decision settles this application, not every permitted or required tuition arrangement.

#### Source and record limits

The [petition and appendices, opposition and reply](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40386005_1011) supply the program terms, statutory conditions and record limits. The [First Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/178/f3d/57/strout-v-albanese) supplies the judgment under review; the petition reproduces both lower opinions and the historical statutory appendix. The stated grant, argument and decision dates are the case's governing merits calendar. No finding about the devotional content of every course or every religious provider is made. The Department's self-identification practice is described as a record finding, not enacted statutory text.

### Board of Regents of the University of Wisconsin System v. Southworth — 2000-03-22

[Record](../records/board-of-regents-v-southworth_merits_2000-03-22.md)

#### Event and question

Board of Regents of the University of Wisconsin System v. Southworth, No. 98-1189. Merits decision, March 22, 2000. Argued November 9, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit, 151 F.3d 717, decided August 10, 1998, Nos. 97-3510 and 97-3548.

The question is whether a public university violates the First Amendment by requiring students to pay an activity fee supporting organizations engaged in political, ideological and religious expression. The live dispute concerns speech and association and the objectors' allocable fees. The general fee power, nonallocable services, ordinary student government, nonideological organizations, nonobjectors' money, the student newspaper and Distinguished Lecture Series are outside the challenge.

In 1995–96 a full-time Madison student paid a mandatory $165.75 segregated fee each semester; nonpayment prevented receipt of grades and graduation. About 80 percent supported unchallenged nonallocable services. The challenged allocation program supported registered student organizations and qualifying services through student-government processes subject to University approval. The ordinary grant and General Student Services Fund routes were stipulated to operate without viewpoint discrimination or University advocacy of a particular view. Student organizations controlled their own messages, which included lobbying and other political advocacy; the University disclaimed their expression. Most funding reimbursed expenses. WISPIRG received lump-sum funding, and all uses of that money are not established.

The stipulation expressly excludes the referendum. Student government treated referendum outcomes as binding, permitting allocation, termination or veto of a group's funding. The record contains a WISPIRG funding vote and an October 1996 termination vote, without establishing a constitutional finding about each ballot's operation. The lower court had already confined relief to objectors' allocable fees and removed the district court's all-students injunction and prescribed detailed administrative mechanism. It nevertheless sustained the general compelled-subsidy liability rule challenged here.

#### The genuine educational private-expression program may be supported by a general fee

**Holding and operative rule:** A public university may require a generally applicable student activity fee to support a broad program of independent extracurricular student expression serving its educational mission when eligibility, allocation and administration are objective and viewpoint neutral. Within that program, a student's disagreement with a recipient's political, ideological or religious viewpoint does not require an ideological opt-out or pro rata refund.

**Authority:** Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg, supplies six direct rationale votes for this holding and its limits. Stevens, Souter and Breyer support the judgment on the distinct ground described below.

**Controlling explanation:** Compelled financing implicates expression even without personal attribution. Abood and Keller protect against compulsory ideological support outside the representative duties that justify union and integrated-bar charges; Lehnert applies those distinct union principles. Here the common educational function is maintaining opportunities for independent and competing student expression. It does not appoint one organization to speak for every payer. Requiring courts to sort student ideas by agreement with an official educational orthodoxy would defeat that function.

Glickman recognizes expressive burdens while permitting a specified common expense within collective agricultural regulation. Its nonideological product-promotion holding does not itself authorize student ideological subsidies; this different program supplies its own justification. Rosenberger protects viewpoint equality in its controlled private-publication benefit and reserved the compulsory-fee question now presented. Independent student control, broad educational opportunity and viewpoint-neutral administration together answer that question here. Lack of attribution, common benefit, majority approval or enrollment alone would not suffice.

The ordinary SGAF/GSSF allocation and approval process is stipulated neutral, and the University does not advocate a particular viewpoint through it. Students need not adopt recipients' views, join their groups or surrender their own expression. The fee therefore supports a forum, not governmental endorsement of each message. The University may choose optional contributions or refunds, but the Constitution does not require ideological exemptions on these facts. No finding that opt-outs are impossible or every subsidy indispensable is made.

#### Neutrality governs the actual program, not every university expenditure

**Holding and operative rule:** The protection for a compulsory independent-expression program requires viewpoint-neutral eligibility, allocation and administration; neither an educational label nor popular approval permits selection by viewpoint. Reasonable neutral limits on program scope, financial accountability, performance and fraud prevention remain available; the rule does not require equal grants or funding every activity.

**Authority:** The same six Justices join Justice Thomas's opinion on this holding and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Rosenberger distinguishes independently attributed student expression from official advocacy and forbids viewpoint exclusion within the benefit actually opened. A neutral program may define the work it supports and require an account of its performance; those conditions cannot conceal approval or rejection of ideology. The decision addresses the actual broad student-expression program and establishes no constitutional requirement that curricular choices, faculty instruction or governmental speech follow the same forum rule. It does not immunize those activities from other constitutional review.

This case establishes no particular devotional purchase. Agostini's unified Lemon inquiry, Jackson's funded-function rule and Strout's integrated-tuition application remain distinct from protected religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger. Neutrality alone does not authorize governmental worship or devotional instruction as such. The Court neither decides nor revives an Establishment, free-exercise or RFRA claim, and does not conclusively approve a particular external payment without its facts and proper claim.

#### The referendum requires separate examination

**Holding and operative rule:** A university cannot distribute access to its compulsory private-expression program according to majority approval of a group's viewpoint. The binding referendum is outside the ordinary-allocation neutrality stipulation, so the Seventh Circuit's general compulsory-subsidy judgment must be reversed and the case remanded only to determine the referendum's actual operation and appropriate relief.

**Authority:** The same six Justices join Justice Thomas's complete referendum rule. Stevens, Souter and Breyer independently support the referendum remand without adopting the complete necessity framework.

**Controlling explanation:** The referendum can allocate, terminate or veto funding, and the student government treats its result as binding. Ordinary committee neutrality does not establish referendum neutrality. Minority expression cannot depend on majority ideological consent. Yet the record does not decide every ballot's operation or establish that every possible voting arrangement is unconstitutional. The Court assumes no neutral final override and orders examination of the existing procedure.

The lower court already removed the district court's all-students reach and prescribed administrative scheme. Neither is restored. The late partisan-policy challenge is not reopened by a general reference to unresolved questions; the operative remand concerns the referendum alone and permits only relief its actual defect warrants. No damages, fixed refund, specified WISPIRG payment, universal opt-in procedure or order to fund every recipient is entered.

#### Limits and questions not reached

The operative remand does not reopen a late partisan-policy challenge foreclosed by the reiterated ordinary-allocation stipulation, and no written restriction is assumed rescinded, amended or universally valid. The separate limits on GSSF lobbying or primarily political groups are not exceptions to a different religious-activity provision. The decision does not adjudicate every outside expenditure or every written exclusion. Off-campus location alone supplies no categorical constitutional disposition of an otherwise eligible student expressive activity; the actual program relationship and proper claim remain necessary.

No Establishment, free-exercise, RFRA or state claim is revived or decided. Religious editorial viewpoint is not identical to governmental worship or devotional instruction. No actual devotional purchase is established here, and the ruling does not authorize one. The two reported WISPIRG sums are not treated as the same accounting measure or used to calculate relief. Unchallenged program components remain outside the judgment.

#### Precedent treatment

- **Abood v. Detroit Board of Education; Keller v. State Bar of California:** Their protection against compelled nongermane ideological support remains effective in exclusive-representation and integrated-bar settings. Their representative-duty inquiry does not itself decide this different educational expression program.
- **Lehnert v. Ferris Faculty Association:** Its union chargeability framework and divided applications remain intact; the statutory-duty separate account is not converted into a universal Court rule for universities.
- **Glickman v. Wileman Brothers & Elliott, Inc., June 25, 1997:** Its specified permission for germane nonideological promotion within collective agricultural regulation is distinguished, not expanded into a prior approval of ideological student subsidies. Its recognition that financing can burden expression remains controlling at that scope.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, June 29, 1995:** Its private-publication viewpoint equality and controlled printing-benefit holdings remain intact. The compulsory-fee question it reserved is resolved here for the defined broad educational expression program, with separate safeguards for payers and recipients.
- **Gibson v. Florida Bar, December 4, 1991:** Its advance-protection, chargeability-burden and impartial-review requirements remain for established compulsory nongermane charges. It does not itself establish a violation in the ordinary program upheld here, so those procedures are not imposed on that program.
- **Air Line Pilots Association v. Miller, May 26, 1998:** Its distinction between offering impartial arbitration and requiring nonconsenting objectors to exhaust it remains unchanged. No new mandatory arbitration mechanism is created.
- **Agostini v. Felton, June 23, 1997; Jackson v. Benson, March 16, 1999; Strout v. Albanese, February 28, 2000:** Their distinct rules concerning the actual governmental religious function remain effective. This private-expression decision does not replace Agostini's unified Lemon inquiry, authorize a devotional purchase or treat religious identity alone as governmental religious work.

#### Resulting law and limits

Effective March 22, 2000, a genuine broad educational program for independent extracurricular student expression may be financed through generally applicable student fees without ideological opt-outs when its eligibility, allocations and administration are objective and viewpoint neutral. This is a justification for the defined program, not a conclusion that compulsory financing is nonexpressive or that every useful subsidy is permissible. The protection extends to political, ideological and religious viewpoints within the program's lawful scope. Neutral accountability and program limits remain available; popularity cannot replace viewpoint neutrality. The ordinary-allocation stipulation does not validate the separate referendum. The earlier compelled-representation, private-publication and religious-function rules retain their actual fields.

The [Seventh Circuit opinion, 151 F.3d 717](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/151/151.F3d.717.97-3548.97-3510.html) supplies the program, lower-court reasoning, narrowed decree and claim history. The [published record recitations, 529 U.S. 217](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USREPORTS-529/USREPORTS-529-217) supply the ordinary-allocation stipulation and its express referendum exclusion. [Lehnert, 500 U.S. 507](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep500/usrep500507/usrep500507.pdf) supplies the preexisting union framework and separate accounts. ### Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe — 2000-06-19

[Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe](../records/santa-fe-independent-school-district-v-doe_merits_2000-06-19.md)

#### The school may not delegate its official religious choice to a student majority

**Holding and operative rule:** A public secondary school may not organize an invocation as a representative component of its own football pregame ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that official component will include religious exercise. The challenged board-created election mechanism violates the Establishment Clause under both its unrestricted and nonsectarian/nonproselytizing versions; relief reaches that mechanism while preserving genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes for the rule, explanation and limited affirmance. The particular government-created representative religious choice is essential to all seven joins; the Court adopts no independent religious-pressure holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini requires identification of the governmental act and its function, using purpose, effect and institutional relationships as evidence within the modified Lemon inquiry. Here the board creates an invocation or message slot within its own ceremony; the student council conducts a majority election under the principal's advice and direction; the electorate selects a single volunteer; and the speaker must serve the board's prescribed purposes. This is an official representative component, not generally available access for independently chosen private speakers. Lee forbids official school organization of worship, and Ingebretsen explains why student initiation or delivery cannot alone make that exercise private. A majority vote cannot privatize the board's delegated religious choice.

The District stresses secular purposes, student authorship, no required prayer, optional attendance and the possibility of silence or a secular message. Those features do not remove the existing governmental mechanism. The Court assumes no official textual preapproval and no delivered October prayer; it does not rest on a finding that every secular purpose is a sham. The ability to decline use of the delegated religious authority does not transform it into neutral access. Pinette and Bronx Household protect actually private expression, where government has not adopted the devotion as its own event component. Tanford likewise distinguishes official organization from private composition, without making its adult setting identical to this school policy.

The backup's religious-content limits retain the same official mechanism and do not cure it. The Court does not direct officials to edit private religion into acceptable language. Nor does it prohibit every election, invited student speaker or religious message. The existing mechanism suffices to affirm; Lee and Ingebretsen's separate protection against substantial practical religious pressure remains intact without requiring legal compulsion or compulsory attendance. No additional pressure finding, universal facial-challenge rule, new forum entitlement, graduation ruling or damages award is necessary.

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing governmental-act/function inquiry; purpose, effects and entanglement remain evidence within that inquiry, not independent surviving prongs.

- **Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District:** Its rejection of automatic insulation by student delivery and its protection of lawful private expression apply. Its provision-sensitive interim ruling is not recast as a universal rule invalidating every future religious message.

Government cannot make its own religious choice private by delegating it to a student majority. The independent substantial-pressure rules of Lee and Ingebretsen continue without requiring legally compulsory attendance; this decision need not decide that additional ground.

### Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston, No. 94-749

Merits decision, June 19, 1995

Argued April 25, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court, which sustained application of the public-accommodations law requiring the private organizers to include GLIB. A continuing injunction against the traditional parade and the organizers’ desire to resume it keep the controversy live despite a differently organized 1995 event.

The Court reverses and remands, 9–0. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer support the judgment requiring dissolution of the compelled-inclusion injunction. Justice Souter delivers the single Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices. No separate writing is published.

Applying a public-accommodations law to compel private parade organizers to include an identified contingent conveying a message they do not choose violates the First Amendment. A parade remains expression despite broad admissions, multiple themes and the absence of a single precise message; the rule does not exempt ordinary nonexpressive goods or services from nondiscrimination law.

All nine Justices adopt this protection for private expression in Justice Souter’s Opinion of the Court.

The Court holds that the right to speak includes control over the composition of a private expressive presentation. Barnette prohibits compulsory affirmation, and Tornillo protects editorial choice against compelled inclusion. Turner preserves the importance of a speaker’s selection within a particular medium; its separate cable context is not mechanically imported here. A parade presents expression through the groups and messages its organizers choose, even if those selections are permissive or varied. GLIB’s bannered unit has its own protected message, but that protection creates no entitlement to insert it into another private speaker’s presentation. Use of public streets under a neutral permit does not convert this parade into a governmental forum. Roberts and PruneYard concern access without the same compelled alteration of expression. Independent review of intertwined expression characterizations, with credibility deference, rejects the conclusion that absence of a single theme eliminates protection. The Court invalidates only this compelled expressive inclusion, without finding every customer identity a message or requiring proof that spectators endorse each contingent.

West Virginia State Board of Education v. Barnette supplies protection against compelled expression.

Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo supplies protection for editorial selection.

The Court applies the medium-specific editorial protection recognized in Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC without importing its unrelated cable results.

Roberts v. United States Jaycees and PruneYard Shopping Center v. Robins are distinguished because they concern access that does not materially alter a private expressive presentation.

Bose Corp. v. Consumers Union and Fiske v. Kansas supply independent review of characterizations intertwined with the First Amendment issue, while retaining deference to credibility determinations.

The respondents’ state-action/public-forum theory against the City was dismissed and not cross-appealed; it is not revived here. The importance of message misattribution in another setting remains open. No general antidiscrimination exemption, facial invalidation or judgment about the correct political message is announced.

Ordinary nondiscrimination applications remain distinct and enforceable.

Private parade composition receives protection against compelled insertion of an identified expressive unit. Other expressive-association disputes require a comparably material expressive burden; ordinary customer identity alone is not a message.

**Source notes:** Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court, 418 Mass. 238, 636 N.E.2d 1293; respondent’s predecision mootness suggestion and petitioner’s response, Records and Briefs collection, No. 94-749. The continuing injunction supplies the live controversy; no later Court action or fee amount is determined.

The Supreme Judicial Court judgment is reversed and the case remanded. The injunction compelling GLIB’s inclusion shall be dissolved. The public-accommodations law remains enforceable where its application does not compel private expression. No fee amount, fine or sanction is decided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and dissents in part. He joins the closer injunction-specific standard and the Court's component merits dispositions. He would require a replacement conduct-based post-refusal protection on remand, proposing eight feet subject to site-specific proof and a narrower supported implementation if that measure cannot be justified. This preferred mandatory remedy differs from the Court's permission to consider lawful relief. He agrees only in the disposition leaving the independent validity of the particular disputed terms and applications of paragraphs (7)–(9) undecided; that disposition-only agreement supplies no additional rationale.

Outside the fixed access zone, an enjoined person must remain free to make a peaceful initial approach, offer literature or request conversation. Protection begins only after words or clear conduct convey that further individualized contact is unwanted, a reasonable person would understand that refusal, and the enjoined speaker has notice. Silence, disagreement with the viewpoint and entry into the vicinity do not automatically trigger it. Once triggered, that speaker must stop following, shadowing, accompanying, repeated reapproaches, surrounding and close-range individualized speech, including coordinated substitution of another participant to continue the same pursuit. His proposed order would require at least eight feet of separation while the person proceeds toward or away from the facility within a finite, ascertainable, route-related clinic vicinity.

The refusal ends a particular unwanted encounter, not all exposure to a viewpoint. Lawful stationary signs, speech to the public, offers to willing listeners and communication from outside the separation remain protected. An unavoidable passing encounter, a patient voluntarily approaching a stationary protester and momentary inability to separate safely must not be punished. The speaker must stop targeted pursuit and take reasonable safe steps once able to comply; a listener cannot force a speaker from an otherwise lawful stationary location. Renewed voluntary engagement can reopen communication, but repeated unwanted approaches cannot be used to solicit that permission.

Carroll's precision requirement, Claiborne Hardware's protection of advocacy and association, Yamasaki's remedial limitation, Ward's content distinction and Frisby's focused protection support his proposed conduct-based response. The practical aim is to make the termination of a close encounter observable after conduct that defeated earlier protection. Yet a moving personal zone may burden leafleting, be difficult to measure on a narrow sidewalk, or turn aversion to speech into suppression. The stated safeguards answer those dangers without proving the number necessary. The state court would have to define the vicinity, support space and necessity with record-specific findings, and consider concrete less burdensome means. If eight feet cannot lawfully be supported, it must implement a supported narrower protection, rather than an unsupported numerical floor. Eight feet is a proposed operational measure, not a constitutional number or an already established rule.

Legally bound defendants and persons shown to act in concert with notice are the only subjects of that order. Touching, grabbing, intentional obstruction, threats, vehicle interference, surrounding and coordinated physical intimidation may independently be prohibited when the record and governing law support them, without waiting for refusal. These express conduct positions are not blanket approval of every disputed term in paragraphs (7)–(9). Casey protects choice against state interference and creates no direct private-liability right here; Bray preserves independently grounded state relief without weakening speech protection. Stone-Zsela concurs in part and dissents in part, without joins. Kennedy concurs in part and dissents in part, without joins. Scalia concurs in part and dissents in part, joined by Thomas. Their precise grounds and different remedies appear below. Every controlling portion has a direct majority; no combined rationale under Marks is required.

#### Separate Positions

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** Stone joins the complete Casey/Fargo standard and the required protection for grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. He rejects both Salerno’s independent barrier and an independent numerical facial threshold. He would affirm invalidity of the D&X prohibition and deficient penal medical exceptions now. In his application, the definition reaches the predominant D&E method, and courts cannot cure that reach by rewriting the definition. Intentional performance supplies no adequate culpability concerning an erroneous clinical-necessity determination; an affirmative defense does not cure the initial criminal and civil exposure. He therefore opposes the categorical-notice vacatur and the Court’s decision to leave method invalidity for further substantive application. His application does not command the Court.

Stone leaves severability and precise relief to the lower court without reopening his invalidity holdings. Ohio may regulate a genuinely distinct procedure, prohibit postviability abortion outside necessary life-and-health circumstances, punish bad-faith or sufficiently culpable violations, and enforce evidence-based safety rules. His health protection excludes transient distress and unsupported assertion and creates no unrestricted physician veto. His agreement that relief belongs below does not join the Court’s particular saving-construction analysis or its interim-only treatment of unresolved method applications.

**Justice Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** Kennedy joins the actual-wording notice holding, serious necessary mental-health protection, its lawful protective-construction inquiry and ordinary Ohio severability principles. His retained Casey protected-choice baseline and Fargo separate position distinguish the substantive adequacy of care from the intelligibility of a rule. A method-specific health defense cannot cure a separate prohibition that excludes necessary mental-health care.

He would reverse the previability method injunction under his reasonable-regulation approach. The act-defined restriction, actual alternative procedures and enacted health defense defeat this categorical challenge absent an established denial of protected choice or necessary care. He rejects the general residual lesser-burden substantiation rule and therefore does not join the six-step method remand or continued unresolved-method protection under that mandate. He neither requires failure in every application nor finds every alternative equally safe. Supported necessary-care applications remain protected; he does not approve the entire Act. His narrower regulatory approach supplies no substitute for the Court’s controlling framework.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Thomas.** Scalia and Thomas join the bounded notice/fault holding and ordinary Ohio severability principles. Their Fargo position supports attention to the purposeful act and specified professional criteria, without a universal scienter cure, a bad-faith-only safe harbor or invented criminal and civil elements.

They would reverse the constitutional injunction under their continuing objection to Casey’s heightened abortion framework and their ordinary reasonable-regulation approach. They dissent from the required constitutional mental-health protection as well as the method remand. They acknowledge that Casey’s actual necessary-care rule is the controlling premise from which they dissent; they do not redescribe it as merely Doe’s statutory construction or a numerical test. Their position neither supplies a different statutory fact nor establishes professional immunity. Agreement on notice and severability adds no vote to their proposed reversal or to the Court’s health and method-remand rationales.
