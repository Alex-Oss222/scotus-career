#### Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1809.

**Decided:** February 27, 1996.

##### Whether §1738 reaches an incorporated class-settlement release of exclusively federal claims

**Holding:** A state judicial judgment incorporating a class settlement falls within §1738 even when it releases claims subject to exclusive federal adjudicatory jurisdiction. Recognition begins with rendering-state law; class form, consent disposition and exclusive jurisdiction alone create no categorical exemption.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A representative's authority to bind absent members differs from an owner's personal settlement capacity. Silence after notice is not individual contractual consent; release wording supplies no missing constitutional representation. State courts acquire no power to adjudicate Exchange Act liability.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marrese requires state-law inquiry and preserves its distinct settlement-release reservation; its adjudicated-claim competency limit is not mechanically transferred to releases. Kokkonen's incorporation distinction does not make representative authority self-validating.

##### The ordinary Delaware effect of this judgment

**Holding:** The affirmed Delaware judgment's incorporated release receives ordinary Delaware preclusive effect and cannot lose that effect merely because a federal litigant alleges state-law error in settlement approval, subject to unresolved federal constitutional binding limits.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nottingham's identical-factual-predicate and representative limits and Prezant's Rule 23 and express adequacy requirements remain. No same-acquisition universal release rule, finding about counsel incentives or full-and-fair adequacy determination is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marrese supplies the inquiry, not an inflexible first-instance remand. Kremer preserves finality subject to due process and the actual scope of matters determined.

##### Whether Exchange Act §27 impliedly displaces §1738 for this category

**Holding:** Section 27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 for the state settlement judgments at issue. Exclusive federal adjudication of Exchange Act liability is compatible with a state court's approval of an authorized representative release within an action over which it has jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices. Ginsburg reserves this further determination.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Claim valuation is distinct from adjudicating federal liability. Recognition does not approve every state injunction or contempt remedy interfering with a pending federal action; an independently preserved coercive-operation objection retains its own lawful treatment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Marrese and Kremer require a supported basis to displace recognition; uniform federal adjudication does not establish statutory incompatibility here.

##### Constitutional representation and the reserved scope of collateral review

**Holding:** Section 1738 cannot bind an absent class member through constitutionally inadequate representation; notice, objection opportunities and opt-out rights do not independently cure that defect. Actual constitutional adequacy and binding effect remain undecided, so immediate dismissal is not directed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part IV-A, unanimous. Part IV-B's reservation of the complete permissible collateral-review scope, including the claimed full-and-fair adjudication of adequacy, has the eight Associate Justices' joins; Stone-Zsela's structural-failure standard remains separate and noncontrolling.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The state findings, discovery and adversarial hearing are not erased; neither a recital nor criticism conclusively resolves constitutional adequacy. The remand does not authorize unrestricted relitigation. No tender-offer liability, §§14(d)(6)–(7) private right, new class, damages or independent contract-consent theory is decided; valid opt-outs remain excluded.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse, 7–2, and remand for properly presented unresolved constitutional and binding objections and their antecedent collateral availability, consistently with ordinary Delaware effect and §27. Covered claims may be dismissed only after remaining legally available grounds are resolved; no immediate dismissal or wholesale settlement invalidation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hansberry and Shutts require adequate representation distinct from notice and exclusion. Peacock's new-debtor authority limit and Central Bank's confined affirmative-assistance securities remedy remain distinct and supply neither a new cause nor claim value here.

#### Baker v. General Motors Corp.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-653.

**Decided:** 1998-01-13.

##### Judgment recognition begins with lawful rendering-state effect and has no general local-policy exception

**Holding:** Section 1738 applies to state consent judgments in this federal forum. Consent, disagreement with a local evidentiary policy or a decree's mere modifiability does not permit withholding its lawful adjudicative effect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Consent, control, genuine privity, adequate representation or another constitutionally sufficient relationship may bind an absent person; mere shared interests or opportunity to intervene does not suffice.

##### This injunction does not itself require the separate forum to exclude Elwell's testimony

**Holding:** Giving the GM–Elwell decree its lawful adjudicative effect does not require the Missouri federal court to execute its broad personal testimony restriction as an evidentiary rule in the Bakers' distinct action. The decree adjudicated relations between GM and Elwell, not the Bakers' wrongful-death claims or their entitlement to invoke ordinary evidence process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices. Kennedy, O'Connor and Thomas agree in judgment on a distinct Michigan-law rationale that does not control this proposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general nonparty, public-policy or injunction exception to §1738 is adopted. The decree may retain lawful personal enforcement against Elwell; another forum acquires no authority to modify it. No universal witness-access right or rule governing every land, antisuit or evidence decree follows.

##### Neither the instruments nor orderly-review principles compel the Michigan modification proceeding asserted here

**Holding:** The Bakers' request for evidence in their own case is not, on the asserted basis, a request to modify Michigan's injunction. Celotex retains its requirements of actual jurisdictional relationship, sufficient authority, covered restraint and lawful avenues of relief and review; it supplies no missing basis to bind these outsiders or turn their evidence request into disobedience.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, unanimous in the bounded conclusion and preserved limits through the identified writings.

**Material application:** The injunction’s exception concerns the then-pending Georgia case. The separate settlement protects court-ordered testimony elsewhere against a breach action under the agreement or injunction. The instruments and exceptions remain distinct.

##### The remand preserves ordinary evidentiary rules and other appellate grounds

**Holding:** The Eighth Circuit must reconsider the case without treating §1738 as requiring this exclusion. Actual privilege, trade-secret, work-product, admissibility and legally supported preclusion questions retain their own predicates.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III mandate, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reported absence of a particular privilege objection establishes neither blanket admissibility nor waiver of every possible objection. Other appellate grounds remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded unanimously without reinstating the entire $11.3 million verdict, mandating a new trial or Michigan modification proceeding, dissolving the decree or granting Elwell personal-enforcement immunity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Matsushita is applied to lawful rendering-state effect, consent judgments and the distinction between recognition and additional coercive control; its collateral-review reservation remains open. Richards retains the constitutional prerequisites for binding absent persons without creating a universal nonparty exception. Celotex is distinguished by its actual jurisdictional connection, authority, covered restraint and qualified relief/review mechanism; its orderly-review rule remains intact, including the preserved United Mine Workers and Walker qualifications. No precedent is overruled.

#### Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-386.
**Decided:** June 10, 1996.

##### Whether the dispositive nonparty bar is final for limited federal review

**Holding:** Section 1257(a) permits review under Cox Broadcasting's first finality category because the preclusion ruling conclusively bars Richards and Hill's remaining federal claims and leaves its implementation preordained, despite the state interlocutory label. Review concerns only nonparty due process; separate Buck Act proceedings and the removed tax-merits question do not enlarge it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court. All nine participating Justices join Parts I–IV and every following holding and qualification; there is no separate writing.

**Material application:** State Counts 3 and 4 had already been rejected. The ruling disposes of these petitioners' remaining federal claims, while the remaining Buck Act claim belongs to a separate federal-employee group. The actual orders leave implementation preordained; state certification alone establishes neither federal finality nor a presumed later judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reversal permits litigation to continue, so the fourth-category arbitration route used in Southland and Casarotto does not supply this jurisdiction. Quackenbush's §1291 routes concern federal appellate review, not this state judgment under §1257. Neither tax constitutionality, Buck Act liability nor entitlement to a particular tax remedy is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn, 420 U.S. 469, supplies its first category for practically concluded claims and preordained implementation. Southland Corp. v. Keating, 465 U.S. 1, is distinguished as a fourth-category arbitration case. Doctor's Associates, Inc. v. Casarotto, May 20, 1996, preserves attention to actual proceedings rather than certification alone, but its arbitration finality and substantive FAA holdings do not decide this case. Quackenbush v. Allstate Insurance Co., June 3, 1996, retains its distinct §1291 and abstention holdings and the separate tax-comity setting; it supplies no reopening of the earlier federal tax dismissal.

##### Whether shared interests alone authorize binding absent claimants

**Holding:** A State's ordinary preclusion rules remain subject to due process: essentially identical interests, vigorous advocacy, resources and public importance alone do not authorize adjudication of absent persons' own legal rights. Binding may rest on constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or representative litigation, or another recognized relationship or special remedial arrangement consistent with due process; finality policy and a virtual-representation label cannot replace that prerequisite.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** Guardian–ward and trustee–beneficiary relationships illustrate genuine privity; bankruptcy and probate illustrate special arrangements that may lawfully foreclose successive litigation. Each person need not appear individually, but common objectives cannot alone confer representative authority. Section 1738 does not separately command Alabama to bind these strangers to its own earlier judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes without deciding that adequate representation might cure absent notice in some class or representative proceedings. Even on that assumption, this representative basis fails. The assumption neither makes representation an automatic cure nor establishes a universal individual-notice requirement. Conversely, notice, objections or opt-out opportunities do not independently cure inadequate representation, and mere notice of another person's suit supplies neither representative authority nor a general intervention duty.

Ordinary state preclusion and valid finality remain. Correction of this extreme application supplies no general retrial of ordinary state-law errors and decides none of Matsushita's full collateral-review reservation. Absence of a Rule 23 class is relevant here but does not itself invalidate every source-authorized representative judgment; Bedingfield remains effective among persons properly bound.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hansberry v. Lee, 311 U.S. 32, supplies the constitutional requirement of genuine adequate representation while preserving lawful representative adjudication. Martin v. Wilks, 490 U.S. 755, supplies nonparty hearing protection with adequate-representation, control and due-process-consistent special-remedial exceptions; notice alone creates no general intervention duty. Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, February 27, 1996, distinguishes ordinary recognition from constitutional authority to bind, including representation independent of notice and opt-out protections; its complete collateral-review reservation remains unresolved.

##### Whether the stated public-interest rationale binds these direct personal tax claims

**Holding:** The representative rationale used below does not constitutionally bind Richards and Hill to Bedingfield's disposition of their own tax liability and claims for paid sums. Shared taxpayer objectives and fiscal reliance do not establish the required relationship; genuine representative public-right litigation and ordinary reliance on precedent remain distinct and preserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** Richards and Hill were nonparties to the nonclass Bedingfield action. The earlier litigants' common constitutional objective, resources and vigorous advocacy, together with revenue and bond-financing reliance, do not establish authority over these absent taxpayers' personal claims. No deficient advocacy finding is necessary. Petitioners' no-notice assertion is not an appellate finding; no participation or control finding supplies the rationale used below, and every possible connection need not be resolved or found absent to reject it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A direct levy on a taxpayer's own funds differs from a challenge to misuse of public funds or government action affecting the taxpayer only indirectly. The Court assumes wide state latitude over standing and repeated proceedings in the latter setting. Genuine public-right representative judgments remain possible; neither refund relief nor lack of formal class treatment independently invalidates every judgment, and federal class-action requirements are not wholesale constitutional commands to the States.

The finance director's participation does not establish that public officials always adequately represent constituents' personal rights; that broader question remains open. Supposed representation of city taxpayers cannot without more establish representation of these county taxpayers. The stated rationale establishes no exclusive public-action remedial scheme, and common interests and revenue reliance cannot create one by assertion. No cause of action or tax-merits holding follows. A later court may apply precedent without conclusively binding a claimant to another person's judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Brinkerhoff-Faris Trust & Savings Co. v. Hill, 281 U.S. 673, supports an opportunity to contest a direct levy without deciding this tax's validity. Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co., 455 U.S. 422, supplies the protected status of an otherwise available individual claim, or chose in action, reinforcing the same hearing right; it creates no new cause of action or universal state-remedy scheme. Bedingfield retains its proper force among persons lawfully bound and as precedent; only its constitutionally unsupported preclusive use against these petitioners is rejected.

##### Whether narrower issue preclusion sustains the judgment and what reversal requires

**Holding:** Respondents' narrower issue-preclusion theory cannot sustain this judgment because it also requires a constitutionally sufficient basis to bind these nonparties. The dispositive bar on the reviewed Richards–Hill federal claims is reversed and the case remanded, without deciding whether the exemption issue was actually and necessarily determined or awarding relief on the tax.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Stone-Zsela's unanimous Opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** Claim and issue preclusion have distinct requirements, but both require lawful authority to bind the affected person. Narrowing the proposed bar to an exemption issue supplies no missing representative relationship. Earlier pleadings of federal theories, an opportunity to litigate them, and their actual necessary determination are distinct predicates. The binding defect makes resolution of that disputed determination unnecessary; the Court does not find that no relevant issue was ever decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Uncertainty about every notice, authorization or control fact requires no exploratory remand or adverse factual finding. A genuinely available defense or constitutionally valid binding relationship remains subject to its own proof, preservation and procedural requirements. No new theory or finding that one exists is guaranteed. No state-count ruling, earlier Tax Injunction Act dismissal, properly binding judgment or separate Buck Act claim is reopened.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0, as to the dispositive nonparty bar on Richards and Hill's reviewed federal claims. Ordinary legally available defenses, valid preclusion and remedy conditions remain; there is no mandatory discovery, representative-adequacy hearing or opportunity to construct a new preclusion theory. No immediate trial, tax invalidation, refund, damages, injunction or eventual recovery is ordered or guaranteed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** No Supreme Court precedent is overruled; the constitutional prerequisite to binding these nonparties defeats both proposed forms of preclusion without extending collateral review or reopening otherwise binding judgments.

## Event

**Strout v. Albanese**; review of 178 F.3d 57 (1st Cir. 1999). Certiorari granted November 1, 1999; argued January 24, 2000; decided February 28, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the First Circuit, affirming the District of Maine's judgment at 13 F. Supp. 2d 112.

The granted questions concern whether excluding otherwise eligible sectarian schools and their students from a generally available tuition-assistance program solely because the schools are religious violates free exercise, and whether establishment principles require, permit or forbid that exclusion when assistance reaches schools through independent parental choice. Three families in Minot, where no secondary school was maintained, sought ordinary St. Dominic's tuition. Their application concerns integrated sectarian instruction, not a separately identified secular service or expression forum. The case was resolved on cross-motions for summary judgment without a genuine dispute of material fact.

Section 2951 retains basic school approval, nonsectarian status in accordance with the First Amendment, incorporation under Maine or United States law, reporting and auditing under sections 2952 and 2953 and requirements adopted under section 2954. Schools with at least 60 percent publicly funded students, measured by the previous year's October and April average enrollment, must participate in statewide assessment. Upon a school unit's request, the private school must release copies of all student records for students transferring from that private school to the requesting unit. The repealed fourth condition is not restored. Section 5204(4) applies when the resident unit neither maintains a secondary school nor contracts for secondary-school privileges. It permits attendance at an approved private school, a public school in an adjoining unit accepting tuition students, or an approved school in another state or country, subject to receiving-school permission. The resident unit must pay tuition up to the legal rate. The Department's acceptance of schools' self-identification without a classification dispute is a lower-court finding about administration, not a statutory command.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Holdings

### Maine may refuse the integrated devotional tuition presented here

**Holding and operative rule:** The Religion Clauses permit Maine to refuse public payment for the integrated devotional instructional function presented by this ordinary-tuition application. Independent parental choice and neutral student eligibility do not themselves justify public purchase of demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Jackson requires attention to the instructional function purchased; Agostini applies that inquiry within the governing, unified Lemon test. The parents selected St. Dominic's, and the requested ordinary tuition of approximately $3,850 was below the $4,900 statutory ceiling. That distinguishes the amount formula from Jackson's provider operating-and-debt costs, but does not separate the devotional work in the integrated program for which payment is sought. Maine is not buying an identified interpreter, secular remedial service, or controlled printing benefit. Rosenberger preserves its independently directed publication program; it does not make every publicly financed activity a speech forum. The actual funded function therefore supports refusal of this tuition without prohibiting the families' private educational choice. Neither a school's religious name nor its affiliation alone establishes the character of its work. The Court does not approve a universal religious-provider exclusion, decide all voucher arrangements, or create a constitutional entitlement to every education subsidy.

### Free exercise receives its own inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Refusing this public purchase of integrated devotional instruction does not, in this application, violate the Free Exercise Clause. The conclusion does not depend on whether attending the school is a central religious tenet and does not authorize religious hostility, exclusion from a neutral private-expression forum, or categorical rejection of genuinely severable secular work solely because its provider is religious.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Lukumi requires scrutiny of religious targeting; Shahar preserves an independent free-exercise inquiry and rejects theological compulsion as a prerequisite to sincere exercise. Calling a program secular cannot by itself answer that inquiry. Here the challenged benefit is public purchase of the ordinary integrated instructional package, and the State may refuse that financed devotional function while the parents remain free to choose it privately. The lower court's observation that no parent described attendance as a central tenet does not defeat the claim, and the Court does not adopt that reasoning. Agostini protects its separately administered secular service despite religious location, and the private forum decisions protect genuinely private religious expression on their actual terms. Those distinct relationships remain intact; this tuition request presents neither one. The Court therefore affirms on the actual financing ground rather than an affiliation-based presumption or an independent-prong Lemon test.

### The affirmance is limited to the granted claims and this application

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment sustaining the challenged refusal of integrated tuition is affirmed within the granted Establishment and Free Exercise questions. The decision does not reopen the ungranted speech, equal-protection or parental-liberty claims, the unchallenged dismissals of local defendants, or distinct applications for reliably separated secular services.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct joins to this holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The cross-motions were decided on an agreed absence of genuine material factual dispute. No remand is required to adjudicate a different benefit application not before the Court or to restructure the school. The statutory payment duty for eligible schools remains subject to its independent approval, receiving-school permission and tuition-rate conditions. Affirmance leaves the challenged denial in place; it awards no tuition, damages or recoupment, orders no transfer or school closure, and imposes no statewide funding cutoff.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Jackson v. Benson:** Applied to the actual integrated instructional function; its provider-cost formula is distinguished in amount, without making capped tuition sufficient to separate devotional work. Its limits preserving private benefits and secular services remain.
- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing unified Lemon inquiry and to the distinction between integrated devotional work and separately controlled secular instruction; no categorical inference follows from religious location.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues only through Agostini's unified inquiry; the former independent purpose, effect and entanglement prongs are not revived.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its controlled printing benefit for independently directed expression remains valid; that relationship does not determine the ordinary tuition purchase here.
- **Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah:** Its neutrality and religious-targeting inquiry is preserved; refusal of the demonstrated devotional purchase is not a license for hostile religious discrimination.
- **Shahar v. Bowers:** Applied to the independent free-exercise inquiry and the irrelevance of theological compulsion to sincere exercise.
- **Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind:** Its actual individual vocational-benefit relationship remains distinct; it supplies no automatic answer for integrated tuition.
- **Mueller v. Allen:** Its general tax-deduction relationship remains distinct and is not displaced by this application.
- **Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District:** Its antecedent-law remand supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding or general tuition entitlement.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its bounded private-expression and worship-access protection remains intact; forum access differs from purchasing integrated instruction.

## Law After Decision

Effective February 28, 2000, Jackson's funded-function rule applies to the integrated ordinary-tuition application presented here despite the tuition cap and parental choice. Agostini's unified Lemon inquiry continues; its former independent prongs do not return. The separate Free Exercise inquiry rejects theological centrality as a prerequisite, but does not require public purchase of this devotional instructional function. Religious identity alone remains insufficient to establish the financing defect. Genuinely independent private benefits, neutral private-expression forums and reliably separated secular services retain their actual protections. The decision settles this application, not every permitted or required tuition arrangement.

## Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring.** He joins the Court's application and emphasizes the distinction between paying for devotional instruction and allowing genuinely private religious expression. Parental designation does not turn a public expenditure into private money. At the same time, an otherwise eligible, genuinely severable secular service cannot be denied payment solely because its provider is religious, and religious expression in a genuinely neutral forum involving no governmental financing, control, selection or attribution must receive equal treatment. Agostini supplies the secular-service distinction; the private-forum decisions protect private expression. These qualifications preserve lawful separate relationships, not a general right to tuition. No such distinct service or forum application is before the Court.

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas.** They would reverse the exclusion as applied and remand for otherwise lawful eligibility and payment proceedings. The actual benefit is ordinary tuition, capped by statute and here below that cap, selected through independent parental choice at a private school. They distinguish that individual educational benefit from Jackson's provider operating-and-debt-cost formula, while maintaining their disagreement with Jackson's attribution analysis. Rosenberger protects genuinely independent private activity within an available benefit; Witters supports the distinction between a private beneficiary's choice and the State's devotional direction; Lukumi requires scrutiny of excluding religious choices from that benefit. Maine's public-substitute characterization does not, for these Justices, overcome the actual independent direction and capped tuition. They would not excuse sham parental choice, governmental devotional direction, or other independent statutory ineligibility, and would order no immediate subsidy or entitlement to every tuition program.

## Procedure After Action

Supreme Court merits review of the granted Religion Clause questions is complete. The First Circuit's judgment remains in force at the stated scope. No new lower-court factfinding, statutory redesign or subsidy proceeding is directed by the affirmance.
