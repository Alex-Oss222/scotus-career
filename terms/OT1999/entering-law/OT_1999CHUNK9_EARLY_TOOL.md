## Standards and Tests selection: Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

### Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

**Governing question:** Whether a ban on write-in voting violates electoral-choice or independent expressive rights.

**Current rule:** Under the existing Anderson inquiry, the court assesses the character and magnitude of the actual injury against the State's precise interests and the extent to which those interests require the burden. Severe burdens require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may be justified by important regulatory interests. When the complete access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate interests, including primary finality and prevention of evasive candidacies or party raiding. The ballot need not provide an official count and publication of votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent expressive entitlement.

**Authority by component:**
- Governing inherited framework: Burdick v. Takushi, “1. Review of election regulations follows the actual burden”, June 8, 1992; White, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Thomas (five), applying Anderson and the earlier simulated Norman rule.
- Limited-burden write-in presumption: Burdick, “2. Hawaii's complete write-in prohibition is valid in its reasonably accessible system,” June 8, 1992; same five Justices.
- Arbitrary-message claim: Burdick, “3. The ballot need not serve as an official tally of arbitrary political messages,” June 8, 1992; same controlling five-Justice opinion, with all nine agreeing in rejection of that component.

**Present operation effective:** Burdick v. Takushi, June 8, 1992, for the write-in presumption and expressive boundary; the underlying Anderson framework is applied unchanged.

**Exceptions and limits:** No absolute validation of all write-in bans follows. Materially more restrictive access systems may impose severe burdens. The rule does not replace strict scrutiny of content-based campaign restrictions on public sidewalks.

**Holdings navigation:** Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428 (1992) — Ballot access and political parties.

## Standards and Tests selection: Actual federal contractual undertakings and allocated regulatory risk

### Actual federal contractual undertakings and allocated regulatory risk

**Governing question:** Formation, authority, scope and regulatory-risk allocation in a Tucker Act contract claim.

**Current rule:** Section 1491(a)(1) supplies jurisdiction, not a substantive promise: an actual authorized express or implied-in-fact undertaking is necessary, and fairness cannot create an obligation implied only in law. The Government’s ordinary specifications warranty assures performance of the prescribed procurement, but does not itself promise payment of a manufacturer’s third-party tort settlements or defense expenses. Compelled procurement and Defense Production Act §707 protection do not themselves create an indemnity or reimbursement promise. A demonstrated authorized financial undertaking may allocate the risk that valid later legislation prevents the promised treatment. Even assuming public and general sovereign action, ordinary impossibility does not discharge that assumed change-of-law risk. The actual bargain and its qualifications govern; general regulatory permission alone creates no immutable promise.

**Authority by component:**

- Hercules — actual authorized undertaking, specifications-warranty scope and §707 limits, 1996-03-04; Souter, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg (six).
- Winstar — holdings 1 and 3–5, actual authority, allocated risk and ordinary contract loss, 1996-07-01; all nine support the actual-undertaking requirement; Souter, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer (eight) support the common financial-risk and assumed-risk holdings.

**Present operation effective:** Winstar, July 1, 1996, for allocated regulatory risk; Hercules’s scope and authorization limits remain.

**Exceptions and limits:** Congress retains its legislative power and the promisee must obey valid law. Contractual responsibility creates no injunction, automatic causation, goodwill-balance award, guaranteed recovery duration or takings entitlement. Causation, recoverable loss, mitigation, actual periods, conditions and termination provisions remain independent. A genuine authorized undertaking may cover third-party losses without an express indemnity; neither uncertain damages nor tort immunity destroys an actual contract.

**Open question:** Winstar supplies no majority comprehensive unmistakability framework or substantial-contractual-effect exception to sovereign acts. Its four-Justice threshold account and three-Justice substantial-effect alternative are noncontrolling; the eight-Justice assumed-risk holding does not depend on either.

**Holdings navigation:** Hercules, Inc. v. United States, 516 U.S. 417; United States v. Winstar Corp., 518 U.S. 839 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Assigned school productions and teachers’ personal speech

### Assigned school productions and teachers’ personal speech

**Governing question:** Whether a teacher has a personal First Amendment right over an assigned school instructional production.

**Current rule:** Selection, production and direction of material as part of an assigned public-school instructional program are institutional curricular expression subject to the school system’s lawful control. Delegated artistic discretion, public controversy and prior approval do not themselves give the teacher a personal constitutional right against curricular and related employment control or make approval an irrevocable constitutional delegation. A protected personal interest must be established before justification and same-decision rules apply.

**Authority by component:**

- Confined instructional-expression classification and antecedent personal-interest requirement: Canonical Decision Record `boring_v_buncombe_county_board_of_education_merits_1999-02-17.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Assigned instructional production and the asserted personal speech right, 1999-02-17; five direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-02-17.

**Exceptions and limits:** Separate citizen speech, independently forbidden classifications or exclusions, and statutory, contractual, tenure and state-law limits remain. This is not a rule for every duty-related statement or university academic freedom, and a curricular label does not establish an actually lawful motive.

**Holdings navigation:** [Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education — Assigned instructional production and the asserted personal speech right](HOLDINGS.md#assigned-instructional-production-and-the-asserted-personal-speech-right-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Ordinary high-water emergence for natural island status

### Ordinary high-water emergence for natural island status

**Governing question:** Whether a naturally formed coastal feature qualifies as an island for a separate submerged-land belt.

**Current rule:** Convention Article 10(1) requires a naturally formed feature ordinarily to remain above the agreed mean-high-water datum. Frequent ordinary seasonal submergence defeats island status; intermittent emergence does not create alternating island entitlement. Abnormal temporary inundation of an otherwise enduring island is different. Mean high water, averaged over approximately nineteen years, is the datum, not absolute permanence or an adopted percentage-of-days test.

**Authority by component:**
- Island definition and seasonal boundary: United States v. Alaska, United States v. Alaska — II. Dinkum Sands does not satisfy the island definition, O'Connor Parts I–II, all nine, 1997-06-19.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Alaska, 1997-06-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** Natural accretion or erosion of an established island and independently qualifying low-tide elevations retain their distinct questions. This rule does not enlarge the statutory three-mile State grant to the international twelve-mile territorial sea.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Alaska (1997-06-19) — Property and Economic Rights / Submerged lands and fixed coastal baselines.

## Standards and Tests selection: Coordinate fixation of state-federal submerged-land boundaries

### Coordinate fixation of state-federal submerged-land boundaries

**Governing question:** Whether a state-federal submerged-land boundary fixed by coordinates in a final Supreme Court decree moves with the coast.

**Current rule:** Under 43 U.S.C. §1301(b), a state-federal boundary fixed by coordinates in a final decree of this Court remains immobilized at the decreed coordinates and does not move with later coastline change. The decree’s complete coordinate schedule, including its datum, zones, precision, sequence, segments and arcs, controls; an illustrative map does not replace it.

**Authority by component:**

- Coordinate-fixation consequence and schedule priority: Canonical Decision Record `united_states_v_louisiana_supplemental_decree_1998-10-13.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The complete coordinates fix and immobilize the boundary, 1998-10-13.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-10-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule does not allocate an unpresented State’s territory, decide reserved property interests or change the entitlement used to select the decreed line.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Louisiana (Texas Boundary Case) — The complete coordinates fix and immobilize the boundary](HOLDINGS.md#the-complete-coordinates-fix-and-immobilize-the-boundary).
