#### Lockhart v. Fretwell, 506 U.S. 364 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1393.

**Decided:** January 25, 1993.

##### Loss of a displaced substantive rule is not cognizable prejudice here

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to obtain a favorable application of a substantive rule subsequently rejected does not establish Strickland prejudice where the lost benefit is one the governing law does not recognize and the omission otherwise deprived the defendant of no fair or reliable sentencing protection. This limited rule does not replace ordinary reasonable-probability analysis, excuse deficient performance, or require an additional showing of unfairness in the usual ineffective-assistance case.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the rule and its application to the Collins objection.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984): applied and clarified for loss of a subsequently rejected substantive rule; ordinary reasonable-probability analysis remains the usual test.
- Nix v. Whiteside, 475 U.S. 157 (1986): applied by analogy for the distinction between a changed outcome and loss of a legally protected benefit; no finding of perjury by Fretwell is made.
- Kimmelman v. Morrison, 477 U.S. 365 (1986): distinguished; a procedural bar on direct habeas consideration does not eliminate a meritorious underlying claim, unlike the substantive premise here.
- Lowenfield v. Phelps, 484 U.S. 231 (1988): applied for constitutionally sufficient narrowing at the guilt stage despite duplication at sentencing.
- Collins v. Lockhart, 754 F.2d 258 (8th Cir. 1985), and Perry v. Lockhart, 871 F.2d 1384 (8th Cir. 1989): Collins's displaced double-counting rule supplies no cognizable benefit; Perry's rejection of that rule supplies the accepted litigation premise. Whether Collins could independently be revived is not decided.

##### Teague does not preserve the displaced rule for this prejudice inquiry

**Holding:** Teague's limitation on new rules supporting collateral relief does not require a federal habeas court to disregard a subsequent legal correction favorable to the State when deciding whether an omitted objection caused cognizable Strickland prejudice. Performance remains assessed from counsel's perspective at the time, while the prejudice inquiry here does not preserve an entitlement to the rejected Collins rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this answer to the temporal objection.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Teague v. Lane, 489 U.S. 288 (1989): distinguished from an affirmative entitlement to an obsolete rule; its collateral finality limitation is not made reciprocal.
- Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668 (1984): preserves the contemporaneous perspective for evaluating performance without mechanically fixing the prejudice inquiry to every favorable rule then available.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice remains the usual rule; no separate unfairness requirement is added in ordinary cases. Deficient performance is conceded. The Court does not revive Collins, decide the Solicitor General’s separate §2254(a) theory or determine the unresolved penalty-preparation claims. Thomas’s state/federal hierarchy observation is not a majority holding.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Eighth Circuit grant of relief on the Collins-only ground. Unresolved independent claims retain their own treatment; no new sentence, stay or execution is ordered.

#### Lindh v. Murphy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6298.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed and must consider the appeal under pre-AEDPA habeas law; no writ is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the statutory-direction inquiry and Rivers its provision-specific operation. Felker is distinguished because its authorization-review proceedings began after enactment and its other Title I timing questions were reserved; its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain. Hughes Aircraft requires attention to the actual statutory change and does not displace the chapter comparison.

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense.

**Authority by component:**

- Merits-relief restriction, factual presumption/rebuttal and cumulative hearing conditions: Source, authority and operative text, Public Law 104-132, Title I, §104(2)–(4), 28 U.S.C. §2254(d)–(e), enacted 1996-04-24; the Record’s retained complete enacted text supplies these components.

**Present operation effective:** AEDPA enactment, April 24, 1996; Lindh, June 23, 1997, for the chapter 153 amendments at issue in preenactment noncapital §2254 applications. The separate §2255 and capital questions are not thereby decided.

**Effective transition:** Enacted April 24, 1996. “Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments” states Lindh's noncapital chapter 153 application-date construction, effective 1997-06-23. Separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case clause and Felker's successive-petition treatment remain distinct; Lindh does not settle every other Title I provision's application to pending proceedings. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Unresolved interaction:** No controlling construction of “contrary to,” “unreasonable application” or “unreasonable determination,” or general interaction of these restrictions, is established. Thompson’s former-statute custody classification and Keeney’s received hearing rule do not erase a genuinely applicable amendment. Gray requires resolving actual applicable, previously unadjudicated conditions before entering an operative writ without reopening settled issues or inventing barriers.

**Holdings navigation:** Lindh v. Murphy (1997-06-23) — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application.

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

**Authority by component:**
- Application-date construction and preserved exceptions: Lindh, Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

- Pending-capital extension for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions: Carriger v. Stewart — Holding 1. Former-law treatment of the pending capital application, 1998-04-16.

**Present operation effective:** Carriger v. Stewart, 1998-04-16, for the specified capital-application extension; Lindh’s noncapital rule remains effective from 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Carriger does not decide §2254(e)(2)’s temporal application or require an evidentiary hearing.  The rule creates no constitutional entitlement to preserve every filing-date judicial doctrine and decides no capital State qualification, exhaustion, merits or ultimate entitlement to relief.

Thompson — Recall of completed habeas merits mandates.

### Loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice

**Governing question:** What instructions govern loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice?

**Current rule:** An omitted objection does not establish Strickland prejudice when its lost benefit is a substantive rule subsequently rejected, governing law recognizes no entitlement to that benefit, and the omission otherwise deprived the defendant of no fair or reliable sentencing protection. Ordinary reasonable-probability analysis remains; the usual claim acquires no additional unfairness requirement. Performance is assessed from counsel's perspective at the time. Teague does not require disregarding a subsequent legal correction favorable to the State for this limited prejudice inquiry. No actual-innocence prerequisite or general harmless-error rule follows.

O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the rule and its application to the Collins objection.

O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this answer to the temporal objection.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-01-25; Lockhart v. Fretwell, for the components identified above.
