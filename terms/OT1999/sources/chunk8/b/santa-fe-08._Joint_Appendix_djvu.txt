PLAINTIFFS’. JANE DOES’, ORIGINAL COMPLAINT 
(Filed Apr. 4, 1995] 


This lawsuit is brought by two parents with children 
attending schools in the Santa Fe Independent School 
District and a student attending the Santa Fe High School 
against the Defendants, Santa Fe Independent School 
District, its Superintendent of Schools, and the Board of 
Trustees of the School District who presently sit and any 
thereafter appointed, elected or assigned during the pendency 
of these proceedings. This lawsuit is brought to address 
certain constitutional violations of the First Amendment of 
the United States Constitution, to request certain injunctive 
and declaratory relief and for any equitable relief that the 
Plaintiffs may be entitled to under law. The Plaintiffs would 
show unto the Court as follows: 


Parties 


1) The parties have brought this suit as pseudonyms in 
light of the emotional nature of the debate that surrounds any 
attempt to remind administrators, school trustees and/or 
persons in authority of the value of the separation of church 
and state and the need to prevent the government from 
endorsing one religion over another. 

2) Each Plaintiff is a parent of students in the Sante Fe 
School District. Both parents have two children in the 
District. Plaintiff JOHN DOE is of the age of eighteen (18) 
years of age and is a student in the District. 

3) The SANTA FE INDEPENDENT SCHOOL 
DISTRICT is a political subdivision of the State of Texas,

practices that are violative of the First Amendment of the

Gideon Bibles. This practice has been undertaken at least

This case addresses important First Amendment issues 
regarding the extent to which prayer and religious influence are 
Constitutionally permissible in public schools. The purpose of 
this Interim Order is not a comprehensive resolution of those 
fundamental issues. It is simply to provide some certainty of 
immediate action for the benefit of the parties, particularly as 
regards Commencement and related activities, which are now 
shortly scheduled in the Defendant School District. The 
Plaintiffs have requested a T.R.O. and F.R.C.P. Rule 65 
injunctive relief. But, after carefully considering the pleadings 
and relevant case authority, and after having conducted two 
extensive hearings and having conferred extensively with 
counsel in chambers, the Court feels the better course, in the 
short term, is to fashion a basis for ultimate relief which will 
maximize Constitutionally permissible religious expression to 
the vast majority of Santa Fe citizens, while at the same time 
halting manifestly impermissible practices which legitimately 
aggrieve the few. 

While the parties retain full right of timely appeal of this 
and any further Orders of the Court, the courtesy, cooperation 
and professionalism of the parties and their fine counsel in 
providing the Court advice and input in the crafting of this 
Order is genuinely appreciated. If principle is the cornerstone 
of Constitutional freedom, and cooperation provides the 
bedrock of democracy, this product of the collective wisdom 
and beliefs of those involved in this case represents a fine 
example of what this great, but contentious nation is still 
capable of creating.

The First Amendment of the United States Constitution 
provides: “Congress shall make no laws respecting an 
establishment of religion. .. .” The Amendment requires “the 
State to be a neutral [party] in its relations with groups of 
religious believers and non-believers; it does not require the 
State to be their adversary. State power is no more to be used 
so as to handicap religions than it is to favor them.” Everson 


vy. Board of Education, 330 U.S. 1 (1946). 


In Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602, 614, 91 S.Ct. 2105, 
29 L.Ed.2d 745 (1971), the United States Supreme Court has 


recognized that “total separation of the [Church and State} is 
not possible in an absolute sense. Some reiationship between 
government and religious organizations is inevitable.” 

In Lemon, the Supreme court established a-three-prong test 
for determining whether governmental activity constituted an 
establishment of religion in violation of the First Amendment. 
Under the Lemon test, the governmental practice must: (1) 
reflect a clearly secular purpose; (2) have a primary effect that 
neither advances nor inhibits religion; and (3) avoid excessive 
government entanglement with religion. In the present case, 
the initial issue before the Court is whether an invocation and 
benediction at a commencement exercise violates the First 
Amendment. In considering the Constitutionality of permitting 
en invocation and/or benediction at the commencement 
exercises, the Court must apply the three-prong test set forth in 
Lemon. 

First, the Court must determine whether having an 
invocation and/or benediction at the graduation ceremony will 
further a secular purpose. While the Court is ordinarily 
deferential to a State’s articulation of a secular purpose, it is 
required that the statement of such purpose be sincere and not 
a sham. Edwards v. Aguillard, 482 U.S. 578, 586-87 (1987). 


30 


In the present case, the Court notes that having an 
invocation and/or benediction at a graduation ceremony has 
been held to have the secular purpose of solemnizing the 
ceremony. Jones v. Clear Creek Independent School District 
977 F.2d 963 (Sth Cir. 1992); County of Allegheny v. 


American Civil Liberties Union, 492 U.S. 573, 109S.Ct. 3086, 
106 L.Ed.2d 472 (1989). 


Second, it has been held that the primary effect of a prayer 
at a graduation ceremony is to solemnize the ceremony and not 


to advance religion. A requirement that any invocation be 


of salision, inoee 977 F.2d 963. at 967. (Emphasis added.) 
Thus, under explicit Fifth Circuit mandate, which absolutely 
binds this Court, any invocation or benediction permitted must 
be nonsectarian and non-proselytizing. 

Finally, this Court must ascertain whether Defendant Santa 
Fe Independent School District’s permitting an invocation 
and/or benediction would in any way result in an excessive 
entanglement with religion. The United States Supreme Court 
in Lee V. Weisman, 112 S.Ct. 2649, 120 L.Ed.2d 467 (1992), 
found that unconstitutional coercion occurs when: (1) the 
government directs (2) a formal religious exercise (3) in such 
a way as to oblige the participation of objectors. Specifically, 
school officials decided that there would be a prayer at the 
graduation ceremony, selected the clergyman to give the prayer, 
and dictated the content of the prayer by presenting to the 
clergyman a pamphiet setting forth guidelines for 
“nonsectarian” prayer at school graduations. However, in 
Jones v. Clear Creek, 977 F.2d 963 (Sth Cir. 1992), the Fifth 
Circuit has found that where the decision to inciude a prayer at 
the graduation ceremony was made by the students, a student 
volunteer was selected by the students to deliver the prayer, and 


31 


the content of the prayer was not dictated by the school board 
or administration, such prayer did not result in an excessive 
entanglement with religion. 

Having an invocation and/or benediction at the 
commencement exercise serves the secular purpose of 
solemnizing the occasion. A nonsectarian and non- 
proselytizing invocation and/or benediction at the graduation 
ceremony neither advances nor inhibits religion. Permitting a 
member of the graduating senior class who has been elected by 
secret ballot to deliver a non-denominational prayer will not 
result in an excessive entanglement with religion.

interim nature only, and intended to resolve only immediately 
pending problems, it is hereby ORDERED, ADJUDGED and

ARTICLE I 
Name 


This organization shall be known as the Student 
Council of Santa Fe High School. 


ARTICLE Il 
Purpose 


The purpose of this organization shall be: 

1. To bring about closer cooperation between the 
faculty and the student body as 
representatives of the student body. 

2. To cooperate with administration in all 
problems concerning the school and the student 
body. 

3. To create and maintain school spirit, morale, 
and respect. 

4. To help make this a better school for the 

community, the faculty, the student, and 
ourselves.

If the District is enjoined by a court order from the 
enforcement of this policy, then and only then will the 
following policy automatically become the applicable

The board has chosen to permit students to deliver a brief 
invocation and/or message to be delivered during the pre- 
game ceremonies of home varsity football games to 
solemnize the event, to promote good sportsmanship and 
student safety, and to establish the appropriate 
environment for the competition.

student volunteer who is selected by his or her classmates 
may decide what statement or invocation to deliver,
