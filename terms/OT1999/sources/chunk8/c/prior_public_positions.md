## Almendarez-Torres

Source: `terms/OT1997/output/OT_1997CHUNK6.md`

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Scalia, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, Part II.** These Justices regard the tenfold increase in maximum punishment as presenting serious doubt about Congress's ability to assign the aggravating fact solely to a sentencing judge. The element reading is fairly available, so they apply constitutional avoidance and decline to decide that ultimate question. McMillan's unchanged ceiling and Watts's ordinary sentencing setting do not eliminate the doubt. The risk of prejudice from prior-conviction evidence can be addressed through adequate admissions or other proper proof rather than removal of an element from the charge.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in Part I and in the judgment.** The constitutional allocation turns on the fact's legal effect. A fact necessary to increase the authorized maximum from two years to twenty years performs the work of an element and must be charged and either validly admitted as an element of that offense or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Legislative placement cannot transfer that work entirely to the sentencing judge. Gaudin protects element determination; Watts permits ordinary judicial sentencing findings only within conviction-based authority. Custis concerns collateral attack, and Old Chief demonstrates how adequate status proof can avoid unnecessary prejudice. The admitted facts here do not cure an indictment that never charged the aggravated offense. This position permits consideration of prior convictions within a separately authorized range, preserves Custis's collateral-review limits, requires no prejudicial details when adequate admission or stipulation establishes the element, and decides no sentencing fact that leaves the statutory maximum unchanged.

**O'Connor, joined by Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, dissenting.** Subsection (b)(2)'s recidivist subject, reciprocal cross-references and punishment structure make it a penalty provision for the alien already described in subsection (a). Existing conviction status differs from facts about commission of the present crime, and Congress may treat that prior adjudication as a sentencing factor in this setting. The admission supports the application but is not a substitute for their statutory classification. These Justices acknowledge that McMillan and Watts do not alone resolve an increased ceiling; the distinct recidivism ground does that work. They would affirm without adopting unlimited legislative power to remove offense elements or resolving an unpresented subsidiary proof-burden question.


## Jones v. United States

Source: `terms/OT1998/output/OT_1998CHUNK6.md`

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Souter, joined by Stevens, Scalia and Ginsburg.** These four join the Court's sufficient statutory ground and also conclude that a fact about the commission or consequences of the charged offense, indispensable to a greater statutory maximum than the charged elements and verdict otherwise allow, performs the constitutional function of an element. The federal charging, jury and reasonable-doubt safeguards follow, subject to valid admission and waiver rules; those rules do not repair the omitted charge by implication. Their Monge position treated an additional, previously unestablished personal-conduct qualification as an element for the finality question. They now apply that functional reasoning to injury in the present offense, with Gaudin supplying jury allocation. This is their constitutional conclusion here, not a claim that Monge already decided the jury question. Ordinary factfinding within existing conviction-authorized punishment remains distinct. The concurrence leaves bare prior-conviction status, unchanged-maximum minima, general Guidelines classification and capital sentencing unresolved. Four Justices adopt this independent constitutional ground; it is not the Court's holding.

**Stevens, joined by Scalia in Part I.** Their broader position protects facts that change the legislatively prescribed punishment range, including facts increasing the minimum, and recognizes no categorical exclusion merely because the fact is a prior conviction. Stevens relies on Winship's proof requirement, Mullaney's protection against shifting the burden on a fact determining punishment, his objections to Patterson's treatment of offense definition, and Duncan's protection of jury adjudication. Scalia agrees that the legislature cannot avoid the protection by naming a range-determining fact a sentencing consideration. His narrower Monge element-function ground supports that conclusion without having already established this universal range principle. Neither ordinary relevant-conduct sentencing under Witte and Watts nor an existing conviction's validity is the same question as whether a new fact legally authorizes a different punishment range. A charged prior-status element may be established by adequate admission or other lawful proof without relitigating the conviction's validity; Custis's collateral-review limits remain. These positions do not decide a mandatory-minimum application or overrule McMillan here. In Part II, Stevens alone also places facts necessary for death eligibility within his constitutional principle and favors reconsideration of contrary Walton reasoning in an appropriate case. No capital issue is decided, and Scalia does not join that recommendation.

**Stone-Zsela.** The Chief Justice joins the statutory construction and remedy as sufficient to decide this case. He maintains that a fact increasing authorized punishment must receive the jury protection owed an element, but does not make that broader position an additional constitutional ground of this disposition or join Souter's independent constitutional concurrence. Almendarez-Torres supplies a statutory analogue, not an already controlling universal constitutional rule. His maintained position preserves his view while the Court's decision rests on the enacted aggravated offenses. It supplies no decision of the separate questions concerning prior status, unchanged-maximum minima or capital sentencing.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor and Breyer.** The dissent reads the opening firearm-taking paragraph as a complete offense and the dependent injury and death clauses as graded punishment for its consequences. The sharp increases and the traditional use of injury to grade robbery support the Court's reading, but do not overcome that offense-first structure in the dissent's view. Patterson preserves a bounded legislative role in defining an offense; Gaudin protects the jury's determination once an actual element is identified. The dissent directly defends treating injury during this completed offense as a punishment condition, acknowledging that present victim injury differs from recidivist character. Monge accepted both an unproved personal-conduct qualification and a higher ceiling without treating those features as conclusive of offense status, while leaving the independent jury question open. The dissent therefore does not claim that Monge already resolved this claim or that the qualification was previously proved. Almendarez-Torres's statutory reentry holding remains binding but supplies no general constitutional rule either permitting or forbidding this allocation. McMillan's unchanged-ceiling setting and Witte's and Watts's within-authority sentencing do not themselves resolve the increased maximum. The dissent would sustain the challenged injury-based sentencing authority against this classification objection, without adopting unlimited legislative relabeling, resolving unrelated sentencing errors or directing reinstatement of an earlier sentence.


## Withrow v. Williams

Source: `terms/OT1992/output/OT_1992CHUNK5.md`

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

O’Connor concurs in the notice reversal and dissents on Miranda review. Following her Duckworth concurrence, she would extend Powell to a claim involving voluntary but unwarned statements after a full and fair state opportunity. She regards involuntariness review as the appropriate means to remedy genuine coercion and the added deterrent value of later Miranda review as insufficient to outweigh finality and retrial costs. This does not reject collateral review of actual compelled statements.

Scalia, joined by Thomas, also joins the notice ruling but would remand for a determination whether unusual equitable circumstances justify renewed Miranda litigation after the opportunities already afforded. He treats prior full and fair litigation as a powerful equitable consideration under sections 2241 and 2243, subject to considerations concerning fairness or accuracy, rather than a jurisdictional defect. The separate writing urges a broader approach beyond this case; the Court does not adopt it. Herrera’s record-specific holding supplies no categorical innocence bar.


## Davis v. United States

Source: `terms/OT1993/output/OT_1993CHUNK7.md`

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, joins the actual-clarification application and clear-request protections but rejects a mandatory pause for an uncertain reference. Following a knowing and voluntary waiver, these Justices would require words clear enough that a reasonable officer would understand counsel to be requested. Their rule demands no legal terminology or prescribed formula. McNeil requires communication of the particular custodial-counsel interest, and Edwards's cessation protection follows invocation rather than a possible undisclosed wish. The initial waiver remains significant until the suspect objectively communicates a changed choice. Limited clarification is often sensible and is lawful here, but its practical value does not itself establish a legal duty. These Justices accept Smith's prohibition on using later answers to undo a clear request and do not approve coercive persuasion disguised as clarification. They accept the credited first ambiguity, the actual neutral exchange and voluntary disclaimer, and cessation upon the final clear request. They do not rely on a new waiver, decide prewaiver conduct, or adopt the unpressed §3501 theory. Their no-duty rule has four votes; Part I's mandatory rule controls.


## Thompson v. Keohane

Source: `terms/OT1995/output/OT_1995CHUNK1.md`

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Thomas dissents. He would review the legal definition of custody independently but treat the concrete reasonable-person inference from the established circumstances as factual under §2254(d). Patton and Witt demonstrate that applying a legal standard does not itself turn the resulting judgment into a legal question. State judges must assess interacting circumstances and inferences; the absence of live testimony does not categorically remove the factual character of their task.

Thomas distinguishes Miller's particular history and function in independent voluntariness review from every antecedent warning question. National uniformity in the definition of custody does not, in his view, require independent redetermination of its every application. He would affirm the reviewed judgment on this classification ground while preserving the statute's factual exceptions. He does not make a new determination that Thompson was not in custody, adopt an alternative retroactivity bar, decide harmlessness or deny every habeas claim on an unpresented ground. The majority instead regards application of the arrest-equivalence boundary as a distinct constitutional task requiring independent review.


## Neder v. United States

Source: `terms/OT1998/output/OT_1998CHUNK9.md`

### Neder v. United States, No. 97-1985

Merits decision, June 10, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 23, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, reviewing 136 F.3d 1459 (1998).

The questions are whether materiality is an element of federal mail, wire, and bank fraud; whether removal of an offense element permits harmless-error review; and whether the materiality errors require relief here. The trial judge expressly withheld tax and bank materiality and omitted it from the mail and wire instructions over Neder's objections. The court of appeals affirmed the tax convictions as harmless despite that error, and affirmed the fraud convictions on the separate ground that materiality was not an element. Neder seeks new trials on the affected counts; the United States disputes the fraud element and alternatively asserts harmlessness. The challenged tax verdicts are counts 86 and 87. The fraud disposition reaches the convictions affected by the materiality ruling under §§1341, 1343, and 1344, without assigning a new result to an unrelated count.

All nine Justices participate: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding and operative rule:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutes use fraud as a legal term with settled content. Morissette supplies the principle that Congress ordinarily carries that established meaning into the statute; the absence of a separate materiality adjective does not strip fraud of its significance requirement. Durland includes fraudulent future promises, but does not make an inconsequential falsehood a fraudulent scheme or import every element of a completed civil tort. The statutory focus on schemes, and §1344's inclusion of attempts, explains why actual reliance and completed injury are unnecessary. Mail or wire use remains a distinct requirement, and the communication need not itself contain the falsehood if it executes the scheme.

Section 1344 separately covers executing or attempting a scheme to defraud a covered institution and executing or attempting a scheme to obtain its property by false or fraudulent pretenses. Both routes retain materiality for the deceptive schemes presented here; their other possible differences, including mental-state requirements in other applications, remain open. Wells construes the different wording of §1014, which does not require objective materiality in addition to its false-statement or overvaluation and purpose-to-influence requirements. It does not turn fraud language into an unrestricted false-statement prohibition. A purpose to influence under §1014 does not automatically establish the separate fraud element.

The natural-tendency formulation, reflected in Kungys, concerns the targeted decision in context. The Court does not exhaust every common-law formulation, unusual recipient preference, omission duty, or nonrepresentational scheme. Nor does it decide materiality under every differently worded offense. The judgment rejecting materiality under the three fraud provisions is reversed.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding and operative rule:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

**Controlling explanation:** Gaudin assigns the application of an actual element to the jury. Section 7206(1) independently supplies materiality through its express prohibition of willfully subscribing a return declared under penalties of perjury that the signer does not believe true and correct as to every material matter. Part I establishes the element under the fraud statutes. The express tax and bank directions withheld that application, while the mail and wire charges omitted it. The judge's separate materiality ruling is not a jury finding.

Chapman places the burden on the Government for these preserved constitutional errors. Failure to offer a separately labeled factual materiality defense supplies neither waiver nor forfeiture and does not require Neder to prove innocence. Johnson's refusal to correct forfeited error under the fourth condition of plain-error review does not govern these preserved objections. Genuine findings elsewhere in an actual verdict can establish an equivalent determination; whether they do so here, and whether other supported inferences suffice, are distinct questions addressed in the following parts. Neither the Court's recognition of error nor Gaudin alone decides those remedial questions.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding and operative rule:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

**Controlling explanation:** Roy permits examination of a particular omitted element without converting every omission into a structural defect requiring automatic reversal. Its qualified method concerns the error's effect on the jury that returned this verdict. Yates supplies the instructional-focus requirement: a reviewing court must examine whether the instructions directed the jury to evidence supporting the proposed inference, rather than assume the jury decided a question removed from its task. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient, but not invariably indispensable to a reliable noncontribution conclusion.

Sullivan addresses a different defect, an invalid reasonable-doubt standard vitiating all factual findings. These instructions left an otherwise valid reasonable-doubt standard governing the submitted elements. Their express removal of materiality heightens the need to justify the connection; it does not itself destroy every remaining finding or forbid the inquiry Roy allows. Gaudin's allocation rule remains fully operative at trial.

Powerful and unchallenged evidence may support an inference, but neither label creates an automatic safe harbor. A reviewing court must assess defense disputes for what they concern and determine whether the actual findings resolve them. Sufficient evidence for a hypothetical properly instructed conviction is not the required showing. Mitchell likewise distinguishes actual contribution from enough other evidence to sustain a result. This holding does not classify every omitted element, constructive amendment, or total denial of jury trial. Richardson's reserved harmlessness questions concerning its distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect remain unresolved.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding and operative rule:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The instruction required knowing, purposeful falsity of the reported total income: $53,625 for 1985 and minus $980,377 for 1986. It did not permit conviction for an unidentified collateral entry. The jury considered the defenses that the receipts were repayable loans and that professional advice or good faith negated culpability. Its findings reject those defenses insofar as they would make the specified totals truthful or their falsity innocent. The verdict does not resolve every subsidiary advice dispute, but its particular subject and culpability findings furnish the connection required by Roy's actual-contribution inquiry. Annual income is information used to determine income-tax liability and administer the returns.

The objection is substantial: the jury was expressly forbidden to decide materiality, so a court must not supply that missing decision. The Court does not suppose that jurors disobeyed the instruction, treat the judge's ruling as theirs, or declare that they necessarily made the omitted evaluation. It instead asks what effect that withdrawal had on an otherwise valid verdict about these identified income statements. The charge's specific focus and findings establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt under Roy's permitted method.

The conclusion requires no unanimous numerical finding of the alleged additional income, acceptance of every transaction or witness, unpaid-tax calculation, actual IRS response, or successful deception. It does not excuse future courts from submitting materiality. The Government retains the burden in each case; this record-specific conclusion neither creates a tax exception to Gaudin nor decides the fraud counts.

##### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

**Controlling explanation:** The court of appeals rejected the antecedent statutory element and therefore did not undertake the required fraud harmlessness analysis. Unlike the tax charge's specified annual-income findings, the fraud instructions and theories require a distinct inquiry into what each verdict resolved. Inflated appraisals, concealed relationships and down payments, presales, and advance requests may bear strongly on lending decisions. Yet the charge did not require proof of every alleged detail, expressly removed bank materiality, and used cross-references for mail and wire fraud. A generic intent-to-deceive finding does not automatically resolve capacity to influence, and Wells prevents treating a §1014 purpose-to-influence finding as an automatic equivalent. The lending-purpose and transaction-significance disputes must be considered in context.

Part V-B applies Roy's qualified method and Chapman's burden to that unfinished appellate task. The tax affirmance proves neither harmlessness nor harmfulness for any fraud count. No present determination requires a new trial on every affected fraud count or sustains any one of them automatically. If the Government fails the governing showing on an affected conviction, instructional-error relief permits retrial; it does not establish evidentiary insufficiency or require acquittal.

The mandate does not independently dispose of §1014, conspiracy, or racketeering claims, reopen acquittals, or calculate a sentence, restitution, or release date. Any properly available dependent consequence must follow the actual predicate and ordinary mandate proceedings. Those boundaries are part of the limited remand, not an advance resolution of unpresented claims.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Morissette v. United States:** Applies the borrowed-term principle to fraud's established materiality requirement; statutory silence alone does not discard that content.
- **Durland v. United States:** Preserves coverage of fraudulent future promises while distinguishing that breadth from elimination of materiality or importation of every completed-tort requirement.
- **Kungys v. United States:** Uses its natural-tendency or capacity formulation for the financial deceptions presented here; its civil allocation and burden do not govern the criminal jury question.
- **United States v. Wells:** Preserves the different §1014 construction without an added objective-materiality element; it neither removes materiality from fraud statutes nor automatically cures their instructional error through a purpose-to-influence finding.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury determination of each actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt; harmlessness review does not authorize withdrawal of that element at trial.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applies the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to establish that the preserved error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **California v. Roy:** Extends its qualified actual-verdict method to the materiality errors presented here; necessary equivalence is sufficient but not exclusive, and its instructional-focus limitation remains essential.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies attention to the actual charge, findings, and evidence considered under the instructions; proof sufficient for a different verdict cannot replace contribution analysis.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes removal of a particular element from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings; its structural-error rule remains intact.
- **Johnson v. United States:** Distinguishes its discretionary plain-error disposition after forfeiture; it neither supplies a waiver here nor reduces Chapman's burden.
- **Jones v. United States:** Preserves its protection for charged statutory elements without treating it as a classification or harmlessness decision for this error.
- **Mitchell v. United States:** Applies its distinction between an error's actual contribution and sufficient other evidence without transferring its separate privilege holding.
- **Richardson v. United States:** Leaves intact its reservation of availability and application of harmless-error review for the distinct constituent-violation unanimity defect.

#### Law After Decision

Effective June 10, 1999, materiality must be submitted for the charged mail-, wire-, and bank-fraud schemes; §7206(1)'s express materiality element and §1014's different Wells rule retain their respective force. For the materiality errors presented here, the Government may establish harmlessness through Roy's qualified actual-verdict inquiry, but uncontested or overwhelming evidence is not an automatic substitute for that showing. The tax verdicts meet the standard on their specific charge and record; the affected fraud verdicts require initial application below.

The six-Justice tax ground is noncontribution to the actual verdict, not a rule that the jury necessarily decided every omitted materiality question. Stevens's supplementary view and Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive equivalent-findings method do not control. The Chief accepts the governing broader method but disagrees with its application and the further fraud inquiry. The decision establishes no universal omitted-element classification, generic tax shortcut, new no-contest waiver, or automatic effect on a differently worded offense. Richardson's separate harmlessness reservation remains open.

#### Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I, II, and III. Fraud's settled meaning supplies materiality, Wells concerns another enactment, and Gaudin requires submission of actual elements. Roy makes these omissions reviewable trial error and permits reliable whole-record inferences beyond necessary equivalent findings. He does not adopt a universal structural-error rule or Scalia and Ginsburg's exclusive method.

The Government's case must be weighed: the alleged omissions exceeding $1 million and $4 million plainly concern tax computation, the alleged inflated appraisals and nonexistent down payments concern lending decisions, and Neder did not separately contest materiality as a factual matter. Those considerations are substantial, not disqualified evidence. Nevertheless, the instructions removed tax and bank materiality and never asked the jury to assess that dimension of mail or wire fraud. The findings of knowing falsity and fraudulent intent do not reliably show the omission's lack of contribution to the actual verdict on each affected count. The annual-income findings identify the statements and culpability, but do not close that gap. The Government has shown what a properly instructed jury would almost certainly find, not harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt under these instructions and verdicts.

He would reverse the conviction-sustaining judgment, vacate every conviction affected by removal or omission of materiality, and remand for new trials if the United States elects to retry. He therefore dissents both from tax affirmance and from another fraud harmlessness inquiry. Sullivan forbids inventing a verdict, while Roy supplies the governing review; Johnson's forfeited-error disposition does not shift the burden. He would leave unaffected counts to their own preserved issues, disturb no §1014 conviction merely because of this holding, and order no insufficiency acquittal. A valid admission or a record establishing actual-verdict harmlessness could produce a different result.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Ginsburg.** Both join Parts I, II, and V-A. They agree on fraud materiality, the jury's role, the preserved claim and Government's burden, and a first appellate inquiry into the affected fraud convictions. Their Roy position permits review for actual findings that genuinely embrace the omitted element; it does not permit appellate certainty about an unresolved element to replace its jury determination. Gaudin and Sullivan protect that division of responsibility. They withhold assent from Part III's broader method and its classification of the express materiality withdrawal, while retaining Roy's classification within that decision's own personal-purpose category.

The tax verdict establishes knowing, purposeful false annual income. It does not establish the jury's own application of the omitted materiality requirement, which the instruction forbade it to consider. A judge's conclusion about the significance of annual income supplies the missing evaluation rather than identifies a genuine equivalent finding. They accordingly reject both Part IV's application and Stevens's additional rationale. Their ground permits new tax trials under correct instructions; it is not evidentiary insufficiency and does not require acquittal or unconditional release.

For fraud, they join the limited remand because the court of appeals' no-element ruling left a real initial inquiry unfinished. They would confine it to genuine equivalent findings on the affected counts, and do not join Part V-B's wider inference instruction. A valid equivalent determination can establish harmlessness, so their position is not automatic reversal for every omitted element. Ginsburg joins this writing throughout.

**Justice Stevens, concurring.** He joins the entire Court opinion, including its qualified actual-verdict method, tax noncontribution conclusion, and limited fraud remand. He adds that the required finding of knowing falsity of these particular annual-income totals embraces their material relationship to determining income-tax liability. His additional ground rests on the actual findings and their specific subject, not on appellate prediction about what another jury would decide. It requires no finding that every alleged dollar was income, that an unpaid-tax amount was established, or that the IRS was successfully deceived. His view supplies no additional holding beyond the Court's sufficient common ground. He continues to reject hypothetical-verdict substitution and does not use his disagreement with Wells to reopen the different §1014 offenses.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court merits review ends with a mixed judgment. The judgment sustaining tax counts 86 and 87 stands against the materiality-instruction challenge. The Eleventh Circuit must conduct the first proper harmlessness inquiry for the affected convictions under §§1341, 1343, and 1344, applying the qualified actual-verdict method and the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden count by count unless an established common finding lawfully covers multiple counts. If an affected conviction cannot be sustained, it must be set aside, and the United States may retry under instructions submitting materiality and every other element beyond a reasonable doubt. The mandate predetermines neither that review's result nor any otherwise available dependent sentencing or count disposition.

**End of entry: Neder v. United States, Merits decision, June 10, 1999.**
