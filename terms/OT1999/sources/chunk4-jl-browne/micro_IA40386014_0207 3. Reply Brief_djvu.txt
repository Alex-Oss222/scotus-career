1. The decision below conflicts with decisions of 

this Court and other courts of appeals. 

The government asserts that the decision below “does 
not conflict with any decision of this Court or any other 
court of appeals.” Opp. at 5. In fact, Petitioners demonstrated 
several conflicts, including Cheema v. Thompson, 67 F.3d 
883, 885 (9" Cir. 1995)(RFRA requires serious consideration 
and a demonstration of least restrictive means); Craddick v. 
Duckworth, 113 F.3d 83, 85 (7" Cir. 1997); Fraternal Order 
of Police Newark Lodge No. 12 v. City of Newark, 170 F.3d 
359 (3rd Cir.), cert. denied, _U.S.__, 120 S.Ct. 56 (1999). 

The decision below conflicts with these decisions 
because each of these cases required an inquiry to determine 
the burden on the plaintiffs, the Government’s compelling 
interest AND whether the Government used the least 
restrictive means to meet its compelling interest. This 
inquiry demands proof, not merely lawyers’ assertions. In 
this case, which was decided on the pleadings, the burden on 
the Brownes was conceded, but an inquiry is still required to 
determine the Government’s compelling interest in the 
waiver system and whether it used the least restrictive 
means.

2. The standard to grant waivers of penalties and 

interest is facially discriminatory and subjective. 

The Government argues that this case is 
distinguishable from Smith because the waivers in the statute 
and regulation are granted on the basis of “facially neutral 
uniformly applicable standards that do not take into account 
religious or other beliefs.” Opp. 7. Yet, in Smith, the Court 
explicitly reiterated that the standard in Bowen v. Roy, 476 
U.S. 693(1986)(plurality), and Sherbert v. Verner, 374 U.S. 
398 (1963), was to be read that where a Government “has in 
place a system of individual exemptions,” such as the 
hardship system in place here, “it may not refuse to extend 
that system to cases of ‘religious hardship’ without a 
compelling reason.” 494 U.S. at 884. The system at issue in 
this case is not the federal income tax system, but the system 
of waivers for penalties and interest. This waiver system 
cannot be facially neutral when the plain language of the 
agency rule which, according to the introduction onf the 
IRM, applies to all waivers states: “[a]n appeal should not be 
based on moral, political, constitutional, religious or similar 
arguments.” IRM [120.1] 6.1.3. 

The Government again ignores the plain language of 
both the statute and the Internal Revenue Manual. The 
penalties and interest shall be levied UNLESS the taxpayer 
is “unable to pay the taxes or would suffer an undue 
hardship... if he paid on the due date” 26 CFR. § 301.6651-
