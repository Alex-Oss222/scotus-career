COMPLAINT 


The plaintiff Alexis Geier, by and through counsel, sues 
the defendants American Honda Motor Co., Inc., Honda of 
America Manufacturing, Inc. and Honda Motor Co., Ltd. and 
for her cause of action states as follows: 


1. The plaintiff, Alexis Geier, is a citizen of the District 
of Columbia. 


2. Defendants American Honda Motor Co., Inc. and 
Honda of America Inc. are corporations 
regularly doing business in the District of Columbia. 


3. Defendant Honda Motor Co., Ltd. is a foreign 


corporation, headquartered in Tokyo, Japan, regularly doing 
business in the District of Columbia. 


4. On or about January 12, 1992 at approximately 
10:32 p.m., plaintiff Alexis Geier was the driver and occupant 
in the left, front driver's seat of a 1987 Honda Accord, four door 
sedan, serial number L6196563 which had been designed, 
manufactured and distributed by defendants American Honda 
Motor Co., Inc., Honda of America Manufacturing, Inc., and/or 
Honda Motor Co., Ltd., when the vehicle collided with a tree on 
the 4900 block of MacArthur Boulevard, near its intersection 
with Ashley Street, in the District of Columbia. 


5. At the time of the accident, the vehicle in question 


was owned by Thomas J. Whitehead, a resident of the District 
of Columbia. 


6. At the time of the accident, plaintiff Alexis Geier 
was a minor. She reached the age of majority on June 29, 1992. 


JA-2 


7. At the time of the accident, plaintiff Alexis Geier 
was using the shoulder and lap belt that had been designed, 
assembled, tested and installed by defendants American Honda 
Motor Co., Inc., Honda of America Manufacturing, Inc. and/or 
Honda Motor Co., Ltd. for the driver's seating position of the 
1987 Honda Accord. 


8. The defendants American Honda Motor Co., Inc., 
Honda of America Manufacturing, Inc. and Honda Motor Co., 
Ltd. had a duty to design, manufacture, assemble, install and 
test a passenger seating and restraint system for the driver's 
seating position of the 1987 Honda Accord which was 
reasonably safe for use by its occupants, including plaintiff 


9. Defendants American Honda Motor Co., Inc., 
Honda of America Manufacturing, Inc. and Honda Motor Co., 
Ltd. knew or should have known on the basis of the existing 
engineering, medical, safety and other scientific and technical 
literature through 1987 that a failure of the driver's seating and 
restraint systems could cause severe injuries in the event of an 


emergency. 


10. Defendants American Honda Motor Co., Inc., 
Honda of America Manufacturing, Inc. and Honda Motor Co., 
Ltd. had a duty to design, manufacture, distribute and sell motor 
vehicles which contain driver's seating systems which provide 
suitable restraint and protection to an occupant at all times. 


11. Defendants American Honda Motor Co., Inc., 
Honda of America Manufacturing, Inc. and Honda Motor Co., 
Ltd. had a duty to design, manufacture, distribute and sell a

12. Defendants American Honda Motor Co., Inc., 
Honda of America Manufacturing, Inc. and Honda Motor Co., 
Ltd. had a duty to design, manufacture, distribute and sell a 
motor vehicle which incorporated therein the state of the art for 
the automotive it.tustry in the science and engineering of 
passive restraint systems, including, but not limited to, airbags. 


13. Defendants American Honda Motor Co., Inc., 
Honda of America Manufacturing, Inc. and Honda Motor Co., 
Ltd. knew or should have known on the basis of the existing 
engineering, medical, safety and other scientific and technical 
literature through 1987 that the absence of a passive restraint 
system which includes airbags could cause severe injuries, in 
the event of a collision. 


14. The driver's seating and restraint systems of the 
1987 Honda Accord failed to provide suitable restraint at all 
times, including during the collision in which the vehicle was 
involved on January 12, 1992. 


15. Upon the occurrence of that collision, the seat back 
of the left, driver's side seat broke. 


16. Upon the occurrence of that collision, the left, 
driver's side seat broke off of its track. 


17. Upon the occurrence of that collision, there was no 
airbag installed in the front, driver's side of the vehicle. 


18. As a result, plaintiff Alexis Geier suffered serious 
and gric vous injuries caused by the driver's seating and restraint 
systems failure to provide suitable restraint and protection to

19. As a further result, plaintiff Alexis Geier suffered 
serious and grievous injuries caused by the absence of a passive 


20. As a direct and proximate result of the aforesaid 
failure, plaintiff Alexis Geier suffered injury to all parts of her 
body, some of which are believed to be permanent, especially 
to her head, brain, mouth, legs, chest, neck, and back, suffered 
and shall suffer great pain of body and mind, incurred and shall 
incur medical and out of pocket expenses, suffered an 
interruption of her education, suffered a reduced earning 
capacity, was required to undergo multiple surgeries, suffered 
significant scarring and disfigurement, and suffered significant 
emotional and mental stress. 


21. As a further direct and proximate result of the 
aforesaid failure, plaintiffs William Geier and Claire Geier, as 
parents of Alexis Geier incurred medical and out of pocket 
expenses on behalf of their daughter during her minority and 
lost time and income from work during their daughter's 
minority to care for and assist her.

AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES 


American Honda hereby raises the following affirmative 
defense to plaintiffs’ Complaint. 


Defense No. 1 
(Failure to State a Claim) 


Plaintiffs Complaint fails to state a claim upon which 
relief can be granted. 


Defense No. 2 
(Abuse or Misuse of Product) 


All of the injuries and damages alleged by plaintiff were 
caused by abuse and/or misuse of the subject vehicle by 
plaintiff, plaintiff's decedent or third parties for whom 
American Honda is not and was not responsible. 


JA-69 


Defense No. 3 
(Contributory Negligence) 


All of the injuries and damages alleged by plaintiff were 
caused by the contributory negligence of plaintiff, plaintiff's 
decedent or third parties for whom American Honda is not and 
was not responsible. 


Defense No. 4 
(Intervening or Superseding Cause) 


All of the injuries and damages alleged by plaintiff were 
caused by the intervening or superseding acts and/or omissions 
of plaintiff, plaintiff's decedent and/or third parties for whom 
Amenican Honda is not and was not responsible. 


Defense No. 5 
(No Proximate Cause) 


There exists no proximate causation between American 
Honda's alleged acts and omissions and the alleged injuries and 
damages claimed by plaintiff, thereby barring any recovery 
against American Honda. 


Defense No. 6 
(Failure to Comply With Warranty) 


The plaintiff or other third parties failed to comply with 


the terms of applicable warranties thereby barring any recovery 
from American Honda for any purported breach of warranty. 


JA-70 


Defense No. 7 
Limitations) 


Plaintiff's claims are barred by the Statute of 
Limitati 


Defense No. 8 
(Alteration or Modification) 


All of the injuries or damages alleged by plaintiff were 
caused by the alteration or modification of the subject vehicle 
by plaintiff, plaintiff's decedent or third parties for whom 
American Honda is not and was not responsible. 


Defense No. 9 
(Assumption of the Risk) 


The injuries or damages alleged by plaintiff were caused 
in whole or in part by the decedent's assumption of the risk. 


Defense No. 10 
(Preemption) 
Plaintiffs’ claims are preempted by Federal law. 


Defense No. 11 
(Statutory Defenses) 


American Honda pleads all defenses available under the 
laws of the District of Columbia.
