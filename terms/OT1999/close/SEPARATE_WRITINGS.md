## 1999-12-06 — Paul P. v. Verniero; Third Circuit No. 97-5791, 170 F.3d 396; no separate Supreme Court docket number stated.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I–III: public conviction information is unprotected by this claim, the State has a legitimate protective objective, compiled nonpublic residential information carries a limited privacy interest, and disclosure needs safeguards reasonably fitted to its protective function. He dissents from affirming summary judgment insofar as a compelling-interest balance alone defeats the claim concerning compiled nonpublic home-address and identifying information. In his view, the safeguards inquiry determines whether that disclosure is justified and therefore must govern adjudication of the claim itself, not only an ancillary examination of implementation after its rejection. He would vacate that part of the affirmance and remand the claim for decision in the District Court under the fitted-safeguards rule, together with the retained safeguards proceedings. His position requires meaningful controls on recipients, use, scope, confidentiality and redistribution, while allowing targeted notification to persons with an actual safety need. It conceals no public conviction, imposes no injunction or interim restraint, and leaves the ungranted family-relationships ruling undisturbed.

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree with Part I's public-conviction and legitimate-purpose propositions but reject extending substantive due process to this residential-information claim. Their Sheets dissents and Doe concurrences distinguish a supported constitutional source from a desire to restrict the government's handling of information. A home address is not rendered constitutionally intimate simply through compilation, and W.P.'s protection rests on an independent state-created substantive restriction rather than a universal federal information right. They affirm the State's information-disclosure summary judgment because the proposed substantive constitutional right has not been established, without joining the Court's limited-interest and balancing rationale. They would vacate the privacy-right ruling and the safeguard directive insofar as dependent on it, and remand consistently with that rejection while preserving W.P.'s existing independent process requirements. They neither adopt Stone-Zsela's right-based merits remand nor deny every possible constitutional protection of the home.

## 1999-12-07 — Los Angeles Police Department v. United Reporting Publishing Corp.; No. 98-678

## 2000-01-11 — Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents / United States v. Florida Board of Regents; Nos. 98-791 and 98-796

Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissents from the Article I holding and resulting remand. They distinguish a valid federal employment duty from a private action against a nonconsenting State's treasury and expressly seek reconsideration of Florida Prepaid's Article I rule and the Union Gas power conclusion to the necessary extent. They do not claim that a patent-only limitation already governs lower courts or that ordinary age-employment regulation lies outside commerce.

They also reject §5 as an independent basis for these private remedies. Gregory permits rational age classifications, including imperfect generalizations, whereas the ADEA generally demands more than constitutional rationality. Its bona fide occupational qualification requires reasonable necessity to normal business operation; a reasonable factor other than age does not authorize treatment because of age. Good-cause discipline remains distinct. A protected seniority system must be bona fide and not intended to evade the Act, and cannot permit involuntary age-based retirement of a protected individual. For each benefit or benefit package under a bona fide employee benefit plan, the actual payment made or cost incurred for an older worker must be no less than that for a younger worker, as permissible under 29 C.F.R. §1625.10 as in effect on June 22, 1989; the separate alternative is a voluntary early-retirement incentive plan consistent with the Act's purposes; neither excuses failure to hire, and benefit plans cannot permit involuntary age-based retirement. The employer bears the stated burden under those seniority and benefit provisions. The foreign-workplace defense requires that compliance violate the foreign country's law and supplies no domestic exception. No defense is found established.

The four assess the identified governmental complaints, federal-employment newspaper reports, private-sector findings and California survey, principally concerning public-safety age limits, as insufficiently connected to unconstitutional state practices to support this remedy's breadth. They do not infer constitutional validity from state-law permission. The identified recitations are not an exhaustive congressional record, and the dissent does not conclude that Congress heard no evidence. Boerne requires substantive fit; their objection does not impose formal findings, a number of violations, geographical evidence, a sunset or an individual constitutional-tort prerequisite. Florida Prepaid's preventive property remedy presents, for them, a different protected interest and remedial gap; permitting prevention beyond constitutional violations does not itself settle this statute's fit.

Justice Thomas, joined by Kennedy, additionally dissents from statutory authorization. They distinguish express State-employer coverage from unmistakable authorization of this private federal action. Section 626(c) can be read to supply the ADEA's own cause while §626(b) imports FLSA remedial and procedural incidents. The original 1967 incorporation, the 1974 amendment of §216(b), the later removal of §255's incorporation and the competent-court language leave insufficient clarity in their view. They require no universal single-section formula, but regard this statutory ambiguity as independently sufficient to affirm; their constitutional objections remain alternatives if authorization is assumed.

## 2000-01-12 — Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc.; No. 98-822

Justice Stevens joins the Court and adds a distinct monetary-remedy ground. In his view, a valid civil-penalty judgment cannot be retroactively invalidated simply because postjudgment closure makes recurrence impossible. He also concludes that a properly initiated civil-penalty claim survives postcomplaint cessation even before judgment: the Clean Water Act attaches monetary liability to violations, and Tull's distinction between monetary penalties and prospective injunctions supports that treatment. Initial standing and the preserved statutory diligent-prosecution bar remain necessary. He does not authorize suit by an uninjured person, rely on fees for a personal stake, finally approve the $405,800 amount or order collection. His broader survival theory has no other join; he supports the Court's sufficient grounds and ordinary appellate return while disagreeing that proof of permanent cessation necessarily defeats this properly initiated penalty claim.

Justice Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissents on two independently sufficient Article III grounds. First, the science-based findings of adequate recreational water quality and no demonstrated adverse environmental effect undermine an objective connection between these exceedances and the asserted use loss. Expressed concern is not self-proving injury. Curtis's childhood cessation for another reason and Moore's limited visits reinforce their objection to treating desired use as established interference, without requiring ecosystem destruction in every case or resting their objection on litigant default.

Second, even assuming injury, the State's penalty, governmental enforcement, later compliance and litigation exposure make the additional federal penalty's likely marginal effect on these members' future harm too speculative. They accept Steel Co.'s rule that Treasury penalties can deter supported threatened injury; they reject this factual showing rather than all private reliance on public penalties. General deterrent purpose is not enough, although certainty is not required. Their concern about private public-law enforcement supplies no separate Article II holding. If initial standing were assumed, they accept that cessation and closure could sensibly receive factual review, but that conditional view supplies no vote for the Court's operative reversal, restoration or remand. They would direct jurisdictional dismissal.

## 2000-01-12 — Martinez v. Court of Appeal of California, Fourth Appellate District; No. 98-7809

**Justice Kennedy, concurring; no other Justice joins.** He joins the entire Court opinion and affirms Faretta's soundness. The appellate holding rests on review's different constitutional and institutional setting, not on doubt about trial autonomy. It gives no authority for counsel to override the defendant's choice whether to appeal or other basic personal decisions. The concurrence qualifies the holding's possible implications without withholding assent to any controlling part or proposing a different remedy.

**Justice Breyer, concurring; no other Justice joins.** He joins the entire Court opinion while reserving a distinct point about any future reconsideration of Faretta. Practical judicial concerns about self-representation do not establish that Faretta's constitutional premises have failed; no sufficiently established empirical basis for reconsidering its trial holding is supplied here. This caution makes no empirical finding, imposes no new condition on the trial right and directs no evidence gathering. It is distinct from Kennedy's affirmative defense of Faretta, and Breyer does not join Kennedy's separate opinion.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in the judgment; no other Justice joins.** He agrees that California need not provide appellate self-representation, but does not join the Court's explanation. McKane's rule that the Constitution does not generally require a State to create an appeal supports the State's authority to structure the review it provides through counsel, subject to the established fair- and equal-access requirements of Douglas and Evitts. He preserves Faretta's protection of informed trial autonomy and regards due process as a possible stronger source of that protection. A court's belief that counsel would benefit a defendant does not justify extinguishing an existing personal trial choice. His appellate-forum ground is independent of balancing autonomy against fairness and efficiency; it does not decide a hypothetical substitute review system or withdraw existing appellate-access protections.

## 2000-01-19 — Smith v. Robbins; No. 98-1037

Justice Thomas dissents from Parts II–IV and the affirmance, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy. They join Part I's recognition of equivalent alternatives to Anders. The dissent evaluates Wende's full safeguards together: counsel reviews the record, supplies a cited narrative, communicates the available personal-submission route, remains appointed and available, and must provide further briefing if the court identifies a nonfrivolous issue; the court independently examines the entire record. Its ordinary Strickland remand does not make every future elaboration of ineffective assistance retroactive.

## 2000-01-19 — Weeks v. Angelone; No. 99-5746.

The jury's expressed mandatory-death confusion and the inadequate response, considered with the whole charge, establish an unreasonable application of Boyde, Lockett and Eddings. He separately finds substantial and injurious influence on the death verdict from the whole penalty record. He would grant sentence-only conditional relief requiring constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital sentence within a reasonable period. He nevertheless accepts the Court's remand, including possible denial upon independent statutory or causal review, without joining the Associates' reasoning that leaves those questions unresolved. He requires no present writ or sentence vacatur through that judgment and adopts no new Chapman extension. His invocation of a Brecht substantial-effect approach is his separate position, not the Court's description of Brecht's limited controlling holding.

**Souter, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, concurring.** They would extend State-borne Chapman review to this defined interference with the capital jury's lawful nondeath choice. The State should establish beyond reasonable doubt from the whole penalty record that the error did not contribute to the actual death sentence. Coleman supplies a functional analogy, not an already controlling classification of this claim. The constitutional error does not establish causal contribution, and no accepted lower-court effect determination supports present relief. They accept the return without a Court-selected standard while preserving their objection to substantial-influence review as the appropriate rule. Their concurrence supplies no controlling extension.

**Stevens, concurring.** He joins the lead but favors demanding whole-record substantial-influence review, with genuine grave doubt about effect preventing a finding of harmlessness. His Coleman position distinguishes the error's existence from its impact on the sentence. Unlike that case's completed lower-court analysis, the present denial supplies no accepted independent effect determination supporting an immediate writ. He therefore accepts the statutory-first return and reservation without joining the proposed Chapman extension.

**O'Connor, joined in Part I by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The specific reference sent the jury to the very paragraph authorizing life after an aggravator, reinforced by B1's express mitigation command. Under Boyde and Buchanan, the complete judicial directions supply the route; testimony and advocacy confirm it rather than cure a legal restriction. The question does not itself prove the answer failed, just as the poll does not conclusively prove understanding. **Kennedy, dissenting.** He joins the common affirmance grounds. If an available violation required effect review, his Coleman position calls for demanding substantial-influence analysis and treats genuine grave doubt as preventing harmlessness. **Thomas, joined by Scalia, dissenting.** They join the common affirmance grounds. They preserve the established Chapman categories but oppose extending them here. They make no present causal-effect finding and support no automatic relief.

## 2000-01-24 — Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC; No. 98-963

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas.**

The dissent accepts Russell's close-fit standard and the sufficiently important anticorruption interest, but would affirm invalidation of this presented statewide ceiling. The large-gift episodes support some prophylaxis without adequately justifying this amount. The severe statewide contraction in funding, contested primaries and candidates, together with campaign scale, requires a stronger operational answer than general findings of campaign capacity, indexing or examples of successful financing. That ground supports invalidating this limit on this record without overruling Buckley, ordering a replacement ceiling or recognizing unlimited candidate giving.

## 2000-01-24 — Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board / Price v. Bossier Parish School Board; Nos. 98-405 and 98-406

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring. No other Justice joins.**

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the Court's common holdings and denial of clearance but separately resolves the same-decision question the four other denial Justices leave open. When prohibited racial purpose materially influenced adoption, he would permit clearance only if the jurisdiction proves that independently sufficient lawful reasons would have produced the identical change. This preserves ordinary mixed-purpose discipline while retaining the jurisdiction's statutory burden; it neither makes any unlawful consideration automatically outcome-determinative nor treats plausible neutral interests as proof of the actual decision.

The Board fails that showing. Convenience, continuity, precinct administration and incumbent considerations do not establish that these same lines would have been selected absent the prohibited purpose. He shifts no burden to private challengers, requires no finding that every lawful concern was pretextual, and decides no materially different lawful plan. His rule allowing the defense has no additional join and does not become the Court's law. Bossier I left this issue open. The Arlington Heights and Mt. Healthy causation analogy informs his mixed-purpose rule, while the preclearance statute keeps the burden on the jurisdiction; Lesage's distinct admissions remedy does not itself decide section 5.

**Justice O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justices Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.**

The four Justices join the live-controversy ruling, the nonretrogression effects holding and application of the binding broader-purpose mandate. They agree that the current clearance cannot stand, but would vacate and return for the properly framed purpose determination rather than direct denial. Real administrative concerns, competing contextual inferences and the district court's unperformed broader inquiry counsel leaving the ultimate assessment to the statutory factfinding court. They neither equate no retrogression with no unlawful purpose nor treat every feasible minority alternative or historical episode as conclusive.

Prompt clearance and avoidance of precinct splits do not establish that the identical plan would have been chosen without prohibited purpose. The dissent therefore rejects affirmance on a supposed existing same-decision finding and reserves the defense's legal availability. Any further inquiry would retain the jurisdiction's burden, the full contextual evidence and ordinary lawful supplementation if appropriate, without requiring a new hearing or presuming undiscovered papers. The proposed disposition would not approve this plan, compel additional majority-Black districts, order a special election or merge the separate section 2 action.

## 2000-02-07 — U.S. West, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission; Tenth Circuit No. 98-9518; no Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring; no other Justice joins.**

Stone-Zsela joins the Court's entire opinion and separately explains the carrier's capacity to claim constitutional protection. Capacity follows the particular right: commercial-speech protection serves listeners as well as speakers, so the corporation may invoke it; legal personhood alone does not settle the scope of that right. Its charter supplies its existence, and authorization to provide telephone service supplies its service position and access to calling records. That access carries lawful confidentiality terms under section 222, subject to the independent commercial-speech scrutiny the Court applies.

Telephone service is not consent to every later profitable use of its records. But neither charter nor licensing permits government to demand surrender of independent constitutional protections. A penalty for viewpoint or editorial choice would receive the scrutiny governing that discrimination; this rule selects neither. The carrier's wealth or size reduces no protection, natural persons acting through it retain their own speech, and customers retain their interests in their records. Marketing and requests for consent remain available.

Lanphere's marketing trigger and Central Hudson/Fox, rather than a new entity-capacity test, govern the rule's validity. This additional explanation supplies no general charter-condition power, categorical inferiority of corporate speech or new constitutional ownership of all CPNI. No other Justice joins the concurrence.

**Justice Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Kennedy.**

Stevens and Kennedy join Part I's independent speech review and substantial confidentiality interest. They would retain invalidity of the challenged approval application on two independently sufficient grounds, with relief confined to that application and reconciled with the intervening instruments.

First, the agency has not adequately justified the selected distinction under Discovery Network, Edenfield, Lanphere and their United Reporting position. Confidential calling records materially differ from public records, but that difference does not establish every internal marketing use as a new harm. The Commission's own integrated-firm assessment and broad permission for existing-category marketing weaken its explanation for treating the selected new-category use differently. Sensitivity alone cannot supply advancement, and the dissent demands no empirical study in every case or regulation of all information sources.

Second, even assuming advancement, fit independently fails. The FCC considered opt-out and permits oral or electronic approval; neither fact is disputed. Its concern about unread notices does not adequately show why sufficiently clear notice with opt-out would fail to protect the actual internal-use interest. Low affirmative response is not widespread informed opposition; willing recipients may lose effective tailored communication. Fox does not impose least restrictive means, but still requires a supported comparison proportionate to the burden. The additional marketing channels do not eliminate that burden.

The general statutory text can permit affirmative approval, but statutory permission and Chevron deference cannot cure this unconstitutional application. The dissent would not invalidate section 222, require opt-out universally, adjudicate the later express wireless commands, adopt a takings conclusion or treat unresolved administrative claims as rejected. It does not defend blanket vacatur of every collateral provision.

## 2000-02-14 — Richards v. United States; No. 99-731

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins Part I and dissents from Part II. He would reverse the Feres dismissal in full and remand for all remaining FTCA requirements and defenses. His ground is the actual character of this authorized personal trip: active-duty status, lack of a pass or furlough, and on-base location matter but do not override the accepted private journey home in his own car on a public road. Brooks preserves that distinction. His disagreement with the partial affirmance rests on that incident-to-service classification; he adds no separate rule exempting every command allegation, no general commuting exception, and no requirement that only an assigned task can be service-incident. He would decide neither negligence nor damages, and would preserve independently applicable FTCA defenses.

Justice O'Connor dissents from Part I, joined by Justices Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, and joins Part II. They would affirm the entire Feres dismissal. The immediate departure from a military duty station while still on the installation in active service without pass or furlough remains sufficiently connected with service despite authorized early release, a private automobile and the accepted family purpose. Feres and Johnson extend beyond an immediate assigned task; Stanley requires no additional demonstration of actual discipline interference after the service relationship is established. The added on-post-home and connecting-road account reinforces that view if assumed, but is not necessary to it and is not treated as a fact found.

The dissent accepts Brooks's nonservice boundary and rejects an absolute rule for every on-base injury, every private purpose or every absence of formal furlough. It disagrees with the majority's application to this combination of circumstances. The admitted employment scope of the driver does not decide the injured member's relationship to service. Compensation and the federal relationship support the dissent's Feres analysis but create no universal benefits election or offset. The dissent independently joins the Shearer dismissal of the permission and order theories, adopts no unadjudicated discretionary-function ground, and makes no finding of negligence or liability.

## 2000-02-23 — Rice v. Cayetano; No. 98-818

**Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Souter, concurring in the judgment.** They agree that this actual election cannot escape the Fifteenth Amendment but do not join the Court's sufficient state-office rationale. Their ground is the failure of the asserted political-beneficiary analogy on the actual statutory arrangement. The Admission Act trust serves public education, native-Hawaiian betterment, widespread farm and home ownership, public improvements and lands for public use. OHA administers a dedicated twenty-percent revenue share for native-Hawaiian betterment, as well as state and federal appropriations and donations supporting the broader Hawaiian class. Those real trust and beneficiary features cannot be ignored. But the State's broad descent-only electorate, its public functions and its mixed obligations do not sufficiently establish membership in the political beneficiary community invoked to justify exclusion. Morton v. Mancari does not validate that state-created definition merely because it uses descent. The difference between the two ancestry definitions alone is not decisive. They prescribe no blood quantum, reject no genuine indigenous membership rule and reserve a differently constituted federal or political relationship. Their ground supports the same bounded reversal and remand.

**Justice Stevens, dissenting; Justice Ginsburg joins the federal-relationship ground only.** Stevens would affirm because Congress's special responsibility toward indigenous peoples, implemented through Hawaii's trustee arrangements, makes the qualification political and fiduciary in this context. Morton v. Mancari's recognition of indigenous participation in a governing agency does not depend on transplanting a formal-recognition prerequisite into every analogous relationship. The entrusted resources, beneficiary connection and cultural continuity support the elected trustees' relationship to the community they serve. He accepts the prohibition of actual racial disfranchisement but rejects treating this arrangement as ordinary racial control of public government. In his additional ancestry-and-context discussion, which Ginsburg does not join, Stevens distinguishes inherited community and beneficiary ties here from exclusion designed to perpetuate racial political dominance. Benevolent purpose alone would not excuse a racial franchise; the asserted political relationship does the legal work.

**Justice Ginsburg, dissenting.** She shares Stevens's federal-indigenous relationship ground, without joining every additional ancestry, inherited-property or historical-purpose rationale. In her view, Congress's special responsibility and its delegation to Hawaii adequately support the beneficiary-linked electorate under Morton v. Mancari. She would sustain this arrangement under both the Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendments; the federal relationship is the justification, not a general exception for well-intentioned racial voting restrictions.

## 2000-02-23 — Roe v. Flores-Ortega; No. 98-1441

**Justice Breyer, concurring.** He joins the Court's opinion and remand. This case concerns a guilty plea. In his view, a defendant who has just undergone trial will almost always be entitled to consultation about appeal, while the established terms and effects of a plea can materially affect the contextual inquiry. He emphasizes the Court's practical proof rule: a prisoner unfamiliar with appeal need not identify a particular nonfrivolous issue or prove a sophisticated timely demand. The defendant must still establish a reasonable probability that adequate help would have produced a timely appeal. Smith's missing assistance function differs from the unresolved contents of the conversation here, so his agreement with Smith does not require immediate restoration on this record.

**Justice Souter, joined by Justices Stevens and Ginsburg, concurring in Parts I and III and dissenting from Parts II-A, II-B and IV.** They accept the defendant's control of the appeal choice and the entire causal-loss rule, including the reasonable-probability showing and the rejection of an appellate-success prerequisite. Their disagreement concerns consultation duty and the application to these circumstances. Jones reserves an important decision to the client; Strickland requires meaningful assistance with it; Cronic and Rodriquez protect against deprivation of the necessary assistance and appeal. Counsel ordinarily must provide advice enabling an informed choice before time expires, unless an obvious relinquishment of all relevant appellate claims or an extraordinarily informed defendant makes the function unnecessary. Those conditions are not established here. Asking an uninformed client to signal an informed interest can withhold the very assistance needed to exercise the choice. The findings of little or no understanding and no informed nonappeal, together with the lost appeal and later effort to pursue it, satisfy causal loss in their application. They would affirm the conditional restoration order, without finding a promise, particular advice, appellate merit or a right to a new trial. Their ground applies the preexisting assistance principles, not Smith as newly created prefinality law.

**Justice Ginsburg, concurring in Parts I and III and dissenting from Parts II-A, II-B and IV.** She also joins Souter's writing. She favors a clear obligation to provide meaningful appeal advice to a defendant who has not knowingly relinquished the choice and concludes that the duty was not satisfied on these findings. The trial judge's warning cannot substitute for the understanding the factfinder found lacking. She accepts the shared causal-loss requirement and finds it met in this setting; her disagreement does not eliminate causation or require proof of success on appeal. She would preserve conditional appellate restoration.

## 2000-02-23 — Village of Willowbrook v. Olech; No. 98-1288

**Kennedy, dissenting.** He would vacate and remand rather than affirm or authorize immediate blanket enforcement. Reading the complete delivery-and-death sequence in context, he would confine the law to the intact method and rejects the Court's ordinary-D&E access ground. Potential-life and professional-ethics interests permit meaningful regulation; the physician's preferred technique or every asserted comparative advantage does not control the legislature. He also rejects the residual lesser-burden portion of the complete Casey framework.

He retains the necessary-life-and-health baseline he joined in Casey, Fargo and Voinovich. Voinovich's method analysis materially included Ohio's alternative-method health defense and preserved supported necessary-care applications. Nebraska has no corresponding defense: the exception and later licensing evidence concern only the stated life-saving condition. He therefore would require identification of the circumstances in which available alternatives are clinically inadequate for timely safe and suitable care under that retained baseline. He accepts the credited comparative reductions in the described surgical risks without finding every intact-method use necessary or joining the Court's completed health application.

The lower court may make the needed legal application on the existing record if adequate. His remand orders no automatic new hearing, filing or invented condition. It permits only a Nebraska-law-supported protective construction, preserves supported necessary-care and dependent-sanction protection, and requires ordinary consideration of interim relief. It neither inserts a new exception nor automatically dissolves existing protection. The serious necessary mental-health protection in Voinovich supports his adherence to necessary-care protection, not an imported medical test for every physical-risk claim here.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, dissenting.** They would reverse the constitutional judgment at the granted scope and remand to remove the corresponding injunction. Their principal disagreement is with the constitutional abortion and necessary-health requirements themselves. They acknowledge Casey's complete framework and the actual Fargo and Voinovich holdings as the governing law from which they dissent; they do not replace that law with an obstacle-only description or a different facial-review threshold.

They favor the contextual intact-method construction and ordinary reasonable regulation for potential-life and medical-ethics purposes. The Attorney General's representation is not made binding on every prosecutor, and the credited clinical findings are not declared nonexistent or every alternative equally safe. Their constitutional objection supplies the reason for rejecting the Court's protective conclusion notwithstanding those facts. Agreement in earlier notice and severability propositions does not establish substantive validity, universal knowledge of illegality, good-faith immunity or a cure for every enforcement problem. They seek no new prosecution, damages, administrative sanction, fetal-personhood adjudication or injunction governing viable procedures. Their constitutional objection remains independent of their statutory-construction argument.
