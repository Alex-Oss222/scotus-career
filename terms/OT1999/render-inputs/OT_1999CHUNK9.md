## Event

California Democratic Party v. Jones, No. 99-401. Plenary merits decision; argued April 24, 2000; decided June 26, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, reviewing 169 F.3d 646 (1999), which adopted the District Court's post-trial ruling, 984 F. Supp. 1288 (1997).

The question is whether California may require objecting political parties to select their official nominees for state and congressional office through a blanket primary that permits every voter to cross party lines office by office. Proposition 198 gives each party's nomination to its highest vote recipient even though voters unaffiliated with that party participate on equal terms. Elections for party committees remain confined to party registrants. All four plaintiff parties oppose the compelled participation through their governing rules.

The trial record supports likely crossover voting and effects in some contests, while distinguishing sincere crossover from comparatively rare organized raiding. Its estimates vary by race and denominator; the Court does not adopt a universal numerical threshold. The parties retain advocacy and endorsement, but those activities do not themselves select the legal nominee. No separate Elections Clause claim, presidential-delegate judgment, damages claim or retrospective election challenge is decided.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remands for proceedings consistent with the invalidity of compulsory blanket selection of the objecting parties' official nominees.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 7-2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Ginsburg.

The blanket-primary requirement cannot constitutionally compel these objecting parties to submit their official nominations to equal participation by unaffiliated voters. The lower courts must provide declaratory and prospective injunctive relief fitted to that defect. The Court does not reopen an election, award damages, prescribe a replacement ballot, decide every severability question or invalidate an unpresented voluntary or nonpartisan arrangement.

## Holdings

### 1. Compulsory outsider selection of an official party nominee imposes a severe burden

**Holding and operative rule:** A State severely burdens political association when it requires an objecting party to give voters unaffiliated with that party equal participation in choosing its official nominee through this blanket-primary mechanism. The requirement therefore must be narrowly tailored to serve a compelling state interest; ordinary authority to administer elections does not alone supply that justification.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls each holding in this entry by seven direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Tashjian protects meaningful associational choice while preserving the State's role in a workable election system. Eu protects party governance against state direction, and Democratic Party v. Wisconsin ex rel. La Follette rejects state-imposed participation that overrides a party's protected selection rules. Hurley protects the composition of private expression; it does not exempt every association from ordinary regulation. Selecting the official standard bearer is a central party act even when the State supplies the primary machinery. California's system changes who makes that choice, rather than merely regulating its time, ballot or administration. Sincere crossover can alter nominations and candidates' positions without organized raiding. The parties need not prove organizational collapse or await an election that conclusively changes every nominee. Retained endorsement and advocacy do not restore the lost selection choice. Burdick and Timmons require attention to the actual burden: Timmons left the party able to choose a consenting nominee through the available system, while this law makes outsiders participants in the party's own nomination. The public character of elections permits regulation and requires protection of voters' constitutional rights; it does not remove this distinct associational injury. The Court decides neither all conventional open primaries nor every membership or registration rule.

### 2. The asserted interests do not justify this compulsory nomination mechanism

**Holding and operative rule:** The interests advanced for Proposition 198 do not justify its severe interference with the objecting parties' nomination choices. Participation, choice, competition, privacy and stable elections may support appropriate election rules, but the State has not established a compelling justification and necessary fit for imposing equal outsider participation in the party's own official selection.

**Controlling explanation:** The State may seek representative government and robust participation, but it cannot justify this intrusion merely by preferring more moderate party nominees or nominees responsive to voters who have chosen another association. That purpose changes the party's political choice rather than protecting the electoral process from an independent injury. Giving independents or minority-party voters influence in another party's safe-district primary likewise does not establish a right to select that party's standard bearer. Increased choice, turnout and competition are legitimate electoral aims, yet the necessary connection to compelled party nomination is missing. A nonpartisan winnowing election can select candidates without purporting to select each party's official nominee, and a party may itself invite additional voters. Those possibilities show why broader participation does not require this mechanism; their mention decides no unpresented system. A privacy interest in political affiliation also does not establish an entitlement to participate secretly in every other party's nomination. The stability and fairness arguments do not identify a necessity sufficient to overcome this severe burden. The Court does not substitute a numerical prediction for constitutional analysis or treat every participation objective as impermissible. The defect is the asserted purposes and insufficient fit of this particular compulsory means.

### 3. Relief follows the nomination defect and does not decide other election systems

**Holding and operative rule:** The defense judgment must be reversed and the case remanded for declaratory and prospective injunctive relief against compulsory blanket selection of these objecting parties' official nominees. The ruling leaves ordinary election administration and constitutional voting protections intact and reserves the validity of unpresented primary designs and any remaining severability or implementation questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The plaintiffs established a constitutional defect in the compulsory choice mechanism, not a right to have this Court administer elections. The remedy therefore prevents that compelled selection without prescribing a ballot, reopening completed elections or awarding damages. Timmons preserves ordinary regulation that leaves meaningful lawful nomination choices; the white-primary decisions preserve voters' constitutional protection against exclusion. Describing less intrusive alternatives does not hold that every top-two or nonpartisan system, party invitation or registration condition is valid. The same defect governs the state and congressional nominations presented. No independently presented Elections Clause or national-delegate claim supplies an additional judgment.

## Precedent Treatment

- Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut supplies protection for meaningful party associational choices within a regulated electoral system; this decision does not adopt an unlimited power of party leaders to override members.
- Eu v. San Francisco County Democratic Central Committee supplies the protection against state control of party governance and political choice; ordinary election administration remains available.
- Democratic Party v. Wisconsin ex rel. La Follette supplies protection against forced participation inconsistent with protected party selection rules; the present decision applies that principle to the challenged official nominations without deciding national-delegate questions.
- Burdick v. Takushi supplies burden-sensitive review of election regulations. The severe burden here triggers compelling-interest and narrow-tailoring review rather than the lesser justification sufficient for ordinary mechanics.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party remains controlling as to its nonfusion restriction. Its retained means of choosing a consenting nominee distinguish it from compelled outsider participation in the actual choice.
- Reform Party of Allegheny County v. Allegheny County Department of Elections retains its equal-access holding concerning a timely consensual cross-nomination and its limits on the independent association issue. It does not itself decide the present public-primary question.
- Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston supplies protection for the composition of private expression, with its actual limits; this decision neither exempts ordinary services from neutral regulation nor classifies every regulated association as private expression.
- The white-primary decisions, including Smith v. Allwright and Terry v. Adams, retain their prohibitions on unconstitutional exclusion from electoral processes. Associational protection supplies no exemption from those constitutional commands.

## Law After Decision

California may not enforce the compulsory blanket selection of the objecting parties' official nominees. Election regulation remains subject to the burden-sensitive framework, with strict scrutiny for the severe selection injury established here. Timmons and Reform Party retain their distinct holdings about ballot designation and unequal cross-nomination opportunities. The State may pursue broad electoral participation through lawful, less intrusive designs; this decision does not approve every nonpartisan, top-two, voluntary open-primary or registration system. A party's nomination autonomy remains subject to applicable constitutional voting protections.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring.** He joins the Court's complete rule and remedy. Political parties may choose to broaden their own appeal, change their principles or invite participation. The State's effort to achieve moderation by changing the electorate that selects a party's official representative instead directs the association's political development. The distinction is between the party's own adaptation and a compulsory substitution of decision makers, not hostility to broader democratic participation. His concurrence adds no campaign-finance holding or adjudication of another primary design.

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg, dissenting.** They would affirm on the presented associational claim for both state and congressional nominations. They distinguish a publicly required and administered nominating election from the party's private leadership, endorsement and advocacy decisions. Protecting a party's voluntary candidate choices against exclusion does not necessarily give it a constitutional veto over the qualified electorate in that public primary. Timmons's consensual candidate-access dispute and Hurley's private expressive composition do not erase this boundary; Reform Party's equality holding does not resolve it. Independently, the retained party functions, limited and variable injury supported by the trial record, and participation, choice and representative-government interests adequately justify the law under contextual review. They acknowledge that official nomination differs from endorsement and that sincere crossover can matter; neither observation establishes the severe private-association injury the Court finds. Their position does not depend on implied party consent from a popular vote or on the absence of every crossover effect. The unpresented Elections Clause question remains undecided and supplies no different judgment.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for declaratory and prospective injunctive relief confined to the unconstitutional compulsory nomination mechanism and any ordinary implementation questions properly remaining. No new election, damages award or statutory replacement is ordered. State and congressional nominations receive the same disposition; national-delegate arrangements and the amended presidential reporting provisions are not adjudicated.

## Event

Henerey v. City of St. Charles, School District; Eighth Circuit No. 98-3439, 200 F.3d 1128 (1999). Plenary merits decision; certiorari granted March 20, 2000; submitted May 2, 2000; decided June 26, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit, reviewing its December 29, 1999 affirmance of summary judgment for the school district and officials Cook and Watson.

The questions concern the standard governing this school-sponsored student-government campaign and enforcement of its prior-approval requirement. In the 1997 campaign, Henerey signed the election rules, obtained approval for his slogan, and received an express warning that campaign materials required approval. On election day he distributed condoms bearing campaign stickers without submitting those items for approval. He was disqualified before the officials learned that he had received a majority of the votes. Candy and other conventional campaign objects had received longstanding tacit approval; the parties dispute the inference to draw from their different treatment. The principal identified the missing approval as the reason for disqualification. The appellate court separately offered a sexual-content rationale, without a trial finding that it was the principal's actual reason.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated in merits consideration and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment on the supported prior-approval and campaign-administration ground.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 5-4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The defense summary judgment remains in place. The Court does not rely on a hypothetical sexual-content justification, decide alternative immunity or municipal-attribution defenses, validate every provision of the school's distribution policy, or authorize suppression of independent advocacy outside this school-sponsored campaign. No installation in office, damages award or further trial is ordered.

## Holdings

### 1. This supervised campaign is governed by the school-sponsored-expression standard

**Holding and operative rule:** A school-created student-government campaign administered through advisers, campaign rules and material approval as an educational exercise is school-sponsored expression governed primarily by Hazelwood's legitimate-pedagogical-reason standard. Independent student advocacy outside that program retains the ordinary student-speech protections; the school's location alone does not establish sponsorship.

**Authority:** Parts I-II of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices, control Holdings 1-2 unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Hazelwood permits schools to control expression in activities bearing their sponsorship when the control reasonably serves legitimate educational purposes. The supervised election teaches civic participation within established rules; the contract, adviser and approval process establish that function here. Tinker protects a student's independent expression and does not make every educational program an unrestricted forum. Nor does the school's sponsorship make every rival candidate's proposition the government's own assertion. Boring concerns institutional instructional control and does not extinguish a student's personal rights. Good News/Good Sports distinguishes genuine private access from the government's program; Santa Fe turns on its specific official religious-choice arrangement, not the word election alone. The actual function of this campaign, rather than a generic school label, supplies the governing standard. The Court decides no off-program restriction or general student-election rule detached from these features.

### 2. Genuine campaign review can serve legitimate pedagogical purposes

**Holding and operative rule:** Within this sponsored campaign, the school may enforce clear, viewpoint-neutral material-approval requirements reasonably related to fair procedure, equal campaign access and responsible administration, including a reasonable disqualification for a material violation. The rule cannot serve as a pretext for suppressing a candidate's political viewpoint; an imagined lawful reason cannot defeat a supported material dispute about the actual ground of enforcement.

**Controlling explanation:** Hazelwood permits a genuine educational condition, not every restriction an official might describe as educational. Settle requires examination of the actual ground and preserves ordinary proof and causation rules. If protected conduct is established as a substantial or motivating factor, the separate same-decision defense requires proof by a preponderance; mere existence of an available rule does not automatically establish that defense. Advance review can promote evenhanded administration without proof of Tinker disruption or a least-restrictive-means showing for each campaign item. The stated policy calls for submission by the preceding school day but expressly permits more than one school day for difficult material; it is not a guarantee of approval within twenty-four hours. Approval of a slogan does not approve every object carrying it. The Court sustains the genuine approval requirement as applied in this supervised campaign, without endorsing every content category, exception, distribution location or stamping requirement in the broader policy. It adopts no universal viewpoint-neutrality command governing every curricular content choice and no permission to rely on audience hostility as disruption.

### 3. This record supports the actual process ground and the campaign consequence

**Holding and operative rule:** On this summary-judgment record, the signed campaign undertaking, express all-materials warning and admitted failure to submit the new item establish a lawful actual approval-process ground, and the different approval history of conventional items does not create a reasonable material inference of viewpoint pretext. Disqualification for this material participation-condition violation was reasonable, so the defense judgment is affirmed without the appellate court's alternative sexual-content rationale.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, controls by five direct votes. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer disagree with this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Settle forbids replacing a disputed actual reason with possible pedagogy, but it does not require further proceedings whenever a disfavored message accompanies a rule violation. The principal's stated ground was the missing approval. The advance warning and signed undertaking distinguish this enforcement from a concealed condition or approval of the object itself. Customary tacit approval for conventional campaign items matters because it supplies a different approval history, not because comparators must be identical before they can be relevant. The contrary inference from the controversial item and differential treatment is insufficient here when the record is considered favorably to Henerey; the Court does not purport to find his motives or the officials' credibility after trial. Removal from this school-created contest reasonably enforces its material campaign condition. The result requires neither a finding that condoms are necessarily obscene nor a conclusion that Henerey urged irresponsible sexual activity. No disruption or parental confrontation is newly found. The Court makes no alternative finding that the school proved a same-decision defense to an established impermissible motive. Because the actual process ground sustains judgment, separate personal-immunity, municipal-policy and remedial questions need not be decided.

## Precedent Treatment

- Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier governs this sponsored educational activity through genuine, reasonably related pedagogical control; it is not extended to all expression occurring at school.
- Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District retains its protection for independent student advocacy; its disruption formulation is not the primary test for this sponsored campaign.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board controls actual educational purpose, material summary-judgment inferences and the distinct substantial-factor and same-decision inquiries. The Court applies it without relaxing its evidentiary burdens.
- Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education supplies institutional control over instruction within its teacher-specific scope; it does not eliminate the student's own constitutional protection.
- Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of the City of Ladue retains its actual-private-access and program-function distinctions; no private forum is created by analogy alone.
- Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe retains its holding concerning the particular official religious-choice mechanism; student voting alone does not determine sponsorship or attribution here.
- Bethel School District No. 403 v. Fraser supports practical school disciplinary authority within its proper setting. No lewdness finding or general condemnation of a sexual subject supplies the present disposition.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle supplies the distinct causation and same-decision burdens preserved in Settle; the Court does not deem that defense independently proved merely because a rule was violated.

## Law After Decision

This sponsored campaign may be governed by genuine, clear, viewpoint-neutral approval conditions and reasonable consequences for material violations. The particular defense judgment stands on the actual process ground. Settle's actual-purpose and causation rules remain intact, and a materially supported pretext inference would require its ordinary treatment. Neither broad curricular viewpoint discrimination nor a general right to distribute unapproved campaign objects follows. Independent student advocacy and defendant-specific damages defenses retain their separate legal frameworks.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They accept the sponsored-election classification and legitimacy of genuine campaign approval in Parts I-II. They would vacate because the approved slogan, differing treatment of campaign objects and removal from the contest permit a material inference about what was enforced and why. Settle requires that inference to be resolved through ordinary proceedings rather than an appellate court's possible sexual-content explanation. The explicit warning and conventional approval practice are substantial defense evidence, but do not conclusively eliminate the favorable inference for them. Stevens's notice and punishment concerns are included in their treatment of the sanction; no additional notice writing is necessary.

They find neither pretext nor liability established and recognize no substantive entitlement to distribute condoms, universal private-forum rule, least-restrictive-sanction requirement or right to office. A remand could permit another properly supported summary disposition and would preserve district attribution, official and personal capacity, and any properly presented qualified-immunity question under the law at the April 1997 conduct date. Later Boring or Santa Fe law cannot supply retroactive notice. Expiration of the office does not automatically extinguish an otherwise live damages claim, while prospective relief still requires its own live controversy. They would order no trial, discovery, damages amount or reinstatement as an automatic consequence.

## Procedure After Action

The challenged defense judgment remains affirmed, with no remand. The Court's decision ends the presented constitutional challenge to this campaign enforcement at the specified scope. It adjudicates no different independent-speech application and no alternative immunity or municipal-liability ground.

## Event

Mobil Oil Exploration & Producing Southeast, Inc. v. United States / Marathon Oil Co. v. United States, Nos. 99-244 and 99-253. Plenary merits decision; argued March 22, 2000; decided June 26, 2000. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, reviewing its revised May 13, 1999 judgment, 177 F.3d 1331, which reversed the Court of Federal Claims' liability and subsequent bonus-restitution judgments.

Each petitioner paid more than $78 million for approximately one-third interests in five offshore North Carolina leases. Four leases were included in the Manteo Unit final exploration plan submitted August 20, 1990; no plan was submitted for the fifth. The leases conveyed conditional exclusive rights for a ten-year primary term with specified continuations and an incorporated regulatory process, not a guarantee of production. Congress enacted the Outer Banks Protection Act on August 18, 1990. North Carolina separately objected to coastal-consistency certifications, and Commerce refused an override in September 1994. The contract appeal concerns whether the federal process undertaking was materially repudiated and whether restitution follows; it does not review the administrative refusal to override the State or a takings claim.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

In both dockets, the Court reverses the Federal Circuit's rejection of restitution entitlement and remands for the genuinely remaining, properly preserved amount and restoration questions.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded in both dockets, 8-1.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens.

The petitioners are entitled to restitution of proved up-front bonus consideration for the repudiated bargain, subject to return of corresponding rights and lawful credits or offsets. The Federal Circuit must address its previously unreached, properly preserved monetary objections. The Court does not set a rounded dollar award, reopen every benefits finding, order a new hearing regardless of need, award the denied rentals or speculative profits, or authorize interest without an independent legal basis. No plan, drilling permit, state concurrence, Commerce override or production entitlement is ordered.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer | Common contract and actual-breach ground | Clear continuing undertaking, incorporated conditions and failure of submitted-plan approval obligation, Holding 1 |
| Same opinion, Parts II-V | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer | Restitution and remand | Material and anticipatory repudiation, restoration, election and bounded monetary remedy, Holdings 2-5 |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens | No additional join | Joins I; rejects restitution | Actual breach does not amount to total repudiation; no fifth-lease anticipatory ground; only properly presented and proved delay damages remain possible |

All nine join the contract construction and actual submitted-plan breach; eight join material repudiation and restitution. The common rationale requires no resolution of the distinct noncontrolling Winstar canon theories and no narrowest-grounds synthesis.

## Holdings

### 1. The leases contain a clear continuing process undertaking that the Government breached

**Holding and operative rule:** These leases incorporate a continuing obligation to provide the specified OCSLA exploration-plan approval process, including approval, after necessary modifications, of a compliant plan within thirty days of submission unless serious harm to life (including aquatic life), property, minerals, national security or defense, or the marine, coastal or human environment cannot be avoided by modification and requires disapproval. The Government's refusal to provide that approval because of OBPA's new conditions breached the undertaking; independent permits and state coastal-consistency requirements do not make the federal promise disappear.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Court opinion, joined by all other Justices, controls this holding unanimously.

**Controlling explanation:** Winstar requires an authorized actual undertaking and an examination of the risk the Government assumed; a valuable regulatory opportunity alone is insufficient. Section 1's incorporation of OCSLA, sections 302-303 of the Department of Energy Organization Act, existing regulations and limited future regulations, read with the term and conditional operating rights, establish a future process promise. The catchall does not transfer every later statutory change to the lessees and nullify those distinctions. Future regulations for waste prevention, resource conservation and correlative rights remain incorporated. Sections 9-10 require approved plans and compliance; section 13 preserves OCSLA suspension and cancellation, and separately suspension on the Defense Secretary's recommendation during war or a national emergency declared by Congress or the President, with just compensation. Section 19 reserves ungranted rights: other geological and geophysical exploration and other-mineral operations may not unreasonably interfere with or endanger lease operations; easements may serve other lands' work or product shipment. A defense designation requires the Defense Secretary and presidential approval; surface operations then require Defense concurrence. A resulting suspension also suspends rentals and royalties, extends the term and carries constitutionally required compensation. None supplies a blanket no-liability reservation. OCSLA permits suspensions at the lessee's request in the national interest, for proper development or transportation facilities and separate suspensions for threatened serious, irreparable or immediate harm to life, property, minerals or the marine, coastal or human environment; neither predicate is newly found merely because further environmental study is useful. A qualifying suspension extends the affected lease or permit for an equivalent period, except when the suspension results from gross negligence or willful violation of the lease, permit or applicable regulations. Approval remains conditional on compliance, necessary modifications and the specified serious-harm exception. Yet Interior's completed evaluation did not supply the legal approval withheld under the new Act. North Carolina made actual objections here; operations still require a lawful state-consistency resolution and separate discharge and drilling permissions. No concurrence or permit entitlement is found.

### 2. The statutory replacement of the promised process was material repudiation

**Holding and operative rule:** OBPA's prohibition and new prerequisites materially withdrew an essential part of the bargained-for federal process and constituted repudiation, rather than an ordinary permissible delay under the incorporated terms. That repudiation applies to the four submitted-plan leases and anticipatorily to the same promised future process on the fifth lease, without inventing a submitted plan or missed deadline on that tract.

**Authority:** Parts II-V of Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, control Holdings 2-5 by eight direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Roehm v. Horst supplies the contractual consequence of an anticipatory refusal to render essential promised performance. The Act barred the relevant approvals and drilling until the later of October 1, 1991 or forty-five days of continuous congressional session after the required report and certification. Continuity is broken only by adjournment of Congress without a further date, and recesses when either House adjourns for more than three days to a day certain are excluded from the count. The Secretary must consider the scientific panel and explain disagreements, but the panel has no absolute veto. The Court does not treat the gate as forty-five calendar days or an indefinite ban, or attribute every later delay to it. Even at its actual initial scope, the new prohibition and conditions substantially replaced the paid-for process. The statutory and regulatory powers already incorporated had different predicates; their existence did not permit this categorical substitution without contract consequences. Further administrative effort and preserved lease time had value, but did not deliver the withheld timely approval process. Congress retained power to change the law; under Winstar, that sovereign authority does not erase monetary responsibility for the particular risk actually assumed. The fifth lease was subject to the same categorical refusal, making its claim anticipatory rather than an invented completed application breach. The holding does not turn every delay or legislative adverse effect into total repudiation.

### 3. Restitution does not require proof that the project would have earned profits

**Holding and operative rule:** After this material repudiation, the injured lessees may elect restoration of consideration paid for the promised performance without proving that all independent approvals would have been obtained or profitable production would have occurred. Independent regulatory obstacles bear on the actual bargain and any cognizable benefit accounting, but do not automatically defeat restitution by making expectation profits unprovable.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. Behan distinguishes restoration after the promisor prevents the agreed undertaking from compensation measured by expected profits. The companies bought a conditional opportunity through an identified process, not certain oil or immunity from North Carolina's objections. The Government's strongest response is that those objections prevented drilling anyway. That may defeat a claim that the federal delay caused lost operating profits; it does not establish that the Government performed the separate process for which it accepted consideration. The materiality inquiry considers the bargain as a whole, including existing evaluation and preserved time, but those benefits do not cure the repudiated essential undertaking found here. Winstar's contractual responsibility therefore operates with ordinary remedy limits, not a guarantee of success. No cancellation-compensation award under OCSLA is substituted for the contract remedy: the Court makes no statutory cancellation finding or compensation award.

### 4. The supplied continued-performance defense does not bar restoration

**Holding and operative rule:** Urging the repudiating party to perform, submitting the requested plan, pursuing independent approvals or seeking extensions does not by itself surrender the injured party's restitution election. Acceptance of significant substituted performance may matter, but the documented acts here do not establish acceptance of the withheld federal process as fulfilled or a waiver of restoration.

**Controlling explanation:** Roehm and Pence v. Langdon preserve the distinction between seeking performance or retraction and conduct that makes restoration unavailable through an effective contrary election or acceptance of material substitute performance. The companies' submission, unsuccessful override efforts and MOU-related extensions kept the original opportunity alive; they did not obtain the approval process the Government had repudiated. The 1992 restitution suit asserted restoration rather than relinquishing it. Administrative work and additional lease time are not denied; they remain relevant at their lawful scope without being treated as performance of the withheld undertaking. The record also distinguishes earlier rent-free suspensions from the June 1995 letter's rent-bearing, five-year-capped suspension. No later renewal, continuous rent exemption or payment history through decision is assumed. The defense fails on the documented acts even with their real value acknowledged. The Court finds no unproved retraction, settlement, abandonment, forfeiture of a preservation objection or attribution of every act by one petitioner to the other. It does not reopen entitlement merely to decide an unsupported election theory.

### 5. The monetary remand is confined to genuine restoration and amount questions

**Holding and operative rule:** The petitioners' restitution entitlement requires return of proved bonus consideration with legally required restoration of corresponding rights and any established lawful credit or offset. The Federal Circuit must resolve its unreached, properly preserved calculation objections on the actual record; recovery of rentals, profits or interest does not follow automatically, and interest requires independent authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Restitution restores the exchanged value rather than giving both the price and the retained contract rights without accounting. The Court of Federal Claims separately denied rental recovery and, after a benefits hearing, found no cognizable offset established. The Federal Circuit never reached the Government's monetary challenges because it rejected liability. Reversal therefore permits review of genuinely remaining objections; it does not erase the existing benefits determination or command a new evidentiary hearing regardless of what remains disputed. Approximate descriptions of payments cannot substitute for the actual judgment and supported calculations. No decree compels a regulatory approval, invalidates North Carolina's objection, awards speculative production profits or converts OBPA's later repeal into performance at the promised time.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Winstar Corp. supplies actual-undertaking and assumed-risk responsibility while preserving sovereign regulatory power and separate remedy analysis. Its noncontrolling broad sovereign-acts and unmistakability theories are not made controlling here; the particular continuing promise is clear even under the requirement to identify one unmistakably.

- United States v. Behan supplies the distinction between recovery of value after prevented performance and proof of anticipated profits; independent obstacles to profitable production do not erase the restitution claim established here.
- Pence v. Langdon supplies the distinction between preservation of a restoration right and conduct inconsistent with that election; the Court rejects waiver from the documented acts without assuming an unproved acceptance of significant substitute performance.

## Law After Decision

A clearly promised federal regulatory process can be an essential contractual performance even though the contract guarantees no approval of other permits or profitable operations. Congress may change regulatory law while the Government remains financially responsible for the specific risk it undertook. Material withdrawal of that process supports restitution distinct from expectation damages; ordinary election, restoration and amount rules remain applicable. The decision does not create a general right to compensation for unfavorable regulation or adopt a comprehensive new sovereign-acts or unmistakability doctrine.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the clear contractual undertaking and recognizes actual breach of the submitted-plan approval requirement. His position does not depend on an assumed continuously rent-free suspension or a renewal after the documented five-year cap, and he makes no waiver or contrary-election finding. His disagreement concerns total-breach materiality and restoration, not merely unresolved arithmetic.

## Event

Boy Scouts of America v. Dale, No. 99-699. Plenary merits decision; argued April 26, 2000; decided June 28, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of New Jersey, reviewing 160 N.J. 562, 734 A.2d 1196 (1999), which affirmed and remanded.

The question is whether New Jersey's Law Against Discrimination may require retention of James Dale as an adult assistant scoutmaster consistently with the freedoms of speech and association. Dale was an Eagle Scout and became an assistant scoutmaster in March 1989. His adult membership was revoked after a July 1990 article identified his Rutgers Lesbian/Gay Alliance role and participation in a seminar about gay teenagers. The article did not identify him as a Scout leader or announce contrary instruction within Scouting.

New Jersey construed its statute to cover the organization and adult leadership as a privilege or advantage despite the absence of a single fixed location. It rejected the distinctly-private exemption on actual selectivity, the exemption for educational facilities operated or maintained by bona fide religious or sectarian institutions on the organization's nonsectarian character, and the reservation for a natural parent or person in loco parentis to direct a child's education and upbringing because temporary supervision was not maintaining, rearing and educating the child. Those state-law constructions do not decide the federal constitutional defense. The separate duplicative common-law claim remains dismissed.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the state judgment against the presented federal defenses. The existing state remand may proceed within its established scope.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 5-4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The Law Against Discrimination may operate in the adult-leadership application presented. The Court awards no damages amount, prescribes no new trial and substitutes no membership-only compromise for the actual dispute. The rejection of the intimate-association defense remains undisturbed, but only four Justices adopt the separate reasoning in Part IV; Stone-Zsela joins the disposition on that defense without adding a ground. ## Holdings

### 1. An access-law defense requires an actual message, a relevant role and material expressive impairment

**Holding and operative rule:** A generally applicable public-accommodations law may govern an open civic organization's admission or retention of an otherwise qualified participant unless the organization establishes an actual message, shows that the particular role communicates it, and demonstrates that inclusion materially alters expression attributable to the group. An exclusion policy alone does not prove that connection; establishing a material burden would present further questions the Court need not decide here.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls all three holdings by five direct votes. Part IV supplies no controlling rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Roberts, Rotary and New York State Club Association require examination of real expressive impairment, rather than exemption for every activity of an association that engages in some expression. Moral instruction, including quiet persuasion and teaching by adult example, is protected. The inquiry therefore respects the organization's chosen values while independently examining whether this role and inclusion affect its actual expression. Hurley protects control of a particular private expressive presentation without demanding a single theme or unanimous beliefs. It does not make every asserted exclusion an expressive presentation. The Court requires neither a narrow founding purpose nor uniformity on all issues. Genuine instruction can establish a message; silence itself can be protected, and a bona fide direct-speech claim remains distinct. What is insufficient is treating a status-based exclusion as self-proving impairment of a message the organization has not actually shown to be its own expressive endeavor. Constitutional protection does not depend on whether the Court or society approves that message.

### 2. This record does not establish the necessary message-role-impairment connection

**Holding and operative rule:** On the record presented, retaining Dale as an assistant scoutmaster has not been shown materially to alter a defined message attributable to the Boy Scouts or to compel a contrary expressive presentation. The organization's moral instruction and authority over genuine role-related conduct remain protected, but neither Dale's status nor his external advocacy alone establishes the missing connection here.

**Controlling explanation:** The oath, law, adult declaration and teaching by example establish an expressive program. They do not by themselves establish the claimed position about homosexuality or how this adult's inclusion contradicts instruction actually conveyed. Ordinary youth materials do not express that position, and sexual instruction is referred to parents and spiritual advisers. The 1978 exclusion statement and later 1991-1993 statements and testimony are relevant but must be considered in their sequence and institutional use; they are not all litigation inventions, and the 1991 statement predates suit. Neither those later materials nor the other evidence establishes the requisite connection for the completed expulsion or prospective application here. The Court independently reviews the intertwined expression and attribution questions while respecting credibility findings. It accepts that a chosen adult teacher can communicate by example; it rejects automatic attribution from this person's identity. Dale proposed no contrary Scouting lesson and gained no right to disregard lawful program instructions. Hurley's identified bannered contingent altered the organizer's particular presentation; the same predicate is not established here. Jones concerned an established transfer of official-nominee selection to outsiders, not an unproved inference about this leader's message. A leader who publicly epitomizes a position demonstrably incompatible with the group's actual advocacy could present a different case.

### 3. Neutral access enforcement may proceed without balancing away an established expressive burden

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the necessary material expressive impairment is not established, the State may enforce its ordinary nondiscrimination requirement in this adult-leadership application without a further balance against a proved serious expressive burden. The judgment leaves genuine spokespeople, materially expressive leadership roles, direct expressive presentations, intimate relationships and neutral discipline for role-related conduct subject to their own constitutional protections.

**Controlling explanation:** Roberts and Rotary recognize the State's concrete access interest without making equality an automatic answer to every serious expressive injury. The Court does not reach a compelling-interest override of such an injury here, because the necessary premise is absent. Nor does it impose a duty to host contrary teaching or a debate the organization has not chosen. The state court's statutory coverage is accepted as state law; its public-accommodation label does not alone defeat a federal defense. Public assistance and a federal charter likewise do not establish state action by themselves. The judgment concerns the actual adult appointment, not all youth members, customers, employees or future applicants. A membership-only remedy would require resolution of the organization's contention that adult membership is leadership service; the Court does not invent that compromise. The state judgment and existing remand stand, with no new damages award, automatic trial or revival of the dismissed common-law claim.

## Precedent Treatment

- Roberts v. United States Jaycees supplies the actual expressive-burden inquiry and preserves both protected association and ordinary access enforcement; the Court does not decide that an antidiscrimination interest overrides a proved severe burden here.
- Board of Directors of Rotary International v. Rotary Club of Duarte supplies attention to the association's actual activities, relationships and claimed impairment; a statutory label alone does not resolve the federal issue.
- New York State Club Association v. City of New York supplies the distinction between real expressive injury and a general exemption from access regulation; its application-specific limits remain.
- Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston remains controlling for private expressive composition, independent review and credibility deference. Its rule does not demand a single parade theme, and its direct expressive predicate is not established by mere status inclusion here.
- California Democratic Party v. Jones retains its severe-burden holding for compelled outsider selection of official party nominees. The present record has not established the analogous material control of expression; Jones does not automatically decide every leadership or admission dispute.

## Law After Decision

The presented adult-leadership application of New Jersey's law stands. An expressive-association defense requires the actual message, role and material attributable impairment identified in the Court's rule. General moral education is protected but does not make an exclusion policy self-validating. The decision preserves direct-speech protection, genuine message-bearing roles and role-related discipline. It creates no new controlling intimate-association test and no rule that equality defeats an established serious expressive burden.

## Separate Writings

**Souter's Part IV, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer.** These four additionally require the particular claimed position to have been unequivocally adopted and actually advocated over the relevant period through customary organizational channels. That is their stronger proof requirement for this expressive-association defense, not a rule joined by Stone-Zsela or a demand for a single founding theme, unanimity or constant public campaigning. They distinguish Hurley's direct expressive presentation and preserve a genuine claim to remain silent. The common message-role-impairment requirement and this record's failure to establish it are sufficient for the Court's decision; their additional requirement is noncontrolling. These four also reject the intimate-association defense based on the organization's actual size, broad recruitment, openness, selectivity and relationships. They distinguish genuine personal intimacy from the mere existence of adult supervision or smaller constituent groups and do not rest on the state's public-accommodation label alone. This is a four-Justice rationale, not a new rule of the Court. Stone-Zsela supplies no additional reason on this defense.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** They would reverse the compelled adult-leadership application. The organization teaches chosen moral values through the example of adults entrusted to instruct children, a protected expressive activity under Roberts and Hurley. Its official position, adult commitments and Dale's publicly identified contrary position establish material impairment for them. Courts should respect the association's account of its values and the consequences of forced leadership while independently ensuring that more than a bare exclusion assertion is present. They would require neither an explicit lesson in every handbook, unanimity among members nor empirical proof that particular spoken words changed. Referring sexual education to parents does not eliminate an adult example's communicative role.

They distinguish ordinary nondiscriminatory access from direct interference with that choice. O'Brien's incidental-regulation approach cannot dispose of the direct burden they find, and the asserted access interests do not justify it under the compelling-interest and less-restrictive-means framework. Kennedy's concern for voluntary institutional development is reflected in the distinction between a group's own change and state-compelled alteration; the dissent endorses no particular moral belief and grants no universal exemption. Dale remains free to advocate his own views, but those freedoms do not establish a right to this expressive role. They would reverse and remand within this adult-leadership dispute, without deciding intimate association, other classes of participants or a final damages amount.

## Procedure After Action

The New Jersey judgment is affirmed, and its existing remand proceeds within the adjudicated adult-leadership application. No final damages amount, new trial command or membership-only relief is supplied. The duplicative common-law claim remains dismissed. No other membership, employment or service application is decided.

## Event

Hill v. Colorado, No. 98-1856. Plenary merits decision; argued January 19, 2000; decided June 28, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Colorado, reviewing Hill v. Thomas, 973 P.2d 1246 (1999), which affirmed summary judgment for the State on a facial preenforcement challenge.

Petitioners seek declaratory and injunctive relief against Colorado section 18-9-122(3)'s restriction on knowing, nonconsensual close approaches for specified communicative purposes on public ways and sidewalks near health-care entrance doors. They engage in sidewalk counseling about abortion and its alternatives and claim that the rule burdens peaceful initiation, leafleting and conversation. The issues are content classification, appropriate speech scrutiny, practical fit and channels, and the distinct facial vagueness, overbreadth and consent-as-licensing objections.

The legislative materials describe crowding, intimidation, obstruction and confrontation; they establish no such misconduct by these petitioners. The Colorado Supreme Court's authoritative construction requires an approach by the speaker and imposes no retreat duty on a stationary person. Its conclusion that alternative communication is adequate remains a constitutional judgment for this Court to review. No conviction, damages award or individualized injunction is before the Court, and no new forfeiture is imposed on the content-classification question passed upon below.

The Court affirms the Colorado judgment rejecting the facial challenge at the statute's authoritative scope. Discriminatory-enforcement and materially broader as-applied challenges remain available.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The defense summary judgment remains in place. The Court orders no criminal punishment, damages, new injunction, substitute distance, compulsory retreat or speech-free zone. Parts I-III have six direct rationale joins; Part IV has five. Scalia and Thomas do not join Kennedy's additional vagueness rationale, and no narrowest-grounds combination is needed.

## Holdings

### 1. The law regulates a specified knowing approach, not all speech near a facility

**Holding and operative rule:** Colorado section 18-9-122(3) applies to a person who knowingly initiates an approach within eight feet of another person without that person's consent, for passing a leaflet or handbill to that person, displaying a sign to that person, or engaging that person in oral protest, education or counseling, on a public way or sidewalk within a radius of 100 feet from any entrance door to a facility legally licensed, certified, authorized or permitted to administer medical treatment in Colorado. Under the binding state construction, a stationary speaker need not retreat when someone else approaches; the law does not prohibit mere presence, all speech or signs within the area, or consensual communication.

**Authority:** Parts I-III of Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, control Holdings 1-3 by six direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The Colorado Supreme Court's construction identifies what the legislature has prohibited; the federal constitutional question concerns that actual law. Liability requires the speaker's knowing approach for a listed purpose without consent. Another person's movement toward a stationary speaker does not create a duty to withdraw. The protected recipient need not be a patient, and the public-area rule extends around any qualifying entrance; neither limitation is silently rewritten to narrow the statute. At the same time, the statute creates no general speech-free area or power to silence a speaker addressing other willing people. No punishment without the required knowledge is approved. The separate obstruction offense in subsection (2), local-law permission in subsection (5), and civil-liability cross-reference in subsection (6) do not enlarge subsection (3)'s elements. Review of this facial challenge adjudicates no criminal charge, unpresented local ordinance or separate civil cause of action.

### 2. The actual approach restriction is content neutral

**Holding and operative rule:** At its authoritative scope, subsection (3) regulates the manner of a close physical approach across subjects and viewpoints for access and treatment-related reasons independent of disagreement with the speaker's ideas. It is therefore reviewed as a content-neutral legislative time, place and manner restriction under Ward, rather than as a rule selecting a disfavored message or an individualized injunction.

**Controlling explanation:** Ward v. Rock Against Racism requires examination of the regulation's operation and justification. Cincinnati v. Discovery Network and City of Erie v. Pap's A.M. prevent a benign objective or conduct label from concealing communicative targeting. The present law imposes a real burden on protected sidewalk advocacy, but does not require a change in the speaker's message: the same words and signs remain lawful from a stationary position and beyond the restricted approach. The listed communicative purposes identify the encounters governed, without selecting a subject or position within them. Looking at words to distinguish purposeful counseling from casual conversation does not, in this setting, make their viewpoint the basis of liability. Petitioners correctly observe that other approaches remain permitted. That distinction must be assessed with the particular close-delivery mechanism and the supported access concern, not treated as automatically neutral because of the State's stated motive. Unlike Erie's communicative hierarchy, these terms and their construction address the physical encounter itself. The Court adopts no general right to avoid unpopular ideas on a public sidewalk, and emotional offense at abortion advocacy is not an independent noncommunicative harm justifying suppression. Madsen and Schenck retain their distinct, more demanding rules for speech-burdening injunctions.

### 3. The limited approach rule satisfies Ward's fit and alternative-channel requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Taken together, the eight-foot approach distance, 100-foot entrance-area limit, knowledge requirement, consent condition and binding no-retreat construction make subsection (3) narrowly tailored to significant access and medical-treatment interests while leaving ample usable communication channels. The facial challenge therefore fails under Ward, without establishing that every personal-distance rule is valid or foreclosing discriminatory-enforcement and materially broader as-applied challenges.

**Controlling explanation:** Ward requires significant legitimate interests, narrow tailoring and ample alternative channels, but not the least restrictive conceivable means. The legislative record describes crowding, obstruction and confrontational close encounters affecting access and treatment. It does not establish misconduct by these petitioners. A preventive approach limit directly addresses that physical mechanism; the State need not await completed obstruction in every encounter. Subsection (2)'s separate obstruction prohibition and ordinary conduct laws remain substantial alternatives, but do not establish that no additional, limited preventive rule can be justified.

The Court recognizes the burden on initiating a quiet exchange and taking a few steps to place a leaflet in a recipient's hand. The permitted channels must be practical, not merely theoretical: speakers can remain stationary and offer literature to passing people, display signs, speak audibly from a lawful distance, request consent, or converse with willing people who approach them. These opportunities are not identical to every preferred encounter, and amplification is not assumed to replace private conversation in every case. Their combined availability satisfies the present inquiry at the defined scope.

Madsen invalidated a 300-foot advance-invitation restraint under the injunction-specific standard; Schenck invalidated moving fifteen-foot restraints whose practical operation impaired advocacy. Those burdens and remedial records differ materially from this smaller legislative approach rule with no retreat duty. Neither decision establishes a universal permissible distance. Schenck's qualified fixed-zone refusal provision likewise supplies no general listener veto. The Court approves the actual combination of limitations, not eight feet alone, a medical-vicinity exception to public-forum protection, or restrictions justified only by hostility to a message.

### 4. The law is not facially vague at its authoritative scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The knowing speaker-initiated approach, ordinary communicative-purpose terms, consent condition, measurable geographic limits and binding no-retreat construction give adequate notice and constrain enforcement sufficiently to defeat this facial vagueness challenge. This conclusion does not decide every ambiguous encounter or approve punishment outside those elements.

**Authority:** Part IV of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg, controls Holdings 4-6 by five direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of each ancillary facial claim but joins no additional rationale in Part IV.

**Controlling explanation:** Grayned v. City of Rockford supplies the requirements of understandable notice and limits on arbitrary enforcement without demanding mathematical certainty from ordinary language. The present terms identify an affirmative approach for a specified communicative purpose, not accidental proximity or a stationary person's failure to move. Knowledge matters to notice but is not a universal cure for an undefined prohibition. Here it operates with familiar terms, measurable distances and the state court's authoritative construction. The contrary argument identifies mixed encounters, difficult consent signals and uncertain applications of education or counseling. Those issues may matter in a concrete case, but do not show that the defined prohibition as a whole lacks ordinary meaning or leaves enforcement unconstrained. The Court supplies no new statutory definition, exemption for an invented class of speakers, or rule making every difficult boundary question constitutionally irrelevant.

### 5. The facial record does not establish substantial unconstitutional overbreadth

**Holding and operative rule:** Coverage of all speakers and recipients in the specified public areas around qualifying health-care entrances does not, on this record and at the law's authoritative scope, establish a substantial unconstitutional sweep in relation to its valid applications. The facial overbreadth claim fails, while appropriate challenges to discriminatory enforcement, materially different applications, wide entrances or multipurpose buildings remain open.

**Controlling explanation:** Broadrick v. Oklahoma requires substantial unconstitutional reach in relation to a law's legitimate operation before facial invalidation on behalf of others. The approach rule is not exempt from that review simply because it is a time, place and manner regulation. Its general coverage does burden some harmless contacts and persons unconnected to treatment, which must be considered rather than erased by relabeling everyone a patient. But the defined entrance-area and approach mechanism, practical channels and preserved consent supply the legitimate applications established here. Petitioners' claim that virtually all protected communication is forbidden misreads the law: stationary offers, signs, distant speech and willing exchanges remain available. Neither general coverage by itself nor hypothetical difficult settings establish the necessary substantial unconstitutional sweep. A later concrete challenge remains governed by its actual facts and scope.

### 6. Consent to a close approach is not an official speech license

**Holding and operative rule:** Requiring the approached person's consent for the defined knowing close approach does not create an official licensing system or give that person authority to silence other speech, other willing listeners or stationary advocacy. The consent-based prior-restraint challenge therefore fails at this statutory scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Madsen and Schenck distinguish direct place or manner rules from an official permission system, while still requiring substantive scrutiny of the burden imposed. No official here licenses a message in advance or decides whether its viewpoint deserves permission. The other person's consent governs only the close approach described in subsection (3), not the speaker's ability to remain, speak from a lawful position or communicate with someone else. That limited operation answers the proposed listener-veto theory without creating a general right to suppress ideas that a passer-by dislikes. The rule's constitutional fit remains established independently under Ward; the absence of an official licensing mechanism would not alone make a broader approach restraint valid.

## Precedent Treatment

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism supplies content-neutral legislative time, place and manner review: significant interests, narrow tailoring and ample usable channels, without a least-restrictive-means requirement. It is applied to the actual combined limits of this approach rule.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network supplies the limit that a benign objective alone does not cure speech-selective operation. The Court distinguishes this delivery mechanism while retaining that requirement.
- City of Erie v. Pap's A.M. retains its rule against incidental-burden treatment when operation and justification principally target communicative expression. Its adult-entertainment holding does not decide this different, authoritatively confined approach mechanism, and its separate opinions are not combined into a broader rule.
- Madsen v. Women's Health Center retains the more demanding injunction-fit standard and its invalidation of the 300-foot advance-invitation restraint. The smaller statutory approach rule and preserved channels do not revive that injunction or authorize another unsupported restraint.
- Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network retains its invalidation of the particular moving fifteen-foot restraints and its qualified fixed-zone refusal provision. Neither supplies a universal numerical rule, a retreat duty here or a general power to silence an unwilling listener's surroundings.
- Grayned v. City of Rockford supplies ordinary notice and constrained-enforcement requirements without mathematical certainty. It supports Part IV's facial clarity conclusion while preserving concrete application questions.
- Broadrick v. Oklahoma supplies the requirement of substantial unconstitutional reach compared with legitimate operation. Part IV applies it without exempting time, place and manner regulations from overbreadth review.

## Law After Decision

Colorado may enforce the specified knowing, nonconsensual, speaker-initiated approach restriction at its authoritative scope. Stationary speakers need not retreat, willing encounters remain possible, and the surrounding area is not a zone without speech. The actual physical-delivery mechanism supports neutral legislative scrutiny and satisfies Ward here; neither a neutral label, medical setting nor distance alone establishes validity. Part IV separately rejects the ancillary facial claims by five rationale votes. Madsen, Schenck and Erie retain their distinct rules, and appropriate discriminatory-enforcement or materially different application challenges remain open.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, dissenting.** They would reverse and remand for relief against subsection (3). The law permits the same physical approach for other purposes while selecting protest, education, counseling and specified expressive contacts for advance consent. Viewpoint breadth does not eliminate that speech-based distinction, and an access objective cannot by itself supply the stricter justification required. The setting and practical operation reinforce their concern about targeting opposition to abortion, without establishing an authoritative exemption for another viewpoint.

Independently, they find the rule invalid even under Ward. Peaceful initial approaches and hand-to-hand distribution receive protection, yet the rule covers every person near every qualifying entrance without an obstruction, threat or prior refusal. Stationary offers, more distant speech and amplification do not adequately preserve the practical chance to offer literature and begin the quiet exchange petitioners seek. Narrow tailoring and adequate channels are separate requirements; the existence of some speech opportunities does not cure poor fit. Subsection (2) and ordinary conduct remedies expose the mismatch, though Ward does not demand the least restrictive conceivable method or exhaustion of all alternatives. Madsen and Schenck preserve the material protection for initial advocacy; Erie requires examination of actual operation and justification. The dissent accepts the no-retreat construction and knowledge requirement and does not rest on a false duty to flee, assumed misconduct or a right to obstruct. It would leave independent conduct controls intact and prescribes no substitute distance or damages.

**Kennedy, further dissenting.** He joins the common classification and independent Ward objections and adds that the law's predictable operation burdens the disfavored abortion-related viewpoint. He does not treat generally worded categories as a statutory exemption for favored speech, but concludes that their setting and operation reinforce the unconstitutional selection. Independently, the interaction of undefined protest, education and counseling, uncertain consent signals, moving encounters and facility boundaries makes the law facially vague and overbroad. Scienter cannot tell a person what an inadequately defined legal standard means. The no-retreat construction resolves one problem but does not determine whether a mixed encounter is counseling, whether consent exists or when an affirmative approach crosses the prohibited line. ## Event

Mitchell v. Helms, No. 98-1648. Plenary merits decision; argued December 1, 1999; decided June 28, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, reviewing Part III of Helms v. Picard, 151 F.3d 347 (1998), concerning federal Chapter 2.

The question is whether Chapter 2's lending of secular, neutral and nonideological resources for students in public and private nonprofit schools, allocated equally per student without religious classification, violates the Establishment Clause.

Chapter 2 provides federal assistance for instructional resources, including equipment, computers and library materials, distributed to public and private nonprofit schools without religious classification. The statute requires private-school services and resources to be secular, neutral and nonideological; federal funds must supplement, never supplant, nonfederal funds. A public agency must retain fund control and property title and administer the funds and property. Services must come from a public agency or a contractor independent of the private school and religious organizations. Those commands are distinct from findings about actual compliance.

The Fifth Circuit rejected the defendants' summary judgment and declared equipment and library-book loans to sectarian schools in Jefferson Parish unconstitutional as a category, relying on Meek and Wolman while preserving the textbook category. The application now before the Court establishes actual public financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control. That defect is not inferred merely from religious identity, control, fungibility or savings and is not limited to the historical recall of particular books. Its provider-specific extent and quantitative prevalence are not established.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Court vacates the reviewed categorical federal Chapter 2 declaration and remands for effective, confined relief against the established religious financing while preserving lawful severable secular applications.

## Holdings

### 1. Neutral allocation does not cure the actual publicly financed religious function

**Holding and operative rule:** A publicly administered instructional-resource program violates the Establishment Clause when, in the application proved, it actually finances religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under the recipient's control as the funded instructional function. Neutral eligibility, a secular label, public title or allocation tied to school enrollment does not alone cure that defect; genuine individual benefits and independently controlled secular services remain distinct.

**Authority:** Parts I-III of Breyer's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices, control Holdings 1-3 unanimously at their stated scope. The Justices' earlier unjoined theories are not made unanimous by agreement on this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini preserves Lemon through a unified inquiry into governmental authority and function, treating purpose, effects and entanglement as evidence rather than independent old prongs. A specific prohibition controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation requires ordinary attribution and function analysis. Jackson and Strout apply the prohibition on demonstrated financing of devotional instruction while preserving reliably separated secular work. Here the resources are acquired and administered through the public program at schools' requests. Enrollment determines allocation but does not establish that an individual beneficiary directs whether those resources perform the religious function. Rosenberger protects independently edited expression under its actual attribution and payment safeguards; Southworth protects its distinct independent-expression relationship. Neither makes every public purchase a forum. The present rule addresses what the program actually buys, not whether every religious act of a recipient is government speech. The established use crosses that boundary. The Court neither revives categorical religious-school exclusion nor decides every genuinely individual educational benefit.

### 2. Actual use is established here; affiliation and possible diversion cannot replace proof

**Holding and operative rule:** The actual financed religious instruction or demonstrated religious use in this application requires correction and may not be treated as a mere hypothetical risk on remand. Religious location or identity, recipient control, fungibility, potential diversion and incidental institutional savings, separately or merely by accumulation, do not alone establish such a violation in another application.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini rejects the presumption that otherwise safeguarded secular instruction becomes forbidden because it occurs at a religious school. Its actual-control and accountability qualifications also prevent nominal safeguards from conclusively establishing lawful use. Public title, content restrictions, independent provision and supplement-not-supplant requirements can support lawful administration; they are statutory commands whose compliance must be distinguished from their existence. The content restriction, the actual funded use and the supplement requirement do different work, and every statutory violation is not automatically a constitutional violation. The actual defect here cannot be reduced to religious affiliation or to a historically recalled book incident already corrected. Conversely, the established use proves neither the prevalence of misuse nor the failure of all controls. No universal failsafe-monitoring regime, separate corporate structure, wholly different personnel or mandatory new audit is imposed. Effective separation must address the actual financed function with measures appropriate to the application.

### 3. Equipment and library-book form do not justify a categorical prohibition

**Holding and operative rule:** Public provision of equipment or library books to a religious school is not unconstitutional as a category when the actual relationship reliably preserves lawful secular use or another independently protected benefit. Meek and Wolman cease to control insofar as their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns such a relationship by reference to recipient character or presumed diversion; their unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain intact.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini permits separately administered secular instruction with enforceable operational controls and rejects religious location and incidental benefit as automatic constitutional defects. Jackson and Strout require relief tied to the prohibited financed function while protecting genuine secular separation. A distinction between a textbook and other instructional resources cannot override that actual-function inquiry merely because one object is adaptable. The Fifth Circuit's categorical equipment and library-book declaration therefore sweeps beyond the established violation. This conclusion does not approve every historical practice, make direct institutional support harmless whenever eligibility is neutral, or prohibit examination of source, path, control and practical instructional operation. Those features remain relevant evidence. Mueller and Witters retain their actual individual-benefit holdings; no universal entitlement to religious uses of public benefits is derived from them. Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding in the governing law and cannot provide such an entitlement by assumption.

### 4. The categorical declaration must be replaced by effective relief at the proved scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The reviewed federal declaration is vacated and the case remanded to determine the extent and severability necessary for effective prospective correction of the established financed religious use. The lower courts must preserve independently lawful applications and may use the existing record where sufficient; they may not require reproof of the established defect or continue it merely because every provider's use has not been quantified.

**Authority:** Part IV of Breyer's opinion, joined by all seven other Associate Justices, controls by eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with the substantive violation but favors limited affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower judgment was a category-wide declaration, not a decree already confined to particular proved devotional uses. The actual-use finding establishes a need for correction but supplies no school, number of items, prevalence or determination that all applications are inseverable. Jackson's confined remedy and Strout's separate-secular-service limits require both sides of that distinction. Vacatur corrects the reviewed declaration's reach; it does not approve the prohibited use. The lower courts must identify the actual remedial extent without an automatic new trial or an order to create nonexistent evidence. Only the federal Chapter 2 question granted review is decided. Any treatment of a Louisiana counterpart must follow a demonstrated legal dependence on this federal disposition; unrelated programs or separate dockets are not resolved by their presence in the larger litigation.

## Precedent Treatment

- Agostini v. Felton governs through the modified, unified Lemon inquiry, with actual operational control and secular-service qualifications. Religious location and incidental savings do not supply a presumption, while nominal neutrality does not excuse actual devotional financing.
- Lemon v. Kurtzman remains in force only as modified in Agostini; its former independent prongs do not return as an alternative test.
- Jackson v. Benson supplies the actual financed-function prohibition and the conflict-specific remedial inquiry, preserving lawful separable applications rather than treating all benefits to religious education alike.
- Strout v. Albanese preserves that financed-function boundary in its tuition relationship and expressly protects genuine secular separation; it is not a universal voucher judgment.
- Meek v. Pittenger and Wolman v. Walter are displaced insofar as their conclusive equipment-category reasoning condemns reliably lawful secular relationships by religious character or presumed diversion. Unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards are not erased.
- Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia retains its combined private-editing, independent-attribution and controlled-payment safeguards; it does not generally authorize devotional purchases.
- Board of Regents v. Southworth retains its actual independent-expression setting and does not make instructional loans a speech forum merely because allocation is neutral.
- Mueller v. Allen and Witters v. Washington Department of Services for the Blind retain their actual individual-benefit holdings. Per-capita institutional allocation does not establish every predicate of those relationships.
- Zobrest v. Catalina Foothills School District remains an antecedent-law remand without an Establishment Clause merits holding; no unadjudicated interpreter theory is treated as current law.

## Law After Decision

Chapter 2's established religious instructional financing must be corrected, while its independently lawful secular applications remain available. A categorical equipment or religious-recipient rule cannot replace the actual-function inquiry, and neutral allocation cannot excuse a proved prohibited use. The formal federal declaration returns for confined remedial proceedings; its vacatur does not eliminate the established constitutional defect or establish statewide or nationwide misuse. Genuine individual benefits, controlled private expression and reliably separated secular services retain their distinct treatment.

## Event

## Holdings

### 1. The complete Casey standard governs the enacted duty and proved applications

**Holding and operative rule:** The complete six-step Casey standard, as applied in Fargo and Voinovich, governs Nebraska's method prohibition; an established substantial obstacle and denial of timely necessary life-and-health care are independently invalid, and the absence of an obstacle does not end review of remaining severe or meaningful interference. The Court applies that existing rule without substituting a numerical facial threshold, a no-valid-applications barrier, a physician-preference exemption or a new bodily-autonomy framework.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls all four holdings by six direct votes. Kennedy's agreement with the necessary-care baseline does not add a join in this complete framework or the Court's completed applications.

**Governing rule retained from Casey:**

Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

**Burdens and proof:** Challengers establish the duty, population, causation and triggering interference. The State then bears the applicable justification and tailoring or fit burden. This does not alter ordinary proof of disputed historical facts or the enacted burden for a separate statutory defense. No State substantiation burden arises merely because a law concerns abortion.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey preserves the patient's previability decision and necessary care while replacing the former trimester timetable with the complete ordered inquiry above. Fargo applies that framework without confusing notice, culpability and substantive access. Voinovich requires examination of the actual prohibited acts, clinically usable alternatives, statutory defenses and matching relief; it does not make overlap between medical and statutory labels enough to decide every issue. Here the enacted substantial-portion language and developed clinical findings permit substantive application. The Court identifies the affected nonviable procedures and patients, the prohibition's added interference and the actual safeguards before reaching the independent obstacle and necessary-care bars. Neither bar can be overcome by balancing after the violation is established. No separate obstructive-purpose finding or new residual-interference application is needed to sustain this judgment. The remaining steps and their proof allocation remain governing law, not abandoned requirements.

### 2. The enacted definition reaches ordinary D&E and creates a substantial obstacle

**Holding and operative rule:** Nebraska's definition reaches the supported ordinary dilation-and-evacuation sequence when the physician deliberately and intentionally brings a living fetus or a substantial portion into the vagina to perform a procedure the physician knows will cause death and that does cause death before delivery is completed. Prohibiting those ordinary D&E applications removes practical access to the predominant second-trimester method for the affected previability patients and creates a substantial obstacle, independently sustaining the existing bounded declaration and injunction.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutory elaboration includes a substantial portion, not only intact delivery. The described ordinary procedure can deliberately bring a living fetus's limb through the cervix before dismemberment; the act, intent, knowledge, life and sequence elements can therefore be satisfied in ordinary D&E. The Court does not hold that every D&E is prohibited, that a label establishes all elements, or that removal after fetal death satisfies the living-fetus condition.

Nebraska's strongest answer invokes the entire delivery-and-death sequence, the enactment's purpose and constitutional avoidance to confine the prohibition to the intact method. Those considerations cannot erase the enacted substantial-portion alternative or insert an exclusion the text does not contain. The Attorney General's nonenforcement position is not an authoritative Nebraska Supreme Court construction or established control over every county prosecutor. Constitutional avoidance permits a legally available construction; it does not authorize rewriting this statute.

Casey requires concrete interference rather than a presumption against all method regulation. The operative comparison is care with and without this prohibition, with other circumstances held constant, for patients whose timely clinically appropriate nonviable care uses the ordinary D&E acts described. Penal and dependent licensing exposure constrains that usable provider capacity. The availability of other procedures in some circumstances does not establish adequate care for that affected population. Voinovich's rejection of categorical notice reasoning does not cure a demonstrated substantive obstacle; Ohio's distinct alternative-method defenses are absent here. The law's absence of a viability limit does not enlarge the present injunction beyond its nonviable scope, and no categorical vagueness holding is necessary.

### 3. The supported necessary-health applications independently require protection

**Holding and operative rule:** Even if the prohibition could lawfully be confined to an intact procedure, it cannot be enforced in supported circumstances where compelling substitution for that method creates a material risk of death or serious health harm and the method is medically necessary for timely safe and suitable care. Nebraska's specified life-only exception and post-charge life-necessity evidence do not supply a usable exception for those necessary serious-health applications; this independent defect supports the existing protection at its proved nonviable scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey's necessary-care baseline requires protection before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration, and Fargo and Voinovich separate substantive adequacy from an intelligible emergency definition. The District Court credited explained clinical advantages for Carhart's intact method in recurring circumstances involving roughly ten to twenty annual cases: reduced instrumentation, retained parts, trauma, blood loss, operating time and infection risk. The findings support clinically consequential risk reduction in the identified applications, not universal superiority or a right to every preferred method.

The State properly invokes competent medical disagreement, the absence of controlled comparative studies, available alternatives and professional ethics. Those considerations receive weight; no physician or professional association has a constitutional veto. But the developed, credited evidence establishes the necessary-care applications here, and general availability of another method does not establish that it is timely, safe and suitable for those patients. The Court adopts no new numerical risk threshold or presumption that every comparative advantage is necessary.

Section 3(1) authorizes the defined life-saving care; the defect is not an assertion that it protects no life. Its terms do not independently authorize care needed to avoid serious nonfatal harm. The broader emergency-situation definition is not incorporated into that exception. A hearing after charges permits evidence on the same life criterion, not permission to provide the otherwise forbidden necessary-health treatment. Neither admissibility of the Director's findings nor a possible motion to delay trial supplies the missing substantive defense. Voinovich's Ohio alternative-method protections cannot be imported. This conclusion adds no general medical immunity and does not decide every future, precisely drawn method restriction with genuinely usable care safeguards.

### 4. Affirmance preserves the actual nonviable injunction and bars its evasion through dependent sanctions

**Holding and operative rule:** The existing declaration and injunction remain in force for Carhart, his patients and similarly situated persons regarding his D&E and D&X methods on nonviable fetuses, against the defendants and persons bound under Rule 65(d), with the existing county-attorney service direction. Criminal and licensing consequences cannot enforce those same unconstitutional applications, while independently lawful requirements and applications remain subject to Nebraska's section 8 severability direction and the judgment's actual limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Casey's sixth step requires relief corresponding to the proved violation; Voinovich preserves independently lawful provisions and dependent-duty analysis without automatic wholesale invalidation. The District Court expressly declined whole-statute facial relief. Affirming its bounded judgment does not create a nationwide or all-gestation injunction, decide fetal personhood, award damages or independently adjudicate the separate fee appeal. It also does not permit officials to reimpose the invalid prohibition through its dependent license sanctions.

Nebraska section 8 preserves the validity of remaining portions when a section or part is unconstitutional. It supplies no power to insert an intact-only definition or health exception and does not erase the sanction provisions for every lawful application. The enacted Director, petition, hearing and evidentiary procedures remain material to what the law does; the Court neither converts the life-only hearing into a health defense nor decides the unnecessary relationship between section 3(4)'s mandatory wording and the general sanction menu. Patient immunity remains intact. No new hearing, administrative veto, prosecution, damages award or statewide medical-policy revision is ordered.

## Precedent Treatment

- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey retains protected choice, individualized viability, timely necessary life-and-health care and the complete six-step rule. Both independent invalidity grounds are applications of that rule; residual severe- and meaningful-interference protections remain, and no obstacle-only substitute is adopted.
- Fargo Women's Health Organization v. Schafer supplies the distinction between intelligibility, applicable fault and substantive access under the complete framework. An understandable life exception does not itself supply adequate necessary-health protection, and fault words do not automatically cure an exclusion of care.
- Women's Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich requires attention to actual prohibited acts, usable alternatives, distinct statutory defenses and violation-matched relief. Its rejection of categorical notice reasoning remains; its remand on the Ohio method record does not require remand on every method law. Ohio's enacted alternative-method defenses and Ohio severability rules do not become Nebraska law.
- Roe v. Wade and Doe v. Bolton retain the protected decision and clinical safeguards preserved in Casey; the independent trimester timetable remains displaced. No broader reconsideration or new fetal-personhood ruling is undertaken.

## Law After Decision

Nebraska cannot enforce LB23 in the nonviable Carhart-type applications covered by the existing judgment, including through criminal or licensing consequences dependent on the same invalid prohibition. Ordinary-D&E access and supported necessary-health applications each independently support that protection. A defined method, intentional conduct and a life-only exception do not end the substantive inquiry. The complete Casey framework remains controlling, and a materially different precise procedure regulation with clinically usable care protections would require its own full analysis. Patient immunity, independently lawful regulation and ordinary professional discipline remain; no general professional veto or whole-statute invalidity is created.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, dissenting.** He would vacate and remand rather than affirm or authorize immediate blanket enforcement. Reading the complete delivery-and-death sequence in context, he would confine the law to the intact method and rejects the Court's ordinary-D&E access ground. Potential-life and professional-ethics interests permit meaningful regulation; the physician's preferred technique or every asserted comparative advantage does not control the legislature. He also rejects the residual lesser-burden portion of the complete Casey framework.

He retains the necessary-life-and-health baseline he joined in Casey, Fargo and Voinovich. Voinovich's method analysis materially included Ohio's alternative-method health defense and preserved supported necessary-care applications. Nebraska has no corresponding defense: the exception and later licensing evidence concern only the stated life-saving condition. He therefore would require identification of the circumstances in which available alternatives are clinically inadequate for timely safe and suitable care under that retained baseline. He accepts the credited comparative reductions in the described surgical risks without finding every intact-method use necessary or joining the Court's completed health application.

The lower court may make the needed legal application on the existing record if adequate. His remand orders no automatic new hearing, filing or invented condition. It permits only a Nebraska-law-supported protective construction, preserves supported necessary-care and dependent-sanction protection, and requires ordinary consideration of interim relief. It neither inserts a new exception nor automatically dissolves existing protection. The serious necessary mental-health protection in Voinovich supports his adherence to necessary-care protection, not an imported medical test for every physical-risk claim here.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia, dissenting.** They would reverse the constitutional judgment at the granted scope and remand to remove the corresponding injunction. Their principal disagreement is with the constitutional abortion and necessary-health requirements themselves. They acknowledge Casey's complete framework and the actual Fargo and Voinovich holdings as the governing law from which they dissent; they do not replace that law with an obstacle-only description or a different facial-review threshold.

They favor the contextual intact-method construction and ordinary reasonable regulation for potential-life and medical-ethics purposes. The Attorney General's representation is not made binding on every prosecutor, and the credited clinical findings are not declared nonexistent or every alternative equally safe. Their constitutional objection supplies the reason for rejecting the Court's protective conclusion notwithstanding those facts. Agreement in earlier notice and severability propositions does not establish substantive validity, universal knowledge of illegality, good-faith immunity or a cure for every enforcement problem. They seek no new prosecution, damages, administrative sanction, fetal-personhood adjudication or injunction governing viable procedures. Their constitutional objection remains independent of their statutory-construction argument.

## Procedure After Action

The Eighth Circuit's judgment is affirmed. The existing declaration and permanent injunction protect Carhart, his patients and similarly situated persons regarding his D&E and D&X methods on nonviable fetuses. The defendants and their agents, servants and employees remain bound; Stenberg must serve the decision and judgment on Nebraska county attorneys, who and whose agents, servants and employees are bound as directed under Rule 65(d). Dependent criminal and license sanctions cannot enforce those protected applications. The Court adds no whole-statute facial invalidation, viable-fetus injunction, new hearing, damages award or separate fee decision. Independently lawful provisions and applications remain subject to section 8 and the actual judgment's limits.

## Holdings

### 1. Entry implements the settled judgment and formally permits the counterclaim

**Holding and operative rule:** The jointly submitted decree is entered because its terms implement the Court's completed title and boundary judgment without enlarging or reopening it. Alaska receives leave to file its counterclaim as part of the existing original action, with no new merits hearing or alteration of the settled title classifications.

**Authority:** The Court's decree, Paragraphs A-G and original Exhibit A, supplies the binding implementing commands stated in this entry. No individual tally or lineup is published; the decree adds no new coalition for the prior title holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. Alaska, decided June 19, 1997, supplies the normal-baseline and Dinkum rules, the separate clear-inclusion and title-retention determinations, and the direction to prepare a decree. ### 2. The offshore line is fixed and the reciprocal rights retain their qualifications

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph B fixes the federal-state offshore boundary at the original Exhibit A coordinates, with NAD 83/WGS 84 controlling and NAD 27 values serving only as conversion aids; under 43 U.S.C. section 1301(b), that line does not migrate with later coastal change. Against the other sovereign and persons claiming under it, the United States has exclusive exploration and natural-resource rights seaward, subject to section 1337(g) and Paragraph C, and Alaska has corresponding shoreward rights except within the Petroleum Reserve and Arctic Refuge and subject to section 1313 and Paragraph C; the reciprocal noninterference injunctions bind each sovereign, its privies, assigns, lessees and other persons claiming under it at that qualified scope.

**Statutory reservations:** Section 1313 preserves lawfully and expressly acquired federal parcels and their accretions, resources and improvements; lands lawfully held under state law; lands expressly retained or ceded at statehood, apart from a merely general marginal-sea retention or cession; proprietary acquisitions by eminent domain, purchase, cession, gift or otherwise; federal-use filled, built-up or reclaimed lands; existing federal rights in lands presently and actually occupied under claim of right; lands or interests held for tribes, bands, groups or individual Indians; and structures and improvements constructed under the federal navigational servitude. No new parcel-specific application is decided.

Section 1337(g)'s coastal-state revenue rights remain in force without transferring seaward title. Its ordinary account covers specified bonuses, rents, royalties and other bidding revenues from post-September 18, 1978 federal leases in the three-nautical-mile band, excluding federal income and windfall-profits taxes. Partial tracts ordinarily use the percentage of surface acreage within that band. Alaska's special treatment includes a partially covered tract's revenues until seven years after settlement of a boundary dispute under a section 1336 agreement made before January 1, 1986; for other Alaska tracts that special period ended April 15, 1993. Subject to the disputed-boundary provisions, the State receives 27 percent of the covered revenues with accrued interest by the last business day of the month after Treasury deposit, with the remainder sent simultaneously to federal miscellaneous receipts.

For a section 1336 boundary dispute, section 1337(g)(5) credits the agreement account with qualifying post-1978 revenues from tracts wholly or partly within the State-asserted band if not already deposited. Its interim 27-percent payment with interest concerns tracts wholly within the federally asserted band and falls due on the later of thirty days after December 1, 1987 or the last business day of the month after deposit. At settlement, additional sums due are paid from escrow; an insufficiency is met from federal lease revenues under the statutory formula. The statute's information and confidentiality rules, common-deposit notification and optional sharing agreements, account investment, October 1, 1985 accounting-effective date, and equal division of the state share where a tract falls within two or more States' bands remain intact. The decree calculates no statutory award or disputed account balance.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1301(b) distinguishes a decree-fixed offshore line from a coast that otherwise changes with physical features. The original schedule supplies that line; the decree neither commissions a new survey nor substitutes an illustrative map or converted datum. The 1997 judgment supplies the governing baseline and rejects a Dinkum-generated island belt. Its reservation-title rulings and the express statutory and lease reservations prevent either reciprocal injunction from becoming an unqualified resource grant. Section 1337(g) preserves a financial interest at its own limits, while section 1313 preserves different federal estates and interests; neither is interchangeable with Paragraph C's lease rules. No hypothetical parcel, coordinate or revenue dispute is adjudicated.
