## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Standard governing school-sponsored election speech

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Affirm. A school-created student-government election conducted under school rules and presented as part of the school's civic curriculum is school-sponsored expression, so *Hazelwood* rather than *Tinker* supplies the primary standard. The school may enforce clear, viewpoint-neutral campaign rules reasonably related to teaching fair procedure, equal campaign access, and responsible election administration; it may not use those rules as a pretext for suppressing a candidate's political viewpoint.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A school-sponsored student-government election may be governed by clear, viewpoint-neutral campaign rules reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical objectives, including disqualification for a material violation. Independent student advocacy outside the school-sponsored campaign remains governed by the ordinary student-speech rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Affirm. A school-created student-government election conducted under school rules and presented as part of the school's civic curriculum is school-sponsored expression, so *Hazelwood* rather than *Tinker* supplies the primary standard. The school may enforce clear, viewpoint-neutral campaign rules reasonably related to teaching fair procedure, equal campaign access, and responsible election administration; it may not use those rules as a pretext for suppressing a candidate's political viewpoint. The election’s school-created rules and curricular purpose justify applying Hazelwood to this process. Stone’s affirmance depends on the supported relationship to fair campaign administration and the absence of viewpoint pretext, not merely the fact that the speech occurred at school.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The position does not authorize viewpoint discrimination, punishment for off-program independent speech, or reliance on audience hostility as disruption.
