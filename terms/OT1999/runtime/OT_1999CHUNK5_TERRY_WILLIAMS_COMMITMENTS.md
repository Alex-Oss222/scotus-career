## Common statutory propositions and the genuine construction fork

**Common minimum — all eight.** Section 2254(d)(1) contains two alternatives with independent work. A state decision contrary to the governing legal rule established by this Court can satisfy the first without a previous case with identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts is another contrary-to situation. The Fourth Circuit's requirement of square conflict as to both law and fact cannot be a universal prerequisite. The unreasonable-application inquiry is objective. The fact that one or more judges approved the state decision does not establish reasonableness, and the applicant need not prove that every actual reasonable jurist agrees that the state court erred. Judicial disagreement is neither an automatic safe harbor nor a vote-counting test.

Clearly established law means this Court's holdings at the time of the relevant state merits decision, not later holdings or dicta. Strickland's existing general rule can supply clearly established law even though no previous case contains this exact collection of mitigation. Lower federal decisions cannot themselves supply the required Supreme Court law. Thomas's Fretwell observation about state courts and lower federal authority reinforces the latter distinction but does not independently control it. Later precedent cannot retroactively make the state court's result unreasonable; the operative Strickland and limited Fretwell propositions preceded the June 1997 adjudication.

**Distinct-clause construction — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** A state court that identifies the governing rule and applies it to different facts does not act contrary to that rule merely because the federal court would reach another result. The second clause authorizes relief when the application is objectively unreasonable, a legal judgment distinct from whether it is simply incorrect. The federal judge independently assesses that standard rather than deferring to the state court's self-description or conducting a survey of jurists. The generality of the governing rule affects the range of reasonable applications; it does not immunize any result bearing its name. The rule is not limited to gross factual mistakes or identical Supreme Court outcomes, and it imposes no clear-and-convincing burden on a legal question.

Their strongest objection to the broader approach is that classifying every incorrect application of a prescribed rule as contrary would leave little separate function for unreasonable application. Congress chose that latter phrase to restrict collateral relief beyond ordinary correction of legal error. They therefore cannot join a proposition that merely identifying Strickland restores plenary error correction of every application. They can join an actual contrary-rule holding and an independently supported objective-unreasonableness determination here without adopting that broader proposition.

**Governing-rule construction — Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** When this Court has prescribed a rule to govern the kind of claim presented, a state disposition inconsistent with its application is contrary to clearly established law; federal courts must identify and apply that governing rule independently. Strickland prescribes performance and prejudice inquiries rather than leaving States free to choose a different constitutional test. Unreasonable application retains work where established holdings require derivation or extension beyond an already prescribed governing rule. This reading preserves a meaningful legal-review function for the first clause without demanding factual duplication.

Their strongest difficulty is the concern that a general governing standard can make every ordinary application eligible for independent error review, diminishing Congress's second clause. They limit their formulation to rules prescribed for the category of claim, not any high-level constitutional principle that could bear on it; unsettled derivation and extension retain objective-unreasonableness review. They do not treat any federal judge's preferred answer as compelled by clearly established law. They can additionally join a separately reasoned conclusion that this particular state application is unreasonable even under the other four's more restrictive approach. That compatibility on application does not imply agreement on exclusive clause boundaries.

Neither group invalidates section 2254(d), adopts a facial Suspension Clause or Article III holding from the cursory lower challenge, or treats avoidance arguments as an independent grant of relief. Section 2254(a)'s unlawful-custody requirement, section (d)'s gateway and the ultimate constitutional merits remain distinct. Section (d)(2) factual unreasonableness and section (e)(1)'s presumption/clear-and-convincing rebuttal are not substituted for legal analysis; no new federal hearing or section (e)(2) determination is made.

The exact current-term supplement confirms the same limits. Smith v. Robbins restores a defined missing counsel-assisted appellate-screening function; it preserves ordinary Strickland review for defective performance within a supplied function. Roe v. Flores-Ortega separately measures consultation performance and causal loss of an appeal; its unanimous no-probable-appellate-success rule follows from that lost proceeding. Neither is a presumed-prejudice rule for deficient sentencing investigation or a construction of amended section (d)(1); both federal applications preceded enactment. Here counsel performed a sentencing function, and actual sentencing prejudice must be established. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer's Flores-Ortega commitments reinforce contextual performance rather than automatic deficiency; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg's contrary duty application does not dispense with the shared causal requirement. These current-term positions support the stated distinctions without changing the statutory or merits commitments.

## Governing prejudice rule, Fretwell and the jury question

All eight reject using Fretwell as a general extra unfairness requirement. The entering register *Loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice*, effective January 25, 1993, expressly preserves ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice and confines the unavailable-benefit rule to a displaced substantive entitlement where no other fair or reliable protection was lost. O'Connor authored that limiting Court opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas among the sitting Associates. Its limitation is controlling, not a nonbinding concurrence. Stevens's dissent favored the ordinary-probability inquiry still more broadly. Omitted lawful mitigation is not an advantage forbidden by governing substantive law. Lockett, Eddings and Skipper recognize the relevance of such material; they do not demand its indiscriminate presentation or establish ineffective assistance by omission alone.

Prejudice requires a reasonable probability sufficient to undermine confidence, not proof that a different sentence was more likely than not. The entire sentencing picture matters: actual mitigation and aggravation, the evidence competent investigation would have produced, and its adverse consequences. Review assumes a reasonable conscientious sentencer, not nullification, arbitrary mercy or an idiosyncratic juror. Virginia's actual statute requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree. Accordingly, a reasonable probability that at least one reasonable juror would decline death is a legally different outcome; twelve votes for life are unnecessary. A bare possibility of a sympathetic holdout does not satisfy Strickland. All eight can join this legal consequence even though Scalia and Thomas find the required probability absent.

The State's ordinary-prejudice alternative is expressly assessed. Its court recited both unreliability language and the reasonable-probability standard. An incorrect additional gloss does not erase an actually independent and sufficient correct ground merely because the court cited Fretwell. Conversely, reciting the correct words does not cure an inquiry materially narrowed by the wrong framework or an incomplete treatment of the actual mitigation. That is the central application disagreement, not a failure to notice the alternative.

## Relief position — Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

**Gateway.** To the extent the state court demanded an additional showing of fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice, its rule is contrary to Strickland and the Court's express Fretwell limitation. Its nominal ordinary-prejudice discussion does not supply an adequate independent basis on this record. It largely treated the omitted case as favorable impressions of relatives and acquaintances, instead of considering the materially different documentary childhood deprivation, intellectual/educational history and evidence of functioning in structured settings with the aggravation. Even under the distinct-clause construction, that application is objectively unreasonable, not merely an answer the federal court finds less persuasive. The omitted evidence supplies explanations and a response to future danger that the generic character testimony did not.

These six adopt the whole-record unreasonable-application conclusion as an independently sufficient statutory ground, alongside their precise objection to the forbidden Fretwell requirement. The four broader-construction Associates may also support gateway eligibility under their independent application understanding; that additional scope is not part of the six's common statutory ground. The state court's failure is legal evaluation of the full supported sentencing picture, not a finding that every affidavit or proposed diagnosis must be presumed true under section (d)(2).

**Performance.** Strickland assesses counsel's actions from the time of representation. Counsel began sentencing preparation about a week before trial, failed to obtain the youth records because of a mistaken view of access law, and acknowledged he would have used them if he knew their contents. Asking Williams and relatives for helpful material did not reasonably end investigation of readily identifiable records and leads. Their initial stable-home account did not justify an uninformed failure to investigate documented deprivation and limited functioning. Counsel's consultation with experts and the decision to stop cumulative character testimony after damaging cross-examination are real professional acts, but they do not explain or excuse failure to learn what the documentary case contained. No rule requires every available witness or every adverse juvenile file to be introduced; reasonable investigation precedes an informed choice about what to present.

The Warden's preserved adequate-performance defense therefore fails on this supported investigation ground, independently of whether particular wife, daughter or Elliott testimony should tactically have been used. The six do not presume the lower courts' assumed deficiency to be a concession, rely on counsel's alleged mental illness, or reopen the separate expert-access claims. The existing developed record suffices; no additional hearing is necessary to establish the identified investigation lapse.

**Actual prejudice.** The murder and surrounding criminal conduct are grave. The case includes prior armed robbery, burglary and larceny, recent parole release, vehicle thefts, the fire-induced attack and robbery of another elderly man, the devastating assault of an elderly woman, jail arson, violent urges and two experts' future-danger predictions. Those facts do not disappear. The defense also did present character witnesses, limited intellectual evidence and a mercy argument based on Williams's voluntary confession; it is inaccurate to describe a complete absence of mitigation.

Against that record, the childhood materials establish a substantially different mitigating account: severe parental alcoholism, neglected and hungry children, filthy conditions, parental imprisonment for neglect, removal from home, violence and fear. A later improved family report qualifies the history without erasing early deprivation. Limited intellectual functioning and special education give the fuller history significance beyond the limited intellectual description already heard. Supported conduct, training and relationships in structured settings, and the prison commendations, provide concrete information beyond the poorly informed favorable character opinions. Evidence of adjustment addresses future danger but does not prove lifetime confinement, guarantee nonviolence or erase jail arson. Possible parole and the State's society-wide theory remain part of the inquiry.

The juvenile records also carried substantial adverse information, including fourteen described offenses and repeated commitments. The six do not assume that every adverse entry necessarily would be admitted, but assess the State's strongest supported argument that developing this history would expose further persistent criminality. That risk does not make the materially different explanatory mitigation cumulative or negate the reasonable probability of a lawful nondeath outcome. Property offenses and commitments carry real weight without being identical to the severe violence already before the jury. The inquiry concerns the total sentencing picture rather than adding favorable items while discarding adverse ones.

No fetal-alcohol diagnosis or disputed new expert prediction is necessary. No claim is made that wife, daughter and Elliott all testified live or that the state hearing judge credited every proffer. The supported documentary history and concrete conduct evidence, viewed alongside those properly supported relationships and actual trial evidence, suffice. The state hearing judge's recommendation is relevant as a reasoned evaluation by the judge familiar with the trial, not an authoritative personal veto or the final state judgment. The legal conclusion independently follows: there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious juror, applying Virginia law, would have declined death. The confidence required to sustain this sentence is undermined.

**Remedy.** Reverse the Fourth Circuit insofar as it reversed sentencing relief on this mitigation claim, and return for reinstatement or implementation of a conforming conditional writ. The State must receive an appropriate opportunity for a lawful new penalty proceeding; if it does not use that opportunity, the death sentence must be replaced by a lawful nondeath disposition. The federal court determines an appropriate implementation period under ordinary procedure; the Court invents no fixed number of days. This is federal collateral relief concerning the sentence, not a direct merits reversal of every Virginia judgment. The convictions, other rejected/defaulted cross-appeal claims and ungranted rulings remain undisturbed. No acquittal, release, mandatory life-without-parole sentence, new expert appointment, new hearing on collateral claims or new execution/stay action is entered.

## Affirmance position — Scalia and Thomas

Both reject the displaced broad Fretwell proposition and accept that lawful mitigation receives ordinary Strickland analysis. They nevertheless read the state court's separately stated probability conclusion, attention to juvenile history and discussion of aggravation as an independent application of the correct rule. The state court's characterization of family witnesses does not establish that it legally excluded every document or commendation from consideration; the trial judge's failure to assign some items significance likewise does not compel a federal judge to give them decisive weight. The court could reasonably conclude that the added evidence would not change the sentencing result even after removing any unnecessary Fretwell gloss.

The severe repeated violence, incapacitating attack, jail arson and expressed urges provide direct future-danger evidence. Additional childhood explanation and functional limitations are meaningful mitigation, but they do not refute that demonstrated risk. Some limited intellectual and favorable character evidence was already presented. The expanded records also strengthen the picture of persistent offending, and the good-conduct evidence must be tested against serious misconduct in custody and the possibility of parole. The proposed omitted case is thus mixed rather than a substantially unopposed account. They find no reasonable probability of a legally conscientious juror declining death on the full record, not merely no probability that all twelve would vote for life.

Their strongest opposing consideration is the qualitative difference between documented deprivation and sparse generic character testimony, emphasized by the state hearing judge and the federal district court. They accept that difference but find it insufficient against the particular aggravation and adverse records. Reasonable-probability review does not allow a federal court to infer relief from the availability of substantial mitigation alone. On the statute's independent objective standard, this is at least within the range of reasonable applications; on independent constitutional assessment, they also find no prejudice. The latter is an alternative ground for affirmance even if a prohibited state framework opened the gateway. They need not decide whether counsel's investigation was deficient and do not adopt its adequacy as an additional holding.

A framework-only remand is unnecessary for these two because their independent no-prejudice conclusion is dispositive; it is unnecessary for the six because the developed record establishes both requirements. Neither group orders more factual development merely to resolve a disagreement over legal weight. A path dependent on a factual finding about the disputed diagnosis or new expert prognosis would require a new neutral supplement; no modeled disposition depends on it.
