## Common rule and grounds available for assembly

All eight Associates directly support the following reasoning and limits; none is counted merely from agreement with the judgment.

1. FAA §§9–11 supplement rather than displace otherwise applicable federal venue law. An application to confirm, vacate, modify or correct a domestic award may be brought in the district where the award was made or in another district proper under the general venue statute. This is a venue holding; an independent basis for federal subject-matter jurisdiction and the separate statutory and procedural conditions for relief remain necessary.
2. The word “may” alone does not decide the case. The alternative reading is plausible because §§10–11 identify the award-district court as the actor permitted to grant relief, and §4 elsewhere expressly refers to any otherwise competent district court. But the three contemporaneous, closely related postaward provisions contain no exclusion of ordinary venues, and their geographic designation retains meaningful work by independently making the award district available. There is no universal canon that every special venue statute is permissive, or that every use of “may” defeats exclusivity.
3. Read §9's agreed-court clause, its default award-district clause and §§10–11 together. An exclusive reading of §§10–11 would require an attack on the award in the arbitration district even when confirmation could properly proceed in the court selected under §9. Treating an attack as permissible in the selected confirmation court already concedes that the award district is not universally exclusive. The statutory scheme supports coordinated treatment of confirmation and attacks on an award without deciding the interpretation or enforceability of a genuinely exclusive contractual forum clause. This contract names no such court; Mississippi substantive law is not a judicial-forum selection.
4. Sections 3, 4 and 8 corroborate the integrated construction. Section 3 concerns a court in which the arbitrable action is already pending, and §8 expressly preserves the admiralty court's authority to enter a decree after arbitration. Section 4 regulates the place of court-ordered arbitration and uses an express territorial command; the postaward provisions regulate different acts. Section 8's “notwithstanding” formulation could operate as an exception, so it is not alone a logical refutation of exclusivity. No rule for extraterritorial compulsion under §4 or subpoena authority under §7 is announced.
5. Ordinary case-management principles can address competing proceedings. The Mississippi action preceded Alabama's by seven days and arose where the contract was performed. The courts below treated exclusive Alabama venue as a conclusive answer to the request to dismiss, transfer or stay. That premise was wrong; the remand restores consideration of those requests under the applicable ordinary doctrines. Neither first filing nor permissive venue itself selects the eventual forum or decides award validity.

The historical Court additionally relied on the restrictive general venue law prevailing in 1925, the effect of forum-selection clauses, Marine Transit, and the anomalies its opponent's rule could create for nonconvention foreign arbitrations. Those observations support the historical decision; they do not establish a disagreement by a present Associate with that decision. The frozen common ground is independently sufficient without adopting new holdings on foreign awards or treating previously unsupplied historical materials as adjudicated facts. The permitted explanation may discuss the statutory coordination and consequences already in the neutral packet; it must not convert the decision into a holding on international arbitration. No historical policy language overrides the text. ## Remedy, precedent treatment and exclusions

Reverse the judgment reported at 169 F.3d 693 and remand for proceedings applying nonexclusive FAA venue and the otherwise applicable rules governing competing actions. The award-district court is not stripped of venue. No order selects Mississippi, resolves entitlement to stay or transfer, enters money damages, or independently sets aside the arbitration award. Alabama's confirmation cannot be sustained solely on the rejected exclusive-venue rationale; independent matters remain for the remand.

Naples' exclusive-venue interpretation is rejected; its force as former Fifth Circuit precedent explains the Eleventh Circuit's action but cannot bind this Court. Sutter's nonexclusive reading is consistent with the adopted rule; it is not itself binding Supreme Court law. Mastrobuono remains an integrated-contract and remedy-authority decision, Allied-Bruce remains a §2 coverage and state-court enforceability decision, and First Options continues to govern antecedent assent, actual delegation and appellate review. None is converted into an earlier venue holding.

Section 9's one-year application language, command to confirm absent statutory grounds, notice and personal-jurisdiction rules retain their separate operation. The time limit is not adjudicated as a mandatory bar here. Sections 10–11 retain each distinct statutory ground and qualification, including §10's conditional rehearing authority and §11(b)'s exception for a non-submitted matter not affecting the merits of what was submitted. Section 12's notice, service and three-month language are not enlarged. No issue concerning administrative-arbitration amendments is decided.
