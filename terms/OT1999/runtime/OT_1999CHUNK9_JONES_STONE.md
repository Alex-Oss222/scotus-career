## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: May California require political parties to select their

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reverse. Selection of a party's official nominee is a central associational act, and California compels each party to let nonmembers, including adherents of rival parties, determine that choice. That severe burden triggers the most demanding received review, which the State's interests in participation and moderation cannot satisfy through this mechanism when less intrusive nonpartisan, top-two, or party-chosen open-primary structures remain available.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not compel a political party to select its official nominee through a blanket primary in which voters unaffiliated with that party participate on equal terms.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reverse. Selection of a party's official nominee is a central associational act, and California compels each party to let nonmembers, including adherents of rival parties, determine that choice. That severe burden triggers the most demanding received review, which the State's interests in participation and moderation cannot satisfy through this mechanism when less intrusive nonpartisan, top-two, or party-chosen open-primary structures remain available. Timmons left the party able to select a consenting nominee through available means, while this rule makes nonmembers part of the official selection decision. Stone finds that difference severe enough to demand the most exacting justification; he does not infer that every primary regulation has that effect.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The position does not bar nonpartisan or top-two systems, party-authorized open primaries, neutral election administration, or regulation of ballots, timing, and access that does not transfer the nomination decision to outsiders.
