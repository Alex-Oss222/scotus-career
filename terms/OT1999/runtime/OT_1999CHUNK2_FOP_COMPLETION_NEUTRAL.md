* **Current Through:** June 23, 1999, the completed OT_1998 baseline, carried continuously to this event.

### Issue 1: Official-use arms claim and qualifying conviction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Fraternal Order of Police v. United States, decided October 7, 1998, unanimously holds that the individual arms right does not require a governmental-use exemption for persons with a qualifying domestic-violence conviction in the developed official-use controversy. Hiley v. Barrett preserves the conviction safeguards and ordinary equality treatment of the qualifying category.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The individual right is real, and regulation is examined through the actual burden and fit. A constitutionally adequate conviction involving actual or attempted physical force or threatened use of a deadly weapon supplies an adjudicated danger-linked predicate. The statutory definition, counsel and jury safeguards, expungement, set-aside, pardon, and restoration provisions retain independent force.
* **Other Governing Law:** Second and Fifth Amendments; 18 U.S.C. §§ 921(a)(33), 922(g)(9), 925(a)(1).
* **Material Other Authority:** Love v. Pepersack and United States v. Hale establish personal invocation and separate examination of the particular restriction; they do not reopen the 1998 mandate.
* **Open Point:** Private possession outside the official-use controversy, an improperly classified conviction, and any constitutional restoration requirement remain unresolved.
### Issue 2: Equal protection and the official-use exemption

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The October 7, 1998 Fraternal Order of Police decision unanimously holds that Congress may withhold § 925(a)(1)’s official-use exception from the domestic-violence-misdemeanor provisions while retaining it for the older felony provision.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Ordinary equality review permits fitted incremental regulation of conviction and occupational categories. The Fifth Amendment does not require Congress to perfect every older exemption simultaneously.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment; 18 U.S.C. §§ 922(d)(9), 922(g)(9), 925(a)(1).
* **Material Other Authority:** Hiley v. Barrett confirms that the domestic-violence category and differing restoration systems do not fail ordinary equality review on the presented grounds.
* **Open Point:** Purposeful discrimination on a separately protected ground and a materially different statutory classification remain outside the prior holding.
### Issue 3: Tenth Amendment and anti-commandeering

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Printz and Mack preserve the distinction between compelled state administration and direct federal regulation. Hiley’s October 9, 1998 holding already rejects the staffing-based objection to §922(g)(9); the later prepared Condon position is not law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Congress may regulate private persons within enumerated power and may not require state legislatures or executive officers to enact, administer, or enforce federal policy against others.
* **Other Governing Law:** Tenth Amendment and constitutional structure; 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(9).
* **Material Other Authority:** New York v. United States and Garcia retain their distinct anti-compulsion and direct-regulation rules.
* **Open Point:** A federal command requiring state officials to investigate, prosecute, or administer the disability would present a different question.
### Issue 4: Commerce Clause authority

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Lopez requires an identifiable interstate mechanism before aggregation. Hiley specifically sustains the §922(g)(9) recipient-class restriction as a necessary and proper anti-evasion component of the supply prohibition. Hale, Rybar and Kirk separately sustain the machinegun stock regime without an added individual interstate-crossing element. Section 922(g)’s enacted nexus must still be established in the actual application.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A jurisdictional element matters only when it genuinely connects the prohibited possession to interstate commerce; remote movement is not constitutional magic by itself.
* **Other Governing Law:** Commerce Clause and Necessary and Proper Clause; 18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(9).
* **Material Other Authority:** The firearms-market decisions preserve the distinction between the constitutional mechanism and proof of the offense-specific statutory nexus.
* **Open Point:** The Court does not recognize a general federal police power over every locally possessed firearm.
