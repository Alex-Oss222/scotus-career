## Reconciled propositions and supported alternatives

**Product-design rule.** In an action for infringement of unregistered product-design trade dress under section 43(a), the claimant must establish secondary meaning: the design's primary significance to the relevant public is to identify source rather than the product itself. Inherent distinctiveness alone is insufficient. Product design remains eligible for protection after acquiring that source significance, provided nonfunctionality, likelihood of confusion, and the other applicable requirements are separately met. Consumer identification of a source does not require knowledge of its name.

The statutory source relationship supplies the rule. An unusual, attractive, or recognizable design can remain the product consumers want rather than an immediately source-identifying sign. A producer's intention to make a recognizable line, a buyer's recognition of its look, and proof of deliberate copying do not turn those different propositions into the necessary acquired-source finding. Source significance, nonfunctionality and confusion cannot substitute for one another. This conclusion neither declares the garments functional nor decides the Second Circuit's separate confusion presumption.

**Packaging and uncertain classification.** Qualifying packaging and the restaurant presentation accepted in *Two Pesos* retain the inherent-distinctiveness route. A court confronting genuinely ambiguous dress should treat it as product design and require secondary meaning. This caution applies when the distinction between the product and its packaging cannot reliably be made; it does not deem every unusual package inherently distinctive or resolve a particular bottle, container, or other hybrid absent its record. The garments here are product design, so no borderline classification is needed to dispose of this case.

This limited classification guidance is the only clarification of scope beyond the independent handoff. The provisional rows reserved a comprehensive decision about every boundary case; they did not identify an objection to a cautious rule for unresolved ambiguity. The historical opinion supplies strong evidence favoring that guidance. Its source-function ground is already present in the neutral alternatives and actual *Qualitex*, and the guidance preserves those reservations by deciding no specific hybrid. The Justice-specific reasons appear below. No newly discovered factual premise or new independent ground has been added to the neutral packet.

**Strongest competing merits path.** Samara can invoke the broad words of section 43(a), the jury's combination finding, ordinary Rule 50 deference, and the narrowed injunction to argue for a demanding case-specific inherent-distinctiveness test. The statutory text indeed allows designs to serve as marks; the adopted rule preserves that eligibility. But unusual form, producer intent and recognizable appearance cannot reliably separate source identification from the product's desired appearance. Nonfunctionality and narrow relief address different risks and do not supply the absent source distinction. *Two Pesos* accepted restaurant presentation for its limited question and reserved this category. The prospect of copying before acquired meaning develops does not eliminate the source-identification condition.

**Narrower alternative.** A record-specific ruling along Judge Newman's approach could reject this combination as insufficient while leaving rare inherently distinctive product designs possible. Each Associate rejects that narrower resolution here because it would leave the recurrent source-versus-product uncertainty in the governing rule and would make the appeal turn on the perceived distinctiveness of these garments. The category question can be answered without reweighing the jury's historical facts. The applicable Rule 50 standard is not reduced, and no determination of copyright originality is borrowed from the lower dissent.

**Alternative classification path.** A court could leave every ambiguous package/design question to ordinary fact-specific classification without an express caution. Its strongest argument is avoiding a rule reaching beyond clearly designed garments. The limited caution is preferable because the same unresolved doubt concerns whether consumers naturally perceive the feature as a source sign; requiring acquired significance resolves that doubt without barring eventual protection. It creates neither a separate functionality test nor a demand that a claimant prove consumer recognition by survey alone.

## Remedy, independent components, and reserved questions

Each Associate supports **reversal of the Second Circuit's federal trade-dress ruling insofar as it sustains protection for this product design through inherent distinctiveness alone, with remand for proceedings consistent with the acquired-source requirement**. The Second Circuit expressly states that secondary meaning was not asserted. Neither its proof nor a preserved request to develop it can be supplied by inference. The remand does not automatically reopen evidence, command a new trial, or direct an unasserted theory to be tried. The neutral packet's actual *Weisgram* caution protects a supported fair-opportunity claim; it creates no such claim here.

The lower courts must remove reliance on the erroneous federal trade-dress ground and address the consequential allocation of injunction, damages, fees and costs only as necessary. The copyright judgment and the state-law merits lie outside the granted product-design question and are not reversed by this holding. Their independently sustainable relief remains subject to its own bases. The Second Circuit's earlier narrowing of the injunction and removal of the circus-car trade-dress damages are not undone; the separate copyright award for the tow-truck garment is not converted into federal trade-dress damages. No original broad injunction is restored, no new total is computed, and no double recovery is authorized. The lower opinion's inconsistent cents in the initial trade-dress total do not affect this legal disposition and are not silently repaired.

The decision does not adjudicate the garments' functionality, reopen the separate deception/confusion presumption or labeling instruction, decide the copyrights' originality, change the state-law preemption disposition, or establish a general aesthetic-functionality rule. Section 5 of Pub. L. 106-43 allocates the burden of proving nonfunctionality for unregistered trade dress but states no distinctiveness rule. Its temporal application to antecedent conduct or the earlier trial remains undecided. Section 2(e)'s registration-date transition is confined to section 2 and supplies no answer for section 5.
