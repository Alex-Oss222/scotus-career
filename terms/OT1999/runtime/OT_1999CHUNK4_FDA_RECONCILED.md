## Threshold, issue and procedural scope

All eight Associates reach the statutory issue. The Fourth Circuit judgment of August 14, 1998, 153 F.3d 155, arose through the certified interlocutory and injunction appeal routes; the absence of a completed trial judgment supplies no identified bar to this review. No Justice requires a new threshold hearing or an invented jurisdictional finding. Participation is unchanged.

The question concerns FDA authority over tobacco **as customarily marketed**, rather than tobacco marketed with express therapeutic claims. The agency's undisputed-for-this-appeal factual findings supply evidence of pharmacological effects, addiction, serious harm, consumer use, manufacturer knowledge, and deliberate nicotine delivery. They do not concede every statutory construction or regulation's validity. Coverage, combination-product method, particular access or advertising restrictions, and the First Amendment challenge are distinct questions. The court of appeals vacated the district court's advertising construction and did not approve it. Neither disposition here can turn that vacated construction into an additional holding.

## Historical comparator: matches and material limits

Historically, O'Connor wrote for Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas to affirm; Breyer dissented with Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. Every present Associate's frozen judgment position matches that alignment. The historical author and joins are comparison evidence only and do not assign an opinion here.

The historical majority has an integrated statutory ground: the definitions must be read with the FDCA's operative safety and marketing scheme and the later tobacco-specific enactments. It assumes without deciding that intended use may be established without express therapeutic claims. It expressly does not make every dangerous product automatically unmarketable, treat agency change alone as unlawful, or rely on failed bills alone. Those qualifications are compatible with the four affirming commitments.

Some historical reasoning cannot be imported unchanged. The majority cites the new section 393 mission, and invokes historical *MCI* as support for hesitation about a delegation of extraordinary economic or political magnitude. The actual section 422 baseline excludes the new mission from governing tobacco exercise, while actual *MCI* expressly supplies no universal major-questions rule or exception based on importance. The four affirmers have independent grounds in the older operative statutory duties and enacted tobacco context; neither deleted premise is necessary to their commitments. Their contextual conclusion does not convert the precise cigarette-labeling and smokeless-tobacco clauses into a literal all-purpose ban on FDA action. Agency disclaimers and legislative history are context, not enacted prohibitions or automatic ratification.

The historical dissent supplies two separable responses to the alleged ban consequence. Its broader response treats the remedial system as flexible enough to avoid a more dangerous response; at page 177 it construes the cessation command as conditional on selecting the recall remedy. **Its independent alternative at page 191 is that, even if a ban were required, that consequence would limit the agency's remedies rather than negate jurisdiction.** The four reversal commitments adopt that independently sufficient distinction between coverage and lawful exercise. They do not need, and do not adopt, the historical optional-command reading, an unrestricted comparative-risk exception, or the proposition that the safest available policy automatically satisfies every operative FDCA requirement.

This is a narrower selection among the historical dissent's express grounds, not a new reason invented to preserve its vote. Actual *MCI*, *Haggar*, *Iowa Utilities Board*, and the qualified *Medtronic* statutory comparisons reinforce the separation of delegation from compliance. The stronger older commands identified in the repaired packet remain enforceable if their own predicates are satisfied. The historical alternative confirms that rejecting a general dispensing power need not change any of these four Justices' coverage positions.

The historical dissent's reliance on section 422 is consistent with the corrected handoff only at its actual scope: that Act and its amendments do not change the authority question, and whatever authority exists is exercised under the earlier FDCA. It does not establish that every prior or later tobacco enactment is legally irrelevant. Nor may the historical opinion's discussion of particular marketing facts or agency papers expand the clean packet without a fresh neutral source step. No such expansion is needed here.

## Common statutory boundaries for both paths

The exact text remains in the corrected annex; the following are the substantive boundaries adopted by every Associate, not a replacement or unqualified paraphrase of that text.

**The controlling date.** Public Law 105-115, section 422, preserves the tobacco-authority question and separately requires exercise under the FDCA as it stood November 20, 1997. Section 501's general 90-day effective date, February 19, 1998, except as otherwise provided and except for sections 111, 121, 125 and 307, does not displace that baseline. Section 406's mission and compliance-plan provisions are genuine later statutory text, but supply no governing tobacco mission or new delegation here. New classification/default and review-flexibility provisions from that Act are likewise excluded. The derived earlier subsections are mechanically restored reading copies, not falsely certified retrieved earlier Code editions.

**Coverage and combinations.** Section 321(g)(1)(C)'s body-structure/function category is distinct from the disease-use category. Its food and dietary-claim qualifications remain limited to their statutory conditions and the proposition that the specified compliant labeling statement alone does not create drug status. The device definition includes relevant components, parts and accessories but excludes achieving primary intended purposes through chemical action or dependence on metabolism. The nicotine and delivery-component distinction must therefore be analyzed rather than collapsed. Section 353(g) assigns primary jurisdiction by primary mode of action, preserves other necessary agency resources, and requires implementing market-clearance procedures. That assignment is not itself an exemption from otherwise applicable drug obligations. None of the four affirmers decides that all nontherapeutic products fall outside the definitions; they may assume the FDA's intended-use position for purposes of their integrated statutory objection.

**New drugs.** Section 321(p) retains the general-recognition and material-use conditions, the animal-drug exclusions, and the specified pre-June 25, 1938 exception tied to the prior Act and unchanged labeling representations. Sections 331(d) and 355(a) require the applicable effective approval for interstate introduction of a new drug. The earlier section 355(d) retains notice and hearing, its seven refusal grounds, its approval direction as written, and its substantial-evidence definition. Its 1997 single-investigation-plus-confirmatory-evidence sentence is excluded, without inventing a fixed minimum number of studies. No new-drug classification, grandfather qualification, application, hearing outcome or exemption is found for these products merely from the coverage question.

**Misbranding.** Sections 331(a) and 352(j) concern interstate introduction of a misbranded product and danger under its labeled dosage, manner, frequency or duration. Section 352(f)(1) requires adequate directions, with a mandatory regulatory exemption where that directions requirement is unnecessary for public-health protection. Section 352(f)(2) separately requires the warnings necessary to protect users against dangerous pathological-condition or childhood use and unsafe dosage, method or duration; the directions exception does not waive warnings. Section 352(q) separately addresses false or misleading restricted-device advertising and violations of section 360j(e) regulations. Section 352(r)'s advertising statements, special component/formula procedure, extraordinary-circumstances limit on prior advertisement approval, FTC provision and exclusion for material determined to be labeling retain their own terms. Existing tobacco warnings establish no blanket FDCA compliance, and the Court resolves no specific advertisement's validity.

**Classification and evidence.** The earlier section 360c(a) contains graduated Class I, II and III criteria, including the low-risk Class I route with insufficient information and its life-support/health-importance and unreasonable-risk limits. Risk alone is not automatic Class III classification or a judicial ban. Section 360c(a)(2) considers intended persons, labeled conditions and probable health benefits from use against probable injury or illness from use. Section 360c(a)(3)(B)'s alternative valid-scientific-evidence route remains subject to its sufficiency and expert-conclusion findings. The earlier section 360c(f) reclassification petition, required findings, notices, panel recommendations and timing remain; the new 30-day de novo request does not. The earlier section 360c(i) substantial-equivalence criteria, adverse-data/public-information conditions and bar on specified removed or judicially condemned predicates remain; the new proposed-labeling intended-use constraint and expedited or least-burdensome additions do not. No classification shortcut, newly completed approval or general withdrawal-risk exception is inferred.

**Restrictions, bans and recalls.** Section 360j(e) permits sale, distribution or use restrictions upon the required harmful-potential or collateral-measures determination that reasonable assurance of safety/effectiveness otherwise cannot be achieved. Practitioner authorization and other prescribed conditions are distinct routes. Training or facility restrictions require a safe/effective-use determination, and lack of specified board eligibility or certification alone cannot exclude a user. Appropriate restriction statements must appear on the label as prescribed. This permission does not itself excuse misbranding or other mandatory requirements.

Section 360f permits banned-device rulemaking on the specified substantial-deception or unreasonable-and-substantial-risk findings; where the Secretary determined that labeling could correct the problem and supplied the required notice and period, the failure to make that change is separately required. Immediate effect requires the additional unreasonable, direct and substantial danger determination and advance manufacturer notice, followed by prompt notice, informal-hearing opportunity and expeditious affirmance, modification or revocation. Section 360h(a)'s notification and section 360h(b)'s repair/replacement/refund powers retain their distinct findings, processes and limits. A greater-risk determination concerning direct notification requires notice through treating health professionals; it is not a power to dispense with every protection.

Section 360h(e)(1) separately requires cessation of distribution and notification/instruction to professionals and facilities after the specified reasonable-probability finding of serious adverse consequences or death, with an informal-hearing opportunity within ten days and vacatur if grounds prove inadequate. Recall follows a further determination, a timetable and reports. Recall cannot include devices held by individuals; the greater-risk exception concerns recall from device-user facilities and does not rewrite the initial cessation command. Individual risk notice remains required under the recall provisions. Section 360h(d) preserves other liability and requires account of the remedy's value in economic-loss damages; it is no general immunity or unrestricted saving clause. No ban, recall, finding or present enforcement order is entered by this handoff.

**Tobacco-specific statutes and state requirements.** Section 1331 announces a comprehensive cigarette labeling and advertising program, not an express general exemption from all health law. Section 1334(a) bars additional smoking-and-health statements on cigarette packages; subsection (b) has its distinct state-law advertising/promotion scope for conforming packages. Section 4406(a) and (b) separately concern federal and state/local requirements for smokeless-tobacco statements on packages and advertisements, with the outdoor-billboard exception; subsection (c) preserves liability. The differing scope and exceptions cannot be merged. These enacted limits may bear on the integrated authority inquiry without establishing every restriction's illegality.

Section 360k(a) requires an applicable federal device requirement, a different or additional state requirement, and the stated relationship to safety, effectiveness or another covered subject. Subsection (b) requires a state/local application, regulation, notice and oral-hearing opportunity; more stringent requirements are one route, compelling-local-conditions requirements whose compliance does not violate federal law another. The no-federal-violation condition belongs to the second route. Actual *Medtronic* prevents an inference that FDA coverage alone displaces every state youth-access rule. No particular preemption application or exemption is decided.

## Alternative-path and departure conclusions

For the four affirmers, the authority-affirming alternative is legally substantial because the definitions include nontherapeutic structure/function effects, and manufacturer intent is not necessarily confined to claims. Their rejection rests on this claimed regime read against the older operative duties and the specific enacted tobacco system. They do not need the new mission, the new classification pathway, a categorical preemption assertion, or a general rule that consequential policies require express naming. The four disagree with the opposition's coverage/compliance separation on this statutory setting; they do not abolish that distinction throughout administrative law.

For the four reversers, the no-authority alternative is substantial because a permissive restriction power cannot erase other commands and the tobacco statutes make concrete regulatory choices. Their answer is not that the FDA may disregard those choices. They conclude that the earlier definitions and regulatory architecture permit coverage, while any particular inconsistency must be resolved through review of the actual method. The later statutes' limited subjects do not establish the categorical exemption necessary to sustain the court of appeals' judgment. Their historical opinion expressly preserves this answer even if its broader remedial-discretion theory is mistaken.

A narrower decision invalidating one advertising restriction or prescribing a particular classification does not resolve the supplied general-authority question on this packet. No Associate adopts an unverified preservation event, regulatory exemption, additional finding or new evidentiary hearing to make that route available. No missing historical filing is treated as default.

**Historical reconciliation:** No Associate changes judgment or abandons every sufficient ground of the corresponding historical position. The actual legal premises narrow the usable reasoning: actual *MCI* withholds a universal major-questions exception; section 422 excludes the new mission and modernization tools; and the corrected statutory commands retain their independent predicates. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain the historical integrated statutory ground on the older law. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain the historical independent coverage/compliance ground without its broader remedial-discretion alternative. This is not certification that every historical rationale or quotation is adopted. Assembly must preserve the individual grounds and explicit withheld propositions rather than import full historical joins or authorship.

If the affirming position controls, the Fourth Circuit's invalidation of the August 28, 1996 regulations for want of the claimed authority stands in the customary-marketing scope. No independent advertising holding or First Amendment ruling follows, and therapeutic-claim products remain outside the categorical issue.

Neither the full regulation nor any stayed provision is unconditionally reinstated by a bare coverage holding. No replacement rule, immediate tobacco ban, recall, damages, retrospective liability or additional factfinding is ordered by these commitments.
