### Common sufficient grounds

**Equal opportunity for timely cross-filing.** When Pennsylvania permits major parties to cross-nominate for the section 2870(f) offices, it may not reject an otherwise qualifying minor party's pre-primary nomination solely because its chosen candidate has filed major-party nomination petitions or later wins those primaries, absent an interest sufficiently connected to the unequal burden. The offices are common-pleas judge, Philadelphia Municipal Court judge, Philadelphia Traffic Court judge, elective school director and justice of the peace. Childress's nomination papers preceded the primary and she lost neither major-party primary. Ordinary primary finality therefore does not explain the distinction in her application.

Burdick's calibrated review governs: examine the character and magnitude of the actual injury, the precise interests asserted and how far those interests require the burden. Severe restrictions require compelling justification and narrow tailoring; reasonable nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily rest on important regulatory interests. Discrimination matters to the actual burden and fit, but it does not automatically convert every unequal election procedure into a severe restriction. The application fails without choosing strict scrutiny or announcing a new fixed intermediate tier for every discriminatory ballot law.

Timmons sustains the particular Minnesota minor/major nomination restriction in its complete system; its five-Associate merits coalition expressly did not adopt universal anti-fusion approval, a pure rational-basis rule or incumbent-protection justification. Pennsylvania has itself allowed the same overlapping candidate nomination for major parties at these offices. That feature changes the justification inquiry. Neither the candidate's continuing availability nor the Party's ability to endorse removes the unequal legal nomination opportunity. Norman likewise protects real party organization and independently qualified participation while preserving meaningful support requirements.

The four preserved interests do not justify this timely application. Childress is no sore loser and did not await primary results before filing. Additional party association does not add another individual candidate when major-party cross-nomination already exists. Voluntary nomination does not involuntarily divert the Party's votes, and allowing major-party alliances while forbidding this alliance does not itself encourage new candidates. General preventive election administration is permissible under Storer, Jenness and Munro as retained by Timmons; it still must explain the distinction actually imposed. The Commonwealth's failure to intervene is not default, and the Department's inadequate explanation is not a substitute for constitutional analysis. The late nonpartisanship and voter-identification arguments supply no preserved new factual record; neither establishes why timely minor-party participation must be excluded while major-party cross-filing is permitted.

The narrow common proposition leaves otherwise lawful uniform fusion restrictions, actual nomination deadlines, meaningful support rules, consent requirements, counting and ballot design available. It does not require duplicate votes, a separate party line, party-specific totals, judicial enactment of a uniform prohibition or reopening of a completed election. The independent associational claim remains undecided, and the lower court's deletion of that separate declaration remains appropriate.

**Rule 60(b)(6) and distinct prospective authority.** Timmons did not erase the independent equal-protection premise supporting Eshenbaugh's final judgment. ### Reversal ground: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg

**Operative rule.** A traveler who places closed personal luggage in a shared overhead area exposes it to ordinary movement, repositioning, incidental contact and associated compression, but does not thereby expose concealed contents to a physical examination directed objectively at discerning their shape and density. An officer's exploratory squeezing that obtains that concealed information is a search even without opening the bag. The inquiry concerns the character of the physical examination, not the officer's undisclosed motive or the bare fact that touching occurred.

Katz preserves privacy a person seeks to maintain even in a publicly accessible setting; Place recognizes luggage as a repository of private effects. The entering register *Privacy in a rented vehicle and personal luggage*, effective May 30, 1995 and joined by all eight Associates, separates the bag's privacy from the surrounding conveyance. Bond zipped his bag and kept it above his seat. Those measures protect its contents notwithstanding other passengers' practical access to its exterior. Possible unwanted interference does not itself amount to public exposure of every fact an examination could uncover.

The shared minimum here is a tactile examination, not simply shifting luggage to reach a seat or make space. Cantu felt and squeezed bags as he returned down the bus and discerned a hard brick-like object with edges and a different density. His own account describes hard pressure, though not destructive pressure. Bond's more extensive description and the parties' dispute about the pants-leg packaging need not be resolved to classify that examination. The District Court's description of minimal intrusiveness is respected as to the limited physical contact, but it does not itself answer the constitutional question. A brief examination can expose otherwise concealed information.

Dickerson's register, *Contraband recognized through a lawful protective patdown*, effective June 7, 1993, permits recognition from authorized touching and forbids added exploration to create probable cause. Bond is not a Terry frisk case, and there was no initial protective authority to transplant. The relevant principle is the boundary between authorized contact and added acquisition of concealed information. Hicks likewise distinguishes lawful observation from additional physical manipulation; the absence of entry into the bag is not conclusive. Flippo preserves the distinction between a visible container exterior and closed contents without creating a general exception for an officer lawfully present nearby.

Ciraolo and Riley concern information exposed to public observation. Police may observe exposed information deliberately and attentively; the Court does not require accidental perception or blind officers to ordinary publicly available facts. But tactile examination of the contents of a zipped bag differs from seeing its exterior or noticing an object through contact that ordinary handling supplies. The Government's large-object and soft-fabric points are relevant, but neither establishes that Bond offered the concealed package for a contents examination merely by using ordinary carry-on luggage. The rule requires no count of squeezes, fixed pressure measurement, universal contact ban or determination whether the rack had doors.

**Justification.** Before this examination Cantu lacked individualized suspicion, and no separately supported search authority is supplied. A completed lawful immigration inquiry does not authorize this investigation of the bag. The later discovery of drugs cannot retrospectively supply probable cause under Dickerson and Wardlow's objective sequence. No border-search, safety, inventory, canine-sniff or other exception is invented.

**Remedy.** Reverse the no-search ruling that sustained the denial of Bond's suppression motion and remand the criminal case for proceedings consistent with the search holding. The drugs were obtained through the challenged examination and cannot be admitted on the rejected no-search rationale. The Government expressly does not rely on the subsequent consent as an independent admission ground; the remand must not reopen that surrendered route or manufacture a consent hearing. Robinette's separation of voluntariness from causal independence reinforces why permission alone would not automatically cure prior illegality, but no new consent adjudication is required here.

Bond's motion also sought suppression of the subsequent confessions, so the mandate concerns that actual motion rather than silently reducing it to the physical drugs. A search-characterization ruling is not a new, complete adjudication of every unreviewed causal or statement-specific question. The lower courts must implement the reversal in the existing criminal case at the actual scope of those issues; no independent justification or attenuation defense is found preserved by this handoff. The Court neither guarantees a new opportunity to assert one nor declares every later warned statement automatically admissible or automatically suppressed under an unexamined rule. No acquittal, unconditional release, new trial, resentencing or additional evidentiary hearing follows automatically from the granted determination.

### Affirmance ground: Scalia and Breyer

**Operative position.** The protected interest is in information withheld from exposure, not in preventing an officer from perceiving tactile information that ordinary foreseeable handling would reveal. Where a soft bag is placed among accessible, crowded overhead luggage and its sizeable hard contents can be discerned by the limited squeezing incident to such handling, careful or purposeful perception of those exposed contours does not become a search because the observer is an officer. Materially more intrusive manipulation, opening the bag or bodily frisking would present a different question.

These two accept the independent privacy of closed luggage but distinguish that general interest from the particular contours exposed through the fabric here. Bond acknowledged that passengers might handle and move the bag. The District Court characterized the contact as minimally intrusive, and the record does not establish extensive repeated probing. A large solid package in a soft bag can communicate shape and density when gripped or compressed in ordinary stowage. The law should not make the same physical contact constitutional when a traveler moves a bag and unconstitutional when an officer attends to what the contact reveals.

Ciraolo and Riley supply the objective exposure analogy; Katz does not protect an expectation contradicted by ordinary public access to the information at issue. Dickerson concerned a bodily frisk whose limited protective justification had ended before further manipulation. It supplies no universal rule that attentive feeling of an accessible bag is forbidden. Hicks involved moving an object to reveal information not otherwise exposed; the present disagreement is whether the bag's actual contour information was already exposed to foreseeable handling. Flippo's opening of a closed briefcase likewise differs from limited exterior contact.

The strongest objection is that this would transform tolerated physical access into unlimited investigative authority. Their answer is confined to the actual contact and package. They do not authorize all touching, all pressure or opening and rearranging enclosed effects. Nor do they rest on the agent's private purpose. They find no objective difference sufficient on this record to separate the contact from what Bond could reasonably expect other luggage users to do, and therefore would affirm without reaching a fruit-of-illegality question. Later consent need not be revived because their ground is no antecedent search.

### Governing propositions and qualifications

**Personal right and practical burden.** The register *Personal arms protection, incorporation and necessary acquisition*, effective through Love, December 11, 1995, and Hale, February 12, 1996, identifies the individual holder and keeps coverage distinct from lawful regulation. Wright, October 5, 1998, supplies *Core-Preservation Review*. Stage 1 identifies protected keeping, bearing, acquisition, necessary ammunition or transportation through constitutional text, history and precedent. Stage 2 examines the restriction's practical operation. Core deprivation requires a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective materially less restrictive alternative; substantial regulation requires an important objective, substantial relationship and no unnecessary or effective elimination of protected activity; genuinely incidental regulation without meaningful interference receives deferential reasonableness review, retaining prohibitions on arbitrary administration, discrimination and cumulative destruction. The independent boundary against abolishing meaningful ordinary defensive home possession remains. Routine labels do not determine the tier.

FOP, October 7, 1998, already applies that framework to this qualifying disability and official-use exclusion, with all eight Associates joining Souter's opinion. Preventing armed recurrence is important. The adjudicated violent conduct, defined domestic relationship, counsel and jury-where-entitled safeguards, and conviction-relief qualifications connect the disability substantially to that objective. A service firearm remains accessible to its possessor; long service and governmental use do not eliminate that connection. This is a substantial burden, not incidental paperwork, but the present record supplies no distinct home-possession application or general abolition of defensive arms. This rule covers the independent adequacy theory presented, not every railroad duty or every allegation of violation of federal law.

Its missing predicate was an actual federally participating warning installation at the crossing: the proposed gates were abandoned, funds redirected and circuitry at another location did not warn motorists. ### Statutory rule and its relationship to entering law

The opening clause attributes the failure to the applicant. It therefore requires a shortfall in reasonable efforts, or greater fault, in developing the relevant claim's factual basis; it does not punish a prisoner merely because the State denied a reasonable attempt to develop material facts. Diligence concerns effort in light of the information then available, not whether investigation succeeded. Counsel must pursue identified material leads through reasonably available procedures and request the factual development the claim actually requires. A generic request is neither invariably sufficient nor invariably deficient: its adequacy depends on what counsel could reasonably identify and do. Neither omniscient discovery of every public document nor a rule that any requested investigator establishes diligence is adopted.

If applicant-side failure triggers the opening condition, the applicant must satisfy either (A)(i), a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court to collateral review, or (A)(ii), a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and also satisfy (B), clear-and-convincing facts establishing that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. These alternatives and cumulative conditions remain distinct. Williams's concession concerning (B) disposes of a triggered hearing bar without an unnecessary finding about every other exception. It is not a Court finding of guilt or a substitute for deciding whether the trigger applies.

The statute changes the former-law consequence of negligent factual underdevelopment where its conditions apply. This timeline's Keeney retained Townsend's deliberate-bypass qualification and held ordinary negligence insufficient to defeat a necessary hearing on a properly presented claim. That rule was judicial and operated under the former statute. Section 2254(e)(2) newly places an applicant-attributed failure under restrictive exceptions that expressly distinguish due diligence and require an innocence showing. Reading the new trigger as confined to intentional bypass would import a culpability limitation Congress did not state and largely preserve the former hearing entitlement despite the enacted restriction. The Court can respect Keeney as the former-law rule while giving the new provision its independent operation; it does not falsely describe the statute as ratifying a cause-and-prejudice Keeney holding that never controlled here.

The no-fault interpretation has the opposite defect. It treats the attribution to the applicant as unnecessary and would require the exceptional innocence/new-rule route even when a State prevented diligent factual development. The existence of (A)(ii)'s due-diligence alternative does not remove the opening attribution requirement. The exception has work when the applicant is responsible for failure to develop the claim's factual basis but later relies on a genuinely previously undiscoverable predicate; the Court need not decide every possible combination of old and new facts to reject the no-fault reading.

Keeney continues to govern its former-law domain and prevents the lower court's proposed historical cause-and-prejudice rule from becoming current law by citation. Coleman remains independently applicable to an actual adequate state procedural default. Vasquez continues to distinguish supplementation of an exhausted claim from a fundamentally altered new claim. No universal exemption from exhaustion, default or statutory merits restrictions follows from diligence.

### Common statutory propositions and the genuine construction fork

**Common minimum — all eight.** Section 2254(d)(1) contains two alternatives with independent work. A state decision contrary to the governing legal rule established by this Court can satisfy the first without a previous case with identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts is another contrary-to situation. The Fourth Circuit's requirement of square conflict as to both law and fact cannot be a universal prerequisite. The unreasonable-application inquiry is objective. The fact that one or more judges approved the state decision does not establish reasonableness, and the applicant need not prove that every actual reasonable jurist agrees that the state court erred. Judicial disagreement is neither an automatic safe harbor nor a vote-counting test.

Clearly established law means this Court's holdings at the time of the relevant state merits decision, not later holdings or dicta. Strickland's existing general rule can supply clearly established law even though no previous case contains this exact collection of mitigation. Lower federal decisions cannot themselves supply the required Supreme Court law. Thomas's Fretwell observation about state courts and lower federal authority reinforces the latter distinction but does not independently control it. Later precedent cannot retroactively make the state court's result unreasonable; the operative Strickland and limited Fretwell propositions preceded the June 1997 adjudication.

**Distinct-clause construction — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** A state court that identifies the governing rule and applies it to different facts does not act contrary to that rule merely because the federal court would reach another result. The second clause authorizes relief when the application is objectively unreasonable, a legal judgment distinct from whether it is simply incorrect. The federal judge independently assesses that standard rather than deferring to the state court's self-description or conducting a survey of jurists. The generality of the governing rule affects the range of reasonable applications; it does not immunize any result bearing its name. The rule is not limited to gross factual mistakes or identical Supreme Court outcomes, and it imposes no clear-and-convincing burden on a legal question.

Their strongest objection to the broader approach is that classifying every incorrect application of a prescribed rule as contrary would leave little separate function for unreasonable application. Congress chose that latter phrase to restrict collateral relief beyond ordinary correction of legal error. They therefore cannot join a proposition that merely identifying Strickland restores plenary error correction of every application. They can join an actual contrary-rule holding and an independently supported objective-unreasonableness determination here without adopting that broader proposition.

**Governing-rule construction — Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** When this Court has prescribed a rule to govern the kind of claim presented, a state disposition inconsistent with its application is contrary to clearly established law; federal courts must identify and apply that governing rule independently. Strickland prescribes performance and prejudice inquiries rather than leaving States free to choose a different constitutional test. Unreasonable application retains work where established holdings require derivation or extension beyond an already prescribed governing rule. This reading preserves a meaningful legal-review function for the first clause without demanding factual duplication.

Their strongest difficulty is the concern that a general governing standard can make every ordinary application eligible for independent error review, diminishing Congress's second clause. They limit their formulation to rules prescribed for the category of claim, not any high-level constitutional principle that could bear on it; unsettled derivation and extension retain objective-unreasonableness review. They do not treat any federal judge's preferred answer as compelled by clearly established law. They can additionally join a separately reasoned conclusion that this particular state application is unreasonable even under the other four's more restrictive approach. That compatibility on application does not imply agreement on exclusive clause boundaries.

Neither group invalidates section 2254(d), adopts a facial Suspension Clause or Article III holding from the cursory lower challenge, or treats avoidance arguments as an independent grant of relief. Section 2254(a)'s unlawful-custody requirement, section (d)'s gateway and the ultimate constitutional merits remain distinct. Section (d)(2) factual unreasonableness and section (e)(1)'s presumption/clear-and-convincing rebuttal are not substituted for legal analysis; no new federal hearing or section (e)(2) determination is made.

The exact current-term supplement confirms the same limits. Smith v. Robbins restores a defined missing counsel-assisted appellate-screening function; it preserves ordinary Strickland review for defective performance within a supplied function. Roe v. Flores-Ortega separately measures consultation performance and causal loss of an appeal; its unanimous no-probable-appellate-success rule follows from that lost proceeding. Neither is a presumed-prejudice rule for deficient sentencing investigation or a construction of amended section (d)(1); both federal applications preceded enactment. Here counsel performed a sentencing function, and actual sentencing prejudice must be established. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer's Flores-Ortega commitments reinforce contextual performance rather than automatic deficiency; Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg's contrary duty application does not dispense with the shared causal requirement. These current-term positions support the stated distinctions without changing the statutory or merits commitments.

### Governing prejudice rule, Fretwell and the jury question

All eight reject using Fretwell as a general extra unfairness requirement. The entering register *Loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice*, effective January 25, 1993, expressly preserves ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice and confines the unavailable-benefit rule to a displaced substantive entitlement where no other fair or reliable protection was lost. O'Connor authored that limiting Court opinion, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas among the sitting Associates. Its limitation is controlling, not a nonbinding concurrence. Stevens's dissent favored the ordinary-probability inquiry still more broadly. Omitted lawful mitigation is not an advantage forbidden by governing substantive law. Lockett, Eddings and Skipper recognize the relevance of such material; they do not demand its indiscriminate presentation or establish ineffective assistance by omission alone.

Prejudice requires a reasonable probability sufficient to undermine confidence, not proof that a different sentence was more likely than not. The entire sentencing picture matters: actual mitigation and aggravation, the evidence competent investigation would have produced, and its adverse consequences. Review assumes a reasonable conscientious sentencer, not nullification, arbitrary mercy or an idiosyncratic juror. Virginia's actual statute requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree. Accordingly, a reasonable probability that at least one reasonable juror would decline death is a legally different outcome; twelve votes for life are unnecessary. A bare possibility of a sympathetic holdout does not satisfy Strickland. All eight can join this legal consequence even though Scalia and Thomas find the required probability absent.

The State's ordinary-prejudice alternative is expressly assessed. Its court recited both unreliability language and the reasonable-probability standard. An incorrect additional gloss does not erase an actually independent and sufficient correct ground merely because the court cited Fretwell. Conversely, reciting the correct words does not cure an inquiry materially narrowed by the wrong framework or an incomplete treatment of the actual mitigation. That is the central application disagreement, not a failure to notice the alternative.

### Relief position — Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

**Gateway.** To the extent the state court demanded an additional showing of fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice, its rule is contrary to Strickland and the Court's express Fretwell limitation. Its nominal ordinary-prejudice discussion does not supply an adequate independent basis on this record. It largely treated the omitted case as favorable impressions of relatives and acquaintances, instead of considering the materially different documentary childhood deprivation, intellectual/educational history and evidence of functioning in structured settings with the aggravation. Even under the distinct-clause construction, that application is objectively unreasonable, not merely an answer the federal court finds less persuasive. The omitted evidence supplies explanations and a response to future danger that the generic character testimony did not.

These six adopt the whole-record unreasonable-application conclusion as an independently sufficient statutory ground, alongside their precise objection to the forbidden Fretwell requirement. The four broader-construction Associates may also support gateway eligibility under their independent application understanding; that additional scope is not part of the six's common statutory ground. The state court's failure is legal evaluation of the full supported sentencing picture, not a finding that every affidavit or proposed diagnosis must be presumed true under section (d)(2).

**Performance.** Strickland assesses counsel's actions from the time of representation. Counsel began sentencing preparation about a week before trial, failed to obtain the youth records because of a mistaken view of access law, and acknowledged he would have used them if he knew their contents. Asking Williams and relatives for helpful material did not reasonably end investigation of readily identifiable records and leads. Their initial stable-home account did not justify an uninformed failure to investigate documented deprivation and limited functioning. Counsel's consultation with experts and the decision to stop cumulative character testimony after damaging cross-examination are real professional acts, but they do not explain or excuse failure to learn what the documentary case contained. No rule requires every available witness or every adverse juvenile file to be introduced; reasonable investigation precedes an informed choice about what to present.

The Warden's preserved adequate-performance defense therefore fails on this supported investigation ground, independently of whether particular wife, daughter or Elliott testimony should tactically have been used. The six do not presume the lower courts' assumed deficiency to be a concession, rely on counsel's alleged mental illness, or reopen the separate expert-access claims. The existing developed record suffices; no additional hearing is necessary to establish the identified investigation lapse.

**Actual prejudice.** The murder and surrounding criminal conduct are grave. The case includes prior armed robbery, burglary and larceny, recent parole release, vehicle thefts, the fire-induced attack and robbery of another elderly man, the devastating assault of an elderly woman, jail arson, violent urges and two experts' future-danger predictions. Those facts do not disappear. The defense also did present character witnesses, limited intellectual evidence and a mercy argument based on Williams's voluntary confession; it is inaccurate to describe a complete absence of mitigation.

Against that record, the childhood materials establish a substantially different mitigating account: severe parental alcoholism, neglected and hungry children, filthy conditions, parental imprisonment for neglect, removal from home, violence and fear. A later improved family report qualifies the history without erasing early deprivation. Limited intellectual functioning and special education give the fuller history significance beyond the limited intellectual description already heard. Supported conduct, training and relationships in structured settings, and the prison commendations, provide concrete information beyond the poorly informed favorable character opinions. Evidence of adjustment addresses future danger but does not prove lifetime confinement, guarantee nonviolence or erase jail arson. Possible parole and the State's society-wide theory remain part of the inquiry.

The juvenile records also carried substantial adverse information, including fourteen described offenses and repeated commitments. The six do not assume that every adverse entry necessarily would be admitted, but assess the State's strongest supported argument that developing this history would expose further persistent criminality. That risk does not make the materially different explanatory mitigation cumulative or negate the reasonable probability of a lawful nondeath outcome. Property offenses and commitments carry real weight without being identical to the severe violence already before the jury. The inquiry concerns the total sentencing picture rather than adding favorable items while discarding adverse ones.

No fetal-alcohol diagnosis or disputed new expert prediction is necessary. No claim is made that wife, daughter and Elliott all testified live or that the state hearing judge credited every proffer. The supported documentary history and concrete conduct evidence, viewed alongside those properly supported relationships and actual trial evidence, suffice. The state hearing judge's recommendation is relevant as a reasoned evaluation by the judge familiar with the trial, not an authoritative personal veto or the final state judgment. The legal conclusion independently follows: there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious juror, applying Virginia law, would have declined death. The confidence required to sustain this sentence is undermined.

**Remedy.** Reverse the Fourth Circuit insofar as it reversed sentencing relief on this mitigation claim, and return for reinstatement or implementation of a conforming conditional writ. The State must receive an appropriate opportunity for a lawful new penalty proceeding; if it does not use that opportunity, the death sentence must be replaced by a lawful nondeath disposition. The federal court determines an appropriate implementation period under ordinary procedure; the Court invents no fixed number of days. This is federal collateral relief concerning the sentence, not a direct merits reversal of every Virginia judgment. The convictions, other rejected/defaulted cross-appeal claims and ungranted rulings remain undisturbed. No acquittal, release, mandatory life-without-parole sentence, new expert appointment, new hearing on collateral claims or new execution/stay action is entered.

### Affirmance position — Scalia and Thomas

Both reject the displaced broad Fretwell proposition and accept that lawful mitigation receives ordinary Strickland analysis. ### Component definitions and the two supported constructions

The granted component is the §1962(d)/§1964(c) claim for retaliatory termination, treated on this review as neither a racketeering predicate nor otherwise independently wrongful under RICO. The independent substantive fraud, stock, debenture and guarantee rulings are not reopened. The existing appellate vacatur of the pendent-state-claim dismissal and remand remain effective under either proposed Supreme disposition; sanctions, costs and service rulings likewise retain their existing scope.

**Affirmance construction:** Civil recovery for injury by reason of a RICO conspiracy requires injury caused by an act of racketeering or another act independently wrongful under RICO. Agreement can make a conspirator responsible for a qualifying injurious act committed by another; it does not turn every act advancing the agreement into an independently compensable RICO injury. Retaliatory termination of the kind established for this question therefore cannot support this civil claim, even when it directly advances or conceals the alleged scheme. The exact coverage of other nonpredicate substantive RICO wrongs need not be decided to reject this non-RICO termination injury.

**Best objection to affirmance:** Section 1964(c) expressly reaches violations of all §1962, including its separate prohibition of conspiracy. Requiring an injury-causing RICO wrong arguably transfers a substantive-offense limitation into a distinct agreement offense. Salinas makes agreement a distinct basis of responsibility, and no borrowed common-law principle may erase the enacted reference to subsection (d).

**Affirmance answer:** The unlawful agreement and the private compensable injury answer separate questions. Civil conspiracy's background links recovery to an independently actionable injurious act rather than agreement alone. Requiring a RICO wrong preserves subsection (d)'s work in attributing that injury to agreeing defendants who did not personally perform the act. It does not require personal commission of two predicates, personal enterprise direction by every conspirator, a prior conviction or a special injury beyond the injury from the actionable conduct. Holmes's contextual causal rule remains independently required.

**Reversal construction:** A direct business or property injury intentionally caused by an act carrying out a proved agreement to violate §1962 may be injury by reason of the conspiracy, even when the injurious act is not itself a racketeering predicate or an independent RICO violation. Subsection (d) is an enacted wrong; importing the civil-tort rule does not justify choosing the substantive subsections as the only injury-generating violations. This does not permit every foreseeable consequence: factual causation and Holmes's contextual directness remain necessary, and the agreement must satisfy Salinas's actual unlawful-object requirement.

**Best objection to reversal:** This could allow recovery for employment or contract disputes that substantive RICO excludes, simply by alleging a larger scheme. **Reversal answer:** Proving a qualifying knowing conspiracy and direct injury from an act implementing it is substantive work, not a label. This record's asserted direct retaliatory firing differs from Steamfitters's intervening smoker/illness/treatment chain. The broader construction removes the categorical legal exclusion but proves no conspiracy, causation or damages and automatically revives no rejected substantive fraud theory.

They do not convert every optional agreement provision into a mandatory bargaining topic, require identical individual terms, or disregard Moreau's earlier-hire practice route.

**Statutory agency deference.** O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas would hold this advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not receive Chevron's controlling statutory weight. The implementing grant authorizes administration and regulation; it does not automatically give every subsequent advisory response the force of a rule or adjudication. The letter remains eligible for Skidmore consideration according to its actual reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. On the supported content it adds a specific-consent restriction that the statute and permissive agreement regulation do not supply, and is unpersuasive for the reasons stated above. No finding about an unproved change of agency policy is needed.

Scalia would not join that limitation of Chevron by the letter's advisory form. He would treat the responsible Labor Department's statutory construction as eligible for authoritative consideration within the real implementing delegation, but reject this particular construction as impermissible in light of the statute's request trigger and the separate nature of the original agreement condition. His affirmance therefore does not support the four-Associate no-Chevron proposition.

Breyer also rejects the exclusion of Chevron based on this form and would accept the agency's reasonable construction; that different merits evaluation separates him from Scalia. Stevens and Ginsburg need not resolve the general Chevron-form issue: their independent statutory reading suffices, and the letter's consistent-with-text position reinforces it. Neither is provisionally committed to the four-Associate exclusion of controlling statutory weight.

**Own-regulation interpretation.** The five affirming Associates retain Auer but conclude that it does not allow changing §553.23(a)(2)'s permission to include preservation, use or cashout provisions into a requirement of specific consent for compulsory use. The regulation deals with lawful agreement formation and optional consistent terms; §553.25's good-faith, reasonable-period and undue-disruption provisions protect requests without announcing exclusive employee scheduling. On their construction there is no pertinent ambiguity permitting the letter's added restriction. They do not overrule Auer, require formal rulemaking for every regulatory interpretation, or deny its fair-and-considered rule merely because an agency used an opinion letter.

Stevens and Ginsburg would find the consent-based reading consistent with the whole regulatory scheme and the statutory rule they adopt; they would not join the conclusion that the letter contradicts plain regulatory text. Breyer regards that reading as permissible under Auer as well, subject to the actual responsible-agency and fair-consideration predicates. His statutory-deference ground is independently sufficient and does not depend on inventing missing internal procedures. No opinion-letter statement establishes the actual terms of the class members' agreements.

**Agreement and remedy question.** The five affirming Associates would not order an agreement-specific remand merely because agreements might contain restrictions. The granted categorical theory fails, and the record identifies no preserved controlling term establishing a violation; they make no finding that agreements do not exist or that every employee consented to forced use. Independently valid agreement obligations and other applicable law remain at their actual scope. The three reversing Associates would withhold judgment for the County because their adopted consent requirement makes the actual terms material; they would not simply restore the District Court's categorical declaration or presume all employees lack a valid authorizing arrangement.

### Full operative limits and remedy

The creation of compensatory time remains subject to §207(o)(1)'s at-least-time-and-one-half rate and (o)(2)'s proper representative or individual prior-agreement route. Moreau's earlier-hire regular-practice route supplies the agreement without a further individual agreement when its actual predicates are met; its later-hire rule applies where the chosen organization cannot lawfully make a binding representative agreement. No general bargaining right or presumption of identical agreements follows. Section 553.23 permits recorded unwritten understandings; express employment conditions require knowing voluntary acceptance and information about preservation, use and cashout. Notice and failure to object can evidence agreement, but coercion and pressure remain forbidden. Existing representative agreements and the transition qualification retain their own terms.

The public-safety, emergency-response and seasonal cap is 480 hours; other covered work has a 240-hour cap. Additional overtime at the applicable cap requires overtime compensation. Cashout may occur at any time at the regular rate when received; termination payment uses not less than the higher of the last-three-years average regular rate and final regular rate. The permanent-break, prior-cashout and shorter-final-period averaging rules remain; no rate calculation or termination dispute is decided.

Employee requests remain protected by reasonable-period use absent undue disruption. Customary scheduling, peak demands, emergency staffing and substitutes inform the reasonable period; compatible agreement terms govern where applicable. Mere inconvenience is insufficient for denial; reasonable good-faith anticipation of an unreasonable burden on acceptable public service is required. Section 553.25(b) still forbids using time to evade statutory overtime and coercing acceptance of more time than the employer can realistically and in good faith make available within a reasonable request period. The rule excludes separately defined other compensatory time, including the identified pre-April 15, 1986 accruals. No violation of these independent safeguards is found. The court-reporter transcript exception has separate per-page and outside-duty-hours conditions and does no work for this Sheriff's Department policy.

The five affirming Associates would leave the Fifth Circuit's judgment for all defendants in force, with the employees' declaration and dependent $21,360 fee award displaced. They would order no further agreement proceedings, new fee award, damages or negotiation. The three reversing Associates would vacate that defense judgment and remand for actual agreement coverage, terms and validity under their consent rule; the categorical declaration and fee award would not automatically revive. Neither route mandates a hearing, presumes classwide agreement terms, adjudicates constitutional property, creates a collective-bargaining right, permits confiscation, or resolves abandoned refusal/retaliation claims.

These are distinct proposition coalitions: five for the statutory/default and no-Auer grounds; four for excluding Chevron on this letter's supported advisory status; Scalia's affirmance does not augment that latter rationale; three for a consent-based agreement remand, with Breyer's independently sufficient deference ground distinguished from Stevens and Ginsburg's statutory ground. Assembly must determine any additional joins from its separately authorized inputs. No apparent overlap is treated as a fictional unified rationale.
