### Components and operative limits

**R1 — Review of the challenged condition: all eight Associates.** Lanphere, effective April 18, 1995, requires First Amendment review of this speech-selected access-and-use condition. The absence of a freestanding right to every government record does not answer that question. Ordinary Central Hudson/Fox review governs the conceded lawful, nonmisleading commercial activity: substantial interest, real harm and material advancement, then reasonable fit. The agreed first-prong premise for the activity below is not reopened by demanding production of an unexamined particular letter. The distinct proposed own-sales application and the Register's noncommercial or mixed character remain distinct issues; describing the enterprise as a seller does not decide the classification of every expression. Neither a higher tier for all commercial expression nor immunity for every address use is adopted.

**R2 — The own-injury route and facial characterization: all eight Associates at this level.** The plaintiff pleaded and maintained its own injury; it is not restricted to asserting absent customers' rights. Commercial overbreadth cannot substitute for establishing a violation, but that principle does not dispose of its own challenge to the rule applied to it. Nor does the possibility of valid laws against deception, coercion or harassment prove that this particular categorical condition is valid. A defect in a rule applied to the plaintiff may support relief against that application; the adjudicated defect and equitable need determine any further reach. The newly confirmed editorial material also prevents a blanket premise that only hypothetical commercial speakers are involved. These propositions neither approve universal facial relief nor decide a distinct noncommercial overbreadth theory.

**R3 — Four Associates leave the commercial merits open on a bounded remand.** Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand for application of Lanphere to respondent's maintained claims. The database theory's recorded evidentiary failure and the separate dissemination/privacy argument must be kept distinct. The existence of statutory exceptions does not automatically defeat advancement, but the State must explain why the actual selected line materially protects privacy and reasonably fits its burden. The narrower focus on current addresses, the prohibition's limitation to information obtained under paragraph (3), the stated purposes of permitted access, and the competing descriptions of actual dissemination warrant their own analysis. The statutory possibility of publication does not itself establish that permitted and prohibited uses have the same practical effects. Conversely, petitioners' assertions about journalists and resellers are not findings proving their difference. The remand permits assessment of the existing evidence and legal comparison without presuming either side wins.

The agreed lawful/nonmisleading premise and the already litigated address-service activity remain part of that assessment. Lanphere did not require proof of the text of every future letter, and neither does this remand. The court may resolve a supported narrower application on the existing record. It need not conduct a new trial or demand numerical studies. If the State's showing fails under the governing standard, plaintiff-specific relief may follow; if material advancement is established, the unaddressed reasonable-fit question still must be decided. No categorical requirement to regulate all sources or choose the least restrictive means is added. No automatic opportunity to relitigate waived cost arguments or cure the rejected database showing is conferred; ordinary procedural rules govern any permissible supplementation.

**R4 — O'Connor and Scalia decide the confined commercial question for the State.** They would reverse the First Amendment liability determination insofar as it holds this condition invalid for the conceded commercial address-distribution/solicitation activity adjudicated below. They accept R1 scrutiny and the substantial privacy interest, but regard this confined official-disclosure restriction as a permissible balance: it limits acquisition and sales use of current addresses through this statutory route, preserves access for the stated public-information and investigative purposes, and does not prohibit communicating independently acquired information. Official bulk provision for targeted commercial use can expose arrestees to a different dissemination pattern and intrusion than the uses for which the State retains access. Their ground is the disclosure-point privacy and reasonable-fit judgment they expressly adopted in Lanphere, applied to this narrower statutory footprint; it does not depend on proving the speculative unreliable-database theory or reviving cost arguments.

They do not adopt petitioners' disputed quantitative or journalistic-practice assertions as adjudicated facts. Their position is that the limited disclosure distinction and supported predictive privacy judgment suffice without requiring a measured comparative distribution study. They do not decide that every journalist, investigator or commercial requester behaves as alleged, or that every permitted and prohibited use differs. They distinguish Discovery Network's established physical equivalence and 44 Liquormart's suppression of truthful price information from protection of another person's current address at an official disclosure point. Their result does not overrule Lanphere or withdraw its requirement of scrutiny: its as-applied invalidation did not make every later solicitation-related records rule unconstitutional.

R4 decides only the ordinary commercial condition on the accepted premise below. It does not sustain a restriction on independently acquired information, decide the classification of the Register's editorial content, or reject a claim that an agency wrongly denied a qualifying journalistic request. Those questions require their own statutory and constitutional treatment. O'Connor and Scalia therefore do not direct dismissal of the entire action.

**R6 — Stevens and Kennedy affirm the unconstitutional commercial application.** These two retain R1 scrutiny and R2's own-injury route but reject R3's reservation of the already litigated commercial claim. On the accepted lawful and nonmisleading activity, the State has not justified denying respondent access because its address-service publication enables commercial solicitation. The statutory distinction permits non-sales dissemination for journalistic and other enumerated purposes, potentially including public disclosure, while barring the conceded protected commercial distribution at issue. Its current-address and source limitations do not explain why that speech-selected line materially advances privacy. The unsupported database theory supplies no alternative justification. The separately offered home-intrusion rationale and argument about usual journalistic practices do not, for these two, establish a sufficient connection between the selected class and harm; they reject that showing on this record rather than require an empirical comparison of every future publication.

This application does not adopt a rule that every exception defeats a privacy measure or require regulation of every independent information source. The objection concerns the permitted uses and categorical sales condition within this statutory disclosure program. Lanphere's actual advancement holding, Kennedy's Edenfield reasoning and Stevens's Discovery Network reasoning support deciding that failure now. Shapero preserves the protection of the conceded lawful solicitation without deciding a general right to all arrest records. The two do not hold that unwanted mail never affects privacy, that every authorized requester publishes each address, or that there is a proved illicit legislative motive. They need not reach the fourth-prong fit question after finding the demonstrated advancement insufficient. The permitted alternatives and separate confidentiality restrictions retain their lawful operation.

**R6 judgment and remedy.** Stevens and Kennedy would affirm the Ninth Circuit's determination that the challenged commercial condition is unconstitutional as applied to respondent's adjudicated lawful, nonmisleading address-service publication/distribution, and remand for relief confined to that determination and any necessary preserved questions. They do not vacate that merits determination for renewed open reconsideration. The appellate court must identify and conform the actual relief to the adjudicated application and the parties properly before it; the two do not announce that an unrecovered decree actually exceeds that scope or automatically dissolve it. A decree extending beyond that supported scope would require limitation on remand. This is application-specific merits affirmance with a bounded remedial return, not universal facial invalidation, an immediate access order disregarding safety/confidentiality provisions, or adjudication of every distinct proposed own-sales or editorial claim.

**R5 — Remaining claims and remedy: common reservation, different merits instructions.** All eight preserve consideration of the maintained Register theory, the separately identified own-direct-sales use, and the actually preserved alternative grounds within the lawful scope of review. The three identified Fourteenth Amendment theories are not assigned to numbered complaint counts from unrecovered pleadings. Petitioners' cross-petition objection is not a finding of abandonment. Governing X-Citement Video allows a ground appearing in the record to defend an existing favorable judgment without enlarging it; it does not require immediate decision of every alternative or permit overturning a distinct adverse judgment without the requisite review. The exact relief sought and granted must be identified before applying that distinction. No final decision is made here on vagueness, equal protection, due process, the state constitutional theory, or a distinct statutory entitlement.

For the four in R3, the formal commitment is **vacate the appellate judgment and remand**, including reconsideration of the existing declaratory and injunctive relief in light of the maintained claims and the actual decree. It is not a merits rejection of respondent's commercial claim and not a direction to dissolve every injunction against every defendant. For the two in R4, the commitment is **reverse the commercial First Amendment determination and remand the remaining issues**, with relief dependent solely on that rejected commercial ground removed or modified; any independently supportable protection must rest on its own established claim and equitable basis. They do not join an instruction that leaves their decided commercial ground open.

Only LAPD appealed below; the reported Attorney General injunction and the scope of the signed decree are not reconstructed here. The remand must address the judgment and parties properly before the appellate court rather than invent an order releasing every agency. None of the eight guarantees interim protection, automatically reinstates or dissolves an unidentified order, commands universal disclosure, prescribes a replacement privacy scheme, awards damages or fees, or sets a special mandate date. Existing separate confidentiality and safety/investigation restrictions retain their own lawful force. The precise final decree is necessary to implement relief, not to pretend that own-injury claims were absent.
