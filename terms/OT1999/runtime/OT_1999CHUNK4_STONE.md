## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the asserted RFRA entitlement to withhold ordinary income tax and to escape the ordinary additions solely because the nonpayment is religiously motivated.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A sincere objection to governmental expenditure does not confer control over common revenue

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply federal RFRA to the actual tax obligation. Assume for decision that the sincere conflict imposes a substantial burden, so the case does not turn on disbelief or on a rule that neutral taxes are categorically outside RFRA.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The government bears the compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means burden. Lee explains why compulsory taxation has a different structure from a service a person can decline, while the application-specific RFRA decisions require testing that explanation against the relief actually sought.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** RFRA does not require an exemption from this taxpayer’s ordinary income-tax obligation where the proposed relief would withhold or redirect the assessed contribution because of an objection to general governmental spending, and no alternative preserves complete, timely collection on the same terms.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The relevant interest is not the abstract importance of revenue. It is collecting this taxpayer’s assessed contribution through a system that does not let an individual direct or veto the uses of common funds. Exemption leaves a shortfall for other taxpayers. Earmarking an equivalent payment for approved purposes does not preserve the same fiscal system: it transfers part of Congress’s allocation authority to the objector and makes expenditure objections a basis for individualized tax budgets. Full payment accompanied by protest remains available but may not remove the religious burden; that fact must be acknowledged rather than called consent. RFRA can nevertheless permit a justified burden. The requested departure is incompatible with the particular function of compulsory common revenue, and sincerity does not make the cost disappear. This reasoning applies Lee through the government’s RFRA burden; it neither adopts a blanket taxation exception nor relies on the conclusion that commercial or civic participation forfeits religious exercise. Boerne’s state/local holding supplies no reason to avoid the federal statute.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule that all tax accommodations fail. An accommodation preserving full timely payment without shifting costs must be evaluated on its actual operation. The courts decide neither the truth of the religious belief nor the wisdom of the expenditure.

### Issue 2: Ordinary additions and distinct statutory defenses

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Address the associated additions because the supplied question includes them. The tax obligation and each remedy remain legally distinct.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The compulsory-payment interest includes a workable incentive to comply on time; religious motivation alone does not require the government to forgo ordinary consequences of deliberate nonpayment. Any enacted reasonable-cause or calculation defense retains its own elements.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The same conscientious objection that does not excuse the principal obligation does not, without more, require cancellation of the ordinary additions challenged here under RFRA.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Waiving enforcement consequences while retaining the nominal duty would leave the objector with an option to postpone payment that other taxpayers finance. That alternative does not secure the same timely contribution. This is not a conclusion that every additional charge is necessary: an erroneous amount, a penalty for protected advocacy or a defense satisfying an applicable statutory exception would present a different application. On the supplied theory, however, the only asserted reason for exemption is the conviction that made nonpayment deliberate. That establishes the claimed religious burden, not a less restrictive means of obtaining the legally due payment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No amount or statutory subsection absent from the record is invented, and no ordinary statutory defense is displaced by this RFRA conclusion.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The proposed collection alternative must preserve the function it replaces

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Assume sincere substantial burden and apply RFRA’s demanding justification inquiry to this collection proposal, separately from whether the underlying tax is owed. Stone does not reach the merits of the requested prospective relief: the declaration, the order to alter IRS practices, or an order requiring the IRS to collect by levy without interest or penalties. The Government's preserved objections under 28 U.S.C. § 2201(a) and 26 U.S.C. § 7421(a), together with standing and remedial authority, were not decided below, and RFRA's appropriate-relief clause does not itself answer them. This issue's holding is reached only as the ground for rejecting the RFRA refund claims, which rest on the asserted RFRA right to have the IRS collect by prompt levy instead of charging the ordinary additions and interest; it is not a ruling on prospective relief. Jurisdiction over the refund claims is stated under Issue 2.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The government must explain why this requested accommodation fails, rather than invoke a categorical tax exemption from RFRA. The taxpayer’s willingness to have money taken is relevant, but does not by itself show equivalent timely compliance.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** RFRA does not entitle these taxpayers to require the IRS to initiate compulsory collection on their behalf and cancel the ordinary consequences of their refusal to make timely payment where that substitution transfers collection burdens and risk to the government.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The couple’s proposal is closer to complete payment than an outright exemption, and deserves its own analysis. But an authorization to seize money is not the same transaction as timely payment. The agency must initiate the substitute process, use its collection authority and bear the delay and failure risks that self-payment avoids. A cancellation of interest and penalties would require the public to absorb those differences rather than merely accommodate the form in which a full timely payment arrives. No guaranteed, equally timely and cost-neutral mechanism is established in the supplied proposal. Lee’s concern with the practical operation of common revenue therefore applies without treating Lee as a categorical substitute for RFRA. The application-specific cases require the government to address a genuine alternative; they do not require it to regard a legally different collection undertaking as equivalent because its eventual gross proceeds might be the same. This conclusion does not turn on whether the religious distinction between paying and having money taken is persuasive to a judge. That distinction is the burden claimed; the statutory inquiry concerns the justified civil consequences.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A demonstrated arrangement providing complete timely payment without material administrative cost or default risk would require a fresh assessment. No rule that compulsory collection is always forbidden as an accommodation, or that tax administration is outside RFRA, is adopted.

### Issue 2: Interest and penalties retain different justifications

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the requested cancellation of both kinds of charges only on the supplied religious theory. Jurisdiction is decided before the merits. The District Court had jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1346(a)(1) over the RFRA claims to recover the additions and interest collected by levy. That provision reaches any sum alleged to have been in any manner wrongfully collected under the internal-revenue laws, and interest is assessed, collected and paid in the same manner as taxes (26 U.S.C. § 6601(e)(1)). The complaint pleads, and the Government's opposition describes, a claim for refund of the interest and additions that the IRS denied before suit; no section 7422(a) objection is raised. Section 1346(a)(1) is the United States' consent to this refund suit. Whether RFRA made those sums wrongful is the merits question, not a condition of jurisdiction. Section 6404(g), now 6404(i), lets the Tax Court determine whether the Secretary's failure to abate interest under section 6404 was an abuse of discretion. The RFRA interest claim, pleaded as essentially the same claim as the RFRA claim for the additions, seeks the return of interest collected in alleged violation of a different federal statute, not review of discretion under section 6404, and nothing in section 6404(g) withdraws section 1346(a)(1) jurisdiction over such a claim. Stone does not decide whether section 6404(g) is the exclusive route for review of a refusal to abate under section 6404 itself, when the operative abatement request was made, or whether RFRA's appropriate-relief clause supplies any monetary remedy or immunity waiver beyond the refund statute. He does not rely on the argument that the Tax Court lacks interest jurisdiction without a deficiency.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Interest ordinarily accounts for the time during which the Treasury lacks the payment; penalties or additions serve the distinct enforcement function supplied by their statutes. Neither follows solely from the label attached to the other.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The religious objection does not require the public to finance the delay in receiving these taxes or to dispense with ordinary enforcement consequences for the chosen nonpayment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Interest relief would leave the Treasury without compensation for the time it did not have the money. Waiver of ordinary additions would make compelled collection an individually chosen substitute for timely compliance, transferring part of its cost and incentive structure to others. Those consequences are material to whether the alternative preserves the government’s interests. The conclusion is confined to the described nonpayment theory: it does not prove that an incorrect computation, an unauthorized collection act or a charge meeting an enacted exception is valid. Each such question would be resolved under its own source.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No unsupported amount, levy detail or penalty subsection is supplied. Protected religious advocacy and a valid independent statutory defense remain distinct from deliberate nonpayment.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of the Fifth Circuit.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No remand. The Frees' jurisdictional challenge to the June 3, 1999 final judgment (176 F.3d 298) fails, and that judgment, including its affirmance of the dismissal under Louisiana law, stands. Stone does not review or endorse the Louisiana antitrust or Civil Code rulings, settlement approval, class certification, or the § 1367(c) retention ruling; none is within the question granted.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Supplemental jurisdiction over below-threshold class claims

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory question because original diversity jurisdiction exists over the named plaintiff’s anchor claim.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 1367(a) reaches the related claims, and § 1367(b) does not exclude claims joined through Rule 23. The court therefore has statutory supplemental jurisdiction, subject to the statute’s other limits.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a diversity class action includes at least one claim satisfying § 1332 and the remaining class claims form part of the same Article III case or controversy, § 1367 permits supplemental jurisdiction over class members whose claims do not independently satisfy the amount-in-controversy requirement.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The broad grant in subsection (a) covers the claims. Subsection (b) carefully names several joinder rules but omits Rule 23. That omission cannot be replaced by carrying forward a pre-enactment restriction Congress did not preserve in the text. Jurisdiction does not itself establish class certification or liability, and subsection (c) retains its discretionary function.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Complete diversity, Article III relatedness, Rule 23 requirements, and § 1367(c) remain independent. Stone does not permit aggregation to manufacture the anchor claim.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Interpret Title VI; distinguish regulatory validity from private enforceability; reverse and remand for any preserved intentional-discrimination claim.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Private enforcement of Title VI regulations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reverse. Section 601 privately protects against intentional discrimination, while § 602 authorizes agencies to issue compliance regulations and use the administrative enforcement Congress supplied. A regulation may validly prohibit unjustified disparate impact without creating a privately enforceable federal right when the statute contains no rights-creating text supporting that consequence; § 1983 cannot be used to manufacture the omitted right.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Title VI's disparate-impact regulations do not themselves create a private federal right enforceable directly or through § 1983 absent rights-creating statutory text. Agency enforcement remains available, and a properly pleaded intentional-discrimination claim under § 601 is unaffected.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reverse. Section 601 privately protects against intentional discrimination, while § 602 authorizes agencies to issue compliance regulations and use the administrative enforcement Congress supplied. A regulation may validly prohibit unjustified disparate impact without creating a privately enforceable federal right when the statute contains no rights-creating text supporting that consequence; § 1983 cannot be used to manufacture the omitted right. Blessing requires the claimed right to come from the enactment, not the usefulness of private enforcement. Stone’s rejection of this disparate-impact theory therefore leaves actual §601 intentional-discrimination claims and authorized administrative enforcement intact; it does not erase all individual rights in funding statutes.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not invalidate the regulations, restrict agency enforcement or funding remedies, or foreclose claims proving intentional discrimination.
