## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Grant review, reverse the Circuit Court's suppression judgment, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject the general homicide-scene exception. Permit the state courts to consider only preserved and legally supported alternative warrant exceptions and harmless error. Do not direct acquittal, suppress evidence on an undeveloped alternative record, or decide retrial questions not presented.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: General homicide-scene exception

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved federal search question. No threshold defect prevents review of the last state-court merits ruling.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the surviving warrant default and Mincey without modification.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Police may enter a homicide scene to render emergency aid, search promptly for victims or a dangerous person, secure the premises, and preserve evidence while lawful process is obtained. A completed emergency does not authorize an extended warrantless evidentiary search merely because a homicide occurred.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The cabin was secured before officers began a sixteen-hour evidence-gathering process. The trial court authorized examination of anything and everything at the scene, a rationale broader than any recognized exception and untethered to continued emergency need. The gravity of homicide strengthens the interest in prompt investigation but does not eliminate the warrant requirement. As Dickerson distinguishes an authorized frisk from later manipulation, lawful emergency entry cannot justify opening concealed materials after the emergency ends. That boundary supports reversal without inventing a crime-scene exception.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Prompt emergency entry, a protective sweep, search for additional victims or an assailant, and temporary evidence preservation remain lawful when their own predicates exist.

### Issue 2: Closed briefcase and remand

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal consequence of the invalid categorical theory but not unresolved factual alternatives.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary closed-container and appellate-remand principles.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Opening the closed briefcase was a search requiring a warrant or a separately established exception; the container's presence in plain view and its unlocked condition did not expose its contents.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The photographs and negatives were concealed inside the briefcase. The trial court did not find consent, a standardized inventory, or a continuing exigency. Because those theories and harmlessness may depend on facts and state procedure, the lawful remedy is reversal and remand rather than appellate factfinding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone expresses no view on implied consent, a valid inventory, another exigency, attenuation, or harmlessness if properly preserved and supported below.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit insofar as it held the same-decision showing legally irrelevant to outcome-based liability and relief; remand for claim-specific proceedings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require Texas to prove by a preponderance that a specified constitutional admissions process would have produced the same rejection. If it carries that burden, deny admission and compensatory relief for the rejection. Preserve nominal or declaratory relief for a proved unequal process where an available cause, proper defendant, and immunity rule permit it. Resolve §§ 1981 and 2000d separately and dismiss prospective relief unless Lesage establishes concrete continuing exposure.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Same-decision proof

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the legal effect of same-decision proof because the Fifth Circuit treated it as categorically irrelevant.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Hopwood's existing burden and remedy structure, not the historical rule that same-decision necessarily erases liability altogether.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Once an unconstitutional admissions process is established, the school bears the preponderance burden to show that a specified constitutional process would have produced the same rejection. A successful showing defeats relief for the denial and its consequences but does not erase the completed unequal-process injury.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The record contains substantial academic and qualitative evidence supporting the same rejection, but the Fifth Circuit did not assess that evidence under the controlling school-borne burden. The correct counterfactual must be lawful and specified rather than assumed. Remand permits that determination without converting equal competition into entitlement to admission.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not find that every use of race was unlawful, decide the proper lawful admissions model, or award any remedy before the violation and remedial channel are established.

### Issue 2: Nominal, statutory, and prospective relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the remedial separation necessary to frame the remand but does not decide undeveloped defendant- and statute-specific questions.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Hopwood's source-specific relief and Article III rules.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A conclusive same-decision showing bars compensation for the denied admission but not nominal or declaratory recognition of a proved unconstitutional process. Forward-looking relief requires concrete exposure to an ongoing or imminent policy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The alleged injury from unequal consideration differs from the economic or educational loss caused by rejection. Carey permits nominal recognition of a completed violation without proof of compensable loss, subject to a lawful cause and defendant. The pleadings alleged a continuing policy, but the appellate record suggests that the practice may have ceased and that prospective relief may have been abandoned. Those matters require express resolution.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The decision does not establish § 1981 or Title VI liability, waive immunity, impose personal liability, order admission, or direct a broad injunction governing later admissions programs.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Certify to the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania whether Commonwealth v. Scarpone stated the correct interpretation of § 6018.401(a) when Fiore's conviction became final; retain jurisdiction; reserve judgment and all further proceedings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** No habeas judgment, release order, affirmance, reversal, or view of the federal merits issues until the state court answers. After the response, restore the case for decision under the resulting state-law premise.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Certification before constitutional adjudication

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the antecedent state-law uncertainty and stops there because the federal due-process question depends on it.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply ordinary certification and decisional-order principles without changing substantive law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a live federal constitutional claim turns on whether a State's highest court later declared the statute's preexisting meaning or changed it, the Court may certify that precise historical-meaning question and reserve federal judgment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Fiore and Scarpone were convicted under the same provision on materially identical permit facts, but their appeals took different state-court paths. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court's first construction came after Fiore's conviction became final. Only that court can authoritatively say whether its construction described what the statute always meant. Certification avoids deciding a federal claim on an invented state-law premise while preserving this Court's duty to decide the constitutional consequence.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Certification does not decide retroactivity, sufficiency, notice, habeas relief, or the meaning of Pennsylvania law.

### Issue 2: Federal due-process merits

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone does not reach the due-process merits at this event.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reserve application of Winship and Jackson until the element question is fixed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** None at this stage beyond reservation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** If absence of a permit was always an element, the Commonwealth's concession that Fiore possessed a permit may present a conviction without proof of criminal conduct. If the law changed later, a different constitutional analysis may govern. The Court cannot lawfully choose between those paths without the certified answer.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Every federal merits and remedial question remains open.

Public safety can justify targeted notification after the individualized risk procedures required by *W.P.* at *W.P.*’s actual scope, but the State must fit access to an actual safety need and maintain meaningful controls on recipients, use, scope, confidentiality, and redistribution. Stone would not prohibit all address disclosure, but neither would he treat compiled residential information as public merely because the underlying conviction is public. Safeguards must respond to the identified risk of unnecessary redisclosure, not suppress all legitimate notification or create perpetual secrecy for public convictions.
