## Common judgment, rule and remedy commitments

After independent assessment, all eight Associates provisionally **affirm** the Florida Supreme Court's judgment, leaving the suppression ruling in place. The common ground is the insufficiency of this anonymous allegation and its actual corroboration under Terry and White, not a constitutional prohibition on anonymous tips as a class.

They directly support these propositions at this scope:

1. **The accusation needs adequate reliability under the totality.** An anonymous report that the described person has a concealed gun, supported here only by prompt confirmation of publicly observable identity and location and by no further indicium supporting the accusation, does not furnish reasonable suspicion for this forcible stop and protective search. Reasonable suspicion requires less than probable cause, but the lesser threshold still requires objective justification for the intrusion. Confirming which person is accused does not, in these circumstances, adequately test the anonymous claim of concealed wrongdoing.
2. **The subject of this report does not cure its evidentiary defect.** The ordinary safety concern raised by the asserted gun does not create a firearms exception that authorizes this stop and frisk without sufficiently reliable grounds. Terry already permits protective action on less than probable cause when its predicates are met. The State need not invariably wait to see a gun; it must have adequate grounds before the seizure and search it undertakes.
3. **Later discovery supplies no retrospective justification.** The gun was seen as the frisk began toward the waist, not before the intrusion. The discovery therefore cannot be used to justify commencing it. Dickerson's requirement of lawful protective access and Wardlow's rejection of retrospective reliance on the recovered gun are consistent with this conclusion; neither is misdescribed as already deciding anonymous-tip reliability.
4. **The rule remains contextual.** Corroborated predictions are one way to establish reliability, not an indispensable condition for every anonymous report. Other sufficiently concrete indicia may matter under the totality. No holding is made about an actual imminent violent threat, an identified or accountable eyewitness, a school search, airport screening, or another special setting absent from this record. No universal equation between firearm possession, illegality and dangerousness is adopted.

Affirmance directs no acquittal, dismissal of the delinquency proceeding, release, new trial, renewed evidentiary opportunity or additional hearing. It leaves further lawful state proceedings to the ordinary effect of the suppression judgment. No remedy for the frisk of either companion is adjudicated.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

The strongest **reversal** path treats the tip's contemporaneity, specific location and descriptive accuracy, quick confirmation, concealed-gun allegation and the potential danger together as sufficient under the lower reasonable-suspicion threshold. Terry does not require an officer personally to see the weapon, and Adams v. Williams permits reliance on sufficiently reliable informant information. On that path, requiring more could make safe investigation impracticable; reversal would be followed by remand, not immediate adjudication of guilt.

The strongest **affirmance** path distinguishes accuracy about a visible person from a reliable basis for an otherwise untested accusation. The danger exists if the accusation is true; it cannot alone supply the reason to credit it. White's corroborated private itinerary provided an informative link that the match of publicly visible clothing and place lacks. Adams v. Williams had a personally known informant who approached the officer and had supplied information before. None of those facts is imported here. A contrary result on this packet would allow an unidentified caller to trigger an intrusive search of any specifically described person with an untested gun allegation.

The central counterargument to affirmance is that prompt accurate detail can reasonably suggest present observation and that a mistaken delay may have grave consequences. Each Justice gives those considerations weight but rejects the inference that observing the group also establishes knowledge of its concealed possessions. No probability score or mandatory additional-factor rule is used. An unknown caller might have reliable knowledge, but the record gives the officer no adequate reason to make that inference about the gun. The central counterargument to reversal therefore remains unanswered on the supported facts. Safety permits prompt investigation; it does not make the stipulated evidentiary deficiency disappear.

## Justice-specific commitments and join boundaries

### Stevens

**Result and sufficient ground:** Affirm on all three operative components: deficient antecedent suspicion, no ordinary-firearm exception on this record, and no retrospective cure. His White dissent, 496 U.S. 333, challenges reliance even on predictions when a person familiar with routine activity can invent the illicit-content allegation. The present descriptive corroboration is materially weaker. His current Wardlow majority join requires particularized grounds rather than an inference enlarged by the gun eventually found; his Dickerson Parts II–IV joins preserve limits on protective access.

**Alternative tested:** Accurate description and a fast response reduce the chance that officers approached a different person. They do not answer the possibility that a caller accurately identifies the intended target while inventing the accusation. The weapon's possible danger does not repair that distinction.

**Join boundaries and lawful variants:** He can join the common, record-specific rule without demanding reversal of White or requiring predictions in every tip. He would refuse an opinion treating a frisk as negligible, race as an inculpatory fact, or confirmation of identification alone as reliable proof of concealed illegality. No separate writing is necessary if these points remain clear.

### O'Connor

**Result and sufficient ground:** Affirm. Her White majority join endorses a contextual, lower-than-probable-cause inquiry, but also the need for meaningful indicia of reliability. White treated corroborated future conduct as evidence of special familiarity; this record has only currently visible descriptive facts. Her Ornelas opinion requires independent legal assessment and genuine due weight for grounded experience, while forbidding an unproved observation from being supplied by that experience.

**Alternative tested:** Her Wardlow dissent found an immediate observed look-and-run sequence, its route and the credited setting sufficient. That was an individualized inference from conduct the officers actually observed. J.L. presents no comparable independent conduct; replacing it with the anonymous allegation would assume the reliability in dispute. The possibility of officer danger is substantial but does not itself tell the officer whether this allegation is reliable.

**Join boundaries and lawful variants:** She will not join a rule that all anonymous calls require future predictions or that innocent conduct can never contribute to suspicion. She can join the actual-totality holding and rejection of the proposed firearms shortcut, with other reliable reports and genuinely different emergencies left open. No new hearing is justified by a record that supplies the timing and observations necessary to decide the claim.

### Scalia

**Result and sufficient ground:** Affirm. His White majority join accepts informant reliability under the totality, including corroboration of meaningful information. His Dickerson join in the unlawful-manipulation disposition confirms that discovering real contraband cannot supply authority for an otherwise unauthorized intrusion. His current Wardlow dissent rests on an observed response to police and the actual setting, not a presumption that a report or recovered gun supplies its own justification.

**Alternative tested:** The reversal path takes the reduced reasonable-suspicion threshold seriously and invokes the practical danger of concealed weapons. But the visible match is compatible with a wholly fabricated gun accusation, and no other circumstance tests that accusation. The Fourth Amendment predicate cannot be replaced by the subject matter of an untested report.

**Join boundaries and lawful variants:** He applies controlling Ornelas while retaining his prior objection to its appellate-review allocation; that objection is not a new source of facts or an independent reason to reverse suppression here. He requires no separate concurrence if the decision simply applies current law. He will not join speculative rules for unpresented technologies or emergencies, a mandatory-prediction test, or a rule that any lawful gun possession necessarily makes a person dangerous.

### Kennedy

**Result and sufficient ground:** Affirm. His White majority join and Ornelas join require examining what the corroboration actually demonstrates. His Dickerson joins preserve the necessity of lawful antecedent access. The matching shirt, group and location identify the intended person but do not make the unknown assertion of a concealed gun adequately trustworthy in this record.

**Alternative tested:** The fresh detail suggests that the caller knew the immediate scene. Combined with the firearm allegation, that is the State's strongest argument. It still leaves unaddressed the source's knowledge of the concealed gun and any basis to credit that assertion. His Wardlow dissent found particularized observed conduct; that inference cannot be carried into an otherwise uneventful bus-stop encounter.

**Join boundaries and lawful variants:** He will join an affirmance explicitly preserving the possibility of reliable anonymous reporting without prediction. He does not require a mechanical separate demonstration of every informant factor; they remain contextual considerations. Any statement that anonymity alone defeats suspicion, or that all reports lacking predictions fail, would require a narrowing revision. A short concurrence would serve only to preserve those limits if the principal opinion overstated them; it is not independently necessary on the common formulation.

### Souter

**Result and sufficient ground:** Affirm. His Wardlow majority join gives operative force to individualized suspicion and forbids reliance on the search's eventual success. His Dickerson joins recognize the difference between a permissible protective intrusion and information acquired after constitutional authority is exceeded. His Ornelas join supports independent review of the actual circumstances. Under White, the untested assertion here does not gain adequate credibility from the match of openly visible features.

**Alternative tested:** The totality must include the speed and accuracy of confirmation rather than treating each detail in isolation. Taken together, those details still establish familiarity with a public scene, without a reason to credit concealed criminality. Terry's protective rationale cannot be used to reverse that logical sequence.

**Join boundaries and lawful variants:** He supports the ordinary gun-report holding without prescribing a universal checklist, forbidding the use of innocent facts, or deciding what a different emergency would require. His Wardlow position is not recast as a requirement of three factors or an exclusion of flight from suspicion. No additional ground concerning all Florida firearm possession is needed.

### Thomas

**Result and sufficient ground:** Affirm. His Wardlow dissent accepts reasonable inferences from the actual flight sequence and setting but assumes no additional observations. Applying that same discipline here leaves the officer with the untested allegation and the descriptive match. His Ornelas join requires independent ultimate review without inventing supporting observations. His Dickerson Part II join accepts seizure through lawful protective touch; it does not grant authority to start an unsupported frisk. His nonjoin in Dickerson's application is not erased or converted into a contrary commitment here.

**Alternative tested:** The account is fresh, the person is identifiable and the alleged possession could create a safety risk. Those circumstances support prompt attention but do not establish the truth of the gun accusation at the required level. A firearm-specific rule authorizing a frisk on those facts would allow the allegation itself to eliminate the reliability problem.

**Join boundaries and lawful variants:** He can join the narrow insufficiency holding, including the statement that predictions are unnecessary when other adequate indicia exist. He will not join a universal conclusion about emergency reports, a finding of the caller's dishonesty, or a conclusion that the officer could never infer criminality from a sufficiently reliable concealed-firearm report. No second evidentiary opportunity is needed to repair an unestablished alternative factual premise.

### Ginsburg

**Result and sufficient ground:** Affirm. Her current Wardlow majority join preserves particularized suspicion and rejects retrospective reliance on the weapon found. Her Ornelas join requires constitutional sufficiency to rest on established circumstances. White's totality framework explains why confirmation of a description can locate a person without supplying sufficient reason to credit an unknown accusation about him. The officer saw no conduct adding that missing reason.

**Alternative tested:** The State's claim that the information is fresh and specific deserves consideration, but specificity about a target does not establish reliability about his concealed possessions. Safety does not reduce the ordinary inquiry to whether the caller used the word gun. The case does not turn on requiring the police to watch the weapon being brandished.

**Join boundaries and lawful variants:** She supports the common rule without deciding any companion's rights, finding that all gun possession is unlawful, or converting a discretionary consensual inquiry into a mandatory police protocol. A full merits affirmance is supported; a remand merely to seek stronger evidence of the pre-stop circumstances is unwarranted on the supplied record.

### Breyer

**Result and sufficient ground:** Affirm. His current Wardlow majority join applies the totality with individualized grounds; his Ornelas join preserves due weight for actual experience without improving the record. The limited corroboration here has practical value in identifying the person, but it provides no meaningful check on the concealed-gun accusation beyond that. The consequences of an accusation being true do not demonstrate its truth.

**Alternative tested:** A stop and limited frisk may protect officers and bystanders from a serious risk, and reasonable suspicion is deliberately less demanding than probable cause. Those considerations are already accommodated by Terry's authority when its predicates exist. They do not make every sufficiently descriptive anonymous gun allegation reliable. The proposed rule also enables an unidentified caller to direct intrusive searches at innocent persons without an adequate check.

**Join boundaries and lawful variants:** He joins without a numerical risk calculus or a universal priority rule for weapons. A different report with concrete reliability or immediate-threat circumstances remains a different case. Neither police inaction nor an obligation to conduct dangerous questioning is prescribed. Affirmance resolves the encounter actually proved.

## Authority treatment for later assembly

- **Terry:** Apply the need for objectively supported antecedent grounds and the bounded protective purpose. The lower reasonable-suspicion threshold remains; it is not replaced by probable cause or a general prohibition on proactive investigation.
- **Adams v. Williams:** Distinguish the personally known, face-to-face informant and actual assurances supporting that report. Preserve its holding that officers need not personally observe the reported weapon when sufficiently reliable information authorizes the protective encounter.
- **Gates and White:** Apply contextual reliability; distinguish corroboration revealing familiarity with future private conduct from merely identifying a present public scene. Do not transform Gates into a restored rigid two-prong test or White into a mandatory-prediction rule.
- **Ornelas:** Apply independent ultimate review with historical-fact protection and genuine due weight. No officer-correctness presumption or new observation follows. Scalia retains his prior allocation disagreement without reopening it here.
- **Dickerson:** Preserve the need for authorized protective contact and lawful access. Its manipulation holding is not the same issue as this initial-stop defect, and no manipulation finding is made here.
- **Wardlow:** Apply the unanimous totality rule and the prohibition on retrospective gun justification. Preserve both its actual majority application and the dissent's observed-conduct rationale; neither supplies a categorical answer to anonymous reporting.
