## Historical comparison and its limits

The published Third Circuit decision is a verified merits anchor. It assumed federal RFRA's constitutionality, accepted the statutory burden sequence in general, treated the conceded compelling collection interest and Lee's uniformity rationale as resolving least restrictive means, and expressly held that the Commissioner need not produce evidence for that showing. It did not establish that Adams's full-payment proposals were operationally workable; neither did it adjudicate them through the present timeline's application-specific production-and-persuasion rule. It separately rejected the claimed Code excuses and, in note 10, the constitutional individual-exemptions theory.

The five remanding Associates therefore disagree with the lower judgment on a concrete legal premise: Clearwater, Swanner, and American Life League require this-person/this-application adjudication and do not permit a general category to replace RFRA's means inquiry. Each of the five joined that rule; O'Connor authored all three opinions. Lee and Hernandez remain important contrary authority, especially because Lee expressly addressed the general war-tax objection. Their force is retained, but their pre-RFRA results do not displace the later controlling statutory burden. This is not an assertion that RFRA invariably requires empirical proof, a hearing, or remand whenever religion is asserted.

The three affirming Associates also apply RFRA. They find sufficient legal justification in the actual proposal's condition on governmental use, the demand for nonliability until accommodation, and the distinct functions of the identified additions. Their votes preserve a genuine merits alternative: the current record can be sufficient for legal resolution even though the lower court stated an overbroad rule. Their prior RFRA remand dissents support no undocumented rationale beyond the published commitments that the entering-law slice actually supplies.

## Justice-specific reconciliation and permitted joins

**Stevens.** Retain affirmance on each component. His Lee concurrence, 455 U.S. 261–264, distinguishes the Amish substitute welfare arrangement from an ordinary objection to the government's use of general taxes; its footnote at 262 recognizes the importance of an equivalent substitute rather than a wish to direct spending. His Hernandez join supports the general-revenue justification. Clearwater supplies the intervening government burden, which his earlier constitutional presumption cannot replace. The particular conditional contribution and demand to suspend liability furnish his legal answer here, without a new factual finding about administrative cost or appropriation effects. Boyle, which he joined, separately supports timely returns as necessary to ascertain and collect tax; section 6654's amount-and-duration relation to underpayment separately supports timely installment revenue. He rejects conscience alone as establishing the unproved inability or unusual circumstance, but does not use the Code conclusion as a substitute for RFRA. He joins a narrow RFRA affirmance, not an IRS-wide exemption or a constitutional invalidation of RFRA. If the Court remands, his disagreement is that its statutory rule can already be applied to reject these particular demands.

**O'Connor.** Retain all three RFRA vacaturs. Her three RFRA opinions establish the exact production-and-persuasion requirement the lower opinion declined to apply. Her Lee join is a substantial barrier to ordering an exemption, not a reason to erase the enacted inquiry. Her Hernandez dissent concerns demonstrated differential treatment of comparable religious transactions; Adams establishes no comparable secular or religious waiver and receives no favorable finding on that theory. Her Boyle majority and concurrence joins preserve a clear filing duty and genuinely impaired compliance; they neither equate conscience with objective incapacity nor decide RFRA. A remand opinion can retain Lee's tax-system reasoning, require separate consideration of the filing and estimated-payment functions, permit legal resolution on adequate undisputed facts, and direct no hearing or appropriation change. She refuses a formulation that places a universal initial feasibility burden on Adams or treats all Code exceptions as equivalent.

**Scalia.** Retain all three RFRA vacaturs. His Clearwater, Swanner, and American Life League joins adopt statutory obligations independently of Smith. He does not deny that Lee and Hernandez can furnish a powerful legal justification; he refuses to infer a tax exclusion from RFRA or make the prescribed means its own conclusive compelling interest. His Hernandez dissent join supplies no war-tax entitlement. For each addition, the enacted RFRA inquiry remains distinct from the Code's excuse: section 6651 requires reasonable cause and no willful neglect, while section 6654(e)(3)(A) has its own casualty/disaster/unusual-circumstances trigger. He joins the bounded remand and its no-hearing/no-exemption limitations; he refuses a new constitutional rule, compelled earmark, or a claim that religious compulsion automatically satisfies either Code provision.

**Kennedy.** Retain all three RFRA vacaturs. His direct RFRA joins supply the date-eligible basis, and his Boerne opinion leaves federal RFRA outside its state/local section 5 holding. Kennedy did not participate in Hernandez; none of that opinion's joins is attributed to him. Lee's concern about imposing a claimant's religion on others supports potential denial, but the lower court's legal shortcut is not a finding that this proposal in fact imposes such injury. The actual proposal and the separate filing/timely-payment burdens require their own application. He joins a remand allowing Congress's legitimate fiscal objectives and legal reasons to defeat an accommodation, without creating a duty to negotiate, an appropriation command, or a private spending veto.

**Souter.** Retain all three RFRA vacaturs. His three statutory joins forbid carrying the importance of the entire tax system into each disputed means as a conclusive answer. Strout's actual-funded-function reasoning cautions that a private designation does not itself change a public financing relationship, but that Religion Clause tuition decision neither adjudicates federal RFRA tax collection nor supplies a missing tax finding. Boyle's preserved inability cases help identify the bounded operation of the filing exception without establishing this claimant's eligibility. Section 6654's compensatory relationship is a substantial contrary ground that can support ultimate denial, not a substitute for applying RFRA. He joins only an actual-application remand, with no presumption that a book entry works, no theological reassessment, and no mandatory hearing without a material dispute.

**Thomas.** Retain all three RFRA vacaturs. His RFRA joins adopt distinct threshold and justification burdens; his Boerne join does not resolve federal validity. A legal determination may satisfy the statute without a new empirical study, but absence of an established alternative cannot itself transfer the government's statutory burden to the claimant. The failure-to-file and estimated-tax defenses each require their own RFRA application; no general reasonable-cause rule may replace section 6654's enacted qualification. He joins the limited remand and permits denial on a sufficient existing record, while refusing tax immunity, a universal hearing obligation, or an inference that every statutory hardship waiver is a comparable exemption.

**Ginsburg.** Retain affirmance on each component. Her Clearwater join preserves RFRA's applicability and burdens. The proposal is not merely a verified different collection method: it demands protected nonmilitary use and nonliability until accommodation. Lee and Hernandez furnish her legal basis for finding that contribution different from the ordinary general-revenue obligation; no assumed administrative expense is necessary to her ground. Boyle's filing function and section 6654's specific relationship to delayed installments support the two additions independently. She rejects the lower court's statement that the means inquiry answers itself, all judgments about insincerity, and any claim that tax liability validates every sanction. If the Court remands, her disagreement concerns the need for more adjudication on these already identified burdens and proposals, while preserving RFRA for other applications and actual waiver grounds.

**Breyer.** Retain affirmance on each component. His Clearwater join and prior application positions allow legal resolution without treating RFRA as absent. Lee and Hernandez identify the common-finance relationship: equal dollars subject to a legally protected spending condition do not have the same legal character as the required unrestricted contribution. Boyle separately identifies prompt ascertainment through returns; section 6654's amount-and-duration structure connects the addition to timely revenue. He considers those specific functions sufficient for the conscience-only dispensation requested, without conjecturing future claimants, numerical costs, accounting feasibility, or an appropriation conflict. He joins that limited affirmance, not a universal assertion that complexity defeats religious accommodation or that sincerity proves no economic benefit.

## Controlling-formulation compatibility for assembly

The following propositions have direct support from O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas as one coherent statutory-remand rationale:

1. For this federal RFRA defense, a claimant must establish substantial burden; the government must then produce evidence and persuade that applying the burden to this person furthers a compelling interest by the least restrictive means. The burden may be assumed at this review stage without conclusively deciding it. The conceded compelling tax-collection interest does not concede the means question.
2. Lee and Hernandez supply weighty tax-system guidance, including the significance of general revenues and restrictions imposed through religious exemptions. Neither their results nor the label taxation permits dispensing with the application-specific inquiry. The present lower-court ruling did dispense with it, and its RFRA determinations must be vacated.
3. The filing addition and estimated-tax addition impose distinct enforcement burdens. Lawful underlying collection and failure to qualify for a Code waiver do not alone answer the separate RFRA defenses. On remand, the filing function and timely-installment function, the actual relief sought, and legally material alternatives or comparable exceptions must be considered without presuming that an exception is comparable or that a proposal is feasible.
4. Remand establishes no tax exemption, nonmilitary earmark, general-revenue payment condition, Code waiver, constitutional violation, refund, injunction, mandatory evidentiary hearing, deadline, or ultimate failure of the government's justification. Adequate existing facts and legal reasons may support enforcement after the proper inquiry. Sincerity is not an automatic exemption; missing research files are not a litigant default.

Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer share the general statutory burdens and these limits but do not join propositions requiring vacatur or declaring the present legal showing inadequate. Their complete alternative rationale is affirmance under RFRA of these actual proposals and these separately justified additions; a common rule that every taxpayer may determine how receipts are spent is unacceptable to all eight. Assembly must preserve a judgment/rationale distinction if its formulation changes that division. No recorded position authorizes silently moving a Justice from the three to the five or the five to the three. A material circulation change requires the Engine's separate reconciliation audit.

## Remedy and scope precision

The remanding five support vacating the Third Circuit's judgment insofar as it sustains the challenged collection and identified additions against RFRA, and remanding for proceedings consistent with the statutory inquiry. The independent Code and constitutional determinations are outside the granted question and remain undisturbed at their own scope; the Supreme Court does not adopt them as new holdings. This limitation cannot be written as a complete affirmance of enforceable liability that makes the RFRA remand meaningless. Conversely, RFRA vacatur cannot be written as present cancellation or suspension of all liabilities or collection.

Section 6651(a)(1)'s exception requires both reasonable cause and absence of willful neglect. Section 6654(e)(3)(A) bars the addition to the extent the Secretary determines that casualty, disaster, or other unusual circumstances would make it against equity and good conscience. Section 6654(e)(3)(B), not claimed applicable, requires retirement after age 62 or becoming disabled in the taxable year at issue or its predecessor, plus underpayment due to reasonable cause and not willful neglect. These triggers must not be merged. The Court makes no determination of amounts, separate unverified sanctions, selective enforcement, objective incapacity, or tax calculations.
