## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Constitutional self-representation on direct appeal

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional claim because Martinez seeks to displace appointed counsel on that ground.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The Constitution does not extend Faretta’s trial-specific self-representation right to a direct appeal. The appellate guarantee is meaningful review with competent advocacy, not a personal right to control every filing.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may require represented advocacy on a first appeal as of right and deny a defendant’s request to discharge appointed counsel for the purpose of proceeding entirely pro se.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The trial is the proceeding in which the accused personally confronts the prosecution, presents evidence, and may choose to bear the consequences of self-representation. An appeal reviews a completed record through legal claims and institutional procedures. Requiring counsel protects rather than diminishes the meaningful appellate access guaranteed by Douglas and Evitts. California’s refusal to create a personal pro se entitlement therefore does not violate the Sixth or Fourteenth Amendment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: California may employ a procedure different from Anders

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone rejects the State's *Teague* defense as an old-rule matter: the protection applied here was dictated by the law existing when Robbins's conviction became final, because *Anders*, *McCoy* and *Penson* required counsel's active advocacy concerning arguably supportive appellate issues. No new procedural template or *Teague* exception is necessary. Stone therefore reaches the merits of the constitutional adequacy of California's procedure; no other threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone does not treat every procedural detail described in *Anders* as an exclusive constitutional form. He nevertheless concludes that *Wende* is constitutionally inadequate because it permits appointed counsel who has found no nonfrivolous issue to file without identifying any issue that might arguably support the appeal, leaving the appellate court and the indigent appellant to perform the issue-spotting function counsel is appointed to perform. On a first appeal as of right, appointed counsel must remain an advocate until the court has a sufficient adversarial basis to determine that the appeal is frivolous. Independent judicial review and an opportunity for a pro se filing do not substitute for counsel's advocate role.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may use a procedure different from the precise *Anders* format, but it may not permit appointed appellate counsel to withhold every arguably supportive issue and shift the search for such issues to the court or the indigent defendant. California's *Wende* procedure therefore fails to provide the meaningful adversarial representation required on a first appeal as of right.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** *Douglas* and *Evitts* protect meaningful counsel on the first appeal a State provides, while *Anders*, *McCoy*, *Penson*, and *Cronic* make the advocate's role substantive rather than nominal. *Wende* preserves a lawyer's presence but allows the lawyer to remain silent about possible appellate issues and asks the reviewing court to search the record without the focused adversarial assistance that counsel is supposed to supply. That arrangement materially increases the risk that an indigent appellant receives judicial screening instead of representation. Stone therefore affirms the Ninth Circuit's judgment on the narrower ground that *Wende* itself fails the constitutional minimum; he does not hold that every State must copy the exact filing, withdrawal, or labeling mechanics of *Anders*. The identified arguable issues make this a substantive failure of representation, not merely a departure from a preferred filing format. Judicial screening and nominal retention of counsel do not replace that protection. Affirm the Ninth Circuit, preserving its remand for Robbins's remaining claims.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A State remains free to adopt a genuinely equivalent alternative that preserves counsel's active advocate role and gives the reviewing court a focused basis for determining frivolousness. Stone does not require verbatim use of the *Anders* procedure or decide the merits of Robbins's remaining habeas claims.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Missouri’s candidate-contribution limits violate the First Amendment because

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current simulated law rather than a reduced *Buckley*-only formulation. Missouri bears the burden under *Russell*. The identified large contribution, state corruption episodes, and public perception of corruption objectively ground the asserted interest, while the record does not show that the operative ceilings materially disabled meaningful contribution, association, or effective campaigning. The limits are closely fitted on this record, so Stone reverses the injunction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A candidate-contribution limit is constitutional when government establishes an objectively grounded risk of actual or apparent quid-pro-quo corruption or circumvention and closely fits the ceiling to that risk without materially disabling meaningful association or effective campaign advocacy. Missouri satisfied that standard on this record.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** *Russell*, not a generic invocation of *Buckley*, supplies the controlling burden and fit inquiry. Missouri identified an objectively grounded risk of actual and apparent corruption through the record summarized in Section I. The respondents did not establish that the operative ceilings prevented candidates from assembling resources for effective advocacy or contributors from participating meaningfully. No fixed federal-dollar floor or automatic inflation adjustment displaces that record-specific inquiry. Stone therefore reverses the Eighth Circuit, vacates the injunction, and remands for judgment for the state officials. Russell’s office-specific judgments prevent treating every contribution ceiling alike. Stone’s conclusion depends on Missouri’s operational evidence and the preservation of effective advocacy, not on a presumed safe dollar amount or a new requirement of proved completed bribery.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A materially lower ceiling, discriminatory structure, unsupported anticorruption premise, or record showing that candidates or contributors lack a realistic means of effective advocacy could produce a different result.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Tenth Circuit on the First Amendment and statutory-construction grounds for vacating the CPNI order. Uphold the challenged opt-in rule under Central Hudson and as a permissible construction of § 222(c)(1).
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand the unreached takings and arbitrary-and-capricious challenges. The proposed judgment does not reject those claims, compel opt-in for every future rule, or hold opt-out unconstitutional.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS


### Issue 1: Commercial-speech review and the confidentiality harm

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the First Amendment question independently. Because the rule selects the use of information for commercial solicitation, Lanphere requires commercial-speech review; calling it information regulation does not avoid that scrutiny.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing Central Hudson framework, including real harms, direct and material advancement and reasonable fit. Fox requires no least-restrictive means, and the precedents permit supported predictive judgment without invariably demanding studies.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Affirmative approval before a carrier uses confidential calling records for the challenged new-service marketing directly advances the substantial confidentiality interest and reasonably fits it.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Privacy is substantial, as Went For It recognized. This interest is stronger than the interest in avoiding a letter derived from public information: CPNI reveals whom a person calls, when and for how long, is obtained through the telephone service, and carries a statutory confidentiality duty. Went For It and Lanphere dealt with public accident or other records; silencing solicitation there left the information public. Here the harm is the unconsented use of confidential information, and requiring consent prevents that harm. Went For It expressly reserved confidential-record misuse.

  The permitted uses do not reproduce the self-undermining scheme in Greater New Orleans. Uses tied to services the customer already selected differ from using the service records to solicit a new category. The FCC did consider opt-out and explained that silence after notices many customers will not read is not informed approval. That statutory and predictive judgment supplies a reason for requiring an affirmative choice. The carrier’s low opt-in rates support the argument that customers do not affirmatively want this additional use; they do not establish that silence signifies approval. Carriers remain able to market any service, use non-CPNI information and ask permission.

  The carrier’s strongest objection is that Edenfield, Lanphere and Went For It demand support for harm and fit and that opt-out burdens less speech. Those cases demand support, not certainty, and involved public information. The confidentiality and service relationship identify a concrete interest here; consent addresses the unconsented use itself. Having considered opt-out, the FCC need not select it merely because it is less burdensome to the speaker. Fox does not require the least restrictive means.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No holding that opt-out is unconstitutional, no general exemption from commercial-speech review for data-use rules, and no ruling on public or nonconfidential information. Lanphere and Went For It remain unchanged.

### Issue 2: Statutory construction and independent constitutional judgment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the constitutional question independently, then the permissibility of the FCC’s construction of the enacted approval requirement.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Chevron to the statutory choice, with no deference on constitutional validity.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** “Approval of the customer” fairly bears an opt-in interpretation. The FCC’s choice is reasonable; after independent constitutional analysis sustains the rule, constitutional avoidance supplies no basis for withholding ordinary statutory deference.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A duty of confidentiality coupled with customer approval reasonably allows the agency to demand an affirmative indication before a new marketing use. The statute need not compel that choice for it to permit it. Constitutional doubt cannot be invoked as a substitute for resolving the actual First Amendment question; that question is resolved under the governing test, not by deferring to the agency.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The statute is not held to require opt-in exclusively. Takings and arbitrary-and-capricious claims remain for remand; sustaining the presented speech and interpretive questions does not resolve every administrative objection.

### Issue 3: Entity capacity and the authorized service relationship

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Identify the protected speaker and customer interests without treating corporate status or licensing as a waiver of constitutional rights.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Capacity follows the particular right. Commercial-speech protection serves listeners as well as speakers, so a corporation can invoke it; legal personhood alone does not settle its scope.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The carrier retains commercial-speech protection, but its lawful access to customer records does not exempt their subsequent use from the supported confidentiality terms attached to that service relationship.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** U.S. West’s existence comes from its charter, and its common-carrier position and access to calling records arise through federal and state authorization to provide telephone service. Section 222 attaches confidentiality to that position. Information obtained through this legally authorized service is held subject to the lawful terms governing it. That premise does not permit government to condition an entity’s existence on surrendering independent constitutional protections: a penalty for viewpoint or editorial choices would receive the scrutiny governing that discrimination. This rule targets neither.

  The carrier’s size earns no discount in constitutional protection. The rule applies to telecommunications carriers as such and regulates confidential information obtained in a consumer service relationship. Agreeing to telephone service is not consent to every later profitable use of its records. The restriction concerns that use, not a disfavored message, while its marketing trigger still warrants the commercial-speech scrutiny already performed. Customers retain their interests in their records, natural persons acting through the carrier retain their own speech, and the carrier can continue marketing and requesting consent.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general power to demand surrender of constitutional rights as a charter or licensing condition, no reduction based on wealth or size, and no displacement of the native commercial-speech test by a new entity-capacity test.

* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for the remaining FTCA requirements and defenses, without deciding negligence or awarding damages. Do not direct that the FTCA claim must succeed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Apply Feres without turning status or geography into the test

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The authorized question challenges the scope and application of Feres. It supplies no basis to overturn that doctrine and no basis to decide disputed negligence or damages.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing incident-to-service rule. Feres extends beyond combat or compliance with a particular order; Brooks nevertheless leaves space for a servicemember’s independent civilian activity. Neither case permits the entire inquiry to turn on a single label.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Being on a state-maintained road and off an assigned task does not itself defeat Feres; being enlisted on active duty and physically inside an installation does not itself establish it. The judgment must rest on the trip’s actual connection with service. Authorized personal travel home in the servicemember’s own car on a public road is not incident to service: his active-duty status, lack of a formal pass or furlough, and on-base location weigh toward a service connection, but they do not override the journey’s actual character.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An Army driver’s ordinary negligence can injure a person whose activity is incident to service; Johnson prevents converting the absence of a direct challenge to a command into a universal exception. Conversely, the fact that a victim is a servicemember cannot alone extinguish the distinction recognized in Brooks. The public road is evidence about the travel setting, not a jurisdictional island exempt from military relationships. The absence of an assigned task is evidence about the activity, not proof of furlough or private leave. The completed facts establish authorized personal travel home in Richards’s own car on a public road: authorized early departure from his duty station without a pass or furlough, travel in his own vehicle, an on-base public road, and a homeward personal purpose accepted for purposes of dismissal. His active-duty status, lack of a formal pass or furlough, and on-base location weigh toward a service connection, but they do not override the journey’s actual character under Stone’s contextual rule. Brooks preserves the distinction between military service and independent personal activity, and this journey falls on the personal side of it. Minns leaves the underlying Feres authorities intact and does not turn military causal genesis into a majority rule. Its policy-discretion holding also cannot replace the service analysis: driving a military truck does not, by ownership alone, identify protected policy selection.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No new Feres exception, no reversal of its existing holdings, no independent constitutional damages remedy, and no assumption that every military vehicle accident involves protected discretion. This application creates neither a general commuting exception nor an assigned-task-only test.
