## Review, statutory components and preserved factual qualifications

The direct appeal from the three-judge District Court lies under the Act's facial-review provision. That provision is distinct from codified 47 U.S.C. §561. The supplied posture establishes a live burden on Playboy's protected adult programming. No threshold or participation defect is identified.

Section 505, codified at §561, applies to multichannel distributors providing sexually explicit adult or other indecent programming on a channel primarily dedicated to sexually oriented programming. It requires full audio-and-video scrambling or blocking against nonsubscriber receipt. Until compliance, it requires withholding during the FCC-selected child-viewing hours; the permitted period is 10 p.m.–6 a.m. Scrambling retains its enacted understandable-signal definition. Section 504, codified at §560, separately requires complete audio-and-video blocking at a subscriber's request and without charge to that subscriber; it is not confined to sexually explicit channels. Subscriber payment is zero, but equipment and installation are not costless to the operator. Compliance with the requested-household provision does not automatically satisfy §505's systemwide duty.

The challenged programming is nonobscene speech protected for adults. The content selection is explicit. Nonsubscribers can encounter intelligible pictures or audio after tuning to a scrambled premium channel, including children acting without parental knowledge or permission; the record does not establish arrival of the content regardless of channel selection. Protecting susceptible children, assisting parental authority and preventing unwanted exposure within the home supply substantial, compelling interests at their properly confined scope. The lower court recognized a sufficient risk to susceptible minors notwithstanding deficiencies in quantified exposure and clinical proof. No commitment depends on denying that risk or holding the speech obscene.

All eight treat the challenged restraint as requiring demanding content-based review, not Turner's content-neutral intermediate standard or automatic transfer of broadcast scarcity. The government must justify the adult burden and the necessity and fit of its means. A claimant must identify a concrete, plausibly effective less restrictive means; the constitutional burden of establishing the inadequacy of that supported alternative remains with the government. The lower court's inconsistent shorthand about the claimant's efficacy burden does not transfer the ultimate burden. Neither side must prove perfect protection, but the alternative must actually serve the legitimate interest rather than merely exist on paper.

The actual proposed alternative is section 504 plus adequate recurring notice through Playboy's contracts. The lower court lacked jurisdiction to command nonparty operators directly. Notice identifies the channels, intelligible bleed and possible child exposure, free blocking and how to request it; regular notice at reasonable intervals and special mailed notice after a previously blocked channel changes are distinct features. Billing inserts, preview channels and other-channel advertisements are examples, not cumulative mandatory media. No statutory notice command, fixed interval, installation deadline, automatic repair/replacement protocol or binding duty on every other programmer is invented.

The corrected survey measures device distributions below 0.5 percent among its respondents, not national requests or informed parental decisions. The court did not establish adequate notice during that period. The 69-percent text and 68-percent footnote describe the bounded time-channeling survey and are not reconciled into a precise national rate. The 30–50-percent adult viewing evidence and operator-specific buy data have distinct populations and periods. Actual exposure is not the same as potential exposure; the record includes anecdotes without a population incidence rate. Government assertions about delay, device failure and channel changes retain their evidentiary status and supply no universal failure rate. Traps, mapping and digital systems have distinct capabilities and costs. Device price, a mechanism-plus-installation amount, systemwide scenarios and forecasts are not interchangeable.

## Adversarial comparison and precise disagreement

**Invalidation path — Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.** The law directly selects protected content. Its nominal option to install complete systemwide protection does not eliminate its substantial practical daytime burden where operators use time channeling. The lower court's household remedy is concrete only with adequate notice and functioning blocking, but the corrected record does not demonstrate that this less restrictive arrangement would be inadequate enough to justify the broader restraint. The government cannot turn uncertainty about an inadequately publicized observation period into affirmative proof that informed household selection fails. Protecting susceptible minors remains compelling; the defect is the demonstrated necessity and fit of the means at this record and scope.

They consider default protection of inattentive households the strongest objection. They do not require government to ignore those children or accept a sham alternative. But the record supports meaningful parental protection without established widespread technical failure, impossibility of effective notice or a demonstrated comparative deficit sufficient to justify the adult burden. General possibilities of delay, missed notices and determined children do not alone carry that burden. They do not find those possibilities nonexistent or require perfect alternatives.

**Sustaining path — Scalia and Breyer.** Section 505 addresses a specific failure to keep a paid premium service from nonsubscribers, permits complete blocking at all hours, and otherwise preserves late-night transmission. Its default protection reaches children whom a parent-activated system predictably leaves outside the block. The lower comparison only promises conditional effectiveness for concerned parents and lacks a demonstrated adequate-notice implementation history. Real installation, configuration and cost issues qualify its suggested equivalence. These Justices find the narrower trigger, technical alternative and retained period adequate under demanding scrutiny despite substantial adult burdens.

They confront the strongest objection that the government has not quantified this comparative shortfall and that a large share of adult viewing occurs in the restricted hours. Their conclusion does not deny that evidence. They regard the difference between default protection and requested intervention, on the technical and practical record, as a sufficient supported reason for the legislature's choice without a numerical harm threshold or proof that every household alternative will fail. They accept the ultimate governmental burden and differ over whether it is satisfied, not over a presumption that content-selected regulation is valid.

Six Associates support affirmance and two support reversal. That tally excludes Stone and does not establish a final Court coalition. The shared factual and statutory account does not conceal disagreement about comparative effectiveness and justification. No prior concurrence or dissent is silently treated as a binding complete framework.

## Remedies, narrower alternatives and circulation barriers

The six affirmance supporters preserve the lower declaration and injunction against §505, with the existing contractual-notice direction at its actual scope. They do not write publicity obligations into §504, bind nonparty operators or programmers, add a notice deadline or technical repair system, or declare notice already adequate. The source's warning that §504 would cease to be a viable alternative without adequate notice establishes no automatic revival of §505 or newly specified enforcement process. Ordinary administration of the existing lawful decree is distinct from a new Court-designed program.

The two reversal supporters would reverse the First Amendment invalidation and its enforcement injunction and remand for lawful disposition of any properly preserved grounds not reached below. They do not decide those other grounds against Playboy or prescribe interim relief while they are considered. Their merits conclusion permits §505 to operate according to its enacted and FCC-specified terms, subject to such unresolved lawful proceedings; it directs no particular operator to buy equipment and makes no new compliance finding. Section 504's independent no-charge requested-blocking duty remains under either outcome; the lower constitutional substitute is not transformed into an independent statutory command by its reversal.

No supported intermediate disposition divides analog and digital systems into a new constitutional statute, selects different hours, imposes mandatory interim devices, or severs an invented protected-adult branch. Complete blocking remains an enacted compliance route, not a finding about every system's capabilities. The case does not decide a new obscenity prosecution, other independently lawful indecency controls, damages or future improved technical arrangements. Lawful obscenity enforcement remains available through its own predicates. Equal-protection and vagueness theories not reached below are unnecessary to the affirmance ground and are not decided anew.

Any proposed circulation relying on guaranteed notice receipt, actual post-notice request percentages, universal device success/failure, precise future conversion dates, a cost-free operator remedy or jurisdiction over all operators would exceed the supplied record and require a new neutral source review. None is a predicate of these commitments. No remaining material source gap blocks the bounded comparison recorded here.
