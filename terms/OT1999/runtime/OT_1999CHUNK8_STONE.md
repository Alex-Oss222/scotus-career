## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Must a nonrecidivist fact authorizing punishment above

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. The New Jersey Supreme Court's judgment is final and reviewable under 28 U.S.C. § 1257(a), and Apprendi's guilty plea expressly reserved his challenge to the enhancement. The question decided is who must find a fact that raises the authorized maximum, and by what standard of proof. Stone does not decide whether, or in what form, the Constitution requires a State to charge such a fact. The jury and proof holding alone resolves the judgment; the Appellate Division dissent records that Apprendi did not separately raise a grand-jury indictment claim in that court; whether New Jersey law requires such a fact to be indicted is a question of state law; and no party addressed whether a federal charging requirement applies to the States.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone adopts, as a new constitutional holding that no earlier decision of the Court made, the jury and reasonable-doubt protection he stated in his *Almendarez-Torres* concurrence and maintained in *Jones*, here for a nonrecidivist fact. Under the Sixth Amendment's jury guarantee, applied to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment, and the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment, a nonrecidivist fact legally necessary to authorize punishment above the verdict- or plea-supported maximum is an element that must be either admitted as an element or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. In this state prosecution Stone decides only those jury and proof requirements; he does not decide whether, or in what form, a State must allege such a fact in its charging instrument, and he does not apply the Fifth Amendment's grand-jury clause to the States. Apprendi admitted an offense carrying a ten-year maximum; the judge alone found biased purpose by a preponderance and used that finding to impose twelve years. The enhanced sentence therefore exceeds the punishment authorized by the admitted offense.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Under the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments, a nonrecidivist fact legally necessary to authorize punishment above the verdict- or plea-supported maximum is an element that must be either admitted as an element or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. New Jersey could not authorize Apprendi's twelve-year sentence through a judicial preponderance finding when the admitted offense carried a ten-year maximum.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The jury guarantee, which applies to the States through the Fourteenth Amendment (*Duncan*), and the due-process requirement of proof beyond a reasonable doubt (*Winship*) protect the facts the law makes necessary to the punishment imposed. Simulated *Gaudin* requires jury determination of an element once it is identified. Whether a fact is an element turns on its legal effect; legislative placement cannot transfer that work entirely to the sentencing judge. Simulated *Almendarez-Torres*, *Jones*, *Castillo* and *Carter* classified aggravating facts as elements by statutory construction and expressly left this constitutional question open. Federal statutory construction cannot rewrite New Jersey's authoritative reading of its own statute, so the constitutional question must now be decided. *McMillan*'s minimum left the maximum unchanged; *Witte* and *Watts* govern findings within conviction-based authority; *Monge*'s double-jeopardy classification of a prior-conviction qualification reserved independent indictment, jury and due-process claims and does not govern this nonrecidivist fact. Apprendi admitted only the offense carrying a ten-year maximum. The judge's later preponderance finding of biased purpose supplied the sole legal authority for a twelve-year sentence, so the finding performed the work of an aggravated-offense element without the required admission or jury verdict. Stone reverses and vacates the enhanced sentence on this constitutional ground. The rule creates no prior-conviction exception; facts of prior conviction are outside the question decided and are not decided.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Judicial factfinding remains permissible within the punishment authorized by the conviction. Mandatory minimums that do not change the maximum, consecutive-sentence rules, capital selection, and materially different sentencing systems remain outside the granted question. Facts of prior conviction are not decided. Whether, and by what source, a State must charge a fact covered by this rule is not decided. The holding does not apply the Fifth Amendment's grand-jury clause to the States, leaves unaffected the federal charging consequences recognized in *Almendarez-Torres*, *Jones* and *Castillo*, and does not withdraw Stone's view, stated in his *Almendarez-Torres* concurrence, that in a federal prosecution such a fact must be charged.
