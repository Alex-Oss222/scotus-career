## Actual judgment, question and text

The Frees seek reversal of the jurisdictional basis for the June 3, 1999 final judgment, reported at 176 F.3d 298. That judgment completed the Fifth Circuit's disposition after Louisiana declined certified antitrust questions and affirmed dismissal on state-law grounds. The January opinion at 164 F.3d 270 is an earlier step, not the final judgment. The action began in October 1993, so the applicable diversity amount is $50,000. No party is credited with alleging ordinary individual damages over that amount: the assumed named-plaintiff anchor arose from the lower court's fee-attribution treatment.

The granted question assumes one named plaintiff's claim within original diversity jurisdiction and same-Article-III-case relatedness of the absent members' smaller claims. Those assumptions define the legal question; they do not resolve contested fee allocation generally or authorize aggregation to manufacture an original anchor. The Court does not review the correctness of Louisiana antitrust law, restore a never-approved settlement, decide certification, reconsider discretionary retention, or decide a complete-diversity defect.

Section 1367(a), subject to (b), (c) and express contrary federal statutes, grants supplemental power over other claims forming the same Article III case as claims within original jurisdiction and includes additional-party claims. Subsection (b) separately restricts diversity-only actions: claims by plaintiffs against persons made parties under Rules 14, 19, 20 or 24, claims by persons proposed as plaintiffs under Rule 19, and claims by persons intervening as plaintiffs under Rule 24, when inconsistent with §1332. Rule 23 is absent. The Rule 20 restriction addresses claims against joined persons; it is not an enacted exclusion of every Rule 20 plaintiff. Subsection (c)'s distinct conditions for discretionary nonretention do not create or erase antecedent power, and no new holding on (c), tolling or the statutory definition of State is needed. The enactment applies to actions commenced on or after December 1, 1990.

## Affirmance ground: Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas

These three directly join the following rule at the granted scope: when a named plaintiff supplies the assumed original diversity claim and absent class members' claims form the same Article III case, §1367(a) supplies supplemental jurisdiction over their below-amount claims, and no exclusion in §1367(b) removes that power merely because they are Rule 23 members. Their claims are added to an existing anchor; they are not aggregated to create it.

Finley required statutory authorization for additional parties; Congress enacted such language. City of Chicago's actual Part I rejects the reading that every claim must independently satisfy original jurisdiction before supplemental power can operate, while its diversity reservation leaves this case for independent decision. Zahn governed the former statutory scheme; §1367 changes its operation for this defined class setting. Snyder's prohibition on aggregation to manufacture the anchor remains intact. The diversity-only list is given its actual terms rather than an added Rule 23 exception.

The strongest counterargument reads the original-jurisdiction condition in light of Zahn before reaching the list of exclusions, with the committee's stated preservation of Zahn confirming that context. The three reject that reading here because the assumed named claim already supplies original jurisdiction, while the enacted additional-party grant would be denied effect for related claims without an applicable listed exception. Legislative history cannot insert that omission into an otherwise operative grant. Their reasoning is not a free-standing appeal to efficiency and decides no constitutional expansion of Article III or cure of every §1332 defect.

## Reversal ground: Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

These four directly join the distinct rule that §1367 did not remove Zahn's independent amount requirement for the absent class members' separate diversity claims in this action. The antecedent condition requiring an action within original jurisdiction must be read against §1332's established claim-specific amount limits and Congress's separate treatment of diversity. The express legislative preservation of Zahn confirms a construction they find the enacted condition can bear; it does not supply a free-floating exception to unambiguous contrary text.

They acknowledge the force of the additional-party sentence and the absence of Rule 23 from (b). Their answer is that (b) adds prohibitions against enumerated joinder routes; it does not exhaustively define the antecedent scope of a diversity action or silently displace every preexisting limitation. They preserve the assumed named plaintiff's qualifying claim. They do not demand original jurisdiction over every supplemental theory in a federal-question action, discard City of Chicago's actual mixed-complaint holding, or deny the constitutional sufficiency of same-case relation. Their disagreement is over the enacted statutory authority for these distinct diversity plaintiffs' separate claims.

Finley's statutory-authorization requirement remains respected. Stevens's Finley dissent expressly distinguished diversity restrictions in Zahn and Kroger from the federal forum interests of the federal-question setting; it therefore does not compel him to read the later enactment expansively in this case. Souter and Breyer's City of Chicago joins preserve a federal-question anchor and expressly reserve diversity-only questions; no inconsistent all-claims-original-jurisdiction rule is adopted. Ginsburg and Stevens's City of Chicago dissent concerned antecedent statutory power, not a discretionary refusal to retain otherwise authorized claims.
