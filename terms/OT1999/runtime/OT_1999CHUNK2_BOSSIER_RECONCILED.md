## Historical comparison

Historically the Court affirmed, 5–4. Part II's mootness rejection was unanimous. Scalia's Parts I, III and IV were joined by Rehnquist, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas and limited the purpose prong to retrogression in this dilution setting. It does not materially weaken the second group's historical record-based reason to deny clearance. The independent model's universal-remand conclusion therefore does not survive intact. ## The developed record and the genuine remedy fork

The Board's burden is to establish absence of the prohibited purpose; it is not the challengers' burden to show that every lawful concern was invented. Dilution itself, racial awareness or rejection of a feasible alternative is not conclusive. The administrative explanations also receive real consideration: the Police Jury plan needed no precinct splits, while the NAACP map required forty-six, Cooper II twenty-seven and a one-majority-Black alternative fourteen; prior approval gave a practical reason to expect prompt clearance. The availability of seeking precinct changes does not prove that such a request would have been granted. Polarization data have limits, successes in other-office elections have their own qualifications, and Musgrove denied the attributed statement. No disputed statement is silently converted into an admission.

**Why four would deny now:** The conclusion follows from the combined evidence, not any isolated factor. The Board's stipulated resistance to desegregation and participation history supplies context. It ignored the already precleared Police Jury plan for more than a year despite an early proposal to adopt it, pursued its own districting work, and changed course after the NAACP demonstrated feasible minority districts. The adopted plan departed from ordinary school, incumbent, compactness and contiguity concerns. The record supports that the Board was told it could seek precinct changes but did not pursue them; the blanket split-precinct objection does not explain why no majority-Black option could be considered. This combination undermines the claim that ease and anticipated clearance independently explain the actual timing and choice. The contested Musgrove account is not indispensable to that inference. The admitted dilution is corroborating impact, not a substitute for purpose.

For Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, the administrative considerations may exist yet fail to dispel the demonstrated purpose to preserve racial dilution and defeat consideration of an effective minority alternative. They would find the contrary ultimate broad-purpose conclusion clearly erroneous if the lower decisions are read to contain one; if they are instead read as failing to perform that inquiry, the developed record and the Board's burden still do not require another opportunity to establish the same entitlement. The exact first remand reserved purpose without predetermining it; it did not immunize a future record-based appellate denial or guarantee repeated remands. These four therefore reject the provisional assertion that resolving purpose now necessarily entails unsupported original credibility findings.

**Why four would remand:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas regard the return court's legal error as decisive but would leave the broader contextual inference and weight of real administrative reasons to the statutory factfinding court. They do not find the Board entitled to clearance, and they do not treat no retrogression as no unlawful purpose. They require the proper inquiry with the existing burden, while declining to select the denial group's conclusive inference from the same evidence. Their path is lawful without presuming a new document, additional hearing, litigant default or material missing source.

## Same-decision issue

All eight reject affirmance based on a supposed existing finding that the identical plan would have been adopted without prohibited purpose. The actual findings of prompt preclearance and ease of implementation do not contain that counterfactual finding. The factual record is not silently supplemented to supply it.

The four favoring denial would reach the same disposition even assuming a same-decision defense is legally available: on their appraisal the Board has not proved independently sufficient lawful reasons that would have produced this identical plan absent the discriminatory purpose. Conceivable benefits, a different lawful map, or the mere existence of some administrative concerns cannot establish that showing. They therefore need not decide the defense's ultimate statutory availability.

The four favoring remand likewise reserve that legal question. Arlington Heights/Mt. Healthy supply a serious causation analogy; section 5's preventive language and the unchanged jurisdictional burden, together with Lesage's distinction between process injury and particular relief, prevent automatic transplantation. If the remand court considers a same-decision argument, neither the absence of current findings nor the unresolved legal issue permits it to presume the factual showing or direct clearance on an abstract lawful objective. No new proof quantum, burden shift or categorical same-decision clearance rule is adopted by any Associate here.
