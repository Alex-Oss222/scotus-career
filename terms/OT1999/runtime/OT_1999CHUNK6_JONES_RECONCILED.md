## Historical confirmation and concrete legal difference

Historically all eight Associates joined reversal on statutory grounds. The Court used the building's actual function, distinguished the three asserted household connections from qualifying use, and reserved the constitutional question. Its explanation also used commercial-purpose language, avoidance, lenity and federal-state balance. The separate Stevens writing, joined by Thomas, emphasized clarity before displacing state criminal-policy choices. Thomas, joined by Scalia, expressly reserved the constitutionality of every application to commercial buildings. Neither separate writing supplied a contrary judgment. These results confirm the independent statutory direction; they do not dictate present authors, joins or additional rationales.

The material changed legal premise is the Court's April 6, 1998 Hinds decision. Each of these eight Associates directly joined its complete property-function construction, including rejection of profit-making, rental, sales or exclusively business use as a universal prerequisite. That earlier controlling construction must be applied here. Accordingly, historical references to active commercial employment cannot be imported as a new exclusively commercial condition, and owner occupancy cannot become a categorical exemption. The actual use and the three asserted connections decide this case; a genuinely different use remains subject to Hinds.

The in-world Lopez/Morrison baseline also differs from a simple economic/noneconomic divide. Its controlling inquiry identifies the enacted class and a constitutionally eligible interstate connection before magnitude, with the fitted necessary-regime and supported noninternalizable-externality routes preserved. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain that reasoning. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain their published proposal for a broader rational substantial-class-effects approach; that proposal does not control. None of those constitutional positions requires reading §844(i) beyond the property-use limit Congress enacted, and none produces a new constitutional vote here.

**Historical departure:** The eight Associates preserve the historical statutory reversal but use Hinds's already binding functional-use rule without an exclusively commercial prerequisite. Stevens's authorship and the other seven Associates' full joins in that earlier construction provide a concrete Justice-specific changed legal premise for withholding broader commercial-only language. The present unanimous statutory rationale needs neither a common constitutional-avoidance ground nor a common lenity ground; Hinds supplies an independently sufficient construction, and Scalia alone retains optional residual lenity at the limited scope frozen below.

The remedy is more explicit than the comparator's general reversal and remand: the approved existing-record premise and Hinds/Burks require entry of acquittal on §844(i), without another evidentiary opportunity. ## Operative rule and reasons available for a common writing

Section 844(i) requires the damaged property itself to be used in interstate or foreign commerce or in an activity affecting that commerce. Courts identify the function the property actually performs and then determine whether that function has the required relationship. A private residence is not brought within the provision solely by an out-of-state mortgage lender, out-of-state insurer and interstate gas supply. Those transactions finance, protect or support habitation; they do not by themselves establish that the building is used in the businesses supplying them.

The Government's strongest argument is that affecting commerce is broad, the house continually serves as collateral and insured risk, and the statute contains an express case-specific element. The answer gives effect to the element actually enacted: broad commerce effects cannot erase the independent use condition. Treating virtually universal incidents of ownership as sufficient would make that condition do no limiting work. The result rests on the actual residential function and identified prosecution theory rather than a categorical exclusion of homes.

Russell's rental use remains sufficient in its established setting; it is not an exclusive permitted class. Hinds continues to reject a universal rental, profit-making, sales or exclusively business prerequisite and any religious/nonprofit exemption. No home office, mixed use, institutional function or former rental use is invented here. A different supported function requires its own Hinds application. No individual-fire substantial impact, interstate travel or new statutory nexus is required.

These propositions are statutory holdings, not constitutional avoidance as an independently sufficient holding. No Associate must surrender an earlier Commerce position to join them. Scalia's possible residual lenity adds no vote to the common rule and should be separated if stated. No general federalism clear-statement holding follows merely from the historically published separate concurrence.
