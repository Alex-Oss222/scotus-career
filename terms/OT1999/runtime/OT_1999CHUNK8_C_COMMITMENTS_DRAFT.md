## Apprendi — issues and operative law

The Court can reach the preserved allocation and proof challenge through review of the final New Jersey judgment under section 1257(a). The guilty plea admitted the firearm offenses and waived their jury trials, but expressly reserved the enhancement challenge. Awareness that the State would request an extended term is neither admission of racial purpose nor surrender of that reservation. The firearm offense's ordinary maximum is ten years; the judge's preponderance finding of biased purpose supplied authority for twenty, and Count 18 received twelve. Possible consecutive punishment on other counts does not itself authorize twelve years on this count. Dismissed harassment counts contained racial-bias allegations; none became an adjudicated or admitted element of Count 18.

The applied subsection (e) concerns purpose to intimidate because of race, color, gender, handicap, religion, sexual orientation, or ethnicity. The mandatory extended-term command follows the prosecutor's application and the subsection-(e) finding; it must not be confused with discretion governing other extended-term grounds. The exclusions identified in the neutral packet concern sections 2C:12-1(a), 2C:33-4, and gender-based applications involving 2C:14-2 or 2C:14-3. No identified exclusion applies to this firearm offense. The lower court's view that the record satisfied the amended purpose standard is not a jury finding or admission, and this federal question does not reopen the ungranted state-law articulation issue. The distinct Graves Act provision supplies the parole restriction; the challenged bias statute does not itself prescribe the four-year bar.

### Supported competing paths

**Path A — present-offense maximum rule.** A fact about the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to exceed the maximum authorized by the offense otherwise admitted or found by the jury performs the constitutional function of an element. The State must prove it beyond a reasonable doubt, and the defendant is entitled to jury determination unless that entitlement is validly waived; a valid admission of the aggravating fact may obviate disputed proof. This case requires both the jury-allocation and reasonable-doubt conclusions. The rule does not silently incorporate the federal grand-jury requirement against a State or resolve bare prior-conviction status, unchanged-maximum minima, capital eligibility or selection, every Guidelines system, or general consecutive-sentencing rules.

The strongest objection is that legislatures traditionally define crimes and judges consider motive when selecting punishment; choosing a maximum as the constitutional boundary can leave superficially similar discretion below the ceiling unaffected. The answer supporting Path A is the legal consequence: discretion to choose within existing authority differs from finding the indispensable present-offense fact that first creates greater authority. Otherwise a legislature could make the admitted offense a preliminary shell and transfer the aggravating offense's factual adjudication to a judge through placement alone. Here the rule governs the disputed racial purpose used to cross ten years, not consideration of motive within ten years.

**Path B — bounded legislative sentencing allocation.** The firearm offense is complete upon the admitted unlawful possession and purpose. The separate, broadly applicable aggravator may permit a judge to consider motive under the legislature's express sentencing scheme. An adversarial hearing, notice, the absence of a presumption displacing the admitted offense, the traditional consideration of motive and the provision's limited design support that conclusion. This path would affirm against the presented federal objection, without approving every relabeling or declaring a prior-conviction exception.

Its strongest objection is that the very purpose finding creates permission to exceed the completed offense's maximum; a completed baseline offense cannot alone answer why the additional culpability is exempt from Winship and Duncan. The answer supporting Path B is that the Constitution has not made every circumstance affecting a punishment range an element and that Patterson preserves meaningful legislative responsibility. The path must defend that actual allocation here; McMillan, Witte, Watts and Monge cannot supply a nonexistent controlling answer about this maximum increase.

**Path C — narrower present-culpability rule.** Racial purpose is an additional mental-state condition of aggravated criminal liability and therefore receives element protections, while other maximum-increasing facts remain unclassified. This is a lawful narrower alternative, but its boundary must rest on the constitutional role of culpability rather than an unsupported assertion that all motives are elements. The present record does not justify a proof-only disposition that leaves the jury allocation unexplained, or a state statutory rewrite.

## Dickerson — issues and operative law

The federal interlocutory suppression appeal presents whether section 3501 permits case-in-chief use of a statement excluded under Miranda. The United States supports Dickerson on the statute's inability to displace Miranda and opposes overruling that decision. Cassell's amicus brief supplies the principal defense of the judgment. The separate question whether the circuit should have taken up the statute, the refusal to reopen the suppression record, and the physical-evidence grounds do not become additional merits components.

The district court found custody and found the disputed statements preceded the warnings and waiver. The later affidavit and handwritten statement were proffered on reconsideration; they did not become accepted substitute findings. The Fourth Circuit treated the statements as voluntary by inference from the derivative-evidence ruling. That is the appellate court's account, not a new finding here. The constitutional question can be decided on the presented claimed authority to admit a voluntary but unwarned statement without replacing either court's factual work.

### Statutory and precedent boundaries

Section 3501(a) directs admission of a voluntary confession, judicial determination outside the jury's presence, relevant jury evidence and a weight instruction. Subsection (b) requires consideration of all circumstances, including the applicable arrest-to-arraignment interval, knowledge of the suspected offense, advice or knowledge concerning silence and use, prior advice of counsel rights, and actual absence of counsel. Presence or absence of a listed factor need not be conclusive. It does not make warnings irrelevant, but it does not make their absence invariably dispositive either.

Subsection (c) independently addresses exclusion solely for presentment delay: voluntary statements within six hours of the relevant arrest or detention receive its protection if weight is left to the jury. The limit does not apply when the judge finds a longer transportation- and distance-related delay reasonable to the nearest available qualifying officer. **Alvarez-Sanchez**, May 2, 1994, preserves the separate relevant-duty inquiry and reservations. Subsection (d) preserves voluntary statements given without interrogation or while the speaker is not under arrest or detention. Subsection (e) includes both confessions of guilt and oral or written self-incriminating statements. No position below requires declaring every subsection void.

**Miranda** establishes custodial safeguards and permits equally effective alternatives. **Harris** and **Hass** address impeachment; **Quarles** supplies its limited public-safety setting; **Tucker** and **Elstad** distinguish derivative evidence and later warned voluntary statements. They prevent equating every unwarned statement with actual compulsion for every purpose; they do not already decide Congress's present replacement claim. **Connelly** preserves police coercion as a predicate of due-process involuntariness.

**Withrow**, April 21, 1993, preserves properly available Miranda habeas review and the distinction from a separately pleaded coercion theory. **Stansbury**, April 26, 1994, and **Communicated circumstances and Miranda custody** retain objective arrest-equivalent custody. **Davis**, June 24, 1994, and **Clarifying a possible present request for interrogation counsel** retain the mandated neutral clarification after a reasonable possible present request, clear-invocation cessation and the valid postwaiver application; they expressly reserve section 3501. **Thompson**, November 29, 1995, and **Independent Miranda custody review under the former habeas statute** classify ultimate custody as a constitutional mixed question while protecting historical facts; the AEDPA transition does not decide this federal criminal question.

**Smith v. Robbins**, January 19, 2000, permits equivalent safeguards rather than mandatory repetition of Anders's precise form. Its five-Justice inadequacy holding requires the advocate's function and refuses to substitute other useful but functionally different protections. The four dissenters accepted Wende's full package as adequate. Neither side's application automatically decides confession safeguards. **Boerne**, June 25, 1997, distinguishes enforcement from replacement of the substantive right; its section 5 standard is not transplanted as a new test for federal confession legislation. **Hubbell**, June 5, 2000, concerns compelled testimonial production and statutory direct/derivative immunity, not every unwarned statement.

### Supported competing paths

**Path D — constitutional implementation and insufficient substitution.** Miranda is an authoritative constitutional implementation of the privilege in custodial interrogation. Its allowance for effective alternatives does not permit substitution of the retrospective voluntariness test it held insufficient. Sections 3501(a) and (b) cannot authorize this case-in-chief use simply because the statement is voluntary under that test. The statute's other lawful functions, independent voluntariness review, existing exceptions and the possibility of genuinely effective alternative safeguards remain.

The strongest objection is that Tucker, Elstad, the impeachment cases and other exceptions repeatedly distinguish Miranda violations from actual compulsion. The response is that a constitutionally grounded prophylactic rule can prescribe an enforceable safeguard without making each departure actual compulsion for every remedial purpose. Direct case-in-chief admission without the safeguard is the core function, not the derivative-use or later-valid-waiver question. Reliance and established administration support retaining that core; neither empirical success nor uniform warning persistence is treated as a proved fact.

**Path E — legislative replacement of supplemental protection.** The Constitution prohibits compelled self-incrimination; Miranda additionally excludes some statements that are actually voluntary. Congress can regulate federal evidence and replace that supplemental regime while preserving actual coercion review. The section 3501 package considers warnings, judicial screening and jury weighting, with other lawful protections considered only within their real availability. It would sustain the circuit's rejection of warning omission as a sufficient ground of exclusion here.

The strongest objection is that Miranda binds States and supports constitutional habeas review, making it untenable to characterize its entire operation as federal supervisory law. A lawful defense must address that problem directly: either the judicial safeguard is an implementation open to legislative alteration without redefining actual compulsion, or the Court must reconsider its own asserted categorical constitutional requirement. Congress's enactment alone cannot settle the constitutional meaning. Civil remedies are not universal, and their possible existence does not establish equivalence by assertion.

**Path F — judicial reconsideration.** The Court may separately consider the amicus request to abandon the categorical unwarned-statement bar where voluntariness is established. This requires a conclusion about constitutional compulsion and a stare-decisis justification independent of congressional preference. Its strongest objection is that the Court has repeatedly retained case-in-chief suppression while defining exceptions, that officers and courts have an administrable existing system, and that a return to voluntariness removes the prospective safeguard without an equivalent replacement. It is a supported competing ground, not an inference that every Justice who accepts alternative safeguards wishes to overrule Miranda.

His Boerne opinion distinguishes constitutional enforcement from replacing the judicially enforceable right, without supplying a mechanical section 5 test here.

**Strongest contrary ground and limits:** The rules exclude some voluntary statements, and their limits legitimately consider law enforcement. Those points justify bounded rules and exceptions; they do not show that retrospective voluntariness protects the informed choice his Roberson alternative itself required. Path F would remove that core rather than merely prevent a new extension. He will join an opinion recognizing constitutional implementation, equivalent alternatives and existing exceptions, but not one declaring every particular warning formula irrevisable or every departure actual compulsion. His unanimous Hubbell join concerns actual compelled production and does not erase this distinction. The bounded suppression remand leaves the physical evidence and prosecution intact.

#### Souter

**Commitment:** Reverse and remand on Path D. Section 3501 cannot authorize admission contrary to Miranda merely through a finding of voluntariness, and neither replacement theory nor judicial reconsideration warrants the different result.

**Justice-specific support and competing paths:** His Withrow opinion preserves Miranda's trial safeguard and its federal review while expressly distinguishing independent coercion. His Davis opinion supplies a practical means of protecting an effective custodial counsel choice after ambiguity. His Robbins join rejects proposed substitute protections that omit the advocate's necessary function. These are concrete grounds to test the statute's warning factors and post hoc review against the safeguard's actual purpose rather than treat the label prophylactic as dispositive. Path E does not show that a hearing about coercion gives a suspect an informed choice before speaking. Path F's voluntariness approach would remove the safeguard he has repeatedly applied and developed.

**Strongest contrary ground and limits:** Elstad and Tucker recognize that warning violations differ from actual compulsion; Souter's own Withrow opinion makes that distinction too. Constitutional authority therefore cannot rest on equating the two in every case. He instead supports a rule necessary to implement the privilege in custodial interrogation, with the existing tailored consequences and genuinely equivalent alternatives preserved. He will not constitutionalize every judicial procedural choice or invalidate unrelated statutory functions. Stare decisis supports the core's continued operation; contested advocacy about costs or training does not establish an empirical basis for abandoning it. No fresh credibility finding or dismissal of the indictment is warranted.

#### Thomas

**Commitment:** Affirm against the presented Miranda-displacement challenge on Path E; join the expressly identified Path F alternative if the categorical rule is treated as constitutional content requiring judicial reconsideration. Actual compulsion remains prohibited, and the statutory circumstances must actually be considered.

**Justice-specific support and competing paths:** His Robbins dissent assesses the full substitute procedure instead of requiring a particular judicial form; his Withrow join in Scalia's separate writing and Davis join in O'Connor's writing distinguish the underlying protection from asserted additional enforcement. His Alvarez-Sanchez opinion expressly reserved the overall section 3501 issue, so it supplies no prior vote for either outcome. His Thompson dissent concerns the legal/factual classification of concrete custody application and does not itself deny Miranda's authority. Tucker, Elstad and Connelly supply the substantive distinction between compelled evidence and a voluntary unwarned statement that makes the replacement argument legally available. His Boerne join bars legislative redefinition of the constitutional right, not every replacement of a judicial safeguard that extends beyond actual compulsion.

**Strongest contrary ground and limits:** Path D correctly points to state and habeas enforcement and the risk that retrospectively proving coercion misses pressures the warning rule addresses. Thomas's contrary conclusion is that those considerations do not make absence of warnings alone actual compelled self-incrimination or render Congress powerless over federal admissibility. If Miranda's constitutional characterization forecloses that conclusion, he would judicially reject its irrebuttable exclusion of voluntary statements at that level rather than posit federal supervisory power over state courts. The recognized difference between actual compulsion and warning prophylaxis supplies the doctrinal reason; Congress's desire alone does not.

**Join barriers and remedy:** His Hubbell concurrence, joined by Scalia, reserves reconsideration of what compelled incriminating evidence the privilege covers; it concerns compulsion and does not establish a broader warning rule. He will not claim all constitutional safeguards are legislatively replaceable, universal damages remedies, actual compliance with warnings, or final voluntariness findings beyond the stated appellate posture. He would sustain the circuit's statutory admissibility basis against this challenge without deciding ungranted search or reopening questions and without a conviction or sentence disposition.

#### Ginsburg

**Commitment:** Reverse and remand on Path D, retaining the case-in-chief safeguard and the possibility of genuinely equivalent alternatives.

**Justice-specific support and competing paths:** Her Davis majority join protects meaningful choice through neutral clarification, her Thompson join preserves constitutional custody review, and her Robbins majority join rejects formal substitutes that omit the required legal-assistance function. Her Alvarez-Sanchez concurrence expressly preserves the section 3501/Miranda question; it cannot be converted into an earlier invalidity holding. Path E's five circumstances, judicial hearing and jury weighting address the evaluation of a statement after it exists, while warnings inform the suspect's choice before the statement. Proposed civil remedies require their actual predicates and cannot be presumed universally available. Path F supplies no adequate ground to abandon that prospective function.

**Strongest contrary ground and limits:** Elstad and the impeachment/derivative cases permit some use following a warning violation. Their specific rationales survive; they do not authorize direct use of the unwarned statement to prove the case without an effective substitute. She will not join a rule requiring exact words in all warnings, a conclusion that every violation is actual coercion, or wholesale invalidation of section 3501. Section 3501(c)'s distinct delay protection and its transportation proviso remain governed by Alvarez-Sanchez and its reservations. The ordinary remand concerns statement suppression alone; neither the trial judge's refusal to reopen nor the independent physical-evidence disposition is reversed through this holding.

#### Breyer

**Commitment:** Reverse and remand on Path D. An effective alternative may be considered, but the statutory voluntariness regime and its asserted accompanying protections do not supply one; reject judicial abolition of the case-in-chief rule here.

**Justice-specific support and competing paths:** His Robbins majority join gives concrete weight to whether an alternative actually preserves the protected function rather than merely contributing other helpful procedures. His Thompson join treats the ultimate custody boundary as a constitutional legal task. His Hubbell join protects compelled testimonial use under its different predicates and does not decide whether every unwarned statement is compelled. Path E has force because safeguards need not be frozen in their initial form and warnings can remain relevant under section 3501. But the statute makes the prospective informed-choice safeguard dispensable rather than supplying a demonstrated functional replacement. The judge's later coercion assessment and jury weight instruction perform different tasks.

**Strongest contrary ground and limits:** The statutory defense invokes costs, training, other remedies and the precedents permitting particular uses of unwarned evidence. Those claims warrant assessment, not presumed factual success or failure. The existing rule and its recognized exceptions supply administrable protection, and the supplied record establishes no adequate reason to replace it wholesale with the prior voluntariness inquiry. Path F therefore fails on the constitutional function and stare-decisis question, rather than because the Court can never revise an implementation rule. Breyer will not join categorical hostility to legislative alternatives or an equation of warnings with actual coercion for every purpose. His supported scope is the bounded federal suppression issue and remedy.

### Proposition and remedy boundaries for subsequent assembly

Six Associate commitments — Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer — share the constitutional-implementation ground, insufficiency of section 3501 as the asserted substitute, retention of Miranda's core under stare decisis, allowance for effective alternatives and preservation of the existing exceptions. Their agreement requires those qualifications; it does not create a universal rule that every court-devised safeguard is beyond legislation. Scalia and Thomas support legislative replacement in this setting and the stated judicial-reconsideration alternative. These are only the eight Associate commitments, not an assembled Court vote or author assignment.

For each Path D commitment, reverse the reviewed judgment insofar as section 3501 supplies the basis for reversing statement suppression, and remand for proceedings consistent with the surviving Miranda requirement. Sections 3501(a) and (b) cannot make a statement admissible merely because it is voluntary when the applicable Miranda rule excludes its case-in-chief use. Do not declare every provision of section 3501 void. Voluntariness remains a separate prerequisite, and the holding leaves lawful spontaneous or noncustodial statements, statutory presentment-delay questions, impeachment, public safety, derivative evidence and later warned statements to their governing rules.

The Court does not dismiss the indictment, acquit Dickerson, vacate a nonexistent conviction, order a new suppression hearing, reverse the refusal to reopen, or decide the separate physical-evidence issue. The finding concerning warning chronology remains the district court's finding; the circuit's voluntariness inference remains identified as such. No universal civil-liability consequence follows from calling Miranda a constitutional decision. Any future alternative must be evaluated for its actual protection, not accepted or rejected solely by its label.

For Scalia and Thomas, affirmance concerns the claimed constitutional obstacle to the section 3501 admissibility ground; it does not supply a new factual finding, abolish actual-coercion review or make the statute's listed considerations optional. Their alternative judicial revision is expressly a proposed change, not entering law or a claimed existing holding.
