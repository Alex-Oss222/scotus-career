## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Clear notice, voluntary acceptance and scope

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the IDEA immunity ruling and the funding ground supporting it.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Enforce Arkansas’s acceptance of an identified, clearly disclosed federal funding condition.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Acceptance of an identified federal funding condition may constitute consent to the disclosed remedy; participation in regulated activity alone does not.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 1403 gives specific notice of federal-court exposure, and Arkansas accepted the relevant IDEA assistance after that notice. This is an affirmative choice to receive a defined federal benefit on disclosed terms, unlike the ordinary commercial conduct College Savings refused to treat as consent. Cedar Rapids confirms that an express IDEA obligation does not disappear because compliance is substantial or costly, but consent and remedial scope must still be established separately. The present record identifies no retroactive surprise or coercive demand that defeats the described acceptance. Consent extends only to the recipient, claim and remedy encompassed by the bargain.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general waiver for Arkansas or every education-related remedy follows; an award still requires statutory authority and proof.

### Issue 2: Independent constitutional theories

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Consent suffices; Stone does not make a new §5 ruling necessary to the judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Preserve preventive enforcement under Boerne and Florida Prepaid without equating IDEA with constitutional education rights.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The consent ruling leaves the independent abrogation alternatives unresolved except insofar as needed to reject an automatic constitutional-violation prerequisite.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A State can violate IDEA without violating equal protection. That difference identifies the need for a preventive-fit inquiry; it does not itself establish that all broader legislation fails. Florida Prepaid expressly permits some statutory protection beyond individual constitutional torts. The informed funding choice disposes of this appeal, so Stone neither invents a mandatory four-part test nor announces blanket §5 invalidity. Article I enforcement likewise requires analysis of the actual obligation and measure, not an assumption that the patent judgment preapproved every funding remedy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Neither an implied constitutional education right nor blanket abrogation is adopted.

### Issue 3: Officers and the §504 companion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone preserves the distinct remedial channels and the recorded limits of review.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Analyze §504’s federal-suit notice and acceptance independently; decide only the granted IDEA component.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** IDEA consent does not by itself decide §504 immunity, and a prospective officer claim requires a connected officer and ongoing federal violation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** For §504, §2000d-7 supplies a different textual notice provision and the recipient must have accepted the relevant assistance with adequate notice of the asserted consequence. The program and remedial scope cannot be borrowed from IDEA. A proper §504 claim could therefore warrant its own analysis even though the historical panel rejected that immunity theory. Without a recorded grant covering Jim C., Stone states the governing distinction and reserves disposition. For an IDEA officer claim, future compliance must be the object; a treasury claim for past injury cannot become prospective merely by naming an employee.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No companion grant, new defendant, waiver finding for §504, or retrospective officer award is invented.
