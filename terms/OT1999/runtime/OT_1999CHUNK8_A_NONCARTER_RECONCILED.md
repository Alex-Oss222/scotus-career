## Bradley v. Arkansas Department of Education

### Reconciled result and operative ground

All eight Associates affirm the denial of immunity on the granted IDEA component and return the case for ordinary proceedings. Each fully joins the following sufficient rule: a State that voluntarily accepts IDEA funding after the effective express federal-suit condition receives unmistakable notice under §§1403 and 1415 of federal judicial enforcement of the funded program's obligations. Arkansas's actual program acceptance under that condition supplies consent to this IDEA suit. This requires the identified program and actual acceptance; general receipt of federal money or continuation of ordinary commercial activity is not enough.

The language identifies States, federal court and IDEA violations. Section 1415 supplies the civil-action route within the program. Calling §1403 an abrogation provision does not negate the disclosed forum consequence of the voluntary transaction. Congressional intent alone would not establish consent; the supported acceptance supplies the additional State choice. Atascadero's federal-suit clarity requirement is satisfied, rather than displaced. Dellmuth identifies the earlier statutory defect. College Savings rejects consent inferred from ordinary commercial activity while expressly leaving accepted-benefit conditions open. Dole's relatedness requirement is satisfied by judicial enforcement of this very education program. Arkansas conceded both spending-power validity and receipt of IDEA funds. No unsupported funding-dependence or coercion premise is supplied.

The statutory limits remain operative. Section 1403(b) allows remedies only to the extent otherwise available against another public entity; it creates no new remedy. Section 1403(c) applies to violations occurring in whole or in part after October 30, 1990. Section 1415's administrative decisions, appeal route, record and evidence provisions, appropriate-relief requirement, and applicable exhaustion rule remain distinct. Immunity denial does not satisfy them. The actual request is for prospective compliance relief, not compensatory or punitive damages.

### Historical comparison and changed law

The historical anchor is the Eighth Circuit's published opinion, 189 F.3d 745, official printed pp. 12–14 and notes 6–7. The panel affirmed IDEA immunity denial on actual funding consent and independently discussed prospective officer relief. Its Bradley judgment supports the result, but does not predict any Associate's vote. Its separate Jim C. judgment is outside the grant.

## Pegram v. Herdrich, No. 98-1949

### Reconciled result and bounded propositions

All eight Associates reverse the Seventh Circuit's revival of the reviewed amended ERISA count and remand for dismissal of that count. The $35,000 malpractice judgment and unreviewed counts remain undisturbed. This is a §409/§502(a)(2) claim seeking plan-focused relief; Mertens's §502(a)(3) remedial limitation is not the ground for dismissing it.

Each fully joins these propositions:

1. ERISA fiduciary status follows the particular statutory function. Ordinary treatment, including a medical-necessity or referral decision inseparable from furnishing that treatment, does not become discretionary plan administration merely because the decision also affects the care the HMO supplies. A separate discretionary decision applying plan-coverage terms can be fiduciary administration, whoever performs it. The rule is not an HMO exemption and does not make every decision involving medical information nonfiduciary.
2. Establishing or carrying out an ordinary general compensation and risk-sharing arrangement for furnishing medical care does not, by that fact alone, establish fiduciary conduct or breach. The HMO is not itself the employer's ERISA plan, and all its revenues or profits are not automatically plan assets. Actual control over a plan asset remains subject to the statutory asset branch without an extra discretion condition.
3. Even assuming the amended allegation about disputed and nonroutine claims reaches genuine discretionary administration, the pleaded breach theory remains the general year-end incentive arrangement. That arrangement alone does not plead an independently disloyal claims determination, administration contrary to the plan, or a direct reward for denying a claim. The proposed restoration of supplemental medical-expense payments likewise cannot follow simply from identifying the entire HMO as the plan. No general determination of ownership under every premium arrangement or legality of every incentive is made.

The third proposition is an express assumption in the claimant's favor for purposes of deciding the legal sufficiency of the already pleaded structural theory. It does not find a new pure-administration event, disregard the claim, or assert that the historical court adopted this alternative. This clarifies the frozen commitment's reference to allegations that “can describe” administration; its judgment and grounds remain those already independently modeled.

## Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., No. 99-536

### Reconciled result and operative rule

All eight Associates reverse the employer's Rule 50 appellate judgment. The actual record permits the age-discrimination verdict; the court of appeals may not reconsider that resolved sufficiency question under a disguised demand for additional proof. The case returns for any remaining properly preserved appellate issues, including an unresolved alternative new-trial ground if otherwise reviewable. No particular new trial, monetary award or front-pay calculation is directed.

Each fully joins the rule that a prima facie case plus evidence permitting rejection of the employer's material explanation can sustain a finding of intentional discrimination without a mandatory further category of direct or independent proof. It does not compel liability or always defeat judgment as a matter of law. The strength of the prima facie inference, the probative force of falsity and the rest of the evidence lawfully credited govern ordinary sufficiency. A conclusively established other lawful reason, or weak falsity evidence combined with otherwise conclusive independent evidence against discrimination, can defeat an otherwise suggested inference. Merely imagining another motive cannot defeat a reasonable inference actually available to the jury.

Rule 50 requires review of the whole record with reasonable inferences for the verdict winner, without weighing or credibility choices. The court disregards movant-favorable evidence the jury need not believe; it does not exclude every item merely because the movant introduced it. Uncontradicted and unimpeached evidence the jury must accept remains relevant, with disinterested testimony a recognized circumstance rather than a rigid witness-label rule.

Here the recordkeeping proof permits material disbelief, and age-directed comments can be connected to the discharge through Chesnut's role and influence. Competing testimony about timing, other decisionmakers and the workforce does not conclusively defeat the jury's inference. Oswalt's earlier departure is not an actual younger-supervisor discharge. The inference concerns age motivation under Hazen Paper, not liability merely for unfairness or falsehood. The plaintiff retains ultimate persuasion under Burdine; employer production and a compelled presumption are distinct.

### Historical comparison and independent legal foundation

The full historical Court opinion unanimously reverses on the same permissible-inference and whole-record sufficiency grounds. Ginsburg's full concurrence joins the opinion and anticipates that cases demanding further evidence will be uncommon where a substantial prima facie/falsity showing survives evidence the court must credit. That concern is compatible with the frozen Ginsburg row's warning against disguising a pretext-plus rule as case-by-case review; it is not an additional element, burden shift or absolute trial entitlement.

The actual simulated Hicks already expressly rejects both automatic liability and a mandatory extra-proof category. Its majority does not adopt a replacement persuasion allocation; Burdine remains governing. The historical Court's authorship and broader historical Hicks discussion are therefore not imported as sources of simulated law. Hazen Paper and Consolidated Coin preserve the causal and circumstantial-proof distinctions, and current Weisgram preserves legitimate appellate Rule 50 authority with fair treatment of supported new-trial grounds. None authorizes reweighing this valid record.
