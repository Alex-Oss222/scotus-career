## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Missouri’s candidate-contribution limits violate the First Amendment because

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current simulated law rather than a reduced *Buckley*-only formulation. Missouri bears the burden under *Russell*. The identified large contribution, state corruption episodes, and public perception of corruption objectively ground the asserted interest, while the record does not show that the operative ceilings materially disabled meaningful contribution, association, or effective campaigning. The limits are closely fitted on this record, so Stone reverses the injunction.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A candidate-contribution limit is constitutional when government establishes an objectively grounded risk of actual or apparent quid-pro-quo corruption or circumvention and closely fits the ceiling to that risk without materially disabling meaningful association or effective campaign advocacy. Missouri satisfied that standard on this record.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** *Russell*, not a generic invocation of *Buckley*, supplies the controlling burden and fit inquiry. Missouri identified an objectively grounded risk of actual and apparent corruption through the record summarized in Section I. The respondents did not establish that the operative ceilings prevented candidates from assembling resources for effective advocacy or contributors from participating meaningfully. No fixed federal-dollar floor or automatic inflation adjustment displaces that record-specific inquiry. Stone therefore reverses the Eighth Circuit, vacates the injunction, and remands for judgment for the state officials. Russell’s office-specific judgments prevent treating every contribution ceiling alike. Stone’s conclusion depends on Missouri’s operational evidence and the preservation of effective advocacy, not on a presumed safe dollar amount or a new requirement of proved completed bribery.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A materially lower ceiling, discriminatory structure, unsupported anticorruption premise, or record showing that candidates or contributors lack a realistic means of effective advocacy could produce a different result.
