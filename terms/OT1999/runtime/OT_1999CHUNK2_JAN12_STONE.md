* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply the commerce categories; distinguish direct regulation from commandeering; dissolve the facial injunction. Dissolve the injunction and direct judgment for the United States on the facial federalism challenge.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Congress may regulate the disclosure and interstate dissemination

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Congress may regulate the sale, disclosure, resale, and redisclosure of motor-vehicle information as an article of interstate commerce; a law directly regulating States as database owners does not commandeer them when it requires no legislation or enforcement against private parties. This preserves anti-commandeering's institutional boundary without converting it into immunity from direct federal regulation of a State's own data-disclosure activity. The ruling does not authorize Congress to command state legislation, require state officers to enforce federal law against others, or regulate noneconomic state activity without an enumerated-power foundation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Congress may regulate the sale, disclosure, resale, and redisclosure of motor-vehicle information as an article of interstate commerce; a law directly regulating States as database owners does not commandeer them when it requires no legislation or enforcement against private parties.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit. Congress may regulate the sale, disclosure, resale, and redisclosure of motor-vehicle information as an article of interstate commerce; a law directly regulating States as database owners does not commandeer them when it requires no legislation or enforcement against private parties. This preserves anti-commandeering's institutional boundary without converting it into immunity from direct federal regulation of a State's own data-disclosure activity. The ruling does not authorize Congress to command state legislation, require state officers to enforce federal law against others, or regulate noneconomic state activity without an enumerated-power foundation. The State is the custodian and discloser of the information Congress regulates. That direct duty differs from assigning state officers to enforce a federal program against others. Printz’s limited assistance permission need not be extended, and its unresolved fiscal issues need not be decided to sustain this conduct rule.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment denying a constitutional right to self-representation on direct appeal.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave appointed appellate counsel in place and allow the direct appeal to proceed under California’s lawful procedures, without foreclosing supplemental pro se submissions the State chooses to accept.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Constitutional self-representation on direct appeal

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the constitutional claim because Martinez seeks to displace appointed counsel on that ground.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The Constitution does not extend Faretta’s trial-specific self-representation right to a direct appeal. The appellate guarantee is meaningful review with competent advocacy, not a personal right to control every filing.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may require represented advocacy on a first appeal as of right and deny a defendant’s request to discharge appointed counsel for the purpose of proceeding entirely pro se.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The trial is the proceeding in which the accused personally confronts the prosecution, presents evidence, and may choose to bear the consequences of self-representation. An appeal reviews a completed record through legal claims and institutional procedures. Requiring counsel protects rather than diminishes the meaningful appellate access guaranteed by Douglas and Evitts. California’s refusal to create a personal pro se entitlement therefore does not violate the Sixth or Fourteenth Amendment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A State remains free to permit self-representation or supplemental pro se briefing. Stone does not decide ineffective assistance, conflict of interest, competency to waive counsel, or denial of access to the record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Tenth Circuit’s mootness judgment and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore consideration of the live challenge without deciding the validity of the contracting program, awarding a contract or prescribing a replacement scheme.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Uncertain certification and continued exposure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches only the mootness ruling.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the actual 1995 remand and the requirement of a concrete, continuing competitive controversy.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A certificate whose acceptance and continuing validity remain uncertain does not establish that Adarand’s challenged competitive disadvantage has ended.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Adarand’s repeated participation in bidding supplies the relevant prospect of future exposure. A certificate is legally significant only insofar as it reliably changes the terms on which the bidder may compete. Treating contingent certification as conclusive would terminate the case while the same disadvantage could recur without any change in the challenged program. The earlier judgment expressly remanded the program’s justification for adjudication; it did not finally uphold the program. The proper consequence is renewed consideration of the actual live issues, not a new ruling on racial preferences.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** If the complete record establishes durable removal of the disadvantage and no concrete future exposure, mootness must be reassessed on those facts.
