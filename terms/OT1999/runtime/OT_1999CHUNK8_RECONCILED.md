## Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., No. 99-536

### Reconciled result and operative rule

All eight Associates reverse the employer's Rule 50 appellate judgment. The actual record permits the age-discrimination verdict; the court of appeals may not reconsider that resolved sufficiency question under a disguised demand for additional proof. The case returns for any remaining properly preserved appellate issues, including an unresolved alternative new-trial ground if otherwise reviewable. No particular new trial, monetary award or front-pay calculation is directed.

Each fully joins the rule that a prima facie case plus evidence permitting rejection of the employer's material explanation can sustain a finding of intentional discrimination without a mandatory further category of direct or independent proof. It does not compel liability or always defeat judgment as a matter of law. The strength of the prima facie inference, the probative force of falsity and the rest of the evidence lawfully credited govern ordinary sufficiency. A conclusively established other lawful reason, or weak falsity evidence combined with otherwise conclusive independent evidence against discrimination, can defeat an otherwise suggested inference. Merely imagining another motive cannot defeat a reasonable inference actually available to the jury.

Rule 50 requires review of the whole record with reasonable inferences for the verdict winner, without weighing or credibility choices. The court disregards movant-favorable evidence the jury need not believe; it does not exclude every item merely because the movant introduced it. Uncontradicted and unimpeached evidence the jury must accept remains relevant, with disinterested testimony a recognized circumstance rather than a rigid witness-label rule.

Here the recordkeeping proof permits material disbelief, and age-directed comments can be connected to the discharge through Chesnut's role and influence. Competing testimony about timing, other decisionmakers and the workforce does not conclusively defeat the jury's inference. Oswalt's earlier departure is not an actual younger-supervisor discharge. The inference concerns age motivation under Hazen Paper, not liability merely for unfairness or falsehood. The plaintiff retains ultimate persuasion under Burdine; employer production and a compelled presumption are distinct.
