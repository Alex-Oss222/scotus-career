## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Reincarceration after mistaken release

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Affirm. A person has a serious liberty interest while lawfully living in the community, but a mistaken executive release does not extinguish a concededly valid uncompleted sentence or create a fundamental right to retain liberty obtained through the error. Correcting the mistake after roughly twenty months, without evidence of bad-faith inducement, manipulation, or an effort to evade a completed sentence, does not shock the conscience.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Reincarceration to complete a still-valid sentence after a good-faith mistaken release does not violate substantive due process merely because the person lived successfully at liberty for a substantial period. The result may differ where the State affirmatively induces irreversible reliance, acts in bad faith, or seeks custody after the sentence has legally ended.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Affirm. A person has a serious liberty interest while lawfully living in the community, but a mistaken executive release does not extinguish a concededly valid uncompleted sentence or create a fundamental right to retain liberty obtained through the error. Correcting the mistake after roughly twenty months, without evidence of bad-faith inducement, manipulation, or an effort to evade a completed sentence, does not shock the conscience. Lewis makes the State’s conduct and context decisive. On the supplied account there is no supported manipulation or bad-faith inducement making correction conscience-shocking; Harper nevertheless prevents treating that substantive conclusion as resolution of every procedural claim concerning retaking.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide state-law credit, estoppel, compensation, procedural defects in the retaking, a much longer state-caused delay, or deliberate manipulation of release and reincarceration.
