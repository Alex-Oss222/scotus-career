## 1. Arizona v. California, No. 8, Original

### Provisional component disposition and sufficient grounds

Original action; exceptions to the Master's report; argument April 25, 2000. There is no appellate judgment to affirm or reverse. All eight Associates would reach the presented exceptions and settlement recommendations. They would:

1. Reject the asserted Arizona I/Arizona II categorical bar to further adjudication of the unresolved Fort Yuma boundary-water claim. The pertinent amended decree expressly accommodates adjustment when the five reservations' boundaries are finally determined by agreement or decree; Arizona II's distinction between omitted acreage within settled boundaries and unresolved boundaries prevents treating its finality discussion as a decision of this different claim. This conclusion does not make the Interior position binding, reopen every omitted water claim, or find that the States waived their defense.
2. Sustain the United States' and Tribe's exception to the Master's Docket 320 preclusion ruling. The compromise finally settles the parties' claims against one another within that docket, but it neither adjudicates the disputed boundary/title predicate nor clearly releases the distinct reserved-water claim against competing users. Claim preclusion and issue preclusion each fail at the asserted scope for their own reasons. The no-admission clause confirms the absence of an intended title determination; it does not deprive the $15 million judgment or its reciprocal releases of finality.
3. Reject section 22 of the Indian Claims Commission Act as a separate conclusive bar on the demonstrated record. Subsection (a)'s payment discharge and subsection (b)'s adverse-determination bar concern claims against the United States. Payment of this compromise, which resolved alternative theories without selecting an extinguishment premise, does not by itself establish that the boundary lands ceased to be reserved or that third-party reserved-water claims were discharged. No general rule about every taking award, aboriginal-title claim or compensation judgment is adopted.
4. Approve the Fort Mojave settlement on the supported recommended terms: specified Hay and Wood Reserve boundary; additional diversion limited to the lesser of 3,022 acre-feet or the water sufficient for 468 acres; no further reserved Colorado River water claims by the United States and Tribe for the Hay and Wood Reserve lands; preservation of the stated riverbed title/jurisdiction and private-land issues.
5. Approve the Colorado River Reservation settlement on the supported recommended terms: the lesser of 2,100 acre-feet or the water sufficient to irrigate 315 acres; no further reserved Colorado River water claims by the United States or Tribes for reservation lands in California; no adjudication of the disputed boundary in this proceeding; preservation of competing riverbed-title/jurisdiction claims; effectiveness upon the Master's and Court's approval. The Master's recommendation supplies the presented approval posture; no additional unrecited condition is supplied.

The Fort Yuma boundary, title, practicably irrigable acreage and resulting water quantity return to the Master for further proceedings under the continuing original jurisdiction. Approval of the two settlements implements only their supported terms and does not award Fort Yuma water. The precise implementing decree must preserve the two settlements' different boundary treatment and future-claim limitations.

### Enhanced comparison and source limits

**Earlier-original-action fork.** The States' strongest path invokes stability of the allocation and the United States' earlier possession of relevant boundary facts; finality cannot be evaded simply by changing an administrative legal opinion. The contrary path distinguishes a claim actually settled or omitted within accepted boundaries from a disputed boundary whose later determination the amended decree expressly contemplates. The latter is the sufficient ground accepted here. The States' 1979 stipulation, their earlier requests for boundary adjudication and the timing of their 1989 defense provide context, but none substitutes for construction of the decrees. No Justice's proposed result depends on determining forfeiture, a preserved objection to lateness, consent to a waiver exception or the unproduced 1989 responses.

**Compensation-judgment fork.** Final compromise of alternative land-loss theories might foreclose another demand within the same released claim, and consent judgments cannot be denied effect merely because their factual basis was never tried. But these reciprocal releases are confined to claims between the Tribe and United States in Docket 320; neither claim identity nor an actual or intentionally binding determination of title follows from the same land appearing in both proceedings. International Building distinguishes a compromise's finality from unproved issue determination. Matsushita and Baker support faithful reading of actual judgment effect, while their section 1738 holdings are analogies, not governing federal-consent-judgment statutes. Richards supplies the requirement of a lawful binding relationship, not a blanket nonparty exemption. Mendoza's protection against nonmutual estoppel of the United States is consistent with the result but need not be an additional holding once the necessary title determination is absent.

**Statutory fork.** The States' strongest statutory argument is that a paid Indian land claim may settle the underlying property interest as well as the damages demand. That argument requires identification of what interest the judgment paid for or conclusively determined. Here the compromise preserves alternative grounds and disclaims admission; section 22 does not independently provide the missing determination or an express release of every person's competing claim. The narrow result leaves the legal effect of a judgment actually determining a taking or extinguishment for a record presenting it.

**Settlement fork.** The Court must independently approve equitable original-action settlements rather than treating absence of exceptions as consent to every imaginable term. The Master recommends both agreements, the supported terms identify bounded exchanges, and no supported inequity or unrecited condition is necessary to approval. That supports approval without further research into irrelevant execution details. If an implementing order would depend on a term beyond the neutral recitations, the full instrument is required first. Missing source files do not establish litigant default.

## 2. Crosby v. National Foreign Trade Council, No. 99-474

### Provisional disposition and operative rationale

Argument March 22, 2000. All eight Associates would affirm the First Circuit's judgment on the granted federal-preemption ground and leave the injunction against enforcement of the challenged Burma procurement restrictions in place. Affirmance on a sufficient statutory ground requires no adjudication of the lower court's independent dormant-foreign-affairs or Foreign Commerce Clause theories; those theories are not adopted as Supreme Court holdings and no vacatur of their judgment consequence is necessary.

The Massachusetts Burma restrictions conflict with section 570's enacted arrangement for calibrated national sanctions and a coordinated multilateral strategy. Congress selected covered United States persons and defined new investment; it required sanctions upon particular findings, specified the sanctions' removal and waiver conditions, and charged the President with developing the multilateral strategy. Massachusetts imposes a separate procurement disability for a substantially broader class of Burmese dealings, including foreign firms and ordinary trading relationships, with no corresponding mechanism responsive to the federal statutory determinations. That combination obstructs the concrete federal method, even though obedience to both laws is physically possible and both governments oppose Burmese repression.

The holding does not treat section 570(e) as an express delegation to the President to waive state laws. It is the Supremacy Clause consequence of the state sanctions' demonstrated conflict with the enacted national scheme. Nor does federal permission for a business transaction alone create immunity from every state purchasing choice. The operative combination of calibrated coverage, conditional sanctions, waiver and coordinated strategy supplies the federal premise missing in a case of mere silence or overlapping objectives.

### Material statutory qualifications retained

Section 570(a)'s endpoint requires presidential determination and certification of measurable and substantial human-rights and democratic-government progress. Its bilateral-assistance exceptions preserve humanitarian and human-rights/democracy assistance and separately conditioned counter-narcotics/crop-substitution assistance. Treasury's institutional-voting command is distinct from the visa provision's use of “should,” which retains treaty and mission-staffing exceptions. Section 570(b)'s new-investment prohibition is mandatory upon the specified post-enactment harm, politically motivated rearrest or exile of Daw Aung San Suu Kyi, or large-scale repression/violence finding and certification. Section 570(c) requires the coordinated comprehensive strategy, and (d) the specified six-month reports. Subsection (e) permits temporary or permanent waiver of (a)/(b) sanctions upon determination and certification of conflict with national-security interests. Subsection (f) defines covered new investment by the certification date and the identified development/ownership/profit arrangements while excluding contracts to buy or sell goods, services or technology.

Massachusetts's restricted-list scheme is a procurement restriction, not a general legal ban on private trade. Section 22H(b)'s written certification requires both essential procurement, as separately defined, and loss of the only bid or inadequate competition. Section 22H(d) permits a restricted award only when no unrestricted comparable low bid exists; “comparable” retains the responsive/responsible requirement and ten-percent boundary. The reported ten-percent administrative addition is not statutory wording. Solely-news and solely-international-telecommunications operations retain their separate exemption. Section 22I's medical-purchase permission includes the life-preserving/disease-related class or the no-substitute alternative; no-substitute necessity is not a universal prerequisite. Professional standards and written certification/notice retain their stated operation. Its separate supplier exemption covers only the defined medical dealings and excludes mixed medical/nonmedical dealings. These qualifications reduce the state disability in particular procurements but do not align its operative scope or termination with the federal sanctions arrangement.

### Enhanced lawful-path comparison

**Preservation path.** Boston Harbor protects real proprietary purchasing, Congress enacted no express preemption clause, and federal choice of limited sanctions does not normally prohibit a State from declining a contract on a broader basis. Barclays refuses to treat foreign complaints, Executive preference or congressional silence as law. The strongest objection to displacement is consequently that the asserted federal ceiling is inferred rather than enacted.

**Displacement path.** Boston Harbor itself reserves actual congressional displacement and concerns a project-specific proprietary objective, while this restriction conditions unrelated state purchases on external Burma dealings. Geier's unanimous method requires the particular state duty and affirmative federal means; its divided application supplies no automatic outcome. Here Congress's own linked conditions, coverage, national-security waiver and multilateral assignment establish the method. The state economic penalty continues across the very conduct and participants over which Congress deliberately structures leverage and negotiation. The problem is the maintained parallel sanction, not simply moral disagreement or extra cost. Myrick's regulatory absence is absent here. Barclays involved no binding sanctions arrangement prohibiting the asserted state method. The alternative therefore fails at the level of this statute and restriction, without a broad foreign-affairs field holding.

Executive Order 13047 implements the statutory new-investment authority. Executive litigation assessments and foreign objections may illuminate the practical conflict but are neither independent sources of preemptive law nor necessary substitutes for the enacted comparison. Neither congressional awareness nor failure expressly to mention Massachusetts decides intent. No general rule is announced about local procurement, divestment, ordinary contract eligibility or every state measure with foreign effects.

## 3. Hawkins v. Freeman, 195 F.3d 732

### Provisional disposition and sufficient common conclusion

Argument May 1, 2000 following the approved March 6 grant. All eight Associates would affirm the en banc Fourth Circuit's judgment denying habeas relief on the presented substantive-due-process claim. The 1995 application receives the pre-AEDPA merits treatment appropriate to its filing; amended section 2254(d) supplies no additional merits gate.

On this record, the Constitution does not require permanent release from an unchanged valid sentence because the State mistakenly granted unauthorized parole, then corrected that eligibility error after approximately twenty months of conditional community life. The actual liberty and reliance are substantial and cannot be dismissed as fictitious because the release was mistaken. But the valid fifty-year sentence, the earlier correct eligibility notice, continuing parole supervision, correction directed to enforcement of the lawful sentence, opportunity to present the favorable adjustment material, and credit for the period at liberty defeat the asserted substantive prohibition here. Neither successful adjustment nor official negligence supplies a constitutional discharge of the unserved sentence.

This conclusion does not rest on a finding that state law required revocation; the lower court reserved that matter. It does not require proof of a malicious purpose in every deliberative executive case, apply Lewis's rapid-pursuit standard, adopt a history-only method for all liberty, or resolve whether a materially more extreme official abandonment, irrevocable representation, prolonged lapse or interruption of a completed sentence could present a different constitutional case. The judgment does not recognize such an exception, create an estoppel test or prescribe a new balancing procedure; those materially different situations remain outside the decision.

### Record and doctrinal distinctions controlling the commitment

Young and Morrissey make the actual community liberty legally important and require appropriate procedure for its withdrawal. They do not erase legal custody or make unauthorized parole substantively irrevocable. Hawkins waived the preliminary inquiry and received a counseled formal hearing, with time to submit favorable evidence; no independent procedural claim is decided. No sentence increase or withdrawal of time credit is established. Community-service and financial arrears occurred, but revocation rested solely on the eligibility error; those arrears cannot be substituted as a new lawful cause or portrayed as a judicial rejection of the rehabilitation evidence.

Glucksberg rejects the particular assisted-suicide entitlement and reserves other particularized claims; it does not supply the lower court's universal methodology. Lewis's constitutional holding addresses instant pursuit decisions, expressly leaving deliberative settings open. Saenz distinguishes affirmative interference, the interest involved, culpability and causal connection; its damages immunity questions do not apply to this habeas remedy. Daniels excludes mere negligence as a constitutional injury but does not dispose of the separate deliberate decision to return Hawkins to custody. That decision is examined in light of the actual unchanged lawful sentence and reliance record.

### Enhanced lawful-path comparison

**State path.** Correction enforces a valid uncompleted sentence and uniform statutory parole eligibility; an administrative mistake and favorable personal adjustment do not themselves authorize federal judges to substitute an early-release date. The strongest objection is that the State deliberately revoked actual liberty created through formal investigation and supervision, rather than merely miscalculating a date on paper.

**Prisoner path.** Officially induced liberty, genuine reintegration and reliance can be invoked as fundamental-fairness limits on executive return to confinement. The strongest objection is that this applicant remained on conditional parole, had earlier been correctly informed of 2018 eligibility, received process and release credit, and never obtained a new lawful sentence or adjudication ending the old one. The sources support the importance of reliance without establishing that these circumstances extinguish the valid sentence. DeWitt's judicial sentence-reduction/reinstatement problem and the lower-court installment-service/estoppel cases cannot be converted into Supreme Court rules or identical facts.

**Remand path.** A remand could be necessary if the controlling rule depended on unexamined state-law discretion, a promise of finality, an uncredited interval, concealed motive or an actually disputed factual predicate. The bounded rejection here depends on none of those absent facts. The courts below received discovery, the summary-judgment record and the favorable adjustment evidence. Correcting their overbroad references to Glucksberg and Lewis therefore does not itself require another evidentiary hearing. Affirmance leaves no claim for damages, permanent release, sentence reduction, state-law credit or a new procedural claim pending by implication.

## 4. Miller v. French / United States v. French, Nos. 99-224 and 99-582

### Governing premises and live components

Both dockets concern the Seventh Circuit's judgment sustaining the injunction against the automatic stay; argument April 18, 2000. All eight Associates reach statutory construction before the constitutional question and distinguish Indiana's mandatory construction from the United States' retained-equity position and alternative constitutional defense.

The operative Standards entries are **Prison-conditions prospective relief** (current amended federal-requirement condition effective November 26, 1997), **Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays** (statutory amendment November 26, 1997; current-evidence and judicial-duty boundaries effective Benjamin, May 7, 1998), and **Present legal change and prospective relief from an adjudicated injunction** (Agostini, June 23, 1997). Plyler, Rouse, Benjamin, Gavin and Hadix preserve independent adjudication of the termination predicate and each retained obligation. They expressly leave the automatic-stay issue open. Morgan Stanley and Plaut permit their defined securities-dismissal reopening; neither their majority nor the dissenters' completed-judgment objection automatically decides interim prospective relief.

The stay starts on day thirty for the presented subsection (b)(2) motion, subject to good-cause postponement for no more than sixty days; general calendar congestion is excluded. The statute separately requires prompt ruling, supplies mandamus and makes a blocking order appealable under section 1292(a)(1), except the stated postponement. The retained other-law 180-day clause is not broadly construed here. The stay ends with the final order deciding the motion. Subsection (b)(3)'s current **and** ongoing violation, necessity, narrowness and least-intrusion findings remain for the court; the absence of original ritual words does not decide substantive sufficiency. The old permanent decree does not become a new preliminary injunction under subsection (a)(2).

### Provisional component positions

**Mandatory-construction group — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.** The combined text displaces an ordinary equitable power to stop this automatic stay beyond the express postponement. The mandatory operation, specific bounded postponement and exclusion of calendar congestion would lose their intended effect if a court could continue the same decree through ordinary injunction balancing. The appeal provision describes how a purported blocking order is reviewed; it does not itself grant the substantive power to enter it. Traditional equitable authority and avoidance do not justify a contrary reading of this detailed scheme.

These six would reject the Seventh Circuit's categorical Article III invalidation and reverse its judgment in both dockets, with remand for the actual statutory adjudication and any properly supported distinct constitutional claim. The stay adjusts the interim prospective effect of a decree under newly enacted remedial law; it does not command false factual findings, adjudicate current prison compliance, erase accrued relief or direct the outcome of the termination motion under unchanged law. Pendleton's decree followed adjudication in material part, but that does not make all future operation immune from valid changed law.

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas** would adopt the mandatory-valid construction at the presented facial structural scope. They would expressly leave an adequately supported due-process or other application challenge open, without announcing that inability to finish by a particular date has already been established or automatically creates an exception. The present categorical injunction cannot stand on the Seventh Circuit's reasons.

**Souter and Ginsburg** also reject the categorical invalidation, but require a fuller reservation: statutory expedition cannot constitutionally eliminate a real opportunity to apply the new law to the material current evidence. This record does not demonstrate that the statutory interval necessarily prevents that task. They would reverse the categorical ruling and allow a genuinely supported application objection to be presented below under ordinary procedure, without requiring an inquiry merely because time passed or continuing the existing injunction automatically. They do not authorize ordinary equity to override the statute, hold the timetable universally sufficient, or determine the legal source and remedy of a hypothetical proven denial in advance.

**Retained-equity group — Stevens and Breyer.** They would construe the statute to preserve traditional equitable power in an exceptional case supported by the ordinary demanding prerequisites of injunctive relief, including likelihood of success and irreparable injury. The ordinary automatic stay and limited good-cause postponement have independent work: the latter can postpone operation without the distinct injunction showing; the express appeal treatment can review an exceptional injunction rather than serving only to identify prohibited orders. On that construction, no facial Article III invalidation is necessary. They would vacate the Seventh Circuit's judgment and remand for consideration of the district court's preservation order under the retained equitable authority and the actual amended termination standards. They do not find the injunction prerequisites proved, authorize indefinite continuation or adjudicate prison compliance. Pending remand, they would permit the district court to consider appropriately supported temporary protection rather than command automatic dissolution on the premise that equitable authority is absent.

### Enhanced adversarial comparison

**Construction.** The Government's strongest argument is that Congress normally leaves equity intact unless it clearly withdraws it and that an appeal provision expressly contemplating a blocking order makes an absolute prohibition less natural. The mandatory group's strongest answer is the particular text: a motion shall operate as a stay, the only expressly permitted postponement is tightly limited, and the appeal clause distinguishes that postponement rather than authorizing another method of extension. The retained-equity group answers that good cause and extraordinary injunction prerequisites are not interchangeable, so giving each a field does not nullify either. ## 5. Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe, No. 99-62

### Provisional disposition and proposition commitments

Argument March 29, 2000. All eight Associates reach the football-policy challenge; they do not reopen graduation, the damages denial or later incidents outside the closed record.

**Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer** would affirm the Fifth Circuit's judgment against the challenged football policy, including its nonsectarian/nonproselytizing backup. The six accept this narrow governmental-mechanism proposition: the board cannot organize an official pregame invocation opportunity through its own selective representative election and retained event-purpose constraints, then transfer the constitutional choice of whether the event includes religious exercise to a student majority. This is a policy for a representative component of the school's own event, not neutral access for independently selected private speakers. Student composition, absence of a school-written prayer and the option to select no invocation do not cure the governmental delegation embedded in the challenged mechanism. A policy can be invalid at that operative scope without presuming the words of an undelivered October prayer or holding that every possible secular student message is unconstitutional.

Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the official-organization ground independently of a separate coercion finding. Kennedy supports the identified official representative delegation as applied to the secondary-school setting under his full Agostini join, while insisting that the opinion not equate every private religious statement at an official event with governmental worship. His earlier Lee/Ingebretsen pressure positions and Tanford dissent remain relevant limitations, not categorical permission for this school-organized majoritarian mechanism. He would not make an additional independent pressure holding on asserted attendance obligations that the packet does not establish as findings. The other five also need no independent pressure holding to affirm; the actual policy structure supplies the sufficient ground.

**Scalia and Thomas** would reverse the facial invalidation of the unrestricted October policy and remand for bounded adjudication of any actual official-adoption or unconstitutional-pressure application. They accept the governing prohibition on governmental worship under Agostini, and Tanford's authoritative distinction between actual official devotion and private access. But this policy permits a secular message or no message and leaves the selected volunteer the content choice. Unlike Tanford, no actual October devotional message is supplied. They find the conversion from permitted student choice to official worship insufficiently established for facial invalidation of the unrestricted version. This is a conclusion about the demonstrated mechanism and remedy, not a finding that every potential application is lawful or that school direction cannot be inferred without a written script.

For the **backup policy**, Scalia and Thomas would not require the school to censor private expression into judicially acceptable nonsectarian/nonproselytizing form. They would vacate the football disposition as to that alternative with the unrestricted-policy remand, allowing the lower courts to determine the lawful reach and severability of any needed relief under the proper official/private distinction. They do not supply a separate vote to reinstate the district court's compulsory religious-content screen or decide that all aspects of the backup are independently valid. The backup never becomes operative if the unrestricted policy is not enjoined; no independent continuing application is presumed.

### Governing framework and remedy limits

Agostini's modified **Lemon test** governs: purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence in the governmental-act/function inquiry, not separate surviving prongs. Lee's official-organization rule and Ingebretsen's rejection of student-delivery and disclaimer immunity govern supported school worship. Tanford extends the official-worship rule to its actual university devotional program; its full official-attribution holding was unanimous even though the permissibility question divided the Court. Bronx Household, Pinette, Chabad, Lamb's Chapel and Rosenberger preserve genuinely private access on their actual terms. Strout's financed-function analysis illustrates attention to what the government does; it supplies neither a football rule nor a basis to treat every private decision as governmental.

The October policy allows an invocation and/or message and does not expressly command prayer. The student council conducts the election on the principal's advice and direction, the majority selects whether the event includes the statement/invocation and selects a single volunteer, and the content must conform to the board's stated purposes. Those terms are distinct from the disputed continuing effect of interim no-preapproval instructions. The affirming group would reach the same governmental-mechanism conclusion even assuming no official textual preapproval; it makes no finding that an official reviewed or dictated a particular October message. The earlier chaplain practice and August elections corroborate the setting but are not silently treated as an October invocation or as dispositive proof of unchanging motive.

The affirming group would preserve the judgment only against this official representative invocation mechanism and its religiously screened backup. Genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student religious expression, lawful neutral access and nondevotional ceremonial remarks remain protected. The Court orders no new forum, prescribed substitute message, school-event redesign, damages or adjudication of graduation. The dissents' remand likewise creates no entitlement to a particular microphone slot or automatic permission for actual official worship.

### Enhanced lawful-path comparison

**Private-expression path.** A secular purpose, student content choice, optional attendance and absence of school preapproval support genuinely private messages; viewpoint suppression can itself violate the Constitution. A facial disposition should not rest on invented future words. Scalia and Thomas find that objection decisive for the unrestricted October policy, consistently with their Ingebretsen objection to predicted implementation.

**Governmental-mechanism path.** The school selected the event component, its solemnizing function, the representative election and policy constraints; it did not open a generally available student forum. Majority selection is the government-prescribed means of deciding the religious character of the school's own representative ceremony. Lee and Ingebretsen forbid insulating that act by delegation. The six affirming Associates find the defect in the existing structure, not in an imagined later student's message; the option not to exercise a government-created religious authority does not transform that authority into private access.

**Pressure path.** Lee independently protects students from substantial practical religious pressure, and Kennedy has expressly embraced that protection without law-backed punishment. Football is not automatically graduation; the packet distinguishes asserted participation obligations from established policy terms. No Justice's affirmative vote here is conditioned on a newly found compulsory-attendance fact, quantified pressure, an October prayer text or a claim that mere offense is coercion. If an independent pressure ground were proposed, its record predicates would need fresh neutral validation rather than being inferred for coalition purposes.

**Remand versus affirmance.** The disputed legal effect of interim directions could matter if actual editorial supervision were essential. It is not essential to the six Associates' representative-mechanism holding. The dissenters give the independent-choice possibility greater force and would require an actual application or further necessary determination before restraining the unrestricted policy. Neither side can revive separate Lemon prongs, declare a universal all-applications facial test or treat an archive gap as proof of a constitutional fact.
