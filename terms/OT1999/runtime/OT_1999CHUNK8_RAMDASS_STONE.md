## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Did Due Process require parole-ineligibility information

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reverse and require a new penalty proceeding. Simulated *Simmons* and *Allridge* ordinarily require legally fixed parole ineligibility and reject speculation about future parole-board decisions, but this case presents a different defect: the State used an already returned guilty verdict to establish dangerousness while withholding the virtually certain legal consequence of that same verdict. Under an express doctrinal change, Stone would modify the trigger because formal-entry timing otherwise lets the State create a materially false sentencing premise; the change does not reach verdicts facing a genuine unresolved merits issue.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When the prosecution relies on an outstanding guilty verdict to prove future dangerousness and that verdict will make the defendant parole-ineligible upon entry of judgment absent a genuine unresolved substantive defect, due process requires accurate parole information.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reverse and require a new penalty proceeding. * **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The rule does not require predictions about discretionary parole, treat every unentered verdict as final, or apply when substantive postverdict alteration is genuinely in dispute; the conviction remains untouched.
