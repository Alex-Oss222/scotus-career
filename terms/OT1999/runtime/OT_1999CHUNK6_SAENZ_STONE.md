## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: An official can cause a deprivation without becoming a general guarantor of safety

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the presented legal possibility and its limits. The factual truth of the alleged order and the existence of an independently sufficient ground remain matters for the actual summary-judgment record.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Preserve DeShaney’s no-general-duty rule and Pinder’s distinction between nonprotection and an attributable affirmative deprivation. Applying that distinction to interference with another officer’s response is a proposed application, not a claim that Pinder already established liability on these facts.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An affirmative command that actually interrupts an available protective response and thereby creates or materially increases exposure to a known danger can present an official-deprivation claim. Issuing an instruction not to investigate, without that effect and the requisite culpability, remains insufficient.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Constitution does not require the police to arrest every dangerous person or guarantee the safety of everyone on a road. But an official cannot answer an allegation that he affirmatively disabled an available response simply by observing that the eventual collision involved a private driver. The causal question concerns what his intervention changed. If the other officer would not have acted, or the direction merely describes a discretionary decision not to supply protection, the affirmative wording adds nothing. If a command actually stopped a protective intervention otherwise underway or available, the analysis must examine that attributable increase in danger. Pinder keeps those categories distinct; it does not establish that either causal account is true here. The victims’ being unidentified at the moment of intervention also does not alone defeat an allegation that the official exposed road users to a specific known danger. That proposition supplies no liability to every foreseeable victim: causation and the constitutional culpability requirement still do separate work. Lewis requires attention to the time and circumstances in which the official acted. A considered interference is not automatically governed by the emergency-pursuit intent rule, while negligence or a mistaken enforcement choice does not become conscience shocking because the resulting injury is grave.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No duty to investigate or detain based only on knowledge, no constitutionalization of ordinary traffic negligence, and no finding that the alleged instruction in fact meets the test. No new damages cause of action beyond §1983 is created.

### Issue 2: Recognizing a possible claim does not settle immunity or responsibility

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Address immunity and each defendant only at the scope the supplied record supports. The incident date is not provided.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Use Pinder’s conduct-time notice inquiry and Lanier’s fair-warning rule. Later recognition of a constitutional application cannot itself establish what the officer should have understood earlier.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The proposed legal ruling requires a separate determination of whether the particular conduct’s unlawfulness was apparent when it occurred, and a separate lawful basis for liability of every institutional or private defendant.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Pinder itself affirmed immunity while leaving the affirmative-deprivation merits open. That result prevents treating the present legal distinction as an automatic denial of immunity. It also prevents using immunity as a reason to rewrite DeShaney into categorical permission for official interference. The court must examine all relevant then-existing law at the appropriate level of specificity, without demanding an identical collision or relying on a later case to create notice. The corporate defendant’s inclusion in the caption supplies neither state action nor personal participation. A municipal claim, if properly presented, requires its own institutional connection and does not follow merely from employing an officer.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No incident date, governing contemporaneous authority, municipal policy, joint participation or damages amount is invented. Unrelated state tort claims remain governed by their own elements.
