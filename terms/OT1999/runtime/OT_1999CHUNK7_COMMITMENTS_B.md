## Castillo v. United States — June 5, 2000 — No. 99-658

### Reach, components and sufficient rule

All eight reach the granted construction of the offense-date, pre-1998 §924(c)(1). All reverse the Fifth Circuit's sentencing-factor classification and remand. The firearm types defining the increased statutory grades are offense elements: they must be charged in the federal indictment and, absent a valid element admission or jury waiver, proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge's weapon finding alone cannot establish that element. The existing thirty-year terms cannot be sustained merely on the circuit's sentencing-factor rationale.

The statute describes the particular firearm used or carried as part of the aggravating offense in the same connected provision and makes the selected category produce a dramatically different punishment. Machinegun, destructive-device and silencer characteristics describe the weapon employed, rather than a generic appraisal of offender conduct within an authorized range. Gaudin supplies the consequences after element classification. The simulated Almendarez-Torres and March 1999 Jones decisions reinforce ordinary indictment and verdict limits without establishing a universal maximum-fact rule. The former statute's construction suffices; neither the constitutionality nor interpretation of the reorganized 1998 provision is decided.

### Alternative paths and operative limits

The strongest sentencing-factor path treats the initial use/carry language as a completed offense and the remaining grades as sentencing directions. The sentencing setting and penalty phrasing are real textual considerations. They do not overcome the connected weapon descriptions, the resulting distinct grades and the absence of an established tradition treating this substantive weapon character as a mere sentencing circumstance. McMillan concerns a minimum within an unchanged statutory maximum and does not classify this statute. Witte and Watts preserve judicial findings within conviction-based authority, not authority to supply an omitted aggravated offense.

The contrary argument to an element construction is that it reads too much into penalty magnitude and constitutional concern. The accepted ground therefore does not use magnitude alone, a generic presumption that every sentencing fact is an element, or a constitutional holding left unresolved in the entering law. Scalia's join does not depend on legislative history. Nothing in the record supports declaring that the indictment omitted the aggravator. The ordinary remand must determine the actual charge, verdict, preservation and any legally available error analysis; it orders no fixed replacement term, automatic retrial, acquittal or disposition of unrelated counts. Neder's qualified actual-verdict rule concerns the identified materiality errors and does not mechanically decide this different error.

## Mixon v. Ohio — June 5, 2000 — lower No. 98-3368

### Components and record boundary

The published district and circuit recitations establish an actually pleaded racial-impact voting claim resolved on categorical appointment noncoverage. They do not establish liability or a distinct preserved constitutional purpose count. The Court can review that categorical statutory ground without possessing the complete complaint. All eight preserve Ohio's immunity from the direct constitutional and state-law claims; the mayor's municipal status supplies no state immunity. The §2 claim against Ohio retains the entering Fifteenth Amendment enforcement basis. No §5 preclearance issue or private §1971 action question is decided.

The statute creates a genuine appointment scheme: the mayor chooses nine members from the nomination panel's list, with district-residency, outside-Cleveland representation and expertise requirements. Election of the appointing mayor is not election of board members. The later districtwide referendum and possible restored election must be acknowledged; their timing neither erases the challenged intervening withdrawal nor establishes that later events occurred.

**Coverage path:** A direct replacement of an existing election by appointment is a practice affecting voting and may be tested under §2 for unequal racial political opportunity. Holder rejects categorical exemption for structural choices and permits an independently grounded practical comparator. The former lawful board election supplies an available institutional reference here; it does not establish racial inequality, freeze that system permanently, or require elections for all offices. Plaintiffs must establish unequal racial opportunity caused by the withdrawal under the statutory totality. Universal loss of a vote proves no such inequality by itself. Valdespino's dependable-crossover rule belongs to its district-capacity setting and supplies neither automatic proof nor a formula for withdrawal claims.

**Noncoverage path:** Section 2 governs equal participation where the State elects an office; genuinely appointing an office removes no racial class alone from an existing electorate. Chisom's distinction between elected and appointed judges supports leaving this institutional choice to the State absent an independent constitutional wrong. Holder concerns the size of an elected body and does not compel extending a results claim to abandonment of elections. The strongest objection is that this permits a State to evade voting protection through the very replacement challenged. The response is that ordinary appointment is not vote dilution and purposeful discrimination remains prohibited; a nominal appointment that is actually an electoral device presents a different case.

The strongest objection to the coverage path is a perpetual-election command and an unconstrained comparison between unlike institutions. It is answered by confining coverage to this direct restructuring, accepting the actual former system only as a rooted comparator, and retaining proof of racial inequality, causation, totality and genuine governmental interests before liability or remedy. Presley reserved effective replacement and did not declare all transfers immune. It is a §5 case and supplies no automatic §2 liability.

## Sims v. Apfel — June 5, 2000 — No. 98-9537

### Reach and distinct paths

All eight agree that Sims completed the prescribed administrative route and obtained a final agency decision; omission of particular objections from her Council request is not itself a statutory jurisdictional defect. The question is whether a court should nevertheless impose issue forfeiture at that stage. The merits of disability and of the two unreviewed objections are outside this decision; the selective-record issue already rejected on its merits is not reopened by correction of the other issues' exhaustion treatment.

**Path A — Council-stage structure:** No judicially created issue-exhaustion rule applies to a claimant who seeks Appeals Council review under this scheme. Congress and the regulations impose no such requirement. The Council's responsibility extends to the entire benefits claim; its plenary review, optional briefing, informal nonadversarial procedure and short request form differ materially from adversarial appellate litigation. McCarthy makes actual institutional design relevant where Congress has imposed no requirement. Darby supplies respect for enacted exhaustion choices but does not itself decide this non-APA issue-exhaustion question.

**Path B — actual notice and optional-presentation ground:** The existing rules and notices reasonably invite a short request and tell claimants that the whole decision is reviewable, without identifying particular objections as a condition of judicial review. It is inappropriate to apply an additional forfeiture rule to this claimant on those actual terms. This sufficient ground does not decide that every nonadversarial system precludes issue exhaustion or that every prudential exhaustion rule requires special notice.

**Path C — prudential issue exhaustion:** The Council can grant full relief and correct the actual errors only if given a fair opportunity to consider them. A represented claimant should identify the disputed objections even though no statute makes that requirement jurisdictional. The institutional interest is materially stronger than the damages-unavailable procedure in McCarthy. Informality does not erase a claimant's obligation to identify alleged error. This path would affirm the refusal to entertain the two omitted issues as prudential forfeiture, while rejecting its jurisdictional characterization.

The strongest objection to A is that whole-claim power does not ensure meaningful review of each latent error; the answer is the agency's own allocation of investigatory and review responsibility and optional presentation, rather than speculation that the Council actually found every issue. The strongest objection to B is that counsel knew review was sought and could have listed objections; its answer is that counsel's presence does not alter the scheme or the misleading consequence of converting permitted short requests into undisclosed forfeiture. The strongest objection to C is that it imports adversarial appellate preservation into a differently designed system. Its adherents distinguish cooperation in identifying error from full adversarial litigation and consider the capable Council the appropriate first reviewer. None treats a jurisdictional label as a substitute for statutory authority.

## Troxel v. Granville — June 5, 2000 — No. 99-138

### Authorized inputs, reach and operative distinctions

This section uses the [validated Troxel neutral packet](../freeze/OT_1999CHUNK7_NEUTRAL_TROXEL.md), its [exact canonical slice](../entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK7_TROXEL.md), and the [mechanically copied prior public positions](../entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK7_TROXEL_PRIOR_PUBLIC.md). The raw packet's generalization about Glucksberg is not used. That decision rejects the specified physician-assisted-suicide entitlement and adopts no exclusive history method for every liberty. No current Troxel historical Supreme Court opinion, reasoning or lineup was retrieved.

All eight reach the granted §1257 review. Only the Troxel/Granville visitation controversy is before the Court; Smith and Wolcott's separate disputes and collateral fee questions do not become new federal components. Washington's construction permitting this petition outside a pending custody action is a state-law premise. The federal disposition must distinguish the dismissal of this petition from the state court's broader constitutional reasoning.

The material order overrides Granville's proposed monthly daytime contact and family holidays with monthly overnight weekends, a summer week and birthday visits. The dispute is not complete exclusion of the grandparents. Neither parental unfitness nor concrete harm from the mother's proposal is established. The trial court invoked the benefits of grandparent contact, the absence of contrary harm, and its own favorable grandparent experience. Those reasons are the supported findings and explanations, not a finding that every parental-capacity concern has been adjudicated. The grandparents' existing relationship after their son's death is substantial context, but beneficial contact and constitutionally necessary intervention are different propositions.

### Competing legal and remedial paths

**A — particular parental-choice violation:** The liberty protected in Meyer, Pierce, Stanley and the surviving parental cases includes a fit parent's direction of the child's care and upbringing. Parham's parental-judgment principle supports a presumption that such a parent acts in the child's interests. A court may not displace that decision simply because it believes additional contact would be better; the parent's judgment must receive special weight. The actual order treated grandparent visitation as ordinarily beneficial unless contrary harm was shown, and did not give the mother's continuing-contact decision that protection. Affirm dismissal of this petition on this application, without adopting a universal parental-unfitness prerequisite, a universal harm threshold, strict scrutiny for every family decision, or invalidating every possible visitation statute.

The strongest counterargument is that limited visitation preserves important child relationships and does not transfer custody or terminate parenthood. That distinction limits the holding and leaves lawful intervention possible. It does not permit replacing an unchallenged parent's concrete proposal by an ordinary better-arrangement comparison. Baby Richard recognizes a bounded child interest in serious compulsory-transition harm, but expressly rejects using best interests to defeat parental custody and creates no permanent nonparent contact entitlement. A. St. P. C. protects against a different severe contact deprivation and supplies no universal visitation proof burden. Brown and Curtis denied school-program vetoes while preserving parental choice and custody; they do not make a visitation order mere school administration.

**B — statutory authorization as construed:** The extraordinary authorization for any person to seek visitation at any time on best interests, whether or not circumstances changed, gives the judge authority to replace parental judgment without a required substantive limiting principle. Its state-law construction supplies no protective limitation. Affirm dismissal because that unrestricted authorization is constitutionally inadequate, without deciding that harm is invariably the only sufficient justification. The strongest objection is that some applications could preserve indispensable existing relationships against concrete harm. Its adherent responds that those possible grounds cannot supply restrictions absent from the authority the State has actually construed, and that the Court cannot rewrite it. This is a broader statutory-scope objection than A, not an automatic consequence of its record application.

**C — reject categorical invalidation and remand:** Recognize parental liberty and a presumptive parental role while permitting a contextual inquiry into the child's independent interests, the actual relationship and the extent of intrusion. A universal harm prerequisite or facial invalidation goes beyond the cases; the state court should address the actual order under a correct standard. Vacate the federal constitutional dismissal and remand without reinstating the trial order. The strongest objection is that this record already demonstrates benefit substitution and continued offered contact, making another proceeding an unnecessary invasion. The response is that the state court used a broader constitutional premise and did not decide the individualized balance with the appropriate parental protection; the remand corrects that legal premise, not a presumption of new harm evidence. It requires no new trial or supplementation and guarantees no visits.

**D — no newly established substantive visitation veto:** Preserve the actual holdings protecting education, custody loss and parental procedure, but reject a newly inferred federal substantive rule governing this limited third-party visitation decision. The parents' important liberty and the State's family-law responsibilities do not themselves identify the judicially enforceable new constitutional boundary. Reverse the state constitutional dismissal and return for ordinary state disposition without adding a federal weighting or harm formula. Its strongest objection is that a forced visit directly affects parental care already protected by precedent; its adherent distinguishes the existing holdings' specific deprivations and refuses to infer all disputed child-rearing choices from them. This path does not overrule those holdings or assert that all parental liberty is beyond judicial enforcement.

## United States v. Hubbell — June 5, 2000 — No. 99-166

### Reach, rule and remedy

All eight reach the immunity question and affirm the D.C. Circuit's protective disposition at the scope stated below. The conditional agreement leaves a live controversy. The Independent Counsel authority ruling and other defendants' charges are outside review.

Identifying, locating and assembling the documents responsive to these eleven broad demands communicated the witness's knowledge of their existence, location and responsive character. This was testimonial production, not a disclosure whose informative aspects were already a foregone conclusion. Sections 6002–6003 and Kastigar bar both direct use of that compelled information and evidence derived from it. A promise not to place the production act or documents before the trial jury does not establish an independent source for the evidence used to investigate and indict.

Fisher separates voluntary contents from production testimony, and Doe (1984) permits compelled production only with the required immunity where the act is incriminating. The Government's proposed contents exception cannot erase the testimonial means by which it learned which documents existed and obtained investigative leads. Conversely, the rule does not privilege all preexisting contents or grant immunity for every transaction they describe. Genuine foregone conclusions, third-party production, collective-entity rules, and genuinely independent evidence retain their separate operation. Balsys concerns independent foreign risk and leaves domestic Kastigar protection intact.

The Government presently claims no wholly independent evidentiary source. That position is distinct from, and more consequential than, its earlier concession concerning reasonable particularity. Under the resulting legal rule and the conditional agreement, the indictment must be dismissed through ordinary mandate implementation. The Court does not give the prosecution a fresh independent-source search merely because the circuit earlier remanded. No transaction-wide immunity, outside-defendant disposition, broad contents privilege or revived authority ruling is entered.

### Alternatives and individual support

The competing limited-act-use path would prohibit evidentiary use of implicit existence, possession and authenticity admissions but allow investigative use of the produced contents. Its strongest justification is that the contents were voluntarily made and therefore unprivileged. Its decisive defect is causal: the Government acquired the previously unidentified contents by compelled testimonial selection, and derivative-use protection reaches that investigative use. The protected-production path's strongest objection is practical breadth; the independent-source and genuine-foregone-conclusion limits answer it without diluting the actual immunity.

## Harris Trust and Savings Bank v. Salomon Smith Barney Inc. — June 12, 2000 — No. 99-579

### Reach, sufficient rule and alternatives

All eight reach the certified categorical nonfiduciary-defendant question, reverse the Seventh Circuit's contrary judgment and remand. The existing §502(a)(3) equitable claim is not transformed into a new claim merely because plaintiffs articulate the fiduciary's prohibited transaction as the statutory violation being redressed. This decision resolves neither the disputed transaction predicates nor entitlement to the requested money.

Section 502(a)(3) defines the persons who may sue and the equitable relief available; it does not restrict defendants categorically to fiduciaries. When a fiduciary's prohibited, nonexempt transfer violates §406(a), appropriate traditional equitable relief may reach a nonfiduciary recipient with actual or constructive notice of the circumstances making the transfer unlawful. The statute's fiduciary duty and the identity of a person from whom equity may recover property are different questions. The recipient is not made a fiduciary by receiving property.

The strongest opposing path reads §406(a)'s fiduciary duty as implying that only the violator may be sued and invokes ERISA's careful remedial design. Its difficulty is the actual enforcement text: redress of a statutory violation may include traditional recovery from a knowing transferee even when that person did not bear the transferor's duty. Mertens expressly reserved the antecedent nonfiduciary-action issue while rejecting ordinary compensation, and therefore settles neither categorical eligibility nor every monetary equitable remedy. Varity confirms the distinct individual-equity channel without overriding its conditions; Peacock requires an actual statutory/plan violation, supplied here only as an assumed legal predicate, not a found fact.

The strongest objection to allowing suit is that every unsuccessful investment may be relabeled restitution. The answer is to retain the prohibited-transaction and exemption requirements, appropriate notice, bona fide purchaser protection, and the traditional equitable character of the actual requested remedy. Calling purchase-price compensation restitution does not establish equity. Party-in-interest status, PTE75-1, actual or constructive notice, the property/proceeds actually recoverable, and allocation of the purchaser-for-value-without-notice burden remain open. No loss-at-purchase finding, general damages action, strict recipient liability or mandatory award follows.
