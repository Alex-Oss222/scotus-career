## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Commerce Clause authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Congress documented serious national economic consequences, but § 13981 does not regulate direct interference with an interstate market, form an integral, reasonably adapted component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, or address a material interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably internalize. The chain through lost productivity, employment, spending, and medical costs is therefore insufficient under the controlling doctrine, although noneconomic conduct is not categorically beyond the commerce power in every setting. The judgment rests on § 5, addressed under Issue 2.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The economic consequences identified by Congress do not establish the direct market interference, necessary-inclusion relationship, or noninternalizable interstate externality required by the controlling commerce doctrine.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress documented serious national economic consequences, but § 13981 does not regulate direct interference with an interstate market, form an integral, reasonably adapted component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, or address a material interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably internalize. The chain through lost productivity, employment, spending, and medical costs is therefore insufficient under the controlling doctrine, although noneconomic conduct is not categorically beyond the commerce power in every setting. Unlike Hiley’s prohibited-recipient rule, this civil action does not protect an identified interstate supply restriction against evasion. Its employment and spending consequences repeat the chain Lopez found insufficient. The Commerce ground does not decide the judgment, which rests on § 5. Other VAWA provisions rest on their own text and powers.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Congress remains free to regulate commerce-linked offenses and fitted components of valid interstate schemes.
### Issue 2: Fourteenth Amendment Section 5 authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reverse. Section 5 permits Congress to create an independent civil remedy against private wrongdoers as a corrective substitute for discriminatory governmental denial of protection when (1) the constitutional injury involves discriminatory governmental denial of protection or of access to legal remedies; (2) Congress has a materially adequate basis to find that denial systematic; (3) the private conduct reached falls within the class of wrongs affected by that failure; (4) the substitute remedy meaningfully addresses the consequences of the deprivation; and (5) its reach is congruent and proportional. These are conditions of validity, not elements a plaintiff must prove. State action remains the constitutional injury; the private assault is not itself the violation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 5 sustains § 13981's civil action against the perpetrator of gender-motivated violence as a corrective substitute remedy for the systematic discriminatory denial of protection Congress documented in state justice systems. The remedy requires qualifying violence and gender animus, does not federalize all assaults and commandeers no state official. National scope is sustained as prophylaxis; it would fail if the record showed only isolated violations or if liability extended substantially beyond the affected class.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reverse. Congress had before it gender-bias task-force reports from at least 21 States and found that stereotypes in state justice systems produced insufficient investigation and prosecution, discounting of victims’ credibility and unduly lenient punishment for gender-motivated violence; that is discriminatory denial of equal protection by state actors, in a classification that demands an exceedingly persuasive justification. The gender-animus requirement confines liability to the class of wrongs those official failures affect, and a civil action against the perpetrator restores the redress the biased response withholds. Requiring each victim first to prove an official constitutional wrong would undermine the remedy, so national coverage is permissible prevention. Florida Prepaid’s undifferentiated patent liability reached every infringement without differentiating unconstitutional deprivation; this remedy answers a documented, systematic denial and reaches only animus-based violence. The Civil Rights Cases and Harris keep the state-action requirement; the statutes they held invalid lacked remedial fit, which this record supplies. Other VAWA provisions rest on their own text and powers.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The private assault is not itself a constitutional violation, and private parties do not become state actors. Section 5 does not authorize a general federal law of private violence, a remedy untethered to a documented, systematic official denial, or liability reaching substantially beyond the affected class. Florida Prepaid’s Section 5 holding remains intact. Congress also remains free to enact remedies directed at discriminatory States and state officials.
