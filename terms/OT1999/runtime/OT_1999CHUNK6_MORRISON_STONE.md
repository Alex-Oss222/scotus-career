## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Commerce Clause authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Congress documented serious national economic consequences, but § 13981 does not regulate direct interference with an interstate market, form an anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, or address a material interstate externality that individual States cannot reasonably internalize. The chain through lost productivity, employment, spending, and medical costs is therefore insufficient under the controlling doctrine, although noneconomic conduct is not categorically beyond the commerce power in every setting. Section 5 also fails because the remedy imposes liability on the private assailant rather than on the State or state official responsible for the constitutional equality violation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The economic consequences identified by Congress do not establish the direct market interference, anti-evasion relationship, or noninternalizable interstate externality required by the controlling commerce doctrine. Section 5 does not authorize a nationwide damages action against a private assailant untethered to unconstitutional state conduct.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Affirm. The chain through lost productivity, employment, spending, and medical costs is therefore insufficient under the controlling doctrine, although noneconomic conduct is not categorically beyond the commerce power in every setting. Section 5 also fails because the remedy imposes liability on the private assailant rather than on the State or state official responsible for the constitutional equality violation. Unlike Hiley’s prohibited-recipient rule, this civil action does not protect an identified interstate supply restriction against evasion. Its employment and spending consequences repeat the chain Lopez found insufficient. The §5 problem is independently the private assailant’s liability, not a denial that government discrimination is subject to federal enforcement. Other VAWA provisions rest on their own text and powers.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Congress remains free to regulate commerce-linked offenses and fitted components of valid interstate schemes, and to enact remedies directed at discriminatory States and state officials.
