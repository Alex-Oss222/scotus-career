## Material cross-doctrinal comparisons

**Carter and Apprendi.** The unanimously joined value rule is the closest new sentencing-related authority. It rests on the value language in the prohibited-conduct description, repetition in the two grades and differentiated penalties. It expressly applies Almendarez-Torres, Jones and Castillo as statutory analogues. Consequently O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer may accept Carter while retaining their frozen view that New Jersey may constitutionally allocate this particular present-offense consideration to sentencing. Thomas's new constitutional conclusion in the C freeze remains a conclusion for Apprendi, not a rule attributed to his earlier statutory joins. Scalia's agreement with the Carter majority about specific theft intent and movement does not retract his published Jones constitutional position. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg's Carter dissent follows their own statutory construction without adding a new general constitutional holding. No row needs to change.

Carter's charging discussion also does not authorize importing federal grand-jury rules into the state prosecution, treating a fact as admitted merely because an amount was alleged, or inferring waiver from a proposed instruction. Those are the same distinctions the C commitments already preserve. Carter's preserved remedial and grade-election questions do not establish a new jury proceeding, acquittal, mandatory resentencing sequence or harmlessness result for Apprendi.

**Miller and Dickerson.** The new statutory-stay holding is materially considered because it concerns legislative alteration of judicial protection. Its valid prospective-law premise and preservation of the court's actual adjudicative task are essential. It does not hold that Congress may prescribe a different constitutional meaning or eliminate a constitutionally required safeguard by calling the change procedural. Accordingly the six Associate commitments retaining Miranda remain compatible with accepting Miller's bounded rule; they can distinguish a valid adjustment to future injunction enforcement from the disputed voluntariness-only replacement. Scalia and Thomas's replacement position also remains bounded by constitutional compulsion, rather than expanded into a general power over constitutional safeguards. Stevens and Breyer's Miller dissent relies on the particular statute and traditional equitable authority; it creates no prerequisite that they employ the same avoidance construction for section 3501. No join or remedy revision is required.

**Other potential shortcuts rejected.** Reeves's civil whole-record sufficiency inquiry is not substituted for Neder's actual criminal-error scope. Hawkins's unchanged-sentence enforcement is not a higher-maximum authority. Arizona's civil forfeiture is not a waiver of an expressly reserved claim, and its judicial-initiative discussion does not reopen Dickerson's ungranted issue. Harris Trust and Pegram create no generally available civil remedy that could be assumed to replace warning safeguards. Santa Fe's functional attribution and preventive protection are confined to their First Amendment grounds, without creating a universal prophylaxis or actual-injury test. Crosby and Bradley do not convert congressional power in their particular statutory settings into authority to redefine the jury guarantee or the privilege.
