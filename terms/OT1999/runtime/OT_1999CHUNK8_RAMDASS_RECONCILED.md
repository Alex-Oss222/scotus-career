### The sufficient affirmance grounds

For Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, Simmons protects information about the legally available alternative when the jury chooses punishment. Domino's had a guilty verdict and recommendation but no entered judgment at that stage. Virginia's accepted definition therefore did not yet make it a qualifying conviction. Anticipated entry differs from clemency or legislation changing an existing disability. Rule 3A:15 preserves judicial authority after verdict without proving that any particular motion or defect existed. The prosecution could use the robbery conduct, prior trial and false denial as future-danger evidence without representing that an entered conviction already existed. That evidentiary use does not supply a missing strike.

The strongest competing argument is that the jury's choice concerned the sentence ultimately imposed after entry of the separate judgment, and that conditional truthful information would explain the actual capital process without falsely describing present legal status. Those three reject a constitutional duty to predict the separate proceeding's outcome as an application of Simmons's existing trigger. Later entry before capital judicial sentencing does not retrospectively change the jury-stage legal alternative. The ruling does not determine a distinct unpresented challenge to the capital judge's later proceedings.

All four affirming Associates independently conclude that the November 1994 state adjudication neither contradicted a governing Supreme Court holding nor applied it objectively unreasonably. Simmons's established legal-ineligibility premise, Gardner's opportunity to answer adverse sentencing information, Skipper's future-danger rebuttal and Ramos's latitude concerning truthful release information permit this distinction. Objective reasonableness is not established merely because another court agreed, or because the facts are not identical to an earlier case. Virginia's surviving power to set aside the separate verdict supplies a legally substantive difference from a foreordained remaining entry. This ground would remain sufficient even if a direct-review court could adopt a wider information rule. Neither Teague nor causal effect is necessary to affirmance.

O'Connor preserves a narrower federal qualification: if state law left only a purely ministerial, legally inevitable entry, the federal entitlement could not be defeated by that formality alone. Her affirmance does not estimate the probability that a motion would succeed. Under the established Rule 3A:15 power, the frozen facts do not establish the legal inevitability her qualification requires. She therefore regards the state distinction as objectively reasonable without settling every direct-review formulation. Mere high likelihood of entry, later actual entry, and absence of proof of a particular defect are not substituted for that legal premise.

### The bounded-return ground and its actual reach

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain frozen Path B. The complete-adjudication question cannot be answered solely by Virginia's formal definition of a qualifying conviction. A supported substantive adjudication with only entry remaining, coupled with the exact consequence at final capital sentencing, can differ from Allridge's forecast of a parole board's eventual discretionary action. These four require examination of that distinction under the effective-response protection. They do not find the distinction satisfied, require a false statement of existing January 29 ineligibility, or adopt a general prediction entitlement. The possible response would truthfully state the conditional consequence; its entitlement still requires supported predicates.

The strongest objection remains substantial: if Rule 3A:15 leaves a real adjudicative step, treating its outcome as certain would create the very prediction right Allridge excludes. The four's answer is limited. Existing power alone does not establish a pending substantive controversy, but neither does a verdict establish that every substantive step was completed. The federal classification must address the actual supported proceeding and the claimed response. If that record does not establish the completed-adjudication premise, this theory cannot yield a violation by assumption. No proof-producing order follows merely because the operator has not recovered a source.

The return reaches the **actual independent grounds** identified by the neutral supplement, not an invented reasonable-jurist formula. The appellate court treated practical rather than legal ineligibility as an extension outside clearly established law and separately barred by Teague. For these four, that classification fails to decide whether the supported claim concerns a completed adjudication and a fixed conditional consequence, or instead a forecast of a future substantive adjudication. Their Williams governing-rule position and the common statutory holdings require the first comparison; generality alone does not exclude Simmons. But a general principle of fair sentencing does not itself dictate every proposed instruction.

They would therefore vacate the adverse no-error, statutory-extension and Teague-extension determinations and return this claim for the properly bounded inquiries. **They do not find §2254(d)(1) satisfied.** The receiving court may deny relief on a supported independent application of either statutory clause even if it identifies a constitutional error. No universal unreasonable-refusal-to-extend rule is imported from historical Williams. O'Dell now defeats an automatic new-rule characterization of the confined protection; it does not make every practical-certainty proposal old. If the claim proves materially different, its own finality and exception inquiries remain. No later constitutional decision becomes a November 1994 holding.

All eight preserve the appellate rejection of the separate state-law-as-fact rationale. Virginia's meaning of conviction is a legal premise, not a §2254(d)(2) factual error merely because the District Court disagreed. The federal opportunity-to-answer question leaves that definition intact. Section 2254(a)'s unlawful-custody requirement remains distinct. No separate arbitrary-manipulation claim is silently adjudicated.
