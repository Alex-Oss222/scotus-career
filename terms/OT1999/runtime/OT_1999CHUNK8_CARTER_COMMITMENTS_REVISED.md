## Statutory classification and common propositions

All eight adopt Schmuck's binding elements comparison: the legally construed elements of the lesser crime must be a subset of those of the charged greater crime. Factual overlap alone does not suffice. A statutory inclusion ruling is separate from the requirement that the evidence permit a rational jury to convict of the lesser while acquitting of the greater.

All eight classify value exceeding **$1,000** as an element of subsection 2113(b)'s ten-year felony grade. Its placement in the conduct description, the repeated lower-grade provision, and the different maximum support that construction. Jones, Almendarez-Torres and Castillo supply statutory aggravated-offense analogues, not a universal constitutional maximum-fact rule. Once an aggravated element is identified, the ordinary indictment and jury requirements apply. Because the charged first-paragraph subsection (a) robbery has no value threshold, the ten-year larceny grade is not itself necessarily included under the statutory-elements comparison. The indictment's approximately $14,000 allegation and the trial's approximately $16,000 account do not add an element to the statutory robbery definition.

The requested charge's obsolete $100 threshold is not the governing statute and may not be repeated as current law. The historical instruction request is not rewritten. Nor do the Associates decide the case merely by faulting counsel's stale number: the legal inclusion and request-scope questions remain even if a correct instruction would use $1,000.

No Justice derives a bank-larceny holding from the simulated Mosley disposition. Scalia's actual Schmuck dissent addresses the mailing nexus, not the elements test; neither an objection nor assent to that test is inferred from the dissent's silence. Rule 31(c), not the distinct capital Beck/Hopkins doctrine, governs.

## Competing constructions of the basic offense

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas:** Both grades of subsection (b) require intent to steal or purloin and taking plus carrying away. The charged first paragraph of subsection (a) requires knowing conduct satisfying its forcible-or-intimidating taking elements, but not those additional requirements. The second paragraph's express purpose to commit a felony or larceny cannot be transplanted into the first. Morissette and Staples preserve appropriate criminal fault; they do not invariably require an additional theft purpose when the statute already identifies culpable knowing force or intimidation. Bell demonstrates that the bank statute is not simply an unaltered codification of common-law crime labels. These Justices therefore would affirm without deciding whether the lower-value language itself requires affirmative proof of an upper limit. The independent intent/carrying-away distinctions resolve either lower-grade construction.

These four do not equate textual differences with element differences automatically. They construe the contrasting provisions and their functions, rather than count words. They would not make an accidental innocent taking robbery or use the separate attempt provision alone to defeat inclusion in the completed taking charged here. Nor do they decide the contested contact with the customer or whether the evidence necessarily establishes intimidation.

**Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer:** The completed forcible taking offense carries the inherited theft purpose and movement of the property. Morissette's contextual presumption, the common-law robbery setting and the asserted stylistic removal of feloniously support that reading; the offense title alone is not dispositive. Staples and X-Citement Video require identifying the actual culpability and statutory context rather than presuming that omission of a phrase eliminates its substance. Bell broadened subsection (b)'s covered theft modes; it does not hold that the completed taking branch of subsection (a) lacks the theft ingredients relevant here.

These four construe subsection (b)'s lower paragraph as the basic theft grade, punishable at the stated one-year maximum when the aggravated value element is not established as part of the offense. The prosecution need not prove affirmatively that the money could not exceed $1,000 to convict of the basic theft; proof of a larger theft does not make the underlying theft innocent. The lower-value language limits the basic punishment and distinguishes the aggravated finding required for ten years. It is not erased: a conviction lacking that aggravated element remains confined to the lower grade even if evidence would support a larger amount. Under that construction, basic larceny's elements are included in the completed robbery offense; the separate ten-year grade is not.

The strongest objection is that the lower paragraph expressly describes property not exceeding $1,000, and that the robbery provision separately omits carrying away and specific theft intent. These four answer the first through the statute's basic/aggravated structure and the ordinary role of value in grading a theft, and the second through the legal meaning of the completed robbery taking rather than a factual-overlap rule. They would not extend their conclusion to every attempt, extortion, entry or other offense configuration. Their basic-inclusion position is a genuine statutory-elements conclusion, not revival of Schmuck's rejected inherent-relationship test.

## Request scope, reciprocity, charge and election

The original letter and proposed charge must be considered together. The letter identifies Carter's defense as larceny under subsection (b), rather than robbery, because force or intimidation is disputed. The attached proposed instruction describes the higher grade using the obsolete threshold. The four affirmance Justices regard the latter as an actual request for the felony grade; independently, their intent and movement construction forecloses either grade. They would not order consideration of an entirely different instruction solely because a new analytical route can now be formulated.

The four basic-inclusion Justices regard the broader letter and theory as sufficient to prevent treating the attached felony-grade formulation as a conclusive abandonment of every basic-larceny option. The categorical Mosley noninclusion ruling bypassed whether the substance of the request calls for a legally included basic charge and whether the evidence permits it. Their change from the earlier value-only commitment is this distinction: the additional felony element defeats automatic entitlement to that grade, but does not settle the actual broader request after basic larceny is independently classified as included. They would vacate that categorical rejection and remand for lawful treatment of the request's scope, correction of its obsolete threshold, and the separate evidentiary condition. They do not find that an otherwise unpreserved claim is preserved by newly invented waiver, or that a trial judge must give every unrequested lesser offense.

Keeble supplies the function of a genuinely included, evidence-supported alternative when the jury might otherwise choose between an overinclusive conviction and acquittal. It does not dispense with inclusion or the evidence requirement. For these four, a properly bounded return protects that function while permitting denial if the actual request and ordinary preservation rules do not support the basic option, or if a rational jury could not acquit of robbery while finding the included theft. A showing that the evidence supports robbery is not, by itself, the latter determination. No trial credibility finding is made here.

**Higher-grade election:** No Associate treats a defendant's request as an already existing exception allowing conviction of any factually related offense. Schmuck expressly addressed reciprocity: a notice-waiver route available only to the defendant was a difficulty with the rejected inherent-relationship approach. Independently including basic larceny narrows the proposed election, but does not make the felony's additional value element a statutory subset or eliminate that stated difficulty. The four affirmance Justices reject the election as a route to relief on this record. The four remand Justices do not adopt it; their sufficient route concerns the independently included basic offense. A new grade-election rule would require an express, supported explanation of its relationship to Schmuck and the distinct charging safeguards. It cannot enter their joins merely because it could supply a more convenient remedy.

**Notice and indictment:** The indictment actually alleges approximately $14,000; no conclusion rests on falsely calling value absent. Awareness of that factual allegation is not by itself legal inclusion, a returned felony-larceny charge, a valid waiver of grand-jury protection, an admission as an element or waiver of jury determination. Stirone protects the grand jury's actual charging choice against enlargement; its prosecution-created constructive amendment does not itself decide this defendant-requested proposal. The existing indictment's complete legal effect must be assessed under the adopted offense construction if that distinct route is properly reached; the statutory citation alone does not settle it. No Associate here holds that the present indictment independently charges every element of felony larceny or that a formal indictment waiver occurred. The claimed election therefore supplies no present automatic entitlement to the ten-year instruction.

The grade distinction preserves jury responsibility. Where the felony grade is lawfully charged or otherwise lawfully available, the jury must determine value beyond reasonable doubt unless the applicable element/admission and waiver requirements are satisfied. Carter's admission to taking money is not a substitute for those distinct requirements. No new amount finding, implied grand-jury waiver or hypothetical trial stipulation is entered.

## Remedy, supersession and source limits

The four affirmance Justices leave the challenged judgment intact without deciding the factual entitlement alternative, lower-grade upper-value construction or independent charging theory. The four vacatur Justices remove the categorical bar and return for proper scope and preservation treatment of the actual request and the Government's independent rational-jury objection. They order neither acquittal, entry of a larceny conviction, a fixed sentence nor an automatic new trial. If a properly requested and evidence-supported basic instruction should have been given, the ordinary remedy inquiry follows; the handoff makes no unsupported finding of harmlessness or of its contrary. Separate Pennsylvania convictions and unrelated matters remain outside review.

The changed commitments are Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's judgment positions. The new neutral analytical supplement exposed a distinct basic-offense/request path that their earlier value-only treatment did not resolve. Their agreement that felony value is an element is unchanged; no historical outcome or new fact caused the revision. They now supply affirmative basic-inclusion constructions and support a bounded return, while refusing to invent a grade-election waiver. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's dispositions and sufficient grounds remain unchanged, with their answers to the additional alternatives now explicit.
