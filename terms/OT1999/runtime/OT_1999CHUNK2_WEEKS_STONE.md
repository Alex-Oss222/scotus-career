## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Capital jury’s unresolved question about the continuing availability

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits through the amended 28 U.S.C. § 2254(d)(1) threshold and finds it satisfied: the state court unreasonably applied, rather than merely misapplied, clearly established Federal law as determined by this Court, namely the already-established *Boyde*, *Lockett* and *Eddings* requirement of effective consideration of mitigation. Later *Buchanan* or *Williams* reasoning is not used to establish that law retroactively. Stone rejects the State's fair-presentation, exhaustion and default defenses on their predicates: assignment 44 presented the subsequent question-and-response claim and incorporated assignment 41's federal effective-mitigation grounds, and the state court considered and rejected that claim on the merits; no independent state procedural ground rests on it. He rejects the asserted *Teague* bar because the protection applied is a functional application of the preexisting *Boyde*, *Lockett* and *Eddings* effective-mitigation requirement, not a new right to particular wording or mandatory rephrasing; no exception is necessary. No other threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit and grant conditional federal habeas relief from the death sentence. When a capital jury asks whether an aggravator makes death mandatory, the response must clearly confirm that life remains authorized and mitigation may justify it. This ensures that a capital jury actually understands its lawful life-or-death discretion when its own question defeats the ordinary presumption of comprehension.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When a capital jury asks whether an aggravator makes death mandatory, the response must clearly confirm that life remains authorized and mitigation may justify it. A state-court adjudication that leaves the jury's expressed mandatory-death confusion unresolved when the instructions are read together unreasonably applies the clearly established *Boyde*, *Lockett* and *Eddings* requirement of effective consideration of mitigation under § 2254(d)(1).
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit and grant conditional federal habeas relief from the death sentence. When a capital jury asks whether an aggravator makes death mandatory, the response must clearly confirm that life remains authorized and mitigation may justify it. This ensures that a capital jury actually understands its lawful life-or-death discretion when its own question defeats the ordinary presumption of comprehension. No particular wording is required, and reference to an existing instruction may suffice when it unambiguously resolves the jury’s actual question. Buchanan sustained its charge because the complete proceedings allowed mitigation its proper effect. Stone distinguishes the unresolved question here as indicating that the jury may have understood eligibility as requiring death; he must assess that inference and Brecht prejudice from the entire penalty record, not from a rule demanding new instructions whenever jurors ask a question. Under § 2254(d)(1), the state court unreasonably applied, rather than merely misapplied, the already-established *Boyde*, *Lockett* and *Eddings* requirement of effective consideration of mitigation. That conclusion rests on the jury's expressed mandatory-death confusion and the response's failure to resolve it when the instructions are read together, not on a requirement of particular wording. That unresolved confusion also had a substantial and injurious effect on the death verdict under *Brecht*. Reverse and grant sentence-only conditional relief: a constitutionally adequate penalty proceeding or a lawful noncapital sentence, with the conviction left intact. Later *Buchanan* or *Williams* reasoning is not used to establish the governing law retroactively.
