## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Definition of grazing preference

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial statutory challenge.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The revised definition preserves the statutory priority without treating a preference as ownership of a fixed forage quantity.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Secretary may define grazing preference as a priority in permit allocation rather than a vested entitlement to a fixed amount of grazing.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The public lands remain federally owned and administered. The statutes protect priority and process but do not freeze every historic permit quantity against lawful resource management.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A particular reduction or cancellation remains reviewable under the applicable statute and procedure.
### Issue 2: Stock-ownership eligibility

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the statutory challenge because the regulation changes program eligibility.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The statute does not make livestock ownership the exclusive qualification, and the revised rule remains connected to responsible use of the allotment.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Secretary may permit an otherwise qualified applicant to hold a grazing permit without personally owning livestock at every moment, subject to lawful program conditions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The challenged rule addresses administration within the field Congress assigned. It does not dispense with every qualification or authorize paper permits disconnected from actual grazing responsibility.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone reserves materially different arrangements that evade statutory eligibility or transfer permits contrary to law.
### Issue 3: Title to future permanent improvements

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the facial challenge to the prospective title rule.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The Secretary may require that permanent improvements built under future cooperative agreements on federal land be titled in the United States when the agreement and statute so provide.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The prospective federal-title provision is within the Secretary’s authority and does not on its face take an existing private improvement.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The rule concerns future arrangements on federal property and allows the parties to know the title consequence before construction. It does not adjudicate ownership already vested under earlier law or contract.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Existing improvements, compensation, reliance, and site-specific agreement terms remain open.
