## Common operative framework and separate components

All eight Associates adhere to the RFRA burden sequence adopted in Clearwater and retained in Swanner and American Life League: the claimant establishes substantial burden; the Government then bears production and persuasion that this application furthers a compelling interest through the least restrictive means. Their disagreement concerns whether the undisputed proposal and the tax authorities supply a sufficient legal resolution now. No Justice treats the word taxation, commercial participation, or Smith's constitutional neutral-law rule as an exemption from RFRA. No Justice decides federal RFRA's constitutionality, which was uncontested below and reserved by Boerne.

The following components remain distinct:

1. **RFRA and collection.** The present assessment and collection demand, including Adams's demand for nonmilitary designation or nonliability pending accommodation. The conceded interest in collecting taxes does not itself concede the least-restrictive-means question.
2. **RFRA and the failure-to-file addition.** The additional burden associated with timely ascertainment and filing, not simply the amount of underlying revenue. Section 6651(a)(1)'s exception requires both reasonable cause and absence of willful neglect. Boyle's ordinary-care rule and its reservations concerning genuine objective inability remain relevant. Religious sincerity alone does not establish either objective inability or statutory qualification.
3. **RFRA and the estimated-tax addition.** Section 6654(e)(3)(A) concerns casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstances making imposition against equity and good conscience, to the extent determined by the Secretary. It is not a general reasonable-cause exception. The separate subsection (B) requires retirement after age 62 or disability in the relevant taxable year or its predecessor, together with reasonable cause and absence of willful neglect; it is not claimed to apply here. The amount-and-duration structure of §6654(a) makes the Government's interest in timely receipt analytically relevant, but does not decide RFRA by its label.
4. **Ungranted independent grounds.** The lower court's statutory conclusions and its note-10 constitutional determination are left undisturbed at their own scope. A failure to establish a Code waiver does not dispose of the separate RFRA defense. Conversely, vacating an RFRA determination does not establish statutory eligibility or a constitutional violation.

All eight can assume the substantial-burden threshold for the present legal decision. Neither the existence of sincere belief alone nor a judicial judgment about Quaker theology is used as a substitute for that threshold. The five supporting remand do not foreclose an ordinary determination of any genuinely disputed threshold issue on remand; they do not require redundant evidence of an undisputed religious objection.

## Enhanced comparison: collection

The two supported paths are (A) affirm because the undisputed requested spending restriction is legally different from paying one's unconditional share of common revenue, with Lee and Hernandez supplying a sufficient justification in this setting; and (B) vacate because the lower court made uniform participation itself conclusive and declined the production-and-persuasion inquiry required by the governing RFRA cases. Path B does not presuppose that an earmark can work. Path A cannot rest on the assertion that Congress alone may ever accommodate religion, because RFRA is itself a congressional accommodation command. Neither path allows the Court to invent a feasible accounting arrangement or an appropriation conflict established by no source.

### Stevens

**Commitment and sufficient ground.** Affirm. His Lee concurrence, 455 U.S. 261–264 and especially 262 n.1, distinguishes an actual substitute welfare arrangement from the ordinary taxpayer's objection to the Government's use of money. His join in Hernandez, 490 U.S. 699–700, supports applying the common-revenue justification to income tax. His Clearwater join preserves RFRA's later statutory burden; his present conclusion is that the undisputed conditional-payment request already identifies the relevant impairment. Adams offers equal dollars subject to her restriction, not the same unrestricted contribution to collective public finance. The Government need not accept that legally different contribution or suspend liability pending agreement about its use.

**Alternative and counterargument.** The strongest remand case is that the enacted standard requires the Government to demonstrate why a nonmilitary designation cannot preserve its legitimate collection interest. His prior Clearwater join prevents answering simply that taxes are general laws. He nevertheless regards the restriction on the use of the claimant's contribution, coupled with nonliability until accommodation, as the material difference established on this record; a numerical projection is not necessary to identify it. His earlier Swanner and American Life League dissents establish only that he did not require remand in those settings, not an undocumented rationale imported here.

**Join boundaries.** He can join a narrow affirmance applying RFRA to these proposals. He will not shift RFRA's burden to the claimant under his pre-RFRA constitutional formulation, decide federal RFRA invalid, or hold that courts lack authority to remedy any RFRA violation in tax administration. No separate writing is necessary if the opinion preserves those distinctions; otherwise a concurrence in judgment would identify the sufficient common-revenue ground.

### O'Connor

**Commitment and sufficient ground.** Vacate. Her authorship of Clearwater, Swanner and American Life League directly establishes the application-specific statutory inquiry, the Government's burden, and the need to compare actual alternatives. The lower court expressly treated the means question as already answered by uniformity and required no evidence. Lee, which she joined, supplies strong substantive grounds against a tax exemption, but not a replacement for the intervening statutory inquiry or a finding about Adams's full-payment proposal.

**Alternative and counterargument.** A narrow legal affirmance is substantial: a judicial spending condition can restrict the common system even if the taxpayer pays the same sum. Her Lee join is a real obstacle to ordering accommodation, not to a properly bounded remand. Her answer is that the record identifies a proposed restriction but does not establish which collection or allocation operations it would require or why the Government's proposed legal characterization fully disposes of it. Under her American Life League opinion, a claimant need not first prove that an alternative works before the Government's burden exists. The Government may demonstrate on remand that the requested benefit is a legally or operationally unacceptable substitute; the Court does not decide that premise for it here.

**Join boundaries.** She rejects both an automatic tax exception and an automatic exemption for a sincere objector. Her Hernandez dissent, joined by Scalia, addresses materially comparable religious transactions and discriminatory administration; it supplies no finding of a comparable waiver in Adams. She can join a remand that leaves Lee's force intact, permits legal resolution without a hearing where adequate, and orders no change in appropriations.

### Scalia

**Commitment and sufficient ground.** Vacate. His joins in Clearwater, Swanner and American Life League adopt RFRA's enacted demonstration requirement separately from constitutional neutral-law doctrine. RFRA reaches federal law without a tax exception supplied here. The Government's definition of its interest as uniform collection cannot make the demanded means its own justification. The lower court's rejection of any need for production is inconsistent with the statutory allocation he has joined.

**Alternative and counterargument.** Lee and Hernandez give an unusually close legal answer to a war-tax objection. Moreover, accepting payment restricted to a claimant's preferred purposes differs from collecting the tax Congress prescribed. Those are powerful reasons to deny relief after applying RFRA, and he does not reject their legal sufficiency in an adequately stated application. He rejects using their results to foreclose the inquiry that the later statute requires, especially when the asserted statutory exception concerns all federal law and the proposal includes full payment. His commitment does not depend on legislative-history rhetoric or on a constitutional right to choose public spending.

**Join boundaries.** No transfer of the Government's statutory burden to Adams, no presumption that all ordinary tax exceptions are comparable, and no inference from his Hernandez dissent that war-tax earmarks must be allowed. He can join a bounded statutory remand, including an express statement that no evidentiary hearing follows merely from asserting religion. He will not join an appropriation mandate or a revision of Smith.

### Kennedy

**Commitment and sufficient ground.** Vacate. His direct joins in the three RFRA decisions supply the relevant statutory commitment. His Boerne opinion expressly preserves federal RFRA and the earlier statutory reasoning within a valid field. The lower opinion's categorical approach cannot be treated as a factual determination that the proposed alternatives impair collection. His position rests on those simulated writings; he did not participate in Hernandez, and no join is attributed to him.

**Alternative and counterargument.** A limited affirmance can identify the Government's interest as receiving the same contribution required of other citizens, without a personal restriction on public use. Lee also shows that an accommodation can impose the claimant's religion on others. Kennedy accepts both points as potential justification, but the assumed equivalence between any designation and that injury is precisely the application that must be explained. He does not infer from a proposal to consider accommodation that Treasury has an effective alternative, and does not convert his RFRA joins into a general duty to negotiate.

**Join boundaries.** His remand preserves Congress's legitimate fiscal choices and permits rejection of an accommodation that defeats them. It must not announce a private budgetary veto, deem the IRS outside RFRA, or decide the reserved federal-validity issue. No independent constitutional judgment is within this grant.

### Souter

**Commitment and sufficient ground.** Vacate. His Clearwater, Swanner and American Life League joins require a separate statutory justification for the actual burden. The lower court's reliance on the importance of the entire tax system leaves the specific means question unanswered. The existence of a full-payment proposal does not prove feasibility; it does make it necessary to explain the relation between the claimed burden, the requested restriction, and the Government's permissible objectives.

**Alternative and counterargument.** Lee's close tax reasoning and the legal difference between a restricted contribution and general revenue strongly support ultimate denial. His recent Strout opinion also cautions that private designation alone does not transform the actual public financing relationship. But Strout decides a financed devotional service under the Religion Clauses, not an exemption from federal RFRA, and cannot supply the missing tax finding. He chooses remand because the lower court expressly dispensed with the statutory demonstration, not because he has concluded that either earmarking or reallocation is harmless.

**Join boundaries.** He requires the same actual-function discipline of both sides. No presumption that a book entry suffices, no theological reassessment, no automatic proof of burden from sincerity alone, and no requirement of a hearing without a genuinely material dispute. His support extends to the RFRA inquiry and its limited remedy, not to an independent constitutional exemption.

### Thomas

**Commitment and sufficient ground.** Vacate. His joins in Clearwater, Swanner and American Life League treat RFRA as an operative statutory protection rather than a restatement of Smith. His Boerne join does not invalidate the federal field. The text's separate requirements cannot be collapsed by defining the compelling interest to require precisely the means challenged. The concrete proposal may fail, but the lower court's announced exemption from evidentiary production cannot be retained as the governing rule.

**Alternative and counterargument.** Lee's tax reasoning and the lack of an established alternative are formidable. A legal determination can satisfy RFRA without a new empirical study, and an unsupported suggestion cannot itself win an exemption. He nevertheless rejects treating the lack of an established alternative as Adams's dispositive failure after the Government's burden has arisen. American Life League expressly preserves that allocation. The appropriate response is further statutory adjudication, not immediate nonliability or a universal judicial power to redesign tax collection.

**Join boundaries.** He can join only the statutory remand at that level of generality. No new constitutional test, no holding that hardship exceptions necessarily are comparable, no mandatory new evidentiary proceeding, and no presumption that historical tax judgments themselves define RFRA's full reach.

### Ginsburg

**Commitment and sufficient ground.** Affirm. Her Clearwater join establishes that RFRA governs and that substantial burden triggers the Government's burden. Her rejection of the particular Swanner and American Life League remands does not alter that rule; it shows that applying the rule does not invariably require further proceedings. Here the undisputed restriction sought is part of the proposed payment, so a legal determination can resolve the application. Lee and Hernandez explain why taxpayers' religious objections to expenditures cannot make each share of general revenue conditional on the contributor's approval of its use.

**Alternative and counterargument.** The lower court's categorical language is wrong under governing RFRA law, and full payment raises a real distinction from a simple revenue exemption. Nevertheless, the relief sought is not merely full payment through an independently established collection method. It demands a legally protected nonmilitary use and suspends liability until accommodation. She concludes that this restriction is enough to sustain the Government's application here, without assuming an administrative cost figure or relying on an unproved inability to maintain separate accounts.

**Join boundaries.** She would affirm the judgment on that sufficient ground, not endorse the lower court's description of the means inquiry as self-answering. The rationale must preserve RFRA review of other IRS conduct and all actual waiver grounds. Her current Strout join supplies no federal-tax holding and is not used as one. No constitutional validity decision or finding of insincerity is acceptable.

### Breyer

**Commitment and sufficient ground.** Affirm. His Clearwater join recognizes the statutory burden sequence; his earlier votes against the Swanner and American Life League remands are limited application positions, not an exemption from RFRA. Lee and Hernandez identify the practical and institutional relationship that matters here: general revenues support collectively chosen purposes, and tax payment does not confer authority to limit that contribution's public use. He treats the present proposal's own conditions, rather than a speculative estimate of future claimants or administrative expense, as sufficient to sustain collection under RFRA.

**Alternative and counterargument.** If Adams had proposed an established means preserving the full legal and practical character of payment while removing a distinct religious burden, merely reciting uniformity would not answer it. This packet establishes no such arrangement, but its absence is not itself her failure of the RFRA burden. The decisive distinction for him is that her actual relief changes the legal character of the contribution and postpones liability until accommodation. Further proceedings about hypothetical methods need not be ordered to resolve that demand.

**Join boundaries.** He can join an affirmance that expressly applies RFRA and corrects the lower court's overstatement. He will not join a rule that tax administration always wins by invoking complexity, or one treating sincerity as proof that the taxpayer receives no economic benefit. No accounting fact, appropriation conflict or quantified cost is invented.

## Enhanced comparison: the two additions

The competing paths are (A) affirm because the distinct filing and timely-payment purposes, applied to this deliberate conscience-based refusal, provide a sufficient legal justification for the identified additions; and (B) vacate their RFRA determinations because lawful underlying tax liability does not automatically establish the least restrictive means of enforcing either filing or estimated-payment obligations. The latter path may prevail even if the base tax ultimately is sustained. The statutory exceptions demonstrate that the systems contain qualifications; they do not establish that a qualifying casualty, objective inability, retirement, disability, or comparable secular deliberate refusal exists here.

The three affirming Justices do not rely on a proposition that the Government must prove nothing. They conclude that the undisputed nature of the claimed dispensation, together with the identified statutory functions and surviving tax authority, supplies a sufficient legal showing. The five remanding Justices do not find that showing necessarily impossible; they reject affirmance of the present RFRA determinations after the courts below dispensed with it. This is a dispute about the adequacy of the present application, not a dispute about whose statutory burden exists.
