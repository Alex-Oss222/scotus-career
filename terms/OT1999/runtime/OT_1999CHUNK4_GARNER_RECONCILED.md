## Common propositions and the six-Associate remand ground

All eight accept these propositions as governing law in this case: Jones must identify an offense-date legal reconsideration entitlement, actual retrospective withdrawal and a concrete link to legally available earlier release. He need not prove the omitted occasion would have yielded parole. A State invoking a substitute must substantiate its legal basis, practical availability and timely capacity; ultimate constitutional proof stays with the prisoner. An initial prediction, even reasoned and reviewable, does not itself preserve consideration of later rehabilitation, changed facts or a materially mistaken forecast. Current Morales expressly leaves a demonstrated materially different arrangement open.

Jones's actual eight-year deferral removed the three-year consideration opportunity applicable at his offense date while parole remained legally available. Two murders, escape and repeated adverse assessments do not establish legal impossibility of earlier release. This satisfies the initial opportunity showing; it is not an adjudication that he is suitable for parole or that every Georgia life prisoner will serve longer.

The 1996 policy and the custodian's sworn account materially differ from an advocate's hypothesis that someone could advance review. They assert an existing individualized and expedited mechanism. A court must assess that evidence, together with actual supported practice, rather than disregard it solely because the policy uses discretionary language or lacks an invariably enforceable hearing demand. Conversely, the affidavit's assertion of existence does not by itself establish an ordinary usable route and timely consideration as a matter of law. “May receive” remains permission, not a mandatory hearing on every request or a guaranteed response deadline.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer directly join a bounded application: vacate the appellate determination that the asserted discretionary arrangement cannot suffice and remand for assessment under the actual opportunity/substitute rule. The district court's historical-Morales premise likewise cannot supply a judgment for officials. The return is under ordinary summary-judgment and appellate procedure, not an instruction to start an administrative study, guarantee the State a chance to cure every evidentiary failure, or decide factual questions for either side. Jones's actual initial showing remains established; the replacement question is not reset into a requirement that he prove likely parole.

Scalia, Thomas and Breyer apply the existing rule without adopting its enumerated evidentiary formulation as a new independent preferred constitutional test. They can fully join an opinion that accurately states controlling Morales and makes this application. They cannot be counted as newly joining Morales's original independent allocation holding merely because they comply with it here. Kennedy accepts the bounded substitute inquiry; O'Connor and Souter retain their prior direct joins. No new reusable presumption, probability threshold, administrative filing form or numerical deadline is authorized.

## Stevens and Ginsburg: existing reversal should stand

Stevens and Ginsburg agree that discretion and actual practice are relevant and that formal enforceability is not invariably indispensable. They would nevertheless affirm the reversal of defendants' summary judgment and remand with the appellate rationale confined to the present record. Jones has established the lost opportunity, while the officials' generalized account and permissive policy do not demonstrate practical availability and timely capacity sufficiently to sustain judgment for them. The appropriate appellate result already returns the dispute to ordinary proceedings; the Court can correct the lower court's categorical language without vacating that result.

Their position is not a finding that expedited review never occurs, a holding that every eight-year interval necessarily violates the Constitution, or a universal duty to grant every requested hearing. They require no proof that Jones would actually have been paroled. They support no immediate release, damages, statewide timetable or newly prescribed administrative process. Their refusal to join vacatur is remedial and record-specific; they do not join the six's conclusion that the appellate disposition must itself be displaced to secure proper assessment of the asserted replacement.
