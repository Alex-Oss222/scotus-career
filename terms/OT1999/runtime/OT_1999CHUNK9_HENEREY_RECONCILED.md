## What the historical anchor does and does not establish

The court below treated this supervised campaign as a school-sponsored educational activity governed by Hazelwood. It upheld advance approval, found the conventional candy practice a sufficient distinction defeating selective-enforcement inference, and held disqualification reasonable. Its separate alternative assumed protected expression and content disagreement yet relied on maturity, apparent school approval, sexual subject matter and educational mission. The principal's stated reason remained failure to secure approval; the appellate alternative was not a trial finding of actual motive, harmful sexual advocacy, disruption or a parental confrontation.

Wolle identified a possible message-based pretext, differential application to campaign objects, notice and sanction concerns. He sought further adjudication of possible damages against appropriate defendants. He did not establish liability, a right to office or a requirement that the school approve condoms. Both opinions reported summary judgment. The count revealing Henerey's majority came after disqualification, so no punishment for winning is inferred.

The operative law now includes the unanimous simulated Settle holding: genuine pedagogical requirements remain permissible, but an imagined lawful justification cannot defeat a supported material inference of an impermissible actual reason. Protected conduct and a substantial or motivating role must be established; the separate same-decision defense requires proof by a preponderance. Boring does not erase a student's personal protection, Good News/Good Sports concerns actual private access, and Santa Fe concerns the specific official religious-choice mechanism. Those rules must govern even where the historical lower opinion used a broader formulation. Their existence is not a fictional change in any Supreme Court Justice's historical Henerey vote.
