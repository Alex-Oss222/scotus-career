* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The PLRA's automatic stay is not facially unconstitutional merely because it suspends prospective relief before final adjudication, but its application may not eliminate a court's meaningful opportunity to decide the termination motion under the new substantive law.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reverse the Seventh Circuit's categorical invalidation and remand. Congress may alter the standards governing continuing prospective relief and may suspend relief by operation of the new law rather than by revising past findings. Stone would reserve an as-applied Article III objection if the statutory deadlines, including available extensions, leave the court no meaningful opportunity to adjudicate the motion in this litigation. This replaces the old categorical rule with a narrower boundary. The simulated Plaut is not the historical bar on reopening. Congress’s change in the conditions for a continuing decree can operate without revising the court’s findings; Stone’s reserved concern is whether this timetable actually prevents adjudication, not a categorical legislative inability to affect existing injunctions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Congress may prescribe valid termination standards, deadlines, and temporary statutory consequences; no court may preserve obsolete relief by ignoring the new law, and no as-applied violation is presumed.
