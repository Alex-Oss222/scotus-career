## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Does Chapter 2 violate the Establishment Clause

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Affirm invalidation of Chapter 2 as administered. Neutral eligibility and private enrollment do not alone settle whether the equipment is publicly financing devotional instruction, but religious identity, fungibility and incidental savings are likewise insufficient by themselves. Genuinely student-directed benefits and publicly controlled services remain distinct.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Neutral allocation does not validate aid that, on a supported operational record, publicly finances religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under the recipient’s control. The proposed affirmance concerns Chapter 2 as administered on that factual premise; religious identity, fungibility, recipient control and incidental expense substitution alone do not establish the violation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The approved disposition is to affirm the as-administered invalidation on a supported finding of publicly financed religious instruction or religious use. Neutral eligibility and private enrollment do not cure that proven funded function. Agostini nevertheless prevents treating religious-school location, recipient control, fungibility or saved institutional expense alone as proof of the violation. The inquiry concerns what the government actually finances and how the resources are controlled and used. Genuinely student-directed benefits and separately controlled secular public services remain distinct. If the complete record establishes only neutral secular use with effective controls, the required premise fails and the retained affirmance cannot be sustained on identity or fungibility alone.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Publicly staffed services, tightly controlled short-term loans, secular student-directed benefits, and programs preventing institutional substitution remain available.
