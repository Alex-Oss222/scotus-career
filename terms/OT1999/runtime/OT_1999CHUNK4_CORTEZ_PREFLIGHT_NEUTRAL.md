## Competing lawful paths and remedy

The permissive reading treats the award district as an additional specified forum without excluding districts independently proper under general venue law. It draws support from the permissive formulation, absence of an exclusive-venue term, the need to read §§9–11 consistently, and the relation to the Act's preaward mechanisms, including §8's retained admiralty jurisdiction and §4's district limits. This argument must explain what the award-district language still does rather than making it surplusage: it supplies an assured venue for an award made there when ordinary venue might not. It cannot assert that the word may is always nonexclusive or that arbitration policy overrides a specific location command.

The strongest exclusivity reading treats permission to grant postaward relief as confined to the court Congress specifically identifies. On this reading, may authorizes the remedy, not a free choice among districts; the territorial organization of the Act and a single postaward forum favor coherent supervision and avoid competing confirmations. It must answer why the text lacks an exclusive command, how its reading operates with §9's agreed-court alternative and §§3/4/8, and why ordinary venue law is displaced.
