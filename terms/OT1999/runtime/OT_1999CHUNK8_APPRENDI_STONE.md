## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Must a nonrecidivist fact authorizing punishment above

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply current simulated law. A nonrecidivist fact legally necessary to authorize punishment above the verdict- or plea-supported maximum is an element that must be charged and either admitted as an element or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Apprendi admitted an offense carrying a ten-year maximum; the judge alone found biased purpose by a preponderance and used that finding to impose twelve years. The enhanced sentence therefore exceeds the punishment authorized by the admitted offense.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A nonrecidivist fact legally necessary to authorize punishment above the verdict- or plea-supported maximum is an element that must be charged and either admitted as an element or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. New Jersey could not authorize Apprendi's twelve-year sentence through a judicial preponderance finding when the admitted offense carried a ten-year maximum.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Simulated *Gaudin* requires jury determination of offense elements, and simulated *Almendarez-Torres* establishes that maximum-increasing effect makes a fact an element. Simulated *Jones* applies that rule to an aggravated statutory offense. Apprendi admitted only the offense carrying a ten-year maximum. The judge's later preponderance finding of biased purpose supplied the sole legal authority for a twelve-year sentence, so the finding performed the work of an aggravated-offense element without the required charge, admission, or jury verdict. Stone reverses and vacates the enhanced sentence under existing law rather than creating a new exception-bearing rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Judicial factfinding remains permissible within the punishment authorized by the conviction. Mandatory minimums that do not change the maximum, consecutive-sentence rules, capital selection, and materially different sentencing systems remain outside the granted question.
