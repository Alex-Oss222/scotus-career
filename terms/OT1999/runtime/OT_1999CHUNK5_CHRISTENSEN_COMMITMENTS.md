## Separate decisional components and competing rules

**Statutory question.** Five Associates—O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas—would affirm the judgment for defendants against this categorical FLSA challenge. Section 207(o)(5) secures use within a reasonable period after the employee requests it, absent undue disruption; it does not make a request the exclusive legal trigger for using accrued time. Section (o)(2) conditions provision of time in place of overtime cash, not every scheduling decision after lawful accrual. An otherwise lawful agreement can restrict employer-directed use, but the statute supplies no additional categorical requirement that such use be specifically agreed. Paid time off consumes credits in the form defined by (o)(7); this is not uncompensated leave or confiscation.

Their strongest contrary path is that prior consent and protected requests make the substitute an employee-controlled asset whose involuntary consumption defeats the cash-overtime trigger. The answer is that the cap limits accumulation and requires cash for additional overtime at the cap; it guarantees no right to bank the maximum. Authorized cashout likewise prevents an absolute retention right, although cash payment and paid absence are not identical transactions. These are textual and structural reasons, not adoption of a general economic presumption favoring employer control.

Stevens and Ginsburg would reject the employer-control default as inconsistent with the conditioned, consensual substitute for ordinary cash overtime. They would require an agreement authorizing compulsory use and reverse the defendants' final judgment, remanding to determine the applicable lawful terms. Breyer would reach that same remand because the responsible agency's consent-based construction is a permissible construction entitled to statutory deference, without requiring the employees' construction to be the only possible judicial reading.

Their strongest contrary path is that paragraph (5) states only a minimum employee right and §553.23 says agreements may address use. Their answer is that the system requires an agreed substitute for cash, protects actual availability and specifically permits unilateral cash payment; scheduling forced consumption without an agreed term is a different power that need not be inferred. They do not convert every optional agreement provision into a mandatory bargaining topic, require identical individual terms, or disregard Moreau's earlier-hire practice route.

**Statutory agency deference.** O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas would hold this advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not receive Chevron's controlling statutory weight. The implementing grant authorizes administration and regulation; it does not automatically give every subsequent advisory response the force of a rule or adjudication. The letter remains eligible for Skidmore consideration according to its actual reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. On the supported content it adds a specific-consent restriction that the statute and permissive agreement regulation do not supply, and is unpersuasive for the reasons stated above. No finding about an unproved change of agency policy is needed.

Scalia would not join that limitation of Chevron by the letter's advisory form. He would treat the responsible Labor Department's statutory construction as eligible for authoritative consideration within the real implementing delegation, but reject this particular construction as impermissible in light of the statute's request trigger and the separate nature of the original agreement condition. His affirmance therefore does not support the four-Associate no-Chevron proposition.

Breyer also rejects the exclusion of Chevron based on this form and would accept the agency's reasonable construction; that different merits evaluation separates him from Scalia. Stevens and Ginsburg need not resolve the general Chevron-form issue: their independent statutory reading suffices, and the letter's consistent-with-text position reinforces it. Neither is provisionally committed to the four-Associate exclusion of controlling statutory weight.

**Own-regulation interpretation.** The five affirming Associates retain Auer but conclude that it does not allow changing §553.23(a)(2)'s permission to include preservation, use or cashout provisions into a requirement of specific consent for compulsory use. The regulation deals with lawful agreement formation and optional consistent terms; §553.25's good-faith, reasonable-period and undue-disruption provisions protect requests without announcing exclusive employee scheduling. On their construction there is no pertinent ambiguity permitting the letter's added restriction. They do not overrule Auer, require formal rulemaking for every regulatory interpretation, or deny its fair-and-considered rule merely because an agency used an opinion letter.

Stevens and Ginsburg would find the consent-based reading consistent with the whole regulatory scheme and the statutory rule they adopt; they would not join the conclusion that the letter contradicts plain regulatory text. Breyer regards that reading as permissible under Auer as well, subject to the actual responsible-agency and fair-consideration predicates. His statutory-deference ground is independently sufficient and does not depend on inventing missing internal procedures. No opinion-letter statement establishes the actual terms of the class members' agreements.

**Agreement and remedy question.** The five affirming Associates would not order an agreement-specific remand merely because agreements might contain restrictions. The granted categorical theory fails, and the record identifies no preserved controlling term establishing a violation; they make no finding that agreements do not exist or that every employee consented to forced use. Independently valid agreement obligations and other applicable law remain at their actual scope. The three reversing Associates would withhold judgment for the County because their adopted consent requirement makes the actual terms material; they would not simply restore the District Court's categorical declaration or presume all employees lack a valid authorizing arrangement.

## Justice-specific support and join barriers

### Stevens

**Commitment:** Reverse judgment for defendants and remand for application of the consent requirement to actual agreements. His Moreau opinion treats the statutory substitute as dependent on its actual agreement routes, including the April 1986 practice exception, without presuming consent outside them. His Auer and Smiley joins respect responsible agency implementation within text; his FDA dissent distinguishes delegated coverage from unlawful method. His U.S. West position agrees that broad approval language may permit affirmative consent, without making deference a cure for an independent defect.

**Opposing path and barrier:** The request clause can be read as a floor, but read with the agreed substitution it does not grant an unstated power to consume the employee's benefit unilaterally. Cashout delivers the statutory money equivalent and is separately authorized; it does not establish equivalence to compelled leisure. He would accept agreement-specific permission, including a properly applicable transition practice, but not a universal employer default. He supports the statutory rule without requiring a broad holding about which informal agency formats receive Chevron.

### O'Connor

**Commitment:** Affirm; join the statutory no-additional-consent rule, the advisory-letter/Skidmore ground and the Auer textual-fit limit. Her Moreau join keeps the accrual agreement routes distinct. Her Auer join permits considered interpretation but no amendment of text. Her Smiley and NationsBank joins relied on actual authoritative exercises of delegated responsibility, not all informal pronouncements. Her FDA join requires authority and statutory fit before deference; her U.S. West join recognizes genuinely delegated regulatory choices after independent constraints are satisfied.

**Opposing path and barrier:** Protective purpose and agency experience may support a reasonable preference but cannot convert optional scheduling terms into compulsory ones through this advice. She would preserve an actual agreement restricting use; absent an established relevant claim she declines a new factual-remand entitlement. No denial of paragraph (5)'s mandatory protection or permission to avoid statutory overtime is acceptable.

### Scalia

**Commitment:** Affirm the judgment and join the statutory construction and Auer fit conclusion, but withhold assent from the no-Chevron treatment of this opinion letter. **Opposing path and barrier:** Eligibility for deference does not make an added restriction permissible. ### Kennedy

His FDA and U.S. West positions independently preserve statutory text, actual delegation and other legal constraints; no constitutional issue is adjudicated here.

## Full operative limits and remedy

The creation of compensatory time remains subject to §207(o)(1)'s at-least-time-and-one-half rate and (o)(2)'s proper representative or individual prior-agreement route. Moreau's earlier-hire regular-practice route supplies the agreement without a further individual agreement when its actual predicates are met; its later-hire rule applies where the chosen organization cannot lawfully make a binding representative agreement. No general bargaining right or presumption of identical agreements follows. Section 553.23 permits recorded unwritten understandings; express employment conditions require knowing voluntary acceptance and information about preservation, use and cashout. Notice and failure to object can evidence agreement, but coercion and pressure remain forbidden. Existing representative agreements and the transition qualification retain their own terms.

The public-safety, emergency-response and seasonal cap is 480 hours; other covered work has a 240-hour cap. Additional overtime at the applicable cap requires overtime compensation. Cashout may occur at any time at the regular rate when received; termination payment uses not less than the higher of the last-three-years average regular rate and final regular rate. The permanent-break, prior-cashout and shorter-final-period averaging rules remain; no rate calculation or termination dispute is decided.

Employee requests remain protected by reasonable-period use absent undue disruption. Customary scheduling, peak demands, emergency staffing and substitutes inform the reasonable period; compatible agreement terms govern where applicable. Mere inconvenience is insufficient for denial; reasonable good-faith anticipation of an unreasonable burden on acceptable public service is required. Section 553.25(b) still forbids using time to evade statutory overtime and coercing acceptance of more time than the employer can realistically and in good faith make available within a reasonable request period. The rule excludes separately defined other compensatory time, including the identified pre-April 15, 1986 accruals. No violation of these independent safeguards is found. The court-reporter transcript exception has separate per-page and outside-duty-hours conditions and does no work for this Sheriff's Department policy.

The five affirming Associates would leave the Fifth Circuit's judgment for all defendants in force, with the employees' declaration and dependent $21,360 fee award displaced. They would order no further agreement proceedings, new fee award, damages or negotiation. The three reversing Associates would vacate that defense judgment and remand for actual agreement coverage, terms and validity under their consent rule; the categorical declaration and fee award would not automatically revive. Neither route mandates a hearing, presumes classwide agreement terms, adjudicates constitutional property, creates a collective-bargaining right, permits confiscation, or resolves abandoned refusal/retaliation claims.

These are distinct proposition coalitions: five for the statutory/default and no-Auer grounds; four for excluding Chevron on this letter's supported advisory status; Scalia's affirmance does not augment that latter rationale; three for a consent-based agreement remand, with Breyer's independently sufficient deference ground distinguished from Stevens and Ginsburg's statutory ground.
