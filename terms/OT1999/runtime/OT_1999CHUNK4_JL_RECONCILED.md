## Reconciled judgment and rationale

All eight Associates participate and **affirm the Florida Supreme Court's judgment**, leaving suppression in place. Their common full-rationale joins cover:

1. This unknown caller's report of a concealed gun, supported only by prompt confirmation of the described persons, shirt and public location and no further indicium supporting the accusation, did not supply reasonable suspicion for the initial forcible stop and frisk. Accuracy identifying the person is different from adequate reliability of the assertion of concealed illegality. The totality remains the governing inquiry and reasonable suspicion remains less demanding than probable cause.
2. The ordinary gun-safety concern does not excuse that deficiency. Terry permits a protective weapons search on reasonable suspicion when its predicates are met; it does not permit the otherwise unreliable accusation to furnish its own authority merely by naming a gun. Police need not personally see the weapon when sufficiently reliable information supplies the necessary grounds.
3. The weapon's discovery during the frisk does not justify beginning the intrusion. The officer's later view of the gun is not a pre-stop observation.
4. Predictions are one way to support an anonymous report, not a universal requirement. Other concrete indicia may suffice under the totality. This decision establishes no rule for a different imminent threat, accountable eyewitness, school search, airport screening or other setting not presented, and no new limitation on an independently lawful Terry protective search after a valid stop.

These are direct same-level joins to the adopted ground, not a judgment coalition assembled from incompatible reasons. No Marks analysis is needed. No Justice conditions a join on finding the caller dishonest, the officer incredible, every gun unlawful, or all gun possession dangerous. No Justice requires a universal checklist, a probable-cause threshold, mandatory consensual questioning or a new evidentiary hearing.

## Alternative path and result of testing

The strongest reversal path takes the reported scene, specific description, quick confirmation and concealed-gun danger together, allowing reasonable suspicion to arise from less reliable information than probable cause requires. Adams v. Williams establishes that personal observation of the gun is unnecessary. The lower opinions' public-safety objection is substantial: waiting for display or use of a weapon is not always a safe investigative option.

The common affirmance ground answers that argument without demanding display or use. Adams v. Williams had a personally known informant who appeared before the officer and could be held accountable; White's corroboration showed special familiarity with future conduct. Neither authority dispenses with some adequate basis for crediting an unknown concealed-weapon accusation. Matching a publicly observable target remains consistent with fabrication of the asserted illegality. The ordinary gun allegation cannot make its own truth sufficiently likely, and Terry's protective purpose does not eliminate its antecedent requirements. No source correction or concrete changed premise supports moving any Associate to reversal.
