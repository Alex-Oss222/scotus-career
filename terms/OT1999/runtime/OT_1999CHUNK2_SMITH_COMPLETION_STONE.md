## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: California may employ a procedure different from Anders

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone rejects the State's *Teague* defense as an old-rule matter: the protection applied here was dictated by the law existing when Robbins's conviction became final, because *Anders*, *McCoy* and *Penson* required counsel's active advocacy concerning arguably supportive appellate issues. No new procedural template or *Teague* exception is necessary. Stone therefore reaches the merits of the constitutional adequacy of California's procedure; no other threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone does not treat every procedural detail described in *Anders* as an exclusive constitutional form. He nevertheless concludes that *Wende* is constitutionally inadequate because it permits appointed counsel who has found no nonfrivolous issue to file without identifying any issue that might arguably support the appeal, leaving the appellate court and the indigent appellant to perform the issue-spotting function counsel is appointed to perform. On a first appeal as of right, appointed counsel must remain an advocate until the court has a sufficient adversarial basis to determine that the appeal is frivolous. Independent judicial review and an opportunity for a pro se filing do not substitute for counsel's advocate role.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may use a procedure different from the precise *Anders* format, but it may not permit appointed appellate counsel to withhold every arguably supportive issue and shift the search for such issues to the court or the indigent defendant. California's *Wende* procedure therefore fails to provide the meaningful adversarial representation required on a first appeal as of right.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** *Douglas* and *Evitts* protect meaningful counsel on the first appeal a State provides, while *Anders*, *McCoy*, *Penson*, and *Cronic* make the advocate's role substantive rather than nominal. *Wende* preserves a lawyer's presence but allows the lawyer to remain silent about possible appellate issues and asks the reviewing court to search the record without the focused adversarial assistance that counsel is supposed to supply. That arrangement materially increases the risk that an indigent appellant receives judicial screening instead of representation. Stone therefore affirms the Ninth Circuit's judgment on the narrower ground that *Wende* itself fails the constitutional minimum; he does not hold that every State must copy the exact filing, withdrawal, or labeling mechanics of *Anders*. The identified arguable issues make this a substantive failure of representation, not merely a departure from a preferred filing format. Judicial screening and nominal retention of counsel do not replace that protection. Affirm the Ninth Circuit, preserving its remand for Robbins's remaining claims.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A State remains free to adopt a genuinely equivalent alternative that preserves counsel's active advocate role and gives the reviewing court a focused basis for determining frivolousness. Stone does not require verbatim use of the *Anders* procedure or decide the merits of Robbins's remaining habeas claims.
