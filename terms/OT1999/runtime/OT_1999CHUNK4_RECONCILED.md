## Reconciled result and common propositions

All eight Associates retain **affirmance of the Third Circuit's reversal of the municipal dismissal within the two granted questions**, with further ordinary proceedings. Each joins the following rationale in full at its stated scope; this is not merely agreement in the judgment:

**Institution and policy.** The Commonwealth's immunity does not bar these Philadelphia DA office training, supervision and administrative-policy claims. The legal, financial and institutional structure, taken together, establishes the independent local office: local legal status and selection, City fiscal responsibility, and ordinary local control of office administration. The Commonwealth is not the primary judgment obligor. Enforcing State criminal law does not alone make an office the State's arm. This conclusion is sufficient under either the whole-office or relevant-function characterization considered below; the Court need not decide categorically whether an entity can share State immunity for some functions but not others.

For the challenged training and supervision of the office's own prosecutors, the DA speaks finally for the local institution under Pennsylvania law. McMillian does not require delegation by the City Council where an independent local office itself supplies final local authority. Final operational discretion is not by itself attribution, and neither election, a constitutional label, territory nor money alone decides the question. The home-rule/optional-government exception in article IX section 4, Philadelphia consolidation, the narrow agreement in Chalfin, and the state classifications in Specter and Duggan must be retained. The 1980 Commonwealth Attorneys Act's request and judicial-intervention powers do not establish ordinary Attorney General control of personnel training. Its section 205(a)(4) partial-repeal annotation is not adjudicated beyond the structural point supported by the 1999 opinion. No all-functions rule for prosecutors or all-officials rule for Pennsylvania is adopted.

**Fault and cause.** Carter must prove a federal deprivation, an attributable municipal policy or legally effective custom, deliberate indifference in the challenged training or supervision omission, and an identified deficiency closely related to and actually causing his injury. A conscious choice in the face of actual or constructive notice is required. Notice may arise from a sufficiently established pattern or a sufficiently obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in known recurring circumstances; prior identical incidents are not invariably necessary. Negligence, an employee's isolated mistake, mere supervisory responsibility, or hindsight that more training could have prevented injury does not suffice. Perjury's evident wrongfulness does not itself establish an obvious need for specialized instruction; an alleged known pattern of fabrication or related misconduct presents a distinct possible omission theory. No pattern, notice, actual policy, deliberate indifference or causation is found proved here.

**Pleading and remedy.** Leatherman and the operative Rules require ordinary Rule 8/Conley pleading, not heightened particularity concerning internal policies before discovery. The pleaded pattern and policy-related omissions defeat the categorical dismissal grounds at this stage; Rule 11, discovery management and Rule 56 remain available. This is no guarantee of discovery without limits, trial, damages, guilt or innocence, a new prosecution, or particular personnel policies. Any failure-to-control-police theory requires proof of actual legally and factually supported authority and causation; police do not become DA employees merely because the offices cooperate. The exculpatory-evidence allegation remains its own alleged conduct and constitutional duty, not an established fact or a synonym for perjury.

**Distinct defenses.** Municipal responsibility cannot borrow an individual's absolute or qualified immunity. Conversely, a municipal deliberate-indifference allegation does not automatically defeat an individual's conduct-date qualified immunity. Buckley and Kalina require classification of the actual personal function, and otherwise protected advocacy retains immunity despite allegations of dishonesty. These are limits needed to keep the remand accurate, not new judgments on every personal act or personal defense. The lower court's suggestion that deliberate indifference necessarily establishes objective unreasonableness is not adopted.

## Common rule and grounds available for assembly

All eight Associates directly support the following reasoning and limits; none is counted merely from agreement with the judgment.

1. FAA §§9–11 supplement rather than displace otherwise applicable federal venue law. An application to confirm, vacate, modify or correct a domestic award may be brought in the district where the award was made or in another district proper under the general venue statute. This is a venue holding; an independent basis for federal subject-matter jurisdiction and the separate statutory and procedural conditions for relief remain necessary.
2. The word “may” alone does not decide the case. The alternative reading is plausible because §§10–11 identify the award-district court as the actor permitted to grant relief, and §4 elsewhere expressly refers to any otherwise competent district court. ## Five-Associate common constitutional ground

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg directly join every proposition in this section, at this scope.

**Operative rule.** A public university may impose a generally applicable student activity fee to sustain a broad program of independent extracurricular student expression serving its educational mission, including political, ideological and religious viewpoints, when viewpoint neutrality governs eligibility, funding allocation and administration. In that program, disagreement with a recipient's expression does not establish a constitutional right to an ideological opt-out or pro rata refund. The program must actually facilitate independent expression; a selected official ideological campaign does not become such a program merely by receiving an educational label.

The objectors' compelled-financing interest is real. Abood and Keller protect against compelled support for ideological expression outside the duties justifying a union or integrated bar's representative role. Lehnert's particular union chargeability applications do not dictate how a university may sustain the exploration of competing ideas. The common educational function here is the maintenance of opportunities for that exploration, not requiring all students to support a designated representative's preferred political program. Asking courts to identify which ideas are germane to an official educational orthodoxy would frustrate that function. Neither lack of attribution nor shared benefit alone answers the burden; independent student control, the broad educational purpose and viewpoint-neutral administration together provide the justification and protection.

Current Glickman is distinguished in its concrete setting, not overruled or enlarged retrospectively. It establishes that a germane expense of its specified collective economic system can be supported under its own conditions; it also rejects the premise that money, lack of attribution or majority approval alone eliminates speech interests. This case supplies a different and independently examined justification. It does not hold that ideological speech is nonexpressive or that useful programs always permit compelled subsidies.

Current Rosenberger supplies viewpoint equality within its actual independently controlled student-publication benefit, while reserving the compulsory-fee question now presented. That reservation is resolved for this broad educational expression program. No student is required to adopt a recipient's views, join its organization, or refrain from criticism or competing expression. A university may choose an optional or refund arrangement, but the First Amendment does not require one merely because a student opposes particular views in a constitutionally operated program. No empirical finding that all opt-outs are impossible or that every subsidy is indispensable is made.

Viewpoint neutrality does not mean equal dollars for every organization, funding every activity, or abandoning lawful program limits. The University may use reasonable neutral limits defining the activity supported, financial accountability, performance of the funded work and protection against fraud. Neither its educational label nor a majority's preference licenses discrimination among viewpoints within the program. No constitutional campus-boundary rule applies: off-campus location alone does not make an otherwise eligible student expressive activity invalid. Conversely, no unexplained payment to an external campaign is conclusively approved merely because a recipient labels it educational. Any genuinely preserved question concerning a particular expenditure must be addressed on its actual facts and the proper claim.

The ordinary allocation stipulation defeats the categorical liability rule for that scheme on the issues presented. It does not validate the binding referendum, which the parties excluded from the stipulation. If funding or defunding turns on majority approval of a group's viewpoint, the referendum defeats the protection the program requires. Access for minority views cannot depend on majority ideological consent. The described binding treatment must be examined; no neutral override is assumed. Remand is required to determine the referendum's actual operation and the appropriate relief, rather than uphold it unconditionally or declare every conceivable voting procedure unconstitutional.

This rule concerns independent extracurricular expression. It does not subject the University's own curricular choices, faculty instruction, institutional advocacy or government speech to this same forum rule, and it decides no general rule immunizing those activities from constitutional review.

## Reconciled propositions and supported alternatives

**Product-design rule.** In an action for infringement of unregistered product-design trade dress under section 43(a), the claimant must establish secondary meaning: the design's primary significance to the relevant public is to identify source rather than the product itself. Inherent distinctiveness alone is insufficient. Product design remains eligible for protection after acquiring that source significance, provided nonfunctionality, likelihood of confusion, and the other applicable requirements are separately met. Consumer identification of a source does not require knowledge of its name.

The statutory source relationship supplies the rule. An unusual, attractive, or recognizable design can remain the product consumers want rather than an immediately source-identifying sign. A producer's intention to make a recognizable line, a buyer's recognition of its look, and proof of deliberate copying do not turn those different propositions into the necessary acquired-source finding. Source significance, nonfunctionality and confusion cannot substitute for one another. This conclusion neither declares the garments functional nor decides the Second Circuit's separate confusion presumption.

**Packaging and uncertain classification.** Qualifying packaging and the restaurant presentation accepted in *Two Pesos* retain the inherent-distinctiveness route. A court confronting genuinely ambiguous dress should treat it as product design and require secondary meaning. This caution applies when the distinction between the product and its packaging cannot reliably be made; it does not deem every unusual package inherently distinctive or resolve a particular bottle, container, or other hybrid absent its record. The garments here are product design, so no borderline classification is needed to dispose of this case.

This limited classification guidance is the only clarification of scope beyond the independent handoff.
