## Record, enactment and lower judgment

Pap's operated Kandyland at 1402 State Street in Erie's commercial area, offering nude dancing. The parties agreed at the October 19, 1994 hearing that the business did not serve alcohol but allowed customers to bring it. The relevant joint-appendix portion records those agreed facts and the entire transcribed Council video admitted at that hearing. A claim that the club itself caused prostitution, violence or other identified crime is not established merely by those agreed facts.

Council enacted Ordinance 75-1994 on September 28, 1994; the hearing stipulation states that the mayor signed September 30. The state opinion describes October 12 as the effective date; the contemporaneous hearing stipulation describes October 20. This source conflict is retained. No prosecution, penalty or retroactive liability during that interval is presented, so neither date needs to be selected to decide the current prospective challenge. If a proposed remedy makes that interval material, obtain the original signed instrument or authoritative effective-date record first.

The [record-extract annex](../entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK4_ERIE_RECORD_EXTRACTS.md) script-copies the lower court's operative ordinance quotation and preamble, the agreed facts and Council transcript, and the sanitized closure materials. The challenged §1(c) makes knowingly or intentionally appearing in a state of nudity in a public place a summary public-indecency offense. Section 2 defines nudity through insufficiently opaque covering of genitals, pubic area or buttocks, exposure of the female nipple, and specified simulated anatomy or realistic nipple/areola coverings. These are the actual definitional components, not a generic ban on all erotic conduct. Section 3 includes outdoor and enclosed public places, entertainment premises, taverns, restaurants, clubs, theaters, dance halls, banquet/party halls, and premises limited to members, adults or invited patrons, whether admission is charged. Section 4 exempts children under ten and breast exposure while nursing an infant under two. Those precise ages and the breastfeeding condition must not be replaced by general child or nursing exceptions.

Section 6 calls for construction and enforcement causing the least possible infringement of enumerated constitutional rights consistent with the ordinance's purposes and provides severance of an unconstitutional portion, specific application or enforcement. It is not itself an express theatrical-production exemption. Other provisions separately prohibit public sexual intercourse, deviate sexual intercourse as defined by Pennsylvania law, and genital fondling. The state judgment leaves those three prohibitions intact.

The preamble identifies limiting a recent increase in nude live entertainment associated with violence, harassment, intoxication, prostitution, sexually transmitted disease and other harmful effects. The Council video is mixed evidence, not a single stipulated purpose. Sponsor Thompson referred both to a longstanding indecency tradition and to erotic entertainment, said theater/art was protected, and described indecent or immoral nudity as the target. Brabender discussed public values, school swimming and young patrons among drinking adults. Bagnoni invoked enforcement, residential/church proximity, broader crime/morality concerns and the modest clothing requirement. Maras argued that adult entertainment could remain financially viable with minimal covering. Opponents described a zoning issue and concerns about judging private choices. These are recorded statements, not verified crime statistics, a legislative finding of causation at Kandyland, or a binding enforcement exception. The parties disputed Pap's offered characterization of the enactment as solely moral/religious; the City did not stipulate to it.

Pap's sought declaratory/injunctive relief and attorney's fees. The trial court invalidated the ordinance for facial overbreadth, enjoined enforcement and denied fees. The Commonwealth Court reversed the constitutional ruling. The Pennsylvania Supreme Court then held the nudity restriction content based because its secondary-effects justification was intertwined with suppressing the erotic message; it applied strict scrutiny, found the City's strict-scrutiny presentation lacking, and independently found no narrow fit between requiring pasties/G-strings and preventing sex crimes. It suggested more focused conduct, distance, hours or zoning responses. That account describes what the **state court** decided; it establishes neither a Supreme Court briefing default nor that any hypothetical replacement rule has been approved.

The Pennsylvania Supreme Court did **not** merely issue an as-applied ruling and did not invalidate the entire ordinance. It severed §§1(c) and 2, leaving the other prohibitions. It rejected judicial addition of language exempting expressive nudity. Its majority did not reach the Pennsylvania Constitution, substantial overbreadth, or the standing objection to overbreadth. Justice Castille, joined by Justice Zappala, agreed in the result on independent Pennsylvania free-expression grounds and believed *Barnes* required rejection of the federal challenge. That concurrence is not a controlling independent state ground of the majority's judgment. The state constitutional claim remains available for the state court if a federal reversal calls for further proceedings; this Court may not decide it as federal law.

## Continuing controversy and reachability

The clean [objective-facts handoff](../preparation/OT_1999CHUNK4_ERIE_OBJECTIVE_FACTS.md) records Pap's June 3, 1999 motion to dismiss as moot, its representation that it retained no interest in these premises or another nude-dancing establishment, closure/property sale in 1998 as recited in the published source, a September 8 affidavit reporting cessation, and the owner's asserted retirement at age seventy-two. The City represented that Pap's remained incorporated; continuing corporate existence is also supplied in the original packet. These are supported filings and party representations, not a judicial finding that recurrence is impossible or a proved plan to reopen. No strategic manipulation finding, substitution of parties, damages entitlement, continuing enforcement violation or new establishment is supplied.

Simulated *Arizonans for Official English* requires a live personal controversy throughout review; voluntary departure without intent to return, realistic recurrence or a surviving claim may moot a prospective challenge. Its unavailable State/official-capacity §1983 damages holding does not automatically decide remedies against a municipality. Here the supplied complaint sought injunction, declaration and fees, not an established surviving damages award. Fees alone do not establish a live merits controversy. A possible future claim cannot be manufactured to preserve jurisdiction.

Current-term *Laidlaw*, January 12, 2000, requires it to be absolutely clear that challenged injurious conduct cannot reasonably recur before a defendant's voluntary cessation moots a live case, and requires actual factual assessment of closure and retained authority. Its pollution-permit and penalty setting differs from a prevailing challenger ceasing business while the City continues to defend an enactment. The model must explain the relevant cessation actor, continuing adverse effect of the judgment, and whether realistic recurrence or another concrete interest remains; corporate existence alone does not establish a reopening plan, while a party's assurance alone is not automatically conclusive. General interest in adjudicating an ordinance is not a substitute for Article III.

If the dispute is moot, merits adjudication stops. *Bancorp* and *Arizonans* require an equitable decision about the judgment and proceedings under §2106; vacatur is not automatic, and a prevailing party's unilateral cessation differs from voluntary settlement or abandonment of review by the loser. A threshold remand for a genuinely material unresolved fact is a lawful possibility, but there is no general command to hold a new hearing whenever an original affidavit is unavailable. The record supports consideration of both continued jurisdiction and mootness; no threshold result is selected here.

## Law entering the merits

The [entering-law slice](../entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK4_ERIE_ENTERING_LAW.md) copies controlling *Ino Ino*, *Forsyth*, *Arizonans*, current *Laidlaw* public law/positions and the operative Standards. Those Standards are **Visibility of protected nonobscene cabaret expression**, effective September 28, 1998; **Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages**, effective March 3, 1997; and **Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review**, effective November 8, 1994. Their exact triggers and limits are copied. No blanket “no additional test” assertion survives from the compact original packet.

*O'Brien* requires governmental power, an important/substantial interest, an interest unrelated to suppressing expression, and an incidental burden no greater than essential to further that interest. *Ward* preserves real tailoring and available channels without a universal least-restrictive-means test. *Renton* permits reliance on relevant experience elsewhere concerning noncommunicative secondary effects; it does not convert moral or audience disagreement with a message into a neutral effect. *Forsyth*'s independently sufficient audience-hostility holding rejects a content-based burden disguised as secondary-effect regulation. Its demonstration-fee facts are not identical to an adult-business regulation, so the causal and legal analogy requires explanation.

The surviving June 21, 1991 *Barnes* decision has four distinct writings, copied from their primary pages in [Barnes reading copy](../entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK4_ERIE_BARNES.md). The Rehnquist plurality, joined by O'Connor and Kennedy, applies *O'Brien* to Indiana's general public-nudity prohibition and treats public order/morality and a minimal covering requirement as sufficient. Scalia's judgment concurrence relies on a law general as to expressive and nonexpressive conduct, not a categorical assertion that expressive performance can never receive protection; he expressly identifies targeting expressive nudity in practice as a materially different issue. Souter's judgment concurrence relies on crime-related secondary effects, permits relevant nonlocal experience and a current justification without proof that it historically motivated the legislature, and regards minimal covering as a limited burden. His footnote reserves other theatrical contexts not readily analogous to *Renton* establishments without supporting effects evidence. White's dissent, joined by Stevens, Marshall and Blackmun, treats suppression of the communicative element as the purpose in consenting-adult venues and rejects the asserted fit. No single rationale has five direct joins. A proposed *Marks* rule must show an actual logical subset and the applicable factual predicates, not count any fifth judgment vote as a rationale join.

Later simulated law is particularly important: **Ino Ino v. Bellevue**, September 28, 1998, unanimously applies expressive-conduct scrutiny to actual cabaret spacing, stage, lighting and hours limits. It rejects a rational-basis-only treatment of the specific stage-dancing restriction. Kennedy's Court opinion distinguishes real contact-prevention/detection effects and supported fit from hostility to erotic communication, preserves meaningful expression and requires the actual scope of each provision. All eight Associates also join its invalidation of the separate outdoor-visibility rule insofar as it suppresses protected nonobscene performance/images merely because anatomy is visible. It creates neither a general invalidity rule for public-nudity laws nor an unqualified morality-only defense. A model invoking Scalia's older *Barnes* theory must account for his *Ino Ino* join and identify an actual general-law distinction, not ignore the later case.

*Ino Ino* also preserves independent state-law protection, provision-specific severability, the distinct substantial-overbreadth test and authoritative narrowing constructions. A narrowing or exception in one provision cannot be imported into another. Bellevue's precise operational rules do not establish a national distance or clothing safe harbor. Here the issue concerns total nudity in expressive performance, not Bellevue's fixed separation while nude performance continued. Those differences bear on both scrutiny and tailoring.

## Supported alternative paths

These are §14(4) analytical alternatives, not fabricated assertions that a current Supreme Court brief adopted them.

* Reach the merits only after establishing a continuing controversy and a reviewable federal basis. The federal majority's reasoning is reviewable; the state concurrence alone does not foreclose review. Preserve unreached state constitutional and overbreadth questions for the proper court and stage.
* Sustain the general-law application under *Barnes*'s supported rationale or *O'Brien* if the ordinance's actual operation and justification are unrelated to communicative suppression and its minimal covering requirement sufficiently furthers supported harms while preserving erotic performance. Answer the pointed Council statements, contemporary enactment purpose, absence of an enacted theatrical exception, and the distinct obligations *Ino Ino* imposes. Neither a provocative purpose remark alone nor an appealing preamble alone decides that inquiry.
* Treat the application as content based if the operative purpose or selection targets the erotic communication rather than a separable harm. Then apply the actual compelling-interest/narrow-tailoring burden; the small amount of cloth does not itself answer whether the suppressed component is protected. Explain why the causal theory differs from permissible *Renton* effects and why the given record and alternatives suffice for the disposition.
* Apply intermediate scrutiny yet reject the fit or evidentiary justification, or remand for consideration under the correct legal framework where existing findings leave a material issue undecided. Identify the missing **decision**, not simply a research document. No automatic demand for local studies, guarantee of profit, prohibition of all cabaret regulation or presumption of litigant default follows. Relevant outside experience, record-supported inference, adverse evidence and practical effect must receive their proper roles.
* Affirmance on a federal ground must specify whether and why the state's severance remains the proper remedy. Federal reversal ordinarily returns the unreached independent state and overbreadth questions without adjudicating them, restoring the whole ordinance unconditionally, ordering a replacement law, imposing a fine, or finding a crime. Mootness has its own equitable disposition, independent of all those merits routes.
