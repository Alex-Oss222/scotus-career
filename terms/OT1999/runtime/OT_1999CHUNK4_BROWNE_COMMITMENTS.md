## Components, governing limits and lawful alternatives

1. **Paid section 6651(a)(2) addition:** RFRA challenge to the failure-to-pay addition, in the ordinary paid-charge refund branch. The Brownes filed timely returns. The addition's payment-enforcement function, not Adams's failure-to-file rationale, is at issue. Its Code exception requires both reasonable cause and absence of willful neglect. The regulation's inability-to-pay or undue-hardship rule does not itself confer a religious waiver; failure to meet that Code rule does not itself dispose of RFRA.
2. **Paid section 6654 addition:** RFRA challenge to the estimated-tax addition, separately in the paid-charge refund branch. Its amount-and-period relationship concerns installment timeliness. Paragraph (e)(3)(A) concerns casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstances making the addition contrary to equity and good conscience to the extent the Secretary determines; it is not a general reasonable-cause exception. Paragraph (e)(3)(B) additionally requires retirement after age 62 or disability in the relevant taxable year or its predecessor, plus reasonable cause and no willful neglect. The supplied retirement date does not establish that branch. No charge is calculated here.
3. **Interest:** The original section 6404 discretionary-abatement theory must be distinguished from the asserted independent RFRA refund theory. The original ministerial-act provision governs these 1993–95 liabilities, including written-contact and no-significant-taxpayer-contribution qualifications. The later managerial-act expansion does not govern them. The separate judicial-review enactment applies to post-July-30-1996 requests, has claimant-eligibility and 180-day conditions, and does not by its terms require an ordinary deficiency petition. No original request date is established by the date a form was signed. No Tax Court filing, tolling, transfer entitlement or new filing window is invented. Section 6601 interest and section 6654 remain separate; section 6601(h) excludes estimated-tax failure from its operation.
4. **Prospective collection/declaratory relief:** A demand for prompt levy without ordinary charges presents separate standing, statutory-remedy and forum questions. RFRA's coverage and appropriate-relief phrase do not automatically resolve section 7421, section 2201, sovereign immunity or the proper remedial channel. A lower merits judgment does not establish those matters by silence.
5. **Other original claims and common reservations:** The independent constitutional and Code merits were not separately granted. Their actual status is preserved without importing Adams's differently reasoned independent lower holdings. A necessary jurisdictional inquiry is distinct from expanding the grant. Federal RFRA's constitutional validity, disputed substantial burden, amount calculations, selective enforcement and particular waiver eligibility remain undecided.

For the two paid additions the principal alternatives are: **RFRA vacatur**, because the lower courts did not perform the application-specific inquiry and cannot resolve genuinely material operational facts against the pleadings; or **application-specific affirmance**, because the accepted proposal itself retains late payment and requires compulsory Government collection, making the actual timing consequences legally sufficient without invented cost findings. Neither path may rely on a tax-wide RFRA exclusion or on principal liability as the sufficient ground for every charge.

For interest and prospective relief the principal alternatives are: **threshold remand**, allowing classification of claims and resolution of the preserved forum/remedy questions before contested merits; or **final disposition now**, through a fully supported forum construction or, where lawful jurisdiction is established, an application-specific merits decision. Merits simplicity alone is not a reason to assume adjudicatory power. Conversely, Steel Co. is not a holding that every disputed statutory issue is Article III jurisdiction or that all thresholds have a mandatory universal order. Ruhrgas preserves its bounded nonmerits sequencing rule.

Adams governs the RFRA burden sequence: the claimants establish substantial burden; the Government then bears production and persuasion on compelling interest and least restrictive means in the actual application. For this pleadings review substantial burden may be assumed without being found. No Justice below would make the taxpayers prove an alternative's feasibility as a universal threshold, infer an authenticated oral concession, treat available assets as completed payment, presume an alternative impossible, or command an evidentiary hearing merely because religion is asserted. None would award a direct refund or compulsory-levy order on this record.

## Handoff limitations and checks

- The ordinary paid-addition refund branch has no independently established jurisdictional defect in the frozen packet. The Government's preserved objections concern the interest-abatement route and prospective relief. The paid-addition commitments do not establish any unproved filing prerequisite or decide a new construction of section 1346. If a material actual defect in that branch is subsequently established, return it to fresh affected modeling rather than applying these merits commitments across the defect.
- Every interest and prospective-relief commitment is a present threshold-remand position, not a conditional merits vote. No Justice is counted as supporting an interest compensation holding, an exclusive Tax Court holding, an injunction bar holding, or a substantive right to prompt levy.
- The proposed remand returns to the Second Circuit for further proceedings consistent with the bounded decision. It creates no new Tax Court period, transfer order, tax cancellation, suspension, refund, automatic evidentiary hearing or IRS production obligation. Ordinary proceedings may resolve genuinely material questions; adequate legal reasons may suffice without new evidence.
- The independent Code and constitutional questions retain their actual procedural status. Nothing asserts that Browne already has the separately reasoned Code determinations present in Adams. A necessary forum correction remains separate from deciding those independent merits.
- The preserved narrower post-contact-delay contention is not silently replaced with the blanket-waiver request. The RFRA-remand positions leave its actual legal and factual significance for proper adjudication. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer reject that RFRA contention as well as the blanket request: even accepting the alleged opportunity for earlier levy, the Government had not received payment and the taxpayers sought to replace the ordinary payment duty with compulsory collection initiated by the IRS. For these Justices that actual legal difference justifies retaining the ordinary payment addition during the unpaid period without a finding of additional expense; they do not find every asserted delay unavoidable. Their affirmance does not resolve a separate proven calculation error, ministerial-delay entitlement or other ungranted Code claim.
- No factual finding of equal cost, practical impossibility, guaranteed timely seizure, waiver of levy protections, authenticated concession, adverse motive or source-based litigant default is made.
- These are provisional individual commitments. Historical reconciliation must test them before assembly. No final opinion author, final join, Court vote count or current law is established by this file.
