## Operative rules and applications

The five-Associate temporal rule applies amended section 2253 to an applicant's appeal initiated after April 24, 1996, even though the identified pre-AEDPA law governs district adjudication of the earlier application. Its reason is the distinct appellate event regulated, not a finding that every amendment or claim first appearing after enactment creates a new case. Hohn permits review of this denial but did not decide this transition. The State's earlier legal position cannot supply a missing certificate under the governing statute; no general holding makes every subsection jurisdictional or excuses every forfeiture. The proper request may be considered despite its CPC label, and Rule 22 treats the notice as a request where appropriate.

When the district court dismisses on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, a COA requires reasonable debate both about whether the petition states a valid denial-of-constitutional-right claim and about the correctness of the procedural ruling. Both are threshold showings. Final merits success is unnecessary, but procedural error alone or a bare constitutional label is insufficient. A plain and correctly applied procedural bar can defeat certification; a court may begin with the more apparent component without being required to decide every constitutional question first or every procedural question first. For an actual merits rejection, reasonable debate or error in the constitutional assessment supplies the corresponding standard. A certificate must identify qualifying issues. Review of a denial and an unaccompanied notice do not themselves issue that certificate; Peguero's actually issued authorization is a different posture.

The six-Associate classification rule treats a renewed application after this without-prejudice exhaustion dismissal, before any federal merits adjudication, as a first application for second-or-successive purposes, including additional claims. Rule 9(b)'s opening category must be satisfied before its new-ground branch applies. Rose's complete-exhaustion procedure supports a return for first consideration; former section 2244(b)'s prior-merits setting and Lonchar's insistence on lawful predicates reinforce the construction. Stewart's actually presented, premature Ford claim is a limited analogy. This decision supplies the present exhaustion-return rule; it does not pretend Stewart already resolved every nonmerits dismissal or omitted claim.

Scalia and Thomas read Rule 9(b)'s new-ground branch as retaining independent work despite the earlier nonmerits dismissal. The actual State pleading identified the omitted grounds and the District Court required the recognized cause/prejudice or miscarriage response; Slack challenged classification instead. Their disagreement concerns the legal availability of that abuse route here, not a newly found deliberate-withholding fact or the merits of those constitutional claims. The return with additional unexhausted claims distinguishes their permitted renewal of the original claims after exhaustion. Their support for both certificate showings remains; their legal conclusion supplies the procedural reason for refusing a certificate. Counting dissenting judges does not substitute for the reasonable-debate inquiry.

Other lawful controls remain on either account: proper state procedural bars, genuinely applicable statutory restrictions, ordinary exhaustion, lawful pleading/amendment requirements and source-authorized case management. The Court may recognize a properly communicated, lawful return condition and its consequences without finding one violated here. The original without-prejudice dismissal and permission to renew supply no basis for a new finding of intentional disobedience or sanction. A lawful dismissal permission does not become an automatic command, and no free-floating equitable sanction replaces governing predicates. Rule 9(a)'s prejudicial-delay inquiry remains distinct; no such delay finding is made.
