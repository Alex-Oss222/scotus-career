* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment insofar as it sustains the additional supervised-release term.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Retroactivity of § 3583(h)

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches retroactivity because the Government invokes a statute enacted after the original offense.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 3583(h) cannot govern if it authorizes a punishment unavailable under former law. Postrevocation sanctions are attributable to the original offense.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 3583(h) may not be applied to an offense committed before its enactment when doing so increases the authorized postrevocation punishment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The violation triggers the later proceeding, but the sovereign’s authority to punish derives from the original conviction. A later statute cannot add a new custodial or supervisory consequence to that offense.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not disturb prospective application of § 3583(h) or decide retroactivity where the later statute is genuinely ameliorative.
### Issue 2: Authority under former § 3583(e)(3)

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the alternative ground because it is necessary to determine whether the same sentence was authorized without § 3583(h).
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Former § 3583(e)(3) authorized revocation and imprisonment, not creation of a new supervised-release term after the original term was revoked.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Under former § 3583(e)(3), a court may revoke supervised release and impose the authorized imprisonment, but it may not then impose a new term of supervised release.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** “Revoke” ends the supervised-release term. The provision then specifies imprisonment as the consequence. It contains no language authorizing another term, while Congress later added that authority expressly in § 3583(h). Criminal sentencing cannot be enlarged through an implication that the later enactment makes unnecessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not limit modification or early termination of an existing term under other subsections, or the authority Congress supplied prospectively in § 3583(h).
