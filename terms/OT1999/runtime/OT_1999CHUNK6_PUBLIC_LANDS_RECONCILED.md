## I. Preference and permitted use

The Secretary may define grazing preference as priority against competing applicants attached to owned or controlled base property and express the forage allocation separately as permitted use. The Taylor Act's direction adequately to safeguard recognized grazing privileges, so far as consistent with the Act, continues to bind administration; the definition does not create an immutable forage quantity or erase that safeguard.

Section 2 authorizes protection, regulation, improvement, rules and cooperative administration of the range. Section 3 leaves stock numbers and seasons to management, limits the permit's duration, and expressly denies that a permit creates an estate in the public land. Those provisions coexist with recognized grazing privileges, water rights and renewal protections. A compliant permittee's renewal cannot be denied where denial would impair the value of a grazing unit pledged as security for a bona fide loan. The no-estate clause does not nullify that condition or an independently existing private right.

The strongest challenge is that nominal priority emptied of useful forage would fail to safeguard actual privileges. All eight accept that legal limit. They reject the assertion that the definitional separation itself accomplishes that deprivation. The agency's representation that existing active/suspended-use priority remains supports this facial assessment, but cannot excuse a later contradictory deprivation. No future allocation is presumed lawful merely because the definitions survive.

The connected rule conditions remain in their own terms:

- Permitted use is specified for preference holders. The ordinary rule includes authorized livestock use and suspended use. For designated ephemeral ranges where use is authorized according to forage availability, and designated annual ranges, the rule preserves its different treatment; a land-use or activity plan may prescribe vegetation standards rather than the ordinary forage-allocation measure. The quoted amendment's conservation-use references do not restore the separately invalidated conservation-use provision.
- Changes in permitted use require support from monitoring, field observations, ecological inventory or other acceptable data. Where the regulatory conditions require reduced use or changed management, the ordinary implementation route requires consultation, cooperation and coordination with the affected permittee or lessee, the relevant State and interested public, followed by documented agreement or a proposed decision.
- If soil, vegetation or other resources need immediate protection because of conditions such as drought, fire, flood or insect infestation, or continued grazing presents an imminent likelihood of significant resource damage, the separate immediate-protection rule applies. After consultation or a reasonable attempt to consult the specified participants, the officer must close the relevant area or modify use; the notice may be a final decision effective when issued or on its specified date. It remains effective during appeal unless the Office of Hearings and Appeals grants a stay under 43 C.F.R. §4.21.

These are existing safeguards and distinct procedures, not a new judicial process or universal factual checklist. Their preservation defeats the proposed reading of boundless quantity discretion without deciding a particular reduction, land-use plan or APA record challenge.

## II. Commercial occupation, statutory eligibility and actual use

Congress did not make engagement in a commercial livestock business a universal condition for every eligible grazing applicant. Section 3 separately authorizes permits for bona fide settlers, residents and other stock owners who qualify under the regulations, and specifies preference for nearby landowners engaged in the livestock business, bona fide occupants or settlers, and owners of water or water rights where necessary for proper use of their property. Deleting the commercial-business condition does not delete those statutory categories or equate noncommercial stock ownership with ownership of no stock.

The statute's separate citizenship and entity requirements remain: permits are limited to United States citizens, persons who have filed the required declarations of intention, and groups, associations or corporations authorized to conduct business under the relevant State's law. Regulatory base-property and other lawful qualifications likewise remain, with no particular applicant adjudged entitled to a permit or an exception. The preserved acquired-land provision applies when an acquisition agreement, Act or Executive Order requires BLM to honor existing permits or leases; those instruments then retain their acquisition-date terms rather than receiving an invented qualification exemption in this case.

The challengers' strongest concern is that deleting commercial occupation could permit acquisition and permanent nonuse of grazing rights to eliminate grazing. Actual grazing and commercial occupation are different conditions. The retained use/nonuse regime answers that asserted necessary consequence without deciding every conceivable arrangement:

- If substantial use as authorized has not occurred for two consecutive grazing fee years, **or** water base property has not been maintained or used in the grazing operation, the officer **may**, after consultation, coordination and cooperation with the permittee or lessee and any lienholder of record, cancel the amount of permitted use not made. This is discretionary cancellation, not an automatic forfeiture. The two-year phrase is not silently added as a new condition to the separately worded water-base ground.
- Temporary nonuse may be approved only in conformity with the applicable land-use, allotment-management or other activity plan and the rangeland-health rules. It requires stated reasons and annual approval and may last no more than three consecutive years; reasons may include financial circumstances or annual livestock fluctuations. Temporarily available forage may be offered to qualified others under the governing procedure.
- Those temporary provisions are distinct from the conservation-use rule that the Secretary did not bring before the Court. The decision does not grant a no-stock organization a conservation-only permit, validate a ten-year conservation nonuse entitlement or restore that excluded rule by reading its terms into these holdings.

The common holding is the permissibility of deleting the commercial-business prerequisite, not a new comprehensive adjudication of eligibility, nonuse, cancellation or the terms of an actual lease.

## III. Prospective title under cooperative improvement agreements

Sections 2 and 4 authorize cooperative arrangements for range improvements. Within that authority, the Secretary may specify federal title from the outset for future permanent improvements authorized after August 21, 1995, subject to valid existing rights and the rule's contribution protections. Section 4's protection of improvements already constructed and privately owned does not command private ownership of every improvement under a later cooperative arrangement.

The strongest contrary argument invokes the protection of private investment and the absence of confiscation authority. All eight preserve that protection. The challenged title rule concerns prospective terms, not appropriation of an existing improvement merely because it stands on public land. Section 4 still requires reasonable-value payment before a later permit entitles its holder to use improvements constructed and owned by a prior occupant, and State partition-fence cost and maintenance law remains applicable. No existing title, taking, valuation, reliance injury or compensation claim is decided here.

The regulation's operative qualifications remain:

- Permanent improvements covered by the prospective cooperative title rule include such structures as fences, wells and pipelines. All new permanent water developments must use cooperative agreements. Each agreement must specify the division of cost or labor, or both, between the United States and cooperators.
- BLM must document the permittee's or lessee's contributed funds, labor and materials for the credits governed by §§4120.3-5 and 4120.3-6(c). This preservation of the contribution requirement is not an adjudication of a credit formula or an award in this case.
- Nonstructural improvements are federally titled. A permittee or lessee **may** hold title to authorized removable livestock-handling improvements and temporary structural improvements under the separate range-improvement permit provision; private title is not declared compulsory for every such improvement.
- Performing work does not confer an exclusive right to use the improvement or affected land. Where temporary forage use by another operator is lawfully authorized under the connected rule, the existing operator's reasonable use/maintenance compensation and fair-share procedures remain; the officer may mediate and determine the applicable share after consultation, and an unresolved settlement proceeds through a temporary authorization containing the compensation requirement. This is no individual award or validation of an excluded conservation-use predicate.

Prospective ownership terms do not authorize indefinite permit reopening or override valid existing rights. Mining's express authority to correct mining permits supplies no such Taylor Act power. Its distinction between actual future regulation and new consequences for completed conduct supports keeping these situations separate; it does not determine a concrete grazing title or compensation dispute.
