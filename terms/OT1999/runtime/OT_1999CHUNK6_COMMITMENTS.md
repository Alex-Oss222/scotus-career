## Common scope and the disputed rule

All eight Associates participate and reach the presented federal question. Counts 7–10 concern the fourteen- or fifteen-year-old complainant and offenses from June 1992 through July 1993. Under the offense-date rule, uncorroborated testimony was sufficient only following a report to another person within six months; the exception for complainants younger than fourteen did not apply. The amendment effective September 1, 1993 extended the age exception to those younger than eighteen and the report period to one year. The March 1995 report satisfies neither period for these counts. Thus the age-exception change, rather than selection between the two report periods, presents the operative retrospective issue. The six younger-age counts and five post-amendment counts, including the life sentences, remain outside this challenge.

The lack of corroboration is assumed for the legal question; it is not finally established. Texas preserves its contention that actual corroboration supported the convictions. Every Associate declining the amendment's retrospective use therefore supports a remand for that existing-record issue under the older law, not an immediate acquittal based on an invented evidentiary finding. If the record fails the older sufficiency requirement, the stated state-law consequence is acquittal on the affected counts, not a new opportunity to add prosecution proof. No blanket new-trial order or release from unaffected sentences follows.

Two supported paths remain. The first treats removal of the corroboration condition as a reduction in legally sufficient proof, within Calder's fourth category as preserved and bounded by Collins, Hopt and Thompson. The second treats the amendment as removal of a witness-specific credibility restriction comparable to the permissible competence and admissibility changes in Hopt and Thompson, leaving ultimate facts and proof beyond reasonable doubt unchanged. Neither path may rest simply on procedural labels, disadvantage, the worth of the former policy, or the continuing legality of the underlying conduct.

## Individual commitments and enhanced comparison

### Stevens

**Result:** Reverse the constitutional ruling sustaining retrospective application to counts 7–10 and remand for the preserved corroboration issue under offense-date Article 38.07.

**Ground:** The legislature may change ordinary trial methods, but may not retrospectively make the complainant's testimony alone sufficient when the former law made additional proof indispensable absent its reporting or age conditions. The witness was already competent and the testimony admissible; what changes is the legal sufficiency of the prosecution's existing proof.

**Authority and individual support:** His Collins concurrence, 497 U.S. 37, 52–61, distinguishes a new corrective remedy for an already valid conviction from a change in safeguards used to obtain a valid conviction. His Morales join and Garner separate position protect the actual operation of an existing legal safeguard rather than the mere hope of a favorable outcome. Those parole positions do not independently decide trial proof; Calder's fourth category and Hopt's sufficiency reservation supply the necessary trial-law ground here.

**Adversarial comparison:** The competence analogy is strongest because the corroboration rule expresses distrust of a particular class of testimony. It fails for him at the legal operation: the same admitted testimony that previously required acquittal can now support conviction without the formerly indispensable additional evidence. Thompson adds an admissible comparison source and leaves evaluation to the jury; it does not remove an independently prescribed minimum basis for conviction. The contrary concern that any useful evidentiary reform could be frozen is answered by this distinction, not by denying the validity of Hopt or Thompson.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** He can join a bounded Calder/Hopt sufficiency rule without reviving the substantial-protection formulation rejected by the Collins majority. His broader published concern may explain a concurrence, but is not necessary to the judgment. He rejects both automatic acquittal before the corroboration issue is resolved and a prosecutorial repair trial.

### O'Connor

**Result:** Affirm the rejection of this Ex Post Facto challenge. No remand to test offense-date corroboration is required by that federal ruling.

**Ground:** The amendment removes a special credibility disability associated with the complainant's age and delayed report; it does not alter the offense's ultimate facts, the jury's reasonable-doubt burden or the lawful punishment. Hopt and Thompson permit retrospective changes in the evidentiary means by which the unchanged facts may be proved.

**Authority and individual support:** She joined Collins's Court opinion, including its refusal to treat every substantial procedural disadvantage as forbidden and its express retention of ordinary evidentiary changes. Her Morales Court opinion and Garner Court opinion require a concrete effect within the Clause's punishment protection; they do not constitutionalize every former procedural advantage. Her trial-proof conclusion is an application of Collins and Hopt, not a transfer of a parole vote.

**Adversarial comparison:** The defendant's strongest point is that Texas labels failure to corroborate legal insufficiency and requires acquittal. She treats that remedy as evidence of the rule's force but not as controlling its federal constitutional character: restrictions on testimony can be outcome-determinative without defining the offense-date amount of proof in the constitutional sense. The age and report alternatives regulate a source's reliability, while guilt still requires persuasion beyond reasonable doubt. An actual reduction in that burden or a change to ultimate facts would present a different case.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** She will not join a categorical procedural exemption or deny that some laws described as evidence rules fall within Calder. She also will not infer actual corroboration or decide an unpresented state-law claim. A contrary majority's remand must preserve Texas's contention and confine any acquittal consequence to the challenged counts.

### Scalia

**Result:** Reverse the adverse federal ruling on counts 7–10 and remand for the preserved existing-record corroboration question.

**Ground:** Calder's fourth category, expressly recounted in Collins, remains distinct from a generic prohibition on disadvantage. A law that removes a required evidentiary minimum permits conviction with less proof; the exception for ordinary competence or admissibility changes does not erase that category.

**Authority and individual support:** His Collins majority join includes both its limited account of the Clause and its Calder/Hopt qualifications. His Garner join demonstrates adherence to the operative controlling boundary without manufacturing an actual-outcome requirement; it supplies no separate trial-proof rule. Hopt, 110 U.S. 574, 588–590, expressly leaves the required amount or degree of proof untouched when permitting the new witness. Thompson, 171 U.S. 380, 386–388, likewise distinguishes admitting new comparison evidence from reducing required proof.

**Adversarial comparison:** Texas's strongest argument is that its rule regulates only credibility and the formal reasonable-doubt standard survives. For him, the statute's legal command is decisive: without the timely report or age exception, testimony alone cannot establish guilt even if the jury believes it. Removing that command changes legal sufficiency, not merely admissible sources. Limiting the fourth category to a changed verbal burden would leave its evidence language without independent work.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** No general substantial-rights test, no restoration of Kring or Thompson v. Utah, and no constitutional rule preserving every witness restriction. He supports the existing-record remand; a rule requiring proof that corroboration would have changed the jury's mind would mistake the statutory sufficiency requirement.

### Kennedy

**Result:** Affirm the rejection of the presented federal challenge.

**Ground:** Article 38.07's age/report conditions are witness-specific reliability restrictions. Their removal operates like the competence restriction in Hopt and the exclusion in Thompson rather than a lowered element or burden of persuasion. The Ex Post Facto Clause protects defined crime, punishment and constitutionally relevant proof requirements, not every evidentiary advantage existing at the offense date.

**Authority and individual support:** His Collins majority join rejects a free-standing substantial-protection category while retaining Calder's finite categories. His Garner join preserves the binding Morales opportunity rule on its concrete punishment premise, illustrating why a procedural rule's function matters. Hopt and Thompson provide the decisive trial-method analogy; no own earlier published ruling on this precise Texas corroboration provision is attributed to him.

**Adversarial comparison:** The claimant's strongest response is the acquittal consequence under Texas law. Kennedy does not dismiss it as harmless, but concludes that an evidentiary exclusion can equally foreclose a conviction and still be changed under Thompson. Treating the State's label or chosen appellate remedy as dispositive would make equivalent credibility rules constitutionally different according to drafting form. He reserves a true reduction in proof of the charged facts or in the degree of persuasion.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** No claim that every procedural amendment escapes scrutiny; no finding the preserved corroboration contention has been proved; no use of unaffected sentences to dismiss the challenge as moot. He could accept the limited remand mechanics if the Court rejects his merits view without joining the invalidation rationale.

### Souter

**Result:** Reverse the constitutional ruling for counts 7–10 and remand under the offense-date requirement.

**Ground:** Removing the need for corroboration under these age and report predicates withdraws a legally required minimum evidentiary basis for guilt. The distinction is between admitting a source that a jury may evaluate and declaring already admissible proof legally sufficient without the additional evidence previously required.

**Authority and individual support:** His Morales join and Garner Court-opinion join insist on identifying what the earlier law actually guaranteed and what the later law withdrew. They also preserve Collins's refusal to constitutionalize disadvantage alone. Here the guarantee is not parole procedure but a conviction prerequisite independently recognized by Calder, Hopt and Thompson. Those authorities supply the trial-specific basis.

**Adversarial comparison:** The credibility characterization is plausible and the continuing reasonable-doubt standard significant. But if the amendment applies, the formerly conclusive legal deficiency disappears without any new evidence. Hopt's newly competent witness and Thompson's newly available exemplar do not establish that result. Neither the reporting exception nor the age exception makes the former condition less binding when both are absent.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** Do not require that the witness be disbelieved or characterize her testimony as inherently unreliable. The rule concerns legislative sufficiency, not the truth of her accusation. Do not import Morales's burden allocation or a parole-risk formula into this trial inquiry. Preserve the State's actual-record argument on remand.

### Thomas

**Result:** Reverse the adverse federal ruling for counts 7–10 and remand on the existing record under the earlier rule.

**Ground:** The fourth Calder category retains an operative evidentiary minimum distinct from the other categories. The later statute authorizes conviction without additional evidence the offense-date law required when neither exception applied. Ordinary witness-competence reform remains permissible.

**Authority and individual support:** Calder's historical formulation, as adopted and confined in Collins, and Hopt's express distinction supply the ground. His Garner join is compatible with applying an established legal protection at its exact scope but does not establish the trial result. No participation in Collins or own preexisting trial-corroboration holding is invented.

**Adversarial comparison:** The State's strongest position is that no offense element or reasonable-doubt standard changed. That does not answer the separately recognized category governing a reduction in required proof. Hopt cannot be read to abolish the very distinction it uses to uphold witness competence. Conversely, treating all new evidence as forbidden would contradict both Hopt and Thompson and is rejected.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** The adopted rule must rest on the identified historical category, not an open-ended substantial-rights balancing test. No fresh test, numerical proof threshold or universal constitutional corroboration requirement follows. The State may establish compliance with the older law from the trial record; it receives no automatic chance to supplement deficient proof.

### Ginsburg

**Result:** Affirm the rejection of this Ex Post Facto challenge.

**Ground:** The amendment removes a special restriction upon the use of a particular witness's testimony, analogous in constitutional function to Hopt's removal of a witness disability. The jury must still find the same criminal acts beyond reasonable doubt. The legislature's alteration of the age/report categories does not necessarily prescribe a smaller quantum of persuasion or substitute a new offense fact.

**Authority and individual support:** Her Morales join and Garner separate position protect an actually withdrawn avenue to release and reject a purely formal assurance of an adequate replacement. Those decisions require examination of operation rather than labels; they do not decide corroboration. Here Hopt and Thompson supply a permissible evidentiary-reform path, and Collins supplies the restriction on general procedural-disadvantage reasoning. The supplied sources contain no Ginsburg preexisting holding specifically classifying this kind of corroboration rule; this commitment is a stated inference from the actual alternatives, not an invented prior vote or a claimed quotation.

**Adversarial comparison:** The defendant's best objection is that the same record changes from legally insufficient to potentially sufficient. That consequence also attends permissible removal of categorical witness disabilities, and the Texas remedy alone does not determine constitutional classification. The special age and reporting triggers indicate a credibility rule rather than a uniform requirement to establish guilt through a specified quantity of independently sufficient evidence. She preserves the fourth category for an actual reduction in constitutionally relevant proof requirements and does not erase it through a blanket procedural label.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** No assumption that the State actually corroborated the testimony, no criticism of a victim or invention of a legislative motive, and no displacement of Morales or Garner. If the contrary sufficiency distinction controls, the unresolved corroboration contention forecloses an immediate acquittal directive.

### Breyer

**Result:** Reverse the constitutional ruling on counts 7–10 and remand for actual-record application of the earlier rule.

**Ground:** The practical and legal change is that a conviction becomes available with an evidentiary basis formerly insufficient as a matter of law. That is distinct from improving the jury's information through new admissible evidence. The proof requirement can be protected without freezing ordinary trial administration.

**Authority and individual support:** His Garner join applies an existing guarantee according to its demonstrated operation while leaving ordinary procedure and ultimate constitutional proof intact. That analogy is limited; Collins, Hopt and Thompson supply the trial-proof distinction. He was not a member of the Collins Court and no such join is claimed.

**Adversarial comparison:** The State's strongest argument is that allowing a new witness and removing corroboration can have comparable effects on trial outcomes. The relevant difference is legal sufficiency, not a prediction about those outcomes: Hopt and Thompson permit additional material to be evaluated, while the amendment makes the old material enough without the extra evidentiary support. Preserving that line gives ordinary reform room to operate and prevents retrospective legislative relaxation of an essential conviction condition.

**Join barriers and alternatives:** No statistical probability inquiry, no requirement to show actual innocence or a likely different verdict, and no automatic acquittal on an assumed fact. He joins the bounded existing-record remand and preservation of the eleven unaffected counts.

## Scope and component positions

The State's litigation assurance supplies neither a binding limiting construction nor a reason to require the proposed certification to the Board. 2. **Political-committee definition:** Each Associate would affirm the facial invalidation of section 163-278.6(14). Its combined primary-or-incidental-purpose and election-influence coverage subjects genuine independent issue discussion to the full committee regime without a determinate electoral-financing trigger. This substantial defect appears in the definition itself and in the described administration; it does not depend on guessing the second guide's exact words. The sufficient ground is that coverage defect, not a universal organizational exemption whenever electioneering is not a group's major purpose.
3. **Expenditure coverage within the second granted question:** Each Associate would sustain relief against treating genuine independent issue advocacy as a prohibited expenditure merely because it seeks to influence an election. Leave the separate corporate direct-contribution, session-contribution/solicitation and donor-identification portions of the lower judgment undisturbed, without adjudicating their merits anew. Preserve the existing relief within the reviewed scope; do not replace the statute, compel new rulemaking, order registration or reporting, impose liability, or direct a new factual study. No construction remand is necessary to decide the supported legal defect. This disposition does not independently approve the breadth of an ungranted corporate-direct-gift ruling or announce facial invalidity of every corporate regulation.

## Justice-specific commitments

Preserve Maine Right to Life's limited contextual express-advocacy construction and decline to adopt an exclusive electoral vocabulary.

**Sufficient ground and authorities:** His Maine Right to Life Court opinion (September 14, 1998, Parts II–III) permits only an unmistakable electoral direction under cumulative restrictions, leaving constitutional vagueness open. His Akins Part III join (June 25, 1997) requires actual covered financing rather than an organizational label. His Clifton join (September 14, 1998) protects ordinary candidate inquiry at its statutory and review-specific scope. Those positions together distinguish ascertainable financing from a law extending full committee duties to independent policy discussion merely because it might influence an election.

**Two paths:** A narrow construction preserving actual financing is a lawful objective and preferable to a blanket exemption; its strongest case is Buckley's avoidance construction. Here the statute separately reaches incidental purpose and election influence, and no binding construction supplies the missing restrictions. An affirmance based on that defect survives the State's valid-application objection because it preserves the legitimacy of actual candidate-financing regulation while addressing the substantial issue-speech sweep. A fresh merits remand would have no identified unresolved legal task necessary to this result.

**Join limits:** No categorical constitutional approval of every contextual test, no invalidation of tailored disclosure by analogy to an individual leaflet, and no new direct-gift exemption. Day's nonprofit application supports keeping distinct corporate-financing predicates separate; it is not needed as a new independent holding here.

**Commitment:** Same disposition; full agreement with the coverage-defect rationale and rejection of categorical organizational immunity. Join the statement that Maine Right to Life permits limited context at its existing statutory scope without deciding its reserved constitutional question.

The narrower construction alternative lacks the authoritative and textual predicates that supported the federal construction in Maine Right to Life.

**Join limits:** No rule making all reporting the equivalent of a prohibition, no permanent safe harbor for every predominantly issue-oriented entity, no automatic invalidation of valid ordinary reports under other authority, and no relaxation of genuine candidate-financing requirements.

## Components and common operative scope

All eight Associates participate and reach the two granted questions. None selects an author or supplies Stone's vote. All eight support the same component dispositions below, after the individual assessments recorded next.

**A. Current and former control.** Affirm the invalidation of permit blocking based merely on an applicant's former ownership or control. Section 510(c)'s ordinary rule requires a currently violating operation currently owned or controlled by the applicant. Actual indirect downstream control can satisfy control; mere upstream relationship is not substituted for the statutory direction. Once ordinary control ends, the separate former-control provision requires a demonstrated pattern of willful violations of the chapter, of the requisite nature and duration, with resulting irreparable environmental damage indicating an intent not to comply. Every one of those statutory qualifications remains necessary. Ending control does not erase a claim satisfying that provision or other independently authorized law.

The ordinary block lasts until the applicant proves that the violation is corrected **or is in the process of being corrected** to the satisfaction of the authority with jurisdiction. It does not invariably require completed correction. The three-year notice schedule and the independent use of other information perform different jobs: a violation is not outside the current-condition rule merely because it began before that schedule's period.

**B. Pre-rule dates and present eligibility.** Reverse the appellate rule insofar as it categorically prohibits using an otherwise lawful indirect-control construction for a present permit decision involving a presently controlled operation's current violation merely because both the violation and acquisition of control predate November 2, 1988. The regulated subject at that scope is current eligibility for prospective operations, on the basis of current control and a current violation. The acquisition and violation dates alone do not convert that current condition into a new disability imposed solely on a completed transaction.

The distinction is substantive, not a label: an agency cannot impose new obligations solely on completed past conduct by calling the enforcement prospective. But the ordinary rule, properly confined by component A, does not impose liability on a former controller merely for the old relationship, and present abatement or satisfactory correction in progress can satisfy it. Smiley further forecloses the lower court's premise that an interpretation is impermissibly retroactive simply because it was permissible rather than compelled before an authoritative regulation. A lawful current construction of statutory ambiguity may inform antecedent transactions without backdating the rule; the source does not establish clear contrary prior official guidance displaced here. No finding about actual reliance is made.

The appellate footnote's distinct pre-rule-violation/post-rule-acquisition application remains permissible at this temporal level. Neither it nor today's rule establishes actual control or a violation in any particular permit matter. Unauthorized new liability for wholly completed conduct, displacement of clear prior official guidance, penalties, and adjudication-specific finality objections remain outside this bounded prospective-permit decision. No general temporal immunity for old violations follows, and no general retrospective rulemaking power is recognized.

**C. Correction authority and finality.** Affirm rejection of the categorical claim that section 510(c)'s reference to permit issuance eliminates power to suspend or revoke an improvidently issued permit. **Freeman changes the public-protection premise.** Freeman v. Ferguson, 911 F.2d 52 (August 6, 1990), distinguishes the allegation that a chief directed officers not to stop a known assailant because of personal friendship from a general failure to answer pleas for police protection. ## Competing paths and the selected level of decision

**Institutional-assistance path.** A participating hospital can receive benefits under a federal insurance/assistance program although the same payments reimburse services and benefit insured patients. Medicare's supported program structure uses continuing provider participation and institutional reimbursement to maintain the provision of covered care; the hospital is a participant through which that assistance is furnished, not simply a seller making an isolated purchase transaction with the Government. This conclusion concerns the ordinary Medicare participation established here. It does not make every public payment, regulated vendor, commercial contract or government customer a source of §666 benefits.

The strongest objection is that reimbursement and quality conditions also accompany ordinary purchases, so federal purpose or improved cash flow alone cannot distinguish assistance. The answer adopted by the six Associates below is that §666(b) expressly includes insurance and contracts within federal assistance and does not require exclusive benefit to one class or a gratuitous transfer. Program operation and the recipient's actual role establish the distinction here; generalized federal purpose, net profit and regulatory conditions alone do not. This is a concrete construction and application, not a mandatory checklist whose factors can compensate for absence of assistance.

**Ordinary-compensation path.** The two opposing Associates regard the proved hospital payments as compensation for treatment under an insurance benefit furnished to patients. Continuing quality and payment regulation does not convert every provider into an institutional beneficiary. Reading benefits and assistance with subsection (c)'s ordinary-course compensation protection supplies a meaningful criminal boundary without adding Salinas's rejected traced-dollars requirement. The strongest objection is that this treats insurance and contract language too narrowly and can exclude assistance intentionally furnished through service providers. Their answer is to preserve coverage where genuine aid to the institution is established, while refusing to treat the ordinary purchase of treatment, without the missing additional premise, as enough.

**Subsection (c).** The six adopting the first path treat subsection (c) as protecting bona fide salary, wages, fees, other compensation and ordinary-course expenses from being the prohibited transaction. Legitimate Medicare reimbursement is not itself criminalized. Subsection (c) also counsels against treating every federal receipt as aid, but does not remove otherwise qualifying institutional participation from subsection (b) merely because that participation includes reimbursement. Alleged diversion and a corrupt kickback are not transformed into bona fide compensation by the lawful source of the institution's other revenues. The two opposing Associates give subsection (c) additional structural force in classifying the institution's ordinary service receipts, without calling the challenged diversion or kickback lawful compensation.

No Associate imports an agency's administrative deference into this criminal construction. Mining preserves statutory fit and supplies no benefits classification. Saenz supplies no material change to this statute. Salinas controls the absence of a demonstrated federal-dollar effect requirement and expressly leaves the benefits distinction open.

## Component A — temporal attribution and subsection (h)

**Offense-date path adopted by all eight Associates.** Postrevocation imprisonment and supervision enforce the sentence for the original offense. The statutory supervision limits refer back to that offense; revocation uses a preponderance finding and can respond to noncriminal violations. Treating these sanctions as punishment for a new crime would mismatch the procedure that supplies them and their continuing relation to the original judgment. The original offense is therefore the relevant event for deciding whether the 1994 subsection (h) governs.

The strongest contrary path treats the later violation as new conduct to which Johnson could conform after enactment. That is a real current event, but the existence of current misconduct does not decide the legal source of its consequences. Mining likewise requires identification of the operative consequence rather than reliance on an application date. Its present permit-eligibility rule does not make punishment attached to an earlier conviction a new prospective regulatory condition. The original sentencing statute, rather than the moral novelty of the breach, answers this attribution question.

Congress supplied no instruction making subsection (h) govern these earlier offenses. It is therefore unavailable here. The common conclusion does not depend on proving that the exact disposition increases punishment beyond former law; that distinct issue must be decided by construing former law. Nor does it import Landgraf's entire organizing method into the separate relevant-activity positions of Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. An adequate old-law ground would permit affirmance while rejecting the lower court's temporal rationale.

All eight avoid a constitutional Ex Post Facto holding about application of subsection (h), which they do not apply. Carmell's evidence-sufficiency category, Garner's release-opportunity rule and Saenz's conduct-time immunity method preserve their actual functions; none mechanically classifies this sanction.

## Component B — the authority conferred by former law

**Authorization path selected by seven Associates.** Former §3583(e)(3), read with §3583(a) and the separately expressed termination-and-discharge power in (e)(1), permits prison for part of the imposed supervised-release term followed by supervision for a remaining portion. Revocation withdraws the present conditional release; it does not invariably discharge every remaining authority over that term. The command allowing service in prison of all or part of the supervised-release term, the absence of a discharge command in (e)(3), and subsection (a)'s supervision-after-imprisonment structure together support the continuing remainder. Supervision's distinct transition function, recognized in the March 1 Johnson decision, confirms rather than creates that construction.

The strongest objection is that revoke ordinarily cancels the term, (e)(3) expressly mentions prison alone, and (e)(2) cannot modify an extinguished term. The seven do not rely on (e)(2) as authority to create a new term or treat rehabilitative desirability as a free-standing sentencing power. They distinguish the express ending/discharge provision from revocation's prison-for-all-or-part mechanism and read the latter with (a). Congress's later express clarification in (h) does not itself prove or disprove the earlier authority. The construction must be supported without applying that amendment.

**Nonauthorization path selected by Scalia.** Revocation cancels the term. The phrase all or part measures the imprisonment authorized after cancellation, not a residuum of release capable of revival. Subsection (e)(2) modifies an existing term only; subsection (a) authorizes the original sentence's supervision and does not silently add a postrevocation release power absent from (e)(3). The distinct termination provision permits early discharge without a violation and does not make revoke mean partial continuation. The strongest counterargument is the original-term measure and the supervision-after-custody structure. For Scalia, those features cannot replace an omitted kind of penal consequence; any genuine residual uncertainty reinforces that limit.

**Actual calculation under the seven-Justice construction.** The relevant former-law measure is the actual three-year term, not the amended reference to the term authorized by statute for the offense. Eighteen months of prison plus twelve months of renewed supervision totals thirty months, within the thirty-six months actually imposed. Eighteen months of prison also falls below the former two-year Class C/D cap. Former (e)(3) withholds credit for the time previously spent on postrelease supervision; the approximately seven earlier months therefore do not consume the statutory amount used in this bounded calculation. The remaining supervision does not exceed the original term after subtracting the revocation imprisonment. No new term or extension beyond that remaining amount is recognized.

The proposed rule does not decide the old statute's treatment of different imposed terms, different felony caps, repeated revocations, or every manner of combining release and imprisonment. It does not import (h)'s distinct less-than-the-prison-maximum trigger or statutory-maximum supervision formula. Termination after one year under (e)(1) and condition changes under (e)(2) remain separate powers with their own predicates, not methods of bypassing a limit.

## Component I — preference and permitted use

**Rule supported by all eight:** The Secretary may define grazing preference as priority attached to base property and separately express authorized forage in permitted use, rather than freezing an immutable animal-unit quantity into the preference definition. The actual system must continue adequately safeguarding recognized grazing privileges so far as consistent with the Act and must honor the Act's separate valid-rights, renewal and water protections. The challenged definitional separation does not itself eliminate those safeguards.

**Support and counterargument:** Taylor §2 expressly authorizes range protection and administration; §3 leaves stock numbers and seasons to management, couples renewal preference with discretion and denies that a grazing permit itself conveys an estate in the land. The new definition retains priority; permitted use continues to state allocation, including suspended use within its actual regulatory scope. Those connected provisions defeat the claim that prior regulatory wording made quantity permanently unalterable.

The challengers' strongest path is that a nominal priority emptied of forage would violate the statutory safeguard. That objection is legally sound as a limit on implementation, but is not established by splitting the definitions. The agency's representation that existing preference and suspended-use priority remain is relevant to operation; it cannot authorize a later inconsistent deprivation. Rejecting the facial statutory challenge does not presume every future reduction valid or convert the no-estate clause into absence of all enforceable rights.

**Operative qualifications:** Permitted use is specified for preference holders and encompasses authorized and suspended use, with the actual ephemeral/annual-rangeland exceptions retained. Changes require monitoring, field observations, ecological inventory or other acceptable supporting data. Ordinary reductions require consultation and a documented agreement or proposed decision. Immediate protection for conditions such as drought, fire, flood, infestation or imminent significant resource damage follows the separate consultation-or-reasonable-attempt route; final effective action remains appealable under its stay procedure. These provisions answer the facial assertion of unbounded quantity control, without deciding a future APA or application claim. No new universal process or factual proof requirement is added by this decision.

## Component II — deletion of the commercial-livestock-business qualification

**Rule supported by all eight:** Section 3 does not impose engagement in a commercial livestock business as a universal eligibility requirement. The Secretary may remove that regulatory condition while retaining the Act's stock-owner eligibility, applicable regulatory qualifications and distinct statutory preferences. Commercial engagement in the identified preference category cannot be converted into a prerequisite governing every otherwise eligible stock owner.

**Support and counterargument:** Congress distinguishes the authorized stock-owner class from preference for nearby landowners engaged in the business, bona fide occupants or settlers, and owners of water or water rights as necessary for use of their property. Giving those separate words their separate work permits qualified noncommercial stock owners. It does not validate grazing permits for every organization owning no animals or the conservation-use rule already left outside review.

The strongest contrary path treats actual grazing and the Act's livestock purpose as incompatible with removal of business engagement. Actual grazing and commercial occupation are not identical conditions. The regular use and nonuse rules retain concrete grazing requirements, and no contrary no-stock entitlement is created by deleting only the commercial condition.

The officer may, after two consecutive grazing fee years without substantial authorized use or failure to maintain or use water base property, and after the specified consultation with the permittee and recorded lienholder, cancel the unused amount. Cancellation is discretionary, not automatic. Temporary nonuse remains subject to plan/rangeland-health conformity, reasons and annual approval, for at most three consecutive years; temporarily available forage may be allocated to others. These limits are distinct from the unreviewed conservation-use provision. The listed qualification exceptions for acquired lands and other specified routes retain their own scope; no actual entitlement under an exception is found.

## Component III — prospective title to permanent cooperative improvements

**Rule supported by all eight:** Taylor §§2 and 4 permit the Secretary to specify federal title for future permanent range improvements authorized through cooperative agreements after August 21, 1995, subject to valid existing rights and the rule's contribution protections. Section 4 protects improvements previously constructed and privately owned; it does not require private ownership of every improvement developed under a future cooperative arrangement.

**Support and counterargument:** Section 2 expressly authorizes cooperative agreements, and §4 distinguishes construction under a permit from approved cooperative arrangements. Its reasonable-value provision applies to use of improvements constructed and owned by a prior occupant. Prospective federal title leaves that protection with its actual predicate; it does not confiscate a prior occupant's improvement. State partition-fence requirements likewise remain in force.

The strongest contrary path is that the rule extinguishes the investment protection §4 recognizes by requiring new permanent developments to take federal title. The answer is the distinction between protecting existing ownership and specifying ownership at the beginning of a future authorized arrangement. Public ownership of the land alone would not defeat a separate vested private right; the rule expressly preserves such rights. The Court need not assume that every future agreement will be fair or valid to reject the facial claim of mandatory private title.

All new permanent water developments must proceed through cooperative agreements. The agreement specifies cost or labor allocation; BLM must document contributed funds, labor and materials for the credits specified in §§4120.3-5 and 4120.3-6(c). Work itself confers no exclusive use of the improvement or affected land. Nonstructural improvements belong to the United States; authorized removable livestock-handling and temporary structural improvements may remain privately titled under the separate permit rule. Temporary shared use retains the stated reasonable compensation and cost-allocation procedures. These are retained terms, not an award in this case. The decision adjudicates neither existing-title extinguishment nor an actual taking, compensation amount, final permit reopening or reliance injury.

Mining's express mining-permit correction power is not imported into the Taylor Act. Its temporal distinction illustrates why a rule about future improvements is not a title transfer merely because an applicant already owns base property, but supplies no permission to impose a genuine past-conduct disability or disregard existing rights. Saenz adds no rule governing these components.

## Component I: Commerce authority

### Ground supporting constitutional dismissal

Gender animus is an important enacted limitation, but it does not make interference with a market an element or necessary operation of the class.

The findings support serious economic consequences through injury, curtailed activity, treatment, lost work and consumption. Under the actual Lopez rule, their extent cannot convert those downstream consequences into the antecedent constitutional connection. This does not depend on denying Congress's empirical account, demanding formal findings, requiring an individual interstate crossing, or classifying all noneconomic conduct and traditional state subjects as immune.

The strongest supporting argument for power is that pervasive gender violence excludes people from labor, education, travel and services, and that the national barrier is more concrete than an isolated crime's costs. The four distinguish a barrier that operates on a market as its object and operation from the class actually enacted, which covers personal assault whether or not it targets those activities. American Life League and Bird upheld targeted interference with actual medical services; they did not license a general assault remedy because injured people later cannot buy, work or travel. Robinson similarly requires the business-asset connection that its offense actually makes necessary. Condon concerns the identified information-disclosure market itself. The Court cannot substitute the most economic example for §13981's class.

The Necessary and Proper route is separately insufficient. Hale, Kirk, Hiley and the recipient/supply cases require a valid identified interstate regime that exclusion of this class would materially undermine or permit evasion of. The surrounding Act's comprehensiveness and the shared concern with violence do not by themselves identify that dependence. This record supports no claim that the private civil action is necessary to enforce a specified interstate supply restriction or that its absence would defeat the interstate operation of an identified regime. The four do not invent such a mechanism from the Act's title or from domestic violence's role in different firearm statutes.

Home Builders' independent externality route is considered on its own terms. A concrete substantial cross-border effect which affected States cannot reasonably internalize can support national regulation of noneconomic conduct. The supported findings here identify consequences that occur nationally and can affect interstate choices, but not a distinct cross-border mechanism and state inability of the kind that route requires. Similar private injuries in many States, unequal protection within state systems, and the desirability of a national remedy do not by themselves establish that externality. No fixed geographical, numerical or legislative-findings threshold is imposed. The problem is the asserted mechanism's legal and practical relation to the enacted class, not a demand for more pages.

The four therefore reject Commerce authority for this stand-alone private-assault action. They decline an existing-record remand: the supported alternatives have been identified and assessed, and no concrete potentially eligible mechanism awaits a genuinely unresolved lower application. Home Builders' remand did not grant a general second opportunity to invent a federal connection. They also decline to insert an omitted commerce element. No present conclusion about a materially different, validly enacted market-directed or interstate-remedial statute follows.

### Ground supporting reversal on Commerce authority

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the broader rational, class-wide substantial-effects approach they adopted in the actual public Lopez dissent. Congress could reasonably determine from the supported findings that gender-motivated violent conduct, considered as a class, materially obstructs participation in interstate work, travel and economic activity, and that a civil remedy against its perpetrators is a reasonable means of reducing that obstruction. Their position does not require each assault or each plaintiff's lost opportunity to cross a border or separately have a substantial effect. The gender-motivation and serious-violence requirements identify the legislated class; the remedy aims at the conduct causing the documented impairment.

The strongest objection is decisive under the *existing controlling* Lopez architecture: Congress's causal economic account is mediated through injury and foregone activity, while §13981 does not require a market-directed object or another independently eligible mechanism. These four do not disguise that objection as a missing factual finding. Their preferred class-effects approach differs from the controlling connection-before-magnitude restriction at precisely that point. They reject applying the restriction to invalidate this supported class regulation, and their position would require displacement of that restriction to the extent it forecloses this ground. It is a proposed constitutional position, not a claim that the current majority's ordinary test already authorizes §13981.

Their limiting principle is a rationally supported, substantial relationship of the actual regulated class to interstate commerce and reasonable regulatory means, subject to judicial examination. It is not any imaginable economic consequence, deference to the mere assertion of national importance, or Congress's findings being conclusive. They regard the supported account of barriers to economic participation as sufficient under that test and the direct civil liability of the perpetrators as a reasonable means. They accept that the competing connection rule draws a narrower boundary; they reject its placement of this supported class outside national authority rather than supply an invented market or externality.

Their joins in American Life League, Robinson, the supply cases and Condon confirm those grounds are sufficient, not that those are the only permissible routes. Ginsburg and Breyer's Home Builders joins require particular care: they joined the anti-evasion and externality inquiries *given* the independently controlling Part I, expressly preserving their disagreement with that part's enacted-class restriction. Their current position honors those limits and does not claim that national prevalence automatically satisfies the externality test they joined. Stevens and Souter's absence from that coalition supplies no invented separate rationale.

These four would reverse the constitutional dismissal and return the statutory claim for ordinary adjudication. They would not require a new pleading of an interstate-commerce element that Congress omitted, decide liability or damages, or reopen the unrelated university claims.

## Component II: Fourteenth Amendment §5

### Governing distinction and the strongest opposing paths

The constitutional injury Congress invokes is unequal state protection, not a constitutional right against every private assault. State action can include discriminatory official administration contrary to written law, judicial enforcement and customs carrying legal force; it is not confined to discriminatory statutes. The Civil Rights Cases also contemplated legislation in advance of anticipated state wrongs and remedies reaching private persons in a genuinely corrective relation. Harris (1883) concerned direct private conspiracy without an alleged state constitutional denial; custody of victims by an officer did not convert the private attackers into state actors. Harlan's Harris dissent was jurisdictional only and supplies no contrary merits rule.

Boerne permits congruent and proportional preventive coverage of some conduct that would not independently violate the Constitution. Florida Prepaid actually upheld such coverage for patent deprivations, where exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative remedies supplied the supported connection between state infringement and constitutional redress. No universal requirement of a completed constitutional violation by every defendant, a prior unsuccessful state proceeding, a particular quantity of findings, geographical limits or a sunset may be introduced here. College Savings nevertheless requires the asserted remedy to address an actual constitutional guarantee rather than a statutory wrong alone.

The strongest §5 case for §13981 is therefore substantial. Congress identified discriminatory state justice, and a federal civil claim can substitute an avenue of redress when biased protection fails. Requiring each victim first to prove state discrimination could preserve the obstacle the preventive remedy aims to remove. The action is civil, requires gender animus and serious violence, and targets the perpetrators of the injury for which equal protection was withheld. The findings distinguish this account from the nineteenth-century majorities' descriptions of legislation that did not profess to correct state constitutional wrongs.

The strongest objection concerns the enacted relationship rather than the mere label of the defendant. Liability and relief are triggered by the private assault itself, whether the State denies equal protection or supplies and faithfully enforces it. Neither gender animus of the assailant nor the seriousness of the assault identifies discriminatory state administration. The action does not replace a specified constitutionally deficient state procedure, respond to a defined official practice, or limit its relief by a supported relation to the state deprivation. Legislative identification of a real state problem therefore does not itself answer whether these means prevent or remedy that problem rather than establish a substantive federal right against the underlying private violence.

### Ground rejecting §5 authority for this private-assault action

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas find that remedial connection insufficient. They acknowledge Congress's express account of discriminatory enforcement and do not treat the Civil Rights Cases or Harris as a rule forbidding every action naming a private person. Their objection is the combination of defendant, statutory trigger, breadth and relief: all liability follows from the assault, without a fitted relation between the covered private conduct and the state's denial Congress invokes. The civil nature of the remedy and the difficulty of proving discriminatory enforcement do not, on this design, establish the preventive fit needed to bridge that gap.

Florida Prepaid's authorization of some ordinary state infringement is not ignored. The patent remedy confronted the States' own deprivation of protected property and a jurisdictional structure potentially leaving that deprivation without adequate redress. Its preventive inclusion of additional infringement did not make every federal statutory tort a constitutional wrong. Here the assailant's private act and the discriminatory state's administration are distinct wrongs, and the remedial design does not sufficiently connect the former's nationwide liability to prevention or redress of the latter. This is a qualitative fit judgment; no requirement to prove a constitutional violation in every suit is substituted for it.

The explicit state-action language remains meaningful. The four reject §5 as authority for the private-assault cause presented; they do not adjudicate a hypothetical claim against a person acting under color of state law, decide every possible valid application, or strike the phrase from the text. No established severability issue or different application is invented. Nor do they convert this scope limit into a newly pleaded state-action claim for Brzonkala or judicially insert an absent state-failure element.

### Reserved alternative ground for the four supporting reversal

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer do not reach a dispositive §5 conclusion because their Commerce ground sustains the enacted cause and supplies complete relief from the constitutional dismissal. Their reservation is explicit: it contributes no vote to either the four's rejection of §5 or a broader §5 validity holding.

Each recognizes the genuine state-discrimination/prophylaxis argument and the private-injury/constitutional-injury objection above. Their actual Florida Prepaid support makes a per-victim completed-violation requirement unacceptable; their varying Boerne positions cannot be turned into an agreement that every remedy Congress labels preventive is valid. Stevens and Ginsburg joined Boerne's no-redefinition rule. Souter and Breyer's Boerne reservations concerned the substantive Smith baseline and did not authorize legislative replacement of rights. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined College Savings' refusal to infer a constitutional property injury from the particular federal tort alone. These are meaningful constraints on a possible §5 position, not predictions of a position they need not decide here.

They would decline to join a categorical private-defendant exclusion, a rule that discriminatory official nonenforcement can never be state action, or a requirement of an independently proved constitutional violation in every covered case. They also decline to infer that the legislative record and civil remedy necessarily establish congruence and proportionality. A narrower future holding would have to identify how the specific coverage and relief remedy or prevent unequal state protection while retaining the actual state-action boundary; no such additional holding is counted in their present commitment.

### Saenz at its actual scope

Saenz's actual official-causal-deprivation ground matters without deciding §5. A private immediate attacker does not preclude a constitutional wrong when an official affirmatively removes available lawful protection with the required culpability and attributable causal effect. Conversely, general nonprotection, callousness or private violence alone is insufficient. It supplies no official action in this complaint and does not turn Morrison's private assailants into state actors. Equal-protection discrimination is its own constitutional wrong; Saenz's due-process rule does not erase it.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's Saenz joins support attention to actual attribution and constitutional function; they do not supply a §5 vote. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's different treatment of the canceled investigation likewise supplies no vote to reject every preventive law addressing unequal protection. Saenz did not adjudicate congressional prophylaxis, and its direct-claim elements cannot become a universal individual-violation prerequisite for valid §5 coverage. Its immunity application lacks a majority and has no bearing on these power questions.

## Components and the two lawful paths

### A. Installation as a search

**No-search ground adopted by seven Associates.** On the established installation record, attachment to the exposed vehicle undercarriage acquired no concealed information from the effect or a protected place. The subsequent ordinary road movements were publicly exposed; they are distinct from covert information inside a home or closed container. Physical contact and investigative purpose alone do not establish a privacy search.

The strongest opposing path distinguishes observation from the purposeful use of another's effect as an information-gathering instrument. Hicks and Bond show that public access or exposure to some contact does not authorize every added physical investigation. The seven answer that those decisions involved an examination acquiring concealed information; attachment here obtained none at installation. They do not treat the automobile as entirely unprotected or decide installation requiring an interior intrusion, inspection of concealed contents or a different record of private-location monitoring.

Karo's actual original installation was consented to before the respondent acquired the container; it does not directly decide this nonconsensual installation. Its distinct rejection of a search based on mere transfer/potential monitoring nevertheless supplies the relevant information-acquisition distinction. Class expressly calls entry into a vehicle interior a search despite absence of privacy in the VIN; its narrow reasonableness rationale supplies no general attachment authority. Cardwell's exterior/probable-cause rationale had only four adherents and is not restated as a universal majority rule that every exterior manipulation is outside the Amendment.

Stevens does not need to resolve this independent search theory because his seizure ground fully reaches the installation and remedy. His Karo opinion's broader search observations remain his published position, not an automatic additional present holding or a later property-search doctrine.

### B. Installation as an independent seizure

**No-seizure ground adopted by seven Associates.** Meaningful interference with possession remains necessary even when privacy is absent. The unwanted attachment occupied part of the vehicle and assisted police observation but did not on this record dispossess McIver, impair his driving or control, damage the effect or alter its mechanical operation. This is insufficient interference under Jacobsen and Karo. Neither damage nor dispossession is declared indispensable to every seizure; the conclusion concerns this supported interference.

The strongest contrary path is that exclusion and control include the right to prevent the Government from installing its own operational device on an existing owner's effect. Lack of visible injury does not necessarily make government appropriation insignificant. The seven recognize that interest but do not treat every unwanted physical occupation as meaningful constitutional interference. Karo's transfer analysis rejects exactly that automatic equivalence. The difference in original ownership and consent requires independent consideration, but does not by itself establish a greater actual impairment after the small external attachment. Speculative electrical or mechanical danger cannot substitute for a finding of such impairment.

**Seizure ground adopted by Stevens.** Secretly attaching and using an operational tracking device on this existing owner's vehicle meaningfully appropriates the effect for the Government's own investigative use. The invasion concerns control over the physical object, not privacy in the view of its exterior, and does not require damage or inability to drive. His own Karo separate opinion specifically treats attachment and electronic occupation as interference with the exclusionary right; the present absence of owner consent at installation strengthens that position without claiming that his former dissent already controls. Soldal's independent protection of possession prevents resolving this claim solely through public exposure.

His strongest obstacle is Karo's majority rejection of meaningful interference from an unwanted beeper's occupation of space. He distinguishes its consented installation before transfer and proposes the confined rule for governmental attachment to an already owned effect. He does not claim that Karo was overruled, that all trespasses are seizures, or that every device capable of later use has already searched private information. The device's actual appropriation and use, not its mere technological existence, perform the work. The result is a proposed development at this installation boundary, not mechanical application of Soldal's home-removal facts.

### C. Public-road monitoring and preservation

All eight decline a new independent holding on the monitoring question because McIver expressly did not contest public-road beeper monitoring below. The supplied grant does not establish withdrawal of that concession or preserve a different aggregate-surveillance claim. Knotts's ordinary public-travel rule supplies context for the installation analysis only. No new rule about ten-day duration, continuous surveillance, private-home information, or all future location technology is adopted.

Stevens's installation-taint objection remains independent: evidence can be fruit of unlawful installation even though a separately authorized observation of exposed road movement would not itself invade privacy. The seven's lawful-installation position removes that derivative premise; it does not turn an unpreserved monitoring claim into a merits victory against every possible future claim.

### D. Justification and remedy

The seven finding no search or seizure affirm the reviewed installation objection and do not reach a warrant exception, exclusion, independent-source finding or constitutional harmlessness. The affirmance therefore does not adopt Kleinfeld's separate harmlessness assessment.

For Stevens, no warrant, consent or established installation exception justifies the seizure on the supported record. Labron/Kilgore supplies the actual automobile rule without an additional exigency prerequisite, but does not automatically authorize installing a continuing device or supply unadjudicated probable cause. He rejects the no-seizure ground and would vacate and remand for the remaining lawful consequences of the installation error. A genuinely preserved justification, if one actually exists, remains confined to its own established facts and procedural availability; no new justification, waiver or evidentiary opportunity is invented.

The Government's strongest remedy argument is extensive antecedent camera and independent visual evidence. The contrary argument is that the device may have directed particular observations and the later warrant. The lower ruling removed house-entry observations from the truck affidavit, not every tracking-derived fact, so it does not itself settle tracker causation. Kleinfeld's concurrence provides a reasoned harmlessness position, not a lower majority finding binding this Court. Stevens would require the actual causal and harmless-error inquiries on the existing record rather than order automatic suppression of everything or an acquittal. The first inquiry identifies the actual causal connection and any established lawful independent source; independent admissibility is not constitutional error made harmless. For constitutionally tainted evidence actually admitted, Chapman requires the Government, as the beneficiary, to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the guilty verdict. The quantity or strength of untainted evidence alone does not settle that question. Actual Neder confirms the preserved-error burden and an actual-verdict inquiry, but its treatment of omitted materiality instructions is not an automatic formula for this evidentiary error, and its separate necessary-equivalence position is not a general Fourth Amendment rule. Stevens's full Neder join supports bounded appellate review rather than automatic retrial, while his Karo objection forbids first-instance appellate replacement of a materially unexamined causal record. Remand permits the lawfully available review of the existing record, not a reopened opportunity to create missing independent-source or harmlessness proof. If the Government meets the relevant admissibility or harmlessness standard, the affected conviction survives this objection; if admitted constitutional taint is not harmless under that burden, the affected conviction must be set aside with a new trial permitted. No evidence-insufficiency determination or acquittal follows from this instructional direction.
