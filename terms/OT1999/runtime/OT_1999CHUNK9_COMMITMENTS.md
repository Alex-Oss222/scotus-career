## Reach, components and common remedial boundary

All eight Associates reach the presented First and Fourteenth Amendment challenge through certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). The Ninth Circuit affirmed a final defense judgment after a four-day bench trial. No supplied fact supports a jurisdictional dismissal, mootness disposition, recusal, DIG, or a remand merely to obtain a finding that crossover exists. The statutory mechanism and the trial findings already permit constitutional review. The trial court's significant-but-nonsevere description is a legal conclusion subject to independent review; its underlying findings are not erased because a Justice disagrees with that characterization.

The adjudicable component is compulsory blanket selection of official party nominees for state and congressional offices. Each voter's ability to cross party lines office by office, combined with §15451's designation of the highest recipient within each party as its nominee, distinguishes the system from selection of a nonpartisan slate. The protected interest asserted is control of the party's legally operative nominee. It does not depend on treating all publicly funded election machinery as private property or denying the State's authority to require primaries and fair administration.

No Associate decides an independent Elections Clause attack on initiative lawmaking, national presidential-delegate treatment under the amended reporting provisions, or a general entitlement to a closed primary. The conventional one-party-ballot open primary, a party's voluntary invitation to additional voters, and a nonpartisan first round remain distinct and unadjudicated. No source establishes that voter popularity amended the plaintiff parties' contrary bylaws.

For every reversing commitment, the remedy is reversal of the Ninth Circuit's defense judgment and remand for declaratory and prospective injunctive relief against compulsory blanket selection of the plaintiff parties' official nominees, fitted to that unlawful operation. The mandate does not reopen completed elections, award damages, dictate a substitute ballot, establish the severability of every conforming amendment, or decide the rights of parties that consent to a different arrangement. The lower court implements relief consistently with the merits holding and determines any necessary implementation details actually presented. No postponement of constitutional relief until a party proves an outsider-caused nomination loss is required. If the affirmance path were selected instead, the existing defense judgment would remain in place and no new injunction would issue.

## Competing paths and the entering-law boundary

**Path A — severe compelled selection.** Reversal can rest on the qualitative transfer of an objecting party's official nominee selection to a constituency that includes competing-party members without any affiliation requirement for the party whose nomination they influence. Actual predicted crossover and its possible effect on candidates reinforce the nature of that burden; no invented California percentage or organized raid is necessary. Under Burdick, this severe burden requires narrow tailoring to a compelling interest. Electoral participation, voter choice and privacy are legitimate concerns, but the State has not established why they require this compulsory alteration of an objecting party's legal nomination. A purpose of changing that party's nominee or message is not itself a sufficient justification for compelling it.

The strongest objection to Path A is that a state-run primary distributes publicly conferred ballot access, the trial record predicts continued vigorous party activity, and routine primary rules can affect whom a party nominates. The sufficient answer is limited: this law does more than regulate support, timing or the availability of a candidate; it changes who has the legal power to choose the party's official nominee. Private-party rights remain subject to voting-rights constraints and ordinary election regulation. The answer does not constitutionalize every party preference.

**Path B — public primary and proportionate regulation.** Affirmance can rest on the State's authority over the public first stage of choosing officeholders, significant but nonsevere burden, surviving party speech and governance, infrequent organized raiding, and the direct relation between wider voting and wider participation. Timmons and Burdick foreclose automatic strict scrutiny merely because an election law affects association; predictive election administration need not await demonstrated collapse or abuse.

The strongest objection to Path B is that its retained-activity account does not restore the legal nomination which §15451 assigns, and it can discount sincere outsider influence by demanding evidence of malicious raiding or organizational destruction. Popular approval is not consent. The separate commitments identify whether the State's otherwise substantial participation interests overcome that objection for the particular Justice; they do not treat the trial court's constitutional label as a fact binding the Court.

**Path C — inadequate fit without resolving severity.** Reversal can alternatively rest on a material burden and insufficient justification for compulsory official-nominee selection even assuming that the burden is less than severe. This path applies Anderson–Burdick, not rational basis, and does not announce a new intermediate tier. Its strongest objection is that California's public-election interests genuinely differ from the weak explanations for excluding an otherwise qualified consensual nomination in Timmons or Reform Party. Its answer must therefore address those interests on this record, not infer invalidity from those earlier results.

Timmons remains controlling for its specific nonfusion restriction. Reform Party adds an equal-access holding for a timely opportunity that Pennsylvania already gave to major parties; California's blanket rule is not invalid merely because of that equality holding, and no unequal-treatment finding is invented. Its proposition that endorsement does not replace a legal nomination is useful in identifying the burden, not an independent rule deciding the outcome. Voluntary cross-nomination involved party and candidate consent; compulsory outsider voting does not. Conversely, that consent distinction does not establish that every nonconsensual electoral rule is unconstitutional. Hurley's expression principle is an analogy requiring attention to the official nominee's party significance, not a rule declaring a state primary a private parade.

## Individual commitments and adversarial comparisons

### Justice Stevens

**Commitment:** Reach the merits; reverse and remand on Path C. The State has not adequately justified requiring these objecting parties to confer their legal nomination through office-by-office voting by nonmembers. He does not commit to the proposition that every instance of unwanted participation is a severe burden requiring categorical strict scrutiny. The sufficiency of his ground is the actual burden and inadequate fit in this system.

**Own-source basis:** His Timmons dissent treats a willing standard bearer and the legal nomination as a real association that endorsement cannot replace; his join in Reform Party Part II retains that distinction while requiring an actual-interest inquiry. His Eu concurrence supports protection of association while cautioning against conclusions produced by scrutiny labels alone. His participation in the Hurley holding supplies a reason to take attributable expressive selection seriously, subject to the public-primary distinction.

**Competing path and strongest objection:** Path B has genuine support in his recognition, in the Tashjian qualifications dissent, that primaries participate in the constitutional selection of public officials. Public participation is a more direct state purpose here than the asserted confusion from adding a consensual fusion label in Timmons. Against reversal, this means neither private-association terminology nor the absence of raiding decides the case. Nevertheless, the broad electorate's interest in influencing a general election does not by itself explain entitlement to select each opposing party's legal standard bearer. The record establishes a particular loss of party choice that surviving endorsements and committees do not cure. The qualified turnout predictions and representativeness interests do not supply a sufficient reason for that compulsory consequence.

**Join boundary and writing function:** He can join the statutory account, the real-nomination burden, the contextual scrutiny framework, insufficient justification, and confined remedy. He withholds a commitment to Path A's severity classification and any declaration that publicly administered primaries are wholly private expression. A separate concurrence would explain the fit ground if an opinion makes severity indispensable. His earlier qualifications dissent creates no separate federal/state disposition here: no equivalent disparity is supplied or within the presented basis for relief.

### Justice O'Connor

**Commitment:** Reach the merits; reverse and remand on Path A. The comprehensive authority to participate in each objecting party's official nomination imposes a severe burden; the asserted participation and privacy interests do not justify that means under the required scrutiny.

**Own-source basis:** Her Tashjian dissent join concerns protecting members' democratic candidate selection and allows a State to require a primary; it is not a prior commitment to government-compelled outsider selection. Her joins in Eu and Hurley protect actual associational and expressive choice. Her Burdick and Timmons joins preserve ordinary preventive election regulation; her Reform Party Part II join requires attention to the precise withheld nomination opportunity rather than treating endorsement as its substitute.

**Competing path and strongest objection:** Path B draws substantial support from her Burdick and Timmons positions and from her Tashjian willingness to sustain state regulation despite a party's contrary preference. The strongest objection to reversal is that this is another publicly structured primary with surviving party advocacy and uncertain practical effects. The distinction is the subject of the regulation: support and nomination-route limits preserve an electorate's choice for the party, while this system compels competing affiliates' participation in that choice. The finding that some races probably will turn on crossover and campaigns may adjust confirms a material consequence without proving a particular outcome. Statewide administrative uniformity and nondiscrimination do not reduce the nature of that burden.

**Join boundary and remedy:** She supports the severe-burden ground only for the compulsory blanket mechanism presented. She would not join a freestanding exemption of parties from election law or an automatic extension to conventional open primaries. Her Reform Party Part III join supports a remand confined to the unlawful operation, without reopening elections or legislating replacement rules.

### Justice Scalia

**Commitment:** Reach the merits; reverse and remand on Path A. Giving outsiders legal power to help choose an objecting party's official nominee infringes members' associational choice at a level not justified by the asserted state interests.

**Own-source basis:** His corrected Tashjian dissent expressly recognizes the associational significance of members selecting their candidate; its permissive treatment of a required member primary does not endorse compulsory outsourcing. His Eu, Hurley, Burdick and Timmons joins distinguish protected choice from routine regulation and do not make every party-law conflict severe. His Reform Party joins preserve both an actual-nomination inquiry and confined relief.

**Competing path and strongest objection:** His own Tashjian insistence on contextual accommodation and state control of democratic primary machinery strongly supports taking Path B seriously. A rule invalidating any primary procedure contrary to a party convention's wishes would contradict that position. But the party members whose democratic selection that dissent protected are here required to share the selection with voters who retain other affiliations. The State's wish to enlarge influence cannot simply negate that protected interest. Rare raiding is beside the decisive point: a sincere outside vote has the same legal weight in selecting the official nominee.

**Join boundary and remedy:** He can join strict scrutiny of the demonstrated blanket-selection burden, not a holding that a single outsider vote always violates the Constitution or that Tashjian gives parties every ballot arrangement they request. He would preserve the requirement of lawful primaries and the unresolved status of other systems. His join in Stevens's Tashjian qualifications dissent supplies no present independent Elections Clause or qualification judgment; those issues are not established here. Confined reversal and remand follow the common boundary.

### Justice Kennedy

**Commitment:** Reach the merits; reverse and remand on Path A. An official party nominee conveys and organizes the party's electoral choice; the forced office-by-office nominating electorate materially changes that choice. The record supports a severe burden, and the precise interests advanced do not justify it.

**Own-source basis:** His Eu and Hurley joins protect actual political and expressive selection. His Burdick dissent accepts calibrated scrutiny but resists treating system-level access as a complete answer to a concrete electoral injury. His Timmons merits join nevertheless permits meaningful selection restrictions when the burden and interests fit. His Reform Party joins distinguish the availability of the person on the ballot from the legal nomination opportunity and limit relief to what the case proves.

**Competing path and strongest objection:** Path B is supported by his Timmons join: parties may be denied a preferred nomination mechanism even while other expression remains available. The strongest objection to Path A is therefore that every formal nomination constraint could be redescribed as interference with a message. This law's operative distinction is that the State selects a party's legal nominating constituency over its objection, rather than withholding the additional nomination route sustained in Timmons. The prediction of outsider influence in some races supplies record application; no requirement to prove party collapse follows from the earlier case. General political openness can be pursued without treating influence over an opposing party's nominee as an unqualified individual entitlement.

**Join boundary and remedy:** He supports the rule for compulsory partisan nomination and its application to the challenged state and congressional offices. He would not join automatic validation of a nonpartisan substitute, a general closed-primary command, or a dismissal of voting-rights limits on party nomination. The remedy follows the common confined remand.

### Justice Souter

**Commitment:** Reach the merits; reverse and remand on Path C. Even if the burden falls below severe, the State has not shown an adequate connection between the particular interests asserted and compulsory participation in an objecting party's legal nomination. No decision that all open participation is severe is necessary to this commitment.

**Own-source basis:** His separate Timmons dissent expressly maintains meaningful fit review below the severe-burden threshold and reserves a demonstrated governmental-stability justification. His join in Burdick permits preventive regulation; his Hurley opinion protects selection of expression despite retained opportunities to speak. His Reform Party Part II join carries forward the difference between endorsement and legal nomination within its equality limits.

**Competing path and strongest objection:** Path B has force because ballot administration need not await past disaster and because broad participation, secrecy and public representativeness concern the electorate rather than mere protection of party officials. The strongest objection to his reversal is that his Timmons reservation allows substantial institutional interests and this record includes a tried, functioning system. His answer does not demand empirical proof of collapse or impose a novel evidentiary burden. It asks what the interests justify: continued party vigor does not restore nomination control, and public influence over officeholders does not alone require voters to help confer a party's status while remaining outside it. The supplied record supports no more specific stability necessity that answers this injury. General claims of improved turnout and competition remain qualified.

**Join boundary and writing function:** He supports the insufficient-fit ground and may join a common opinion that does not make a categorical severity judgment indispensable. He reserves whether a materially different demonstrated stability need, different participation rule, or conventional open primary could be sustained. A separate concurrence would state that qualification if needed. His prior broader-remedy preference in Reform Party concerned the decrees there and is no warrant to reopen completed elections or redesign this system; he accepts the present common remedy.

### Justice Thomas

**Commitment:** Reach the merits; reverse and remand on Path A. The compulsory blanket primary severely burdens the objecting parties' choice of official nominees. California has not supplied a compelling justification fitted to this intrusion.

**Own-source basis:** His Timmons Part II validates a specified fusion restriction through the actual alternatives left by that system, not a universal rule that endorsement always cures a nomination injury. His Burdick join preserves state selection rules when the full system leaves only reasonable burdens; his Hurley join protects actual compelled expressive composition. His Reform Party joins preserve the independent significance of a legal nomination and confine relief without expanding fusion rights.

**Competing path and strongest objection:** Path B is particularly supported by his own Timmons writing: the Constitution does not give a party every desired ballot option, and state regulation need not prove past disruption. The strongest objection to Path A is that parties still can support preferred candidates, organize and campaign, just as they could in Timmons. Here, however, those activities coexist with the legally controlling nominee selected by the imposed electorate. There is no retained alternative through which the objecting party can choose its own official nominee while complying with the challenged mechanism. That difference concerns the injury, not an overruling of his earlier rule. The State's direct participation interests still must justify the severe burden; their general value does not supply that fit.

**Join boundary and remedy:** He will not join an extension of Reform Party into a freestanding fusion or nomination-mechanism right, a holding that Timmons was wrongly decided, or a categorical equation of all ballot regulation with compelled speech. He supports the common prospective remedy without a judicially prescribed replacement ballot.

### Justice Ginsburg

**Commitment:** Reach the merits; reverse and remand on Path C. The burden on the plaintiff parties' legal nomination is not sufficiently justified under contextual review, whether or not that burden is classified as severe. This does not establish a party veto over all voting qualifications or primary procedures.

**Own-source basis:** Her complete join in Stevens's Timmons dissent gives direct support for treating the actual standard-bearer choice as meaningful association and rejecting endorsement as its replacement. Her Reform Party Part II join preserves that distinction while deciding only unequal timely access. Her Hurley join protects actual expressive selection, subject to the different public-election setting here.

**Competing path and strongest objection:** Path B has the substantial argument that equal access to public primary ballots expands individual participation without suppressing party advocacy; Reform Party itself demonstrates attention to participation and equal access. The strongest objection to reversal is that associational choice in a public nominating institution cannot be identified with leaders' exclusive control of a private expressive event. The commitment therefore does not deny public regulation or individual voting rights. It holds that these interests do not on this record justify using the objecting party's official nomination to enlarge outsiders' electoral influence. Reform Party's consensual association and unequal-exclusion premise do not independently resolve that question, but they preclude treating the surviving right to endorse as restoration of the legal choice taken away.

**Join boundary and writing function:** She can join the actual-burden and inadequate-fit holding without deciding severity. She does not commit to a categorical rule for every open primary, every minor-party restriction, or every candidate carrying a party label. A concurrence joining Stevens's fit explanation would preserve her ground if the principal opinion requires a severe-burden classification. No separate remedy or independent equal-protection holding is proposed. The common confined reversal and remand suffices.

### Justice Breyer

**Commitment:** Reach the merits; reverse and remand on Path A, applied to the concrete statutory operation and trial record. The combination of official nominee status, compulsory office-by-office crossover and the predicted effect on selection and campaign behavior makes the burden severe; the asserted interests do not justify that method.

**Own-source basis:** His Timmons join permits practical electoral restrictions without strict scrutiny of every preference. His Reform Party opinion demands comparison between the actual burden and precise interests, rejects endorsement as a replacement for a denied legal opportunity in that context, and limits prospective relief to the proved application. His Hurley join supplies the analogous protection of materially altered expressive selection, not a decision that a primary is wholly private.

**Competing path and strongest objection:** Path B draws force from the trial evidence of continuing organizational vigor, uncertain California effects, nondiscriminatory operation, and his Timmons acceptance of preventive rules. The strongest objection to reversal is that practical accommodation should permit a State to test a participatory electoral reform without waiting for conclusive studies. That allowance does not eliminate the distinction between regulation of candidacy and transfer of official nominee selection. The supported prediction that some outcomes and campaign behavior will change is enough to give the statutory burden practical substance; the parties need not await collapse. California's participation, competition and privacy interests have weight, but the account supplied does not establish a necessity or adequate fit for compulsory partisan nomination by this electorate.

**Join boundary and remedy:** He supports a severe-burden conclusion tied to this mechanism and record, not a general presumption that every primary reform is severe or a requirement of numerical proof for all regulatory interests. He reserves other systems and accepts the common remedy. His Reform Party Part III position particularly bars turning this judgment into a ballot blueprint or relief beyond the unconstitutional application proved.

## Common legal scope; separable components

**A — Governing framework.** Each Associate accepts Hazelwood's legitimate-pedagogical-concern framework for the school's organization and administration of this supervised election, including the distribution of materials as part of the prescribed campaign. The contract, adviser, defined campaign period and advance review establish an educational activity in leadership and democratic participation, rather than indiscriminate private access. This conclusion does not make every candidate's proposition the school's own assertion, extinguish the student's personal interest, or authorize punishment merely because officials dislike a candidate. Boring concerns the teacher's claimed entitlement to direct institutional instruction and does not eliminate this student's protection. Santa Fe's particular official religious-choice mechanism supplies no automatic classification of all election speech.

**B — Approval rule.** Each Associate rejects the proposition that an advance-approval rule is inherently unconstitutional in this program or that Tinker's disruption condition governs every curricular restriction. Reasonable review of material, location, maturity and program suitability is permissible. None commits to wholesale facial validation of the unrecovered policy. The quoted policy has separate categories and a distinct fundraising exception, allows more than a school day for difficult material, and does not promise a fixed twenty-four-hour response. The approval requirement is separable from the lawfulness of this enforcement and sanction.

**C — Actual enforcement and summary judgment.** The Associates divide on whether the supplied evidence permits a material inference that the principal used the process rule to penalize the student's message without a genuine pedagogical ground. All preserve Settle: possible pedagogy does not establish actual lawful cause; protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role must be established, and a defendant may prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred absent it. All reject conversion of Settle into universal viewpoint neutrality for every curricular selection. The dispute concerns the supported inference on this record, not that general rule.

**D — Sanction.** The Associates divide on whether disqualification may be sustained on this summary-judgment record. None recognizes an automatic right to office from the later count or holds that every rule violation permits every sanction. Cook acted before the count revealed the majority. This case supplies no finding of punishment for winning.

**E — Defendant-specific relief.** Four Associates affirm on the merits and therefore need not decide the alternative immunity and attribution questions. Four vacate and remand for ordinary further proceedings, leaving those questions open. For either disposition, school-district liability, official-capacity liability and personal-capacity immunity are distinct. A later merits rule cannot establish April 1997 notice. No Associate finds an immunity defense proved, waived or adjudicated below from the missing material. No automatic damages, trial, discovery, reinstatement, message approval or mootness determination follows.

## Enhanced comparison of supported paths

### Classification: program control or independent expression

**Program-control path, adopted at the stated scope.** Hazelwood permits control of faculty-supervised expression designed to impart knowledge or skills, including activities outside a classroom. This election's documented structure supports the framework. The strongest objection is that competing candidates speak for themselves: a school does not adopt every campaign proposal by running an election. That objection prevents a government-speech immunity and preserves scrutiny of actual enforcement; it does not eliminate the educational program or its review conditions. Good News/Good Sports concerned independently organized private access and does not make the present activity such a forum.

**Tinker path.** Individual advocacy in school corridors can remain personal student expression, and controversy does not establish disruption or invasion of others' rights. The strongest ground for this path is the personal campaign sticker and the students' own choice among rival advocates. Its difficulty here is treating materials explicitly offered as part of the regulated campaign as wholly incidental speech on school premises despite the actual program arrangements. The Associates do not adopt that classification on this packet. If a distinct independent distribution outside the campaign were presented, it would require its own facts and Tinker analysis; no such separate adjudication is made.

### Actual cause and sanction: affirm or vacate

**Affirmance path.** An express requirement, a signed undertaking, an adviser warning that all materials required approval, and undisputed failure to seek review support the principal's stated process ground. Established tacit approval of conventional campaign candy supplies a relevant difference from a new item never submitted. On this reading, differential treatment alone does not reasonably show forbidden discrimination. Disqualification enforces the conditions of participation in the election and is not constitutionally unreasonable merely because a lesser sanction could have been selected. The strongest objection is that the tolerated unsubmitted items and approved slogan permit a contrary inference about motive, notice and the severity of the sanction. The four affirmers conclude that the established conventional practice, the specific all-materials warning and the wholly new distribution defeat that inference at the level needed to make it material. They do not find historical pretext absent after a trial; they hold the supplied inference legally insufficient under the stated facts.

**Vacatur path.** The same evidence, favorably construed, permits a factfinder to question whether the rule was actually enforced as the school now describes, and whether the abrupt elimination of candidacy reflected an educational judgment or disapproval of the message. Settle prevents deciding that dispute merely by identifying an otherwise lawful rule or conceivable content concern. The strongest objection is that conventional candy was already approved in practice and that Henerey received an explicit warning; no reasonable factfinder should equate it with novel condoms. The four vacaters treat that distinction as substantial defense evidence, but not as conclusive proof of actual cause and justified sanction against the competing inference. They do not find pretext, inadequate notice or disproportionate punishment established. Ordinary proceedings may produce another lawful summary disposition.

**Appellate content alternative, separately tested.** Hazelwood permits genuine judgments about sexual material, maturity, decorum and association; a school need not show disruption to make every such judgment. But the appellate suggestion that condoms could encourage sex is not a finding about this student's actual message or Cook's actual reason. None needs to declare safe-sex advocacy categorically unprotected. The affirmers can sustain the process ground without adopting that alternative. The vacaters reject it as an independently sufficient ground on this packet because a possible legitimate educational judgment cannot substitute for the disputed actual ground. This reservation does not prevent a later supported finding that a genuine content judgment was lawful.

## Enhanced alternative-path analysis

### Promise, suspension and materiality

**Government-judgment path.** The leases conveyed a priority to pursue uncertain development under extensive existing environmental and permitting powers, with suspensions and extensions preserving the opportunity. Independent state objections prevented drilling, and a changed review interval might produce no compensable loss. The strongest support is the whole-contract regulatory structure and the lessees' negotiated 1989 MOU. The strongest objection is that this approach collapses the promised consideration process into eventual drilling and reads a new statutory prohibition as though it were an exercise of the original conditional powers. All eight decline that collapse. Their Winstar positions require enforcing the actual undertaking with its limits, rather than either a guarantee of profits or a right to retain consideration without the purchased performance.

**Breach/process path, adopted.** The express incorporation and specified future changes identify a contracted legal route. OBPA withholds that route for a substantial period and substitutes new conditions; this materially changes the paid-for opportunity even if state concurrence might ultimately fail. The strongest objection is that the Secretary already could conduct environmental analysis or suspend, and the panel did not have a veto. Those points preserve genuine original discretion, but do not establish that the categorical legislative prohibition rested on or complied with the predicates of that discretion. No actual cancellation entitlement is presumed. All eight accept liability at this scope without determining what later delay after the congressional period was independently caused by the Secretary. The established initial displacement is sufficient; uncertain later history is not needed to enlarge it.

### Fifth lease: anticipatory repudiation or wait for an application

**Wait-for-performance path.** No plan was submitted for the fifth tract, the legislation could end, and the Government did not announce that it would never permit drilling. The strongest objection to recovery is therefore the absence of a presently denied individual application.

**Anticipatory path, adopted at the process level.** Roehm recognizes an immediate claim when a party unequivocally disables the promised future performance; the legally significant promise here is performance under the contracted route, not eventual performance under any substituted conditions. The enacted prohibition covered the fifth lease as well as the four unit leases and materially withheld that route. Its duration was qualified but not an ordinary processing interval. The petitioners need not submit a futile application merely to demonstrate that statutory bar. This does not establish a fifth-tract permit entitlement, actual drilling plan, particular benefit received, or identical dollar recovery. Restitution and any election inquiry must still treat the tract and each petitioner's interest at their supported scope.

### Election: restoration available now or further adjudication first

**Restoration-entitlement path, adopted by seven Associates.** A request that the breaching promisor perform or retract does not itself accept substitute performance or surrender the response to a material breach. The plan submission and efforts to overcome a separate state obstacle are consistent with seeking the original bargain. The promised MOU suspensions and preservation of time while the dispute proceeds do not, on these supplied facts, establish acceptance of the withdrawn process as performed. The 1992 suit gives an affirmative restoration election; subsequent preservation of contested rights is not automatically its abandonment. No new promise, settlement, retraction, successful approval, or equivalent substitute performance is found.

The strongest objection is substantial: extensions are contractual rights of value, and the companies affirmatively sought them after knowing of OBPA, including during litigation. Calling the requests mere protest would understate the defense. The seven distinguish the value of preserved time, which may bear on restoration and cognizable benefit accounting, from acceptance of the repudiated performance sufficient to foreclose the remedy. They resolve the legal defense even allowing the documented requests and extensions; they do not depend on the broader, contradicted claim that all suspensions were continuously rent-free. They do not hold that actual acceptance of materially useful substituted performance could never be an election.

**Election-first path, adopted by Stevens.** Requests to compel an original promise do not themselves waive a remedy, but obtaining and retaining extensions of the very leased opportunity can amount to continued contractual performance. The preexisting MOU character does not remove their contractual nature. The combination of the post-enactment application, pursuit of authorizations and accepted extended term therefore requires an actual election determination before a present entitlement to undo the bargain is declared. The strongest objection is that the suit made the restoration choice clear and the extensions merely preserved the dispute's subject. Stevens finds that explanation legally possible, but not conclusive from the packet's recitation alone. He would reject the no-breach rationale while remanding the properly presented election issue, including its preservation and actual scope for each petitioner and lease, before deciding entitlement. He does not find waiver established, rely on an unrecovered letter, or impute Mobil's individual conduct to Marathon without a basis.

### Restitution, expectation and amount

Restoration of transferred consideration for the unperformed bargain does not require proof that the venture would have yielded profits. Behan distinguishes proven outlay from speculative profits, but its damages analysis is not itself a complete restitution formula. Pence concerns election after fraud, not this contract's performance, and supplies only the caution against treating demand, delay or continued conduct without context as an automatic election. Roehm supplies the anticipatory framework, not an exact refund amount.
