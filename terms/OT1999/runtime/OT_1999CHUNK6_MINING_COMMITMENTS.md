## Components and common operative scope

All eight Associates participate and reach the two granted questions. None selects an author or supplies Stone's vote. All eight support the same component dispositions below, after the individual assessments recorded next.

**A. Current and former control.** Affirm the invalidation of permit blocking based merely on an applicant's former ownership or control. Section 510(c)'s ordinary rule requires a currently violating operation currently owned or controlled by the applicant. Actual indirect downstream control can satisfy control; mere upstream relationship is not substituted for the statutory direction. Once ordinary control ends, the separate former-control provision requires a demonstrated pattern of willful violations of the chapter, of the requisite nature and duration, with resulting irreparable environmental damage indicating an intent not to comply. Every one of those statutory qualifications remains necessary. Ending control does not erase a claim satisfying that provision or other independently authorized law.

The ordinary block lasts until the applicant proves that the violation is corrected **or is in the process of being corrected** to the satisfaction of the authority with jurisdiction. It does not invariably require completed correction. The three-year notice schedule and the independent use of other information perform different jobs: a violation is not outside the current-condition rule merely because it began before that schedule's period.

**B. Pre-rule dates and present eligibility.** Reverse the appellate rule insofar as it categorically prohibits using an otherwise lawful indirect-control construction for a present permit decision involving a presently controlled operation's current violation merely because both the violation and acquisition of control predate November 2, 1988. The regulated subject at that scope is current eligibility for prospective operations, on the basis of current control and a current violation. The acquisition and violation dates alone do not convert that current condition into a new disability imposed solely on a completed transaction.

The distinction is substantive, not a label: an agency cannot impose new obligations solely on completed past conduct by calling the enforcement prospective. But the ordinary rule, properly confined by component A, does not impose liability on a former controller merely for the old relationship, and present abatement or satisfactory correction in progress can satisfy it. Smiley further forecloses the lower court's premise that an interpretation is impermissibly retroactive simply because it was permissible rather than compelled before an authoritative regulation. A lawful current construction of statutory ambiguity may inform antecedent transactions without backdating the rule; the source does not establish clear contrary prior official guidance displaced here. No finding about actual reliance is made.

The appellate footnote's distinct pre-rule-violation/post-rule-acquisition application remains permissible at this temporal level. Neither it nor today's rule establishes actual control or a violation in any particular permit matter. Unauthorized new liability for wholly completed conduct, displacement of clear prior official guidance, penalties, and adjudication-specific finality objections remain outside this bounded prospective-permit decision. No general temporal immunity for old violations follows, and no general retrospective rulemaking power is recognized.

**C. Correction authority and finality.** Affirm rejection of the categorical claim that section 510(c)'s reference to permit issuance eliminates power to suspend or revoke an improvidently issued permit. Section 201(c)(1), 30 U.S.C. §1211(c), expressly authorizes suspension, revocation or withholding for failure to comply with the chapter or its valid regulations. That express ground suffices; no unlimited inherent reopening power is adopted. The correction must rest on actual statutory or valid regulatory noncompliance under the proper temporal rule, not on an agency's desire to revise a lawful completed decision.

No specific finally adjudicated agency permit, vested title or Article III judgment is before this facial rule review. The Court therefore neither declares a general finality exception nor authorizes reopening any particular final adjudication. An Article III final-judgment rule cannot be transferred wholesale to this different institution and posture.

**D. Remedy and matters outside review.** Affirm the former-control limitation and statutory correction authority, reverse the categorical pre-1988 temporal bar at component B's scope, and remand through the Court of Appeals and district court for the existing rule-correction proceedings to conform to these holdings. The earlier mandate-enforcement dismissals and the ungranted presumption, information-demand, section 2462 and separate procedural-rule dispositions remain at their existing scopes; no new merits judgment is entered on them. The reversal authorizes no named permit denial, suspension, damages award or finding of liability, and requires no general reliance study or new evidentiary program.

The statutory state-primacy limits remain. For ordinary non-imminent violations, federal intervention must respect the applicable notice, abatement opportunity, public-hearing opportunity, written finding of an unabated violation and prescribed corrective steps. The separate imminent-danger provisions remain distinct. General correction authority cannot circumvent those requirements. The proposed replacement rules mentioned below are not treated as adopted or as rendering the challenge moot.

## Justice-by-Justice assessment

### Stevens

**Commitment:** Join A–D and their common sufficient grounds.

**Authorities:** His Landgraf Court opinion (April 26, 1994) makes actual new legal consequences, rather than old facts alone, decisive. His Smiley Parts III and V joins (June 3, 1996) permit qualified current statutory interpretation for antecedent transactions and reserve clear prior guidance. His Brown & Williamson dissent (March 21, 2000) distinguishes delegated coverage from independently binding constraints on method.

**Two paths and strongest objections:** The industry's strongest temporal argument is that an old investment becomes less valuable because a new indirect-control rule adds permit ineligibility. Landgraf requires attention to such consequences. Here, however, the permissible rule addresses the applicant's continuing control and current violation when seeking present permission; it does not demand compensation for a finished acquisition. The corrected former-control rule prevents the simple attachment of an ordinary block to a completed relationship. Smiley also defeats reliance solely on prior statutory ambiguity. The opposite path of upholding every historical application would be too broad because new past-conduct liability and displaced clear guidance remain different.

**Join boundaries:** He will not join a general vested-right-only retroactivity test, unlimited reopening authority, or a reading that eliminates the former-control qualifications. He can decide the current eligibility issue directly without a speculative factual remand.

### O'Connor

**Commitment:** Join A–D at their defined scope.

**Authorities:** Her Landgraf general-framework join requires examination of the particular legal consequence. Her Smiley Parts III/V joins distinguish authoritative interpretation from automatic permission to alter old legal consequences. Her Brown & Williamson join insists on the connected statutory scheme; her Greenwich Collieries Court opinion (June 20, 1994) respects the actual statutory allocation rather than an inferred policy exception.

**Two paths and strongest objections:** A new permit date cannot alone cleanse a genuinely retrospective disability. But the current control/current violation conditions are actual continuing predicates, and the cure alternative confirms that the rule regulates present eligibility rather than imposing an unalterable sanction for a completed acquisition. The strongest State argument for retaining former-control blocks is preventing evasion through sale; Congress already supplied a carefully qualified former-control route, whose limits the agency cannot delete. Those conclusions coexist.

**Join boundaries:** Preserve every willful-pattern and cure qualification; do not import Greenwich's adjudicatory persuasion rule into the separate procedural-regulation appeal. No general finding that permit blocking is punitive or remedial is needed.

### Scalia

**Commitment:** Join A–D's common operative propositions. He does not adopt a renewed general fairness-based account of Landgraf.

**Authorities:** His Landgraf concurrence, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, identifies the activity regulated; its examples distinguish new consequences for completed conduct from future regulation affected by antecedent facts. His Smiley Parts III/V joins preserve authoritative construction within genuine ambiguity. His Brown & Williamson join and Christensen separate position (May 1, 2000) require statutory and regulatory textual fit despite agency interpretive authority.

**Two paths and strongest objections:** If the relevant activity were a completed pre-1988 acquisition with liability then newly attached, Bowen's bar would apply absent the required delegation. At the supported lawful scope, the activity is current permit eligibility involving ongoing actual control and violation; earlier acquisition is not the operative transaction being punished. The industry's reliance on the rule's first precise construction is insufficient under Smiley. Interior's textual obstacle is equally direct: present control and the separate qualified has-controlled provision cannot be made interchangeable.

**Join boundaries:** No open-ended fairness or policy exception substitutes for enacted authority. He can join a common opinion that applies the current-condition distinction and accurately states controlling Landgraf without requiring assent to its entire general methodology. Express section 1211(c) resolves correction authority without an unnecessary implied-power holding.

### Kennedy

**Commitment:** Join A–D and the same sufficiently narrow temporal proposition.

**Authorities:** His Landgraf relevant-activity concurrence join distinguishes regulated conduct from the date of litigation. His Smiley Parts III/V joins permit current authoritative interpretation in the absence of established clear contrary guidance. His Auer Court opinion (February 19, 1997) and Christensen Court opinion (May 1, 2000) preserve interpretation within text while forbidding amendment by interpretation.

**Two paths and strongest objections:** The court must not disguise a past-control penalty as a current licensing condition. Component A answers that concern by requiring the actual current relationship or the separate statutory pattern. Conversely, calling the control acquisition an old transaction does not stop the legislature's continuing statutory condition from governing present operations. Smiley defeats a categorical assumption that the choice among permissible interpretations is necessarily forbidden retroactivity. Genuine finality concerns would require an identified adjudication, absent here.

**Join boundaries:** No generic power to withdraw every previously issued permit, no importation of Article III finality into ordinary agency correction, and no deeming an unsupported control presumption valid by calling it an interpretation. Ungranted challenges retain their own dispositions.

### Souter

**Commitment:** Join A–D.

**Authorities:** His Landgraf majority join and Smiley Court opinion require attention to actual temporal operation and the force of prior guidance. His Christensen full join separates authoritative statutory interpretation from mere advisory weight and preserves the text's independent limits.

**Two paths and strongest objections:** The lower court's best point is that regulations can create new disabilities even while resolving ambiguity. Smiley agrees with that limit but rejects the inference that ambiguity and later specification alone establish forbidden retroactivity. The actual supported application concerns presently controlled operations, present violations and present permits, and the statute itself makes those conditions relevant. A completed relationship is dealt with through the separate willful-pattern provision. There is no need to order factfinding about a specific vested permit the record does not identify.

**Join boundaries:** Preserve Smiley's clear-guidance reservation; absence of an established contrary direction is not a finding nobody relied on anything. Do not treat the agency's general authority as proof of any specific factual violation or procedural compliance.

### Thomas

**Commitment:** Join A–D's shared narrow propositions, preserving the Landgraf relevant-activity approach.

**Authorities:** His Landgraf concurrence join and Smiley Parts III/V joins support the prospective-activity/antecedent-facts distinction. His Brown & Williamson and Christensen joins require actual statutory authorization and prohibit an agency from converting text into a contrary duty.

**Two paths and strongest objections:** A permit block attached solely to a finished relationship would be a materially different disability, and the ordinary current-control rule does not authorize it. Once the statute is properly confined, the contrary categorical temporal rule would immunize ongoing current violations based on the age of an acquisition even though the operative text speaks to current status. Nothing in Bowen requires that result. The statutory former-control provision cannot be bypassed for practical convenience.

**Join boundaries:** No generalized balancing of regulatory benefit against reliance, no broad implied correction power, and no adoption of unpresented stockholder, evidentiary-presumption or burden-shifting rules. No exception to the state-primacy sequence is inferred.

### Ginsburg

**Commitment:** Join A–D.

**Authorities:** Her Landgraf majority join examines actual new legal effects; her Smiley Parts III/V joins distinguish interpretive authority from backdating. Her Brown & Williamson dissent accepts that broad delegation does not erase connected statutory conditions. Her Christensen dissents (May 1, 2000) preserve attention to the enacted arrangement and to the actual basis of agency authority, not unlimited deference.

**Two paths and strongest objections:** The industry's strongest concern is that corporate acquisition decisions were completed before the agency specified indirect control. That concern would matter if the rule imposed a new completed-transaction obligation or displaced established guidance. It does not alone answer regulation of a present controlled violation, particularly where correction in progress may restore eligibility. The agency's contrary interest in preventing strategic transfers cannot erase Congress's demanding former-control provision. Ordinary correction authority remains compatible with statutory process.

**Join boundaries:** Do not presume regulatory procedure complied merely because substantive authority exists; do not invent actual reliance or finality facts; preserve the distinction between no general temporal bar and the validity of every concrete application.

### Breyer

**Commitment:** Join A–D and the current-condition temporal ground.

**Authorities:** He did not participate in Landgraf; its controlling framework governs rather than an invented join. His Smiley Parts III/V joins provide his own published support for authoritative current interpretation, reasoned change and the qualified antecedent-transaction rule. His Brown & Williamson dissent and Christensen separate grounds support lawful implementation while retaining statutory fit and independent limits.

**Two paths and strongest objections:** The argument for affirming the categorical temporal bar mistakes every present regulatory burden linked to old facts for retroactive legislation. The operative conditions are ongoing, and Congress expressly permits denial until completed or satisfactory ongoing correction. At the same time, the agency cannot evade a completed-transaction problem simply by choosing a present enforcement date. Enforcing the actual current/former-control distinction resolves the supported rule challenge without speculative balancing or an undefined administrative study.

**Join boundaries:** No universal deference to an agency's temporal characterization; no disregard of actual statutory qualifications; no general reopening command or finding about particular permits. The correction remand follows the identified defects only.
