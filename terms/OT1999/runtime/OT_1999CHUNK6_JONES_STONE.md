## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Owner-occupied dwelling used only for ordinary family life

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the merits of this issue. No separate threshold or vehicle bar identified in Section I prevents decision.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reverse. “Used in” commerce requires the building's active commercial employment; a home used only for family life does not qualify merely because it carries an interstate mortgage or insurance policy, receives utilities, contains out-of-state materials, or was once rented. A current rental property, business premises, or materially mixed commercial use is different.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** An owner-occupied dwelling used only for family life is not property “used in” interstate commerce under § 844(i); passive, past, or ordinary incidents of home ownership do not satisfy the active-use element.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reverse. “Used in” commerce requires the building's active commercial employment; a home used only for family life does not qualify merely because it carries an interstate mortgage or insurance policy, receives utilities, contains out-of-state materials, or was once rented. A current rental property, business premises, or materially mixed commercial use is different. The outer constitutional boundary is reserved. Hinds directs attention to what the property does. Utility, insurance and financing relationships attend ownership; they do not show this home’s active employment in the regulated commerce. Stone’s reversal applies the statutory element without announcing a new Commerce Clause test or authorizing additional proof to repair evidentiary insufficiency.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide whether Congress constitutionally could reach farther or exclude rental, business, and materially mixed-use properties from the statute.
