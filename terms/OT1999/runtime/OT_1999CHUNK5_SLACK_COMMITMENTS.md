## Competing legal paths and their boundaries

**Appellate-date COA versus pending-case CPC.** Applying section 2253 to a post-enactment appeal gives effect to the event that its text regulates: taking an appeal. It does not change the law governing adjudication of the 1995 application. Felker's provision-specific treatment of post-enactment authorization/review and Hohn's recognition of certificate adjudication as judicial business support that distinction. The strongest objection is Lindh's chapter comparison and a single case continuing from application through appeal; however, Lindh preserved separately directed questions and Hohn deliberately left this one open. The opposing route would retain former CPC treatment for the whole pending case. The commitments adopt the appellate-date construction. The State's earlier contrary legal position cannot authorize an appeal without a certificate required by the governing statute; this conclusion does not label every subsection jurisdictional or excuse every litigation forfeiture. The CPC label does not prevent consideration as the proper certificate request.

**Procedural certificates: prohibition, procedure alone, or two necessary showings.** The State's categorical position would make an erroneous procedural dismissal unreviewable even when it blocks a substantial constitutional claim. Hohn's separation of claim characterization from eventual relief and Barefoot's meaningful threshold review weigh against that reading. Conversely, a substantial procedural objection alone does not satisfy Congress's constitutional requirement. The commitments adopt a threshold requiring reasonable debate both about a valid constitutional claim and about the procedural dismissal. This is a legal conclusion, not an attributed agreement among the parties. Bare allegations or procedural error alone do not guarantee a certificate; final constitutional success is not required. A court may resolve the more apparent failing threshold first without a mandatory order or an unnecessary merits adjudication.

**Exhaustion return as the first merits application versus independently abusive new grounds.** The first path reads the opening second-or-successive requirement in Rule 9(b) with the exhaustion-dismissal setting, former section 2244(b), Rose's complete-exhaustion procedure and Lonchar's insistence on lawful dismissal predicates. With no prior federal adjudication and a without-prejudice return contemplated, the renewed application remains the first opportunity for federal merits review, including additional claims. The strongest objection is the new-ground clause's omission of an express merits qualifier, Sanders's recognition of abuse after a nonmerits denial, and the burden of serial incomplete filings. The six adopting this path distinguish a complete-exhaustion dismissal from a deficient pleading or a completed merits application and retain other lawful sanctions and barriers.

The second path gives Rule 9(b)'s new-ground clause independent operation: earlier omitted grounds can be abusive without a prior merits decision, while repeated grounds remain subject to the first clause's merits requirement. McCleskey supplies particularized State pleading and the applicant's responsive cause/prejudice or miscarriage showing. The strongest objection is that this treats compliance with the first exhaustion dismissal as a forfeiture of additional claims and gives inadequate weight to the opening second-or-successive phrase and former section 2244(b). Scalia and Thomas adopt the new-ground path at this setting's confined scope. They do not claim that every nonmerits dismissal, every amendment or the mere existence of a prior pleading automatically establishes abuse.

## Shared certificate propositions

Each Associate adopts S0, S1 and S2 at the following scope:

1. Hohn permits review of this certificate denial; it does not issue the certificate or determine the constitutional merits.
2. Amended section 2253 governs the applicant's appeal initiated after April 24, 1996, although the identified pre-AEDPA law continues to govern the earlier district-court application under Lindh. A party's legal concession does not change that required certificate regime. No general claim-by-claim temporal rule for every post-enactment amendment is adopted.
3. When dismissal rests on procedure without reaching the constitutional claim, a certificate requires a showing that reasonable jurists could debate whether the petition states a valid claim of denial of a constitutional right and whether the district court's procedural ruling was correct. Both showings are required; the court may address the one whose answer is more apparent. A plain, correctly applied procedural bar can defeat certification without a needless constitutional-merits ruling. Where merits were actually rejected, the ordinary substantial showing asks whether reasonable jurists could debate the constitutional assessment or find it wrong; that is background explanation, not a finding that Slack's claims received merits adjudication.
4. Certification remains a threshold inquiry, not proof of entitlement to the writ, and a qualifying certificate must identify its issues. Neither the notice nor this Court's review itself supplies certification. Rule 22 permits the request to be considered under the correct label.

## Rule scope, disposition consequences and unresolved matters

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support this additional proposition: when the first federal application is dismissed without prejudice to allow completion of state exhaustion before any federal merits adjudication, the renewed application is not second or successive merely because it follows the dismissed filing or includes additional grounds. Their rule concerns this exhaustion-return setting. It does not automatically cover every nonmerits dismissal, a previously adjudicated petition, a genuinely abusive maneuver under an independently applicable rule, or unlimited serial amendments. A claim first appearing in the 1997 amendment does not by itself supply the missing successive-application predicate; the commitments do not decide every possible claim-date temporal question because that predicate fails here.

Those six would vacate the July 7 certificate refusal and remand to the Ninth Circuit for application of the amended certificate requirements. The classification holding supplies the procedural answer on this issue. The lower court must still decide the required substantial constitutional showing for the claims properly presented for certification and identify the qualifying issues if it grants a certificate. A certificate authorizes only the specified appeal; it is not a writ, a merits reversal of every district ruling or a finding that every issue may proceed.

Scalia and Thomas support the shared S0–S2 propositions but reject that S3 classification rule and the resulting S4 disposition. They would retain the new-ground abuse basis on the record's pleading and response history and affirm the certificate denial on the granted question. They do not find the additional constitutional claims meritless, find deliberate withholding as a new fact, merge the repeated-claim and new-ground branches, or extend the granted review into a fresh decision of every exhaustion objection. Their procedural conclusion supplies their reason for refusing the requested appellate relief; disagreement over that legal conclusion does not replace the adopted threshold with an arithmetic test of judicial votes.

Under the six-Associate remand, the separately unexhausted ground 3 and the State's unresolved exhaustion objections to the five abuse-dismissed claims retain their actual posture. Removing abuse is not a finding of state presentation, an excuse for a proven adequate and independent state default, or immunity from Edwards where its real predicates arise. The lower court's old thirty-day abandonment/reopening language is not restarted. No fresh limitations period, unconditional right to amend, mandatory evidentiary hearing, trial, release, vacatur of the conviction or stay is ordered. Both Williams decisions retain their proper domains; neither automatically imposes amended section 2254(d) on this pre-enactment application or triggers section 2254(e)(2) because a merits record is incomplete.
