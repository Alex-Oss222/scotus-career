## Intervening Southworth law and individual compatibility

Its controlling rationale requires that neutrality within the program and rejects majority approval as a substitute. It does not extend a forum rule to all university expenditures, does not make every useful subsidy permissible and does not decide public-nudity regulation. Compelled financing can burden expression without personal attribution; lack of attribution alone does not justify the charge. The referendum receives separate examination because it falls outside the neutrality stipulation, not because every voting process is conclusively invalid.

Their agreement is not a rationale join. All retain the prohibition on disguised viewpoint exclusion within the private-expression benefit actually opened.

The Erie judgments and grounds below were reconsidered against that actual law. None changes. The distinction between a neutral explanation and the actual operation of a measure does reinforce the need to examine Erie's ordinance, Council record and claimed effects. It does not establish that all public-conduct rules are content based or that a general-law label immunizes censorship. *Southworth* supplies no new mootness, state-judgment jurisdiction, *Marks*, public-nudity or severability rule.

## Record and operative ordinance limits

Section 1(c) reaches knowingly or intentionally appearing nude in a public place. Section 2 includes insufficiently opaque covering of the specified genitals, pubic area, buttocks and female nipple, and the specified simulated anatomy and realistic nipple/areola coverings. Section 3 reaches outdoor public places and the listed enclosed premises, including theaters and clubs restricted to members, adults or invited patrons, with or without admission charges. Section 4 exempts children under ten and breast exposure in nursing an infant under two. Section 6 calls for the least constitutional infringement consistent with the ordinance's purposes and permits severance of a portion, application or enforcement. It supplies no enacted theater exemption. The complete operative quotation remains in the record-extract annex; no omitted penalty provision is decided.

Pap's provided nude dancing at Kandyland in a commercial area. It did not sell alcohol but allowed patrons to bring it. Those agreed facts do not establish crime at Kandyland. The Council transcript is evidence of mixed purposes: it includes traditional indecency claims, moral and erotic-message objections, concerns about alcohol and crime, and an assertion that minimal covering leaves adult entertainment commercially possible. None is a stipulated crime finding. The City expressly refused to stipulate that the enactment was solely moral or religious. Neither a legislator's favorable assessment of some theater nor the construction clause becomes an authoritative exception. The source conflict between October 12 and October 20, 1994 as the effective date remains immaterial to this prospective dispute; no interval prosecution or penalty is supplied.

The state majority applied federal strict scrutiny, reasoned that the asserted effects were intertwined with erotic communication, and separately found no narrow fit between minimal clothing and preventing sex crimes. Its observation that the City's state briefing omitted a strict-scrutiny presentation is not a Supreme Court litigation default. It severed sections 1(c) and 2 under state severability law and left the intercourse, deviate-intercourse and genital-fondling prohibitions intact. It did not decide the Pennsylvania Constitution, substantial overbreadth or the standing objection to overbreadth. The two-Justice state constitutional concurrence supplies no controlling independent ground.

## Threshold paths and supported conclusions

**Continuing reviewability.** The City seeks first federal review of an adverse state judgment that has removed two ordinance provisions and disabled their enforcement. The injury is the present legal effect of that judgment, not an abstract desire to defend legislation or a conjecture that Pap's will reopen. *ASARCO* recognizes that a state judgment can supply concrete injury to the party first invoking federal judicial power where actual adversity and tangible legal consequences remain. The corporate respondent still exists and defends its judgment. The federal invalidation's continuing scope distinguishes an individual prospective employment action in federal court after the employee leaves covered work. The judgment can be reversed on its federal ground without deciding that Pap's would now have standing to commence a new federal action.

The strongest objection is that a continuing adverse judgment alone cannot preserve every case after the underlying dispute ends. *ASARCO* itself distinguishes moot appeals and requires a genuine controversy; *Arizonans* and *Laidlaw* reject fees, generalized interests and speculative recurrence. Here the state judgment actually severs the challenged law rather than merely protecting the closed premises from a prospective enforcement threat. On the reviewability path, that concrete municipal disability and the parties' adverse claim to the continuing judgment supply the requisite stake. The path neither invents a reopening plan nor declares every facial constitutional judgment permanently reviewable regardless of its effects.

**Mootness.** Pap's closure and sale, its representation that it retains no interest in any nude-dancing establishment, and the owner's retirement can establish the end of its prospective personal controversy. Mere corporate survival does not prove realistic recurrence. Fees alone do not supply a merits case. On this path, *ASARCO*'s original-standing rule does not extend to a business controversy that later ceased; the City's broad enforcement interest cannot substitute for a currently affected opposing claimant. *Laidlaw*'s demanding defendant-cessation rule does not automatically burden a plaintiff who leaves the regulated activity while the defendant keeps defending the law.

The strongest objection is the continuing legal injury to the City from a state, rather than federal, judgment. This path must answer that injury and cannot simply copy the federal-court dismissal disposition in *Arizonans*. State courts are not bound by Article III; a moot federal appeal does not authorize a command that Pennsylvania dismiss its action for lack of federal jurisdiction. *ASARCO*'s state-court discussion supports dismissal of federal review without erasing an otherwise competent state adjudication; any requested vacatur requires its own sufficient equitable basis under *Bancorp*. The City's involuntary loss of review weighs for relief, but no strategic manufacture of mootness is found and no automatic vacatur follows from that single consideration.

## Merits paths, precedent and join boundaries

**Sustain this application under expressive-conduct scrutiny.** The text regulates public nudity without an expressive-intent element, reaches nonexpressive settings and leaves erotic performance with minimal covering available. The preamble identifies crime and health effects. Under *O'Brien*, *Ward*, *Renton* and the supported part of *Barnes*, a city may draw reasonable inferences from relevant experience concerning adult establishments; it need not wait for local injuries or produce a new local study in every case. Mixed motive remarks do not automatically make the enacted rule content based. The modest clothing condition can be treated as a limited response to the particular adult-business effects while leaving substantial communication available.

The strongest objection is specific, not merely an accusation of moral purpose: this new enactment expressly addresses nude entertainment, the Council repeatedly identified erotic stimulation as indecent, and the City has not demonstrated that the final increment of covering reduces the harms alleged. *Ino Ino* upheld concrete physical arrangements on supported contact and detection grounds while invalidating suppression of the visible message. The sustaining path answers that the public-nudity rule concerns the live physical condition under a general prohibition, not pictures or merely being visible outside; *Barnes* supports a narrower inference for this familiar adult-entertainment setting. The path cannot extend that inference without support to every dramatic production, simulated anatomical costume or other expressive application.

**Invalidate this application as suppression of the expressive component.** The new law's identified target, the Council record and the absence of a demonstrated physical-harm connection permit the conclusion that the law suppresses the erotic communication itself. A small amount of cloth is a small physical burden but directly changes the expressive element selected. *Forsyth* forbids relabeling a message-dependent reaction as a neutral secondary effect; *Ino Ino* makes the distinction concrete in cabaret regulation. Under strict scrutiny, crime prevention is compelling but does not justify this means on this record, given the missing relationship and available direct controls.

The strongest contrary argument is that the ordinance applies beyond performances and *Barnes* sustained a substantially similar covering requirement. This path does not deny its judgment or turn its separate rationales into nothing. It distinguishes Erie's demonstrated communicative target and applies the later controlling insistence on actual nonsuppressive justification. Nor does it declare all adult-business effects communicative; *Ino Ino*'s supported physical controls remain valid. A legislature's single ill-chosen remark would not suffice, and a direct-crime alternative is not a universal least-restrictive-means rule under intermediate scrutiny.

**Apply intermediate scrutiny but require an actual fit decision.** The state's proposition that every secondary effect necessarily operates through the dance's message is too broad. Yet rejecting that proposition does not establish that this ordinance is justified. The state court evaluated narrow tailoring under strict scrutiny and offered alternatives; it did not decide whether the existing mixed evidence, permissible relevant outside experience and the actual scope of the law satisfy *Ino Ino* and *O'Brien*. A limited remand can require that decision without holding the present record sufficient, compelling new studies or reopening evidence automatically. Lack of a source in the research packet is not itself proof of a litigant's evidentiary failure.

*Barnes* is not converted into a five-Justice secondary-effects rationale by adding Scalia's judgment concurrence to Souter's. Souter's crime-effects rationale and the plurality's general public-order/morality rationale are not the same explanation, and Scalia rejected the required scrutiny. Even if their results overlap on some facts, a logical subset must be demonstrated before a generalized *Marks* rule is announced. Here *Ino Ino* supplies direct controlling expressive-conduct rules, and each earlier writing supplies the Justice-specific comparison. No Associate adopts a new *Marks* formula or treats the lower state court's abbreviated description of Scalia's position as superior to his actual writing.

## Disposition and compatibility limits

The supported operative positions are distinct. Scalia would dismiss federal review as moot; his conditional merits position cannot be counted as an operative rationale or judgment join. Stevens would affirm the federal protection and existing state severance. Ginsburg would vacate and remand for the intermediate-scrutiny determination without ruling that the ordinance satisfies it. O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer would reverse the federal invalidation of the granted adult-establishment application and remand for the unadjudicated state and other preserved questions. These statements are individual Associate positions, not a final Court disposition.

The sustaining positions do not require agreement on a morality-only theory, Scalia's general-law approach, a broad *Marks* rule or the validity of every ordinance application. Souter's narrower secondary-effects ground and theatrical reservation are material, as are Thomas's and Breyer's direct *Ino Ino* basis rather than nonexistent *Barnes* joins. Ginsburg's agreement that a nonsuppressive effects theory is legally available is not agreement that the City has established the required fit. Stevens's willingness to preserve genuinely physical operational rules is not assent to this covering restriction.

No path awards damages or fees, orders Pap's to reopen, finds a crime, specifies a replacement ordinance or retrospectively imposes a penalty. A federal merits reversal removes the federal ground of invalidity within the decided scope; it does not command the state court to abandon stronger state protection. A federal affirmance does not rewrite the Pennsylvania severability rule. Mootness permits no merits holding and does not make Article III a command to the state judiciary.
