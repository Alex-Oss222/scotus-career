## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Mootness

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone holds that the consolidated matter remains live and therefore reaches the statutory merits. The 1992 plan's legal status can affect the lawful baseline against which the next covered voting change is measured.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** A preclearance controversy is not moot when the challenged plan retains a concrete legal consequence by supplying, or materially affecting, the baseline for a later § 5 comparison. The fact that the plan will not govern another election does not eliminate that consequence.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The case is not moot because the validity of preclearance for the 1992 plan can affect the baseline governing the next covered voting change.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The next redistricting cycle does not erase the legal significance of the present preclearance judgment. Section 5 compares a proposed change against the lawful operative baseline, so whether the 1992 plan lawfully entered that baseline remains a concrete issue with future legal effect. Stone therefore proceeds to the statutory questions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not hold that every superseded voting plan remains perpetually reviewable; the continuing baseline consequence is the material feature here.
### Issue 2: Section 5 effects

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the effects question. The parties' nonretrogression premise permits direct application of the controlling simulated rule.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies *Bossier Parish I* without change. Section 5's effects prong asks whether the proposed change worsens protected voters' effective electoral position relative to the lawful operative baseline. Possible § 2 liability, the availability of a majority-Black district, or a more favorable alternative does not substitute for retrogression.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The 1992 plan does not fail § 5's effects prong merely because it may preserve dilution or because a more minority-favorable plan was available; prohibited effect requires retrogression from the lawful baseline.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The parties stipulated that the plan was not retrogressive. Under the controlling simulated rule, that defeats the effects theory while leaving a separate § 2 action and the distinct § 5 purpose inquiry untouched.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not decide whether the plan violates § 2, require proportional representation, or require maximization of Black voting strength.
### Issue 3: Section 5 discriminatory purpose

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the purpose question because the controlling 1997 decision already fixed the scope of § 5’s purpose clause and the present judgment requires application of that rule to the developed record.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone applies the controlling 1997 rule. Nonretrogression is not a safe harbor from the separate purpose clause. The full record must be considered, and the covered jurisdiction bears the statutory burden to establish absence of prohibited purpose.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 5 bars preclearance of a nonretrogressive voting change adopted with an unconstitutional racial purpose to deny or abridge voting rights. On this record, the Board has not carried its burden to establish absence of that purpose.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The 1997 decision already resolved the legal scope of purpose. The districting sequence, rejected alternatives, historical resistance, racially polarized conditions, asserted neutral explanations, and evidence of pretext must therefore be assessed together rather than excluded as merely relevant to § 2. The record supports material influence by prohibited purpose, and the Board has not established the contrary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Racial awareness, disparate impact, rejection of a more minority-favorable plan, or possible § 2 liability does not alone establish prohibited purpose. The finding rests on the combined evidence and the jurisdiction’s statutory burden.
### Issue 4: Same-decision showing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the same-decision question because the purpose evidence and the Board's asserted lawful explanations require a rule for mixed-purpose preclearance, and the earlier simulated decision expressly reserved that question.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Stone openly resolves the same-decision question left open in 1997. A covered jurisdiction may overcome proof that prohibited purpose materially influenced adoption only by establishing that independently sufficient lawful reasons would have produced the identical plan.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When prohibited racial purpose materially influenced a covered voting change, the jurisdiction may obtain preclearance only by proving that independently sufficient lawful reasons would have produced the identical change. The Board has not made that showing.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The rule preserves ordinary mixed-motive discipline without weakening § 5’s allocation of the preclearance burden. It avoids treating any unlawful consideration as automatically outcome-determinative, but it requires proof of the actual identical decision rather than a list of plausible neutral interests. Convenience, continuity, precinct administration, and incumbent considerations do not establish that these same lines would have been adopted absent the prohibited purpose.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Stone does not shift the burden to private challengers, require proof that every lawful objective was pretextual, or decide the consequence of a materially different lawful plan.
