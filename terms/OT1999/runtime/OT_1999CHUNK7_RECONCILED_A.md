**Question, record and vehicle:** The reviewed judgment is the First Circuit's ordinary collateral dismissal, 178 F.3d 34, No. 96-2355. **Shared disposition:** All eight Associates affirm the reviewed collateral dismissal. No part of the separate authorization refusal is affirmed, vacated or reviewed. **Adversarial alternative:** The strongest contrary construction treats bankruptcy allowance as a uniform federal procedural system: once the trustee rebuts the proof of claim, the creditor must persuade. ## Ohler v. United States — May 22, 2000

**Validated inputs and posture:** Separately validated `OT_1999CHUNK7_NEUTRAL_OHLER.md`, its entering-law slice, and the already authorized public Current-Term Neutral Projection's Hill (January 11, 2000) and Martinez-Salazar (January 19, 2000) paragraphs. The Ninth Circuit, 169 F.3d 1200, barred review after Ohler introduced her prior methamphetamine-possession conviction on direct examination following a timely objection and definitive adverse Rule 609 ruling. The prosecutor then elicited that it was a felony; redirect distinguished possession for personal use from distribution. The circuit did not decide the Rule 609 balance. Neither the exclusion under Rule 404(b) nor the challenged impeachment ruling is reversed on its merits by a preservation decision.

**Term-effective source limit:** Original Rule 103(a)'s substantial-right, timely-objection and specific-ground requirements govern. The definitive-ruling/no-renewal amendment effective December 1, 2000 supplies no rule on this date. Rule 609(a)(1)'s accused-specific requirement is that qualifying felony impeachment's probative value outweigh its prejudicial effect to the accused; its other-witness and dishonesty provisions remain separate. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse that preservation ruling and remand for ordinary review of admissibility and any harmless error. **Adversarial test and exact disagreement:** The affirmance path rests on a recognized distinction between receiving adverse evidence over objection and choosing to offer that evidence oneself. Its four supporters treat intentional introduction as the relevant surrender of the exclusion claim, without inventing an express appellate-waiver colloquy or invoking plain-error review for mere inaction. The reversal path rests on the preserved objection to a definitive judicial ruling and the difference between mitigating expected damage and approving that ruling. Neither side treats the prosecutor's felony question as proof of every unasked contingency.
