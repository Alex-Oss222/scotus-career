Source: official U.S. Reports 530 U.S. 392, 425–427, available in `../sources/chunk8/b/arizona-official-control-only.pdf` and corresponding text. This source extraction does not resolve the quoted documents' legal effect.

1. The official report at 425–426 reproduces, citing the State parties’ exception brief at 8–9, a representation by counsel for the United States in the original proceedings that its testimony and maps defined the maximum water claim it was asserting. The report also reproduces the original Master’s warning that omitted potential claims could be lost in a decree settling the controversy. This establishes that the reported original-proceeding representation and warning are material source evidence; it does not itself establish what claim identity or binding scope follows. The underlying transcript and State brief have not been independently recovered in this extraction.

2. The report at 426–427 describes the factual materials underlying the United States’ enlarged Fort Yuma claim as possessed at the original proceedings. The current packet already establishes that the later Interior action changed the government’s legal view. No new post-decree physical boundary event or newly discovered underlying fact is established here.

3. At 427 the report identifies the original objection to deciding Fort Mojave/Colorado River boundaries as the absence of private claimants whose land title or political jurisdiction could be prejudiced; it cites Arizona II, 460 U.S. 605, 629. It contrasts the asserted Fort Yuma dispute as between the United States and Tribe, without private title claimants. Treat the contrast as a published characterization requiring independent neutral evaluation, not a finding resolving whether Fort Yuma could lawfully have been adjudicated in Arizona I.

4. A legal alternative for neutral assessment is that continuing-decree adjustment language preserves a capacity to modify upon a lawful final boundary determination but does not itself settle whether an unasserted claim can obtain such a determination. The converse construction remains supported by the existing neutral packet. This is an analytical construction of already supplied language, not a new fact or invented litigant concession.
