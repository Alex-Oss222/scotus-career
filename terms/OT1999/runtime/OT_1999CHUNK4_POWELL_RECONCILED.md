## Posture, threshold matters and exact instruments

Count I invokes 34 C.F.R. §100.3(b)(2); Count II invokes §1983. The complaint seeks declaratory and prospective injunctive relief, not damages. The individual children's alleged racially unequal treatment in distribution of state educational funding supplies the presented concrete comparative injury; it is not simply a generalized wish for better schools. Redressability is not defeated by speculation that local sources might change. Statutory enforceability is separate from Article III standing. State officials sued prospectively are not categorically nonpersons under §1983; Will and Ex parte Young defeat that stated ground below without establishing liability or relief against every defendant. The municipal plaintiffs' separate standing need not be decided where the individual controversy suffices.

The funding comparisons, regression and educational harm remain allegations. No statistical model, discriminatory purpose, causation, actual amount, recipient status, statewide funding responsibility or ultimate violation is found. The lower opinion's trial burden framework is not adopted merely by permitting the complaint to proceed. No new right to equal dollar expenditures follows from either side's enforcement analysis.

Section 601 protects individuals from exclusion, denial of benefits and discrimination on race, color or national origin in federally assisted programs; its established intentional-discrimination action remains. Section 602 separately authorizes and directs agencies to effectuate that protection through general rules consistent with the objectives of the funding statute; it addresses implementation and public enforcement, including approval requirements, not an express private cause of action. The disputed regulation prohibits a covered recipient's specified criteria or methods of administering services, aid, benefits or facilities when they have the proscribed discriminatory effects on individuals or defeat or substantially impair program objectives for protected individuals. Its recipient, program, practice, protected-status and causal terms remain indispensable; it is not a prohibition of every statistical disparity.

Agency termination or refusal of funding under §602 requires the prescribed express finding after hearing opportunity and is confined to the recipient and program or part found noncompliant. The agency must advise the proper persons and find voluntary compliance unavailable before covered enforcement action. A termination/refusal requires a report to the appropriate congressional committees and the thirty-day waiting period. Other means authorized by law remain distinct. These conditions do not become private exhaustion rules, and a private-action ruling orders no funding cutoff.

## Affirmance grounds: Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer

**Direct route.** These four would recognize prospective private enforcement of this particular authorized prohibition on discriminatory administration of covered benefits. Congress's beneficiary-centered individual protection in §601, its express §602 delegation to implement that protection, and the established private-action setting recognized in Cannon support reading the implementing effects prohibition with the underlying individual right. The grant is not inferred solely from the desirability of more enforcement, scarce agency resources or the regulation's validity. They do not announce that every valid agency rule creates a private action.

**Section 1983 route.** These four independently identify the right in the particular statutory/regulatory combination: the named beneficiaries' protection from the prohibited racially discriminatory administration of covered educational benefits, elaborated by the authorized effects regulation. It is not an abstract right to good administration, sufficient funding, specified staffing or a preferred statewide formula. Blessing requires intended individual benefit, a binding duty and a sufficiently definite entitlement; the regulation supplies concrete content in the setting of §601's identified beneficiaries. Wright permits regulatory elaboration rather than treating all implementing rules as legally irrelevant.

Once this right is identified, §1983's presumption applies. Section 602's administrative oversight and funding sanctions furnish no comparable personal remedial procedure or established incompatibility. Blessing and actual Bruneau reject categorical foreclosure of an identified individual right merely from general funding administration or an existing implied remedy. Failure to imply a direct action would not itself foreclose this enacted vehicle. Conversely, a finding of no individual right would defeat it before the foreclosure inquiry.

The strongest opposing construction treats the effect standard as an additional agency condition beyond §601's intentional-discrimination entitlement, while §602 speaks only to agencies. The four answer through the particular integration of beneficiary protection and express implementation, not by claiming §602 alone is an individual-right clause. Guardians and Choate support the regulatory effects account but do not automatically decide the private remedy. They preserve the intent requirement for the distinct §601 claim while holding that proving this regulatory violation does not additionally require proving §601 intent.

If these grounds control, affirm the appellate reversal of dismissal at the scope of the permitted prospective claims and remand for ordinary further adjudication. No guaranteed trial, automatic discovery program, immediate equalization, liability finding, mandated appropriation or damages award follows. A surviving complaint does not prove every allegation legally sufficient on every independent element.

## Reversal grounds: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas

**Direct route.** These four would reject a directly implied impact-only regulatory action. Congress supplied an individual statutory protection against intentional discrimination and agency authority to implement it. The validity of an agency condition does not itself demonstrate congressional intent to create the particular private remedy asserted for the broader effects obligation. Cannon's established action to enforce the statutory right does not automatically expand to every implementing condition. The remedy cannot create its own substantive predicate.

**Section 1983 route.** These four would reject the asserted impact-only entitlement because the right conferred by §601 is intentional nondiscrimination, while §602's agency instructions do not independently confer the broader individual right alleged. The regulation can define the content of an existing statutory entitlement, as in Wright, but cannot alone create the missing broader individual statutory entitlement in this case. This is a right-creation conclusion, not a finding that Congress comprehensively foreclosed an otherwise existing right through funding sanctions. It does not make failure of a direct implied action dispositive of §1983 availability.

The opposing integrated reading is substantial: identified beneficiaries, express implementation and a definite recipient-facing prohibition can resemble enforceable regulatory elaboration. The four distinguish that circumstance from extending the substantive statutory right beyond its established intent boundary. They do not assert that qualitative rights are always too vague, that regulations never matter, or that all funding statutes are privately unenforceable. Blessing, Suter and the CAPTA investigation decision require the actual entitlement, and Bruneau preserves enforcement of an already established Title IX right without enlarging the duty.

If these grounds control, reverse recognition of both impact-only private theories and remand for a mandate fitted to those theories. Leave agency enforcement and the regulation's validity unresolved rather than invalidating them. The complaint had no separate statutory §601 count; counsel's suggestion that discovery might show intent is neither an adjudicated intentional claim nor an automatic right to amend. Any genuinely preserved theory or ordinary amendment question keeps its actual procedural footing. No general dismissal for want of Article III standing, categorical immunity for prospective state officers, or finding of nondiscrimination follows.
