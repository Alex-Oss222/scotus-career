# Carter: proposed neutral analytical supplement

Scope: the June 12, 2000 Carter statutory-inclusion question. This is a proposed legal-alternatives supplement for independent neutral validation and affected modeling. It supplies no current-case vote, disposition, opinion, attribution, commitment recommendation, or new fact. It does not replace the approved brief or existing frozen packet. Research cutoff: immediately before June 12, 2000; entering law effective through June 5.

## Existing record and source support

The actual requested charge at Joint Appendix 4–9 asks for bank larceny and includes a value finding above an obsolete $100 figure. The applicable statute uses $1,000. The existing packet already establishes that distinction. The indictment at JA2–3 alleges approximately $14,000 in the charged forcible-or-intimidating taking. The statutory-elements inquiry does not become a comparison of this indictment's surplus allegations or the amount shown at trial.

The petitioner's actual merits brief, printed pp. 40–44, develops the relationship between basic larceny, its higher and lower grades, and robbery. It argues that the value division grades one larceny offense and advocates treating the threshold as a sentencing factor. Its account of historical Almendarez-Torres cannot be used as current law: the supplied simulated Almendarez-Torres holding requires the aggravated prior-conviction element. The reply, printed pp. 15–17, repeats the basic-larceny/grading distinction and disputes deriving element status simply from the felony/misdemeanor distinction. Neither passage is treated here as an adopted holding.

The Government's actual merits brief, printed pp. 25–29, responds that the two paragraphs define complete grades, that the felony value threshold must be charged and proved, and that robbery lacks that threshold. It identifies the indictment distinction between felony and misdemeanor prosecutions. Its separately stated evidence argument, pp. 43–46, remains independent of statutory inclusion. The reply's pp. 17–20 dispute that alternative and distinguish sufficiency to convict of robbery from the rational possibility of acquitting of robbery while convicting of larceny. Those contested applications remain as already supplied in the neutral packet.

## Additional analytical alternative requiring evaluation

The following is a developed legal possibility, not a representation that either party filed or preserved this exact formulation. Accepting the felony threshold as an element need not end analysis of every larceny instruction. First determine whether the basic lower-grade offense has an affirmative upper-value element or instead punishes the underlying theft without requiring the Government to prove a particular amount. The latter reading would avoid the anomalous defense that proof of stealing more than $1,000 defeats a charge of basic larceny. Whether its intent and movement requirements are included in the charged robbery must still be decided independently.

If basic larceny is necessarily included, a further argument is available: the defendant's own request could elect the higher grade of that same included crime, with the value element submitted to the jury, while relinquishing the notice objection that ordinarily protects him from an uncharged aggravated grade. The argument would distinguish an election between grades of an already included crime from a request for any independently related crime merely because the trial evidence supports it. It must explain why the defendant may make this election although the prosecutor could not impose the same new grade without the defendant's consent. No waiver is assumed as an already found historical procedural fact, and the existing requested instruction is not retroactively rewritten.

The strongest objection is substantial. [Schmuck, 489 U.S. 705, 716–720](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/489/705), requires statutory inclusion, rejects the evidence-based inherent-relationship approach, and treats reciprocal availability to the prosecution and defense as supporting the elements test. At 718 it expressly identifies the notice-waiver asymmetry as a defect in the rejected approach. A proposed limited election rule must show why grading an independently included theft is materially different or acknowledge that it would limit that reciprocity principle. Merely saying that the defendant requested the charge does not answer the objection.

[Stirone, 361 U.S. 212, 215–219](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/361/212), protects trial on the grand jury's actual charge against enlargement by prosecutor or court. It establishes the constitutional concern; it does not itself decide this requested-grade election. The existing indictment's amount, the legal sufficiency of a charge, factual notice, waiver of indictment protection, and waiver of jury determination are separate matters. An ordinary factual admission cannot be used to collapse them.

The actual simulated Almendarez-Torres and Jones entries in `entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK8_A.md`, and Castillo's public entry in `entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK8_C_CURRENT_PUBLIC.md`, must supply the governing aggravated-element and charging rules. They do not decide this particular Rule 31(c) election. Analyze their operative limits, including the distinction between an admission of a fact and a valid admission as an element of a charged offense. No general constitutional maximum-fact rule is supplied.

## Required decisional distinctions

The affected modeling should separately address: felony value as an element; the lower grade's value structure; theft intent and carrying away under the charged first paragraph of subsection (a); the proposed limited election and its reciprocity/indictment objection; actual requested-charge scope; and the independent rational-jury prerequisite. Agreement that felony value is an element does not identify an answer to the other questions.

An inclusion decision would remove only the categorical legal bar unless the remaining instruction-entitlement issues are lawfully resolved. It does not require acquittal, an immediate larceny conviction, or automatic new trial. A decision rejecting the higher-grade election need not silently decide that basic larceny has an affirmative upper-value element. No new default, factual concession, waiver finding, or preserved motion is created by this supplement.
