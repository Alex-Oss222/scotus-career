## Baral v. United States — No. 98-1667 — February 22, 2000

**Historical control.** The complete unanimous opinion, including both footnotes, is [528 U.S. 431–439](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-528/pdf/USREPORTS-528-431.pdf). Its premise is a timely claim whose recoverable amount is zero because the two specified income-tax remittances were deemed paid before the lookback. It does not hold the filing itself late. It distinguishes Rosenman's estate-tax suspense arrangement and reserves remittances outside an applicable deemed-payment provision.

**Reconciled components.** All eight Associates retain B1–B3. B1 applies section 6513(b)(1) to tax actually deducted and withheld under chapter 24, with respect to the recipient's year for which the amount is allowable under section 31: payment is deemed on the fifteenth day of the fourth month after that year. B2 applies section 6513(b)(2) to estimated income tax: payment is deemed on the unextended last day prescribed for filing that year's return. Return filing and assessment do not postpone either date. B3 independently applies section 6511(b)(2)(A)'s three-years-plus-extension amount window. The $4,104 withholding and $1,100 estimate are paid April 15, 1989; the June 1, 1993 claim's window starts February 1, 1990, including the four-month filing extension. No part of the requested $1,175 credit is recoverable through this route.

**Premise comparison.** Current Lundy and Brockamp preserve the separation between claim timing and amount. Lundy's notice-date hypothetical and Stevens's and Thomas's different positions in that case do not govern this actual timely claim. Brockamp's retained deposit alternative makes it necessary to preserve, not erase, the distinction between a true deposit and these statutorily treated remittances. Sections 31, 6151 and 6315 defeat the argument that withholding and estimates are independent taxes transformed only upon assessment. No changed factual, statutory or procedural premise supports departing from any Associate's historical vote.

## Hunt-Wesson, Inc. v. Franchise Tax Board — No. 98-2043 — February 22, 2000

**Historical control.** The complete unanimous opinion and statutory appendix are [528 U.S. 458–469](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-528/pdf/USREPORTS-528-458.pdf). The parties conceded that the relevant nonunitary receipts bore no independent nexus to California. The defect was the formula's unreasonable allocation of expense, not the mere existence of an allocation method or the absence of tracing every loan.

**Reconciled components.** All eight retain H1–H3. H1: Due Process and Commerce Clause limits prevent enlarging a nondomiciliary corporation's taxable unitary base by a deduction adjustment measured by constitutionally excluded receipts unless it reasonably allocates expense to the income that expense generates. Expenses properly attributable to excluded income need not be deducted from taxable income; reasonable ratios or other supported allocation can suffice. H2: Section 24344 first allows interest expense against formula-allocated interest income, then allows the remaining expense only above nonformula interest and dividends, except dividends deductible under section 24402 because declared from income included in the measure of the specified California taxes. Remaining expense is directly offset against the nonformula receipts. The challenged receipts-based mechanism effectively attributes borrowing first to nonunitary investment and does not reasonably reflect the borrowing devoted to it. Fungibility and tax-arbitrage concerns do not validate this method. H3: The defect exists under both the forms-based unitary-interest interpretation and California's all-interest interpretation; that state-law disagreement need not be resolved.

**Premise comparison.** Current Allied-Signal retains the unitary/operational boundary, a separate fair-allocation inquiry and the taxpayer's clear-and-cogent burden. O'Connor's and Thomas's different application positions there do not create a nexus conceded absent here. The historical direct/indirect taxation distinction is compatible with current law. National Life supplies the substance-over-deduction-label analogy; Denman and First National Bank preserve reasonable expense allocation. The present ground does not convert those examples into a compulsory replacement formula.

**Historical control.** The complete unanimous opinion and all thirteen footnotes are [528 U.S. 440–457](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-528/pdf/USREPORTS-528-440.pdf). ## Rice v. Cayetano — No. 98-818 — February 23, 2000

**Historical control.** The complete opinion package is [528 U.S. 495–548](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-528/pdf/USREPORTS-528-495.pdf). Four present Associates—O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas—joined the Court's state-election rationale. Souter and Breyer agreed with reversal on a different trust/electorate rationale and expressly rejected the broader premise that state-agency status supplied the decisive answer. Stevens dissented on overlapping indigenous-relationship, trust and contextual-classification grounds. Ginsburg joined only his Part II and separately applied the delegated federal relationship to both Amendments. These are historical comparison facts, not authorship or joinder instructions.

**Judgment.** Retain six Associate votes to reverse the Ninth Circuit's validation of the voting qualification: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm. Conforming relief addresses the ancestry exclusion; no underlying program or trust is invalidated.

**Reconciled proposition map.** The frozen R1–R3 labels must be read at the following distinct levels. They do not, merely by sharing labels, establish six votes for every explanation.

- **R1 — case-specific racial qualification.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas regard the pre-1778 descent condition as a racial voting qualification: wording in terms of ancestry, an incomplete correspondence with present racial identity and absence of hostility do not change its operation here. Souter and Breyer agree that the actual arrangement cannot escape the Fifteenth Amendment, but their agreement depends on rejection of the particular political/trust analogy below. Stevens and Ginsburg dispute the racial characterization in this special federal-indigenous relationship.
- **R2-A — independently sufficient public-state-election ground.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas conclude that even assuming substantial congressional authority over native Hawaiians and delegation to Hawaii, Mancari does not authorize the State to restrict election of its public officials by race. OHA administers state laws and public obligations; its election is not the internal election of a separate political community. This is not a denial of congressional recognition power or of lawful indigenous programs.
- **R2-B — actual trust and electorate ground.** Souter and Breyer reject the claimed analogy on this statutory arrangement. The Admission Act public trust serves five purposes for Hawaii's people, with native-Hawaiian betterment among them; OHA also performs public functions and receives ordinary appropriations and other funds. The State's broad descent-only electorate does not sufficiently resemble membership in the political beneficiary community invoked to justify exclusion. The dedicated twenty-percent revenue share and funding for the broader Hawaiian class are real, so neither a literal denial that any trust exists nor the blood-definition mismatch alone states their ground. A State-created descent definition without a sufficiently demonstrated political-membership connection does not establish the needed analogy. This does not prescribe a blood quantum, condemn every descent-based membership rule or decide a differently constituted federal relationship.
- **R3 — special-purpose and beneficiary defenses.** The four R2-A Associates reject limited governmental purpose or beneficiary selection as exceptions to a race-neutral public franchise; Salyer and Ball's one-person-one-vote analysis does not waive the Fifteenth Amendment. Souter and Breyer reject those defenses in the actual arrangement after R2-B's failed analogy, not on a universal rule that every state role defeats every political or trust-based electorate. All six leave underlying benefit programs, trusts, the separate sovereignty ballot, candidate qualifications, tribal internal elections and recognition power outside the disposition; the Fourteenth Amendment claim need not be reached to reverse.

**Reconciliation of the provisional six-way join.** The frozen model expressly reserved Souter's and Breyer's objection to categorical state-agency and recognition propositions. Full source review confirms that this reservation is indispensable, not optional explanatory color. Their historical concurrence rejected the Court's critical rationale even while agreeing with much of its account. Current Adarand and Hopwood require attention to actual fit and burdens, but do not displace the Fifteenth Amendment or answer the indigenous political-status issue. Current Shaw and Miller distinguish territorial design from personal exclusion; they likewise supply no reason to abandon the historical trust/membership reservation. No changed record or congressional act supplies a different premise.

Accordingly, preserve a six-Associate case-specific invalidity conclusion and the possibility of a common formulation that actually makes R2-B's qualification part of its operative explanation. Do not certify six joins to a lead whose sufficient rationale is R2-A alone, or whose reservation of future cases merely disguises that broader ground. Souter and Breyer then supply judgment-only agreement and a separate explanatory position. A later draft that resolves their objection must be tested in its actual terms; this handoff neither invents that circulation nor requires a fracture irrespective of formulation. No Marks aggregation is supplied: the two reasons are not declared a logical subset merely because one is described as narrower.

## Roe v. Flores-Ortega — No. 98-1441 — February 23, 2000

**Historical control.** The complete opinion package is [528 U.S. 470–494](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-528/pdf/USREPORTS-528-470.pdf). O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer supported vacatur and remand. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg opposed the consultation-duty treatment, but each expressly joined Part II-B in full. That section includes both the absence of a probable-merits prerequisite and the reasonable-probability requirement that adequate consultation would have produced a timely appeal. Breyer separately emphasized the guilty-plea question and counsel's almost-always duty to consult after a trial. Ginsburg separately emphasized the clearer duty rule and her conclusion that consultation was unsatisfied here.

**Threshold and record retained.** The July 27, 1995 federal filing places this noncapital case under pre-AEDPA law through current Lindh. No amended section 2254(d) gate or section 2254(e)(1) presumption is imported. The limited promise hearing found an unproved promise, no consent to nonfiling, little or no appeal understanding and an actual conversation. Those are distinct findings. No full advice, express instruction, informed declination, complete appeal waiver, agreed precise sentence or appellate-merits finding is supplied. The late notice and the apparent post-sentence communication difficulty carry their actual evidentiary status.

**F1 — all eight.** The defendant decides whether to take an available first appeal. Counsel must comply with an instruction to appeal and respect an informed direction not to appeal; choosing appellate issues is a different counsel function. When deficient assistance actually causes loss of an appeal the defendant otherwise would take, restoration does not depend on proving likely appellate success. No express instruction is found on this record simply because a promise was alleged.

**F2 — five contextual-duty Associates.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer retain ordinary Strickland review. First determine whether counsel explained the advantages and disadvantages of appeal and reasonably sought the client's wishes. Adequate consultation requires compliance with the resulting instructions; silence does not itself mandate a protective appeal. If consultation was absent, it was constitutionally required when counsel had reason to think a rational defendant would want appeal, including because of potentially nonfrivolous issues, or this defendant reasonably indicated interest. Consider all information counsel knew or should have known. A plea, retained rights and a sentence matching a bargain matter only as established; they are not categorical permission to abandon advice. Exceptionally informative judicial advice and actual understanding can make repetition unnecessary, but the warning's occurrence alone does not establish that condition here. This contextual rule is expected to require consultation in the vast majority of cases, not merely when the defendant already understands how to demand it. Breyer specifically expects an almost-always duty after trial and regards the guilty-plea context as material to this case.

**F3 — all eight, correcting the frozen refusal attribution.** Where deficient nonconsultation is established, the defendant must show a reasonable probability that adequate consultation would have led him to take a timely appeal. This proves loss of the proceeding; it is not a probability that the appeal would succeed. Potential nonfrivolous grounds or prompt expressions of interest can be relevant without either being indispensable. The duty and causal-loss inquiries can overlap but are not identical: an indication of interest can trigger consultation without conclusively proving the eventual choice. An uninformed, unrepresented petitioner need not identify arguments that missing assistance would have disclosed; other substantial reasons can establish the probable choice. Conversely, deficiency alone does not invariably establish causal deprivation. Once that loss is shown, no further probable-merits showing is required for restoration.

**F4 — five vacatur/remand Associates.** The lower court's no-consent rule undertook neither contextual duty nor causal-loss analysis. The limited promise findings do not settle the content or sufficiency of consultation, the duty on actual known circumstances, or causal loss. Vacate and remand for those inquiries; do not deny relief outright or command a new hearing regardless of ordinary need. Strickland's individual performance and causal-deprivation principles remain available; the District Court's Teague view of the categorical Stearns rule does not by itself dispose of that unexamined individual claim. No holding approving categorical Stearns retroactivity or a new Teague exception is necessary. Distinct proposed new protections keep their own nonretroactivity inquiry.

**Three-Associate alternative on duty and application.** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg retain the view that meaningful advice is almost always necessary when a defendant retains an appeal decision he cannot knowingly make unaided. An obvious relinquishment of all relevant appellate claims or the extraordinary fully informed defendant differs; those conditions are not established here. The personal choice identified in Jones, the important-decision duty in Strickland and the assistance principles of Cronic and Rodriquez support affirmative assistance before time expires. They regard the actual findings of no understanding and no informed nonappeal, together with the lost appeal and later effort to pursue it, as sufficient in this case to sustain conditional restoration. That application is the reconciled inference from the frozen supported facts and position; it is not attributed to an express historical finding that the opinion never made. They accept F3 and find its causal-loss condition met in context. Their practical criticism concerns a duty rule that asks an uninformed client to signal an informed interest, and unrealistically demanding applications of F3, not elimination of F3 itself.

**Why F3 changes from the provisional table.** The provisional table said that Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg refused F2–F4, and its competing-ground paragraph could be read as rejecting the causal-loss inquiry. The historical full-writing evidence contradicts that scope. The actual premise of their Part II-B join was the distinction between proving that deficient help caused forfeiture and proving the hypothetical appeal's merits. Current Peguero, which all eight joined, retains the same distinction. Current Smith changes Wende's validity and recognizes a missing screening function within an existing appeal; it expressly preserves individual Strickland review. It does not establish that every failure to advise caused a defendant to lose a desired appeal. No concrete changed premise supports discarding the historical causal-loss agreement. The supported correction therefore makes F3 common while leaving the different duty and application commitments intact. This corrects the reconciliation, not the immutable independent-model file.

**Treatment of current Smith.** The historical Flores-Ortega opinion cited historical Smith for the proposition that Wende counsel followed required procedures. That characterization cannot enter this timeline: current Smith holds the necessary counsel-assisted screening absent. The operative distinction nevertheless survives—failure within a supplied function, deprivation of a necessary function, and forfeiture of a whole proceeding are different matters. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain their published Smith disagreement but apply its actual holding; Breyer, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg joined that holding. Its different treatment of Wende supplies additional support for the latter three's insistence on assistance with a personal choice, but no automatic finding of deficient consultation or causal loss here. It does not require Breyer to leave the contextual-duty group, because he distinguishes whether a function was missing on these incomplete consultation findings from the established missing screening function in Smith. No later January 2000 decision is used to manufacture prefinality law for Teague.
