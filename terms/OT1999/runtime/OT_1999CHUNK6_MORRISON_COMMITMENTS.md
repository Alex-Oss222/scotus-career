Section 13981 reaches the person committing gender-motivated violence, expressly including persons acting under color of state law. Gender must motivate the conduct and gender animus must account for it at least in part. The violence definition covers the specified felony-against-person conduct and felony-against-property conduct carrying a serious physical-injury risk within the referenced state or federal offenses, including conduct that would so qualify but for the actor-victim relationship. It does not depend on special federal territorial jurisdiction or a prior criminal complaint, prosecution or conviction. The claimant must prove gender motivation by a preponderance; random violence unrelated to gender is excluded. Available relief includes compensatory and punitive damages, injunctions, declarations and other appropriate relief. Concurrent jurisdiction and the exclusion of federal jurisdiction over the enumerated state divorce, alimony, marital-property and child-custody decrees remain part of the scheme. Those limitations narrow the action but supply neither a commerce element nor a state-denial requirement.

## Component I: Commerce authority

### Ground supporting constitutional dismissal

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas apply the actual Lopez sequence: identify the statutory class and its interstate mechanism before aggregating magnitude. The regulated class is the defined gender-motivated violence, not violence directed at provision or receipt of a particular interstate service, taking an active business's operating assets, or participation in an actual commercial transaction. Gender animus is an important enacted limitation, but it does not make interference with a market an element or necessary operation of the class.

The findings support serious economic consequences through injury, curtailed activity, treatment, lost work and consumption. Under the actual Lopez rule, their extent cannot convert those downstream consequences into the antecedent constitutional connection. This does not depend on denying Congress's empirical account, demanding formal findings, requiring an individual interstate crossing, or classifying all noneconomic conduct and traditional state subjects as immune.

The strongest supporting argument for power is that pervasive gender violence excludes people from labor, education, travel and services, and that the national barrier is more concrete than an isolated crime's costs. The four distinguish a barrier that operates on a market as its object and operation from the class actually enacted, which covers personal assault whether or not it targets those activities. American Life League and Bird upheld targeted interference with actual medical services; they did not license a general assault remedy because injured people later cannot buy, work or travel. Robinson similarly requires the business-asset connection that its offense actually makes necessary. Condon concerns the identified information-disclosure market itself. The Court cannot substitute the most economic example for §13981's class.

The Necessary and Proper route is separately insufficient. Hale, Kirk, Hiley and the recipient/supply cases require a valid identified interstate regime that exclusion of this class would materially undermine or permit evasion of. The surrounding Act's comprehensiveness and the shared concern with violence do not by themselves identify that dependence. This record supports no claim that the private civil action is necessary to enforce a specified interstate supply restriction or that its absence would defeat the interstate operation of an identified regime. The four do not invent such a mechanism from the Act's title or from domestic violence's role in different firearm statutes.

Home Builders' independent externality route is considered on its own terms. A concrete substantial cross-border effect which affected States cannot reasonably internalize can support national regulation of noneconomic conduct. The supported findings here identify consequences that occur nationally and can affect interstate choices, but not a distinct cross-border mechanism and state inability of the kind that route requires. Similar private injuries in many States, unequal protection within state systems, and the desirability of a national remedy do not by themselves establish that externality. No fixed geographical, numerical or legislative-findings threshold is imposed. The problem is the asserted mechanism's legal and practical relation to the enacted class, not a demand for more pages.

The four therefore reject Commerce authority for this stand-alone private-assault action. They decline an existing-record remand: the supported alternatives have been identified and assessed, and no concrete potentially eligible mechanism awaits a genuinely unresolved lower application. Home Builders' remand did not grant a general second opportunity to invent a federal connection. They also decline to insert an omitted commerce element. No present conclusion about a materially different, validly enacted market-directed or interstate-remedial statute follows.

### Ground supporting reversal on Commerce authority

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the broader rational, class-wide substantial-effects approach they adopted in the actual public Lopez dissent. Congress could reasonably determine from the supported findings that gender-motivated violent conduct, considered as a class, materially obstructs participation in interstate work, travel and economic activity, and that a civil remedy against its perpetrators is a reasonable means of reducing that obstruction. Their position does not require each assault or each plaintiff's lost opportunity to cross a border or separately have a substantial effect. The gender-motivation and serious-violence requirements identify the legislated class; the remedy aims at the conduct causing the documented impairment.

The strongest objection is decisive under the *existing controlling* Lopez architecture: Congress's causal economic account is mediated through injury and foregone activity, while §13981 does not require a market-directed object or another independently eligible mechanism. These four do not disguise that objection as a missing factual finding. Their preferred class-effects approach differs from the controlling connection-before-magnitude restriction at precisely that point. They reject applying the restriction to invalidate this supported class regulation, and their position would require displacement of that restriction to the extent it forecloses this ground. It is a proposed constitutional position, not a claim that the current majority's ordinary test already authorizes §13981.

Their limiting principle is a rationally supported, substantial relationship of the actual regulated class to interstate commerce and reasonable regulatory means, subject to judicial examination. It is not any imaginable economic consequence, deference to the mere assertion of national importance, or Congress's findings being conclusive. They regard the supported account of barriers to economic participation as sufficient under that test and the direct civil liability of the perpetrators as a reasonable means. They accept that the competing connection rule draws a narrower boundary; they reject its placement of this supported class outside national authority rather than supply an invented market or externality.

Their joins in American Life League, Robinson, the supply cases and Condon confirm those grounds are sufficient, not that those are the only permissible routes. Ginsburg and Breyer's Home Builders joins require particular care: they joined the anti-evasion and externality inquiries *given* the independently controlling Part I, expressly preserving their disagreement with that part's enacted-class restriction. Their current position honors those limits and does not claim that national prevalence automatically satisfies the externality test they joined. Stevens and Souter's absence from that coalition supplies no invented separate rationale.

These four would reverse the constitutional dismissal and return the statutory claim for ordinary adjudication. They would not require a new pleading of an interstate-commerce element that Congress omitted, decide liability or damages, or reopen the unrelated university claims.

## Component II: Fourteenth Amendment §5

### Governing distinction and the strongest opposing paths

The constitutional injury Congress invokes is unequal state protection, not a constitutional right against every private assault. State action can include discriminatory official administration contrary to written law, judicial enforcement and customs carrying legal force; it is not confined to discriminatory statutes. The Civil Rights Cases also contemplated legislation in advance of anticipated state wrongs and remedies reaching private persons in a genuinely corrective relation. Harris (1883) concerned direct private conspiracy without an alleged state constitutional denial; custody of victims by an officer did not convert the private attackers into state actors. Harlan's Harris dissent was jurisdictional only and supplies no contrary merits rule.

Boerne permits congruent and proportional preventive coverage of some conduct that would not independently violate the Constitution. Florida Prepaid actually upheld such coverage for patent deprivations, where exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative remedies supplied the supported connection between state infringement and constitutional redress. No universal requirement of a completed constitutional violation by every defendant, a prior unsuccessful state proceeding, a particular quantity of findings, geographical limits or a sunset may be introduced here. College Savings nevertheless requires the asserted remedy to address an actual constitutional guarantee rather than a statutory wrong alone.

The strongest §5 case for §13981 is therefore substantial. Congress identified discriminatory state justice, and a federal civil claim can substitute an avenue of redress when biased protection fails. Requiring each victim first to prove state discrimination could preserve the obstacle the preventive remedy aims to remove. The action is civil, requires gender animus and serious violence, and targets the perpetrators of the injury for which equal protection was withheld. The findings distinguish this account from the nineteenth-century majorities' descriptions of legislation that did not profess to correct state constitutional wrongs.

The strongest objection concerns the enacted relationship rather than the mere label of the defendant. Liability and relief are triggered by the private assault itself, whether the State denies equal protection or supplies and faithfully enforces it. Neither gender animus of the assailant nor the seriousness of the assault identifies discriminatory state administration. The action does not replace a specified constitutionally deficient state procedure, respond to a defined official practice, or limit its relief by a supported relation to the state deprivation. Legislative identification of a real state problem therefore does not itself answer whether these means prevent or remedy that problem rather than establish a substantive federal right against the underlying private violence.

### Ground rejecting §5 authority for this private-assault action

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas find that remedial connection insufficient. They acknowledge Congress's express account of discriminatory enforcement and do not treat the Civil Rights Cases or Harris as a rule forbidding every action naming a private person. Their objection is the combination of defendant, statutory trigger, breadth and relief: all liability follows from the assault, without a fitted relation between the covered private conduct and the state's denial Congress invokes. The civil nature of the remedy and the difficulty of proving discriminatory enforcement do not, on this design, establish the preventive fit needed to bridge that gap.

Florida Prepaid's authorization of some ordinary state infringement is not ignored. The patent remedy confronted the States' own deprivation of protected property and a jurisdictional structure potentially leaving that deprivation without adequate redress. Its preventive inclusion of additional infringement did not make every federal statutory tort a constitutional wrong. Here the assailant's private act and the discriminatory state's administration are distinct wrongs, and the remedial design does not sufficiently connect the former's nationwide liability to prevention or redress of the latter. This is a qualitative fit judgment; no requirement to prove a constitutional violation in every suit is substituted for it.

The explicit state-action language remains meaningful. The four reject §5 as authority for the private-assault cause presented; they do not adjudicate a hypothetical claim against a person acting under color of state law, decide every possible valid application, or strike the phrase from the text. No established severability issue or different application is invented. Nor do they convert this scope limit into a newly pleaded state-action claim for Brzonkala or judicially insert an absent state-failure element.

### Reserved alternative ground for the four supporting reversal

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer do not reach a dispositive §5 conclusion because their Commerce ground sustains the enacted cause and supplies complete relief from the constitutional dismissal. Their reservation is explicit: it contributes no vote to either the four's rejection of §5 or a broader §5 validity holding.

Each recognizes the genuine state-discrimination/prophylaxis argument and the private-injury/constitutional-injury objection above. Their actual Florida Prepaid support makes a per-victim completed-violation requirement unacceptable; their varying Boerne positions cannot be turned into an agreement that every remedy Congress labels preventive is valid. Stevens and Ginsburg joined Boerne's no-redefinition rule. Souter and Breyer's Boerne reservations concerned the substantive Smith baseline and did not authorize legislative replacement of rights. Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined College Savings' refusal to infer a constitutional property injury from the particular federal tort alone. These are meaningful constraints on a possible §5 position, not predictions of a position they need not decide here.

They would decline to join a categorical private-defendant exclusion, a rule that discriminatory official nonenforcement can never be state action, or a requirement of an independently proved constitutional violation in every covered case. They also decline to infer that the legislative record and civil remedy necessarily establish congruence and proportionality. A narrower future holding would have to identify how the specific coverage and relief remedy or prevent unequal state protection while retaining the actual state-action boundary; no such additional holding is counted in their present commitment.

### Saenz at its actual scope

Saenz's actual official-causal-deprivation ground matters without deciding §5. A private immediate attacker does not preclude a constitutional wrong when an official affirmatively removes available lawful protection with the required culpability and attributable causal effect. Conversely, general nonprotection, callousness or private violence alone is insufficient. It supplies no official action in this complaint and does not turn Morrison's private assailants into state actors. Equal-protection discrimination is its own constitutional wrong; Saenz's due-process rule does not erase it.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer's Saenz joins support attention to actual attribution and constitutional function; they do not supply a §5 vote. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's different treatment of the canceled investigation likewise supplies no vote to reject every preventive law addressing unequal protection. Saenz did not adjudicate congressional prophylaxis, and its direct-claim elements cannot become a universal individual-violation prerequisite for valid §5 coverage. Its immunity application lacks a majority and has no bearing on these power questions.

## Remedy, compatible grounds and reserved matters

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas affirm the constitutional dismissal of the §13981 private-assault claim because both asserted powers fail on the grounds stated. Their common Commerce ground is the actual connection-and-fit rule, not a categorical exclusion of noneconomic conduct or a new findings requirement. Their common §5 ground is the insufficient remedial relation between the private-assault liability and state denial, not a rule that only States can be defendants or every covered act must independently violate the Constitution.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer reverse on their stated Commerce ground and remand for ordinary litigation of the statutory allegations. The four do not count as supporters of §5 validity, nor as joining the four rejecting it. Their Commerce position openly differs from the existing controlling Lopez restriction; it is not described as current law before assembly establishes a controlling coalition. Neither group supplies an author assignment.

Affirmance does not extinguish the separate university litigation, adjudicate state remedies, hold the allegations false, decide every color-of-law application or create a severability ruling. Reversal establishes only congressional power for the presented action; it establishes no assault, animus, causation, liability, damages or entitlement to a particular form of proceeding. No new statutory element, defendant, constitutional factual allegation, broad evidence reopening or invented default is supplied.

Mining has no Commerce or §5 holding and provides no constitutional authority by analogy to delegated administrative correction. Saenz enters only as described above. All existing source limits and the pre-May15 cutoff remain intact. The present supported grounds require no additional factual premise; a materially new market, interstate spillover, official act or state-practice claim would require fresh neutral validation before use.
