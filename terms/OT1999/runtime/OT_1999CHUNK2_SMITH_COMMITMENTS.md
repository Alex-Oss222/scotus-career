Douglas and Evitts protect meaningful, effective represented access on the first appeal as of right. Anders supplies a constitutionally sufficient procedure for a lawyer who finds no nonfrivolous ground; McCoy permits an additional explanation supporting withdrawal and distinguishes the no-merit screening brief from an advocate's merits brief. Penson forbids decision of an arguable appeal after counsel's withdrawal without replacement, and presumes prejudice for actual or constructive loss of counsel altogether. Jones permits professional selection among arguments but does not authorize abandoning a nonfrivolous appeal. Strickland's performance and reasonable-probability prejudice components remain distinct; actual Fretwell is a limited displaced-entitlement rule, not an additional general unfairness requirement.

Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas's former temporal dissent does not alter governing law. Peguero distinguishes actual loss of an appeal from probable appellate success and formal rule noncompliance. January 12 Martinez preserves the Fourteenth Amendment assistance right while rejecting a personal right to displace counsel; it does not decide whether Wende provides adequate advocacy.

## Complete decisional premises

Wende requires counsel's review, a factual/procedural account with record citations, client communication and notice, an opportunity for personal submission, and independent judicial review of the entire record even without a personal submission. Counsel remains appointed and available; the court orders briefing upon finding an arguable issue. Counsel does not expressly identify potentially supportive issues or state frivolousness. Appellate-project consultation occurred here but is not established as a mandatory statewide safeguard.

Robbins's own submission raised insufficiency and Brady issues that the state appellate court found unsupported. Federal habeas identified two arguable grounds—law-library access for trial self-representation and refusal to allow withdrawal of the trial counsel waiver—but made no probability-of-success finding. The District Court granted a conditional new-appeal-or-release writ on presumed prejudice from noncompliance with Anders. The Ninth Circuit treated Anders as exclusive and approved relief where arguable issues existed; it did not decide bare departure without such issues. Eleven exhausted trial-error claims remain for adjudication. Neither an arguable issue nor this research record establishes its ultimate merit.

## Adversarial paths by separable component

**A. Permissible alternative procedure.** Every Associate accepts that the Constitution does not prescribe each word or mechanical detail of Anders. A genuinely equivalent alternative is possible. The issue is whether retained counsel's review and narrative, client participation, whole-record judicial examination and ordered further advocacy supply meaningful protection, or omit the essential legal assistance needed to test the no-merit conclusion.

The strongest State path treats the full bundle as sufficient: counsel has already used legal skill to review the record, remains responsible, and must advocate if an arguable issue is found. The judicial check is additional protection, not the first or only legal review. It is not Penson's withdrawal and decision without replacement. Its strongest objection is that an unguided narrative reveals neither counsel's identification of possible legal grounds nor the basis for rejecting them; a court may miss precisely the point an advocate would identify. The State-path Justices conclude the Constitution requires adequate assurance of effective assistance, not the exclusive screening instrument of an Anders brief.

The strongest Robbins path requires counsel to identify possible legal grounds sufficiently to make the screening meaningful. It accepts equivalent procedures but finds Wende's silence on legal issues materially inferior; judicial review cannot replace the advocate whose assistance reveals hidden legal significance. Its strongest objection is the concrete retained-counsel work and mandatory further briefing, rather than Penson's complete withdrawal. The Robbins-path Justices answer that formal availability after the court independently discovers an issue does not supply advocacy at the decisive screening stage; retaining the appointment does not by itself cure that substantive omission.

**B. Individual performance and prejudice.** If Wende is constitutionally sufficient, the individual claim that counsel unreasonably missed a nonfrivolous ground requires Strickland performance and a reasonable probability of a different appellate result had adequate advocacy occurred. No obligation exists to advance every requested weak issue; there is an obligation not unreasonably to conclude the whole appeal is frivolous. Penson's presumption remains for an actual or constructive denial altogether, not every error in a retained lawyer's screening. No such presumption follows solely from divergence from Anders. The contrary path treats this procedure's omission of all issue advocacy, in an appeal containing the two supported arguable grounds, as constructive denial at that stage; it requires a properly counseled appeal without predicting whether either ground will succeed.

**C. Remedy.** The State path reverses the appellate-counsel basis for the conditional writ and remands for ordinary performance/prejudice adjudication and the unresolved trial claims. It does not deny habeas on the merits or hold the two issues frivolous. The Robbins path affirms the requirement of a new counseled appeal under an adequate procedure if the other habeas claims do not afford relief making it unnecessary; it preserves the lower remand of those claims. It does not invalidate the conviction or direct immediate release, and does not invent a fresh thirty-day period beginning at this Court's opinion.

## Provisional coalitions and assembly constraints

Eight Associates reject an exclusive word-for-word Anders requirement. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support the described Wende procedure and reversal/remand of the current appellate-counsel ground; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer oppose its constitutional adequacy and would preserve the conditional appellate relief on the substantive advocacy ground. The four-four division is a provisional Associate division, not a Court judgment or a published tally. Stone and final compatibility belong only in assembly.

Do not combine the opposing groups' acceptance of alternative procedures into approval of Wende, and do not count the four Robbins-side Justices as adopting ordinary Strickland prejudice for the deprivation they identify. Their willingness to preserve equivalent alternatives is not a narrower subset of Wende's validation. A selected separate writing must explain that disagreement, the retained-counsel counterargument, and the remedy distinction. No author is predetermined.
