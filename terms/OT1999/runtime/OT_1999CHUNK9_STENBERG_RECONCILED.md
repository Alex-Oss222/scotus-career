## Justice-specific reconciliation

### Stevens

His historical full join in both independent grounds confirms the proposed affirmance. His separate concurrence went farther: it questioned a legitimate basis for requiring a physician to use a procedure other than the one reasonably thought best for the patient and treated the distinction between the two procedures as irrational. Those views do not become the common rationale by incorporation.

The concrete changed legal premise is his own complete-framework join in the actual Casey decision, followed by his complete joins in Fargo Part I and Voinovich Parts I, IV, and V. Those positions distinguish an established clinical necessity or access obstacle from a preference, require evidence of the duty's additional interference, permit substantiated regulation, and match relief to the proved applications. They also expressly require objective support for an obstructive-purpose conclusion. They allow him to affirm on the decisive enacted coverage and medical findings without a categorical conclusion that all intact-method distinctions lack a legitimate purpose. His concern for the patient's liberty and safety remains; the freeze does not establish abandonment of that concern or a new endorsement of the disputed distinction. A separate concurrence is unnecessary if the common opinion adequately expresses the actual sufficient grounds. Historical separate authorship does not itself require one.

The strongest competing path is an intact-only construction preserving usable care. His objection survives: the elaborating definition includes a substantial portion, while the described intentional ordinary-D&E acts can meet every relevant element. Neither the title nor a nonbinding prosecutorial assurance supplies the omitted exclusion. Voinovich rejected categorical notice reasoning; it did not require overlooking actual coverage and access evidence. His independent health ground does not depend on a universal claim that intact delivery is always safer.

### O'Connor

Her historical concurrence expressly identifies health and ordinary-D&E coverage as independent grounds. It rejects the assertion that health protection rests merely on Carhart's individual preference and recognizes medically supported safety advantages for some patients. That closely confirms both initial grounds and the protection's clinical qualifications.

The significant difference concerns her historical assurance that an intact-only prohibition with life-and-health protection and safe alternatives would be constitutional. Here she personally joined Casey's complete six-step rule and the Fargo and Voinovich applications. The absence of a substantial obstacle therefore does not automatically end her inquiry into remaining severe or meaningful interference. A hypothetical more precise ban could survive the actual rule, but those two drafting changes alone cannot be announced as sufficient for every application. This is a concrete prior-position difference, not a generic statement that precedent changed. Her frozen insistence on distinguishing permissible method regulation from this enacted coverage faithfully preserves both her regulatory latitude and her actual full-framework commitments.

The State's best route is still narrower construction, adequate alternatives, and genuinely usable health protection. The present statute lacks the critical excluding text and independent health protection. The administrative life-necessity hearing does not supply the latter. No new separate opinion is required if the common opinion preserves this qualified alternative; historical concurrence authorship does not determine current authorship or a mandatory writing.

**Historical departure:** O'Connor's earlier complete joins in actual Casey, Fargo, and Voinovich foreclose treating the absence of an obstacle, an intact-only definition, and a health exception as an automatic guarantee of validity; her support for a potentially lawful narrower method regulation is preserved subject to the remaining steps she personally adopted.

### Scalia

The historical dissent and his join in Thomas support reversal. His separate writing insists that the constitutional objection to Casey itself not disappear behind a narrower claim that Casey was misapplied. His actual Fargo and Voinovich positions preserve that same objection while joining bounded notice and severability propositions. The common dissent he is willing to join must therefore state his rejection of the constitutional abortion and necessary-health rules, accurately identifying them as the law from which he dissents. It cannot merely redescribe historical obstacle-only Casey as current law.

His contextual construction is consistent with the historical text-and-avoidance objection. It does not convert the Attorney General's representation into a binding construction or erase the trial findings. The strongest affirmance argument remains serious under controlling law: the express substantial-portion elaboration can reach intentionally performed ordinary D&E, and the credited clinical differences matter. His separate constitutional premise supplies the reason he nonetheless votes to reverse. His Fargo/Voinovich agreement on intelligibility does not prove substantive validity or establish every future criminal or civil fault element. A shared dissent containing his constitutional objection can perform the function of his historical separate writing; an extra writing is not mandated merely by history.

### Kennedy

Kennedy's complete historical dissent is strong evidence against affirmance. Parts I and II emphasize legitimate moral and professional interests, legislative authority in medical uncertainty, and the continued availability of safe abortion. He rejects treating a physician's preferred technique or every asserted marginal advantage as constitutionally controlling. Part III reads the complete delivery-and-death sequence as an intact-method restriction and favors a legally available narrowing construction. The frozen commitment preserves those substantive objections: it does not join ordinary-D&E coverage, a physician veto, general residual lesser-burden review, or the five Associates' conclusion that the health findings support affirming the existing protection now.

The historical premise supporting outright validation was not merely respect for legislatures. It was that Nebraska could conclude its restriction deprived no patient of safe abortion, so that the asserted comparative advantage did not require a constitutional health exception or invalidate the law. His historical reliance on Casey's obstacle formulation and medical-regulation authorities supported ending the case on that assessment. The objective Nebraska text and clinical record have not changed merely because this is a different timeline; no new medical fact or discredited witness is posited here.

The concrete contrary prior commitments are his own actual Casey retained-choice and necessary-life-and-health join; his Fargo joins separating an intelligible emergency definition from substantive adequacy; and, most directly, Voinovich Parts III.A and III.B and his published separate position. In Voinovich he personally joined protection for grave, medically established necessary mental-health care and the duty to seek a lawful protective construction, with the unconstitutional exclusion remaining unenforceable if no such construction exists. His separate position expressly made the combination of an act-defined restriction, actual alternatives, and Ohio's enacted alternative-method health defense material to rejecting the categorical method challenge. It expressly reserved supported necessary-care applications. This is a more specific personal commitment than general adherence to a changed precedent.

Nebraska supplies no counterpart to that alternative-method defense. Its exception permits only the specified life-saving care, and its licensing hearing permits evidence only on that same life criterion. The broader general emergency definition does not enter section 3 by implication. Kennedy therefore cannot carry his Voinovich method disposition across unchanged on the premise that the same safeguard exists. Nor can intelligibility or legislative disagreement alone answer the independent necessary-care protection he has joined. The serious mental-health criterion from Voinovich is not transplanted as the medical test for every physical-risk application; it supplies evidence of his commitment to substantive necessary care and lawful, limited relief.

That difference supports his bounded remand rather than a new affirmance. He accepts the credited comparative reductions in the described surgical risks without declaring every intact-method use necessary. He requires the lower court to identify the supported circumstances in which the available alternatives are clinically inadequate for timely safe and suitable care under his retained baseline. The judgment's existing protection is limited to nonviable Carhart-type applications, but it is not itself a finding that every covered use independently satisfies his necessity test. Further legal application can occur on the existing record if adequate; a new hearing, new filing, or proof of a newly invented condition is not ordered automatically.

The strongest objection to the remand is his historical willingness to defer even with credible disagreement over relative risks. That objection prevents transforming his vote into support for the five Associates' health reasoning. It does not eliminate his expressly retained necessary-care inquiry where the health defense material to his earlier method position is absent. The remand must preserve supported necessary-care applications and their dependent-sanction protection, permit only a Nebraska-law-supported construction, and address interim protection under ordinary standards. It does not dissolve existing protection automatically, insert a new statutory exception, or validate the law in all applications. His disposition is vacatur/remand now, not a conditional promise to reverse later.

**Historical departure:** Kennedy maintains the historical contextual intact-method reading and objection to physician control of regulation, but seeks a bounded necessary-care remand rather than outright validation because his own actual Voinovich III.A/III.B joins require protection of supported necessary care and his separate method position materially relied on Ohio's enacted alternative-method health defense, which Nebraska's life-only exception and post-charge hearing do not provide. His earlier personal reservation of supported necessary-care applications makes that statutory difference material without compelling him to accept every comparative advantage as necessary.

### Souter

His historical full join in the Court's two grounds confirms affirmance, with no historical separate writing to carry over. His actual authorship of Fargo and Voinovich gives specific content to the present limitations. Fargo separates statutory clarity, actual culpability, and substantive care. Voinovich rejects categorical invalidity based on overlapping labels, yet requires examination of actual conduct, usable alternatives, health defenses, and corresponding relief. Nebraska's express substantial-portion language and the developed account of deliberate ordinary-D&E conduct supply that application rather than the categorical reasoning Voinovich rejected.

The strongest opposing route is a contextual intact-only construction plus adequate alternatives. He rejects the proposed construction at the actual text's scope and treats the missing health protection separately. The full framework does not require a remand once the sufficient ordinary-D&E application is supported. His health position concerns the identified clinical circumstances, not all possible patient preferences, and imports neither Ohio's defenses nor its severability law. No material judgment departure is established.

### Thomas

The historical dissent supports his constitutional objection, contextual construction, and reversal position. Its alternative analysis also characterizes historical Casey as an obstacle-only rule, distinguishes obtaining an abortion from choosing a method, and invokes a no-valid-applications or alternative numerical facial test. Those premises cannot be copied as the present governing law: actual Casey, Fargo, and Voinovich expressly preserve an independent necessary-care baseline, the complete framework, and violation-specific relief. His actual Voinovich separate-position join expressly acknowledges that baseline while dissenting from it.

His historical footnote 21 invokes the historical Voinovich denial of certiorari and its unanswered mental-health concern. Here the actual Voinovich merits decision and his own dissent resolve that doctrinal setting. The historical denial cannot replace the actual merits decision. Likewise, the historical facial characterization does not enlarge the actual district judgment reproduced in the neutral packet. That judgment expressly protects Carhart, his patients, and similarly situated persons concerning his methods on nonviable fetuses; it is not a whole-statute facial decree. The underlying historical district record is not asserted to have changed; the reconciliation uses its precise operative scope rather than a later opinion's generalized characterization.

These corrections do not change Thomas's result. His continuing constitutional objection is independently sufficient for reversal, and his actual notice/severability joins preserve bounded statutory analysis without a universal fault cure. His strongest opposing argument is that controlling law plus actual coverage and clinical findings warrants protection; his answer is disagreement with the governing constitutional premise, not a claim that those facts are nonexistent. No new fetal-personhood or wholesale-reconsideration question is added to the grant. His proposed reversal does not itself impose prosecution, civil liability, an administrative sanction, or a damages award.

### Ginsburg

Her historical full join confirms both independently sufficient grounds. Her concurrence and join in Stevens emphasize liberty, medical choice, and the claimed absence of a legitimate purpose in forbidding one distressing procedure while permitting another. Her actual complete Fargo and Voinovich joins, however, already supply the controlling distinction between supported necessary care, actual access interference, evidentiary scrutiny of the State's function, and properly limited relief. They do not make every stated preference dispositive.

Her present commitment carries the decisive statutory and clinical applications without an extra purpose holding. This does not declare that the historical concern was mistaken; it leaves an unnecessary issue undecided. The changed framework requires an objective purpose assessment and allows lawful substantiated regulation, so mere legislative opposition cannot be substituted for that assessment. The strongest alternative is a genuine intact-only restriction with usable care. The text and developed evidence do not establish that alternative here, and the post-charge hearing does not authorize necessary nonfatal care. No separate concurrence is required if the common opinion carries her sufficient grounds and limits. Any later proposed broader purpose or preference proposition would require its own supported join inquiry; it is not silently supplied by the historical concurrence.

### Breyer

His historical Court opinion expressly gives two independent reasons, acknowledges the absence of controlled comparative studies, and nevertheless relies on credited, explained clinical advantages in some circumstances. It expressly rejects characterizing its health protection as unfettered physician discretion. These propositions closely confirm the provisional ordinary-D&E and bounded clinical commitments.

His own full Fargo and Voinovich joins add the actual six-step framework and distinguish a substantive application from categorical notice reasoning. His Voinovich support for remand rested on the unresolved actual applications and distinct Ohio defenses, not a general command to remand every method case. The developed Nebraska ordinary-D&E sequence and access effect support deciding this case. The best opposing paths remain narrower construction and general availability of alternatives; neither eliminates the express coverage or shows adequate care for the identified health applications. His common-opinion willingness is retained, but the historical author designation is not a current assignment criterion. No material judgment departure is established.
