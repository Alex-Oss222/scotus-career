## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of the D.C. Circuit.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the judgment sustaining § 922(g)(9) in place. Treat the 1998 official-use and equal-protection holdings as controlling, reject the preserved Commerce Clause and anti-commandeering challenges, and leave unpresented private-possession and restoration questions open.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Official-use arms claim and qualifying conviction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone applies the Court’s 1998 mandate and does not reopen the already resolved official-use claim.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The prior decision controls. The individual right requires serious review of an actual burden, but the adjudicated violent domestic-abuse predicate and statutory conviction safeguards supply the required justification and fit in this official-use setting.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A person with a constitutionally adequate qualifying domestic-violence conviction has no constitutional entitlement to a governmental-use exemption from § 922(g)(9) while the conviction remains legally operative.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The restriction prevents possession, a substantial burden, but it is tied to an adjudicated offense involving physical force or threatened deadly force and to a demonstrated risk of armed recurrence. Public employment does not remove that relationship. The statute’s exclusions for expungement, set-aside, pardon, and qualifying restoration keep the rule tied to an operative conviction. The 1998 judgment already resolved this application, so fidelity to precedent rather than a second balancing controls.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general arms-disability tier, private-possession rule, nonviolent-misdemeanor rule, fixed duration, or constitutional restoration procedure is adopted.
### Issue 2: Equal protection and the official-use exemption

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone applies the unanimous 1998 equal-protection holding.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Conviction status and public employment are not suspect classifications, and Congress may close the domestic-violence misdemeanor gap without simultaneously revising every older exemption.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 925(a)(1)’s different treatment of the domestic-violence-misdemeanor provisions survives ordinary equality review in the developed official-use controversy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The distinction is connected to the statute’s purpose of preventing armed recurrence after a qualifying domestic-violence conviction. The continued felony exception does not make the newer, targeted exclusion irrational.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The holding does not decide discriminatory administration, a deliberate proxy for a protected class, or another statutory category with materially different predicates.
### Issue 3: Tenth Amendment and anti-commandeering

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved structural objection decided on remand.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Section 922(g)(9) regulates the individual possessor. It does not command a State to legislate, retain a firearm policy, investigate federal disabilities, prosecute violations, or supply officers to administer federal law.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Application of § 922(g)(9) to state and local officers does not commandeer state government because the statute directly regulates possession and leaves employment policy to the State.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A State may choose whether an affected employee can perform another job. The federal disability may make the State’s preferred armed assignment unavailable, but that consequence is not a federal command to administer the law against third parties.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No compulsory state administration is imposed here. Printz’s confined temporary records-assistance holding and its reserved fiscal questions remain intact.
### Issue 4: Commerce Clause authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Stone reaches the preserved enumerated-power objection.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** The domestic-violence possession restriction is a fitted component of the federal interstate firearms regime when the offense-specific commerce connection is genuinely established.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 922(g)(9) is constitutional in the presented application because the regulated possession is tied through the enacted and proved nexus to the interstate firearms market and the disability protects the regime against prohibited-recipient retention.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The constitutional mechanism is not the bare fact that an object once crossed a border. It is the fitted relationship between prohibited-recipient possession, the interstate supply and transfer regime, and the case-specific nexus Congress required. That relationship is present on this record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule authorizes federal regulation of every local possession, permits judicial insertion of a missing nexus, or dispenses with proof of the statutory element.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the dismissal and remand for application of the incident-to-service rule to the actual duty and travel circumstances. Reject both a categorical public-highway exception and a categorical active-duty-status bar.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the lower court to identify the established duty or leave status, purpose of the trip and service connection using the proper dismissal posture. Do not award damages or direct that the FTCA claim must succeed. If those facts establish an incident-to-service injury, dismissal remains required.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Apply Feres without turning status or geography into the test

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The authorized question challenges the scope and application of Feres. It supplies no basis to overturn that doctrine and no basis to decide disputed negligence or damages.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing incident-to-service rule. Feres extends beyond combat or compliance with a particular order; Brooks nevertheless leaves space for a servicemember’s independent civilian activity. Neither case permits the entire inquiry to turn on a single label.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Being on a state-maintained road and off an assigned task does not itself defeat Feres; being enlisted on active duty and physically inside an installation does not itself establish it. The judgment must rest on the trip’s actual connection with service.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An Army driver’s ordinary negligence can injure a person whose activity is incident to service; Johnson prevents converting the absence of a direct challenge to a command into a universal exception. Conversely, the fact that a victim is a servicemember cannot alone extinguish the distinction recognized in Brooks. The public road is evidence about the travel setting, not a jurisdictional island exempt from military relationships. The absence of an assigned task is evidence about the activity, not proof of furlough or private leave. The supplied record stops before those decisive circumstances. Affirming would require supplying the service connection, while ordering the claim to proceed would require supplying its absence. Remand follows from that concrete missing predicate, not a general preference for avoiding an FTCA decision. Minns leaves the underlying Feres authorities intact and does not turn military causal genesis into a majority rule. Its policy-discretion holding also cannot replace the missing service analysis: driving a military truck does not, by ownership alone, identify protected policy selection.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No new Feres exception, no reversal of its existing holdings, no independent constitutional damages remedy, and no assumption that every military vehicle accident involves protected discretion. A fuller record establishing duty-connected travel could require affirmance rather than further proceedings.
