## Components and common reach

All eight Associates reach the granted facial preenforcement challenge to section 18-9-122(3). No new forfeiture is inferred from the lower opinion's account of a concession, because the complaint raised content discrimination and the state court passed on neutrality. No Justice relies on an unverified current briefing concession. This is not a criminal conviction, damages case or individualized injunction. No case-specific nonparticipation is supplied.

The components are classification and corresponding scrutiny; advancement, fit and alternative channels; vagueness; overbreadth; and relief. The rule applies only to a knowing speaker-initiated approach within eight feet of another person without that person's consent, for passing a leaflet or handbill, displaying a sign, or oral protest, education or counseling, on a public way or sidewalk within one hundred feet of a health-care facility entrance door. A covered facility is one legally licensed, certified, authorized or permitted to administer medical treatment. Colorado's construction imposes no duty on a stationary speaker to retreat when another person approaches. It neither bans every word or sign within one hundred feet nor requires consent for all presence there.

Subsection (2)'s obstruction offense remains distinct. Subsection (5)'s permission for local laws does not establish the constitutionality of every such law. Subsection (6)'s civil-liability cross-reference supplies no complete civil cause-of-action ruling in this facial challenge. Legislative testimony about obstruction, intimidation, crowding and confrontation is not a finding that these petitioners committed misconduct.

Erie's actual controlling rule requires attention to operation and justification; a conduct label alone cannot disguise communicative targeting. Its precise adult-entertainment holding does not decide every law that identifies a communicative mode. Ward remains the generally applicable neutral-legislation standard, while Madsen and Schenck require the closer injunction fit. None supplies a universal constitutional distance. The competing assessments below independently address the actual burdens and grounds rather than importing an Erie vote or combining its distinct opinions into a nonexistent broader holding.

## Justice-specific enhanced commitments

### Stevens

**Commitment and grounds.** Affirm the facial rejection of the speech, vagueness and overbreadth challenges to subsection (3), as authoritatively construed. The specified purposes identify the close communicative encounters regulated, across subjects and viewpoints; the actual access and treatment justification addresses nonconsensual physical approach rather than disagreement with the message. His fuller Erie content-selection opinion does not insulate general wording, but its municipal hierarchy of expression and unsupported change to the performance differ from this immediate physical-approach rule. He would apply Ward's existing legislative standard despite his historical disagreement with Ward's application.

**Alternative and strongest counterarguments.** Invalidation has substantial force because peaceful initiation and hand-to-hand leafleting are protected, the law reaches every speaker and passer-by in its defined area, and the oral categories distinguish communicative purposes. His Madsen support for an invitation condition concerned identified enjoined actors and cannot itself validate a general rule. Independently, however, the eight-foot limit, health-care-entrance setting, knowing affirmative approach, consent exception and no-retreat construction connect this preventive rule to close access encounters while retaining stationary offers, distant oral and sign communication and consensual exchanges. The strongest objection to affirmance is that subsection (2) already prohibits obstruction. Ward does not require the State to await completed obstruction or use only the least restrictive remedy, although that alternative must be considered and is not itself dismissed as irrelevant.

**Joins, clarity and relief.** Will join this neutral-legislative rationale only with its physical-harm and channel limits; not a general right to suppress unwanted ideas, a captive-audience rule for all sidewalks, or a claim that every retained channel is equally effective. Ordinary contextual meanings, knowledge and the binding approach construction provide sufficient facial notice; they do not adjudicate every close enforcement question. The defined operation does not establish substantial unconstitutional reach sufficient to invalidate it facially. Affirmance grants no immunity from a distinct unconstitutional application and decides no unpresented civil remedy. No separate writing is necessary if the lead opinion preserves these distinctions.

### O'Connor

**Commitment and grounds.** Affirm the facial judgment. Her Madsen opinion requires provision-specific fit and protects peaceful initiation against the much broader advance-invitation restraint; her Schenck joins reject the particular moving fifteen-foot restrictions. Both preserve materially different justified conduct controls. Her Erie position likewise requires affirmative justification but accepts supported regulation of noncommunicative harms with a limited expressive burden. Colorado's narrower, clearly construed legislative approach rule satisfies Ward on this record.

**Alternative and strongest counterarguments.** The strongest invalidation path treats the listed speech purposes and advance consent as suppressing protected initial contact, with stationary leafleting an inadequate substitute when a recipient is moving. She accepts the real burden rather than describing hand-to-hand distribution as unaffected. But the law prohibits only the speaker's knowing close advance without consent; it does not generate liability from an approaching listener or force speakers to calculate retreat around moving persons. Oral requests from the permitted distance, visible offers, stationary distribution and willing contact remain workable channels. All-facility coverage and access testimony support an administrable preventive boundary without proving wrongdoing by every regulated speaker.

**Joins, clarity and relief.** Will join content neutrality, affirmative advancement and fit, adequate but not identical channels, and rejection of the facial vagueness and overbreadth challenges. Will not join transposition of Madsen's injunction formula, automatic validation from benign purpose, or a universal personal-space entitlement. Knowledge, speaker approach and ordinary communicative-purpose terms supply the facial clarity; consent is not converted into an official licensing scheme. The same limits defeat the broad facial challenge without deciding every enforcement dispute. Affirm, leaving ordinary remedies for a properly supported application. A separate writing is needed only if a broader rationale omits these limits.

### Scalia

**Commitment and grounds.** Reverse the judgment sustaining subsection (3) and remand for declaratory and prospective relief against that unconstitutional communicative-approach restriction. The operative line permits an identical physical approach for other purposes while conditioning protest, education, counseling and the listed expressive contacts on advance consent. The law therefore selects communication, and the access label does not establish the required justification for this burden on peaceful sidewalk advocacy. His Madsen/Schenck writings and his fuller Erie Part II join make actual operation decisive.

**Alternative and strongest counterarguments.** The strongest affirmance path invokes a uniformly applicable rule of close approach, a narrow distance, all health-care facilities, consent and no retreat. These are genuine limitations and distinguish Schenck's moving retreat problems. They do not explain why peaceful communicative approach, rather than independently harmful obstruction, threats or pursuit, is selected. The State's interests in actual access and medical safety are substantial, but offense or reluctance concerning advocacy supplies no adequate substitute. Neither general applicability nor viewpoint neutrality alone removes the speech-selective operation.

**Joins, alternative fit and relief.** Will join the content-selection ground and its failure of required narrow justification; he does not adopt an independent intermediate-scrutiny alternative merely because he doubts the practical need. His Erie position preserved that same distinction. Will not join a hypothetical duty to retreat, a right to coerce a declining listener, or invalidation of subsection (2). Separate facial vagueness adjudication is unnecessary after the sufficient speech ground; knowledge does not cure the underlying selection. The protected initial contacts are central, not an incidental fringe of subsection (3)'s operation. No replacement distance or post-refusal scheme is judicially enacted. Will write or join a substantive separate position if the Court affirms.

### Kennedy

**Commitment and grounds.** Reverse the facial validation of subsection (3) and remand for relief limited to its unconstitutional approach restriction. Ward, which he authored, requires a real content-neutral justification and sufficient fit; it does not authorize converting protected solicitation of a listener into an activity requiring advance permission solely because it is protest, education or counseling. His Madsen/Schenck positions protect peaceful initiation and require an actual connection between burdens and lawful access protection. Erie left a disputed municipal classification for remand; here the controlling statutory operation is already authoritatively given and requires no newly invented factual proceeding.

**Alternative and strongest counterarguments.** Affirmance can distinguish Madsen's large radius and Schenck's moving retreat duties, emphasizing the eight-foot rule, known approach, consent and retained stationary speech. He accepts all those differences. The remaining burden still conditions precisely the initiating personal contact through which quiet persuasion and literature often occur, without obstruction, a prior refusal or other harmful act. The listed communicative purposes cannot be treated as irrelevant merely because all viewpoints within them are covered. The record's crowding and intimidation evidence establishes genuine interests but does not supply the required justification for this communicative selection across all covered persons and facilities.

**Joins, scope and remedy.** Will join the classification ground and, independently, the conclusion that the rule's protected-initiation burden and diminished leafleting channel fail Ward's fit even if neutrality is assumed. That alternative is his own sufficient conclusion; it cannot be attributed to Scalia without a join. No least-restrictive-means rule, required local study or prerequisite of completed injury is added. Vagueness need not be independently decided; the ground does not depend on a false retreat obligation or accidental approach. Independent lawful obstruction and physical-protection rules remain. Will join or write a separate opinion giving these grounds if the Court affirms; no author is now selected.

### Souter

**Commitment and grounds.** Affirm the facial judgment under the actual construction. His Madsen and Schenck joins protected initial approach and rejected excessive moving restraints while preserving qualified fixed access and refusal conditions. His Erie writings require a genuine showing of classification, advancement and fit and did not validate a disputed measure by label. Here the line is between a knowing close communicative approach and communication that leaves that immediate physical access space intact, with no movement imposed on a stationary speaker. The regulated mechanism and access/treatment justification supply a neutral Ward analysis rather than an unexplained taste-based ranking of messages.

**Alternative and strongest counterarguments.** Invalidation's strongest case is that restricting a peaceful first offer, even from a nonwrongdoer, can sacrifice effective leafleting and quiet personal conversation to a listener's reaction. Neither consent nor scienter alone answers it. Unlike Erie's unsettled explanation of why changed performance would reduce asserted harms, a bounded limit on unsolicited close approach directly reduces the approach and crowding that the legislative materials identify; no new local study is required to see that connection. The remaining channels are less convenient for some petitioners but meaningful, and the facial record does not establish that a lawful conversation or leaflet offer must always begin with the prohibited advance.

**Joins, clarity and relief.** Will join affirmance with explicit affirmative advancement, the binding no-retreat reading, and adequate-channel analysis. Will not join presumptions that medical settings eliminate public-forum protection, that offense is itself physical harm, or that anyone sharing a viewpoint is responsible for others' misconduct. The knowledge and approach elements, ordinary purpose descriptions and consent condition defeat facial indeterminacy; hypothetical hard applications remain open. No substantial facial overbreadth is established at the actual scope. No remand for clarification is necessary because the disputed moving-party construction is already settled. A separate writing is unnecessary if the common opinion faithfully states these limits.

### Thomas

**Commitment and grounds.** Reverse the facial validation of subsection (3), with a limited remedial remand. His Madsen and Schenck joins protect lawful advocacy against inadequately justified direct restraints; his Erie Part II join rejects reliance on a general conduct description where operation selects expression. Colorado's communicative-purpose condition distinguishes otherwise identical approaches by what the person seeks to communicate. Genuine access interests do not establish that this advance-permission mechanism is narrowly justified.

**Alternative and strongest counterarguments.** Affirmance rests most strongly on the small distance, knowledge, consent, all-facility scope and no-retreat reading. Those terms answer accidental liability and some moving-zone concerns but do not establish the need to foreclose peaceful initial expressive contact while allowing equivalent close contact for unlisted purposes. Existing obstruction controls and the lack of a prior refusal sharpen the mismatch; they do not impose a constitutional command to exhaust every alternative first. Protecting medical treatment cannot alone make any communication-specific restraint necessary.

**Joins, alternative and relief.** Will join the content-selection ground and an independently sufficient failure of advancement/fit under Ward if neutrality is assumed. His own Erie Part III join supports demanding a real connection rather than assuming it from a benign label; it establishes no universal evidentiary format. The burdens on initial leafleting and counseling are central to the statute's operation. No separate vagueness ruling is required for the result, and no new consent definition or retreat obligation is invented. Relief addresses subsection (3); it leaves independent obstruction laws and properly justified conduct protection available without selecting a new numerical boundary. Will join or write a separate position if the Court affirms.

### Ginsburg

**Commitment and grounds.** Affirm the facial judgment. Her Madsen/Schenck joins require real fit and preserve peaceful expression. Her Erie full Part II and III joins distinguish supported physical controls from changing protected expression without adequate justification. Colorado's law, in its authoritative construction, regulates a limited affirmative physical approach rather than requiring a speaker to modify a viewpoint, remove a sign or stop speaking from a lawful position. Its actual access and treatment justification, geographic boundary, small distance and retained communication channels satisfy Ward.

**Alternative and strongest counterarguments.** The best invalidation case stresses peaceful speakers, ordinary sidewalks, purposes identified by speech and the difficulty of hand delivery without approaching. She treats those as real burdens. They do not make every communicative-mode restriction content based, and the actual law neither ranks the worth of different viewpoints nor suppresses every exposure to an unwanted view. Unlike Erie's unestablished covering-to-harm relation, a limit on unsolicited close approach bears a direct relation to avoiding crowding and confrontation near entrances. Subsection (2) alone need not be the only preventive response, although the State must still establish sufficient fit for this additional restriction.

**Joins, facial challenges and relief.** Will join affirmance only on the bounded rule and affirmative justification, not a categorical right of listeners to silence public advocacy or an assumption that every individual is a captive patient. The knowledge, approach, distance, setting and ordinary communicative-purpose terms give sufficient facial guidance; the law is not invalid merely because edge applications may be disputed. The combination of ordinary lawful applications and retained channels defeats the asserted substantial facial overbreadth. No distinct civil remedy or local ordinance is validated. Willing to join the complete common opinion; no separate writing is required if these qualifications are carried.

### Breyer

**Commitment and grounds.** Affirm the facial judgment after applying Ward to the actual state construction. His Schenck separate position preferred clarification of uncertain mobile terms before invalidation; here the highest state court has supplied the crucial speaker-approach and no-retreat construction. His Erie remand join reserved classification and fit where the municipal interpretation was unresolved; it did not prescribe remand when the legal operation and physically germane access rationale permit decision. The limited rule directly addresses close nonconsensual encounters while preserving practical means to seek willing engagement.

**Alternative and strongest counterarguments.** The strongest invalidation path emphasizes the very communications most dependent on proximity: a quiet first conversation and a hand-delivered leaflet. A constitutional explanation must account for those burdens and the peaceful character of petitioners' asserted activity. The rule nevertheless leaves standing speakers free to offer literature, permits communication at the boundary and consent to closer contact, and does not penalize a listener's voluntary movement toward the speaker. A general preventive rule can protect access before an encounter escalates, without treating petitioners as past wrongdoers or proving that every permitted alternative is equally persuasive.

**Joins, clarity and remedy.** Will join a content-neutral, affirmative advancement-and-fit opinion with adequate-channel and facial clarity analysis. Will not join a loose balancing formula that makes statutory knowledge or medical need alone sufficient, an unbounded privacy right in public, or an argument that the statute has no speech burden. The ordinary purpose descriptions and precise approach construction suffice for this facial case; overbreadth does not establish substantial unconstitutional operation at the actual scope. No clarification remand, new study or hearing is necessary. A separate writing is optional only if needed to preserve the limited construction and genuine burden assessment.
