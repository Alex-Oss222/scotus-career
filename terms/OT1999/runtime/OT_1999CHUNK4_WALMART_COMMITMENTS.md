## Entering law and actual record

**Inherently distinctive nonfunctional restaurant trade dress**, effective *Two Pesos*, June 26, 1992, protects qualifying restaurant presentation without an additional acquired-meaning requirement. The accepted findings in that case and its explicit reservation of other product-design categories matter. White's controlling rule had direct joins from O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter. Stevens and Thomas supported the judgment on separate statutory accounts. Ginsburg and Breyer were not then seated.

**Source-identifying color and functionality**, effective *Qualitex*, March 28, 1995, permits nonfunctional color with acquired source significance. Souter's opinion had every Associate's full join. The case rejected an exclusion of color from trademark eligibility while requiring secondary meaning for bare color. It did not extend inherent distinctiveness to every aesthetic product feature. The rule leaves source identification, nonfunctionality and confusion independent: source significance never makes a functional feature protectable.

The applicable post-1988 §43(a)(1)(A) protects the source relationships identified in its text, and §1127 defines marks through their source-identifying function, including an unknown source. These provisions permit design protection when design performs that function. They do not equate an unusual or recognizable product with an inherently source-identifying mark.

Pub. L. 106-43 §5, August 5, 1999, adds the claimant's burden of proving nonfunctionality for trade dress unregistered on the principal register. That is not a distinctiveness rule. Its application to antecedent conduct or an earlier trial burden is neither established by the source admission nor necessary to decide this grant. Section 2(e)'s registration-date provision governs section 2, not section 5. No Associate uses the amendment to order a new trial or supply an unmade functionality finding.

The disputed dress is the design of a clothing line: the claimed combination and placement of seersucker, appliques, undecorated backgrounds and garment forms. It is not the manufacturer's word mark or a separate package. The jury found deliberate infringement and the circuit sustained confusion on its own rationale. Those propositions do not establish the independently necessary source significance. The lower opinion expressly states that secondary meaning is not asserted. No survey, acquired-source finding, preserved request to reopen proof or functional-design finding is invented.

The district injunction was broader than the dress the circuit found protectable. The circuit had already ordered a narrower injunction and reduced trade-dress damages, while retaining independent copyright protection for one excluded garment. The limited grant cannot restore the original overbroad injunction, recompute an uncertain total or erase the independent claims by a shorthand reversal of the whole case.

## Supported alternatives and their strongest objections

**Require acquired source significance for product design.** Product design ordinarily constitutes the goods' appearance or utility that purchasers seek, rather than a separate sign designating origin. Even an arbitrary-looking combination can be attractive or recognizable as a kind of product without immediately indicating one producer. A claimant must therefore establish secondary meaning: the claimed design's primary significance to relevant consumers is to identify source, rather than the product itself. Once that condition is met, nonfunctionality and likelihood of confusion remain independently necessary.

The strongest objection is that the statute lists broad symbols/devices/combinations without distinguishing design from packaging, while *Two Pesos* already rejected a universal acquired-meaning condition. The answer is a source-function distinction, not removal of design from statutory eligibility. *Qualitex* already demonstrates that a category may be eligible but require acquired significance. Restaurant presentation and packaging can operate as source presentation apart from the goods' own desired design; *Two Pesos* left this different category open. No decision about every borderline package/product hybrid is necessary for these garments.

Functionality and confusion alone do not answer the source issue. Nonfunctional ornamental designs remain part of the product consumers may desire, and copying can reproduce that appeal without appropriating an inherently source-identifying signal. Confusion arising from the total circumstances cannot supply protectable distinctiveness if the asserted product feature has not become a source indicator. This is not a finding that these garments are functional, that all copying is innocent, or that copyright and state law offer no remedy.

**Permit inherent product-design distinctiveness on a demanding case-specific showing.** Samara's strongest position combines the broad statutory terms, the specifically articulated combination, favorable Rule 50 treatment of the jury's verdict, and an injunction narrowed to preserve ordinary design competition. A design might be so unusual that it immediately identifies source, and a rule demanding acquired meaning can expose an emerging producer to copying before recognition develops.

The objection that defeats this path for the commitments below is that a legal standard based on unusual appearance and designer intent does not reliably distinguish source signaling from the desired product design. Making the standard demanding does not identify the missing legal criterion; the source association must be demonstrated. *Two Pesos* accepted inherent distinctiveness of restaurant presentation for its limited question and did not adjudicate the category presented here. The cost of delayed trademark protection cannot make the statutory source function disappear.

**Reject this record without deciding every product design.** Judge Newman's view offers a narrower route: ordinary features and varying combinations cannot reasonably sustain this product-line claim even if rare inherently distinctive designs exist. This path respects Rule 50 only if it identifies legal insufficiency, not a judicial preference about aesthetics. All eight Associates prefer answering the presented category question, because merely tightening the present fact application would leave the recurrent product/source confusion in the legal standard. The categorical prerequisite also avoids an appellate retrial of how distinctive these garments look.

## Shared scope and remedy

All eight Associates support the same product-design prerequisite and the identified reversal/remand. This is a tally of independent Associate commitments only. It does not predict Stone's position, assign an opinion, or itself create Court law.

The shared proposition is: **Product-design trade dress cannot receive §43(a) protection through inherent distinctiveness alone. The claimant must prove acquired source-identifying significance, and must separately satisfy nonfunctionality and the remaining infringement requirements.** The design is not categorically ineligible. *Two Pesos* remains effective for its qualifying restaurant presentation; *Qualitex* remains effective for nonfunctional color with acquired significance. *Inwood* supplies the meaning of secondary meaning and the separate functionality boundary. No precedent is overruled.

The lower opinion says secondary meaning was not asserted. The federal trade-dress judgment therefore cannot be preserved by inventing an acquired-meaning finding or treating copying/confusion evidence as one. The ordinary remand determines the necessary procedural disposition and consequences for the injunction, damages and fees attributable to the removed federal trade-dress ground. It provides no automatic opportunity to retry an unasserted theory, no compelled evidentiary hearing and no new damages total. Current *Weisgram* preserves actual fair opportunity and genuinely supported procedural grounds; it does not create them here.

The Court's answer does not adjudicate the copyright or state-law merits beyond the grant. Independently sustained liability and relief remain subject to their own lawful bases, with consequential allocation addressed only as necessary to remove reliance on the erroneous federal trade-dress ground. The circuit's prior narrowing/reductions are not undone. No double recovery, revived original broad injunction or blanket cancellation of all relief follows.
