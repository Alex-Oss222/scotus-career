### 2. The offshore line is fixed and the reciprocal rights retain their qualifications

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph B fixes the federal-state offshore boundary at the original Exhibit A coordinates, with NAD 83/WGS 84 controlling and NAD 27 values serving only as conversion aids; under 43 U.S.C. section 1301(b), that line does not migrate with later coastal change. Against the other sovereign and persons claiming under it, the United States has exclusive exploration and natural-resource rights seaward, subject to section 1337(g) and Paragraph C, and Alaska has corresponding shoreward rights except within the Petroleum Reserve and Arctic Refuge and subject to section 1313 and Paragraph C; the reciprocal noninterference injunctions bind each sovereign, its privies, assigns, lessees and other persons claiming under it at that qualified scope.

**Statutory reservations:** Section 1313 preserves lawfully and expressly acquired federal parcels and their accretions, resources and improvements; lands lawfully held under state law; lands expressly retained or ceded at statehood, apart from a merely general marginal-sea retention or cession; proprietary acquisitions by eminent domain, purchase, cession, gift or otherwise; federal-use filled, built-up or reclaimed lands; existing federal rights in lands presently and actually occupied under claim of right; lands or interests held for tribes, bands, groups or individual Indians; and structures and improvements constructed under the federal navigational servitude. No new parcel-specific application is decided.

Section 1337(g)'s coastal-state revenue rights remain in force without transferring seaward title. Its ordinary account covers specified bonuses, rents, royalties and other bidding revenues from post-September 18, 1978 federal leases in the three-nautical-mile band, excluding federal income and windfall-profits taxes. Partial tracts ordinarily use the percentage of surface acreage within that band. Alaska's special treatment includes a partially covered tract's revenues until seven years after settlement of a boundary dispute under a section 1336 agreement made before January 1, 1986; for other Alaska tracts that special period ended April 15, 1993. Subject to the disputed-boundary provisions, the State receives 27 percent of the covered revenues with accrued interest by the last business day of the month after Treasury deposit, with the remainder sent simultaneously to federal miscellaneous receipts.

For a section 1336 boundary dispute, section 1337(g)(5) credits the agreement account with qualifying post-1978 revenues from tracts wholly or partly within the State-asserted band if not already deposited. Its interim 27-percent payment with interest concerns tracts wholly within the federally asserted band and falls due on the later of thirty days after December 1, 1987 or the last business day of the month after deposit. At settlement, additional sums due are paid from escrow; an insufficiency is met from federal lease revenues under the statutory formula. The statute's information and confidentiality rules, common-deposit notification and optional sharing agreements, account investment, October 1, 1985 accounting-effective date, and equal division of the state share where a tract falls within two or more States' bands remain intact. The decree calculates no statutory award or disputed account balance.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1301(b) distinguishes a decree-fixed offshore line from a coast that otherwise changes with physical features. The original schedule supplies that line; the decree neither commissions a new survey nor substitutes an illustrative map or converted datum. The 1997 judgment supplies the governing baseline and rejects a Dinkum-generated island belt. Its reservation-title rulings and the express statutory and lease reservations prevent either reciprocal injunction from becoming an unqualified resource grant. Section 1337(g) preserves a financial interest at its own limits, while section 1313 preserves different federal estates and interests; neither is interchangeable with Paragraph C's lease rules. No hypothetical parcel, coordinate or revenue dispute is adjudicated.

### 3. Three lease categories govern administration and escrow without altering third-party rights

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph C governs accounting and administration by the three distinct categories below, with the Section 7 escrow deadline running no later than 180 days after entry on June 29, 2000. It preserves the sovereigns' rights and obligations toward lessees and third parties, whether arising under Section 7 agreements or otherwise, and all rights and obligations under present or future unitization, operating, enhanced-recovery, commingling or similar agreements between the parties or with others.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 7 of the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act, 43 U.S.C. section 1336, and the agreements made under it supplied a means of leasing disputed areas while preserving revenues for the litigation's outcome. The fixed line also intersects leases issued in areas not disputed when issued. Those different circumstances explain why an agreement controls its leases while an existing nonagreement lease remains with its original lessor. The specified termination events end that transitional treatment and return the area to Paragraph B. Fixing the line does not retroactively redistribute the protected former-lease revenues or create a new obligation owed by a lessee or third party. Filing the proposal started no payment period; entry of the decree starts the 180 days. No amount, cancellation or present accounting default is determined.

### 4. The two reservation coastal descriptions preserve their different measures

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph D identifies the Petroleum Reserve's coastal boundary as the continuous line beginning at the western bank of the Colville River and following the highest highwater mark westward, closing small-lagoon entrances including Peard Bay, Wainwright Inlet, the Kuk River, Kugrua Bay and River, and other small bays and river estuaries; it follows the ocean side of barrier islands and sandspits within three miles of shore and the ocean side of the Plover Islands to Icy Cape's northwestern extremity, approximately 70 degrees 21 minutes N., 161 degrees 46 minutes W. Paragraph E identifies the Arctic Refuge's coastal boundary from the international boundary between Alaska and Yukon Territory at extreme low water near Monument 1, westward along extreme low water and across lagoon entrances so that all offshore bars, reefs, islands and lagoons separating them from the mainland are within the Refuge, ending at Brownlow Point, approximately 70 degrees 10 minutes N., 145 degrees 51 minutes W.

**Controlling explanation:** The 1997 judgment supplies the identified reservation estates and their separate legal bases. Highest highwater for the Petroleum Reserve is not substituted for extreme low water for the Refuge. Paragraph D preserves the submitted geographic description of the named reserve; it makes no new finding resolving the executive-order citation discrepancy. Paragraph E's reference to Public Land Order 2214, 25 Fed. Reg. 12598 (1960), identifies the descriptive coastline without making that order retroactively defeat state title. The legally operative 1957 segregation remains the earlier judgment's ground, and the 1980 enlargement does not supply the original statehood boundary. Neither paragraph decides title outside the described estate or replaces the original offshore coordinate schedule.

### 5. Reservation coasts may move, and jurisdiction remains for implementation

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Paragraph F the Petroleum Reserve and Arctic Refuge coastal boundaries migrate with changes in the relevant physical features, and the parties may address resulting disputes through negotiation, alternative dispute resolution or invocation of the Court's retained jurisdiction; they may jointly submit a boundary-location agreement for entry as a supplemental decree. Under Paragraph G the Court retains jurisdiction to entertain further proceedings, enter orders and issue writs necessary or advisable to effectuate and supplement this decree and the parties' rights; in all other respects the decree is final.

**Controlling explanation:** The reservation coastlines describe different boundaries from Paragraph B's immobilized offshore line. Preserving their movement does not unsettle the decree-fixed federal-state coordinates. The available methods permit practical resolution of an actual future change without requiring a particular sequence, new survey or hearing now. Retained jurisdiction carries out the 1997 judgment and the present decree; it does not reopen the adjudicated title questions or establish that a future dispute already exists.

Subject to the disputed-boundary provisions, the State receives 27 percent of the covered revenues with accrued interest by the last business day of the month after Treasury deposit, with the remainder sent simultaneously to federal miscellaneous receipts.

For a section 1336 boundary dispute, section 1337(g)(5) credits the agreement account with qualifying post-1978 revenues from tracts wholly or partly within the State-asserted band if not already deposited. Its interim 27-percent payment with interest concerns tracts wholly within the federally asserted band and falls due on the later of thirty days after December 1, 1987 or the last business day of the month after deposit. At settlement, additional sums due are paid from escrow; an insufficiency is met from federal lease revenues under the statutory formula. The statute's information and confidentiality rules, common-deposit notification and optional sharing agreements, account investment, October 1, 1985 accounting-effective date, and equal division of the state share where a tract falls within two or more States' bands remain intact. The decree calculates no statutory award or disputed account balance.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1301(b) distinguishes a decree-fixed offshore line from a coast that otherwise changes with physical features. The original schedule supplies that line; the decree neither commissions a new survey nor substitutes an illustrative map or converted datum. The 1997 judgment supplies the governing baseline and rejects a Dinkum-generated island belt. Its reservation-title rulings and the express statutory and lease reservations prevent either reciprocal injunction from becoming an unqualified resource grant. Section 1337(g) preserves a financial interest at its own limits, while section 1313 preserves different federal estates and interests; neither is interchangeable with Paragraph C's lease rules. No hypothetical parcel, coordinate or revenue dispute is adjudicated.

### 3. Three lease categories govern administration and escrow without altering third-party rights

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph C governs accounting and administration by the three distinct categories below, with the Section 7 escrow deadline running no later than 180 days after entry on June 29, 2000. It preserves the sovereigns' rights and obligations toward lessees and third parties, whether arising under Section 7 agreements or otherwise, and all rights and obligations under present or future unitization, operating, enhanced-recovery, commingling or similar agreements between the parties or with others.

| Lease category | Administration, distribution and transition |
|---|---|
| Existing and former leases subject to Section 7 agreements | Distribute escrow according to the agreements within 180 days after entry. Carry out all applicable agreement provisions and administer the leases under their terms. |
| Existing nonagreement leases intersected by the fixed line | The original lessor continues administration and has the exclusive right to all past and future lease revenue. Upon expiration, relinquishment or termination, Paragraph B alone governs exploration and resource-exploitation rights in the formerly leased area. Fixing the line does not change distributions from former nonagreement leases that expired before entry and would have been intersected. |
| Existing Section 7 agreement leases also intersected by the fixed line | The agreement governs escrow distribution and administration. Upon expiration, relinquishment or termination, Paragraph B alone governs the area's exploration and resource-exploitation rights. Fixing the line does not change distributions from former agreement leases that expired before entry and would have been intersected. |

**Controlling explanation:** Section 7 of the Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act, 43 U.S.C. section 1336, and the agreements made under it supplied a means of leasing disputed areas while preserving revenues for the litigation's outcome. The fixed line also intersects leases issued in areas not disputed when issued. Those different circumstances explain why an agreement controls its leases while an existing nonagreement lease remains with its original lessor. The specified termination events end that transitional treatment and return the area to Paragraph B. Fixing the line does not retroactively redistribute the protected former-lease revenues or create a new obligation owed by a lessee or third party. Filing the proposal started no payment period; entry of the decree starts the 180 days. No amount, cancellation or present accounting default is determined.

### 4. The two reservation coastal descriptions preserve their different measures

**Holding and operative rule:** Paragraph D identifies the Petroleum Reserve's coastal boundary as the continuous line beginning at the western bank of the Colville River and following the highest highwater mark westward, closing small-lagoon entrances including Peard Bay, Wainwright Inlet, the Kuk River, Kugrua Bay and River, and other small bays and river estuaries; it follows the ocean side of barrier islands and sandspits within three miles of shore and the ocean side of the Plover Islands to Icy Cape's northwestern extremity, approximately 70 degrees 21 minutes N., 161 degrees 46 minutes W. Paragraph E identifies the Arctic Refuge's coastal boundary from the international boundary between Alaska and Yukon Territory at extreme low water near Monument 1, westward along extreme low water and across lagoon entrances so that all offshore bars, reefs, islands and lagoons separating them from the mainland are within the Refuge, ending at Brownlow Point, approximately 70 degrees 10 minutes N., 145 degrees 51 minutes W.

**Controlling explanation:** The 1997 judgment supplies the identified reservation estates and their separate legal bases. Highest highwater for the Petroleum Reserve is not substituted for extreme low water for the Refuge. Paragraph D preserves the submitted geographic description of the named reserve; it makes no new finding resolving the executive-order citation discrepancy. Paragraph E's reference to Public Land Order 2214, 25 Fed. Reg. 12598 (1960), identifies the descriptive coastline without making that order retroactively defeat state title. The legally operative 1957 segregation remains the earlier judgment's ground, and the 1980 enlargement does not supply the original statehood boundary. Neither paragraph decides title outside the described estate or replaces the original offshore coordinate schedule.

### 5. Reservation coasts may move, and jurisdiction remains for implementation

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Paragraph F the Petroleum Reserve and Arctic Refuge coastal boundaries migrate with changes in the relevant physical features, and the parties may address resulting disputes through negotiation, alternative dispute resolution or invocation of the Court's retained jurisdiction; they may jointly submit a boundary-location agreement for entry as a supplemental decree. Under Paragraph G the Court retains jurisdiction to entertain further proceedings, enter orders and issue writs necessary or advisable to effectuate and supplement this decree and the parties' rights; in all other respects the decree is final.

**Controlling explanation:** The reservation coastlines describe different boundaries from Paragraph B's immobilized offshore line. Preserving their movement does not unsettle the decree-fixed federal-state coordinates. The available methods permit practical resolution of an actual future change without requiring a particular sequence, new survey or hearing now. Retained jurisdiction carries out the 1997 judgment and the present decree; it does not reopen the adjudicated title questions or establish that a future dispute already exists.
