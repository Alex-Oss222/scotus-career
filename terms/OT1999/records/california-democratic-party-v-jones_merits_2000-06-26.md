## Controlling holdings

### 1. Compulsory outsider selection of an official party nominee imposes a severe burden

**Holding and operative rule:** A State severely burdens political association when it requires an objecting party to give voters unaffiliated with that party equal participation in choosing its official nominee through this blanket-primary mechanism. The requirement therefore must be narrowly tailored to serve a compelling state interest; ordinary authority to administer elections does not alone supply that justification.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, controls each holding in this entry by seven direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Tashjian protects meaningful associational choice while preserving the State's role in a workable election system. Eu protects party governance against state direction, and Democratic Party v. Wisconsin ex rel. La Follette rejects state-imposed participation that overrides a party's protected selection rules. Hurley protects the composition of private expression; it does not exempt every association from ordinary regulation. Selecting the official standard bearer is a central party act even when the State supplies the primary machinery. California's system changes who makes that choice, rather than merely regulating its time, ballot or administration. Sincere crossover can alter nominations and candidates' positions without organized raiding. The parties need not prove organizational collapse or await an election that conclusively changes every nominee. Retained endorsement and advocacy do not restore the lost selection choice. Burdick and Timmons require attention to the actual burden: Timmons left the party able to choose a consenting nominee through the available system, while this law makes outsiders participants in the party's own nomination. The public character of elections permits regulation and requires protection of voters' constitutional rights; it does not remove this distinct associational injury. The Court decides neither all conventional open primaries nor every membership or registration rule.

### 2. The asserted interests do not justify this compulsory nomination mechanism

**Holding and operative rule:** The interests advanced for Proposition 198 do not justify its severe interference with the objecting parties' nomination choices. Participation, choice, competition, privacy and stable elections may support appropriate election rules, but the State has not established a compelling justification and necessary fit for imposing equal outsider participation in the party's own official selection.

**Controlling explanation:** The State may seek representative government and robust participation, but it cannot justify this intrusion merely by preferring more moderate party nominees or nominees responsive to voters who have chosen another association. That purpose changes the party's political choice rather than protecting the electoral process from an independent injury. Giving independents or minority-party voters influence in another party's safe-district primary likewise does not establish a right to select that party's standard bearer. Increased choice, turnout and competition are legitimate electoral aims, yet the necessary connection to compelled party nomination is missing. A nonpartisan winnowing election can select candidates without purporting to select each party's official nominee, and a party may itself invite additional voters. Those possibilities show why broader participation does not require this mechanism; their mention decides no unpresented system. A privacy interest in political affiliation also does not establish an entitlement to participate secretly in every other party's nomination. The stability and fairness arguments do not identify a necessity sufficient to overcome this severe burden. The Court does not substitute a numerical prediction for constitutional analysis or treat every participation objective as impermissible. The defect is the asserted purposes and insufficient fit of this particular compulsory means.

### 3. Relief follows the nomination defect and does not decide other election systems

**Holding and operative rule:** The defense judgment must be reversed and the case remanded for declaratory and prospective injunctive relief against compulsory blanket selection of these objecting parties' official nominees. The ruling leaves ordinary election administration and constitutional voting protections intact and reserves the validity of unpresented primary designs and any remaining severability or implementation questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The plaintiffs established a constitutional defect in the compulsory choice mechanism, not a right to have this Court administer elections. The remedy therefore prevents that compelled selection without prescribing a ballot, reopening completed elections or awarding damages. Timmons preserves ordinary regulation that leaves meaningful lawful nomination choices; the white-primary decisions preserve voters' constitutional protection against exclusion. Describing less intrusive alternatives does not hold that every top-two or nonpartisan system, party invitation or registration condition is valid. The same defect governs the state and congressional nominations presented. No independently presented Elections Clause or national-delegate claim supplies an additional judgment.

## Public Projection

## Event

California Democratic Party v. Jones, No. 99-401. Plenary merits decision; argued April 24, 2000; decided June 26, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, reviewing 169 F.3d 646 (1999), which adopted the District Court's post-trial ruling, 984 F. Supp. 1288 (1997).

The question is whether California may require objecting political parties to select their official nominees for state and congressional office through a blanket primary that permits every voter to cross party lines office by office. Proposition 198 gives each party's nomination to its highest vote recipient even though voters unaffiliated with that party participate on equal terms. Elections for party committees remain confined to party registrants. All four plaintiff parties oppose the compelled participation through their governing rules.

The trial record supports likely crossover voting and effects in some contests, while distinguishing sincere crossover from comparatively rare organized raiding. Its estimates vary by race and denominator; the Court does not adopt a universal numerical threshold. The parties retain advocacy and endorsement, but those activities do not themselves select the legal nominee. No separate Elections Clause claim, presidential-delegate judgment, damages claim or retrospective election challenge is decided.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's judgment and remands for proceedings consistent with the invalidity of compulsory blanket selection of the objecting parties' official nominees.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 7-2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens and Ginsburg.

The blanket-primary requirement cannot constitutionally compel these objecting parties to submit their official nominations to equal participation by unaffiliated voters. The lower courts must provide declaratory and prospective injunctive relief fitted to that defect. The Court does not reopen an election, award damages, prescribe a replacement ballot, decide every severability question or invalidate an unpresented voluntary or nonpartisan arrangement.

## Precedent Treatment

- Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut supplies protection for meaningful party associational choices within a regulated electoral system; this decision does not adopt an unlimited power of party leaders to override members.
- Eu v. San Francisco County Democratic Central Committee supplies the protection against state control of party governance and political choice; ordinary election administration remains available.
- Democratic Party v. Wisconsin ex rel. La Follette supplies protection against forced participation inconsistent with protected party selection rules; the present decision applies that principle to the challenged official nominations without deciding national-delegate questions.
- Burdick v. Takushi supplies burden-sensitive review of election regulations. The severe burden here triggers compelling-interest and narrow-tailoring review rather than the lesser justification sufficient for ordinary mechanics.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party remains controlling as to its nonfusion restriction. Its retained means of choosing a consenting nominee distinguish it from compelled outsider participation in the actual choice.
- Reform Party of Allegheny County v. Allegheny County Department of Elections retains its equal-access holding concerning a timely consensual cross-nomination and its limits on the independent association issue. It does not itself decide the present public-primary question.
- Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston supplies protection for the composition of private expression, with its actual limits; this decision neither exempts ordinary services from neutral regulation nor classifies every regulated association as private expression.
- The white-primary decisions, including Smith v. Allwright and Terry v. Adams, retain their prohibitions on unconstitutional exclusion from electoral processes. Associational protection supplies no exemption from those constitutional commands.

## Law After Decision

California may not enforce the compulsory blanket selection of the objecting parties' official nominees. Election regulation remains subject to the burden-sensitive framework, with strict scrutiny for the severe selection injury established here. Timmons and Reform Party retain their distinct holdings about ballot designation and unequal cross-nomination opportunities. The State may pursue broad electoral participation through lawful, less intrusive designs; this decision does not approve every nonpartisan, top-two, voluntary open-primary or registration system. A party's nomination autonomy remains subject to applicable constitutional voting protections.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring.** He joins the Court's complete rule and remedy. Political parties may choose to broaden their own appeal, change their principles or invite participation. The State's effort to achieve moderation by changing the electorate that selects a party's official representative instead directs the association's political development. The distinction is between the party's own adaptation and a compulsory substitution of decision makers, not hostility to broader democratic participation. His concurrence adds no campaign-finance holding or adjudication of another primary design.

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg, dissenting.** They would affirm on the presented associational claim for both state and congressional nominations. They distinguish a publicly required and administered nominating election from the party's private leadership, endorsement and advocacy decisions. Protecting a party's voluntary candidate choices against exclusion does not necessarily give it a constitutional veto over the qualified electorate in that public primary. Timmons's consensual candidate-access dispute and Hurley's private expressive composition do not erase this boundary; Reform Party's equality holding does not resolve it. Independently, the retained party functions, limited and variable injury supported by the trial record, and participation, choice and representative-government interests adequately justify the law under contextual review. They acknowledge that official nomination differs from endorsement and that sincere crossover can matter; neither observation establishes the severe private-association injury the Court finds. Their position does not depend on implied party consent from a popular vote or on the absence of every crossover effect. The unpresented Elections Clause question remains undecided and supplies no different judgment.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Ninth Circuit for declaratory and prospective injunctive relief confined to the unconstitutional compulsory nomination mechanism and any ordinary implementation questions properly remaining. No new election, damages award or statutory replacement is ordered. State and congressional nominations receive the same disposition; national-delegate arrangements and the amended presidential reporting provisions are not adjudicated.
