## Event, review and chronology

The Frees petition for review of the Fifth Circuit's June 3, 1999 final judgment, reported at 176 F.3d 298, which affirmed dismissal under Louisiana law after the Louisiana Supreme Court declined the certified questions. The January opinion at 164 F.3d 270 is an earlier step, not the final judgment under review. The case was argued March 27, 2000 and is decided April 3, 2000. Research cutoff: April 3, 2000.

The Frees brought a Louisiana consumer class action over alleged infant-formula price fixing in October 1993. Defendants removed the action. The original-jurisdiction anchor accepted for the question granted arose from the lower court's attribution of potential attorney's fees to the representative plaintiffs, not from an allegation that their ordinary individual damages exceeded the applicable $50,000 threshold. The question assumes at least one named plaintiff's claim within original diversity jurisdiction and that the absent class members' smaller claims form part of the same Article III case. It asks whether 28 U.S.C. section 1367 permits supplemental jurisdiction over those smaller claims despite the former separate-claim amount rule of Zahn v. International Paper Co.

The Court reviews the asserted jurisdictional defect in the final judgment, not the correctness of the Louisiana antitrust or Civil Code rulings. Fee attribution generally, complete-diversity defects, settlement approval, class certification and discretionary retention under section 1367(c) are outside the granted question. The proposed settlement received no final approval. No fact about the amount of an individual damages claim, the existence of an original anchor or the approval of a settlement is inferred from the removal history.

Section 1367 applies to this action because it commenced after December 1, 1990. Subsection (a), subject to (b), (c) and express contrary federal statutes, reaches claims forming the same Article III case as claims within original jurisdiction and includes additional-party claims. In diversity-only actions, subsection (b) restricts claims by plaintiffs against persons joined under Rules 14, 19, 20 or 24, claims by persons proposed as plaintiffs under Rule 19, and claims by persons intervening as plaintiffs under Rule 24, when inconsistent with section 1332. It does not list Rule 23. Its Rule 20 provision concerns claims against joined persons, not every claim by a Rule 20 plaintiff. Subsection (c) separately permits nonretention for novel or complex state law, substantial predominance, dismissal of all original-jurisdiction claims, or exceptional circumstances supplying other compelling reasons. None of those discretionary grounds is redetermined here.

Zahn supplies the former separate-claim amount requirement; Snyder v. Harris supplies the prohibition against aggregating distinct class claims to manufacture an original anchor; Finley v. United States requires statutory authorization for additional parties. The parties' statutory dispute concerns the extent to which section 1367 supplies that authorization and changes Zahn's operation. It does not concern Article III's outer capacity or permit jurisdiction based on convenience.
