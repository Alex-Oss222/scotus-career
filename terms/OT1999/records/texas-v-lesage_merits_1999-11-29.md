L2: such proof does not erase a separately established unconstitutional admissions-process injury. L4: separate source-specific remedies, defendants, immunity and future exposure; neither nominal relief nor an injunction is automatically awarded.

The operative rule is Hopwood, not a new race-neutral-only counterfactual. The first inquiry is whether unconstitutional unequal treatment occurred under the governing substantial-justification standard. If it did, Texas bears the preponderance same-decision burden for denial-of-admission consequences. The numbers of applicants with higher grades or scores and the District Court's comparative finding are evidence, not a substitute for specifying the lawful admissions process. Success on the defense defeats compensation for admission that would lawfully have been denied; it does not validate an unconstitutional process. An available claim against a proper defendant may support nominal or declaratory recognition, subject to statutory and immunity limits. Any prospective claim requires supported continuing or imminent unequal competition and ordinary equitable grounds; possible cessation or abandonment cannot be assumed from the abbreviated account.

## Holdings

**Same-decision proof limits relief for the rejection without erasing a completed process injury.** Once an unconstitutional admissions process has been established, the school must prove by a preponderance that a specified constitutional process would have produced the same rejection. Success defeats admission and compensation for that denial and its consequences; it does not erase the separately established unequal-process injury.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion controls every proposition stated here.

**Controlling explanation:** Hopwood separates unequal opportunity to compete from entitlement to a seat and supplies the school-borne defense. The lawful counterfactual must allow any race use that controlling law permits; it cannot silently become a race-neutral-only requirement. Texas offered academic and qualitative evidence supporting the same rejection, but the Fifth Circuit treated its causal significance as irrelevant instead of testing the specified lawful process and burden. Its legal premise was mistaken. The evidence is not newly declared conclusive, and the Court does not decide whether the challenged program was unconstitutional merely because it considered race.

**Relief follows the particular injury and lawful remedial channel.** An established unconstitutional process may support nominal or declaratory recognition even if Texas proves the same lawful rejection, but only under an available cause against a proper defendant and subject to immunity. Prospective relief additionally requires concrete continuing or imminent exposure to unequal competition and an equitable basis.

**Controlling explanation:** Carey distinguishes recognition of a completed violation from compensation for proved loss; Northeastern Florida distinguishes unequal competition from guaranteed selection. Sections 1981 and 1983 and Title VI retain their separate requirements. Possible cessation of the practice or abandonment of prospective relief must be resolved, not assumed. No admission, damages amount, immunity waiver, personal liability or broad future-program injunction follows from this decision.

Existing admissions law remains controlling. A successful same-decision defense defeats relief caused by the lost admission, while an independently proved unconstitutional process and any continuing unequal competition retain their own causes, defenses, standing requirements and remedies. Statistical comparisons alone do not replace the required lawful counterfactual.
