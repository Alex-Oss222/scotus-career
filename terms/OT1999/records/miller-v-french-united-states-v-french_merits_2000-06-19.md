## Controlling holdings

### I. The automatic stay displaces ordinary equitable continuation

**Holding and operative rule:** For the presented motion under 18 U.S.C. §3626(b)(2), subsection (e) automatically stays covered prospective relief beginning on the thirtieth day after filing and ending when the court enters a final order resolving the motion; the court may postpone the starting date for good cause by no more than sixty days, but general calendar congestion is not good cause. Beyond that express postponement, ordinary equitable authority does not permit the court to suspend the statutory stay.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion of the Court has six direct rationale votes: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the judgment without joining this statutory-construction part; Stevens and Breyer dissent from the construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the stay, postponement and appeal provisions together. The enacted command makes the motion operate as a stay; the express postponement both limits its length and excludes general calendar congestion. Preserving the same decree through ordinary injunction balancing beyond those limits would defeat that structure. The United States argues that traditional equitable authority survives because extraordinary injunction requirements differ from good cause and subsection (e)(4) expressly addresses orders blocking the stay. But that subsection makes such orders appealable as refusals to dissolve or modify an injunction under 28 U.S.C. §1292(a)(1), regardless of their label or preliminary or final character; it supplies review, not additional substantive authority to block the stay. The express subsection (e)(3) postponement is excluded from that special appeal treatment. Constitutional avoidance cannot replace this detailed statutory direction with a competing scheme. Subsection (e)(1)'s prompt-disposition duty and mandamus remedy remain. The retained 180-day timing language for motions under other law does not govern the immediate-termination motion presented here; its broader interaction with the amended under-subsection-(b) opening is not decided. Section 3626(a)(2)'s separate ninety-day expiration for newly entered preliminary relief does not turn this existing permanent decree into a preliminary order.

### II. Congress may change interim prospective law without deciding the case

**Holding and operative rule:** The automatic stay is not categorically unconstitutional under Article III merely because it temporarily suspends prospective relief before final adjudication of the termination motion. Congress may change the law governing future enforcement while leaving the court to decide the new law's factual and legal predicates; this holding does not resolve an application that actually deprives the court of a meaningful opportunity to adjudicate.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes for this bounded holding and the remand in Holding III.

**Controlling explanation:** Robertson distinguishes changing governing law from prescribing a judicial answer under unchanged law; Klein preserves the independent judicial task. Wheeling Bridge and Agostini distinguish future operation of an injunction from completed adjudication. Rouse and Hadix apply that distinction to conditional prison-relief termination. Here the interim enforcement rule itself changes prospective law. A party's motion triggers the legislated consequence; the party does not thereby establish lawful prison conditions or defeat the findings required to retain relief. Although the Pendleton decree followed adjudication in material part, the stay neither contradicts those past findings nor cancels accrued damages or completed lawful effects. Morgan Stanley and Plaut permit their defined reinstatement of securities limitations dismissals while preserving independent judicial work; they establish neither categorical immunity for every judgment nor unrestricted legislative control of adjudication. The prisoners correctly distinguish termination after findings from suspension before findings. That distinction requires attention to an actual denial of adjudication, but it does not make every interim prospective rule legislative adjudication. The Court rejects the Seventh Circuit's categorical structural ruling without declaring this timetable sufficient in every complex case or deciding a supported inability-to-adjudicate claim against the prisoners.

### III. The remand preserves a genuine adjudication of supported application objections

**Holding and operative rule:** In each docket, the Seventh Circuit's categorical invalidation is reversed and the case remanded, permitting a properly supported claim that the statutory timing, including available postponement, actually denies a meaningful opportunity to adjudicate to be presented and decided under Article III as well as any applicable due-process protection. No such violation is presumed, no feasibility hearing is required merely because time has elapsed, and no ordinary equitable exception or automatic continuation of the existing blocking injunction follows.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion of the Court; the seven Justices named for Holding II directly join this operative remand and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Benjamin requires a real opportunity to offer material current proof when it is necessary for statutory adjudication; Gavin and Hadix require the unperformed termination inquiry without making a new hearing universal. The reported postponements of the termination hearing establish neither constitutional impossibility nor prison compliance, present violations or deliberate delay. They therefore neither compel an evidentiary hearing about timing nor foreclose a supported application objection. On remand, such an objection must receive genuine consideration under ordinary procedure; this judgment does not restrict it to due process or decide the appropriate remedy in advance. The court must also decide the actual termination conditions: the movant must establish that covered relief was entered without substantive necessity, narrowness and least-intrusion findings, and sufficient findings require no ritual wording. Under amended §3626(b)(3), relief shall not terminate if written, record-based findings establish that it remains necessary to correct a current and ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn and is the least intrusive necessary means. Each retained duty requires those findings; obsolete relief may not survive through disregard of the new law. This appeal neither decides those merits nor terminates the decree. Hadix's distinct pending automatic-stay appeal is not adjudicated here.

### Provisional component positions

**Mandatory-construction group — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.** The combined text displaces an ordinary equitable power to stop this automatic stay beyond the express postponement. The mandatory operation, specific bounded postponement and exclusion of calendar congestion would lose their intended effect if a court could continue the same decree through ordinary injunction balancing. The appeal provision describes how a purported blocking order is reviewed; it does not itself grant the substantive power to enter it. Traditional equitable authority and avoidance do not justify a contrary reading of this detailed scheme.

These six would reject the Seventh Circuit's categorical Article III invalidation and reverse its judgment in both dockets, with remand for the actual statutory adjudication and any properly supported distinct constitutional claim. The stay adjusts the interim prospective effect of a decree under newly enacted remedial law; it does not command false factual findings, adjudicate current prison compliance, erase accrued relief or direct the outcome of the termination motion under unchanged law. Pendleton's decree followed adjudication in material part, but that does not make all future operation immune from valid changed law.

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas** would adopt the mandatory-valid construction at the presented facial structural scope. **Souter and Ginsburg** also reject the categorical invalidation, but require a fuller reservation: statutory expedition cannot constitutionally eliminate a real opportunity to apply the new law to the material current evidence. They do not authorize ordinary equity to override the statute, hold the timetable universally sufficient, or determine the legal source and remedy of a hypothetical proven denial in advance.

**Retained-equity group — Stevens and Breyer.** They would construe the statute to preserve traditional equitable power in an exceptional case supported by the ordinary demanding prerequisites of injunctive relief, including likelihood of success and irreparable injury. The ordinary automatic stay and limited good-cause postponement have independent work: the latter can postpone operation without the distinct injunction showing; the express appeal treatment can review an exceptional injunction rather than serving only to identify prohibited orders. They would vacate the Seventh Circuit's judgment and remand for consideration of the district court's preservation order under the retained equitable authority and the actual amended termination standards. They do not find the injunction prerequisites proved, authorize indefinite continuation or adjudicate prison compliance. Pending remand, they would permit the district court to consider appropriately supported temporary protection rather than command automatic dissolution on the premise that equitable authority is absent.

### Enhanced adversarial comparison

**Construction.** The Government's strongest argument is that Congress normally leaves equity intact unless it clearly withdraws it and that an appeal provision expressly contemplating a blocking order makes an absolute prohibition less natural. **Article III.** The prisoners' strongest path distinguishes valid new termination law from suspending an existing adjudicated remedy before a judge decides whether that remedy remains required under the new law. No Justice may find current violations, actual impossibility or deliberate dilatory conduct from that calendar alone. ## Holdings

### I. The automatic stay displaces ordinary equitable continuation

**Holding and operative rule:** For the presented motion under 18 U.S.C. §3626(b)(2), subsection (e) automatically stays covered prospective relief beginning on the thirtieth day after filing and ending when the court enters a final order resolving the motion; the court may postpone the starting date for good cause by no more than sixty days, but general calendar congestion is not good cause. Beyond that express postponement, ordinary equitable authority does not permit the court to suspend the statutory stay.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Souter's opinion of the Court has six direct rationale votes: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela joins the judgment without joining this statutory-construction part; Stevens and Breyer dissent from the construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the stay, postponement and appeal provisions together. The enacted command makes the motion operate as a stay; the express postponement both limits its length and excludes general calendar congestion. Preserving the same decree through ordinary injunction balancing beyond those limits would defeat that structure. The United States argues that traditional equitable authority survives because extraordinary injunction requirements differ from good cause and subsection (e)(4) expressly addresses orders blocking the stay. But that subsection makes such orders appealable as refusals to dissolve or modify an injunction under 28 U.S.C. §1292(a)(1), regardless of their label or preliminary or final character; it supplies review, not additional substantive authority to block the stay. The express subsection (e)(3) postponement is excluded from that special appeal treatment. Constitutional avoidance cannot replace this detailed statutory direction with a competing scheme. Subsection (e)(1)'s prompt-disposition duty and mandamus remedy remain. The retained 180-day timing language for motions under other law does not govern the immediate-termination motion presented here; its broader interaction with the amended under-subsection-(b) opening is not decided. Section 3626(a)(2)'s separate ninety-day expiration for newly entered preliminary relief does not turn this existing permanent decree into a preliminary order.

### II. Congress may change interim prospective law without deciding the case

**Holding and operative rule:** The automatic stay is not categorically unconstitutional under Article III merely because it temporarily suspends prospective relief before final adjudication of the termination motion. Congress may change the law governing future enforcement while leaving the court to decide the new law's factual and legal predicates; this holding does not resolve an application that actually deprives the court of a meaningful opportunity to adjudicate.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes for this bounded holding and the remand in Holding III.

**Controlling explanation:** Robertson distinguishes changing governing law from prescribing a judicial answer under unchanged law; Klein preserves the independent judicial task. Wheeling Bridge and Agostini distinguish future operation of an injunction from completed adjudication. Rouse and Hadix apply that distinction to conditional prison-relief termination. Here the interim enforcement rule itself changes prospective law. A party's motion triggers the legislated consequence; the party does not thereby establish lawful prison conditions or defeat the findings required to retain relief. Although the Pendleton decree followed adjudication in material part, the stay neither contradicts those past findings nor cancels accrued damages or completed lawful effects. Morgan Stanley and Plaut permit their defined reinstatement of securities limitations dismissals while preserving independent judicial work; they establish neither categorical immunity for every judgment nor unrestricted legislative control of adjudication. The prisoners correctly distinguish termination after findings from suspension before findings. That distinction requires attention to an actual denial of adjudication, but it does not make every interim prospective rule legislative adjudication. The Court rejects the Seventh Circuit's categorical structural ruling without declaring this timetable sufficient in every complex case or deciding a supported inability-to-adjudicate claim against the prisoners.

### III. The remand preserves a genuine adjudication of supported application objections

**Holding and operative rule:** In each docket, the Seventh Circuit's categorical invalidation is reversed and the case remanded, permitting a properly supported claim that the statutory timing, including available postponement, actually denies a meaningful opportunity to adjudicate to be presented and decided under Article III as well as any applicable due-process protection. No such violation is presumed, no feasibility hearing is required merely because time has elapsed, and no ordinary equitable exception or automatic continuation of the existing blocking injunction follows.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Souter's opinion of the Court; the seven Justices named for Holding II directly join this operative remand and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Benjamin requires a real opportunity to offer material current proof when it is necessary for statutory adjudication; Gavin and Hadix require the unperformed termination inquiry without making a new hearing universal. The reported postponements of the termination hearing establish neither constitutional impossibility nor prison compliance, present violations or deliberate delay. They therefore neither compel an evidentiary hearing about timing nor foreclose a supported application objection. On remand, such an objection must receive genuine consideration under ordinary procedure; this judgment does not restrict it to due process or decide the appropriate remedy in advance. The court must also decide the actual termination conditions: the movant must establish that covered relief was entered without substantive necessity, narrowness and least-intrusion findings, and sufficient findings require no ritual wording. Under amended §3626(b)(3), relief shall not terminate if written, record-based findings establish that it remains necessary to correct a current and ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn and is the least intrusive necessary means. Each retained duty requires those findings; obsolete relief may not survive through disregard of the new law. This appeal neither decides those merits nor terminates the decree. Hadix's distinct pending automatic-stay appeal is not adjudicated here.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Breyer, dissenting, joined by Justice Stevens.** They would vacate the Seventh Circuit's judgment in both dockets and remand under a construction preserving exceptional equitable protection. Congress ordinarily leaves the courts' equitable power intact, and subsection (e)(4)'s provision for appeal from a blocking order supports a reading that allows a genuinely exceptional injunction. The sixty-day good-cause postponement and traditional injunction authority require different showings and can coexist without making the automatic stay meaningless. An applicant would have to establish irreparable injury, likelihood of defeating termination under the amended law, inability to resolve the merits before the stay operates, and favorable traditional balancing of the parties' and public interests. No present finding establishes those predicates. Their reading avoids the difficult Article III timing question while requiring the actual current-and-ongoing-violation and remedial-scope findings. Morgan Stanley and Plaut preserve independent adjudication under changed law; Benjamin, Gavin and Hadix require meaningful consideration of material current proof. They do not make the old decree immutable or endorse the Seventh Circuit's categorical constitutional rule. On remand they would permit consideration of appropriately supported temporary protection, without directing indefinite continuation or finding current prison conditions unlawful. They therefore dissent from mandatory statutory operation and the resulting reversal rather than vote to affirm categorical unconstitutionality.
