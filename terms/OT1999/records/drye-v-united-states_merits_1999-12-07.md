## Holdings

**Federal lien law reaches the taxpayer's existing power to accept or redirect inherited value.** A legally effective state-created power to accept an inheritance or channel its value to another by disclaimer is a right to property within 26 U.S.C. §6321 on this record. State law defines the control, but federal law determines whether the lien reaches it; state relation back cannot erase control that already existed for federal lien purposes.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, states the unanimous rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** National Bank of Commerce separates the existence of a state-law interest from its federal tax treatment. Drye had the choice to receive the value or direct its destination. The strongest contrary argument—that state law treats a disclaimer as effective from death—describes the local consequence of exercising that choice; it does not negate the choice itself. Irvine similarly rejects using state relation back to control an independently defined federal tax consequence. Its gift-tax timing analysis does not become a lien condition here. Romani requires respect for Congress's specific priority choices and supplies no broad exemption for a disclaimer. The lien therefore reaches this existing control without creating an inheritance interest federal law alone would supply.

**Limits:** The Court does not reach a disclaimer completed before legally cognizable control arises, every prior expectancy, a spendthrift-trust interest, valuation, the recipient's liability, or any competing priority.

## Precedent Treatment

United States v. National Bank of Commerce is applied for state definition of rights followed by federal lien classification. United States v. Irvine is applied by analogy to the distinction between local disclaimer effects and federal tax consequences; its gift-tax reasonable-time exception is not imported into §6321. United States v. Estate of Romani preserves specific statutory priority protections and is distinguished from the present attachment question. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

The taxpayer's actual power to accept or redirect inherited property is reachable under §6321 despite the state-law relation-back consequence of disclaimer. State law continues to define the interest, and Congress's specific priority rules remain effective.
