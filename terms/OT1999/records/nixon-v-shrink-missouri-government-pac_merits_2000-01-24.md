## Controlling holding

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those six directly adopt the holding, its explanation and limits, and the remedy.

### The presented indexed ceiling satisfies the existing contribution standard on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Russell, government must justify a direct candidate-contribution ceiling by an objectively grounded, sufficiently important corruption or appearance interest and a close fit that preserves meaningful association and effective campaign advocacy. Missouri meets that burden for the presented $1,075 statewide/state-auditor ceiling, indexed biennially from the January 1, 1995 $1,000 base and rounded to the nearest $25; this record-specific ruling establishes no dollar floor, safe harbor, other-office approval or immunity from a future materially different suppression challenge.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Missouri's combined risk and operational evidence justifies this ceiling under Russell. Goode's account, the reported $20,000 bank contribution and related official banking choice, $40,000 brewery and $20,000 bank gifts in the auditor contest, a $420,000 PAC contribution and reported sponsorship-for-kickback accusations ground a concern about official obligation and its appearance. Accusations remain accusations, and the property-related conspiracy plea proves its actual offense only. The Court does not find completed exchanges in every episode, require a proved bribe, or treat ordinary donor agreement, access or resource inequality as sufficient. Proposition A's 74-percent approval supplies context, not constitutional authority.

The District Court's affirmative findings of effective campaign capacity and substantial fundraising, the direct candidate channel, amount and indexing support the practical fit. The contrary statewide figures are serious: combined spending fell from $21.599 million to $9.337 million, primary spending from $14.249 million to $1.625 million, contested primaries from ten to one, and candidates from thirty-two to eleven. Those changes support the burden objection but do not establish this ceiling as the cause of each decline or displace the supported capacity finding for the presented application. The record supplies no finding of an alternative cause, and no decline is treated as harmless. The figure that 97.62 percent of contributors gave no more than $2,000 measures neither contributed dollars nor the effect of a $1,075 ceiling.

Missouri's affirmative combined showing meets its burden; the test is not whether challengers prove literal campaign impossibility. Russell invalidated materially lower statewide restrictions on their actual record while rejecting a universal monetary boundary. Buckley's contribution/expenditure distinction remains, but a generic appeal to deference or nominal dollar comparison cannot replace Russell's fit inquiry. The Court therefore reverses the presented invalidation, leaving materially different burdens and unchallenged tiers unresolved.

**Precedent treatment:** Russell supplies the governing burden and practical-fit inquiry, including its office-specific limits. Buckley's contribution/expenditure distinction survives without a dollar safe harbor; Day and Colorado Republican retain their separate intermediary and genuinely independent-expression rules.

## Continuity and separate writing

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by Justices Scalia and Thomas.**

The dissent accepts Russell's close-fit standard and the sufficiently important anticorruption interest, but would affirm invalidation of this presented statewide ceiling. The large-gift episodes support some prophylaxis without adequately justifying this amount. The severe statewide contraction in funding, contested primaries and candidates, together with campaign scale, requires a stronger operational answer than general findings of campaign capacity, indexing or examples of successful financing. ## Public Projection

## Event

Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC, No. 98-963. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit; argued October 5, 1999; decided January 24, 2000. The challenge concerns Missouri's $1,075 statewide/state-auditor contribution ceiling. The PAC gave Fredman's committee $1,025 in 1997 and $50 in 1998 and wished to give more; Fredman asserted that larger gifts were necessary for effective campaigning. The District Court sustained the ceiling on cross-motions for summary judgment. The Eighth Circuit enjoined enforcement pending appeal, 151 F.3d 763, and then reversed the merits judgment, 161 F.3d 519. The question is whether the State has justified the presented ceiling under the contribution rule established in Russell.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Precedent Treatment

- Russell v. Burris (April 28, 1999): applies its sufficient-interest, close-fit and meaningful-association rule and its government burden. Its invalidation of the named statewide $300 and appellate-office $100 ceilings remains effective; neither those outcomes nor its sustained residual/PAC applications establish a monetary floor or automatic analogy to this category.
- Buckley v. Valeo: retains the distinction between candidate contributions and independent expenditures, but does not replace Russell with a presumption of validity or a fixed federal-dollar benchmark.
- Day v. Holahan (May 15, 1995): preserves meaningful pooling and actual circumvention fit for intermediary receipt restrictions; other associations or aggregate political success do not automatically cure a burden on the chosen association.
- Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC (June 26, 1996): preserves genuinely independent party expression and its actual-connection inquiry; it does not immunize direct candidate cash or decide this ceiling.
