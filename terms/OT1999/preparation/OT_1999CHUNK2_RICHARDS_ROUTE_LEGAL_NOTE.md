# Richards — neutral route and prior-authority note

**Scope:** February 14, 2000 original merits event. This note supplements the validated neutral packet only. It chooses no fact, rule, result, vote or Stone position.

## Conditional relevance of the route account

The [new route extraction](OT_1999CHUNK2_RICHARDS_ROUTE_NEUTRAL_ADDITION.md) attributes three added propositions to the government: the duty station was Cullen Maintenance Facility in the Carpenter Test Area; the assumed home destination was on Fort Knox's main post; and Highway 1638 was the main route connecting those parts of the installation. The existing packet establishes early permission to leave, an accepted personal homeward purpose, active-duty status without a pass or furlough, and an accident within the base on a public state-maintained road. The new account can coexist with all those facts. It does not transform personal homeward travel into an assigned mission or resolve the alternative Education Center destination.

Even assuming the additional residence and route propositions for legal comparison, the service-incident question remains a question of law applied to the complete setting. The government can argue that the journey retained a service connection as travel from the duty facility to quarters across the installation's principal connecting road, with active-duty status and no formal pass or furlough. The claimant can argue that permission ended the day's work and the journey's accepted purpose was private, using an ordinary public road open to civilians. Base quarters and a connecting route do not erase that purpose; public access does not erase the base's geography or the work-to-quarters connection. Neither characterization is an already adopted categorical rule.

This comparison must retain Feres, Brooks, Shearer and Johnson at their actual scope. Brooks does not make every highway injury unrelated to service, and Feres does not define every enlisted person's journey as service-incident merely because its endpoints are military property. The absence of a specifically assigned task is relevant but not universally dispositive. The whole service relationship and the alleged wrongs remain available for legal consideration, without demanding a mandatory three-factor checklist that the governing authorities have not adopted.

If the injury is legally classified as incident to service under the applicable rule, a further requirement to prove concrete disruption of discipline in this particular suit cannot simply be assumed. If the injury is outside that boundary, the government still may invoke any actually available, supported independent statutory defense. The distinct pleaded operation, maintenance and personnel/assignment allegations remain material to any claim-specific legal analysis. The new route account does not establish a binding directive, protected policy choice, military command at the moment of injury, negligent driving or tort liability.

## Stanley's exact contribution and limit

[United States v. Stanley, 483 U.S. 669, 682–684 (1987)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/483/669), is a pre-divergence primary authority. The complete page was mechanically preserved as [prior-authority text](OT_1999CHUNK2_RICHARDS_ROUTE_STANLEY_PRIOR_AUTHORITY.txt). The relevant Court discussion, its posture, opinion joins, footnote 5 and O'Connor's separate position were inspected; no claim is made to have independently analyzed every unrelated passage of the complete saved text.

Stanley's Part I unanimously held that the interlocutory appeal did not authorize reinstatement of the separately dismissed FTCA claim. Part II concerned an implied constitutional damages remedy against individuals. The Court treated the earlier service-incidence determination as settled in that case and did not reopen it. It rejected the narrower proposition that absence of an officer–subordinate relationship or proof of particular disruption of military discipline necessarily permits such a damages action. At pages 682–683, it reasoned that a suit-by-suit inquiry into actual military disruption could itself require intrusive military testimony, whereas a service-incident boundary entails less inquiry. Part II adopted that boundary for the Bivens claim.

Thus Stanley supports distinguishing the threshold classification of an injury as incident to service from a demand for an additional individualized showing of discipline disruption. It is not an FTCA merits adjudication of an ordinary road accident, a rule that on-base residence conclusively establishes service incidence, or a factual finding about Richards. Its precluded service-incidence issue particularly limits using it to bypass the live classification issue here. It also does not merge Feres with §2680(a)'s protected-policy inquiry.

Scalia wrote Stanley; Rehnquist, White, Blackmun and Powell joined throughout. Brennan, Marshall, Stevens and O'Connor joined Part I only. Footnote 5 expressly distinguished Scalia's opposition to a judicially added FTCA exception in Johnson from the different question whether to imply a constitutional damages remedy. His Stanley authorship therefore may not be redescribed as withdrawal of his Johnson statutory objection. O'Connor's separate writing accepted the general service-incident limit but would permit relief for the alleged nonconsensual human experimentation, which she regarded as outside the military mission as a matter of law; it creates no controlling ordinary-negligence exception. Stevens joined Part III of Brennan's separate writing, not the whole writing. No position is attributed to an incumbent Justice who was not then on the Court.

## Permissible disposition reasoning without factual selection

A legal comparison may expressly take the government's new route and residence account at its strongest solely for purposes of analysis. That is an arguendo assumption, not a factual finding, concession, reconstruction premise or entry in the Court's procedural history. The deciding stage may conclude that the legal result follows on the already established facts or remains the same even if the added assertions are credited; it must state the operative ground. It may also conclude that the additional propositions would be decisive under its proposed rule, in which case their factual status must be resolved through targeted source support or an appropriately bounded remand rather than silently promoted to established facts.

The precise additional support, if indispensable, is the cited appellate appendix at pages 79–80 and 83–85 or another authenticated source establishing the propositions and the parties' treatment of them. Source unavailability itself supplies no adverse inference, waiver or need for an in-world production order. The original assumed homeward destination remains the basis of dismissal review unless a lawful procedure changes it.
