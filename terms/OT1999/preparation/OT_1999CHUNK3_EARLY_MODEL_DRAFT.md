## Baral v. United States - No. 98-1667 - February 22, 2000

**Commitment:** All eight Associates affirm the D.C. Circuit judgment denying the requested credit. Each joins B1-B3 in full.

**Components and sufficient ground.** B1: For sections 6511 and 6512, income tax actually withheld under chapter 24 and allowable as a section 31 credit is deemed paid under section 6513(b)(1) on the fifteenth day of the fourth month after the taxable year. B2: Estimated income tax is deemed paid under section 6513(b)(2) on the unextended last day for filing that year's return. Assessment and a delinquent return are not prerequisites to either specified payment date. B3: A timely section 6511(a) claim cannot recover more than tax paid within section 6511(b)(2)(A)'s independent three-years-plus-extension window. The $4,104 withholding and $1,100 estimated payment are deemed paid April 15, 1989, before the February 1, 1990 start of the window preceding the June 1, 1993 claim. The four-month extension enlarges that window without moving the estimated-payment date. The $1,175 requested credit therefore fails on amount, not filing timeliness.

**Alternative tested.** Baral's strongest path treats an unassessed remittance as a deposit until return or assessment, invoking Rosenman's suspense-account estate-tax arrangement. That distinction remains available for other remittances, as Brockamp preserved. It cannot override the specific statutory treatment of these two classes. Section 6151 directs payment without assessment or notice and demand, and section 6315 treats estimates as payments on account of income tax. Characterizing withholding and estimates as separate taxes later converted to income tax does not displace sections 31, 6315 and 6513. No universal rule for all deposits follows.

## Hunt-Wesson, Inc. v. Franchise Tax Board - No. 98-2043 - February 22, 2000

**Commitment:** All eight Associates reverse the California appellate judgment and remand. Each joins H1-H3 with the stated limits integral to the ground.

**Components and sufficient ground.** H1: A State cannot circumvent the constitutional exclusion of these admittedly nonunitary receipts by enlarging the taxable unitary-business base through a deduction adjustment measured by the receipts without a reasonable relationship to expense attributable to them. Reasonable supported allocation of borrowing costs remains permissible; transaction-by-transaction tracing is not constitutionally mandatory, and expenses actually attributable to exempt activity need not be deducted from taxable income. H2: Section 24344's challenged receipts-based offset fails that relationship. It first allows interest expense against formula-allocated interest income, then allows the remaining expense only above nonformula interest and dividends, except section 24402 dividends declared from income already included in the specified California tax measure. Remaining expense offsets those nonformula receipts. Its dollar-for-dollar use of receipts can enlarge taxable income without a corresponding change in expense attributable to them. Fungibility warrants allocation in principle but does not establish this formula's relationship. H3: This defect persists under both the forms-based unitary-interest construction and California's all-interest construction; choosing between them is unnecessary.

**Alternative tested.** California's strongest ground is allocation of fungible financing costs and prevention of arbitrage. A valid allocation need not be perfect, and deduction design is ordinarily legislative. But Allied-Signal separates a lawful base from allocation. A deduction label cannot justify an indirect inclusion of excluded receipts. The actual formula's measure, rather than minor imprecision or failure to trace each dollar, supplies the defect. The taxpayer's clear-and-cogent burden survives; no universal State-borne tracing duty is imposed.

## Weisgram v. Marley Co. - No. 99-161 - February 22, 2000

**Commitment:** All eight Associates affirm the Eighth Circuit on the granted appellate-power question. All join W1-W3; Stevens's scope reservation below is preserved within those propositions.

**Components and sufficient ground.** W1: Following a properly preserved Rule 50 motion, an appellate court may direct judgment when the legally admissible record cannot sustain the verdict, including where insufficiency follows correction of erroneous admission. Neither Rule 50 nor section 2106 requires automatic retrial because the winner relied on admitted evidence. W2: The court must attend to a supported new-trial ground and the litigant's opportunity to present it. Rule 50(d) permits the appellee to assert such grounds and permits a new trial or a trial-court determination; it does not require retrial in every exclusion case. W3: The preserved Rule 50(a)/(b) requests, repeated reliability objections and reported rehearing grounds support affirmance on this bounded question. Those rehearing grounds specify no new evidentiary proffer or independently stated new-trial reason. A general possibility of stronger replacement proof is insufficient.

**Alternative tested.** Reliance on a favorable admission ruling can matter, and the trial judge may better assess an unexpected legal change, blocked proffer or other unfairness. Those concerns justify W2 and appropriate remand where supported; they do not create a right to replace failed proof after ordinary appellate correction. Neely recognizes appellate Rule 50 authority with opportunities to raise new-trial grounds; that reasoning extends to the admissible-evidence deficiency. Daubert, Joiner and Kumho govern original-Rule-702 screening, review and its separate procedural consequences. Kumho supports no automatic second opportunity but does not itself decide this Rule 50(d) issue.

## Rice v. Cayetano - No. 98-818 - February 23, 2000

**Commitment:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer reverse the Ninth Circuit and remand under the Fifteenth Amendment. Stevens and Ginsburg affirm on the political/trust relationship ground. The six reversal commitments share R1-R3. Souter and Breyer refuse broader propositions identified below; those propositions are not included in R1-R3.

**Components of the common reversal ground.** R1: Hawaii's pre-1778 descent requirement for voting in the election of OHA's public trustees is, in this arrangement, a racial qualification. Ancestry terminology and lack of hostility do not remove it from the Fifteenth Amendment. R2: Mancari's political classification tied to federally recognized tribal membership and Indian self-government does not validate this state-created ancestry electorate. Even assuming substantial federal authority and obligations toward native Hawaiians, this particular statutory electorate and public election do not become an internal tribal membership election. R3: Neither limited governmental purpose nor beneficiary selection of fiduciaries creates an exception permitting a racial qualification in this state election. The Fifteenth Amendment resolves the franchise challenge without deciding the Fourteenth Amendment claim, underlying programs or trusts, federal recognition power, benefit eligibility, trustee candidate qualifications or tribal internal elections.

**Competing lawful path.** The affirmance path views the electorate as beneficiary participation in administering a federally rooted indigenous trust, rather than allocating ordinary political power by race. Mancari's governing-agency rationale and the asserted special governmental relationship support that analogy; the Admission Act's native-Hawaiian purpose is an actual federal enactment. Additional OHA funds and duties serve the broader Hawaiian group, so that electorate is not simply unrelated to every administered interest. The strongest objection is that OHA remains a public state agency, section 10-2 makes descent the electoral condition, the Admission Act trust has five purposes and no supplied source establishes this electorate as membership in a federally recognized political community. The reversal group gives those distinctions dispositive force without denying lawful indigenous programs. The affirmance group gives the beneficiary political relationship dispositive force without claiming benevolence excuses an otherwise racial voting restriction.

**Component boundaries.** Classification, the Mancari/federal-relationship analogy, limited-purpose voting and remedy are tested independently. Shaw/Miller preserve actual exclusion as a personal injury without an additional electoral consequence. Adarand's common standard and Hopwood's fitted uses of race do not replace the Fifteenth Amendment's prohibition; neither answers political classification by itself. A decision on the broader Hawaiian electorate does not silently decide the narrower one-half-blood native-Hawaiian benefit definition. The separate sovereignty-election litigation is not before the Court. Limited-purpose voting is a separate analogy, not an assumed exception to the Fifteenth Amendment.

## Roe v. Flores-Ortega - No. 98-1441 - February 23, 2000

**Commitment:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer vacate the Ninth Circuit judgment and remand for contextual performance and causal-loss review. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg affirm the conditional appellate-restoration judgment on the informed-choice/assistance-function ground below. Every Associate rejects probable appellate success as a prerequisite to relief for an actually lost appeal. This is a five-to-three Associate split, not a full Court tally.

**Threshold and record.** The July 27, 1995 habeas application is pre-AEDPA under Lindh; amended sections 2254(d) and (e)(1) do not govern. Weeks's post-AEDPA gate is not imported. The federal hearing addressed a promised appeal and found no promise proved by a preponderance. It also found no consent to nonfiling, little or no understanding of appeal, and a conversation. These findings remain distinct. An unproved promise is not adequate advice; an unspecified conversation is not no conversation; a judicial warning is not necessarily understanding; a guilty plea is not a complete appeal waiver. Full consultation content, preserved appellate prospects and an agreed precise sentence are not established. The March 24 late notice is evidence in context, not an automatic finding of the timely decision.

**F1 - shared by all eight.** The defendant decides whether to take an available first appeal. Counsel must act on an instruction to appeal and respect an informed express direction not to appeal. Jones v. Barnes distinguishes that choice from counsel's selection of appellate issues. A lost appeal caused by deficient assistance is not harmless merely because the prisoner cannot establish likely appellate success. Rodriquez, Peguero and current Smith v. Robbins preserve that distinction. No present finding of an express instruction is added beyond the limited promise finding.

**F2 - contextual performance ground of the five remand Associates.** Ordinary Strickland reasonableness governs a case without clear instructions. Determine first whether counsel consulted by explaining appeal's advantages and disadvantages and reasonably seeking the client's wishes. After adequate consultation counsel ordinarily follows the client's decision; silence alone does not require a protective appeal. If consultation did not occur, it is constitutionally required when counsel had reason to think a rational defendant would want to appeal, including a potentially nonfrivolous issue, or this defendant reasonably indicated interest in appeal. This applies professional reasonableness, not an exhaustive checklist or merits prerequisite. A plea, its benefits, retained rights and whether the sentence matched a bargain matter only in their proved scope; no blanket waiver or agreed sentence is assumed here. Sufficiently informative judicial advice and demonstrated understanding can bear on whether additional advice would be redundant, but the warning's occurrence alone does not decide this record. Consultation will ordinarily be required in many cases; no categorical absence-of-request safe harbor exists.

**F3 - causal loss ground of the five.** If nonconsultation was deficient, the prisoner must show a reasonable probability that adequate consultation would have led him to take a timely appeal. This concerns loss of the proceeding, not its probable merits. Potential issues or contemporaneous efforts can support the showing, but neither an identified nonfrivolous ground nor a technically informed timely request is invariably required. A prisoner unfamiliar with appeal cannot be required to identify the arguments missing assistance would have disclosed. Plea terms and the late notice have evidentiary weight without being conclusive. Established causal loss warrants restoration without merits prejudice; deficiency alone does not.

**F4 - remand and Teague.** The no-consent rule below does not perform F2 and F3. Vacate and remand for application to actual findings, with further factfinding only as ordinary procedure and unresolved material issues require. The promise hearing did not resolve every consultation or causation issue. No unconditional denial, restored appeal or mandatory new hearing follows. Application of existing Strickland to individual performance and causal deprivation does not become a new rule merely through clarification. The District Court's Teague treatment of the different categorical rule cannot independently dispose of the unexamined individual claim. The five need not approve that categorical rule or invoke a Teague exception; materially distinct new theories retain their own nonretroactivity inquiry.

**Competing informed-choice/function path.** Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg require meaningful advice sufficient to permit an informed appeal decision before counsel allows the period to expire, unless the defendant already knowingly makes that choice. An uninformed defendant need not first request advice or demonstrate appeal sophistication. They regard no consent, little or no understanding, the lost appeal and the later effort to obtain it as sufficient to establish the missing assistance function and justify conditional restoration. They do not find no conversation, invent its words or find appellate merit. Their objection to F2 is that it permits counsel to close a personal option without enabling its exercise. Their practical objection to F3 here is requiring proof of the choice adequate advice would have produced when the indispensable advice is missing. Proven informed nonappeal remains different, as in Peguero.

**Strongest contrary ground.** Strickland rejects mechanical performance rules, and actual circumstances may show informed inaction without affirmative consent. Smith concerns a missing screening function within an existing appeal and retains ordinary Strickland for deficient individual performance. The five accept Smith as law but do not treat it as eliminating whether this defendant lost an appeal because of counsel. The three answer that counsel-assisted exercise of the defendant's reserved choice is itself a necessary function before filing. Their Teague position rests on prefinality Jones, Strickland, Cronic and Rodriquez, not January 2000 Smith as newly supplied prefinality law.

## Rotella v. Wood - No. 98-896 - February 23, 2000

**Commitment:** All eight Associates affirm the Fifth Circuit on the conceded-complete claim. Each joins T1-T3; no universal injury-occurrence/injury-discovery choice is included.

**Components and sufficient ground.** T1: Where a civil RICO claim is complete and the plaintiff has discovered the injury, accrual is not postponed until actual or reasonable discovery that the conduct forms a racketeering pattern. The four-year period protects timely investigation and suit; the pattern element does not require discovery of every legal element before accrual. T2: Rotella concedes injury knowledge by 1986 and does not dispute that all elements were complete then. His 1997 suit cannot be timely simply because the 1994 guilty pleas disclosed the alleged pattern. Rejecting injury-and-pattern discovery does not require choosing occurrence versus injury discovery where they would differ. T3: Klehr's distinct routes retain their conditions: a later wrongful act directly causing genuinely new injury may support timely recovery for that injury; damages genuinely too speculative or incapable of proof earlier may accrue when they occur; concealment tolling requires prevention of timely discovery despite reasonable diligence. No finding establishing or defeating these routes is inferred from research silence. No tolling award or new-injury disposition follows from preserving them.

**Alternatives tested.** The strongest pattern-discovery argument is that RICO needs a pattern and limitations should not run before an actionable claim can be identified. A claim with an uncompleted pattern is not this conceded-complete claim and remains undecided. Delaying a completed injury claim until recognition of the pattern can indefinitely extend the borrowed four-year period and undermine Agency Holding's Clayton Act analogy. Diligence-conditioned concealment relief remains distinct. Conversely, the amicus's occurrence rule could provide a broader limit, but the parties' presentation and concession make that selection unnecessary. Klehr expressly left the current trigger open; T1 answers it rather than claiming it already settled. The ten-year predicate interval is not a civil filing period.
