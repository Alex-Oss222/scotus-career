# Nixon v. Shrink - objective factual supplement

Source: official U.S. Reports 528 U.S.377, pp.382-384, 393-396 and 426-427, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-528/pdf/USREPORTS-528-377.pdf. This paraphrase uses the control-extracted factual recitation; no target Supreme Court evaluation, result, author or join is included. Original appendix and party briefs are not represented as independently read.

The challenged statewide/state-auditor contribution ceiling was $1,075, indexed from the statutory $1,000 baseline by the cumulative consumer price index since January 1,1995 and rounded to the nearest $25 each even-numbered year. Other office ceilings existed but are not separately challenged by these litigants. The PAC gave Fredman's candidate committee $1,025 in 1997 and another $50 in 1998 and stated it wished to give more. Fredman alleged that he required larger contributions for an effective campaign. Pages382-384 establish these statutory and litigant facts.

The State's record included Senator Goode's affidavit concerning the potential for large contributions to influence official votes; newspaper accounts concerning a $20,000 bank contribution and the treasurer's banking selection; and $40,000 brewery and $20,000 bank gifts in an auditor race. A related appellate record identified a $420,000 PAC contribution connected with an investment bank and reported scandals, including a legislator accused of exchanging sponsorship for kickbacks and the former attorney general's conspiracy plea concerning misuse of state property. The accounts vary in evidentiary status: an accusation or inference is not a conviction, and the plea establishes only its actual offense. Pages393-395 supply the recitation.

The district court found that state candidates remained able to raise enough for effective campaigns and sizeable campaign funds. Proposition A received 74 percent support. In the 1994 state-auditor races, 97.62 percent of contributors gave no more than $2,000. That last statistic counts contributors, not dollars, and says nothing by itself about the proportion of funding from large donors. Pages394-396 supply these facts and findings.

The contrary record comparison reported spending in primary and general elections for five statewide offices falling from $21,599,000 to $9,337,000; primary spending falling 89 percent from $14,249,000 to $1,625,000; contested statewide primaries falling from 10 of 10 to 1 of 10; and primary candidates falling from 32 to 11. The cited support is Appendix 24-28 as publicly recited at pages426-427. These comparisons establish a reported association over time, not an adjudicated causal finding that the limits produced each change. The office-specific auditor access claim remains the presented application; other-office figures can be considered as evidence without deciding other unpresented applications.

The existing brief's unqualified statement that the record did not show campaign inability must be read alongside both the district findings and this contrary evidence. Russell supplies the governing actual-fit and meaningful-association burden, no permanent dollar floor and no automatic validity from a higher dollar ceiling. Fresh neutral validation must assess the full evidentiary presentation before independent modeling. No merits recommendation is made here.
