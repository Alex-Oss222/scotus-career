The neutral source-map statement that O'Connor joined the majority is erroneous. Marshall's majority was joined by Brennan, White, Blackmun and Powell. Stevens dissented separately, joined by Scalia. Scalia's separate dissent was joined by Rehnquist and O'Connor.

Stevens's dissent, beginning at 230, concerns constitutional equivalence of federal and state legislative voter qualifications. It is not a join in Scalia's separate associational analysis.

Scalia's dissent, beginning at 234, favors contextual accommodation of association and state election administration. It treats members' selection of their own candidate as protected, but finds little associational interest in outsourcing selection to unwilling nonmembers. It permits Connecticut to require a democratic vote by party members, even if party leadership prefers outsiders' participation. Those grounds do not automatically answer compulsory outsider selection.

The majority's footnotes 6 and 13 distinguish invited independents from unwanted participants and reserve different considerations for invitation of other parties' members. Neither writing decides the present blanket-primary question.
