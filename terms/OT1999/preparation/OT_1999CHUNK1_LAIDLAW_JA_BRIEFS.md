## Material confirmations and additions

1. **The thirteen/fourteen mercury-count difference is resolved.** The court's discharge table, JA 195, lists violation 476 on **June 12, 1992**, the complaint date, and violations 477–489 after that date. Thus there are **fourteen on or after June 12 and thirteen strictly after June 12**. The inclusive dates are June 12, 15, 16, 17, 18; July 8 and 16; August 7 and 10; October 5 and 8, 1992; November 3, 1993; November 26, 1994; and January 30, 1995. This agrees with JA 178's eleven mercury excursions between June 12 and October 1992, followed by one annually in 1993–1995. JA 158's nine after June 1992 uses the end of June, not the complaint date. These are counts from the district court's table, not independently reconstructed discharge measurements. PDF visual verification preserved in `laidlaw-ja-views/ja-195.png`.

2. **A different count discrepancy remains in the district court's own text.** JA 159 finds 420 monitoring and 503 reporting violations overall, with overlap; footnote 14 identifies 305 failures to report discharge violations properly. That page states there were thirteen monitoring and ten reporting violations since the complaint was filed in June 1992. JA 178 later states that there was only one alleged monitoring violation and zero reporting violations since June 1992. Both printed pages were visually checked. The appendix does not supply the referenced Revised Exhibits 77–79 and 97 or the full 1995 trial transcript, pp. 181–82 and 187, needed to reconcile this discrepancy. Preserve the specific statements and their sources; do not silently substitute one set of counts. PDF checks remain in `laidlaw-ja-views/ja-159.png` and `laidlaw-ja-views/ja-178.png`.

3. **Individual standing evidence is not reproduced in this appendix.** The full appendix contains neither the Curtis nor Moore deposition, nor the individual member affidavits. The amended complaint alleges members' residence, property ownership, recreation and associated health, economic, recreational, aesthetic and environmental interests (JA 30–36); it separately alleges informational injury from monitoring/reporting failures. These are pleading allegations. Laidlaw's answer denies the adverse effects and pleads lack of standing (JA 44–50). The December 14, 1992 preliminary-injunction order says the court reviewed nearby residents' affidavits but found them insufficient to establish irreparable injury; it also discusses Laidlaw's contrary environmental evidence (JA 61–62). That is an injunction determination, not an express adjudication there that members suffered no Article III injury. The June 30, 1993 docket entries record denial of Laidlaw's summary-judgment motion as to FOE (JA 6); the docket later identifies the transcript of that hearing (JA 25), but its contents are not reproduced. The merits briefs' recitations of Curtis, Moore and other members therefore remain party descriptions of omitted record material unless independently supplied. Omission from this published appendix does not establish absence from the district-court record.

4. **Environmental findings have a defined evidentiary basis and scope.** JA 156–57 finds the river's mercury water-quality standard attained, overall quality sufficient for fish, wildlife and recreation, and no showing of significant environmental harm, based on toxicity tests, fish-tissue studies and testimony. JA 163–64 rejects plaintiffs' expert extrapolation from the unrelated Langley Pond in light of DHEC's North Tyger bass study and finds no demonstrated adverse environmental effect from the effluent, including exceedances. The court made its own findings rather than treating the state administrative decision as binding (JA 150). These are findings about demonstrated environmental effects; they should not be rewritten as a finding resolving every claimed member-use injury. The appendix also reports an earlier May 1988 fish kill attributed to a pH upset, with a $20,000 administrative penalty and fish replacement (JA 88). That event must not be conflated with the mercury violations remaining in this suit: non-mercury discharge claims were removed as not ongoing at filing (JA 139–40, 198–99).

5. **Compliance findings do not erase post-filing violations.** The final findings identify 489 undisputed mercury exceedances (JA 158), and the detailed table continues through January 30, 1995 (JA 195). The court nevertheless found substantial compliance with all permit parameters since at least August 1992; the 1994 and 1995 mercury exceedances were 2.7 and 1.6 ppb and occurred with no mercury being fed into the incinerator (JA 183). The new mercury limits—10 ppb daily maximum and 1.25 ppb monthly average—became effective June 1, 1996, subject to the pending state challenge (JA 148–53, 178, 182–83). The court denied equitable relief because of extended compliance and lack of demonstrated environmental harm (JA 182–84). The newer permit did not retrospectively excuse liability under the older limit (JA 151–53).

6. **The penalty and deterrence findings are distinct from fees or closure.** The district court found $1,092,581 in net economic benefit (JA 165, 197), after credits including the earlier state payment's present value (JA 171, 197). It assessed $405,800: $313,500 for mercury exceedances, $42,000 for monitoring and $50,300 for reporting (JA 181). The court expressly considered the combined deterrent effect of that penalty, anticipated reimbursement of significant plaintiffs' legal fees, and Laidlaw's own direct and indirect litigation expenses (JA 181–82). This is the court's explanation of the selected amount, not a quantified fee award. The request for fees appears in the pleadings (JA 28, 39), but no fee amount is awarded by the printed final judgment (JA 198–99).

7. **State enforcement details are established, but collusion is not.** DHEC's complaint was filed June 9, 1992 (JA 72–75); the consent decree was entered June 10 (JA 83, 94), and the federal complaint followed June 12 (JA 1, 84). The decree imposed $100,000; required the fish-tissue study, specified reporting and analytical steps, and efforts to comply; and contained provisions addressing liability and expiration (JA 76–83). The district court found Laidlaw requested the judicial route to bar the anticipated citizen suit, prepared the complaint/decree, paid the filing fee and delivered the papers; it found the settlement unusually rapid (JA 93–95). It held DHEC's action insufficiently diligent after considering procedure, relief and the failure to calculate or recover economic benefit (JA 117–36). **JA 117 n.14 expressly declined to find fraud or collusion.** DHEC's August 6, 1993 notification of compliance and the state court's August 17, 1993 final order ended the state proceeding (JA 147). These dates do not establish closure of the plant.

8. **The later findings modify earlier technical findings.** The January 1997 order moves the required Lancy installation date from January 1, 1987 to January 1, 1988 (JA 166 n.15) and modifies the 1995 finding that ion exchange and microfiltration were necessary for compliance; later evidence showed they were unnecessary and unused regularly (JA 166–67 n.16). The court found reasonable selection and implementation of Lancy, tempered by delayed installation (JA 153–56). A neutral account should not take the superseded April 1995 equipment findings as the court's final factual position.

9. **Judgment dates are not inconsistent.** The findings/order is signed **January 21, 1997** (JA 184). The separate judgment identifies that order as filed January 22 and itself is dated **January 22, 1997** (JA 198–99); the docket records both findings and judgment on January 22 (JA 23–24). The separate judgment also enters the previously granted partial summary judgment on non-mercury discharges. Neither these documents nor the complete appendix supplies evidence of the later facility closure, permit surrender or fee proceedings. Those matters require the separately supplied original materials reviewed by the other original-record reader; they cannot be inferred from the district docket's routine case-closed entry.
