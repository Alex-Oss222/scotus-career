- The caption identifies Stanley T. Torres and others as appellants and Miguel M. Sablan and others as appellees, on direct appeal from the District Court for the Northern Mariana Islands.
- Under its jurisdiction heading, the United States reports that the district-court judgment and notice of appeal were both entered/filed **May 5, 1999**; the jurisdictional statement was filed **July 6, 1999**; and appellants invoked **28 U.S.C. § 1253**.
- In its account of the proceeding, the United States expressly describes the District Court as having sat as a **three-judge panel under 28 U.S.C. § 2284** when it granted defendants' summary judgment, citing Jurisdictional Statement Appendix page 17a. This directly supports the existence of a panel. It does not name the three judges or reproduce the convening order. A single author identified on the published opinion does not by itself contradict this representation.
- Footnote 2 states the United States' legal position that § 1253 supplies appellate jurisdiction because § 2284 applies to this autonomous territorial commonwealth's apportionment challenge. It relies on **Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht Leasing Co., 416 U.S. 663, 669–676 (1974)** for treating territorial-commonwealth statutes like state statutes for the predecessor three-judge-court jurisdictional provision, and **48 U.S.C. §§ 1821(c), 1824** for Title 28's applicability to the Northern Mariana Islands District Court. The United States supports jurisdiction; it does not request dismissal for want of jurisdiction.
- The same footnote cites a separate jurisdictional ruling in **J.S. App. 18a–24a** rejecting the contention that Covenant § 902 provides an exclusive political remedy. This is evidence of the existence and reported disposition of that separate lower-court jurisdictional challenge. It is not a recovered copy of that ruling.
- The United States identifies itself as having intervened under **28 U.S.C. § 2403(a)** to defend the constitutionality of Public Law 94-241. Its brief describes the plaintiffs as two Saipan residents seeking declaratory and injunctive relief from vote dilution.
