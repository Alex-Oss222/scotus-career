# Hill — narrow entering-law refresh for City of Erie v. Pap's A.M.

**Scope and cutoff:** This supplement adds the completed March 29, 2000 public law and published Justice positions in City of Erie v. Pap's A.M., No. 98-1161, to the neutral Hill packet for June 28, 2000. It revises no fact, statutory term, question, source limit or judgment below in that original packet. The appended complete Erie Public Projection is copied verbatim from the authorized preparation artifact. This is public earlier current-term law, not the historical Supreme Court decision or a private current position in Hill.

**Materiality:** Erie materially supplements the classification and justification inquiry already identified in Hill and supplies recent, differentiated positions for every Associate. It does not displace Ward's legislative rule or transplant the heightened Madsen/Schenck injunction rule into this statute. Its actual five-Justice merits proposition concerns a public-nudity ordinance whose justification and operation principally target the communicative adult-entertainment setting; it rejects incidental-burden treatment and finds Erie's resulting scrutiny unsatisfied while preserving genuinely general conduct rules and supported regulation of nonexpressive harms. That proposition bears on how a court distinguishes genuine control of noncommunicative harms from suppression of expression. It does not decide whether Colorado's specified approach rule has that character or invalidate every statute using communicative-purpose terms.

**Authority limits:** Stevens's full Part II explanation has four joins; the common precise merits proposition has five direct supporters across Part II and the Chief Justice's separate concurrence, without a fifth full join or Marks synthesis. Part III's independent intermediate-scrutiny insufficiency ground has only three votes. Souter's separate conclusion of insufficiency and different remand cannot be added to Part III as another vote for its final disposition. Erie creates no controlling universal local-study requirement, mandatory hearing, strict least-restrictive-means rule, or automatic invalidity of general nudity regulation. Its mootness holding concerns the particular continuing state judgment and adversity after a business closure; no analogous closure or new jurisdictional fact is supplied in Hill.

**Current public positions relevant to fresh modeling:**

- Stevens and Ginsburg joined the fuller finding of actual communicative selection and the independently insufficient advancement/fit ground. Their conclusions rested on Erie's record, including attributed governmental interpretations; they do not supply a Hill result.
- Scalia joined the fuller content-selection ground but not the independent intermediate-scrutiny alternative. Thomas joined both. Their threshold dissent did not remove their conditional merits participation. Their distinct grounds must not be flattened into a shared alternative holding.
- O'Connor found the particular adult-establishment application justified under O'Brien based on the general conduct scope, identified noncommunicative harms, relevant outside inference and limited retained burden. Her approach still required affirmative justification and actual scrutiny; benign purpose or lack of contrary proof did not suffice alone.
- Kennedy and Breyer favored a nonvalidating remand for the actual classification and, if appropriate, advancement and fit. They reserved present evidentiary sufficiency and did not choose final invalidation or validation. They rejected both a conclusive general-text defense and a conclusive inference from a single nonenforcement episode.
- Souter joined that common classification inquiry and nonvalidating remand, but separately found the existing demonstration insufficient even assuming O'Brien. His proposed opportunity to justify remained conditional on unresolved state-law or overbreadth routes; he prescribed neither an unconditional study nor a hearing.

**Neutral application:** Hill's model must compare the statute's actual operation and asserted access/treatment interests with its burdens on peaceful initiation, leafleting and conversation, retaining the binding no-retreat construction and the difference between a generally applicable law and an injunction. Erie strengthens the need to identify the actual noncommunicative harm, the asserted means and any contrary evidence, without deciding that comparison. Both the supported neutral-regulation route and the supported communicative-selection objection remain to be assessed independently. Erie does not eliminate the original packet's separate vagueness, overbreadth, preservation and remedy questions or turn a source limit into litigant default.
