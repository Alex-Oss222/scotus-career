# Weeks v. Angelone — neutral completion law supplement

**Scope:** January 19, 2000 original merits event. This is a neutral source-and-law supplement, not a commitment or disposition. It accompanies the completion source extraction, the focused law slice, the new narrow threshold slice, and the date-bounded current-term neutral state. It contains no matter-specific Supreme Court outcome or private material.

## Earlier constitutional authorities and their limits

[Eddings v. Oklahoma, 455 U.S. 104 (1982)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/455/104), applying Lockett, prohibits excluding relevant mitigation from consideration as a matter of law. The sentencer may determine its weight. Powell's opinion commanded Brennan, Marshall, Stevens and O'Connor's joins; O'Connor's concurrence refused to assume that admitted mitigation had actually received lawful consideration when the sentencing judge's remarks left a legitimate ambiguity. That concurrence explained her own ground; it does not independently replace Boyde's later governing instruction standard. The decision altered the sentence and preserved guilt. The [saved primary text](OT_1999CHUNK2_WEEKS_COMPLETION_EDDINGS_PRIOR_AUTHORITY.txt) retains the separate writings and notes.

[Boyde v. California, 494 U.S. 370 (1990)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/494/370) supplies the reasonable-likelihood inquiry for an ambiguous instruction allegedly preventing consideration of constitutionally relevant mitigation. It requires more than a possible restrictive reading but not proof that prohibited operation was more likely than not. The charge and trial context are examined together. Admission of mitigation alone does not guarantee lawful consideration. Counsel's arguments are contextual evidence, ordinarily less authoritative than judicial instructions; no universal cure follows from counsel's correct explanation. Boyde also rejects a requirement of unfettered sentencing discretion once all relevant mitigation can receive consideration. Its actual no-error application does not decide the effect of a later explicit jury inquiry in another record.

Rehnquist wrote Boyde for White, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy. Stevens joined Parts I–IV of Marshall's dissent, along with Blackmun; Brennan joined it throughout. That dissent challenged the standard and found the instructions insufficient even under the majority's standard, criticizing reliance on admitted evidence and advocacy where the judicial language could prevent consideration. Stevens did not join Part V's categorical opposition to capital punishment. The [saved primary text](OT_1999CHUNK2_WEEKS_COMPLETION_BOYDE_PRIOR_AUTHORITY.txt) preserves the exact opinion topology and complete separate writing for any Justice-specific use. Boyde's majority standard, as retained in the supplied simulated holdings, controls.

[Bollenbach v. United States, 326 U.S. 607 (1946)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/326/607) requires care and accurate guidance when a jury identifies difficulty on a vital issue. Its actual case involved federal direct review, a legally erroneous supplementary presumption, prolonged deadlock, and a verdict five minutes after the misleading answer. It rejected appellate substitution of a different liability theory and refusal to correct that record merely because other evidence supported guilt. It does not itself establish that every reference to a correct prior instruction violates the federal Constitution, or resolve amended §2254(d), a state-capital claim's application, or the collateral error-effect standard. Its correct volume is 326, not the 362 appearing in some OCR. The [saved primary text](OT_1999CHUNK2_WEEKS_COMPLETION_BOLLENBACH_PRIOR_AUTHORITY.txt) includes Frankfurter's Court opinion, Black's dissent, and Jackson's nonparticipation.

These primary constitutional authorities, and the supplied January 25, 1993 simulated Graham holding, predate the November 4, 1994 state merits adjudication. They can therefore be evaluated without pretending the January 21, 1998 Buchanan decision existed at that earlier time. Graham's effective-mitigation rule and its own prefinality analysis remain controlling at their recorded scope. Buchanan now supplies a contextual application and every incumbent Associate's published position, but its record had no equivalent later jury question. The full [Buchanan public entry](OT_1999CHUNK2_WEEKS_COMPLETION_BUCHANAN_PUBLIC.md) agrees with the focused slice and is authorized for that current-law and prior-position purpose.

## Fair presentation and default are separate inquiries

[Picard v. Connor, 404 U.S. 270 (1971)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/404/270) requires presentation of the substance of the same federal claim, not merely presentation of facts from which a different constitutional claim could be constructed. It does not demand an identical formula or constitutional citation in every case. [Anderson v. Harless, 459 U.S. 4 (1982)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/459/4) applied that requirement to a later federal burden-shifting claim that had not been fairly supplied by a state-law instruction objection. Its majority did not treat a citation to a state decision as sufficient merely because a different federal argument had appeared in that other case. Stevens dissented with Brennan and Marshall, stressing substance over labels and the state courts' actual opportunity. O'Connor joined the per curiam disposition. The [Picard text](OT_1999CHUNK2_WEEKS_COMPLETION_PICARD_PRIOR_AUTHORITY.txt) and [Harless text](OT_1999CHUNK2_WEEKS_COMPLETION_HARLESS_PRIOR_AUTHORITY.txt) preserve the complete opinions for focused use.

[Harris v. Reed, 489 U.S. 255 (1989)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/489/255) applies the adequate-and-independent-ground and plain-statement principles to an ambiguous state decision addressing a federal claim. Actual reliance on a state procedural ground matters. A clear independent alternative procedural ground does not disappear because a state court also discusses the merits. Neither Harris nor the current simulated law dispenses with fair presentation or prevents analysis of an actually unpresented claim's remaining state remedies. O'Connor's concurrence, joined by Rehnquist and Scalia, expressly preserved the latter inquiry. Stevens joined the majority and separately explained the special importance of protecting federal rights on habeas; Kennedy dissented. The [saved primary text](OT_1999CHUNK2_WEEKS_COMPLETION_HARRIS_PRIOR_AUTHORITY.txt) preserves those positions. Coleman and the supplied Lambrix/Trest holdings continue to govern actual independent and adequate default and its established gateways; Harris is not an unconditional federal-merits command.

The new [threshold slice](../entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK2_WEEKS_COMPLETION_THRESHOLDS.md) mechanically copies Gray's claim-substance and distinct-claim holdings; Trest's no-mandatory-default rule; Lambrix's nonjurisdictional sequencing and preserved-defense rules; O'Sullivan's former-law complete-round rule with its temporal limits; and enacted amended §2254(b). For this post-enactment application the statute permits denial on the merits despite nonexhaustion and forbids deemed exhaustion waiver without express counsel waiver. Permission is not a duty, and exhaustion is not interchangeable with state procedural default.

## Nonretroactivity without an invented finality event

The threshold slice also supplies Caspari's dictated-at-finality inquiry, both then-existing Teague exceptions, the scope of a preserved subsidiary defense, and Schiro's discretionary treatment of a genuinely late defense. Neither an opponent's allegation of lateness nor presentation in merits briefing alone establishes a mandatory waiver here. Lambrix supplies no inflexible priority between accepted nonretroactivity and disputed default.

[Saffle v. Parks, 494 U.S. 484 (1990)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/494/484) distinguished a demand for a particular manner of sympathetic response from the established right to consider and give effect to relevant mitigation. It treated the proposed antisympathy rule as new at Parks's 1983 finality and outside both contemporary exceptions. Kennedy wrote for Rehnquist, White, O'Connor and Scalia. Stevens joined Brennan's dissent except Part IV's categorical capital-punishment position; Marshall joined throughout and Blackmun joined the same portions as Stevens. The [saved primary text](OT_1999CHUNK2_WEEKS_COMPLETION_SAFFLE_PRIOR_AUTHORITY.txt) retains those writings. Simulated Graham expressly distinguishes unrestricted sympathy from independent culpability mitigation; Saffle cannot be used to undo that governing distinction.

The historical October 2, 1995 finality date is not established as an in-world Supreme Court event. It must not be silently imported. The complete constitutional rules relied upon above already existed before the November 4, 1994 state merits adjudication, hence before any subsequent finality. A bounded old-rule comparison using those authorities does not require selecting an exact later date. A proposed new-rule bar must state the exact rule it classifies and demonstrate why its finality premise matters or does not matter; it cannot merely label a different factual application new. If a decisive intervening authority or actual direct-review event makes a specific day material, that path needs targeted chronology support before commitment. No such additional factual premise is selected here.

## Independent statutory and remedial questions

The February 7, 1997 application postdates AEDPA. Lindh and Carriger's identified pre-enactment holdings do not remove the enacted §2254(d) restriction from this application. The state merits adjudication is the November 4, 1994 disposition, not the 1998 District Court or 1999 appellate judgment. The source packet preserves both contrary-to and unreasonable-application alternatives and the merits-adjudication trigger. Current law supplies no comprehensive Supreme Court construction of those alternatives. The Fourth Circuit's Green/Wright formulations are its reasoning; they are not an adopted Supreme Court rule. No future Williams formulation is available.

Statutory eligibility for a writ, the existence of an Eighth Amendment violation, and the effect of that violation on the sentence must remain distinct. The focused register's Chapman holdings cover the specified Doyle, personal-intent and commutation-misinformation categories. Weeks's question is not automatically within the last category simply because it concerns capital selection. A broader rule or supported analogy requires actual adjudication. Gray illustrates a result satisfying competing effect standards without announcing a universal rule; it does not establish that this record satisfies either. A no-error disposition reaches no error-effect issue. A contrary disposition must specify the governing standard, burden, required causal inquiry and whether further examination is necessary.

Any available relief addresses the penalty, preserves the conviction and unrelated expert-assistance question, and respects actual statutory and procedural conditions. A method error alone supports no automatic writ or immediate release. A valid conditional sentencing remedy or remand must say what remains for the lower court and leave an appropriate prospective period if new sentencing is required; it must not resurrect a nonexistent clock.
