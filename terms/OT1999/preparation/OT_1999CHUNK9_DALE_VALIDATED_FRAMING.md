**Facts and disputed inferences:** Dale was an Eagle Scout and became an assistant scoutmaster in March 1989. BSA revoked his adult membership after a July 1990 article identified his Rutgers Lesbian/Gay Alliance role and participation in a seminar concerning gay teenagers. The article neither identified him as a Scout leader nor said he would advance a contrary message in Scouting. His public identity, the organization's position, and the role's communicative significance remain distinct matters. BSA teaches values through activities and adult example; it invokes the Oath's morally-straight requirement, the Law's clean requirement, the adult religious declaration, officer testimony and statements from 1978, 1991, 1992 and 1993. Their dates differ: the 1991 statement preceded this suit, while some postdated the expulsion. Ordinary youth materials do not expressly discuss homosexuality, and sex education is directed to parents and spiritual advisers. Neither silence in those materials nor BSA's later assertion alone settles the constitutional characterization. The accompanying source facts preserve the evidence and each party's competing inference.

**State-law decision and exceptions:** New Jersey's own construction covers BSA despite the lack of a single fixed location, treats adult leadership as a membership privilege or advantage, and rejects the distinctly-private, religious-educational-facility and parental exceptions. The full exception analysis is mechanically copied below. The distinctly-private inquiry concerns genuine selectivity, including the argument that adult selection is more demanding than youth admission. The religious exception concerns an educational facility operated or maintained by a bona fide religious or sectarian institution; the court relied on BSA's nonsectarian statements. The parental reservation protects the right of a natural parent or one in loco parentis to direct a child's education and upbringing; the court distinguished temporary supervision from maintaining, rearing and educating a child. These state-law rulings neither supply a federal constitutional exemption nor settle whether applying the statute materially burdens expression. The court decided privilege/advantage on the merits after noting a possible waiver issue; no definitive waiver is inferred. The duplicative common-law claim remained dismissed.

**Judgment and reasoning below:** The state court rejected intimate association based on actual size, recruitment, openness and relationships; it rejected expressive association based on its assessment of BSA's communicated program, diversity, and the absence of significant impairment by Dale's inclusion. Alternatively it treated antidiscrimination in public accommodations as a compelling interest and distinguished status access from inserting a message in a parade. Those constitutional and expression characterizations receive the independent review required by Hurley, with credibility deference; they are not binding facts eliminating the federal question. Justice Handler's concurrence emphasized actual selectivity and identity versus imposed advocacy. Judge Landau's intermediate dissent distinguished restored membership from compelled leadership. BSA disputes that adult membership can be separated from leadership service. The record does not establish a final damages amount or a ready-made membership-only remedy.

**Current law and its limits:** The full simulated Hurley and Jones public law is copied below, without alteration. Hurley protects control of a private expressive presentation despite breadth and diversity, while preserving ordinary nondiscrimination applications, independent review and credibility deference. Jones holds that compulsory equal outsider participation in selecting an objecting party's official nominee severely burdens that central party choice, requires strict scrutiny, and is not justified by the asserted electoral interests. Its political-nomination holding does not declare every association a protected expressive presentation or decide every admission dispute. The legal work here is to identify the actual expressive activity, the assistant scoutmaster's role and attributable burden, and the constitutional justification and remedy appropriate to that burden. No categorical exemption follows merely from invoking association; no requirement of a single message, unanimous agreement or constant public advocacy follows from Hurley either.

**Supported paths and strongest counterarguments:**

- Affirmance could rest on a supported determination that restoring this otherwise qualified adult does not materially alter expression attributable to BSA, coupled with the State's actual access interest. Its strongest obstacle is that an adult entrusted with teaching by example may embody chosen values in a way ordinary customers do not; courts may not define a group's message for it or demand explicit denunciation of every disfavored act.
- Reversal of the compelled-leadership application could rest on a supported finding that this particular role materially carries BSA's own expression and that the State's asserted access justification cannot constitutionally compel its alteration. Its strongest obstacle is the need to establish the actual message and material burden from the record rather than treat a status exclusion, or an organization's conclusory assertion, as self-proving. The organization's ordinary program, sex-education instructions, diversity and Dale's proposed conduct must be addressed.
- A bounded remand is available only if a genuinely material unresolved factual or remedial issue prevents lawful final application. It must identify that issue, the existing judgment's treatment, and what further proceedings can lawfully resolve. Membership-only relief requires support for its feasibility and treatment of BSA's contrary role evidence. A source gap does not itself require remand or establish that the court's file lacks evidence.
