# Norfolk Southern v. Shanklin — form and propositions before assignment

Fixed on the legal grounds before consulting any authorship count: one signed opinion of the Court on the actual device-selection preemption question and its bounded remedy, a compatible Breyer concurrence, and a dissent explaining the opposing adequacy-predicate construction. No splitting, combination with another case, or per curiam form is warranted.

The Court applies Easterwood's substantial-subsumption test to an approved, federally participating, installed and operating warning project; the device-choice regulations displace the independent state demand for different or additional devices without an extra individualized-study requirement. The gate conditions, diagnostic-team permission, (b)(4) FHWA approval requirement, distinct (b)(2) route and cumulative local-hazard exception remain intact. Coverage and compliance remain distinct; the dangerous-conditions and missing-pavement-marking arguments establish no new factual finding or preserved federal cause of action. The railroad receives judgment only on the independent adequacy claim; the remand determines the effect on the four-theory unallocated verdict and preserves the other claims' actual procedural footing and defenses. Breyer's concurrence explains why these particular regulations displace this duty without adopting a universal minimum-standard rule. Stevens and Ginsburg would affirm because funding and completion do not demonstrate the necessary substantive federal choice.
