## Wardlow

- Officer Nolan testified that he was in uniform but could not recall whether his car was marked or unmarked. The identified transcript citation is Joint Appendix page 4.
- The reported testimony did not state whether the other three cars were marked, whether the other seven officers were uniformed, whether anyone besides Wardlow was at 4035 West Van Buren, how fast the caravan was moving, whether Wardlow had noticed the other cars, or whether the caravan had already passed him. Nolan described what was normally expected in the areas to which the officers were headed; that is distinct from testimony that customers or lookouts were present beside Wardlow on this occasion.
- The report attributes to Nolan the observation that Wardlow looked in the officers' direction and ran (J.A. 9), and that he was looking at them (J.A. 5). Whether Wardlow actually recognized them as police is an inference to evaluate, not a separately proved admission by Wardlow.
- The Illinois Supreme Court took notice of the arrest report's recorded time shortly after noon; the suppression testimony itself did not state the time. See 183 Ill. 2d 306, 308 n.1, 701 N.E.2d 484, 485 n.1.
- The state-court record account says the officers were not responding to a call or report of suspicious activity in that area. See 183 Ill. 2d at 315, 701 N.E.2d at 488.
- These limits neither establish an innocent motive nor establish wrongdoing. No police recognition, local drug transaction, collective police knowledge about this address, or corroborating suspicious act is added beyond the reported evidence.

Source support: published recitation of J.A. pages 4–11 and state-court materials in [official U.S. Reports, 528 U.S. 119, pp. 135–139](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-528/pdf/USREPORTS-528-119.pdf). ## Laidlaw: additional deposition evidence to consider with the use affidavits

- Linda Moore testified that she had been to the river twice: once in 1980 when visiting someone who lived there, and once after this suit began. Source citation given in the published record: Record document 62, Moore Deposition 23–24. This qualifies the nature of her claimed curtailment; do not turn desired future use into a history of frequent prior recreation.
- Kenneth Lee Curtis testified that he had not visited the river since childhood and, when asked whether pollution was why he stopped going, answered no. Source citation: Curtis Deposition, part 2, pages 38–39. This must be considered alongside his later expressed wish to resume use and his stated present concerns; it does not erase either part of his testimony.
- The published account says the other cited affiants were not deposed. No deposition corroboration is inferred for them.
- The District Court's no-demonstrated-harm finding followed its initial standing determination: initial standing was addressed June 30, 1993; final judgment and the environmental findings issued January 22, 1997. The factfinder's statement that water quality exceeded levels necessary for recreation appears at 956 F. Supp. 588, 600. Keep the temporal sequence and the character of these lower-court findings clear.
