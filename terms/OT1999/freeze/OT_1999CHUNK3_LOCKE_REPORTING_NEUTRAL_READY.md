## Distinct statutory functions and limits

Section 6101(a) requires the Secretary to prescribe reportable marine casualties and the manner of reporting, including death, serious injury, material property loss, material damage affecting vessel seaworthiness or efficiency, and significant environmental harm. Subsection (b) requires the report within five days as provided in the part and its regulations and requires information concerning alcohol's contribution. These are statutory commands; they are not findings that a particular incident met a trigger or that an individual vessel failed to report.

Subsection (d)(1) covers a foreign vessel involved in a marine casualty on United States navigable waters. The separate subsection (d)(2) reaches a foreign vessel constructed or adapted to carry, or actually carrying, oil in bulk as cargo or cargo residue for casualties described in subsection (a)(4) or (5), in waters subject to United States jurisdiction including the Exclusive Economic Zone, only to the extent consistent with generally recognized international-law principles. The latter provision cannot be converted into worldwide reporting jurisdiction over every foreign-vessel casualty. The enacted duplicate subsection (e) numbering remains as printed. The nonfatal-casualty classification provision and the distinct foreign-passenger-vessel provision, with its citizen, location and passenger-service conditions, perform separate work; passenger-vessel coverage does not become a general tanker rule.

Section 6102 independently directs a uniform State marine-casualty reporting system, with federally prescribed casualties and manner of reporting. It directs States to compile and submit reports, information and statistics, including alcohol-related statistics. The statute therefore expressly contemplates state participation. The same provision's uniformity command and that state role both require consideration; neither word alone decides whether this challenged additional requirement survives.

Section 6102(b) also directs federal collection, analysis and publication with appropriate findings and recommendations. Its limiting condition applies when a State's reporting system restricts public disclosure of information derived from reports, except statistical information, or otherwise prohibits its use by the State or any person in an action or proceeding against a person. If that condition applies, the Secretary may use the State-provided information only as the State may use it. This is conditional preservation of the stated use restrictions, not an unqualified federal secrecy rule, a finding that Washington satisfies the condition, or an exemption from all reporting requirements.

## Regulatory timing, triggers and exceptions

Section 4.05-1(a) requires notice immediately after addressing resulting safety concerns. It identifies unintended grounding or bridge strike; intended grounding or bridge strike creating the stated hazard or satisfying another listed criterion; propulsion, primary-steering or associated-system loss reducing maneuverability; occurrences materially and adversely affecting seaworthiness or fitness for service or route; death; the defined treatment-level injury with the additional inability-to-perform condition for those employed or engaged aboard commercial-service vessels; and property damage exceeding $25,000, calculated with the stated included repair costs and excluded salvage, cleaning, gas-freeing, drydocking and demurrage costs. These operative conditions are retained in the exact supplement; no casualty may be presumed from the mere existence of a broader State reporting category.

Section 4.05-5 specifies the notice's vessel, owner or agent, casualty, location, injury and property-damage information. Section 4.05-10 requires a written report within five days, to the identified Coast Guard office and on the specified form with necessary supplements, in addition to immediate notice. Its subsection (b) permits a written report filed without delay after the casualty to satisfy the immediate-notice requirement. Separately, section 4.05-1(b) allows the notice required by 33 CFR 160.215 to satisfy this section when the casualty involves a hazardous condition as defined in 33 CFR 160.203. Neither alternative eliminates the conditions of its own cross-reference. Those cross-referenced provisions are not reproduced here, so this receipt does not expand their terms or decide an application depending on an unquoted definition.

Section 4.01-1 describes the part's reporting, investigation and timely-assistance scope. Section 4.01-3 contains distinct exclusions: vessels subject to 33 CFR 173.51 are outside subpart 4.05; specified diving-accident reports and specified shipyard/harbor-worker accidents have the stated notice exclusions; public vessels are outside the part except as provided in subpart 4.40. The worker provision requires both the absence of the specified vessel or vessel-equipment casualty and the identified OSHA reporting coverage. The historical diving and worker provisions retain old subsection references to section 4.05-1(d)/(e), while the supplied 1999 notice rule uses subsection (a)'s numbered criteria. This textual mismatch is disclosed, not silently modernized. Its exact effect on any particular excluded casualty requires the applicable historical cross-reference authority if material; it supplies no generalized tanker exemption.
