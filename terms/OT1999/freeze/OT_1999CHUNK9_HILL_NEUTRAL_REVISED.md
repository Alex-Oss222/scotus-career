## Standards and Tests selection: Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

### Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

**Governing question:** How a court tailors a content-neutral injunction regulating speech-related conduct.

**Current rule:** A content-neutral injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to serve the significant governmental interest supporting the remedy. This inquiry is more demanding than the ordinary content-neutral legislative time, place and manner rule, without becoming a universal least-restrictive-means test. Each restriction needs a remedial connection to the established interest and conduct. Public access, medical quiet and residential privacy may support relief, but neither those interests nor prior misconduct justifies territorial restrictions or advance-permission conditions broader than necessary. Removing an invalid restriction also removes its operative predicate; it cannot be restored by relabeling the same conduct under another clause. Within an otherwise valid fixed access zone, an exception permitting nonthreatening initial counseling by up to two people per approached person or group may end when the recipient objectively communicates, verbally or nonverbally, that counseling is unwanted. The counselor must end that close encounter and leave the fixed zone, while retaining lawful advocacy outside it; this supplies neither universal silence nor a mobile personal radius.

#### Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 512 U.S. 753

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-880.

**Decided:** 1994-06-30.

For the other questions in Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 1994-06-30, No. 93-880, see Federal Courts — The limited scope of geographic and paragraph (4) review; Federal Courts — An injunction binds responsible parties and actual concert, not a viewpoint; Federal Courts — The independent conduct-language claims remain undecided; Federal Courts — Relief removes invalidated predicates; Federal Courts — Remand permits lawful relief without requiring a personal zone.

##### The decree as confined is not categorically content based

**Holding:** As authoritatively confined to named responsible parties and actual concert with notice, this injunction addresses established interference with access, traffic, medical care and residential privacy rather than abortion advocacy as such; defendant-specific selection does not make it categorically content or viewpoint based. Each restraint still requires independent constitutional scrutiny, and a materially different viewpoint-based application receives no approval.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-A(2), joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this application while accepting the separate actual-concert principle.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*, 491 U.S. 781: applies justification without reference to content; defendant-specific relief still requires scrutiny.
- *Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC*, June 27, 1994: applies its operation-and-justification distinction without importing its cable-specific result or plurality evidentiary application.
- *Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, Inc.*, March 24, 1993: preserves the rule that a benign objective alone does not cure a speech-selective burden.

##### A neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary

**Holding:** A content-neutral injunction burdening protected expression must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve the corresponding significant governmental interest. Its precise terms require a closer fit than ordinary review of generally applicable neutral time, place and manner rules; neither an injunction label nor past misconduct permits suppression of unrelated lawful advocacy.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Stevens's individualized equitable account and the strict-restraint dissent are not joins in this standard.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*: distinguishes generally applicable neutral regulation from this direct injunction; its ordinary rule remains intact.
- *Carroll v. President and Commissioners of Princess Anne*, 393 U.S. 175: applies its demand for precision in speech-burdening injunctive relief.
- *NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co.*: applies the relation between unlawful conduct, responsible persons and permissible relief.
- *Califano v. Yamasaki*, 442 U.S. 682: applies the limitation of equitable relief to what the established violation requires.

##### The qualified public access arrangement survives

**Holding:** The public portion of the 36-foot clinic clearance, with its retained south shoulder, east shoulder subject to the five-foot setback and no-embankment rule, and prompt-crossing exception, burdens no more speech than necessary on this record to secure driveway access and traffic after narrower obstruction commands failed. It remains enforceable at that qualified scope, without creating a generally permissible clinic-buffer distance.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five on geographic fit. Stevens adds a vote to leave the arrangement intact only against the categorical challenge; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the expressive exclusion.

**Material application:** The southern boundary is the south edge of Dixie Way's pavement, leaving the unpaved south shoulder available subject to lawful restrictions. On the east, the U.S. Highway One shoulder remains usable, but defendants may not ascend the embankment and must remain five feet from the clinic's east line. Pending painted lines, prompt crossing uses an eight-foot strip directly from Dixie Way's south to north edge, with its western line coextensive with Highway One's western right-of-way line; once marked, the crosswalk governs. Within the buffer no other pavement entry is allowed. Crossing permits no stopping, hesitation, picketing, congregation, demonstration or traffic impediment. This is an existing crossing dimension, not a personal-separation order.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Carroll*: applies precision to the access boundary and actual crossing and shoulder permissions.
- *Claiborne Hardware*: confines the expressive burden to supported access relief.
- *City of Ladue v. Gilleo*, June 13, 1994: applies attention to usable alternatives without creating an entitlement to every preferred location.

##### The private-property extension lacks the necessary remedial connection

**Holding:** The north/west extension of the 36-foot exclusion cannot suppress otherwise lawful expression on adjoining private land without a demonstrated connection between that full exclusion and the access or operational harm to be remedied. Florida's approval of that extension is reversed; removing the government's expressive restriction neither authorizes trespass nor adjudicates owners' property rights.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on their stricter grounds; Stevens declines distinct geographic review.

**Material application:** The north/west owners and invitees were excepted from the entry prohibition, while the other expressive restrictions still applied when they acted in concert. Removing the governmental expressive exclusion adjudicates neither permission to enter nor private property boundaries.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Claiborne Hardware*: applies the requirement that relief correspond to the established wrong.
- *Carroll*: applies precision to the unsupported geographic extension.
- *Ladue*: preserves practical channel analysis; it supplies no right to use another person's property.

##### The qualified medical-noise restriction survives

**Holding:** Paragraph (4)'s noise restriction is valid in its medical-operation context from 7:30 a.m. through noon, Monday–Saturday, during surgical procedures and recovery periods, as to the listed and other sounds within earshot of patients inside the clinic. Its affirmance rests on the treatment interference established here, not disagreement with a message, and adds no decibel, intent or newly drafted volume threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-C, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse on strict direct-restraint and adequate-unlawful-predicate grounds, despite accepting fit under the Court's assumed standard; Stevens declines merits.

**Material application:** The covered sounds are singing, chanting, whistling, shouting, yelling, bullhorns, auto horns, amplification equipment and other sounds within the earshot of patients inside the clinic, in the lower court's medical-operation context. Both the 7:30 a.m.–noon Monday–Saturday window and the surgery-or-recovery condition apply; neither is replaced by a newly invented sound threshold.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Ward v. Rock Against Racism*: applies the distinction between controlling sound's physical effects and suppressing its content; the heightened injunction rule governs this remedy.
- *Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement*, June 19, 1992: preserves the distinction between a neutral harm and burdens resting on hostility to a message.
- *Carroll*: applies precision through the time, treatment and patient-location limits.

##### The prohibition on observable images is too broad

**Holding:** The prohibition on all images observable to patients inside the clinic, even within the stated morning surgery-and-recovery periods, burdens more speech than necessary on this record and must be removed. Patient vulnerability or distress at a message does not establish a general power to prevent visible advocacy; independently lawful threat or intrusion relief remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-D, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on stricter grounds; Stevens declines merits.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Erznoznik v. City of Jacksonville*, 422 U.S. 205: applies protection against broad suppression merely because images are visible to unwilling viewers.
- *Boos v. Barry*, 485 U.S. 312: applies the distinction between communicative emotional effects and neutral physical harms.
- *Carroll*: requires precision beyond a categorical visual prohibition unsupported by the demonstrated need.

##### Peaceful initial approach cannot depend on advance invitation throughout 300 feet

**Holding:** Paragraph (5)'s requirement of an indication of interest before any physical approach to a person seeking clinic services within 300 feet burdens more speech than necessary and must be removed. This holding protects peaceful initial advocacy, not a right to accompany, encircle, surround, threaten, obstruct or force communication upon a person who declines it, and approves no universal application of disputed harassment or abuse terms.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-E, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal on stricter grounds; Stevens dissents from reversal of this physical-approach condition.

**Material application:** The clause regulates physical approach, not every word or sign within 300 feet. Invited nonthreatening conversation and literature were permitted when a service-seeker approached or inquired, but never inside the 36-foot zone. Protections against accompanying, encircling, surrounding, harassment, threats and physical or verbal abuse of a declining person retain only their independently lawful scope; reversal neither invalidates every conduct protection nor approves every disputed use of those labels.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Claiborne Hardware*: applies protection of persuasion and the connection between misconduct and relief.
- *Boos*: preserves political approach while allowing properly grounded protection against threats and coercion.
- *Carroll*: requires the restriction to distinguish protected initiation from the conduct justifying restraint.

##### Residential privacy does not justify this territorial exclusion

**Holding:** The 300-foot residential prohibition on approaching, congregating, picketing, patrolling, demonstrating and amplification sweeps beyond the supported protection of a targeted home and must be removed. Its advance-invitation qualification does not restore general neighborhood expression; distinct lawful obstruction, trespass, threat and properly focused residential protections are preserved without selecting a replacement distance.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-F, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: six on this bounded ground. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas concur in reversal under their stricter direct-restraint account; the judgment is unanimous.

**Material application:** Paragraph (6)'s separate bans on blocking entrances, exits or driveways and impeding a street providing sole access to the residences' street are distinct from the territorial ban. The separate invited-contact provision allows willing contact outside the clinic buffer, with residential invitation communicated from a distance before contact, not obtained by an uninvited knock or doorbell. Invitation may be revoked, requiring immediate termination and departure. Retained language must conform to reversal and cannot recreate the invalid territorial exclusion.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Frisby v. Schultz*, 487 U.S. 474: applies its distinction between focused home picketing and general neighborhood expression; its narrow residential protection remains.
- *City of Ladue v. Gilleo*: applies practical attention to lost channels while preserving the distinction between one's own home and intrusion on another's.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Florida judgment is affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded for a conforming decree. The qualified public-access arrangement and medical-noise restriction remain; the private extension, images prohibition, initial-invitation requirement and residential territorial ban must be removed, including dependent use under paragraph (9). The predecessor and independently lawful unreviewed terms are not automatically dissolved. The court may consider precisely fitted lawful conduct relief, but no replacement injunction, personal distance, new sound threshold, damages or contempt judgment is commanded.




#### Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1065.
**Decided:** February 19, 1997.

##### Fixed access restrictions satisfy the governing injunction standard on this record

**Holding:** A content-neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve its corresponding significant interest. These fixed fifteen-foot demonstration restrictions at identified doorways, driveway entrances and parking-access points of covered facilities—all facilities where abortions are performed in the Western District of New York—with nonthreatening initial counseling by no more than two counselors per approached person or group, satisfy that requirement on the supported obstruction and crowding record; fifteen feet is not a universal safe harbor.

The injunction binds responsible parties and persons with notice acting in actual concert; shared views, association or independent advocacy alone do not establish that relationship. This coverage boundary neither adjudicates new nonparty liability nor determines whether a particular speech restriction is sufficiently tailored.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's fixed-access holding is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six Justices. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also join the responsible-party, notice and actual-concert boundary, giving that distinct proposition nine votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decree regulates demonstration, not mere presence; the counseling limit is per approached person or group, not two counselors for an entire zone. Substantial temporary-order compliance does not erase supported access interference, and no requirement to disobey a speech-neutral predecessor or exhaust every narrower possibility is adopted. The temporary order already contained personal-distance and counseling restrictions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen supplies provision-specific fit; its distances are not safe harbors. Carroll supplies precision and Claiborne Hardware ties relief to responsible misconduct. Ward's rule for ordinary neutral regulation does not replace the heightened injunction standard.

##### The fixed-zone counseling exception may end after a communicated refusal

**Holding:** Within an otherwise valid fixed access zone, the allowance for nonthreatening counseling by up to two people per approached person or group may end when the recipient objectively communicates verbally or nonverbally that counseling is unwanted. The counselor must end that close encounter and leave the fixed zone, while retaining lawful advocacy outside it; the condition establishes neither silence everywhere nor a mobile personal radius beyond the fixed zone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unexpressed unwillingness does not trigger departure. This condition is an exception to independently valid fixed access relief, not a general unwilling-listener veto or captive-audience rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen's protection of peaceful initial approach remains: permission to begin an encounter within a valid corridor differs from the broad advance-permission requirement invalidated there. Boos preserves the distinction between physical harm and offense at communicative content.

##### The moving-person restriction burdens more speech than the record warrants

**Holding:** The injunction's fifteen-foot demonstration boundary moving with each person seeking access to or leaving a covered facility burdens more protected speech than necessary under Madsen and must be struck, even with its initial counseling exception and communicated-refusal qualification. This record-specific holding does not create a right to threaten, touch, obstruct, crowd or coercively pursue anyone, or invalidate every precisely supported separation rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The initial counseling and refusal qualifications do not cure uncertainty from moving listeners and overlapping zones. The Court invents neither a travel cap nor an overlap rule to sustain the actual restraint. Precisely supported, materially different conduct relief remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen requires independent fit for the additional moving burden; Carroll supplies precision where contempt enforces it, and Claiborne Hardware preserves the misconduct nexus.

##### The moving-vehicle restriction independently fails

**Holding:** The fifteen-foot demonstration radius moving with vehicles seeking access to or leaving covered facilities likewise burdens more speech than necessary on this record and must be struck. The decision preserves traffic safety and fixed access protection without deciding the validity of a materially different, precisely supported vehicle restriction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen requires a separate vehicle-specific fit; fixed entrance protection and traffic interests do not automatically justify a moving demonstration radius.

##### Removal of the moving predicate also removes the dependent retreat distance

**Holding:** Once paragraph 1(b)'s moving-person and moving-vehicle restrictions are invalidated, paragraph 1(c)'s cross-reference cannot recreate an automatic fifteen-foot moving distance outside valid fixed zones. That dependent operation must be removed on remand; independently lawful conduct prohibitions remain, and the Court neither commands a substitute distance nor decides every cessation condition considered apart from the invalidated predicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The fixed access and in-zone refusal components are affirmed 6–3; the person and vehicle moving restraints are reversed 8–1; their automatic outside-zone retreat operation is vacated 8–1. Remand requires a conforming preliminary decree, preserving independently lawful unreviewed conduct terms. No replacement distance, travel endpoint, compulsory substitute, contempt judgment, damages or final permanent decree is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen's invalidated-predicate and no-evasion rules require removal of the dependent distance; its refusal to compel a substitute personal zone remains intact.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and dissents in part. He joins the closer injunction-specific standard and the Court's component merits dispositions. He would require a replacement conduct-based post-refusal protection on remand, proposing eight feet subject to site-specific proof and a narrower supported implementation if that measure cannot be justified. This preferred mandatory remedy differs from the Court's permission to consider lawful relief. He agrees only in the disposition leaving the independent validity of the particular disputed terms and applications of paragraphs (7)–(9) undecided; that disposition-only agreement supplies no additional rationale.

Outside the fixed access zone, an enjoined person must remain free to make a peaceful initial approach, offer literature or request conversation. Protection begins only after words or clear conduct convey that further individualized contact is unwanted, a reasonable person would understand that refusal, and the enjoined speaker has notice. Silence, disagreement with the viewpoint and entry into the vicinity do not automatically trigger it. Once triggered, that speaker must stop following, shadowing, accompanying, repeated reapproaches, surrounding and close-range individualized speech, including coordinated substitution of another participant to continue the same pursuit. His proposed order would require at least eight feet of separation while the person proceeds toward or away from the facility within a finite, ascertainable, route-related clinic vicinity.

The refusal ends a particular unwanted encounter, not all exposure to a viewpoint. Lawful stationary signs, speech to the public, offers to willing listeners and communication from outside the separation remain protected. An unavoidable passing encounter, a patient voluntarily approaching a stationary protester and momentary inability to separate safely must not be punished. The speaker must stop targeted pursuit and take reasonable safe steps once able to comply; a listener cannot force a speaker from an otherwise lawful stationary location. Renewed voluntary engagement can reopen communication, but repeated unwanted approaches cannot be used to solicit that permission.

Carroll's precision requirement, Claiborne Hardware's protection of advocacy and association, Yamasaki's remedial limitation, Ward's content distinction and Frisby's focused protection support his proposed conduct-based response. The practical aim is to make the termination of a close encounter observable after conduct that defeated earlier protection. Yet a moving personal zone may burden leafleting, be difficult to measure on a narrow sidewalk, or turn aversion to speech into suppression. The stated safeguards answer those dangers without proving the number necessary. The state court would have to define the vicinity, support space and necessity with record-specific findings, and consider concrete less burdensome means. If eight feet cannot lawfully be supported, it must implement a supported narrower protection, rather than an unsupported numerical floor. Eight feet is a proposed operational measure, not a constitutional number or an already established rule.

Legally bound defendants and persons shown to act in concert with notice are the only subjects of that order. Touching, grabbing, intentional obstruction, threats, vehicle interference, surrounding and coordinated physical intimidation may independently be prohibited when the record and governing law support them, without waiting for refusal. These express conduct positions are not blanket approval of every disputed term in paragraphs (7)–(9). Casey protects choice against state interference and creates no direct private-liability right here; Bray preserves independently grounded state relief without weakening speech protection. Contempt requires notice and the process appropriate to the actual sanction. The separate writing decides no contempt-classification question and rests on no later personal-zone authority.

Justice Stevens concurs in part and dissents in part. He rejects categorical content discrimination and the association challenge, joins the residential reversal, and agrees with the Court's nonreach and limited remand. He declines separate geographic tailoring of the 36-foot zone and the noise and images merits. Under Izumi, later argument does not alone enlarge the petition. His earlier willingness to reach an indispensable intervention issue does not require resolving additional provisions unnecessary to these selected questions. Leaving those provisions intact on that limited review is not a merits endorsement.

His injunction standard is individualized equitable relief, not the Court's heightened speech-injunction formula or a universal adoption of ordinary Ward review. Yamasaki requires no more burden than necessary to give complete relief; Professional Engineers, Hudson and Paradise explain why a remedy for identified wrongdoers can reach beyond the precise past act when needed to prevent recurrence or eliminate its effects. The trial court's familiarity matters. Those principles do not insulate every remedy or presume that the entire decree is necessary.

He would sustain paragraph (5)'s physical-approach restraint for the bound actors in this established setting. It is not a prohibition on every unsolicited word or all advocacy within 300 feet. Distant expression, offered verbal or written counsel that does not become prohibited approach, and invited nonthreatening conversation remain distinct. The documented approaches and interference support this individualized constraint even though a similar general rule for all speakers would pose a different issue. He therefore disagrees with the reversal of the initial-invitation condition, but does not endorse a general right to suppress unwelcome ideas.

He joins the narrower Frisby-based conclusion that the residential radius exceeds supported protection. Advance invitation does not cure suppression of general neighborhood activity. His agreement there does not join the Court's distinct standard or the private-geography, noise and images merits. He would permit lawful consideration of further precise conduct relief on remand; he does not require a new post-refusal injunction or endorse a numerical substitute. His equitable theory and clinic-approach position remain noncontrolling.

Justice Souter concurs. He emphasizes that actual concert is a matter for individual determination, not a synonym for opposition to the clinic. Regal Knitwear allows an injunction to reach actual assistance to a bound party while preserving a stranger's right to deny that relation. The preliminary appearance exchanges included troubling references to abortion opposition, but also reserved nonassociation and concert defenses for later proceedings and distinguished arrest reports from formal charges. They cannot be treated as a final finding against every absent person.

His concurrence accepts the written order and Florida's authoritative limiting construction without declaring every later enforcement neutral. A person must have notice and must actually fall within the lawful bound category; receiving the order alone supplies neither concert nor guilt. His position differs from the dissent's operational inference, yet grants no immunity to an unconstitutional application. The named petitioners' categorical challenge and Cheffer's independent federal preliminary-relief litigation remain separate. He joins the provision-specific speech review and derivative relief; his additional explanation neither approves a new distance nor imposes a mandatory replacement decree.

Justice Scalia concurs in part and dissents in part, joined throughout by Justices Kennedy and Thomas. They would also reverse the public expressive exclusion and the noise restriction. Their first sufficient objection is that a judicial command directly forbidding selected persons' future protected advocacy demands the rigorous protection for prior restraints even if nominally content neutral. Carroll, Keefe, Vance and Claiborne Hardware support that distinction. Ordinary conduct orders and incidental consequences of neutral sanctions are different. Alexander's distinction between postconviction effects and a direct future-speech prohibition does not shelter this order.

They separately reject the decree's asserted neutral operation. Turner requires attention to what a rule does, not merely a benign description of its aims, while preserving the proposition that speaker identity alone is not content discrimination. Here the already reported preliminary exchanges connecting coverage to opposition to the clinic supply the additional operational concern. The three accept the abstract legality of actual concert with notice and the bar on ideological guilt; they do not treat that legal category as proof of neutral implementation. They determine no absent person's concert or contempt.

The public-zone objection also concerns the legal predicate for this expansion. They accept the facts found but dispute whether incidental slowing and momentary hesitation, without the necessary relation to obstruction forbidden by the predecessor, legally justify the whole exclusion of peaceful expression. This is a legal sufficiency objection, not a retrial through selected video. Precisely directed road, driveway or conduct controls may address genuine interference; none is found sufficient or ordered as a compulsory substitute. The crossing and shoulder permissions do not themselves establish the necessity of the full exclusion.

For noise, they accept that the qualified clause fits the Court's test if that test is assumed. That concession does not affirm it or join the governing standard. The separate objection is that medically desirable restraint is not an identified adequate prior or threatened violation of statute, common law or the predecessor justifying this personalized speech order. They do not find that Florida has no applicable noise law, deny medical harm or invent a decibel threshold. Strict direct-restraint review independently remains applicable. They concur in reversing private-side exclusion, images, initial-consent approach and residential territory under their stricter grounds, without a right to trespass, threaten, pursue a declining person or invade a home.

They join the confined review-scope ruling and decline independent paragraphs (7)–(9) merits despite actual briefing. Every predicate they would reverse must fall from dependent enforcement; at minimum they join removal of the predicates this judgment actually sets aside. Independently lawful obstruction, assault, threats and conduct remedies survive. Their position does not command a replacement personal zone, approve Stone-Zsela's proposed distance, decide an unreported contempt or dissolve the entire predecessor injunction.

## Event

City of Erie v. Pap's A.M., No. 98-1161. Merits decision, March 29, 2000. Argued November 10, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania, reviewing its October 21, 1998 judgment, 719 A.2d 273. The grant concerns the application of Erie's minimal-clothing requirement to adult-entertainment dancers and the threshold question whether the Court can reach that federal dispute after Pap's ceased operating Kandyland.

Erie enacted Ordinance 75-1994 on September 28, 1994. Section 1(c) prohibits knowingly or intentionally appearing nude in a public place. Section 2 defines nudity through specified insufficiently covered anatomy and devices or coverings that simulate specified anatomy; those distinct terms are reproduced below. Section 3 extends public place to outdoor and enclosed venues, including adult or membership clubs, theaters and invited events with or without admission charges. Section 4 excepts children under ten and an individual exposing a breast while breastfeeding an infant under two. Section 6 directs the least constitutional infringement consistent with the ordinance's purposes and requires severance when a court finds a portion, application or enforcement excessively restrictive of the specified rights. It contains no enacted exception for expression judged artistically superior.

Pap's operated Kandyland, a commercial establishment with nude female dancing and no alcohol sales, though patrons could bring alcohol. The Pennsylvania majority invalidated the nudity restriction under the First Amendment and severed sections 1(c) and 2, leaving the separate prohibitions on sexual intercourse, deviate sexual intercourse and genital fondling intact. The majority did not decide the Pennsylvania Constitution, substantial overbreadth or the objection to standing to assert overbreadth. A two-Justice state constitutional concurrence did not provide the majority judgment's independent ground.

The submitted record includes the Council video, the parties' agreed facts and the City's repeated litigation interpretation that theatrical expression such as Equus, Hair and Oh! Calcutta! could receive different treatment as higher protected expression through section 6 and the preamble. Counsel attributed that interpretation to the City and Council, acknowledged its subjectivity, and sought judicial narrowing; the trial court did not adopt a binding theatrical exemption. The parties agreed that Equus featured frontal nudity for several weeks in October and November 1994 without enforcement efforts during a run in which the prohibition became effective. They deleted the proposed characterization of the production's artistic and social merit. The admitted episode does not establish the sole reason for nonenforcement, a complete enforcement policy or official awareness of every performance. The early temporary restraint and differing effective-date descriptions prevent treating every day of the run as an available enforcement opportunity.

Pap's closed Kandyland and sold the property in 1998. Its June 1999 representation disclaimed an interest in the premises or another nude-dancing business; a September affidavit confirmed cessation and the owner's retirement. Continued incorporation establishes no plan to reopen. No surviving damages claim, citation, penalty, substitute claimant or fee-based merits controversy is established.

## Holdings

### 1. The existing state judgment presents a live federal controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** In this state-review posture, the judgment's continuing withdrawal of the City's enforcement authority, coupled with the respondent's continuing adverse defense of that judgment, supplies a live federal controversy despite Pap's cessation of the regulated business. This rule depends on the actual legal disability and adversity; abstract governmental interest, incorporation, fees or an imagined return to business does not suffice.

**Authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela separately votes to reach the merits without joining this rationale; Scalia and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this state-review controversy remains live because the judgment presently withdraws the City's enforcement authority and the respondent continues to defend that concrete alteration of legal rights. ASARCO permits the party first invoking federal jurisdiction to establish its own injury from the state judgment; it does not dispense with a case or controversy. Here reversal would change the City's actual legal disability, rather than answer an abstract question about desirable legislation.

Pap's closure, sale and retirement are credited. Continued incorporation is not proof of future dancing, and no manipulation, prospective reopening or surviving monetary claim supplies jurisdiction. The objection that only one party retains a business interest is substantial, but the operative state judgment and continued adverse defense preserve this particular dispute over municipal authority. Arizonans for Official English involved an expired individual federal claim and supplies no automatic answer erasing this distinct state-review posture. Laidlaw's defendant-cessation burden is not transferred to a plaintiff leaving its business. The Court recognizes no general exception for public-law litigation or fee disputes; without the actual legal disability and continuing adversity, abstract importance would be insufficient.

**Precedent treatment:** ASARCO supplies the state-review injury principle while retaining the case-or-controversy requirement. Arizonans's actual-expiration rule and Laidlaw's defendant-cessation doctrine retain their distinct predicates.

### 2. Communicative targeting cannot receive incidental-burden treatment

**Holding and operative rule:** A public-nudity ordinance cannot receive incidental-burden treatment when its justification and operation principally target the communicative adult-entertainment setting; Erie's record does not satisfy the scrutiny that follows. The granted application therefore cannot be sustained, while genuinely general nudity rules and evidence-based regulation of nonexpressive harms remain available.

**Authority:** Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg in Part II, together with Stone-Zsela's express agreement in his concurrence, directly support this precise rule, its application and its stated boundary: five Justices across writings. Stone joins no portion of the lead. This is direct proposition-level agreement, not a Marks inference or five votes for every Part II sentence.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a general public-nudity label cannot give incidental-burden treatment to an application whose justification and operation principally target the communicative adult-entertainment setting. O'Brien requires an interest unrelated to suppressing expression before its incidental-burden analysis can sustain the restriction. The content-neutrality inquiry concerns what the government actually regulates and why, not only the breadth of its words.

Erie's defense of this application through harms attributed to the message-bearing setting does not satisfy that requirement or the scrutiny that follows. A modest clothing requirement does not by itself cure this defect. The rule leaves genuinely general nudity laws and supported regulation of crime, obstruction, sanitation, zoning or other nonexpressive harms available. It neither establishes that every adult-business effect is communicative nor resolves a different properly justified regulation. The more detailed evidentiary account and strict-scrutiny analysis in Part II have four complete joins; the independent intermediate-scrutiny alternative has three. Neither is attributed in full to the five supporters of this common rule.

**Precedent treatment:** O'Brien's nonsuppression requirement is applied rather than displaced. Existing content-neutrality doctrine continues to test operation and justification. No categorical invalidity of nudity rules, new theater exemption or independently controlling O'Brien record-insufficiency holding follows.

### Scope of the affirmance

Affirmance leaves the Pennsylvania judgment, including its existing severance of sections 1(c) and 2, intact. The common as-applied reason does not replace that state remedy with a newly narrowed federal decree or restore other applications of the severed provisions. The Court does not decide the Pennsylvania constitutional or preserved overbreadth grounds, every simulated-anatomy application or unpresented theatrical claims.

Its distinct advancement-and-fit inquiry remains available for genuinely incidental regulation; Part III's adverse application has three votes only.
- **Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC, June 27, 1994:** The existing distinction between actual content selection and benign purpose remains. Part II applies that principle in its fuller account; the congressional predictive-deference rule and four-Justice present-record application are not transplanted wholesale.
- **Ino Ino, Inc. v. City of Bellevue, September 28, 1998:** Its supported physical-control holding and its separate protection of visible nonobscene expression retain their actual coalitions and limits. Part II's four Associates apply the distinction between operational controls and suppression of communication; Stone's Erie concurrence does not acquire a join in Ino Ino's separate visibility holding.
- **Barnes v. Glen Theatre, Inc., 501 U.S. 560 (1991):** Its judgment and distinct rationales remain; no single rationale is made binding through unshown logical nesting. The writings here disagree over its application to this record, and no general prohibition on public nudity is invalidated.
- **City of Renton v. Playtime Theatres, Inc., 475 U.S. 41 (1986):** Relevant noncommunicative effects and reasonably relevant outside evidence remain permissible; a crime label does not itself establish fit or convert objection to a message into a noncommunicative harm.
- **Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989):** Advancement and adequate fit remain distinct from a least-restrictive-means requirement. The several intermediate-scrutiny writings preserve that distinction, without a majority adopting Part III's present insufficiency ground.
- **Edenfield v. Fane, April 26, 1993; Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC, March 31, 1997; Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC, January 24, 2000:** Their actual justification and evidence principles inform the separate evidentiary positions. None establishes a universal local-study requirement, shifts the government's burden to the challenger or decides this clothing requirement's efficacy in advance.
- **Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement, June 19, 1992:** Its audience-hostility principle remains; it does not equate every adult-business consequence with persuasion. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Effective March 29, 2000, the state-review threshold rule rests on six rationale votes. The merits establish the five-Justice rule against incidental-burden treatment when a public-nudity ordinance's justification and operation principally target the communicative adult-entertainment setting, with Erie failing the resulting scrutiny. The judgment leaves Pennsylvania's existing severance of sections 1(c) and 2 intact and the three other public-indecency prohibitions undisturbed.

There is no controlling independent finding that this record fails intermediate scrutiny, no blanket invalidation of general public-nudity laws, no constitutional entitlement to every form of adult entertainment and no new evidentiary or hearing duty. The full Part II account has four joins; Part III has three. Existing rules for genuinely general conduct regulation and supported control of nonexpressive harms remain. State constitutional law, substantial overbreadth, other simulated-anatomy applications and the claims of absent theatrical producers are not adjudicated.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Stevens's Part II, joined by Justices Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg: the fuller content-selection rationale.** These four Justices find communicative selection from the City's repeated hierarchy of protected expression, its message-control explanation, the intended Kandyland application and the mixed Council record. The agreed Equus episode corroborates that combined inference but proves neither its own motive nor a comprehensive enforcement policy. Counsel's account is attributable governmental interpretation, not an enacted exception or authoritative judicial construction. The deleted artistic-merit characterization remains deleted.

Turner's operation-based classification and Ino Ino's distinction between supported physical controls and suppression of protected anatomy answer the general-text defense. Barnes does not remove the need to identify communicative targeting, and the reservation for general nudity rules remains. The alternative account—constitutional accommodation of theatrical settings with different noncommunicative effects—is substantial, but these Justices reject it as an adequate explanation of the combined municipal account here. The restriction therefore requires content-based scrutiny; the asserted covering means lacks the required narrow fit. They do not equate every secondary effect with persuasion or adjudicate a nonparty theater's claim. Stone agrees with the common operative rule and result but does not join this complete account.

**Justice Stevens's Part III, joined by Justices Thomas and Ginsburg: an independent intermediate-scrutiny alternative.** Even assuming the application is content neutral, these three Justices would affirm because the submitted record does not establish material advancement of the asserted harms through the covering requirement. O'Brien and Ward require a supported relation between means and harm; Edenfield, the actual Turner decisions, Ino Ino and Nixon illustrate the need to evaluate that relation rather than accept a label. The absence of a new local study alone is not the defect, and relevant outside experience remains available under Renton.

They confront Barnes's judgment, the general text, the small retained expressive burden and the constitutional-accommodation account. The combined assertions still do not establish why this alteration of the performance materially reduces the asserted harms. They require neither a completed crime, a formal administrative record, numerical certainty nor the least restrictive conceivable regulation. This is an independently sufficient ground for their affirmance, but has only three votes and is not a holding of the Court. Scalia does not join it; his doubt about efficacy supplies no unexpressed intermediate ground. Souter's different insufficiency conclusion supports a remand opportunity and does not add a join here.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** The case is live. A public-nudity ordinance cannot receive incidental-burden treatment when its justification and operation principally target the communicative adult-entertainment setting, and Erie's record does not satisfy the scrutiny that follows. Government cannot invoke incidental regulation while defending the law through harms attributed to the message-bearing setting. He therefore affirms and leaves the state judgment invalidating enforcement against Pap's in place, without a separate remedial instruction.

His reasoning preserves genuinely general nudity rules and evidence-based regulation of crime, obstruction, sanitation, zoning or other nonexpressive harms. He joins no portion of the lead. His concurrence supplies direct support for the common content-selection proposition, but neither adopts the lead's complete threshold and evidentiary accounts nor adds an independent O'Brien insufficiency ground. His as-applied reasoning does not order new severance or reinstate applications of the provisions the state court severed.

**Justice Scalia, dissenting from the threshold disposition, joined by Justice Thomas.** Pap's complete departure from the regulated business ends the prospective controversy. ASARCO's original-standing problem differs from later mootness: the City's adverse judgment cannot alone supply the missing opposing personal stake. Arizonans requires a real continuing dispute, and Laidlaw's defendant-cessation rule does not automatically govern this plaintiff's closure. Continued incorporation, fees, retirement or an imagined future business does not cure that defect.

They would dismiss federal review, leaving the state judgment undisturbed. Article III does not require the state judiciary to dismiss its own proceeding; federal supervisory vacatur and Bancorp supply no automatic erasure of the state decree. Once the Court rejects their objection and reaches the merits, both expressly participate and affirm. Both join Part II's finding that this actual application selects communication, which defeats the general-conduct and absence-of-targeting defenses. Only Thomas joins Part III's independent O'Brien alternative. Scalia retains a genuine general-law category and does not turn doubt about covering's efficacy into a second merits ground.

**Justice O'Connor, dissenting from the merits judgment.** She joins Part I but would reverse the federal invalidation of the granted adult-establishment application under O'Brien and remand the unadjudicated matters. The general public-conduct scope, identified noncommunicative adult-establishment harms, reasonably relevant Barnes/Renton inference and limited retained expressive burden together justify this application. Ino Ino and Turner still require actual scrutiny, and Nixon requires affirmative justification; a preamble, small burden or lack of contrary proof would not suffice alone.

She reads the City's theater account as constitutional accommodation in contexts potentially lacking the relevant adult-establishment effects, rather than sufficient proof that the Kandyland application selects art by taste. She acknowledges the agreed nonenforcement, repeated subjective interpretations, message-control argument and thin local record. She nevertheless accepts the bounded analogy and preventive inference, without finding a Kandyland crime, adopting a binding theater exemption or making morality alone sufficient. Her proposed remand follows final federal validation within the grant; it is not the nonvalidating remand of Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Pennsylvania constitutional protection, substantial overbreadth and separate simulated-anatomy applications remain open.

**Justice Kennedy, dissenting from the merits judgment, joined by Justice Breyer; Justice Souter joins only the common classification/fit inquiry and nonvalidating remand.** They would vacate and remand to decide whether the City's actual account selects expression by perceived worth or rests on relevant differences in noncommunicative effects. An artistic hierarchy cannot supply a nonsuppressive justification, but neither general wording nor the single Equus episode conclusively decides classification. If the justification is content based, strict scrutiny applies; if independent of expression, O'Brien and Ward require real advancement and fit without least-restrictive-means review.

Ino Ino's actual operational analysis, Edenfield and Turner require connecting the specific harm, means and contrary evidence. Nixon's actual evidentiary treatment does not permit the City's repeated hierarchy and message-control account to disappear behind generic deference. The state majority's categorical equation of effects with persuasion is too broad, while immediate validation leaves the City's actual interpretation unanswered. Kennedy and Breyer also reject a final conclusion that this application is necessarily content selective. They reserve whether the existing record satisfies intermediate scrutiny; Souter does not join that reservation.

The proposed remand would not compel a new local study, hearing or rewritten ordinance. The state court could use the submitted record or resolve preserved state constitutional and federal-overbreadth issues. Common experience and relevant outside evidence remain available; no completed offense, precise numerical proof or inevitable business closure is required. Kennedy and Breyer's possible later outcomes are not present alternative judgment votes.

**Justice Souter, further dissenting from the merits judgment.** In addition to joining the common classification inquiry and nonvalidating remand, he concludes that the existing demonstration is insufficient even assuming O'Brien applies. Erie must establish a germane basis for both the asserted harms and their alleviation by this restriction if that federal justification remains decisive. He reconsiders the evidentiary leniency of his Barnes inference rather than treating citation to Barnes as proof. Relevant outside evidence and current justification may suffice; the amount of proof varies where fit is obvious, but covering's efficacy is not self-evident here.

Edenfield, Turner, Ino Ino and Nixon reinforce independent examination of advancement and fit. Renton permits relevant outside experience without repeated local studies. Reliance must be real and pertinent; an opinion accepting evidence for a similar regulation can supply a relevant basis. The existing zoning alternative and its nonenforcement require an answer, not a command to use zoning. No formal administrative record, completed crime or numerical certainty is demanded.

He leaves open a genuine nonsuppressive distinction between venues and would permit the City an opportunity to justify it. First, however, the state court may resolve Pennsylvania constitutional law or properly presented federal overbreadth, making further evidentiary development unnecessary. Thus he neither orders an unconditional new study or hearing nor promises eventual validation. Kennedy and Breyer join the common inquiry and remand but not his independent conclusion that the present showing is insufficient. His remedy also differs from Part III's final affirmance.

## Procedure After Action

The Supreme Court's review is complete. The Pennsylvania judgment is affirmed, leaving its existing severance of sections 1(c) and 2 in force. The other conduct prohibitions remain undisturbed. No additional federal remedial proceeding, evidentiary reopening, local study or ordinance revision is ordered. The Court does not decide the preserved Pennsylvania constitutional or substantial-overbreadth issues, confer immunity from independent valid laws, impose a penalty, award damages or fees, or determine a claim belonging to an absent theater.
