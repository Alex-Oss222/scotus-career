## Verified lineups, qualifications and discrepancies

### Flippo

Unsigned per curiam; no separate writing or noted dissent. Certiorari and in forma pauperis status granted; reversed and remanded. The comparator's conventional 9–0 description should not become a claim that the opinion publishes an individual vote table.

The final Reports address the writ to the Circuit Court of West Virginia, Fayette County: the state Supreme Court only declined discretionary review. Cornell's preliminary caption and a county reference differ; use the corrected final report. The holding rejects a general homicide-scene exception under Mincey. Emergency aid and a prompt search for other victims or a killer remain permitted within their limits. Consent, another warrant exception, and harmless error remain open on remand if properly raised. No blanket exclusion of all evidence or resolution of those alternatives follows from the judgment.

### Texas v. Lesage

Unsigned per curiam; no separate writing or noted dissent. Certiorari granted; Fifth Circuit reversed to the extent identified and remanded. The conventional 9–0 label has the same disclosure limit as Flippo.

Conclusive same-decision proof defeats § 1983 liability for the discrete adverse decision notwithstanding an impermissible criterion. The Supreme Court did not itself decide that Texas had conclusively proved the defense on this record. A challenge to ongoing or imminent unequal competition seeking forward-looking relief does not require proof the plaintiff would ultimately receive the benefit. The §§ 1981 and 2000d theories, and whether prospective relief had been abandoned, remained for remand. Historical Hopwood procedure is a historical premise, not automatically current simulated law.

### Fiore

Breyer wrote for a unanimous Court; no separate writing. Argued October 12, 1999. The November 30 event certified whether Scarpone stated the correct interpretation of Pennsylvania law at finality or changed that law. The federal judgment and further proceedings were reserved. The opinion's directions transmit the opinion, briefs and record and recommend the designation of parties under Pennsylvania's certification procedure. This event is not the later federal merits judgment; no subsequent answer or later merits holding may be imported into its cutoff.

### United Reporting

Rehnquist wrote for O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven. Stevens dissented with Kennedy: two. Argued October 13, 1999; Ninth Circuit reversed.

- Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurred because the facial theory failed; his writing expressly preserves the possibility that an information-access condition can violate the First Amendment as applied.
- Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, Souter and Breyer, treated selective provision of government information as a subsidy and emphasized that publicly obtained information could be used freely. Viewpoint discrimination and other constitutionally prohibited eligibility criteria would present a different question.
- Stevens, joined by Kennedy, understood the plaintiff to assert its own as-applied injury, without needing third-party overbreadth. He objected to singling out a disfavored commercial purpose while granting broad access to others and found the asserted privacy distinction insufficient.

The Court's disposition rejects the facial invalidation; alternative properly presented and preserved grounds remain open. Do not convert it into categorical immunity of every access condition from First Amendment review. Keep the specified access purposes and declaration requirements distinct from the statute's investigative/safety and victim-confidentiality exceptions.

### Drye

Ginsburg wrote for a unanimous Court; no separate writing. Argued November 8, 1999; affirmed. State law identifies the taxpayer's interests; federal law classifies those interests for § 6321. Drye could take the estate or channel it to a known successor; the state disclaimer did not defeat the federal lien. Section 2518's wealth-transfer treatment is not an additional collection exemption; § 6334 supplies the federal exemptions.

Footnote 7 rejects transferability as a necessary criterion but does not decide whether a spendthrift-trust interest alone suffices. The holding concerns rights following the mother's death, not a mere earlier expectancy, and distinguishes declining an inter vivos gift. These limits matter if a proposed rule is broader than the heir's actual control here.

### Kimel

Argued October 13, 1999; affirmed, five to four on immunity. O'Connor's Parts I, II and IV were joined by Rehnquist, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Part III's clear-statement holding was joined by Rehnquist, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven including O'Connor. The two coalitions must remain distinct.

- Stevens, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissented in part and concurred in part. They joined Part III's clear-intent conclusion and rejected the Seminole Tribe limit on Article I abrogation. They regarded lawful federal substantive duties as enforceable against States through federal remedies and relied on political safeguards. They found it unnecessary to decide § 5; their judgment votes are not assent to the majority's § 5 analysis.
- Thomas, joined by Kennedy, concurred in Parts I, II and IV and dissented from Part III. They found no unmistakably clear abrogation in the ADEA's incorporation of FLSA remedies, emphasizing the ADEA's distinct cause of action, the competent-jurisdiction language and amendment sequence.

The controlling § 5 explanation applies Boerne's congruence-and-proportionality inquiry to rational-basis age review, the ADEA's broader substantive reach and the legislative record. Absence of documented state violations is not announced as an independently sufficient universal rule. Preserve the distinction between the bona fide occupational qualification defense and reasonable factors other than age. Commerce Clause substantive validity was not undone; this is the States' immunity from the private damages actions presented.

### New York v. Hill — material packet/comparator correction

Scalia wrote for a unanimous Court; no separate writing. Argued November 2, 1999; reversed. The historical case concerns **Michael Hill, transferred from Ohio custody under Article III(a)'s 180-day period**, not Alma Hill, federal custody, or Article IV(c)'s 120-day period. The comparator's 120-day statement is wrong. The approved neutral account also requires these objective corrections. This report does not alter an approved position or supply consent to changing it.

Defense counsel agreed on January 9, 1995 to a May 1 trial date; Hill moved on April 17, before trial. The trial court counted 167 nonexcludable days through counsel's agreement. The published account does not establish Hill's personal presence at that scheduling proceeding. The source supports waiver of the objection to that particular agreed delay through ordinary counsel control of scheduling, subject to ineffective assistance principles. It does not decide prospective waiver of all IAD rights, whether an agreed date itself establishes statutory good cause, federal Speedy Trial Act waiver, or a sending State's recourse against inordinate delay without objection. Article III and Article IV are separate routes and clocks.

### Wardlow

Rehnquist wrote for O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five. Stevens concurred in part and dissented in part with Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: four. Argued November 2, 1999; reversed and remanded.

The Court sustained the **initial investigative stop** on the combination of unprovoked flight and the area's context, under Terry's objective reasonable-suspicion requirement. High-crime location alone is insufficient; neither flight always suffices nor flight never suffices. Innocent explanations remain possible. The grant did not encompass the frisk or subsequent arrest. The comparator's unqualified reference to the officers' actions must not imply those later steps were adjudicated.

Stevens agreed that no categorical flight rule applied but found the particular evidentiary record inadequate. His writing identifies missing observations about marked cars, other uniforms, the police approach and Wardlow's recognition, and explains plausible noncriminal reasons for flight, including fear of police. A generalized neighborhood label and an inference of recognition cannot silently become additional testimony. A clean factual addendum attributes the published record limitations without revealing this historical vote; originals are being separately verified.

### Laidlaw

Ginsburg wrote for Rehnquist, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer: seven. Scalia dissented with Thomas: two. Argued October 12, 1999; reversed and remanded.

The Court distinguishes harm to members' use and enjoyment from measurable ecological harm. Civil penalties can redress an ongoing-at-filing violation through deterrence; the opinion does not establish redressability for every penalty request, however remote or insubstantial. Voluntary cessation requires the defendant to make recurrence clearly unreasonable to expect under the demanding standard stated in the opinion. Compliance and asserted closure present remand questions, not an adjudicated permanent shutdown. Denial of injunctive relief and failure to appeal that denial do not necessarily extinguish the civil-penalty controversy. No fee order was yet before the Court; catalyst eligibility was not decided. Voluntary cessation does not automatically entitle the losing party to vacatur.

- Stevens concurred separately that penalties for past wrongdoing could remain appropriate even if permanent shutdown made future violations impossible. That broader position is not the seven-Justice opinion's holding.
- Kennedy fully joined while reserving whether citizen enforcement of public fines raises Article II delegation problems; that question was not adequately presented for decision.
- Scalia, joined by Thomas, disputed initial injury and the redressability of private harm by penalties paid to the Treasury. The writing questions the consistency of affidavits with Moore's and Curtis's depositions and stresses the institutional significance of private enforcement. It does not decide Article II. If initial standing were assumed, it acknowledges the reasonableness of the Court's voluntary-cessation/remand approach; it is not a determination that closure conclusively mooted the case.

The competing published accounts of member use/curtailment and the timing of trial findings require the separate original-record check. Neither opinion's description should be mislabeled as a read original deposition. The lower court's no-diligent-prosecution ruling is procedural history; the Supreme Court did not decide its correctness, and the dissent's footnote 4 identifies it as open on remand.

### Paul P. v. Verniero, 170 F.3d 396

Full judicial text, including Fullam's concurrence, read in the [reporter reprint](https://www.sillscummis.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/170_F3d_396.pdf); [OpenJurist text](https://openjurist.org/170/f3d/396/paul-q-v-verniero) supplies an independent reproduction. Docket 97-5791; argued July 9, 1998; decided March 16, 1999. Saved prefix `paul-p-reprint`.

Sloviter wrote with Roth; Fullam concurred in the disposition solely because E.B. bound the panel and emphasized potential vigilantism and the remedial significance of a fuller record. The panel affirmed rejection of the privacy challenge as a matter of law but remanded consideration of safeguards against unnecessary redisclosure and the proposed record supplementation. Conviction information is public; home-address privacy is nontrivial but outweighed in the balancing presented. The distinct compilation theory was not decided. Indirect family consequences do not themselves establish infringement of protected autonomous choices. There is no Supreme Court merits lineup to import.

### Steamfitters, 171 F.3d 912

The entire [OpenJurist judicial text](https://openjurist.org/171/f3d/912/steamfitters-local-union-no-welfare-fund), appendix and notes 1–24 were read. [Independent opinion reproduction](https://app.midpage.ai/document/steamfitters-local-union-no-420-762629) confirms the docket, panel, date and judicial text; extracted text is saved as `steamfitters-crosscheck`. Direct OpenJurist download returned 403; successful web reading is distinguished in the manifest.

Docket **98-1426**, argued **January 28, 1999**, decided **March 29, 1999**. Becker wrote for Becker, Scirica and Rosenn; no separate writing. Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal was affirmed. The comparator's missing-date/docket limitation is resolved. No Supreme Court merits vote is supplied.

Remoteness and speculative causation defeated the RICO, antitrust and accompanying state claims. Intent to target the Funds did not eliminate the intervening links from misleading information, through smoking choices and illness, to fund payments. The decision applies Holmes and the AGC factors; it does not impose a bare label-based bar on every indirect claimant. Footnote 4 doubts the district court's no-economic-injury and exclusive-subrogation grounds and leaves them unresolved. The majority of antitrust factors need not all independently defeat the claim: duplication/apportionment concerns were relatively slight, while remoteness and speculation were decisive. Statistical aggregation could not repair that antecedent causal defect; the court did not categorically forbid statistical damage proof. Notes 11 and 17 leave pleading deficiency and Rule 9(b) outside the holding. The antitrust violation itself was assumed for standing analysis; private RICO injunction availability and a categorical physical-injury requirement for special duty were not decided. Those limits constrain any use of the lower judgment as historical context.
