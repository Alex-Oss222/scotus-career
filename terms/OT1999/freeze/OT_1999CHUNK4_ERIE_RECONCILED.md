## Operative provisions and record limits

The grant concerns the minimal-clothing application to adult-entertainment dancers and necessary threshold issues. It does not authorize adjudication of Pennsylvania constitutional law or every possible simulated-anatomy application. The following operative quotation is copied mechanically from the saved lower-opinion extract; the quotation retains that source's wording and OCR rather than being retyped:

```text
The Ordinance states in relevant part:
      1. A person who knowingly or intentionally in a public place:
      a. engages in sexual intercourse;
      b. engages in deviate sexual intercourse as defined by the Pennsylvania Crimes Code;
      c. appears in a state of nudity, or
      d. fondles the genitals of himself, herself or another person commits Public Indecency, a Summary Offense.
      2. "Nudity” means the showing of the human male or female genital (sic), pubic area or buttocks with less than a fully opaque covering; the showing of the female breast with less than a fully opaque covering of any part of the nipple; the exposure of any device, costume, or covering which gives the appearance of or simulates the genitals, pubic hair, natal cleft, perineum anal region or pubic hair region; or the exposure of any device worn as a cover over the nipples and/or areola of the female breast, which device simulates and gives the realistic appearance of nipples and/or areola.
      3. "Public Place” includes all outdoor places owned by or open to the general public, and all buildings and enclosed places owned by or open to the general public, including such places of entertainment, taverns, restaurants, clubs, theaters, dance halls, banquet halls, party rooms or halls limited to specific members, restricted to adults or to patrons invited to attend, whether or not an admission charge is levied.
      4.The prohibition set forth in subsection 1 (c) shall not apply to:
      a. Any child under ten (10) years of age; or
      b. Any individual exposing a breast in the process of breastfeeding an infant under two (2) years of age.
      6. CONSTRUCTION AND SEVERABILITY— It is the intention of the City of Erie that the provisions of this ordinance be construed, enforced and interpreted in such a manner as will cause the least possible infringement of the constitutional rights of free speech, free expression, due process, equal protection or other fundamental rights consistent with the purposes of this ordinance. Should a court of competent jurisdiction determine that any part of this ordinance, or any application or enforcement of it is excessively restrictive of such rights or liberties, then such portion of the ordinance, or specific application of the ordinance, shall be severed from the remainder, which shall continue in full force and effect.
```

Section 6 requires severance on its stated determination; its least-infringement construction remains limited by the ordinance's purposes. It does not enact an exemption for a named play or for expression deemed artistically superior. The existing state judgment severed sections 1(c) and 2, leaving the sexual-intercourse, deviate-sexual-intercourse and genital-fondling prohibitions intact. The state majority's federal ruling controlled; the two-Justice state constitutional concurrence did not supply an adequate independent ground. The majority did not decide the Pennsylvania Constitution, substantial overbreadth or the objection to standing to assert overbreadth. It rejected insertion of an expressive-nudity exception.

Kandyland's commercial location, nude female dancing and permission to bring alcohol without sales do not establish criminal incidents there. The Council tape supplies mixed morality, erotic-message, crime, alcohol and business-viability assertions; the City's proposed characterization of that tape was not stipulated. The agreement concerning clothing states what the ordinance required, not actual compliance. The statewide liquor-premises comparison was deleted. Refusal to stipulate a proposed description of the erotic message was not a waiver of expression.

The December trial record establishes by agreement that Equus featured frontal nudity for several weeks in October and November 1994 without efforts to enforce the prohibition that became effective during its run. The proposed artistic-and-social-merit characterization was expressly deleted. Separately, counsel repeatedly asserted that higher protected theatrical expression would be accommodated through section 6 and the preamble, attributed that interpretation to the City and Council, acknowledged subjectivity, and sought judicial narrowing. Co-counsel also argued that a message enticing secondary effects could be limited. Those are material governmental interpretations and legal arguments, not an enacted exemption, a binding judicial construction or proof that the message actually caused crime.

Neither the one agreed nonenforcement episode nor the interpretations establish the exclusive cause of nonenforcement, an exhaustive enforcement policy or specific official awareness of every performance. The early temporary restraint and inconsistent effective-date recitations also prevent an inference that enforcement was legally available on every day of the run. The inference of content selection, where a Justice adopts it, rests on the combined interpretation, intended Kandyland application and Council record, not the episode alone. ## Existing law and historical comparison

Under O'Brien, a regulation must fall within governmental power, further an important or substantial interest unrelated to expressive suppression, and impose an incidental burden no greater than essential to that interest. Ward rejects a universal least-restrictive-means test without eliminating advancement and fit. Renton permits evidence from other places reasonably relevant to the problem; it does not make a crime label self-proving or turn offense at a message into a noncommunicative harm. Forsyth's audience-hostility rule does not mean that every consequence of an adult business necessarily operates through persuasion.

Barnes retains its divided rationales. O'Connor and Kennedy joined the plurality's public-order/morality analysis under O'Brien; Scalia used a general-conduct rationale and separately required communicative targeting before treating conduct regulation as forbidden speech suppression; Souter relied on crime-related effects and a bounded covering inference with a reservation for different theatrical settings; Stevens joined the dissent. Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer were not seated. No judgment arithmetic makes Souter's rationale a majority holding, and no logical-subset finding is invented under Marks.

Actual Ino Ino, September 28, 1998, changes the legal setting through the Associates' own direct rationale joins. Kennedy's opinion requires scrutiny of cabaret-specific physical restrictions, supported contact/detection interests and actual fit; it rejects rational-basis-only treatment of stage dancing. It separately invalidates suppression of protected performances and images merely because listed anatomy is visible outside. Commercial or erotic expression does not lose protection through a general-conduct label. At the same time, its support for physical operational rules and express preservation of independently lawful public-nudity restrictions prevent a categorical conclusion that every covering requirement is invalid. It supplies a concrete analogy and obligation to determine the actual application's character, not an Erie result in advance.

Actual Edenfield requires demonstrated real advancement in its truthful-solicitation setting; it is an analogy, not a transfer of Central Hudson to dance. Actual Turner I's content-classification principle distinguishes operation from benign purpose, while its five-supported predictive principle combines deference with independent review of reasonable inferences from substantial evidence. The four-Justice present-record-insufficiency explanation is not controlling majority law. Actual Turner II sustains a developed preventive showing and supported fit. Congressional deference and cable-specific rules are not transplanted wholesale.

Actual Nixon/Shrink, January 24, 2000, applies Russell to affirmative corruption-risk and practical-capacity findings. O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer among the Associates, does not permit generic deference or force challengers to prove impossibility. Kennedy's dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, accepts prophylaxis but finds the operational response to serious burdens inadequate. Historical Erie's sustaining plurality expressly relied on generalized statements in historical Nixon about local experience and contrary studies. Those historical statements are not automatically current law. This is a specific changed doctrinal premise, but the actual campaign-finance decision neither requires local empirical proof in every speech case nor itself decides clothing's efficacy.

Actual Southworth, March 22, 2000, supplies no new nudity or mootness rule. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg joined its complete defined-program framework; Stevens, Souter and Breyer joined only the judgment on actual-program grounds. Neither their limited joins nor the student-program neutrality stipulation is enlarged here. Its concern with actual operation reinforces rather than substitutes for the relevant expressive-conduct analysis.

Historically, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer joined final validation under O'Brien; Souter accepted jurisdiction and O'Brien but opposed the disposition and required a remand for proof. Stevens and Ginsburg opposed this suppression of expression. Scalia and Thomas preferred mootness, but expressly joined ultimate reversal if the Court rejected that objection and reached the merits, using both the general-law rationale and the alternative absence of communicative targeting rather than the plurality's evidence analysis. Stevens and Ginsburg did not join the historical threshold portion; their merits dissent does not provide an unexpressed jurisdiction opinion.

### Stevens

First, the combined municipal interpretation, message-control explanation and Council record establish selection of expression by perceived worth; the admitted Equus fact corroborates that inference without establishing its own motive. The covering requirement lacks the narrow fit required for this content-based restriction. Second, independently, even if the application is treated as content neutral, this submitted record does not establish material advancement through the covering requirement under O'Brien/Ward. This second ground is intermediate scrutiny, not a concealed least-restrictive-means test.

**Own support and strongest contrary path:** His Barnes dissent join supplies the communicative-component and fit analysis. His full Ino Ino join distinguishes supported contact rules from suppression of visible protected anatomy; his Edenfield, limited Turner I predictive, Turner II and actual Nixon positions require supported inferences. He confronts Barnes's judgment, general text, constitutional accommodation and the theater debate already present in Barnes. He does not treat every secondary effect as persuasion or every nonenforcement episode as discriminatory. The legal deficiency is not merely the absence of a local study: the asserted means-to-harm relation and subjective interpretation do not supply the necessary justification.

**Historical reconciliation and joins:** Retain the revalidated disposition and both grounds. He joins neither final validation nor a remand merely reserving whether this showing suffices. No categorical theater exemption, general right to public nudity, nonparty theatrical judgment or new federal severance rule follows.
