# Saenz: form and governing propositions fixed before counting

One signed lead follows plenary review. The actual legal distinctions require separate constitutional, notice-method, notice-application and municipal portions. One reconciled Associate can carry the complete lead; divided delivery is unnecessary. No author or fresh count has been selected in fixing this form.

- Part I states the controlling possibility of an attributable affirmative deprivation through a command that actually interrupts an available lawful protective response and creates or materially increases exposure to known danger with the required constitutional culpability. It rejects the categorical custody, victim-identity and private-driver exclusions, requires examination of the actual causal intervention, and preserves the nonprotection alternative and unresolved factual predicates. Stone and the four vacating Associates share this ground and the bounded vacatur.
- Part II-A states the conduct-time, all-relevant-law, particularized objective immunity method, with June 19, 1995 as the actual date and no later-law notice or factual-twin requirement. Every Justice supports this method. It does not decide its application.
- Part II-B is the four vacating Associates' additional conclusion that the complete preconduct landscape defeats immunity disposing of the entire qualifying supported favorable account. Stone does not join that application; the four opposing Associates reject it. It is not a Court holding.
- Part III vacates Brooks County's derivative no-deprivation judgment while preserving all separate Monell, attribution and causation requirements. Personal immunity does not resolve county liability. State-law severance/remand and the private company's position remain untouched. Stone and the four vacating Associates support this ruling.

Both federal judgments are vacated and remanded, 5–4. The Court's remand requires the correct merits and conduct-time notice inquiries and preserves independently sufficient grounds; it neither awards liability nor guarantees proceedings of a particular kind. There is no majority resolving Gonzalez's immunity on the entire qualifying account, and no Marks synthesis combines the incompatible applications. Stone's concurrence in part and in the judgment explains that limit. A shared dissent presents the four opposing Associates' particular nonprotection rationale and independent notice ground, without an automatic custody, names or public-protection exemption.

The Chief belongs to both vacatur coalitions and assigns the single lead, including the opinion speaking for the Court in part. Whole-lead eligibility requires supporting the four-Associate notice application as well as the five-vote holdings; a judgment-only or partial join cannot qualify. Counts and pool selection follow this fixed form.
