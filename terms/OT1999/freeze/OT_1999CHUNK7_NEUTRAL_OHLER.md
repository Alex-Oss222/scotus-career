## Verified record and question

The full [Ninth Circuit opinion, 169 F.3d1200](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/169/169.F3d.1200.98-50282.html), including its note, was read. Ohler was convicted of marijuana importation and possession with intent to distribute, sentenced to thirty months and three years' supervision, with a $200 assessment. The Government sought admission of a 1993 methamphetamine-possession conviction under both Rules404(b) and609(a)(1). The District Court rejected character-use admission and initially reserved impeachment; on the first morning of trial it ruled the conviction admissible to impeach if she testified.

Ohler testified after the prosecution rested, denied knowing marijuana was in her van, and described retrieving the van after it had been taken without permission. She disclosed the prior conviction on direct examination; the prosecutor asked on cross whether it was a felony, which she confirmed; on redirect she distinguished personal-use possession from distribution. The circuit held her direct disclosure waived appellate review and did not decide whether Rule609 balancing was erroneous. The granted question concerns timely objection to a definitive adverse ruling followed by defensive disclosure. That question is not the merits of admissibility and does not itself authorize a new trial.

The lower court relied on Williams and other circuit precedent, reasoning that the 1990 removal of Rule609's cross-examination-only wording recognized a previously permitted practice without deciding its effect on appellate waiver. It distinguished the contrary Fifth Circuit practice as grounded in older precedent, not the 1990 amendment. The fact that the Government moved first did not distinguish its controlling Williams rule. These are lower-court grounds, not an answer already established by the Court.

## Term-effective law

Rule609(a)(1), as amended in1990 and quoted in the lower opinion, permits impeachment of the accused by a qualifying felony only if probative value outweighs prejudicial effect to that accused. The rule's treatment of other witnesses and dishonesty crimes remains distinct. Removing the former cross-examination restriction permits direct disclosure; permission to disclose does not expressly answer waiver.

Rule103's applicable source is Pub.L.93-595 §1, January2,1975, 88Stat1930. Control verified the historical amendment sequence and supplied this objective recitation: original103(a) required an affected substantial right and a timely objection or motion to strike with a specific ground unless apparent; subsections(b),(c),(d) concerned the record of ruling, jury hearing and plain error. The definitive-ruling/no-renewal sentence added by the April17,2000 amendment was effective December1,2000. It was not operative for May22. No present-day wording or later committee discussion is imported. [Luce, 469U.S.38](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/469/38), was read in full including the Brennan concurrence and footnotes. Its holding requires the defendant to testify to preserve a Rule609 impeachment objection. It rests on knowing the actual testimony, use of the conviction and context for prejudice/harmlessness. It does not expressly decide defensive disclosure by a defendant who does testify. Justice Stevens did not participate in Luce; no join is attributed to him. O'Connor joined the Court. Brennan's concurrence, joined by Marshall, limits the ruling to that factual context and reserves broader constitutional/evidentiary applications; it is prior noncontrolling authority, not an current-Justice commitment.

## Supported opposing paths and remedial limits

* Preserve review: a party who timely objects, receives a definitive adverse ruling and then reduces the damage by revealing the expected impeachment does not thereby invite or consent to the ruling. Here the trial record contains her testimony and actual disclosure, unlike Luce. The strongest contrary argument is that her own introduction deprives the Government of its choice whether to use the conviction and changes the course of trial; the Court must decide whether that uncertainty defeats review or belongs in ordinary prejudice/harmlessness analysis.
* Bar review after introduction: a party normally cannot complain of evidence she herself introduced, and the tactical advantage of direct disclosure can carry the loss of an appellate challenge. The strongest contrary argument is that the adverse ruling already made that tactical choice necessary in practical terms, and the rule should not penalize mitigation while rewarding a needlessly damaging sequence. The actual prosecutor's felony question matters to this application but does not prove every unasked trial contingency.
* A narrow rule may confine preservation to timely objection, a definitive ruling and defensive disclosure; independent affirmative use, express consent, abandonment, tentative rulings and a defendant who never testifies remain distinct. A broad waiver rule must explain its relation to Olano and Rule103, rather than assume that every tactical choice is an intentional surrender of review.

If review is preserved, the Ninth Circuit can decide actual Rule609 admissibility and any harmless-error issue in ordinary proceedings. Preservation alone establishes neither evidentiary error nor reversible prejudice and does not automatically require a new trial. If review is barred, affirmance concerns that ground and does not endorse the unreviewed Rule609 merits.
