## Proposed common propositions supported by the independent commitments

**A. Express date agreement within counsel's authority.** When defense counsel affirmatively and without an established qualification agrees, before expiration, to a specified trial date outside the applicable Article III(a) period, that scheduling agreement binds the represented prisoner as to the agreed trial date. The compact does not require the prisoner's personal attendance, assent or waiver colloquy for this scheduling act. This is an allocation of authority over the timing of a contested trial, not a transfer to counsel of the accused's choices whether to plead guilty, surrender trial, waive counsel or take an appeal.

**B. Application and intentional assent.** Counsel's express January 9 agreement to May 1 suffices on this record. It is affirmative participation in selecting the date, not silence or an inferred intention from failure to invoke the compact. The law need not distinguish counsel who proposes May 1 from counsel who expressly accepts the same date. No separate statement naming Article III, demonstration of deadline-specific advice or personal authorization is required for this category of scheduling decision. The initial Article III request establishes the prisoner's default entitlement; it does not reserve every later scheduling choice to him. The April 17 motion preserves the legal challenge to the agreement's effect, but does not establish that the agreement was invalid or restore a dismissal entitlement defeated by the agreed date itself. This decides neither a general withdrawal rule nor a later, independently grounded request to alter the schedule.

**C. Consent is distinct from a court-imposed extension.** An express date agreement is not a finding of good cause. Article III(a) continues to require good cause shown in open court, with prisoner or counsel present, for a necessary or reasonable continuance under that proviso. Article VI(a) retains its separate inability-to-stand-trial predicate and judicial determination. Neither provision is established here as an independent ground; prosecutorial calendar convenience is not newly adjudged sufficient good cause. Permitting the represented defendant to agree to the date is compatible with a proviso governing judicial extensions because the proviso does not expressly displace the ordinary allocation of scheduling authority or forbid the represented party's particular agreement. The reference to prisoner **or counsel** supports counsel's recognized role, but does not by itself prove good cause or resolve waiver.

**D. Remedy.** Reverse the New York Court of Appeals' compact ground for dismissing the indictment and remand for proceedings consistent with the agreement's binding effect, including any genuinely remaining, properly available issues. Do not unconditionally reinstate the conviction or decide those issues. Article V(c) still requires dismissal with prejudice when its predicate of an applicable, unexcused timing violation is satisfied; no defense-impairment requirement is added. This agreement defeats this ground for dismissal without modifying the remedy for a violation that actually exists.

These propositions describe particular-agreement waiver, not a universal statutory-waiver presumption. They do not hold that counsel can surrender every compact protection or waive every personal right. They create no mandatory waiver hearing, advice formula, new burden of proof, bad-faith condition, universal estoppel rule or harmlessness defense. The reference to unqualified assent describes the supplied record; it is not a newly prescribed evidentiary test. A supported challenge involving coercion, actual qualification, misunderstanding of the date, counsel's authority or ineffective assistance would have to be resolved under its own law. This case does not find any such predicate or decide its outcome.

## Enhanced alternative-path comparison

**Path A — enforce the express date agreement.** Taylor v. Illinois, 484 U.S. 400, 417–418 (1988), supplies counsel's authority over trial management while preserving personal basic choices. Jones v. Barnes, 463 U.S. 745, 751–754 (1983), likewise distinguishes professional management from the accused's ultimate personal decisions. Brookhart v. Janis, 384 U.S. 1, 7–8 (1966), supplies the limit where counsel effectively surrenders trial despite the accused's contrary choice; no equivalent surrender or contemporaneous personal objection is established here. Applying that division to the concrete date agreement provides the necessary independent basis for who may consent. Mezzanatto's limited consent holding is then corroboration, not the source of counsel's authority.

The strongest objection is that Hill personally invoked an explicit, mandatory deadline and Congress provided qualified extensions; accepting a prosecutor's proposed date without proof of IAD awareness might circumvent both the invoked right and the good-cause condition. Path A answers that the enacted period remains mandatory absent a legally effective agreement or statutory exception, while neither the timing command nor the continuance proviso makes this management choice exclusively personal or prohibits specific consent. Counsel intentionally selected the very date now challenged. For this category, the agreement supplies the operative assent without a separate surrender-of-legal-rights ceremony. That classification must be stated; the label “statutory” alone cannot do the work.

**Path B — affirm because relinquishment was not adequately shown.** The strongest version preserves possible counsel waiver but requires some indication that this agreement knowingly surrendered the already invoked IAD deadline. It draws support from Olano's intentional-relinquishment distinction, Fex's fidelity to the compact's enacted conditions, and the actual informed agreements in Mezzanatto and Libretti. If this path establishes an expired period without waiver, continuance or tolling, Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice; lack of proved trial prejudice cannot save the prosecution.

Its strongest difficulty is that it adds a deadline-specific assent requirement to an otherwise ordinary express scheduling agreement without a textual instruction making the calendaring choice personal. Olano preserves right-specific requirements; it does not classify this IAD choice as a personal trial surrender. Mezzanatto and Libretti did not adjudicate counsel's power over dates. Treating the absence of their different assent facts as indispensable here would import their categories rather than apply them. A categorical personal-waiver rule is weaker still: Brookhart's effective guilty plea and actual protest are missing, and Hill still receives a contested trial. Each Associate below rejects Path B on this supplied record for the individual grounds given; no contrary path is treated as frivolous or procedurally barred.

## Coalition compatibility and precedent treatment

The eight individual commitments support the same judgment ground and the same operative propositions **A–D**, rather than merely converging on a result through incompatible rationales. The qualifications concerning other compact protections, personal basic choices and statutory predicates are within the common ground. Souter's and Ginsburg's informed-relinquishment concerns do not create a separate personal-assent requirement for this counsel-managed choice. No new exception or proof burden is needed to obtain their joins. A later opinion adopting a broad statutory-waiver presumption, waiver from silence, a personal-knowledge finding, a good-cause finding, or a prejudice condition would exceed these frozen commitments and require renewed compatibility analysis.

- **Taylor** is applied to counsel's management authority and extended to this express trial-date agreement; its protection of basic personal choices and its distinct discovery-misconduct facts remain intact.
- **Jones** supplies the professional-management/personal-choice distinction, without converting its list of personal choices into an exhaustive catalogue.
- **Brookhart** preserves the prohibition on counsel's effectively surrendering trial contrary to the accused's personal choice; neither its guilty-plea equivalent nor an expressed personal objection is found here.
- **Fex** retains actual receipt by both statutory recipients, the 180-day period, the separate continuance and inability provisions, and Article V(c)'s dismissal requirement. It did not resolve counsel's authority; this case adds that proposition rather than revising its trigger.
- **Olano** retains intentional relinquishment versus inaction and right-specific waiver requirements. No forfeited-error standard, burden or prejudice analysis enters this preserved compact issue.
- **Mezzanatto** remains its limited knowing, voluntary impeachment-waiver holding. It corroborates enforcement of actual lawful consent after counsel's authority is independently established; it supplies no universal statutory-waiver default.
- **Godinez** retains the distinction between mental capacity and actual informed waiver of plea and counsel rights. Neither competence nor representation proves a personal waiver here; no such personal waiver is necessary for the scheduling act.
- **Libretti** retains the statutory-authority limit and its precise waiver holding and joins. An agreement cannot create unauthorized punishment or replace an enacted substantive predicate. The different personal-assent record there is not a universal ceremony for counsel's calendaring decisions.
- **Reed** retains its assumed Article IV violation and collateral-review limit. It supplies no merits resolution, knowing-waiver finding, valid-continuance finding or defense-impairment condition for Hill's direct Article III claim.
