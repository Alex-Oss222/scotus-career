## Corrected factual and procedural premise

The State argued that Robbins failed to preserve the law-library issue by objecting at trial. The lower federal courts did not address that argument when identifying possible appellate issues. The argument is not a finding that no objection occurred, a determination that a state rule forecloses review, or an adjudicated federal habeas procedural default. The trial-counsel-waiver-withdrawal issue remains distinct and is not established as waived by this passage.

The lower courts' identification of issues that might arguably support an appeal is not an independent present finding that Robbins possessed an available nonfrivolous ground after all preservation and legal-scope questions are answered. Nor does it establish likely success, deficient counsel performance or prejudice. The original phrases describing this as **“this nonfrivolous appeal”** and referring to **“two supported arguable grounds”** as an established predicate for presumed prejudice are superseded to that extent. The accurate premise is a lower-court identification of possible grounds with the newly identified preservation objection unresolved. No Associate makes a new finding of actual nonfrivolity.

## Effect on procedure and remedial paths

The procedure-wide division is unchanged. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas accept Wende's full safeguards; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find its omission of counsel's legal-issue assistance inadequate. All eight permit genuinely equivalent alternatives to Anders. The preserved facts that counsel reviewed the record, supplied a cited narrative, remained appointed and available, and actually consulted the appellate project remain fully incorporated. The addition is no reason to pretend counsel literally withdrew.

**The four accepting Wende:** Reverse the categorical Anders basis for the conditional writ and remand for the unresolved individual Strickland claim and other trial claims. Whether an allegedly missed issue was legally available, including the State's library-preservation contention and its proper treatment under applicable law, bears on performance and prejudice where material. No preservation defect is presumed and no amendment-era habeas restriction is imported contrary to Lindh. The ordinary reasonable-probability question concerns the appellate outcome with adequate advocacy, not an additional Fretwell unfairness requirement. Neither possible ground is now found frivolous or meritorious.

**The four rejecting Wende:** Preserve the conditional entitlement to an appeal with constitutionally adequate counsel-assisted screening, and the existing remand for the other trial claims. Their ground is the procedure's omission of the legal assistance needed to determine frivolousness in the first place, as protected by Anders and the purposes explained in McCoy and Penson. It is not a present finding that either identified trial issue would succeed or is conclusively nonfrivolous. Penson's fact that arguable issues had actually been found is not imported as an established fact here.

For these four, repairing that procedural deprivation does not require Robbins to prove beforehand the legal grounds that the missing advocate-assisted process should identify and assess. The appropriate new appellate process may itself determine that all available issues are frivolous after counsel performs the constitutionally required role; no lawyer must argue a frivolous claim, and no merits briefing on a ground unavailable under valid preservation law is ordered. The State's library objection must receive its proper treatment if that issue is relied upon. The existing conditional new-appeal relief is understood at this bounded procedural scope, not as an award of a winning appeal, an unconditional merits brief on both suggested grounds or immediate release. The other trial claims may make such appellate process unnecessary if they independently afford appropriate relief.

This specifies the basis of the four procedure-side commitments after removal of the overstatement concerning actual nonfrivolity. They continue to treat the absence of the required advocacy at the screening stage as a structural procedural deprivation for the limited corrective process, rather than a Strickland showing that a particular omitted issue probably would have succeeded. The strongest opposing argument remains substantial: retained counsel's actual review and factual work mean assistance was present, so any error should receive ordinary performance/prejudice review. The four sustain their original substantive judgment that the missing legal assistance is essential to this screening, rather than treating formal retention as sufficient. That disagreement must remain visible in reconciliation; it is not resolved by the neutral addition.
