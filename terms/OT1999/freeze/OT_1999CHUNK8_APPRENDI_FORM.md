# Apprendi v. New Jersey — form and governing propositions

**Event:** No. 99-478; plenary merits decision, June 26, 2000; argued March 28, 2000.

**Form fixed before counts or author selection:** One signed Opinion of the Court. This case requires a new constitutional answer to the jury-allocation and proof question expressly reserved by the earlier statutory decisions. A single opinion can state the common present-offense rule, apply it to Count 18, and preserve the state-charging, prior-conviction and sentencing-system reservations. No component division or per curiam treatment is warranted by the legal work.

**Tentative judgment coalition:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg reverse the New Jersey judgment insofar as it sustains the enhanced Count 18 sentence, vacate that sentence and remand for lawful disposition. O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer would affirm against the presented federal allocation/proof challenge. All nine participate. The six supporters share the same governing propositions at the following scope.

**Governing rule and qualifications:** Under the Sixth Amendment's jury guarantee, applied through the Fourteenth Amendment, and the Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause, a fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to authorize punishment above the maximum supported by the verdict or plea is an element: it must be admitted as an element or proved to a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. The decision does not decide state charging requirements, incorporate the federal grand-jury clause, or decide prior-conviction facts. The Court preserves ordinary judicial factfinding within existing punishment authority and does not decide unchanged-maximum minima, consecutive-sentence rules, capital selection or materially different sentencing systems. Any distinct valid-waiver question remains governed by applicable waiver law; the reserved claim here was not waived.

**Application and remedy:** The admitted Count 18 firearm offense supports no more than ten years. The disputed racial-purpose finding, made by the judge under a preponderance standard, cannot authorize its twelve-year sentence. Potential aggregate punishment on other counts supplies no missing authority. The existing pleas and convictions remain; a sentence within the admitted range is permissible, while any further proceeding depends on state law and applicable plea, notice, waiver, sufficiency and double-jeopardy constraints. No new prosecution or enhancement jury is ordered, and neither the aggregate sentence nor the distinct Graves Act question is decided. No universal structural-error classification or automatic application of Neder is adopted.

**Compatibility and distinct writings:** The common present-offense formulation is supported by all five frozen reconciled reversal commitments and by Stone's express discretion envelope. It adds no Associate vote or ground. Broader individual positions of Stevens, Scalia and Thomas remain noncontrolling; Thomas's newly reached broader view must not be suppressed or attributed retroactively to his prior statutory joins. The contrary legislative-allocation position and Breyer's distinct sentencing-administration objection warrant substantive dissent accounts. Precise authorship and joins will follow the frozen grounds and final compatibility, not historical authorship.
