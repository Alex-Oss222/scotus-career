# Mitchell - legal form before assignment count

One signed plenary lead opinion will state the common actual-funded-function violation, the limits on identity or equipment-category reasoning, and preservation of lawful severable secular applications in Parts I-III. All nine support that substantive core. Part IV vacates the reviewed categorical federal equipment/library-book declaration and remands for confined effective relief against the established use, with eight Associate joins. Stone's approved vote instead affirms invalidation at the as-administered scope and is not silently converted to vacatur; he concurs in I-III and dissents from the formal remedial disposition in IV. No broad categorical prohibition is attributed to him.

There is one formal appellate judgment on the reviewed categorical declaration; agreement on the constitutional predicate does not create a separate decree or separable remedial disposition. The complete signed lead can be carried by any of the eight Associates. Stone's partial join does not qualify him for the entire lead. The eight-Justice vacatur coalition's most senior member, Stevens, assigns; agreement on a substantive proposition does not confer assignment of a disposition Stone opposes. A single lead has eligible authors for every portion, so no division of authorship is justified. Assignment counts and author selection follow only after the earlier Hill Record is written.

The common rule applies the actual Agostini/Jackson/Strout funded-function law without converting private schooling into government speech, erasing genuinely individual benefits, reviving independent old Lemon prongs, or demanding a new monitoring system. Meek and Wolman's conclusive equipment-category reasoning is displaced only at its conflicting scope. The established actual-use fact is not remanded for reproof; its provider/item/application extent and lawful severability remain for effective relief. These legal scope and form choices do not depend on the counts.
