## Operative rules and preserved distinctions

The five affirming Associates hold that section 207(o)(5) protects an employee's request for use within a reasonable period absent undue disruption; it does not make requests the exclusive method of consuming validly accrued time. Paragraph (o)(2)'s agreement condition governs time substituted for cash. Neither provision categorically requires a separate term authorizing compulsory paid use. Actual lawful restrictions in an agreement remain enforceable at their proper scope; the absence of an identified controlling provision is not a finding that no agreements exist or every employee consented. No general employer-control presumption is adopted.

The four-Associate deference ground confines this advisory letter to persuasive consideration because its supported status lacks force of law. It does not disregard its reasoning by label, make every informal agency statement irrelevant, or replace Auer's own-regulation inquiry with Chevron's statutory one. The five-Associate regulatory ground reads section 553.23(a)(2)'s permission for additional agreement terms as permission, not a requirement that every compelled-use policy be expressly authorized. Auer remains controlling for a responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation within the text; an unambiguous permission cannot be amended by interpretation. Souter's future-regulation reservation is express: no conclusion here establishes that the Secretary lacks power to adopt a valid new rule limiting forced use.

The three opposing Associates treat the statutory arrangement as a consensual exception to ordinary cash overtime, with the agreement governing compulsory consumption. Cash redemption and paid absence are different transactions; a cashout power does not itself establish forced-use authority. Their remand applies the actual earlier-hire practice and later-hire agreement routes. It does not require negotiation of every optional provision, identical individual agreements, or a general collective-bargaining right.
