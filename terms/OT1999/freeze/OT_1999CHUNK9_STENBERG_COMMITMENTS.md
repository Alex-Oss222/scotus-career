## Common scope and enacted distinctions

All eight Associates reach the granted construction/access and health questions through certiorari. The petition's additional fetal-personhood and wholesale-reconsideration questions do not become granted issues. No source supports a new jurisdictional, preservation or participation obstacle. The independent county-attorney fee appeal is not newly adjudicated here.

LB23 defines an act and sequence, not merely a clinical label: deliberate and intentional vaginal delivery of a living fetus, or a substantial portion, for a procedure known to cause and actually causing death before delivery is completed. The living-fetus element matters where demise precedes removal. The statutory definition has no viability limit, but this case's protection concerns Carhart's methods on nonviable fetuses. Intentional and knowing prohibited performance is a Class III felony. The woman may not be prosecuted under the section or for conspiracy to violate it; that immunity is not a physician defense.

The substantive exception is necessity to save the mother's life endangered by physical disorder, illness or injury, including a life-endangering physical condition caused by or arising from pregnancy. The broader emergency definition in section 2 is not incorporated into section 3's separate prohibition by implication. No general health exception, Ohio alternative-method defense, physician-preference exemption or Nebraska judicial narrowing is invented.

Section 3(4) directs suspension and revocation by the Director of Regulation and Licensure for intentional and knowing unlawful performance, pursuant to the specified professional-discipline statutes. Section 3(5) requires the Attorney General to petition for suspension and revocation when either the Attorney General or a county attorney files criminal charges; it requires a section 71-153 hearing and an opportunity to present the specified life-necessity evidence. A defendant may move to delay trial until the Director's section 71-155 order; filing the motion does not automatically delay trial. The Director's life-necessity findings are admissible, not conclusively binding. This procedure is neither prior permission for treatment nor a health defense.

The amended unprofessional-conduct rule retains intentional/knowing performance and the same life exception. Section 71-155 provides the described summary equity proceeding, discretionary affidavit receipt, subpoena and deposition procedures, required life-necessity findings in these proceedings and enumerated general sanction powers. Optional consultation with the board after guilt requires disclosure and an opportunity to respond. The relation between those general powers and section 3(4)'s mandatory language need not be newly resolved here. Section 71-171.02's separate referral options—a letter of concern, advice concerning a voluntary signed public assurance that is not an admission, or recommendation of a petition—are not a medical-board veto or new treatment defense. Section 4's employment-protection remedy is not an adjudicated patient-damages award. Section 8 preserves remaining valid portions; it authorizes neither rewriting the act definition nor enforcing a sanction dependent on an unconstitutional application.

## Governing framework and decisional components

The current complete Casey framework, applied in Fargo and Voinovich, requires: the actual duty and baseline; actual function and purpose; attributable interference against a stable affected population and a constant-law comparison; independent baseline, purpose and substantial-obstacle protection followed by residual severe and meaningful-interference review; clinical and communication safeguards; and violation-specific relief. Challengers establish duty, causation, affected population and triggering interference before the State bears the corresponding justification and fit burden. A law's abortion subject alone shifts no burden. No independent numerical facial threshold or no-valid-applications formula overrides that rule.

The components are (1) statutory reach and ordinary D&E access, (2) health and usable alternatives, (3) notice and actual fault, (4) purpose and residual justification where necessary, and (5) severability, sanctions and the actual injunction. Voinovich's act-defined Ohio provision, exclusions and criminal/civil defenses are materially different; its rejection of categorical notice invalidity is not substantive approval of Nebraska's act. A clear prohibition may be substantively unconstitutional; a serious sanction does not establish notice failure by itself. No Justice below treats a bare medical assertion as controlling or the absence of controlled comparative trials as automatic defeat of credible clinical findings.

The actual lower relief is an as-applied declaration and injunction for Carhart, his patients and similarly situated persons regarding his D&E and D&X methods on nonviable fetuses, against the defendants and those bound under Rule 65(d), with the specified county-attorney service. The District Court expressly declined whole-statute facial invalidation. An affirmance must retain that scope; it is not a nationwide, all-gestation or new whole-statute judgment.

## Justice-specific enhanced commitments

### Stevens

**Commitment.** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment and the existing bounded protection. His complete Casey, Fargo and Voinovich positions require practical review of the enacted prohibition. The definition's substantial-portion language reaches described ordinary D&E in which a living fetal part is deliberately drawn into the vagina before the known fatal dismemberment. Intentional performance does not remove those ordinary acts from the definition. The affected population is the previability patients dependent on the described D&E care, identified from the duty's operation, not all pregnancies or only eventual successful claimants. Removing a predominant and usable method through the criminal/licensing prohibition creates the demonstrated practical obstacle.

**Alternative and counterarguments.** A State-favoring intact-only construction has a serious contextual basis in the opening words, title and identified method concern. The enacted elaboration nevertheless includes a substantial portion and does not enact an intact-body or anatomical-landmark restriction that excludes the supported ordinary acts. The Attorney General's assurance is neither that missing text nor established binding control over every prosecutor. Voinovich does not require a remand whenever a State disputes a medical label: here the actual procedure evidence and affected method are developed. Conversely, overlap alone would not suffice without the actual acts and access consequences just identified.

**Health, fault, joins and remedy.** Separately supports necessary-health protection in the medically consequential intact-method circumstances the district findings establish; the life-only exception and post-charge hearing cannot protect those applications. He accepts the credited comparative clinical evidence without declaring universal superiority or a physician veto. The ordinary-D&E access ground is independently sufficient for affirmance even if the exact reach of this separate health ground is narrowed. No additional categorical vagueness or obstructive-purpose holding is necessary. Will join the complete framework, these grounded applications and the existing nonviable as-applied remedy; not an automatic inference of unlawful purpose from legislative opposition or a rule immunizing every good-faith choice. No independent writing is needed if a common opinion carries those limits.

### O'Connor

**Commitment.** Affirm the bounded judgment on the actual ordinary-D&E reach and resulting obstacle. Her full Casey/Fargo/Voinovich joins distinguish reasonable method regulation from an enacted restriction that deprives the affected patients of practical timely, clinically appropriate care. The supported sequence in ordinary D&E can satisfy the definition, and the State's intent argument does not exclude intentionally performed ordinary steps. The meaningful clinical and access evidence supplies the trigger; she does not impose justification merely because the statute regulates abortion.

**Alternative and counterarguments.** The strongest alternative confines the law to a distinct intact procedure and preserves usable alternatives. A genuinely operative narrow definition with necessary-care protection could materially change the inquiry, and Voinovich forbids automatic invalidation from overlapping labels. But the actual Nebraska text contains no reliable distinction sufficient to exclude the described ordinary acts, no Ohio alternative-method protection, and no independently usable health defense. Federalism and avoidance permit a construction the words bear, not insertion of the critical omitted boundary. A nonbinding assurance cannot eliminate the exposure on which the practical burden rests.

**Health, notice, joins and relief.** Independently supports protection for the proved intact-method applications in which the credited evidence establishes clinically consequential risk from the prohibited substitution. She does not regard any incremental preference as constitutionally necessary or require an unrestricted health exception in every conceivable method law. The existing life criterion, however, does not permit the relevant nonfatal necessary care, and the licensing hearing does not expand it. No independent categorical notice holding is needed; scienter and substantive adequate care remain separate. Will join a common opinion preserving the possibility of lawful regulation of a genuinely distinct procedure and relief confined to this developed nonviable record. No new statewide severance, damages or fees ruling; no separate writing needed if those qualifications are explicit.

### Scalia

**Commitment.** Reverse the constitutional judgment within the granted method/access and health questions and remand for removal of the corresponding constitutional injunction. He retains his Voinovich and Fargo objection to Casey's heightened abortion framework and its constitutional necessary-health requirement; he accurately acknowledges those as the law from which he dissents, rather than silently replacing them in a purported Court holding. The State's potential-life and professional-ethics objectives support this act-defined restriction under his reasonable-regulation account.

**Alternative and counterarguments.** Affirmance is supported by the substantial-portion wording, its possible application to ordinary D&E and the credited risk differences. Those are genuine objections and cannot be answered simply by invoking fault. He nevertheless reads the partial-delivery language, completion sequence and purpose in context as directed to the distinctive intact procedure, without making every isolated fetal-part extraction conclusive. He does not treat the Attorney General's litigation assertion as binding authority; his position rests on his construction and his constitutional disagreement. Even if the statute has some broader application, his rejection of the asserted abortion right prevents that access argument from establishing the claimed constitutional injunction on his ground.

**Health, notice and relief.** Credited comparative advantages do not, under his constitutional position, require the State to allow every procedure judged safer, and he does not adopt Casey's independent health baseline. The life exception and actual licensing process remain as enacted; no automatic hearing stay or conclusive defense is invented. His Voinovich notice join prevents treating overlap of labels or the absence of an express fault term for every subsidiary judgment as sufficient categorical invalidity. He would reject that categorical notice ground here without determining every future clinical-error prosecution. Will join a dissent accurately stating these distinct grounds; not a claim that all methods have equal safety or that the existing law lacks Casey protection. Ordinary state-law severability remains relevant, but his reversal supplies no new liability, prosecution or damages judgment.

### Kennedy

**Commitment.** Vacate the judgment sustaining the method injunction and remand for a bounded necessary-care application and corresponding relief, without automatic dissolution or blanket validation. He would not affirm the ordinary-D&E ground. His Voinovich position permits reasonable regulation of a distinct method while retaining Casey's protected-choice and necessary-life-and-health baseline, and joins the lawful protective-construction inquiry. The partial vaginal delivery, living-child and completion sequence, read together, permit the State's narrower focus on the intact procedure rather than treating every extraction of a limb as the prohibited act. This is his independent construction, not a claim that the Attorney General has already bound all prosecutors or that Nebraska enacted an anatomical-landmark test.

**Alternative and counterarguments.** Affirmance's strongest point is that the elaborating definition expressly includes a substantial portion, and ordinary D&E intentionally performs acts that may fit its literal terms. He regards the complete sequence and statutory context as sufficient to avoid that broad interpretation, consistent with Voinovich's act-focused analysis; he would not simply adopt the clinical label as the rule. Outright reversal and validation has a different serious defect: Nebraska lacks the actual alternative-method health defense material to his Voinovich method position. A post-charge life hearing does not supply it. His prior approval of reasonable regulation therefore cannot dispose of this record as though Ohio's defense were present.

**Necessary care and source limits.** He accepts the district's credited findings of comparative reductions in the identified mechanical and surgical risks; lack of controlled trials does not erase them. He does not consider those comparative advantages, without further application of the legal necessity criterion, sufficient to decide that every intact-method use covered by the present injunction is required to preserve timely safe and suitable care. The relevant question is which supported applications make the remaining alternatives clinically inadequate under his retained necessary-care baseline, rather than simply less preferred. No new clinical fact is supplied. The lower court may decide that question on its existing record if sufficient; a new hearing or a new physician submission is not automatic.

**Joins and remedy.** Will join correct notice/fault distinctions, the required necessary-care baseline and the enacted-process account. Will not join the full six-step residual lesser-burden rationale, a categorical declaration that professional disagreement requires permission for every use, or immediate blanket enforcement despite the missing health protection. The remand must address each independently supported health application and the sanctions dependent on it, under Nebraska's actual severability direction and ordinary interim standards; existing protection is not dissolved automatically while that inquiry is undertaken. Will write a separate position explaining his different construction, retained health requirement and different disposition if necessary. This is a present vacatur/remand commitment, not a contingent vote for ultimate validation.

### Souter

**Commitment.** Affirm the existing bounded protection. His own Fargo and Voinovich opinions require separating notice, culpability, substantive access and remedy. Nebraska's ordinary-D&E defect is substantive: supported deliberate delivery of a living fetal part followed by known fatal completion can meet the enacted substantial-portion definition. A legislature's choice of a descriptive label does not exclude that actual operation. The affected D&E patients face a demonstrated loss of a predominant usable form of previability care; the finding is neither a numerical facial shortcut nor a speculation based on labels alone.

**Alternative and counterarguments.** The State's strongest argument invokes context and avoidance to confine the law to intact delivery. That route was materially supported in the different Ohio act-definition and defense setting, but the present substantial-portion elaboration and lack of an enacted excluding boundary prevent the asserted assurance from resolving exposure here. No binding Nebraska construction or universal county-prosecutor undertaking is established. Conversely, a categorical vagueness conclusion would repeat the error his Voinovich opinion rejected and is unnecessary; intelligibility does not validate a clearly described restriction on protected care.

**Health, joins and relief.** Independently supports necessary-care protection for the clinically supported intact-method circumstances, respecting the district's credited comparative evidence and its limited recurring scope. The life-only exception is not a timely health safeguard, and later admissible administrative findings on life necessity are no substitute. He neither declares the method always safest nor imports Voinovich's mental-health criterion, Ohio defenses or Ohio severability law into Nebraska. Will join complete Casey application and affirmance of the actual nonviable as-applied injunction, with dependent penalties unenforceable at that same scope. No additional purpose, universal fault or whole-statute vagueness holding is required. Willing to join a complete common opinion; a separate writing is unnecessary.

### Thomas

**Commitment.** Reverse the constitutional judgment at the granted scope and remand for removal of the corresponding injunction. His Voinovich separate-position join preserves the continuing rejection of the constitutional abortion and necessary-health requirements while accepting ordinary intelligibility and severability analysis. Under his own reasonable-regulation position, the State may enact this method restriction for potential-life and medical-ethics purposes; the present grant does not require a new Court-wide reconsideration of fetal personhood or all of Casey.

**Alternative and counterarguments.** Affirmance has substantial support under the controlling framework because the broad elaboration can reach standard procedures and the district credited real clinical differences. He does not claim those facts disappear. He favors the contextual intact-procedure construction for the partial-delivery and completion sequence rather than reading each occurrence of an extracted limb as decisive. More fundamentally, his constitutional disagreement prevents the asserted access and absent-health-exception arguments from supporting the injunction under his own rule. He recognizes that this is a dissent from current law, not a finding that current law contains no health baseline.

**Notice, joins and relief.** Will join rejection of categorical notice invalidity from overlap or from absence of express culpability for each clinical judgment, without an invented knowledge-of-illegality requirement or universal good-faith immunity. The intentional/knowing conditions, life exception, patient immunity and licensing procedures remain distinct. He rejects a constitutional requirement to allow the method merely because credible testimony supports comparative advantages, without finding every alternative equally safe. Will join a substantive dissent stating these grounds and ordinary severability limits. No fee, prosecution, civil award or automatic license order is entered by his proposed judgment; those would require the applicable actual proceeding.

### Ginsburg

**Commitment.** Affirm the Eighth Circuit's judgment and the existing limited injunction. Her full Fargo and Voinovich joins apply the complete Casey standard rather than ending the inquiry at a legislative method label. The enacted sequence and substantial-portion phrase reach the described ordinary D&E acts; criminal and licensing consequences then impose the proved obstacle on the duty-defined previability population using that care. An exception limited to threats to life does not preserve the ordinary protected decision or all necessary health care.

**Alternative and counterarguments.** The strongest State-favoring route combines contextual narrowing with the existence of alternatives and legitimate medical-ethics regulation. Those interests deserve their own assessment, but a nonbinding assurance and a hoped-for narrower administration do not establish that ordinary D&E remains legally and practically usable. Likewise, the existence of another method in general does not show that it is clinically usable for the affected patient. Voinovich requires this comparison; it does not command another remand where the relevant actual conduct and medical findings have been developed.

**Health, fault, joins and relief.** Independently supports the existing protection for the identified intact-method circumstances in which the credited findings show medically consequential risk from the barred alternative choice. She accepts the findings' limited scope and treats professional disagreement as evidence, not a legislative or physician veto. The administrative opportunity to show life necessity cannot make nonfatal necessary treatment lawful or erase the upfront prohibition. Will join the complete framework, actual coverage/access and bounded health reasoning, and violation-matched relief. No inference of prohibited purpose from legislative disapproval alone and no independent categorical vagueness decision is needed. Preserve independently lawful provisions and patient immunity; decide no viable-fetus application or ungranted fee dispute. No separate writing is required if these qualifications are carried.

### Breyer

**Commitment.** Affirm the existing as-applied protection. His complete Fargo and Voinovich joins require actual procedure, care and alternative analysis rather than a categorical conclusion from medical terminology. Here the developed account of ordinary D&E supplies the deliberate act, living-fetus, knowledge and sequence connection to the substantial-portion definition. The additional prohibition and sanctions cause a practical obstacle for the supported population needing that established form of care. No newly invented percentage or whole-statute rule is needed.

**Alternative and counterarguments.** The strongest alternative would read the law as a precise ban of a distinct intact technique and ask whether other usable methods preserve care. The words and supplied record do not supply that precision merely from the title or the State's promise. Nor do intentional/knowing conditions distinguish an intentionally performed ordinary D&E when its steps meet the definition. A statutory construction that genuinely removed that reach would require separate consideration of actual risk and alternatives, not automatic validation. His support for Voinovich's remand was an answer to categorical reasoning and materially different defenses; it is not a standing preference to remand every method case.

**Health, joins and remedy.** Independently supports the limited necessary-health protection grounded in the district's credited recurring circumstances, including the identified reductions in instrumentation and surgical risks; he adopts no new numerical risk comparison, condition or universal superiority finding. Medical disagreement and absence of controlled trials do not justify replacing those findings with an assertion that all alternatives are adequate. The life-only hearing does not supply substantive permission for the necessary nonfatal care. Will join complete Casey application, the actual ordinary-D&E ground and carefully confined clinical protection, while leaving unnecessary notice and purpose holdings aside. Affirmance retains the existing nonviable scope and dependent-sanction limits; no new hearing, nationwide injunction, damages or broad severance is ordered. No independent writing is required if the common opinion fully states those boundaries.
