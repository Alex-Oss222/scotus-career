## What the addition establishes

The State argued that relief would require a new rule not clearly entailed by earlier holdings and was therefore barred by Teague. That is an attributed defense, not a finding that the rule is new, that the defense was consistently preserved, that it is fairly included in this review, or that an exception is unavailable. Its inclusion corrects the earlier neutral account's omission of an affirmative argument. The omission from that account is not itself litigation waiver or grounds to disregard the argument.

This defense is separate from the State's argument that Robbins failed to preserve the law-library issue at trial. It is also separate from whether Wende provides constitutionally sufficient appellate assistance, whether this lawyer performed deficiently, and whether the applicable prejudice requirement is satisfied. Neither the new addition nor its quoted formulation decides those questions.

## Actual opening law

The following complete source sections were mechanically copied into `preparation/OT_1999CHUNK2_SMITH_TEAGUE_OPENING_LAW.md`: Standards and Tests, “Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review” and “Late assertion of Teague nonretroactivity”; and the criminal-procedure Holdings entries for Caspari v. Bohlen, decided February 23, 1994, and O'Dell v. Netherland, decided June 19, 1997. They state actual prior law, not a historical application of Teague to Smith.

Teague is nonjurisdictional. Schiro permits discretionary refusal of a defense first raised in merits briefing after the identified earlier omissions; it does not impose automatic waiver. Caspari distinguishes a consistently asserted subsidiary defense limiting application of the very constitutional rule and habeas relief under review. Preservation and fair inclusion remain distinct. The new excerpt alone proves neither of those complete procedural histories.

If the defense is considered, the new-rule inquiry concerns the relevant finality date and whether the protection sought was dictated by then-existing precedent. Under actual Caspari and its treatment of Graham, factual differences or disagreement among courts do not by themselves make an application new. Equally, invoking a general earlier principle does not without substantive comparison establish that the particular protection was dictated. The rule whose retroactive application is claimed must be identified at the relevant scope, rather than treating every possible ground or remedy in this case as one undifferentiated rule.

Both contemporary exceptions remain: rules excluding conduct or a class of persons or punishments from the State's substantive power, and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. Actual O'Dell applies the watershed exception to its specifically defined capital future-dangerousness and parole-information setting as an independent alternative; it neither abolishes that exception nor establishes that every appellate-assistance protection satisfies it. This receipt classifies no proposed Smith rule under either exception.

Lambrix preserves flexibility in sequencing accepted Teague and unresolved state-default questions. Neither defense creates subject-matter jurisdiction or permits bypass of an established jurisdictional defect. The law-library preservation argument must retain its own predicates and is not resolved by calling the separate Teague argument a threshold issue.
