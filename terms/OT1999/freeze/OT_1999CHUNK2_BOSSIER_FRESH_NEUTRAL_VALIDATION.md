## Existing mandate and return posture

Actual Bossier I establishes the existing-plan benchmark for retrogressive effect, rejects an independent section 2 violation as an automatic section 5 bar, and makes dilution evidence relevant but not conclusive on purpose. Its controlling purpose rule reaches unconstitutional discriminatory purpose beyond an intent to cause retrogression. Any source statement that the earlier Court left that broader question undecided is not the law of this proceeding.

The parties do not now dispute the finding of no retrogressive intent. That finding does not answer the broader unconstitutional-purpose question required by the actual mandate. The 1998 district court again used the narrow retrogression inquiry, leaving the broader purpose issue unresolved. The model must confront that mismatch rather than repeat the narrow historical premise.

The district court's findings that prompt preclearance and ease of implementation were legitimate reasons for selecting the Police Jury plan are not a distinct finding that the Board would have adopted the identical plan absent a racial purpose. The actual earlier decision adopted no same-decision defense or new burden-shifting formulation. Whether and how Arlington Heights/Mt. ## Balanced factual record

The Police Jury plan used twelve single-member districts in a parish approximately twenty percent Black, with no majority-Black district; the Attorney General had precleared that plan for the Police Jury. The Board adopted it on October 1, 1992. Nonretrogression is conceded. Neither the concession nor that earlier approval proves the Board's purpose in choosing its own plan.

The source includes stipulated longstanding resistance to school desegregation, the history of the advisory committee, delay and response to the NAACP proposal, departures from ordinary districting considerations, and the possibility of requesting precinct changes. These matters may bear on purpose but are not individually conclusive. Preserve the attributed Burns statement and Musgrove's denial as contested evidence, not a single undisputed admission. The Board's administrative explanations and concerns about precinct splits must also remain available for assessment.

The existence of a feasible two-majority-Black-district alternative does not by itself establish section 2 liability or discriminatory purpose. The relative precinct-split counts and the Board's ability to request changes matter to the credibility and weight of its explanation. Voting-polarization evidence came with limited-data qualifications. Black candidates' success in other offices must be evaluated with the source's Air Force base and effective-electorate qualifications, rather than treated as conclusive proof either way. Admissions concerning dilutive effect remain distinct from a finding of intentional discrimination.

## Live controversy and remedy limits

The Board's assertion that the plan will never be used again is contested. The record identifies the completed 1998 election and the expectation of a post-census plan before the next regular election, along with possible vacancy elections, failure to secure a replacement plan, and a request for a special election. Those contingencies are arguments about continuing stakes; no entitlement to a special election or future failure of redistricting is established. The continuing benchmark consequences also require legal analysis. Mootness must be decided from the actual legal effect and remaining controversy, not from a bare assertion that the ordinary election has passed.
