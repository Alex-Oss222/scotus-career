## Proposed judgment and separable propositions

All eight Associates provisionally affirm the Third Circuit's reversal of the municipal dismissal, within the two granted questions, and remand for ordinary proceedings. The votes rest on the separate bases below. This permits the municipal claim to proceed; it establishes neither liability nor a right to trial. It does not independently review all personal-capacity dispositions or decide related state claims. The articulated Rule 54(b) certification and discernible separation of the immunity issues permit this review; no broader certification holding is necessary.

**A. State arm and local policy identity.** The Pennsylvania DA office is not an arm of the Commonwealth for these office training, supervision and administrative policies. Its legal and financial structure includes local office status, local selection and fiscal responsibility, and independently exercised ordinary office authority. The Commonwealth's treasury is not the judgment obligor. State law enforcement and the Commonwealth's name on prosecutions do not alone confer sovereign immunity. Treasury exposure is highly probative, not an indispensable universal test; Regents' collateral reimbursement rule does not convert a locally responsible entity into a State arm.

The DA supplies final local policy for the challenged training and supervision of the DA's own prosecutors. Philadelphia's City/County consolidation and the legally constituted independent local office make the attribution possible without delegation by the City Council. The inquiry concerns the entity represented for the particular policy, not merely operational discretion. Pennsylvania's county-office classification must be read with the constitutional home-rule/optional-government exception and Philadelphia's consolidation, and with Specter and Duggan. Chalfin's City-officer result does not turn all of Chief Justice Bell's plurality reasoning into a holding. Under Commonwealth Attorneys Act §§205–206, the Attorney General's statewide role and specified request, conflict, insufficiency and judicial-intervention powers do not establish ordinary control of the DA's personnel training. Section 205(a)(4)'s supplied repeal annotation is not resolved beyond what the office-structure question requires. No all-functions classification of prosecutors or all-officials classification of Pennsylvania government is adopted.

**B. Omission liability and causation.** A constitutional injury is necessary but insufficient. Carter must establish an attributable local policy or custom, a deliberately indifferent training or supervision omission, and the requisite causal connection between that particular omission and his constitutional injury. A failure to train must represent a conscious municipal choice in the face of actual or constructive notice of the relevant constitutional danger; negligence, an employee's isolated error, general supervisory responsibility and the hindsight claim that additional training might have helped are insufficient. The identified deficiency must be closely related to and actually cause the deprivation; mere employment and a bare opportunity for intervention are not enough.

Canton permits notice from a sufficiently established pattern or from a sufficiently obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in recurring circumstances that policymakers know employees will face. It does not invariably require a prior identical incident. Perjury's obvious wrongfulness does not itself show an obvious need for specialized training against it. A known pattern of fabrication, use of false testimony or related misconduct could establish a materially different omission claim. Carter's conviction, later reversal, the witness's account and Ryan's conviction do not themselves establish notice, a policy, or causation. A policy concerning prosecutors also does not make police their subordinates: any theory premised on failure to train or control police requires legally and factually supported authority and causal connection. The disputed exculpatory-evidence allegation must be assessed as the alleged conduct and constitutional duty actually presented, not treated as proved or assimilated automatically to the perjury theory.

Walker may help explain how notice and causation operate, but its three-part formulation is not independently adopted as a new Supreme Court test. The elements are not finally established because the lower court characterized some Walker factors as readily satisfied. Their factual application remains open.

**C. Pleading, distinct defenses and remedy.** Rule 8 does not require Carter to plead unknown internal training policies with heightened particularity before discovery. The described allegations of a policy-related pattern, knowledge and omission suffice to avoid dismissal on the categorical grounds decided below. Their truth, specificity after discovery and legal sufficiency under an appropriate evidentiary record remain contestable. The City can obtain ordinary discovery limits and summary judgment if the required deprivation, policy, fault or causal showing fails. The Court neither orders discovery of an unlimited scope nor guarantees a trial, damages, a new prosecution or particular employment practices.

Municipal responsibility cannot borrow an officer's personal absolute or qualified immunity. Conversely, an allegedly deliberately indifferent municipal policy does not automatically defeat an individual's conduct-date qualified immunity. Buckley and Kalina require a functional inquiry: genuine advocacy retains its protection despite alleged dishonesty, while distinct investigative, administrative and personal factual-oath acts have the protection their actual function warrants. There is no dishonesty exception to advocacy immunity. These propositions prevent confusion on remand; the grant does not require a new disposition of every personal claim, a finding that every challenged act was administrative, or a ruling on individual-immunity pleading.

## Join scope, alternatives and unresolved facts

The eight Associates support A, B and C at the stated generality and the limited affirmance/remand. No separate-writing demand is committed merely to generate different votes. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas require that the opinion expressly reject an inference of municipal fault from criminal wrongdoing alone and preserve the stringent causal inquiry. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also require those limits; their support for permitting factual development supplies no lower substantive threshold. All refuse an automatic qualified-immunity forfeiture, all-functions State/local classification, heightened municipal pleading rule, unconditional entitlement to trial, or present damages judgment.

A narrower lawful decision could affirm the removal of sovereign immunity and remand the final-policy identification for application of McMillian if the office's legal authority were materially unresolved. On the supplied Pennsylvania sources that additional remand is unnecessary for training of the office's own prosecutors; authority over police is materially different and remains open. A broader ruling classifying every decision of the DA, announcing a new §1983 supervision test, or deciding every personal immunity question exceeds what this record and grant require. Reversal of the municipal reinstatement would require a categorical legal defect or a proper proof-stage failure, neither established by the authorized record. Correction of the lower court's overbroad explanations can accompany affirmance without a fictitious liability judgment to vacate.

No material missing-source question prevents these provisional commitments. Actual policies, notice, authority over police, the precise unconstitutional acts, causation, damages and individual defenses remain for ordinary proceedings. They are unresolved merits, not assumptions against either party.
