# Arizona — opinion form before assignment

One signed plenary opinion will resolve the argued exceptions and the two independent settlement recommendations. Its genuine parts are: I, procedural forfeiture of the Arizona I defense, reserving its merits and returning Fort Yuma; II, the distinct Docket 320 consent-judgment limits; III, the separately asserted Indian Claims Commission Act section 22 discharge/title argument; and IV, approval and implementation of each settlement at its specified terms. The legal questions require these divisions independently of author counts. A single eligible Justice can write the full lead, so no division of authorship is needed.

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the Fort Yuma return and the complete procedural ground in Part I. O'Connor and Thomas would entertain and sustain original-allocation preclusion. All nine support the distinct consent-judgment conclusion in Part II and both settlement approvals in Part IV. All eight Associates support the section 22 rule in Part III. Stone's supplement does not expressly address that independent statutory-discharge component: his standing fallback supplies agreement in its disposition without adopting an additional ground, so he does not join Part III. This limited nonjoin is displayed directly; no separate Stone opinion is required to invent reasons the supplement does not give.

One common dissent in part will preserve O'Connor and Thomas's procedural objection, original-allocation rationale and refusal of a Fort Yuma merits remand. They may join Parts II–IV without joining the resulting Fort Yuma return. The lead therefore has six eligible full-ground Associate authors; no author or count has yet been selected for this assignment. The judgment and proposition coalitions are fixed from the frozen reconciliation and Stone's actual approved position before the fresh recount.

Settlement approval uses the existing original-jurisdiction proceeding and the particular Master's record. It announces no new general approval framework, enters no final Fort Yuma boundary or quantity and adopts no historical proposed-decree appendix or objection deadline. Further implementing decree terms must preserve the approved agreements and their stated reservations.
