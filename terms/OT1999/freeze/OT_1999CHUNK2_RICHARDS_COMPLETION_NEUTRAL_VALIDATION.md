## Material predicate validation

The source packet is adequate for review of the dismissal on its actual assumed facts. Richards remained on active duty and in uniform, but had permission to leave work early, had no pass or furlough, and was accepted for dismissal as driving his own car home for a personal family purpose. Those propositions must remain separate. Neither describing him simply as a civilian nor saying that his travel necessarily performed an assigned task would be supported. The Education Center account remains disputed and was not the premise on which the lower courts sustained dismissal.

The public highway, state maintenance/patrol and civilian use coexist with the accident's location within Fort Knox. A rule treating either geographic label as conclusive requires legal justification; it is not an existing factual finding. The petition's red-light account identifies the police report as its source but is not a negligence finding. The lower courts did not resolve maintenance defects, licensing, fault, causation, or damages.

The complaint must be evaluated at its actual breadth: negligent maintenance; permitting a knowingly unsafe truck on public roads; allowing an allegedly unlicensed soldier to drive that class of truck; negligently ordering him to drive despite inadequate skill or experience; and negligent operation. The government denied negligence and the pleaded defects, but admitted that SPC Potts acted within the scope of his employment. The latter is affirmative record support and must not be described as wholly missing. The claimants' losses derive from Richards's death; no separate civilian accident injury is established.

The district court's Rule 12(b)(1) dismissal rested on Feres, using status, place and activity and then each Feres rationale; its concrete command concerns arose from the actual maintenance, licensing and assignment allegations. The alternative Rule 12(c)/56 motions were not decided. The appellate majority sustained the service-incident classification while separately questioning its relationship to discipline in this setting. Its criticism did not reverse the dismissal or decide liability. The rehearing dissent is a lower-court position only.

## Law and supported alternatives

The controlling pre-divergence rule remains Feres's service-incident bar, with Brooks preserving claims unrelated to service. Feres distinguishes furlough and absence of military duty; it does not itself prescribe a mandatory status/location/activity checklist or a rule that every public-road accident or unassigned activity is actionable. Brown preserves the distinction between a service injury and a separate postdischarge wrong. Richards was not discharged, so Brown cannot supply that missing status by analogy alone.

Shearer Part II-B makes the actual command decisions challenged material even when the injured member was off duty and off base. It specifically distinguishes vehicle operation from the challenged supervision and retention of a dangerous soldier. Johnson preserves a bar for an injury concededly occurring on a military mission despite a civilian alleged tortfeasor; it prevents treating the identity of the tortfeasor or a particular form of discipline inquiry as universally indispensable. Neither case establishes that Richards's accepted personal journey was such a mission. Stencel's indemnity holding presupposes service-incident injury and does not decide this threshold application.

Minns supplies the separate, controlling §2680(a) policy-discretion rule. Its fractured whole-dismissal grounds supply no general majority Feres extension. A prescribed course and protected policy choice are distinct; the words military, supervision, operational and negligent do not alone resolve the exception. No binding vehicle/driver directive or independently adjudicated discretionary-function bar is established here. A proposed §2680(a) disposition therefore needs the actual protected-choice predicates and appropriate consideration of its procedural availability; it cannot simply replace the ground below.

The record supports adversarial comparison of (1) affirmance under the existing service relationship on the accepted personal-travel premise, (2) rejection of the Feres application under the surviving Brooks boundary, and (3) a claim-specific or bounded remand disposition if the adopted rule distinguishes operation, maintenance and command theories or makes an unresolved fact material. Each path has the counterarguments identified in the source packet. No outcome follows merely from the new availability of research materials.

The theories are pleaded separately; the source does not establish that they are legally inseparable. Conversely, separate pleading does not guarantee separate treatment if a properly adopted service-incident rule resolves them all. A partial disposition must identify the surviving theory and its legal basis. A remand cannot describe permission, absence of pass/furlough, road character, or scope-of-employment admission as wholly unknown. It may leave genuinely unresolved destination, directive or command facts below if its operative rule actually makes them material.
