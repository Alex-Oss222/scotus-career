The following is a qualified description, not a substitute transcription:

- Section 2 adds §28-326(9). It defines the procedure through partial vaginal delivery of a living unborn child before killing and completing delivery; the elaboration requires deliberately and intentionally delivering a living unborn child, or a substantial portion, into the vagina for a procedure the performer knows will kill the child and that does kill it. These are act, intent, knowledge, life and sequence elements; a clinical label alone does not establish them. The definition has no viability limitation.
- Section 3(1), codified as §28-328(1), prohibits that procedure unless necessary to save the mother's life endangered by a physical disorder, physical illness or physical injury, including a life-endangering physical condition caused by or arising from the pregnancy itself. It supplies no independent health exception. The broader emergency-situation definition in amended §28-326(7), spanning pages 1–2, addresses immediate abortion to avert death or delay creating serious risk of substantial impairment of a major bodily function, on the physician's good-faith clinical judgment. Its presence does not insert those broader terms into section 3(1), which does not invoke that definition.
- Section 3(2) makes intentional and knowing performance of an unlawful procedure violating subsection (1) a Class III felony. Section 3(3) forbids prosecution of the woman upon whom an unlawful procedure is performed under this section or for conspiracy to violate it. It is a distinct patient immunity, not a physician defense.
- **Section 3(4):** Intentional and knowing performance of an unlawful procedure results in automatic suspension and revocation of the attending physician's Nebraska medical license by the Director of Regulation and Licensure pursuant to §§71-147 through 71-161.20. The actor, conduct condition and statutory process cross-reference qualify the provision; it must not be shortened to an unexplained automatic loss of license without the enacted procedure.
- **Section 3(5):** When the Attorney General or a county attorney files criminal charges under this section, the Attorney General shall also file a petition under §71-150 to suspend and revoke the attending physician's license. A hearing shall be set under §71-153. At that hearing the physician shall have an opportunity to present evidence that the conduct was necessary to save the mother's life under the same physical-disorder, illness or injury terms, including the pregnancy-caused life-endangering condition. A criminal defendant may bring a motion to delay the beginning of the trial until after the Director's §71-155 order. The Director's findings on that life-necessity question shall be admissible in the criminal proceeding. The statute does not make a motion an automatic delay, make the findings conclusive, or create a health exception or preprocedure permission requirement.
- Section 5 adds the corresponding intentional-and-knowing procedure violation to §71-148(15)'s unprofessional-conduct categories, expressly retaining the same life-saving exception. Section 6 amends §71-155: the proceeding is summary and tried as an equity action before the Director or designated hearing officer; affidavits may be received at their discretion, and subpoena and deposition procedures are preserved. Where the petition concerns §71-148(15), the Director shall make findings on life necessity under the same specified terms. The general authority to enter orders, irrespective of the petition, includes censure/reprimand, suspended judgment, probation, practice limitations, a civil penalty up to $10,000 based on severity, suspension, revocation, or dismissal. The relation of these general powers to section 3(4)'s mandatory wording is not resolved by inventing an exception or an unreported state construction.
- Under §71-155, after finding guilt the Director may consult the board of examiners on sanctions or conditions; if the Director does so, the licensee receives the request and the board recommendation and an opportunity to respond in the manner the Director determines. Suspension lasts a definite period set by the Director, with the specified reinstatement conditions; revocation is for all time, except that an application for reinstatement may be made after two years under §71-161.04. Section 7's amended §71-171.02 separately permits the board, after Attorney General referral, to send an approved letter of concern, advise on a voluntary assurance of compliance, or recommend a §71-150 petition. The assurance is signed and public; it is not an admission of violation. These are not an invented medical-board veto over treatment or an additional automatic defense.
- Section 4 adds section 3 to §28-340's employment-protection remedy for a person whose employment or position is altered, impaired or terminated in violation of the listed provisions. It does not by itself create a patient damages award in this case. Section 8 expressly preserves the validity and constitutionality of remaining portions if a section or part is declared invalid or unconstitutional. This severability direction requires attention to independently lawful provisions and applications; it does not authorize judicial rewriting of the prohibited act or silently nullify dependent sanctions. Section 9 repeals the superseded sections identified there.

The complete enactment includes older findings and other professional-discipline provisions. Their exact text remains in the linked images. ## Record, judgments and competing arguments

Carhart performs abortions before viability. The supplied lower-court recitations distinguish ordinary D&E, which can draw a living fetus's limb through the cervix before dismemberment, from intact variants and from instances where fetal demise precedes removal. That distinction matters under the enacted living-fetus element. The district court's credited evidence supports specific safety advantages for Carhart's intact method in the circumstances of roughly 10–20 annual cases, including reduced instrumentation, retained parts, trauma, blood loss, operating time and infection risk. It does not establish universal superiority in every patient. The State disputes comparative superiority, notes the absence of controlled comparisons and invokes available alternatives and professional ethics. The credited testimony, its qualified scope, and the district court's credibility assessments must be retained; competent disagreement is not automatically a physician veto or categorical legislative immunity from review.

**Actual relief below:** The District Court expressly declined whole-statute facial invalidation. It declared LB23 unconstitutional as applied to Carhart, his patients and similarly situated persons regarding his D&E and D&X methods on nonviable fetuses, and enjoined the defendants and those bound under Rule 65(d) to that extent, with service on county attorneys. The exact operative directions are copied below. Its grounds concerned the health/access effect of restricting Carhart's intact method, reach to ordinary D&E and vagueness of substantial portion. The Eighth Circuit affirmed on the ordinary-D&E reach and substantial-obstacle ground; it did not decide the separate intact-only burden or vagueness grounds. Its separate county-attorney fee appeal is not independently before the Court under the supplied merits question. An affirmance cannot be casually described as a new whole-statute, nationwide or all-gestation invalidation.

**Nebraska's supported position:** The enacted sequence, context and purpose can reasonably distinguish intact delivery from dismemberment D&E. Constitutional avoidance and respect for state law favor that construction; the Attorney General's D&E nonenforcement position supports its practical operation. Alternatives preserve choice, and the State may regulate a distinct method for potential-life and medical-ethics reasons without allowing an unsupported preference to defeat the law. The strongest counterargument is that the enacted substantial-portion language may reach deliberate limb extraction in ordinary D&E despite those purposes; a litigation assurance is not a binding state supreme-court construction or established control over every county prosecutor. Even a permissible narrowing must answer necessary-care and actual-burden claims.

**Carhart's supported position:** The text and described ordinary procedure satisfy the enacted elements, and the penalty risk restricts the predominant second-trimester method. Separately, prohibiting the intact method without usable necessary-health protection forces clinically significant risk in supported applications. The strongest counterargument is that procedure overlap alone does not establish notice failure, coverage of every D&E, universal method necessity, or a remedy beyond the proved applications. The State's contextual construction and actual alternatives need independent consideration. The evidence must establish interference, causation and an affected population under actual Casey, not a presumption against every abortion regulation.

## Law, independent paths and remedial limits

The full supplied Casey, Fargo and Voinovich standards, holdings, public joins and material separate positions are mechanically copied below. Casey's **complete six-step framework** controls: enacted duty and baseline; actual function and purpose; concrete attributable interference and stable affected population; independent baseline/purpose/obstacle bars followed by residual severe-interference scrutiny and meaningful-interference justification and fit; clinical and communication safeguards; and violation-specific relief. The State's justification burden follows the appropriate established trigger, not abortion's subject matter alone. Usable care and clinical necessity are distinct from every preference. No numerical facial threshold or independent no-valid-applications barrier displaces this rule.

Voinovich rejects categorical invalidity merely because statutory and medical labels overlap or every clinical judgment lacks an express fault term. It equally requires independent substantive method, necessary-care, alternative-care and remedy analysis. Nebraska's enactment is not Ohio's: Ohio's particular criminal and civil alternative-method defenses and burdens cannot be imported. Fargo likewise separates intelligibility, actual fault and substantive necessary-care protection. The words intentional and knowing do not automatically answer uncertainty about every element, establish good faith as a universal immunity, or cure a substantive exclusion of necessary care.

- A construction-and-access path may sustain the existing bounded protection if the enacted prohibition reaches ordinary D&E and the supported affected population loses practical timely, clinically appropriate care. It must explain actual reach and interference, rather than rely on the label D&E or only on rejected categorical vagueness reasoning. An independently sufficient coverage/access ground need not manufacture an additional medical holding.
- A separately supported health path may sustain protection for circumstances in which the prohibition denies timely necessary care, even if a legally available narrowing distinguishes ordinary D&E. It must identify the actual clinical trigger, credible evidence, usable alternatives and limited applications. No universal finding that the intact method is safest follows; no narrow life-only exception becomes a health exception through a favorable label or post-charge hearing.
- A State-favoring construction and application may permit reversal or narrower relief only if that construction is legally available and the record supports lawful care and the relevant justification under the governing framework. It must confront the existing independent grounds and injunction's actual scope. A construction cannot be invented from the desired outcome or borrowed from Ohio.
- A bounded remand may address a genuinely unresolved material construction, clinical application or remedy, with its legal source and ordinary interim consequences specified. No new hearing, automatic dissolution, automatic blanket enforcement or permanent all-applications injunction follows from remand alone. If a proposed path depends on certification, a binding Nebraska construction or additional severability doctrine, validate the narrow missing source before relying on it.

## Stenberg v. Carhart - No. 99-830

**Event and questions.** Merits consideration June 28, 2000; argued April 25, 2000. Certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, 192 F.3d 1142 (Sept. 24, 1999). The state merits brief's two questions concern statutory construction/federalism and the asserted undue burden of the method prohibition. The supplied neutral grant expressly includes both reach to standard D&E and absence of a health exception. Respondent advances health, bodily-integrity, purpose and vagueness grounds supporting the existing protection. The petition's additional fetal-personhood and wholesale-reconsideration questions were not carried into the petitioners' two merits questions; this packet does not enlarge the supplied grant into either subject.

**Operative statute.** Nebraska LB 23, codified at sections 28-326(9) and 28-328, forbids its defined partial-birth procedure unless necessary to save the patient's life endangered by a physical disorder, illness or injury, including a pregnancy-caused life-endangering physical condition. The definition covers deliberate and intentional vaginal delivery of a living fetus or a substantial portion for a procedure the performer knows will kill it and does kill it, before completion of delivery. Intentional and knowing prohibited performance carries a felony sanction and physician-license consequences. The patient may not be prosecuted under this section or for conspiracy to violate it. The definition, conduct fault, patient immunity, life exception, absence of an independent health exception and no viability limitation must remain distinct. The source extracts reproduce the available operative terms, retaining the brief's ellipsis at the end of its licensing recitation; no ruling on the separate licensing procedures is supplied. No invented D&X-only definition, clinical safe harbor or good-faith defense is inserted.

**Material findings and competing medical evidence.** Dr. Carhart performs abortions before viability and uses D&E and, in some circumstances, an intact variant. The lower courts describe ordinary D&E as frequently drawing an arm or leg through the cervix while the fetus is living before dismemberment occurs; they rejected the State's claim that the intent terms necessarily exclude those acts. In some instances fetal demise precedes removal, which differs under the statutory living-fetus element. No claim of unconstitutional restriction on viable-fetus procedures was necessary to the Eighth Circuit's disposition.

The district court credited Carhart, Hodgson, Stubblefield and the State's Boehm while finding the other state expert generally unreliable on procedure knowledge. It particularly credited Stubblefield's experience. The district court found that Carhart's intact method, used in roughly 10-20 cases annually, reduced particular surgical risks in those circumstances compared with available alternatives; it identified reduced instrumentation, retained parts, trauma, blood loss, operating time and infection risks. It did not make a universal finding that every intact procedure is always safest. The State's evidence disputes comparative superiority, stresses the absence of controlled comparative studies and availability of alternatives, and invokes ethical and professional interests. Boehm acknowledged that intact removal might reduce certain mechanical risks but considered comparative safety insufficiently studied. Preserve the evidence/finding distinction and review standard rather than substitute a professional association's conclusory veto or categorical legislative factual immunity.

**Exact lower relief and independent grounds.** Carhart v. Stenberg, 11 F. Supp. 2d 1099 (D. Neb. July 2, 1998), expressly declined to decide whole-statute facial invalidity. It declared LB 23 unconstitutional as applied to Carhart, his patients and similarly situated persons, concerning his D&E/D&X methods on nonviable fetuses; enjoined defendants and persons bound under Rule 65(d) to that extent; and directed service on Nebraska county attorneys. Its three reasons were the health/access effect of prohibiting Carhart's intact method in the identified circumstances, the reach to ordinary D&E, and vagueness of substantial portion.

The Eighth Circuit affirmed because the language reaches ordinary D&E, the predominant second-trimester method, creating a substantial obstacle under its stated Casey analysis. It held that avoiding constitutional doubt cannot give words a meaning they cannot bear, and that intentional/knowing elements do not exclude an ordinary D&E performed intentionally. It expressly did not decide the separate D&X-only burden or vagueness grounds. It also affirmed the county attorney's joint/several fee liability in his distinct appeal; the supplied merits question does not independently place that fee dispute before the Court. Do not describe an affirmance here as a new adjudication of all fees or an automatic nationwide/facial injunction.

**Competing statutory and constitutional arguments.** Nebraska argues that the words partial vaginal delivery, context, statutory purpose and deliberate sequence distinguish the intact procedure from dismemberment D&E. It invokes constitutional avoidance, federalism, legislative materials, the Attorney General's expressed D&E nonenforcement position, and appropriate respect for state construction. It argues that alternative methods preserve access and that a distinct procedure can be prohibited to protect potential life and medical ethics without an exception that would allow any physician preference to defeat the prohibition. The Attorney General's litigation position is not itself a binding state supreme-court construction or a finding that every county prosecutor is bound.

Carhart argues that the enacted substantial-portion definition reaches limb extraction in ordinary D&E; an aspirational purpose and nonbinding assurance cannot replace the text or eliminate penal exposure. Even a narrower intact-only rule, he contends, lacks usable protection where alternatives impose significant clinical risks. He supports those arguments with the district findings, disputes the claimed state interests and presses separate notice/enforcement concerns. He does not need to prove that the intact method is preferable in every case to support relief for an established affected group; he still must prove the relevant interference, causation and scope under actual Casey.

**Entering-law forks and remedy.** Actual Casey's full six-step standard, Fargo and Voinovich govern; the historical lower courts' obstacle-only or numerical language does not replace them. Distinguish (1) permissible construction and actual coverage, (2) independent necessary-care protection, (3) substantial obstacle, (4) remaining severe/meaningful interference and justification, (5) notice/fault, and (6) severability and relief. Voinovich forbids categorical invalidity merely because medical labels overlap or every clinical judgment lacks an express fault term; it equally requires genuine substantive access and necessary-care analysis. It rejected a universal fault cure and left method applications to supported inquiry. Its Ohio affirmative defenses are not Nebraska law.

A narrower lawful construction cannot be invented; the Court may assess whether one is legally available and how much it resolves. No later medical or abortion decision may enter.

## Standards and Tests selection: Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

### Protected choice and the six-step standard for previability regulation

**Governing question:** Whether a legal obligation regulating previability abortion impermissibly interferes with the patient's protected decision or access to timely, clinically appropriate care.

**Current rule:** Before viability the patient decides whether to continue pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons. The State may not prohibit that choice, confer effective control on another person, or prevent timely necessary life-and-health care. After viability, prohibition requires the life-and-health safeguard, including individualized physical and mental health considerations. Viability is individualized and medically supported, not a fixed constitutional week. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons; not every preference is a medical emergency. The following complete standard governs throughout previability:

1. **Identify the duty and protected baseline.** Identify the enacted command, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances. Distinguish an actual obligation from assumed future administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care violates the baseline and cannot be rescued by justification. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Defining all noncompliant treatment as unsafe does not establish medical necessity.
2. **Identify actual function and purpose.** Identify what the obligation does and the asserted permissible objective, including safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration or financial accountability. The State may prefer childbirth and communicate truthful information. Moral disapproval does not independently justify obstruction, punishment of protected choice or burdens imposed for their deterrent effect. An obstructive purpose established by objective evidence of text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the requirement; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish that purpose. Voluntary persuasion differs from prevention through barriers. Evaluate each justification on its terms: medical claims need medical support, and deliberative claims need an explanation linking the particular obligation to informed decisionmaking.
3. **Establish actual interference, comparison and affected population.** Challengers must establish concrete additional interference caused by the challenged obligation. Relevant effects include extra appointments, material expense, consequential delay, loss of usable provider capacity, clinical risk, coercion and disclosure consequences affecting choice or access. Compare the actual system with and without the obligation while holding other relevant law and circumstances constant. For a challenged interacting package, identify the package and compare the system without it. Consider cumulative effects without counting the same burden twice. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding constitutional injury, include evidence-supported subgroups, and keep the population stable. Do not average the burden across everyone pregnant or define the group as only those who prevail. Poverty, distance, transportation, employment and abuse matter insofar as they explain the State's added condition; identify that causal link. Exceptions, bypasses and alternative providers count only if actually usable. Meaningful interference means concrete, nontrivial additional burden on obtaining care or exercising the protected decision. It triggers review rather than establishing invalidity. No universal dollar, mileage or hourly threshold applies. Reliable clinical evidence, records, testimony and reasonable predictions can establish sufficiently demonstrated prospective injury before access is lost.
4. **Apply the substantive protections in order.** First, a baseline violation, unconstitutional obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle is independently invalid. A substantial obstacle seriously obstructs the affected population's practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and success by some patients does not establish adequate access for others. A compelling interest cannot rescue an established obstacle. Second, remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Identify the severe intrusion and explain why the independent prohibitions did not already decide the issue; overlapping protections require no artificial boundary, but an obstacle may not be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical judgment, health or protected confidentiality may require this scrutiny despite usable access. Third, for remaining meaningful interference the State must substantiate a permissible justification and its fit to the added obligation. Abstract interests and conclusory assertions are insufficient. When challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it; inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequences may answer that showing. The State need not select the cheapest imaginable policy or disprove every hypothetical alternative. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice; particular legislative findings, a new study in every case, scientific certainty and numerical benefit calculations are not required. Once justification and fit are established, courts may not substitute their preferred policy. Below meaningful interference this heightened access inquiry ends, while prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary lawful-regulation requirements remain available.
5. **Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication.** Evaluate medical justifications against competent evidence, accepted professional standards, the patient's circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement receives serious evidentiary weight, without a professional association's veto or conclusive weight for a clinician's bare assertion or an official's unsupported preference. Necessary care must remain timely; unnecessary committee vetoes and documentation functioning as prior permission may not displace appropriate clinical judgment. Duties must be sufficiently definite and applicable fault requirements respected. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish every element of a punishable offense; lawful enforcement against fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct remains. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material to informed consent. Concise factual assistance notices may inform choice without ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accuracy does not alone validate burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Distinguish useful confidential oversight records from public disclosure and records enforcing invalid obligations.
6. **Identify the violation and tailor relief.** Specify the duty or application that fails, why, and the corresponding relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under applicable severability law and expressly address dependent certifications, report fields and sanctions. An individual claim does not fail because others comply; broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation imposes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it. Voluntary compliance in some circumstances does not alone validate compulsory compliance. Otherwise confine relief to the established applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not remove proof of causation, scope or inadequate justification. For cumulative burdens, remedy the challenged obligation's or package's contribution without automatically invalidating all regulation. Replacing the trimester timetable does not dissolve injunctions or authorize enforcement of every challenged provision; the mandate must specify the resulting duties.

### Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent

#### Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-744 and 91-902.
**Decided:** June 29, 1992.

For the other questions in Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, June 29, 1992, Nos. 91-744 and 91-902, see Civil Rights — Reproductive liberty and constitutional precedent (retained protection, governing framework and timetable), Abortion emergency care, Abortion informed consent, Minor consent and independent authorization, Spousal control of reproductive choice, and Abortion reporting and public disclosure; Federal Courts — Scope of prospective relief for abortion requirements; and First Amendment — Abortion information and compelled speech. Each question has one primary entry.

##### The framework retaining protected choice

**Holding:** Before viability, the patient retains the right to decide whether to continue a pregnancy without proving medical necessity or obtaining official approval of her reasons; the State may not prohibit that protected choice, give another person effective control over it, or prevent timely care necessary for life or health. After viability the State may prohibit abortion only with the required life-and-health protection, while competent practice, genuinely informed consent, safety and lawful administration remain regulable within these boundaries.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Viability is individualized and medically supported; no fixed week or conclusive calendar presumption is adopted. Necessary care includes relevant physical and mental health and cannot await imminent death or catastrophic deterioration. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe and suitable treatment, not official approval of the patient's reasons. The holding does not decide constitutional fetal personhood, general public funding, conscience claims, every postviability restriction or admission of a particular drug.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The retained liberty governs the separate provision-specific dispositions. Its six supporters do not all join the complete framework, which has five supporters.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Roe v. Wade — Retains the protected previability decision, viability boundary and required postviability life-and-health safeguard; this holding does not itself decide the separate treatment of the trimester timetable.
- Griswold v. Connecticut — Applies protection of reproductive decisionmaking as a foundation of the retained liberty.
- Eisenstadt v. Baird — Applies the individual character of reproductive choice; marital status does not supply official control over the decision.
- Carey v. Population Services International — Applies the principle that substantial restrictions on access can burden the protected decision itself, with no general rule about every law implicating sexual freedom.
- Doe v. Bolton — Retains individualized clinical judgment and meaningful physical-and-mental-health protection; no general professional immunity follows.
- Planned Parenthood of Central Missouri v. Danforth — Applies the prohibition on State-conferred spousal control and preserves the distinction between voluntary informed consent and interference with protected choice.
- Bellotti v. Baird, Planned Parenthood Association of Kansas City v. Ashcroft, Hodgson v. Minnesota, and Ohio v. Akron Center for Reproductive Health — Preserve their particular parental-involvement and independent-authorization safeguards; they do not supply an adult spouse's veto or a universal cure for every added parental burden.

##### The standard governing previability regulation after this decision

**Holding:** The Court adopts a complete six-step standard governing previability abortion regulation. Substantial obstacles and violations of the protected baseline are independently invalid. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring. Remaining meaningful interference requires the State to substantiate its justification and fit and answer identified practicable, substantially less burdensome alternatives. The complete standard replaces the independent trimester timetable in the same holding.

The adopted inquiry has the following complete operative scope:

1. Identify the enacted duty, authoritative construction, exceptions, enforcement consequences and relevant medical circumstances, separating actual commands from assumed administration. A previability prohibition, effective third-party veto or denial of timely necessary life-and-health care independently violates the protected baseline and cannot be justified later in the inquiry. Necessary care must remain available before imminent death or catastrophic deterioration makes intervention unavoidable. Clinical appropriateness concerns safe, suitable care, not official approval of the patient's reasons or a legislative definition declaring all noncompliant care unsafe.
2. Identify actual function and purpose. Safety, informed consent, truthful information, permissible parental involvement, health administration and financial accountability may supply permissible functions. Truthful persuasion and a preference for childbirth are permissible; moral disapproval, punishment or deterrence through blocked access are not independent justifications. Obstructive purpose established objectively from text, design, context and operation independently invalidates the duty; a legislator's opposition to abortion alone does not establish it. Medical and deliberative justifications require support addressing their actual functions.
3. Challengers must establish a concrete, nontrivial additional interference attributable to the duty, including demonstrated expense, appointments, delay, provider-capacity loss, clinical risk, coercion or disclosure consequences. Compare the system with and without that obligation, or the identified challenged package, holding other law and circumstances constant. Consider cumulative operation without double counting. Define the affected population from the duty's operation before deciding injury, retain that group throughout, and use evidence-supported subgroups where appropriate. Do not average burdens over all pregnancies or define the group only as successful claimants. Poverty, distance, work, transportation and abuse matter through their causal connection to the State's added condition; exceptions, bypasses and providers count only if usable. No universal monetary, distance or time cutoff applies. Reliable evidence and reasonable predictions may establish prospective interference without waiting for lost access. Meaningful interference triggers review but does not itself establish invalidity.
4. Apply the protections in sequence. A violation of the baseline, obstructive purpose or substantial obstacle ends the inquiry in invalidity. An obstacle seriously obstructs practical opportunity for timely, clinically appropriate protected care in the affected population; absolute impossibility is unnecessary, and successful compliance by some does not establish access for others. No compelling interest rescues an established obstacle. Remaining severe interference requires a compelling permissible justification and narrow tailoring; identify the severe intrusion and why the first inquiry did not resolve it. The protections may overlap, but an obstacle cannot be relabeled as justifiable severe interference. Serious intrusion into clinical decisionmaking, health or protected confidentiality may require exacting justification even where practical access remains usable. For remaining meaningful lesser interference, the State must substantiate its permissible justification and the fit of the additional duty. If challengers identify a concrete, practicable, substantially less burdensome alternative adequately serving the objective, the State must answer it, including any inadequacy, unavailability or material countervailing consequence. Reliable experience and reasonable predictions may suffice: no particular legislative finding, new study in every case, scientific certainty, numerical benefit calculation or disproof of every hypothetical alternative is required. A court may not substitute its preferred policy once justification and fit are established. Below meaningful interference, the heightened access inquiry ends, but prohibited-purpose claims, independent constitutional protections and ordinary legal requirements remain. Challengers bear duty, population, causation and trigger; the State bears the applicable justification, tailoring or fit burden. Ordinary proof rules for historical facts and separate statutory defenses do not change.
5. Protect clinical judgment and truthful communication. Assess medical reasons against competent evidence, accepted standards, individual circumstances and substantial contrary evidence. Substantial professional agreement has serious evidentiary weight but no association holds a veto; neither a bare clinical assertion nor unsupported official preference is conclusive. Necessary care must remain timely, without an unnecessary committee veto or documentation used as prior permission. Duties must be definite and applicable culpability requirements respected; reasonable later professional disagreement alone does not establish every element of an offense. Fraud, malpractice and misconduct remain enforceable. Required medical information must be accurate, nonmisleading and material. Concise factual assistance notices need not entail ideological assent; clinicians may explain limitations and correct misinformation. Accurate content does not itself justify burdensome timing, delivery or certification. Analyze useful confidential records separately from public disclosure and enforcement of invalid conditions.
6. Identify the particular violation and tailor relief. Preserve independently lawful requirements under severability law and address dependent certifications, reports and sanctions expressly. Another patient's ability to comply does not defeat an individual claim. Broader relief requires a corresponding showing. An indivisible mandatory condition may be invalidated when its ordinary operation causes demonstrated meaningful interference and the State fails to justify it; voluntary compliance in some cases alone does not justify compulsion. Otherwise limit relief to proved applications or groups. This qualification of a strict no-valid-applications approach does not dispense with causation, scope or justification analysis. Cumulative-burden relief addresses the challenged contribution, not all existing regulation. Replacement of the timetable neither dissolves injunctions nor authorizes every challenged duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter: 5 direct rationale joins including the author.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a direct five-Justice rule; no Marks synthesis or addition of Kennedy's separate approach is required. An independent numerical facial-review threshold is not adopted. The spousal-notice holding supplies its distinct provision-specific affected-group analysis.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The complete framework replaces the independent trimester timetable effective June 29, 1992. Existing injunctions must be conformed to the actual component judgments, not automatically dissolved.

- Roe and Doe: preserve the protected decision and clinical safeguards while replacing the independent timetable.
- Carey and Akron: support protection against serious and unjustified interference; the general lesser-burden substantiation rule is expressly adopted here.
- Marks: no aggregation of dissenting rationales is necessary because five Justices directly join the complete rule.
- Ohio v. Akron: its facial-review formulation is qualified at the scope expressly stated in Step 6 and the spousal-notice holding.

##### Stare decisis and the constitutional foundation

**Holding:** Roe's protected reproductive liberty, viability boundary and necessary life-and-health protection are retained because renewed disagreement with the constitutional judgment supplies no sufficient justification for withdrawing that settled protection. The Court may distinguish or alter a separable regulatory rule for a demonstrated legal reason, but such treatment does not silently overrule the liberty that the rule implements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's opinion, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter: 6 direct rationale joins including the author.

##### The trimester timetable and the timing of regulation

**Holding:** The independent trimester timetable is displaced together with adoption of the complete six-step replacement. Five Justices adopt that integrated holding. All nine reject continuation of the former timetable in result, but the other four do not join the replacement standard.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The integrated replacement takes effect with the complete framework; existing injunctions remain subject to ordinary conforming relief. Statistical use of trimester categories remains distinct.

##### The specified emergency terms supply a prospective clinical criterion

**Holding:** The preserved facial vagueness challenge to section 14-02.1-02(7) fails because its specified outcomes and timing, assessed through the physician's prospective best clinical judgment, give notice and enforcement guidance: the pregnancy must require immediate abortion to avert death, or a twenty-four-hour delay must create grave peril of immediate and irreversible loss of major bodily function. Gravity, immediacy and major bodily function call for a professional forecast, not certainty, a numerical prognosis or waiting until the threatened harm occurs; this holding does not adjudicate the distinct claim that the exception's substantive breadth fails to protect particular necessary care.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Clarity is not a ruling that this restricted emergency category satisfies every Casey life-and-health requirement. Casey's broader necessary-care protection, including individualized physical and mental health and care before catastrophic deterioration, remains law. The emergency undue-burden claim was not pressed below and is not decided here. The federal appellate significant-threat construction is not enacted text or an authoritative North Dakota Supreme Court construction; it does not license deleting immediate, irreversible or major from the statute. The present notice ground stands independently of that broader construction and of any presumed default fault rule.

Section 14-02.1-03(1)'s emergency exception excuses that subsection's ordinary requirements, but requires an explanation of the medical indications before the abortion if possible and written certification of those indications. The conditional explanation is no advance-permission requirement, and certification supplies no authority to delay necessary treatment. Later reasonable medical disagreement alone does not establish all elements of a criminal offense or civil damages; disagreement may remain relevant under actual applicable law. Fraud, malpractice and professional misconduct are not immunized.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Vuitch, 402 U.S. 62 (1971): applies the intelligibility of professional judgment at the stated scope; its D.C. statute's life-or-health construction and prosecution burden do not become North Dakota law.
- Doe v. Bolton, 410 U.S. 179 (1973): applies its recognition of clinical judgment without importing Georgia's broader statutory construction or creating professional immunity.
- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): distinguishes its conflicting viability and treatment duties, while retaining independent scrutiny of the actual trigger and culpability for error.
- Kolender v. Lawson, 461 U.S. 352 (1983): applies minimum enforcement guidelines and distinguishes undefined official satisfaction from the stated clinical criteria; no universal all-applications bar is adopted.
- Planned Parenthood of Southeastern Pennsylvania v. Casey, 505 U.S. 833 (1992): preserves broad necessary-care safeguards and conditional emergency explanation; its Pennsylvania construction was not a separate emergency-vagueness holding and does not determine this distinct claim.

##### A doubtful default-fault rationale does not invalidate the general penalty by itself

**Holding:** Section 12.1-02-02 alone does not establish default willfulness for the silent misdemeanor clause of section 14-02.1-11, and neither that default nor the latter section's separately expressed willful rule-or-regulation infraction supplies the criminal or civil elements of every chapter violation. Rejection of that additional rationale does not defeat the independently sufficient definition holdings or establish facial invalidity of the general penalty merely because its first sentence lacks an express culpability term; actual element-specific fault and liability remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The misdemeanor clause covers a chapter violation for which no other penalty is specifically prescribed. The distinct infraction clause concerns a willful violation of a promulgated rule or regulation. The latter's express term does not amend the former. The independent civil remedy provides $10,000 punitive damages plus treble actual damages for an abortion without the required informed consent, and $5,000 punitive damages plus treble actual damages for an attempted abortion without compliance. The Court enters no liability determination or award under either provision and creates no civil scienter defense. No separate facial judgment on an unpresented civil-damages theory is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Colautti v. Franklin, 439 U.S. 379 (1979): preserves the separation of an intelligible clinical standard from fault concerning an erroneous assessment; no particular universal fault level is supplied.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., 513 U.S. 64 (1994): preserves its context-specific distinction between definition and fault; no universal knowledge element or scienter cure is inferred.

The District Court must promptly consider interim protection under the ordinary standard and current record. No dissolved injunction is automatically restored, no final injunction or hearing is ordered, and no emergency-access claim unpressed below or new parental or speech claim is decided.




#### Women’s Medical Professional Corp. v. Voinovich

**Docket or dockets:** Sixth Circuit Nos. 96-3157 and 96-3159; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** 1998-04-20.

##### The complete Casey framework governs the facial and access claims

**Holding:** Casey’s complete six-step standard, as applied in Fargo, governs the challenged previability regulation. Neither Salerno’s no-valid-applications barrier nor an independent numerical large-fraction threshold displaces that inquiry, and failure to establish a substantial obstacle does not end review of remaining severe or meaningful interference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six directly adopt the governing standard, including its proof allocation and qualifications:

**Limits and questions not reached:** The complete governing framework is the six-step rule in Casey, as applied in Fargo: identify the duty and protected baseline; identify actual function and purpose; establish attributable interference and a stable affected population; apply the ordered substantive protections; protect clinical judgment and accurate communication; identify the violation and tailor relief. Challengers prove duty, population, causation and triggering interference; the State bears the corresponding justification and fit burden. No substantiation burden arises from the subject of abortion alone.

##### An act-defined method and professional clinical criteria are not categorically unintelligible

**Holding:** Ohio’s method definition and specified good-faith, reasonable-medical-judgment criteria cannot be invalidated on the categorical ground that statutory and medical procedure labels overlap or that every subsidiary clinical judgment lacks an express culpability term. Courts must examine notice and enforcement guidance, actual fault for the relevant element, criminal and civil liability, and substantive access as distinct questions; this holding neither supplies a universal fault rule nor validates every enforcement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II; Souter and all seven other Associates. Stone-Zsela dissents.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Purposeful skull suction is the defined act, with suction curettage and suction aspiration expressly excluded; knowingly performing or attempting it supplies the separate prohibition. Overlap with some D&E does not itself establish vagueness, ban every D&E or prove substantive validity. Criminal and civil fault remain distinct; no universal Ohio default fault level, professional immunity or knowledge-of-illegality rule is supplied.

##### Postviability prohibition must preserve the presented necessary mental-health care

**Holding:** A State may prohibit abortion after viability, but it must permit abortion necessary in competent individualized medical judgment to avert grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. The patient need not await catastrophe; transient distress, unsupported assertion or mere preference does not establish the protected necessity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III.A; Souter, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct joins. Part III.B’s particular construction and relief directions receive the same joins except Stone-Zsela’s: six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Part III.B requires an Ohio-law-supported protective construction of §§2919.17(A), 2919.16(F)/(J) and genuinely dependent duties if available; otherwise the established unconstitutional exclusion remains unenforceable. Courts may neither invent an unlimited exception nor assume all protective constructions impossible. Other postviability duties are not automatically invalidated.

##### The method prohibition requires application to actual conduct, care and alternatives

**Holding:** The final method-ban disposition must be vacated where categorical notice reasoning or the displaced facial formula substituted for Casey’s substantive inquiry. On remand, the lower court must assess actual affected applications, attributable interference, usable alternative care and the distinct statutory defenses before deciding invalidity and matching relief; previability and postviability applications remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV; Souter, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The physician’s prima facie showing that every available alternative poses greater health risk triggers the prosecutor’s burden to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that at least one available alternative would not. The civil affirmative defense remains separate and receives no imported criminal burden shift. Alternatives must be usable for the patient and circumstances; no new evidence hearing is automatic.

##### Relief must follow Ohio severability law and the component judgments

**Holding:** Independently operable provisions and applications must receive Ohio’s state-law severability analysis; invalidity of one command does not itself eliminate the Act. The lower court must conform relief to the affirmed necessary-care protection, determine the unresolved applications and dependent duties separately, and promptly decide interim protection under ordinary equitable standards without automatic blanket enforcement or permanent blanket invalidation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V.A’s ordinary severability principles receive all eight Associates’ direct joins. Part V.B’s full remaining mandate receives Souter, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer; Part III.B separately supplies the six-Justice health-linked construction directions. Stone’s decision to leave precise relief and severability below does not add a join in these particular analyses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ohio §1.50, Geiger and Maurer require independent operation, fidelity to legislative intent and no judicial insertion or fundamental disruption. Viability testing begins at week twenty-two subject to its emergency exception; the week-twenty-four presumption is rebuttable and is no fixed constitutional boundary. Certifications, facility requirements, method selection, second-physician presence and dependent sanctions require their own treatment; no whole-Act validation or invalidation follows.

Under §2919.17(A), purposeful performance, inducement or attempted performance or inducement of an abortion on a viable fetus is prohibited unless a physician, acting in good faith and using reasonable medical judgment, determines either that abortion is necessary to prevent maternal death or a serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function, or that the fetus is not viable after the assessment required by §2919.18(A). The latter provision requires, beginning with the twenty-second week, a good-faith determination of nonviability using reasonable medical judgment, after examining the patient and performing or arranging the viability tests a reasonable physician would use. Its medical-emergency exception excuses that testing obligation. The separate twenty-four-week viability presumption is rebuttable.

Section 2919.16(F) defines the emergency through the physician’s good-faith, reasonable medical assessment of a pregnancy complication: immediate abortion must be needed to prevent the patient’s death or to avoid a serious risk, created by delay, of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function. Section 2919.16(J) separately defines that serious-risk category by a medically diagnosed condition complicating the pregnancy that directly or indirectly causes such impairment. Its nonexclusive examples are preeclampsia, inevitable abortion, prematurely ruptured membranes, diabetes and multiple sclerosis. These enacted categories remain distinct from the constitutional protection and the method-prohibition defenses addressed below.

The five postviability duties are the attending physician’s written necessity certification; a second physician’s written certification after reviewing records and tests; a facility with access to neonatal services; a survival-favoring method unless it creates significantly greater maternal death risk or serious risk of substantial and irreversible impairment of a major bodily function; and a second physician present to care for the fetus. Their emergency exemption permits noncompliance when its terms are satisfied; it does not require omission. The method-choice exception is distinct from the alternative-method affirmative defense.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical notice/scienter ground is vacated 8–1, all Associates supporting. Necessary-care protection is affirmed 7–2, Scalia and Thomas opposing. The method and remaining bounded mandate are remanded 5–4 by Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Protection must conform to the health holding; unresolved-method interim relief requires prompt ordinary equitable consideration without automatic prior dissolution or permanent blanket affirmance.

#### Separate Positions

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** Stone joins the complete Casey/Fargo standard and the required protection for grave, medically established danger of severe, lasting and potentially irreversible mental or emotional injury. He rejects both Salerno’s independent barrier and an independent numerical facial threshold. He would affirm invalidity of the D&X prohibition and deficient penal medical exceptions now. In his application, the definition reaches the predominant D&E method, and courts cannot cure that reach by rewriting the definition. Intentional performance supplies no adequate culpability concerning an erroneous clinical-necessity determination; an affirmative defense does not cure the initial criminal and civil exposure. He therefore opposes the categorical-notice vacatur and the Court’s decision to leave method invalidity for further substantive application. His application does not command the Court.

Stone leaves severability and precise relief to the lower court without reopening his invalidity holdings. Ohio may regulate a genuinely distinct procedure, prohibit postviability abortion outside necessary life-and-health circumstances, punish bad-faith or sufficiently culpable violations, and enforce evidence-based safety rules. His health protection excludes transient distress and unsupported assertion and creates no unrestricted physician veto. His agreement that relief belongs below does not join the Court’s particular saving-construction analysis or its interim-only treatment of unresolved method applications.

**Justice Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part; no joins.** Kennedy joins the actual-wording notice holding, serious necessary mental-health protection, its lawful protective-construction inquiry and ordinary Ohio severability principles. His retained Casey protected-choice baseline and Fargo separate position distinguish the substantive adequacy of care from the intelligibility of a rule. A method-specific health defense cannot cure a separate prohibition that excludes necessary mental-health care.

He would reverse the previability method injunction under his reasonable-regulation approach. The act-defined restriction, actual alternative procedures and enacted health defense defeat this categorical challenge absent an established denial of protected choice or necessary care. He rejects the general residual lesser-burden substantiation rule and therefore does not join the six-step method remand or continued unresolved-method protection under that mandate. He neither requires failure in every application nor finds every alternative equally safe. Supported necessary-care applications remain protected; he does not approve the entire Act. His narrower regulatory approach supplies no substitute for the Court’s controlling framework.

**Justice Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Thomas.** Scalia and Thomas join the bounded notice/fault holding and ordinary Ohio severability principles. Their Fargo position supports attention to the purposeful act and specified professional criteria, without a universal scienter cure, a bad-faith-only safe harbor or invented criminal and civil elements.

They would reverse the constitutional injunction under their continuing objection to Casey’s heightened abortion framework and their ordinary reasonable-regulation approach. They dissent from the required constitutional mental-health protection as well as the method remand. They acknowledge that Casey’s actual necessary-care rule is the controlling premise from which they dissent; they do not redescribe it as merely Doe’s statutory construction or a numerical test. Their position neither supplies a different statutory fact nor establishes professional immunity. Agreement on notice and severability adds no vote to their proposed reversal or to the Court’s health and method-remand rationales.
