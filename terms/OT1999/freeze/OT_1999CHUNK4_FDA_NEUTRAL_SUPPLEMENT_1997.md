## Enacted temporal rule

Public Law 105-115, approved November 21, 1997, section 422 has two separate commands. First, nothing in the Act or its amendments changes the question whether the Secretary has authority over any tobacco product, ingredient, or additive. Second, whatever authority exists must be exercised under the FDCA as it stood **November 20, 1997**, the day before enactment. The first sentence supplies neither a new delegation nor a new exclusion, and it is not legislative ratification of either litigating position. The second sentence identifies the governing version for the exercise of any authority; it is not merely an optional caution about a mission statement.

Section 501 generally makes the Act and its amendments effective 90 days after enactment, except where otherwise provided and except for the provisions and amendments of sections 111, 121, 125, and 307. The general date is February 19, 1998. That general effective date does not change section 422's expressly identified prior-day FDCA baseline. The complete enacted sections 406, 422, and 501 are script-copied in the corrected annex, including every mission paragraph and the general effective-date exceptions. No judicial construction of an unsettled tobacco-authority question is attributed to Congress.

## Mission correction and consequences for the neutral paths

The original `FDA-statute-393` download is a govinfo error/navigation response, not section 393. It supplies no statutory evidence. Section 406 of the verified Act actually inserts section 393(b)'s mission: timely review and appropriate marketing action; protection of public health with respect to regulated products, including foods, human and veterinary drugs, human-use devices, cosmetics, and electronic-product radiation; international coordination; and appropriate expert consultation and cooperation with the listed regulated and consuming groups. The statutory text is mandatory where it says “shall,” with its own “with respect to such products” and Secretary-appropriateness qualifications. This is not an optional aspiration. Section 406 also renumbers prior subsections and adds the compliance-plan and annual-report duties reproduced in full.

Because this mission provision was inserted by the 1997 Act, it is not part of the November 20 tobacco regime. Delete the original packet's statement “Section 393(b) states FDA's safety/effectiveness mission” as a governing premise in component 3. The lower majority did invoke this new mission wording; that accurately describes the lower opinion's reasoning but cannot establish that the new provision governs tobacco. No inference that Congress newly authorized or newly prohibited tobacco regulation may be drawn from section 406 contrary to section 422.

The underlying safety dispute remains supported by older operative text: sections 352(f), 352(j), 355(a), the earlier 355(d), 360c(a), 360f, 360h(e), and 360j(e). The parties' competing arguments about intended use, serious harm, the relevant benefits of use, labeling, combination-product treatment, and the relationship between general authority and valid regulatory methods therefore remain open. A safety-based path must identify the applicable older commands and their qualifications; the mission correction does not itself decide coverage, require a ban, or sustain the rule. A definition-based path likewise cannot invoke new 1997 flexibility to cure an older statutory constraint.
