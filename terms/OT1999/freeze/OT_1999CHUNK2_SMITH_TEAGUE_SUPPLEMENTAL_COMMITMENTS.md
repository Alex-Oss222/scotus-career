## Rule identification, temporal comparison and alternative paths

**Temporal basis.** Robbins's conviction occurred in 1990. Whatever the precise later finality day, Douglas (1963), Anders (1967), Strickland and Cronic (1984), Evitts (1985), and McCoy and Penson (1988) preceded the earliest possible finality of that conviction. The bounded old-rule analysis below depends only on those substantive sources. It does not infer a finality date from the federal petition's pre-April 24, 1996 filing. M.L.B., Peguero and January 12 Martinez may explain the Justices' present approach; they do not supply a missing prefinality substantive entitlement. Schiro, Caspari, Graham as actually retained, Lambrix and O'Dell govern the present nonretroactivity inquiry without being substituted for the earlier appellate-assistance law. No later source is needed to make the particular pre-1990 comparison.

**Path favoring the defense against the corrective-process claim.** The strongest State argument concerns the exact rule, not merely judicial disagreement. Anders and McCoy specified safeguards associated with ending appointed counsel's advocacy; Penson's no-prejudice remedy involved actual withdrawal, decision without representation, and identified nonfrivolous claims. Wende instead keeps counsel appointed, requires record review and a cited factual/procedural brief, allows personal submissions, mandates full judicial examination, and requires further briefing if a nonfrivolous issue is found. Actual appellate-project consultation occurred. Requiring another appeal without a finding of an available nonfrivolous claim could therefore be a new extension of the old cases' procedural requirement or remedy, even if one would adopt it on direct review. The fact that a requested remedy is limited to screening does not alone make it old. This argument remains substantial after the earlier supplement removed any assumed nonfrivolity finding.

**Path rejecting the defense against that claim.** The four procedure-invalid Justices identify a more specific preexisting protection than a general right to effective counsel: an indigent first appellant may not be denied represented appellate consideration through a frivolousness determination made without counsel's meaningful legal guidance, or an equivalent safeguard, in testing that determination. The advocate must help the court assess possible legal grounds, not merely furnish the factual material on which the court will make an unaided legal assessment. This does not require argument of frivolous claims, a document with prescribed wording, or the identification of a successful ground as a condition of assistance.

Anders, 386 U.S. at 742–745, rejected counsel's conclusion plus the court's own record review as an adequate substitute and required meaningful advocacy in deciding whether further represented consideration could be withheld. Its reversal and remand did not rest on a finding by this Court that an available nonfrivolous trial claim had been established. McCoy, 486 U.S. at 439 and note 11, 442–444, makes the legal-guidance requirement concrete: the process must allow the court to determine both whether counsel searched diligently for arguable claims and whether the frivolousness conclusion is sound. Its distinction between a screening submission and a merits brief prevents equating the assistance now sought with compulsory merits advocacy on a frivolous claim. Penson, 488 U.S. at 81–83, separately explains the same screening functions before addressing the additional failure to appoint counsel after finding arguable claims.

For Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, Wende's safeguards do not perform the missing function. Counsel's factual/procedural narrative and certification show work, and continuing appointment preserves availability, but neither supplies counsel's legal explanation of the possible grounds to the tribunal making the decisive frivolousness assessment. Further advocacy is conditioned on the court first discovering a ground without that assistance. Actual project consultation does not establish that the court received an equivalent legal assessment, or a uniform substitute in the procedure. These Justices treat that functional omission, rather than the absence of a withdrawal order or an Anders label, as decisive under the old cases. They do not treat Wende as identical in all respects to the procedure Anders rejected.

**The remedial comparison is independent.** These four do not simply transport Penson's actual-nonfrivolous-appeal presumption into this record. They find the omitted counsel-assisted legal screening to be the entire necessary function at the stage deciding whether further advocacy will be supplied. Requiring Robbins first to establish through the deficient process the available legal claim that adequate screening should identify would make the safeguard depend on the assessment it exists to test. Anders's restoration of adequate consideration, together with its rule that a properly screened appeal may still be found wholly frivolous, supplies the direct pre-1990 remedial basis. Penson and the actual-or-constructive-denial distinction in Strickland/Cronic reinforce that distinction from a lawyer's defective presentation of a particular issue; they are not a substitute for establishing the missing function here.

Thus, on these four Justices' substantive appraisal of Wende, both meaningful counsel-assisted screening and restoration of that process were dictated before any possible finality of Robbins's 1990 conviction. They reject the State's defense as to this defined rule even assuming the defense is properly entertainable. They neither decline it on an invented waiver nor decide its preservation/fair-inclusion history. Their conclusion is stronger than that the rule is desirable or merely consistent with precedent; it rests on the two specified screening functions and the prior corrective remedy. The opposing four dispute the asserted functional deprivation because counsel actually remained appointed and performed substantial work. Their disagreement is not itself the Teague test.

**Path accepting Wende.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas find the whole procedure constitutionally adequate for the reasons already frozen. Their votes reverse the existing categorical appellate-assistance ground without applying the protection claimed by Robbins to grant relief; they have no need to choose an additional Teague ground for the same result. They therefore reserve the new-rule classification of the contrary procedure-wide protection and its automatic corrective remedy. This is not a holding that a court may always disregard a properly asserted nonretroactivity defense before announcing new constitutional law.

Their remand allows adjudication of the distinct individual claim that counsel unreasonably failed to identify and pursue an available nonfrivolous ground, with ordinary Strickland performance and reasonable-probability prejudice. Strickland, Evitts and Penson already distinguish that claim from total denial before 1990. The State's assertion that the procedure-wide relief would be new does not itself establish that this ordinary claim is barred. No global finding makes every conceivable later elaboration of appellate ineffectiveness retroactive, and no writ may issue on a genuinely different new rule merely because it bears a Strickland label. Any materially distinct rule actually relied on for relief must meet its own applicable habeas requirements. No present automatic denial or new factual hearing follows.
