## Historical verification and the decisive distinctions

The [Cornell syllabus](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/98-1648.ZS.html) was retrieved first, followed by each full writing: [Thomas plurality](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/98-1648.ZO.html), [O'Connor concurrence](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/98-1648.ZC.html), and [Souter dissent](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/98-1648.ZD.html). Every writing and all footnotes were read, with the [official reporter, 530 U.S. 793-913](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-530/pdf/USREPORTS-530-793.pdf), used to verify operative passages and pagination. The supplied comparator truncates the concurrence lineup. Complete history is six supporting reversal: Thomas's plurality with Rehnquist, Scalia and Kennedy; O'Connor concurring in the judgment with Breyer; Souter dissenting with Stevens and Ginsburg. The plurality's complete theory was not a six-Justice holding. No historical author or join is imported as a current assignment.

**The operational premise alone does not change the historical plurality.** Thomas expressly accepted evidence of actual diversion and deficient monitoring, then deemed those matters constitutionally irrelevant where eligibility was permissible and the supplied aid lacked religious content. That is the distinction between content supplied by government and religious use of otherwise secular equipment, not a finding that diversion had never happened. See 530 U.S. 820-825, 832-835 and nn.15-18. The opinion treated per-student allocations following school enrollment as sufficient private direction and used historical Zobrest's interpreter merits holding centrally to explain why a publicly supplied medium could convey religious instruction without governmental indoctrination. It separately regarded the recalled religious books as minor, corrected content violations. An account saying Scalia, Kennedy or Thomas changes only because actual use replaces hypothetical risk would therefore be false.

**O'Connor and Breyer made a different evidentiary and remedial judgment.** They rejected neutrality as a sufficient condition and rejected constitutional indifference to actual diversion. They distinguished individual beneficiary direction from school-based per-capita allocation, rejected a presumption of inevitable diversion, found adequate safeguards, and considered the historical diversion evidence insufficient or at most insignificant. The recalled books showed correction, not failure warranting wholesale invalidation. Their statement that the evidence did not justify invalidating the entire program did not license the proved use of public resources to promote religious doctrine. See 530 U.S. 837-849, 857-867, especially 864-866. The present adopted actual-financing premise removes that evidentiary uncertainty only for the supported application. It does not establish extensive violations or defeat their objection to program-wide relief.

**Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg had broader objections than actual use alone.** The historical dissent examined conscience, institutional religious integrity and public support; it treated evenhandedness as relevant but insufficient, examined the aid's path, content, adaptability, recipient function and safeguards, and found both substantial risk and actual diversion. It did not claim that every public benefit to every religious recipient was forbidden. See 530 U.S. 868-899, 903-913, including 909 n.27. The adopted premise reinforces their actual-use objection; the material present difference is their own intervening adoption of Jackson's conflict-specific financed-function rule and remedy, with Strout's express protection of genuinely severable secular relationships. That supports changing the categorical remedial scope without pretending that they have abandoned all structural concerns.

## Test of the three historically approving plurality Justices

The strongest objection to their initial positions is substantial: each historically accepted actual religious use of neutral equipment, and each still dissents from Jackson and Strout's treatment of genuinely selected individual educational benefits. Mere public title, a direct loan, or a court's instruction to obey precedent would not independently justify changing their historical position. Nor does the adopted premise itself adjudicate attribution or constitutionality.

The supportable changed legal premise is more specific. Each personally joined the simulated Agostini opinion's unified inquiry into the governmental act and funded function, including the rule that neutral eligibility and public employment do not excuse actual devotional instruction or diversion, and that public purchases of secular work require real operational separation and reliable accountability. Each also joined Kennedy's simulated Rosenberger opinion on the combined private-editing, independent-attribution and controlled-payment relationship, expressly declining a general permission for devotional financing. Simulated Zobrest supplies only an antecedent-law remand; the historical plurality's principal adjudicated example of a government-provided medium actually transmitting religious instruction is absent. The omission is not proof that a similar interpreter program would be unconstitutional, but it removes the claimed precedent requiring indifference to use.

Here the public program acquires and administers specified secular instructional resources at schools' requests. Enrollment determines allocation; it does not establish that an individual beneficiary selects whether the financed equipment will perform the religious function. That matters under their own distinction between genuine individual direction and public performance or purchase. It preserves their Jackson/Strout dissents rather than treating defeat there as conversion: those dissents relied on an actually family-selected educational benefit, independent school operation and bounded individual payment, while rejecting a universal parental-signature or neutrality safe harbor. No corresponding individual direction of the present religious use is established. Their prior Agostini rule therefore supplies the narrower classification for this actual publicly administered instructional-resource use. The historical alternative remains the strongest counterargument, but it would now require extending a private-benefit principle beyond its demonstrated individual-direction predicate and retreating from their own explicit operational-control commitment.

As corroboration of that distinction, Kennedy's pre-divergence [Bowen concurrence, joined by Scalia, 487 U.S. 624-625](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-487/pdf/USREPORTS-487-589.pdf), was read in full: recipient religious character is insufficient; actual use of the grant is the relevant as-applied inquiry. That earlier writing alone is not a changed premise because it also preceded historical Mitchell. The material later development is their own simulated Agostini reaffirmation of actual funded-function limits without the historical Zobrest merits bridge. Thomas's distinct later Southworth opinion also expressly preserved Jackson/Strout and denied that neutrality alone authorizes a devotional purchase; its independent-expression result does not convert instructional loans into a forum.

Accordingly, the initial narrow correction positions of Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas survive reconciliation, but the original account must carry this doctrinal explanation rather than suggesting that history merely lacked actual-use proof. No broader abandonment of their private-benefit positions is found.

## Components, joins and writing willingness

**Common supported core, all eight:** The adopted actual financing or demonstrated religious use under recipient control is established for this application, not inferred from affiliation, possible diversion, fungibility or savings. Under the funded-function rule it requires correction; neutral allocations, secular labels and public title do not cure it. Conversely, equipment or library-book form and religious affiliation alone cannot justify the lower court's entire categorical prohibition. Vacate that reviewed declaration and remand for effective relief against the actual defect, preserving lawful severable secular applications. The remand does not reopen whether the adopted fact occurred.

**Design and safeguards:** Public administration and title, secular/neutral/nonideological content, independent provision, and supplement-not-supplant limits remain actual statutory commands. They support lawful operation but do not conclusively prove compliance in every application. The statutory content rule, actual-use restriction and supplement rule remain distinct; not every statutory violation is independently an Establishment Clause violation. Neither a new universal failsafe-monitoring requirement nor a demand for separate corporate governance or wholly different personnel follows. Nor does absence of universal risk permit the established financed religious function to continue. The sufficient rule identifies the function bought, not government authorship of every recipient's religious act.

**O'Connor and Breyer:** Will join the common rule, definite recognition of the actual defect and limited correction without categorical religious-school exclusion. Require retention of the difference between proved unconstitutional use and the prevalence needed for broader relief. They will not join neutrality-only approval, automatic risk-based invalidation or a requirement that all recipients undergo newly prescribed surveillance. Their prior and historical reasoning permits a complete common opinion; no separate writing is required if these limits are stated. Breyer does not acquire Souter's institutional-support theory through grouping.

**Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas:** Will join this common actual-function application and the rejection of category-based exclusion, with genuine private benefits and independently controlled expression expressly preserved. They withhold any broader theory that religious teachers, school integration, direct delivery, fungibility or possible diversion alone proves unconstitutional financing. They do not accept universal attribution of private schooling to government, elimination of Witters/Mueller's actual holdings, or a revival of independent Lemon prongs. Kennedy may explain the individual-benefit distinction; Scalia and Thomas may join it. Separate writing is optional if a common opinion already carries the required distinction, but would become necessary if an opinion purports to repudiate their preserved Jackson/Strout grounds. None joins an unlimited constitutional entitlement to all religious uses of public benefits.

**Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg:** Will join effective relief against the financed religious function and protection of lawful separable services, without treating neutrality as sufficient. Their structural concerns survive: the source, path, control, actual institutional relationship and operation of instructional support remain relevant evidence. They refuse a proposition that only publicly attributed religious speech can violate the financing prohibition, that direct support is harmless whenever neutral, or that any operational scrutiny is religious hostility. Their narrower common ground does not affirmatively approve every historical administrative practice. They may state their stronger institutional concerns separately if the common opinion unnecessarily rejects them; a separate writing is not required merely because the narrower sufficient rule is shared. Ginsburg's procedural Agostini objection is not imported into this vehicle.

**Precedent scope:** Meek and Wolman cannot retain a conclusive equipment/library-book prohibition insofar as it condemns otherwise reliably separated secular use by reference to religious recipient character. The eight support express displacement at that conflicting scope. They do not agree to erase every safeguard or unrelated holding of those cases. Agostini's actual secular-service qualifications survive; Jackson and Strout supply their supported applications, not universal voucher holdings. Rosenberger and Southworth protect their actual independent-expression relationships. Zobrest has no current establishment merits holding; historical citations to it cannot be presented as current controlling law. No Marks synthesis of historical writings is needed to create the present common position, and no one is deemed to adopt an unjoined broader rationale.

## Remedy and departure account for assembly

The reviewed federal Chapter 2 declaration is vacated and the case remanded to determine the provider/item/application extent, lawful severability and effective prospective correction of the established violation. Courts may use the existing record where sufficient. Neither an automatic new trial nor new simulated filings are necessary to re-prove the adopted premise. The fixed fact establishes no particular school, count of items, quantitative prevalence, statewide defect or absence of effective severability. It cannot support immediate universal exclusion, school closure, damages or recoupment. It also cannot be treated as nothing more than an already cured historical book incident. Relief must actually prevent the supported financed religious use.

Only the supplied federal grant is adjudicated. Treatment of any Louisiana counterpart must follow a demonstrated legal dependence on that federal disposition; no unrelated program or separate historical docket is resolved merely because it appeared in the larger litigation. No threshold or participation obstacle is established by the authorized inputs.

**Historical departure:** O'Connor and Breyer move from historical program approval to confined corrective vacatur because the adopted actual-use fact resolves the uncertainty central to their historical concurrence, while their own Jackson rule and remedy supply correction without whole-program invalidation. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg retain the constitutional objection but replace historical affirmance of the categorical declaration with confined vacatur because each later personally joined Jackson's secular-severability and conflict-specific remedy and Strout's protected-secular-service limits. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas depart from their historical express tolerance of actual diversion because their own simulated Agostini commitments require accountable secular use of public instructional resources, their Rosenberger commitments protect a distinct controlled private-expression relationship, and simulated Zobrest supplies none of the historical interpreter-merits premise on which indifference to religious use rested; their preserved individual-benefit dissents do not establish beneficiary direction of this publicly administered religious use. The operational premise alone does not explain their departure.
