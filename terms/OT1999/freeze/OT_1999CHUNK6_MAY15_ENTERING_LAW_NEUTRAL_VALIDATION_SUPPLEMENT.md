## Earlier holdings now available at their actual scope

**Mining, May 8.** Ordinary section 510(c) blocking requires actual current ownership/control and a current violation, including qualifying downstream control. The separate former-control provision retains its cumulative willfulness, pattern, nature, duration, irreparable-damage and intent requirements. A three-year notice schedule is not a universal violation-age limit, and satisfactory correction in progress can permit issuance without completed abatement. Anti-evasion policy does not erase those statutory distinctions.

Its limited temporal holding permits otherwise lawful present eligibility rules addressing actual current control and a current violation despite antecedent acquisition and violation dates. A present application label alone cannot sanitize a new disability based solely on completed conduct. Smiley's qualified authoritative-interpretation rule does not backdate regulations, displace clear contrary official guidance, or create general retroactive legislative-rule power. The actual common ground does not merge the previously distinct Landgraf approaches. No particular permit, clear-guidance displacement or actual reliance finding is decided.

Its correction holding rests on express statutory suspension, revocation and withholding authority, subject to lawful substantive/temporal predicates and state-primacy procedures. It supplies neither unlimited implied reopening power nor a general finality exception. Ordinary notice, abatement, hearing-opportunity, written-finding and cessation-order requirements remain distinct from imminent-danger procedures. The ungranted control presumptions, burdens, section 2462 and separate procedural litigation remain outside the new holdings. The complete public copy, not a shortened analogy, supplies these qualifications.

**Saenz, May 9.** An affirmative official command that actually interrupts available lawful protection and thereby creates or materially increases exposure to known danger can present an official-deprivation claim with the required culpability and attributable causation. Custody, the victims' known identities, and the private origin of the immediate injury are not categorical prerequisites. Conversely, affirmative phrasing, callousness, foreseeable harm, ordinary nonprotection, an unavailable response, insufficient culpability, or an instruction without the required causal effect does not establish that deprivation. No general duty to investigate or arrest, universal noncustodial-danger test, or minimum fault rule for every lesser account is adopted.

Five Justices adopt the affirmative-interference and county-remand holdings. Nine adopt objective, particularized conduct-time immunity analysis from all relevant law available June 19, 1995, without a factual-twin or Supreme-Court-only requirement and without later-law notice. The lead opinion's additional immunity application has only four votes; no majority resolves Gonzalez's immunity on the entire qualifying account. County policy/custom, attribution and causation remain separate from the officer's personal immunity. Vacatur guarantees neither liability nor discovery, hearing, trial or damages, and preserves genuinely independent grounds. The public separate views are available as published positions at their exact scope; their joins do not predict any May 15 vote.

### Johnson

Mining's current-condition/completed-conduct distinction is materially relevant, but its civil permit-eligibility holding does not classify postrevocation punishment. The model must independently identify the consequence's connection to the original offense, Congress's temporal direction, and the actual authority conferred by offense-date section 3583. Later misconduct does not automatically make an added criminal consequence prospective regulation; antecedent facts alone likewise do not complete a retroactivity argument. Express mining correction authority cannot create sentencing power. The separate March 1 Johnson supervision-start rule and Carmell proof-rule holding remain limited as the base packet states.

Saenz likewise separates a present constitutional rule from conduct-date notice. That separation is relevant as a limited temporal analogy, not as a construction of section 3583, an Ex Post Facto rule, or a new reason to equate qualified immunity with penal lenity. Its immunity application has no majority holding to import. The alternatives concerning original-offense versus violation-date reference and old-law sentencing authority remain genuinely open for modeling. **Ready for independent modeling with the actual Mining rule and this limited Saenz distinction.**

### Public Lands Council

Mining directly reinforces analysis of all connected statutory commands, separately qualified routes, valid authority and required process. Administrative change within delegated power is not automatically invalid; a policy preference cannot erase Taylor Act safeguards or valid existing rights. Mining's expressly conferred mining-permit correction power is not a general power to reopen grazing permits, seize improvement title, or disregard the grazing rules' own conditions.

The three presented rules retain separate predicates: priority/permitted use, deletion of a commercial-business qualification without erasing stock-owner eligibility, and prospective title under cooperative agreements with existing-rights and contribution protections. Saenz's official-interference and immunity doctrines decide none of those statutory questions. Its insistence that a remand does not guarantee additional proceedings creates no basis to expand this grant to the unpresented arbitrary-and-capricious or conservation-use questions. **Ready for independent component-by-component modeling with Mining added at its real statutory scope.**

### McIver

Neither Mining nor Saenz changes the governing search/seizure definitions, automobile-exception scope, conceded noncurtilage location, monitoring-preservation boundary, or exclusion/harmlessness requirements. Saenz recognizes a possible constitutional wrong involving affirmative official interference, but it does so under due process with its own protection, danger, culpability and causation predicates. That holding does not make every physical governmental intervention a Fourth Amendment search or seizure, resolve the magnetic attachment issue, or replace the actual Katz/Jacobsen/Karo/Soldal/Bond comparisons.

Nor does Saenz's civil summary-judgment remand supply a new criminal evidentiary opportunity. The distinction between house-derived and tracking-derived information and the separate harmlessness question remain as validated. No automatic culpability, immunity, warrant-exception or evidence ruling is transplanted. **Ready for independent modeling on the unchanged substantive Fourth Amendment baseline and retained source/preservation limits.**

### Morrison

Mining adds no Commerce Clause or Fourteenth Amendment section 5 holding. Delegated administrative discretion cannot substitute for Congress's constitutional authority. Its statutory discipline does not itself decide the fit of this federal civil remedy.

Saenz is relevant to the distinction between **an official's own affirmative deprivation** and **private harm that government did not prevent**. The private identity of the immediate injurer does not categorically defeat a claim where an official actually removes available protection with the required causal and culpable relation. But Saenz retains DeShaney's rejection of a general protective duty and requires the official's own attributable intervention; it does not classify every private assault, discriminatory enforcement allegation, or omission as a due-process violation. Its county holding also preserves independent attribution and causation rather than liability by association.

That distinction leaves the section 5 issue to the actual equal-protection guarantee, statutory cause of action, documented state discrimination, and the Boerne/Florida Prepaid preventive-remedial framework. The alleged private assailants in Morrison are not transformed into state actors by Saenz, and no governmental interference, county policy or causal removal of protection is supplied as a fact. Conversely, Saenz's actual-state-action requirements for an individual direct constitutional claim cannot be converted into a rule that every person reached by valid section 5 prophylaxis must independently have committed a provable constitutional violation. The state-action supplement already identifies the nineteenth-century cases' corrective-legislation and prospective-remedy qualifications; they remain necessary.

Proponents may distinguish private immediate injury from the state constitutional harm Congress says it is remedying, while challengers may require the enacted defendant, trigger, coverage and relief to remain adequately connected to that state harm. Those are supported analytical paths, not findings that either connection exists or fails. Saenz decides neither Congress's authority to impose this remedy on private assailants nor the constitutional fit of nationwide coverage. It supplies no Commerce Clause mechanism, no new statutory state-failure element, no severability result, and no present-case vote. **Ready for independent modeling with the actual Saenz boundary and the verified state-action supplement.**
