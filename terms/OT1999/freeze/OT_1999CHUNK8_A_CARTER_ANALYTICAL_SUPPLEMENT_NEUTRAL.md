## Record and law that the alternative must preserve

The indictment alleges approximately $14,000 in a knowing and willful taking by force, violence, and intimidation under section 2113(a). The trial account describes approximately $16,000. The requested bank-larceny charge at JA4–9 contains a value element using the obsolete $100 threshold. The governing statute uses **$1,000**. Neither the actual request nor the indictment is rewritten by this supplement.

The statutory text separately describes taking and carrying away with intent to steal or purloin in section 2113(b), followed by the higher and lower value formulations and their respective penalties. The charged first paragraph of section 2113(a) lacks the separate entry paragraph's express intent-to-commit wording. Whether its taking offense nevertheless includes theft intent and movement requires statutory construction, not transplantation from the entry offense. Staples and X-Citement Video support contextual attention to factual culpability; neither mechanically supplies a specific intent to steal for every knowing taking offense.

Schmuck's binding Rule 31(c) elements test remains the starting rule. The correction is mandatory: Scalia's dissent concerns the mail-fraud mailing nexus; it supplies no recorded objection by Scalia or O'Connor to the elements test. No current position may be inferred from that mistaken attribution or from silence about an issue.

Almendarez-Torres and Jones establish statutory aggravated-element and charging principles at their stated scope. Castillo applies the same principles to the former firearm-type provision. A grade-defining element must receive the required indictment and jury treatment; admitting a historical fact is not automatically a valid admission as an element of the charged offense, and an admission or jury waiver does not automatically cure an indictment omission. These decisions do not create a general constitutional rule for every maximum-increasing fact. They also do not decide this particular Rule 31(c) election.

## Validated analytical alternatives and their objections

**First distinguish the grades.** If the higher-value requirement is an element of felony bank larceny, its presence is an obstacle to finding that grade necessarily included in robbery without a value requirement. It does not alone establish that basic lower-grade larceny is outside Rule 31(c). The lower paragraph must receive its own construction: does it demand affirmative proof that value does not exceed $1,000, or does it supply the basic theft offense when no aggravated value finding is required? The latter argument avoids making theft of more money a defense to the basic offense. Its strongest textual objection is the enacted lower-value language itself. A court must explain the provision's operation, proof requirement, and penalty consequences; it cannot erase that language by describing the lower offense as basic. Intent and movement remain independent inclusion questions on either reading.

**Then distinguish statutory inclusion from an election of a higher grade.** If basic larceny is necessarily included after those questions are resolved, a defendant might argue that his request can elect the higher grade of that already included crime while putting the value element to the jury. That is a proposed limited rule, not a conclusion that the higher grade is itself a statutory subset of robbery. The limiting premise is inclusion of the underlying theft after a genuine elements comparison, rather than factual relatedness alone.

The proposed rule encounters Schmuck's explicit reciprocity objection: an approach available to a defendant through waiver of notice but unavailable to the prosecution was a stated difficulty with the rejected inherent-relationship test. Calling the new proposal an election does not answer that objection. An adopting court would have to explain why grade selection within an independently included crime is materially different, or expressly identify the scope in which it limits the existing reciprocity principle. A defendant's request, standing alone, cannot be treated as a preexisting exception to Rule 31(c).

**The indictment problem is separate from notice.** Stirone supplies the protection against prosecutorial or judicial enlargement of the grand jury's actual charge; it does not itself resolve this defendant-requested grade election. Accurate awareness of the trial facts, legal sufficiency of the indictment, consent to a proposed instruction, waiver of grand-jury protection, admission of an element, and waiver of jury determination are distinct. This record does not establish all of them merely because Carter requested larceny or admitted taking money.

The indictment actually alleges approximately $14,000. That allegation does not convert the Rule 31(c) statutory inquiry into an indictment-specific elements test. It also means that an indictment-defect analysis cannot falsely say that the indictment contains no value allegation. Any argument that the existing indictment legally charges all elements of a particular larceny grade must assess its full allegations under the adopted offense construction, rather than decide the issue solely from its statutory citation or treat factual notice as sufficient. No conclusion about that independent charging question is adopted here.

If relief instead depends on an actual waiver of indictment protection, the court must identify a legally sufficient waiver rule and its supported procedural predicates. The present material establishes an instruction request, not a completed formal waiver under an identified procedure. A hypothetical election cannot retrospectively manufacture a historical waiver or a new preserved motion. The request's obsolete threshold likewise needs lawful treatment; the Court cannot silently substitute a corrected request and then treat that substitution as a historical fact.

**Instruction entitlement remains independent.** Statutory inclusion, a legally permissible higher-grade election, and the evidence permitting a rational jury to convict of larceny while acquitting of robbery are different issues. The Government's reported evidence argument is independent of its noninclusion ground; Carter disputes its application. A showing that the evidence supports robbery is not automatically the same inquiry as whether a rational jury could choose the included offense. Conversely, factual support for larceny cannot itself create statutory inclusion or a lawful waiver.

## Readiness and remedial limits

**Ready for independent modeling as a proposed legal alternative with substantial identified objections.** The supplement is validated for consideration, not certified as an already established route to relief. A model may accept or reject the lower-grade construction, the separate intent or movement constructions, and the limited election argument for supported reasons. It must not assume that accepting felony value as an element resolves every other issue, or that a request automatically supplies a waiver exception.

Any decision removing a categorical legal bar must separately determine what remains of request scope, preservation, the actual instruction's threshold, the independent evidence question, and any necessary remedial inquiry. It does not by itself require acquittal, immediate conviction for larceny, or an automatic new trial. Rejecting the higher-grade election does not silently decide that the basic offense contains an affirmative upper-value element. No new default, concession, factual finding, or procedural compliance is supplied by this neutral supplement.
