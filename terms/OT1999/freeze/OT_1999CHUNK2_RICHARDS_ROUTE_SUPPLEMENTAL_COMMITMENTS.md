## Governing distinction after Stanley

Stanley, 483 U.S. 669, 682–684, confirms that once an injury is properly classified as incident to service, the government need not additionally prove that this particular action will disrupt discipline or expose a particular superior–subordinate relationship. A suit-specific inquiry into military disruption can itself cause the intrusion the rule seeks to avoid. That conclusion is consistent with Johnson and prevents making the panel's doubts about actual discipline effects dispositive after a finding of service incidence.

Stanley's Part II concerned an implied constitutional damages remedy, and its service-incidence premise had already been decided against that plaintiff. It did not adjudicate the FTCA merits through Part I's interlocutory-jurisdiction disposition, classify every trip between military properties as incident to service, or settle Richards's still-open classification. Scalia's footnote 5 expressly preserves the difference between the FTCA's statutory authorization and judicial implication of a constitutional remedy. O'Connor accepted the general service-incident limitation but proposed a distinct exception for nonconsensual human experimentation; that separate position does not supply an ordinary-road-negligence exception. Stevens joined Part I and Part III of Brennan's separate writing, not the whole Brennan opinion. No Stanley join is attributed to Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg or Breyer.

The claim distinctions below concern whether the injury asserted through a particular alleged wrong falls within the surviving Feres–Brooks boundary, including Shearer's command-supervision application. They do **not** create a second, individualized discipline-disruption test that a plaintiff may use to escape Feres after the applicable injury has been classified as service-incident. Nor do they make absence of a current assigned task a necessary condition of the bar. Minns's independent §2680(a) rule remains unchanged; the route account establishes neither protected policy discretion nor a mandatory maintenance or licensing instruction.

### Stevens

**Result and ground.** Confirm partial affirmance, partial reversal and remand. On-post residence and a principal connecting route give the government a closer analogy to military living arrangements than the earlier packet expressly supplied. Nevertheless, even credited for argument, they do not make Richards's private automobile journey after authorized release an activity furnished, commanded or undertaken as military service. The injury under the operation and roadworthy-equipment theories arises from use of an ordinary public highway during that private journey; its endpoints alone do not make it like service medical care or injury from the military provision of barracks.

**Own authorities and competing path.** His Shearer Part II-B join preserves the personnel/assignment dismissal; his Johnson dissent join and Minns join to Ginsburg oppose enlarging Feres from military connection alone. His Stencel join remains consistent because that case began with a service-incident injury. The strongest affirmance argument is now the combination of nonfurlough status and movement between duty facility and on-post home, not merely the accident's geography. He rejects treating that combination as sufficient for these road-safety theories after the accepted release, without denying that other work-to-quarters journeys may be incident to service. His limited Stanley separate-opinion join does not license him to demand particular discipline disruption in every FTCA case. The decisive disagreement remains classification; no new factual hearing or residence finding is necessary to his result.

### O'Connor

**Result and ground.** Confirm whole affirmance. The established immediate departure from the duty station within the installation while remaining on active duty without pass or furlough already supplies the service relationship in her application of Feres and Johnson. If the government account is assumed, travel toward an on-post home on the installation's principal connecting road strengthens that relationship: the journey remains within the setting of military work and residence. Her vote does not depend on finding that additional account true.

**Own authorities and competing path.** Her Johnson majority join, Shearer join and Minns concurrence support a bar independent of particular protected discretion. Her Stanley writing expressly accepts a general service-incident limit; its exceptional human-experimentation conclusion does not encompass this accident. The strongest opposing argument is that permission ended the day's work and that a publicly available road, private car and family purpose place the operation claim within Brooks. She continues to reject that application because those circumstances do not sever this particular immediate on-base departure from service. She does not require proof of particular disruption, does not make every on-post residence injury barred, and does not make formal furlough indispensable in every case outside Feres. The command theories remain additionally barred under Shearer.

### Scalia

**Result and ground.** Confirm partial affirmance, partial reversal and remand even on the government's arguendo account. The fact that an off-duty personal destination is within the installation strengthens but does not complete the legal service-incidence inference. The accepted release and family purpose, private vehicle and ordinary public road remain sufficient to place operation and equipment safety outside Feres in this setting. Neither the road's principal connecting function nor the home's location establishes that the travel itself was required military activity. This is not a claim that an assigned order is indispensable in every Feres case.

**Own authorities and competing path.** His Johnson dissent and Minns published positions resist adding military exclusions beyond the surviving precedent. His authorship of Stanley creates the strongest additional objection to a reasoning-based separation of claims: he rejected inquiries into whether a particular suit would actually disrupt discipline. He therefore expressly refuses that basis for reversal. His conclusion rests instead on the threshold not being met for these traffic and equipment theories, even after the geographic account is assumed. Stanley did not reopen that threshold in its own case; footnote 5 preserves his statutory objection in Johnson. Shearer's existing command holding still governs the permission and negligent-order allegations. He does not withdraw that partial affirmance, overrule Feres, or infer an actionable directive or a statutory-discretion defense.

### Kennedy

**Result and ground.** Confirm whole affirmance. The added account describes a more specifically intrainstallation journey from military work to home than the first packet expressly identified. That reinforces his characterization of the accepted trip as immediately connected with service despite its private purpose. The existing facts already sustain his vote, so there is no need to authenticate the home location before affirming on his ground.

**Own authorities and competing path.** His Minns Parts I and II joins required attention to the actual relation between an allegation and the military activity; they reserved Feres rather than settling this question. The service relationship here is the injured member's own immediate on-base departure from duty, not a child's separately manifested injury. The best reversal argument remains that neither an on-post home nor an often-used connecting road eliminates ordinary personal travel or public-road duties. He accepts that neither fact is categorical, but finds the complete circumstances sufficiently service-related under Feres and Johnson. Stanley reinforces that, after that classification, lack of a demonstrated superior–subordinate dispute supplies no additional escape. He joins that limited use of Stanley, not an FTCA holding falsely attributed to its Part I or an all-military-property rule. The command-theory affirmance remains independently supported by Shearer.

### Souter

**Result and ground.** Confirm partial affirmance, partial reversal and remand. An on-post home removes any possible inference that Richards was necessarily about to leave the installation. His earlier commitment did not rest on that inference, and he now expressly rejects it as a premise. Even if the journey remained wholly within Fort Knox, permitted private travel in a private car on this public road does not become service activity for the operation and equipment-safety claims merely because the road joins military facilities and the home lies on the post.

**Own authorities and competing path.** His own relevant position is his join to Ginsburg's Minns opinion and Thomas's Part I: military causal origin and actual policy dependence must be distinguished, and distinct execution allegations cannot simply be collapsed into the same legal ground. The strengthened contrary case is that the Army work-and-residence setting itself supplies service incidence, much as the setting of the barracks did in Feres. He finds the distinction sufficient here because the wrong concerns road safety during authorized personal movement, rather than the military provision or use of living facilities, required transportation or a service mission. He adds no requirement of showing concrete disruption after a service-incident classification; Stanley forecloses using the panel's discipline reservation that way. Shearer still disposes of the actual personnel/assignment theories, and the additional route facts do not alter them.

### Thomas

**Result and ground.** Confirm whole affirmance. Richards's direct relationship to his military duty station, immediate departure within Fort Knox and continued active-duty status without pass or furlough remain sufficient to classify this injury as incident to service. The asserted journey to an on-post home reinforces that application but is not indispensable. Accordingly his vote requires no adverse factual choice about the government's new source account.

**Own authorities and competing path.** Thomas's own Minns opinion distinguishes an established legal exclusion from an allegation's description as ordinary execution. Its Feres reservation did not settle the threshold presented here. The best opposition is that personal use of a public highway should remain within Brooks despite work and home both being on military land. He rejects the proposed Brooks application on the whole established setting, while conceding that neither endpoint alone establishes service incidence. Stanley strengthens the distinction between classifying the injury and proving disruption in a particular trial. He does not treat it as adjudicating Richards's classification or as a new discretionary-function rule. He retains the independent Shearer ground for driver selection and negligent orders; the maintenance and operation claims need no separate §2680(a) holding once his Feres ground applies.

### Ginsburg

**Result and ground.** Confirm partial affirmance, partial reversal and remand even if the government route and residence description is accepted for argument. The addition makes the connection with military living and working arrangements more concrete. It does not remove the affirmative authorized release or change the journey's accepted personal object. For the road-operation and equipment-safety claims, an on-post home and a road's connecting function do not establish the relation between injury and service that Feres requires.

**Own authorities and competing path.** Her Minns opinion distinguishes military connection from the actual legal character of a claimed wrong, while her join to Thomas's Part I preserves genuine statutory exclusions. Here she does not rely on the children's civilian status or recreate Minns's separate bodily-injury claim: all family losses remain derivative. The strongest affirmance argument is that the combined work-to-residence setting supplies service incidence before the court ever examines the type of negligence. She rejects that application to the accepted private journey, not the rule that an established service-incident injury would be barred without additional proof of disruption. Stanley thus qualifies the explanation but does not change the disposition. The unlicensed-driver permission and negligent-order theories continue to challenge the Army's command decisions within Shearer, whereas routine roadworthy-equipment allegations cannot acquire that character solely from the new travel geography.

### Breyer

**Result and ground.** Confirm whole affirmance. Travel from a military work facility toward an on-post home on the route principally joining them, assumed only for comparison, makes the functional relationship to service stronger. His result already follows from the established immediate on-base departure from duty in continuing nonfurlough status; the additional assertion therefore need not be found true.

**Own authorities and competing path.** His Minns Parts I and II joins attend to what litigation of the actual allegations would require, but did not adopt Feres there. His present application of surviving Feres and Johnson classifies this direct servicemember injury as service-incident. The strongest opposing position is that private family travel and a road open to everyone remain ordinary civilian activity despite the endpoints. He accepts that this would defeat any categorical military-geography rule, but finds it insufficient to sever the particular relationship presented. Stanley confirms that the absence of special discipline costs in an ordinary traffic trial does not defeat a bar once that relationship is established. He adopts no inference that every military-maintenance decision is protected discretion and no damages comparison based on benefit amounts. Shearer remains an additional sufficient basis for the two personnel/assignment theories.

## Operative scope, remedy and preservation

No Associate changes the prior component disposition. Four confirm whole affirmance on the already established service circumstances: O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. Four confirm affirmance of the two personnel/assignment dismissals and reversal with remand of the operation, maintenance and knowingly unsafe public-road-use dismissals even on the added geographic account: Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg. Those four do not require that Richards intended to cross the base boundary, lived off base, or had civilian legal status. Their sufficient ground is the threshold application described above, not a right to relitigate particular military disruption after Feres applies.

All eight retain the actual Shearer command-theory conclusion. Mere use of the word supervision would not suffice in another case; here the complaint challenges the Army's permission and order for the allegedly unqualified soldier to carry out the driving assignment. The route addition does not alter what those theories challenge. None of these positions establishes protected policy choice under §2680(a), and no Justice infers a binding directive or negligence finding.

On partial reversal, the surviving claims return for ordinary proceedings with the admitted Potts scope-of-employment fact intact. Negligence, causation, local tort law, damages, contributory negligence and properly presented independent FTCA defenses remain open. A genuinely material unresolved destination question remains for lawful proceedings; no new hearing, production order, discovery entitlement, filing deadline or automatic benefit deduction is required. A purely source-driven remand to discover residence or route is unnecessary because each present disposition remains the same on the expressly arguendo geographic account. If a later proposed ground instead depends on an adverse finding concerning that account, the cited appellate-appendix pages or an authenticated equivalent would be required through the neutral process.

The split on the surviving road theories is still four Associates to four; this is not a Court tie or majority. Five of the nine participating Justices are required for a controlling proposition or judgment component. Stone remains unmodeled. Reconciliation and assembly must preserve the distinction between these judicial application grounds and factual findings, and must carry the Stanley limitation without converting its Bivens holding into a new FTCA merits rule.
