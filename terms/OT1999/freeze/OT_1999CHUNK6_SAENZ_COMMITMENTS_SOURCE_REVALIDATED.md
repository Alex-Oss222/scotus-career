## Corrected June 1995 source landscape

**Controlling method and actual source roles.** Harlow and Anderson require objective, particularized notice, not a precedent with identical facts. The simulated Elder requires consideration of all relevant conduct-date law. DeShaney forecloses a general right to governmental protection from private violence while distinguishing an official's own creation of danger or increased vulnerability. Daniels excludes negligence; the simulated 1992 Collins preserves the separate underlying-deprivation and municipal-attribution inquiries. Those rules control the inquiry. The Fifth Circuit's Salas and Piotrowski are particularly pertinent to the legal setting in which Gonzalez acted; the other circuits' published decisions are relevant persuasive notice evidence, not binding Fifth Circuit holdings. Neither a count of circuits nor their failure to adopt a general theory mechanically resolves a specific affirmative-interference claim.

**Freeman changes the public-protection premise.** Freeman v. Ferguson, 911 F.2d 52 (August 6, 1990), distinguishes the allegation that a chief directed officers not to stop a known assailant because of personal friendship from a general failure to answer pleas for police protection. The otherwise available protection was other officers' public service. It therefore directly addresses supervisory suppression of public protection; the prior analysis may no longer rely on absence of any such preconduct analogue or on a private/public boundary as dispositive.

The actual holding permits amendment to present the specific theory; it does not find the proposed facts proved, resolve every defendant's liability, or adjudicate qualified immunity. The majority expressly leaves unsettled the degree of state participation and increased danger necessary. Those qualifications limit its force, but do not erase its published identification of the concrete wrong. Magill's dissent disputes the procedural permission to amend, not a supposed merits holding that an actual chief's interference is always constitutional. Friendship and the identified threatened family are material facts, not an announced exhaustive checklist or a requirement that arbitrary interference always take that precise form.

**Wood adds an adjudicated personal-notice analogy.** Wood v. Ostrander, 879 F.2d 583 (June 27, 1989), permits the adult passenger's claim and conditionally rejects personal immunity on her favorable version. The alleged removal of driver, vehicle and keys, compelled exit and abandonment supply affirmative deprivation of existing protection rather than simple failure to rescue. Its reasoning recognizes out-of-circuit sources and rejects an identical-fact requirement even for its September 1984 notice inquiry. The opinion available by June 1995 is therefore stronger than a general observation that a danger claim might someday exist. It nevertheless preserves disputed assistance, awareness, causation and culpability questions; it establishes no duty to protect everyone in a dangerous place. Carroll's dissent contests duty, proof and notice without erasing the majority's rule.

**Ross and Salas retain their different applications.** Ross protects the alleged immediate qualified rescue of a known drowning child from coercive obstruction without a meaningful alternative, and rejects the individual's immunity on that bounded account. Salas distinguishes ongoing replacement rescue despite its inadequacy and failure; it does not hold that all public rescue is mere nonprotection. Its separate August 1989 notice conclusion cannot be transplanted into June 1995. The absence here of a replacement response is relevant, but it does not prove that Martinez's proposed investigation would have stopped the danger or that every declined investigation is a rescue obstruction.

**Gregory requires a real affirmative causal act.** Gregory v. City of Rogers, 974 F.2d 1006 (September 9, 1992) (en banc), recognizes both state limitation of self-care and affirmative placement into otherwise absent danger. Its majority affirms on insufficient evidence of intoxication awareness and, independently, the absence of affirmative endangerment: the lawful arrest left the sober driver opportunities to arrange aid or retain the keys, and his private choice supplied the intoxicated passengers' access to the vehicle. It distinguishes rather than rejects Wood. It decides no qualified-immunity question and reserves the disputed culpability standard. The dissent's more favorable awareness and intervention inferences are not majority findings. Saenz has to be assessed on its own command and causal facts; the driver-and-keys opportunities in Gregory cannot be invented here.

**Piotrowski is relevant but qualified.** Piotrowski, 51 F.3d 512 (April 21, 1995), identifies blocked warning and assistance to conspirators as possible affirmative interference. It reserves adoption and pleading sufficiency, decides no officer immunity, and actually resolves the municipal-policy pleading defect with leave to amend. Its acknowledgment of circuit nonadoption is relevant to certainty, not an immunity talisman. Its discussion is neither a holding of liability nor legally irrelevant dicta.

**Lower Pinder must be used at the correct date and scope.** The en banc May 30, 1995 opinion, 54 F.3d 1169, was available before Gonzalez acted, but its nine-four immunity judgment addresses Johnson's March 10, 1989 conduct. Five judges join Wilkinson fully; Widener joins except three sentences distinguishing affirmative state-created danger; Motz joins Parts I–IV and limits the holding to 1989 immunity; Hamilton and Luttig concur only in judgment; four judges dissent. The direct-causation distinction has six express adherents rather than all nine judgment supporters, and the broad policy discussion cannot be attributed to Motz or the judgment-only joiners.

That opinion supplies meaningful contrary evidence concerning assurance, reliance, charging and release as purported affirmative deprivation. It does not adjudicate a direct rescue blockade or overrule Freeman, Wood or Ross. Its exclusion of Freeman, Gregory and Wood as notice sources for March 1989 cannot exclude them from June 1995. The later simulated Supreme Court Pinder remains current merits law at its own scope and an account of each Justice's published position; it is not a June 1995 notice source.

## Revised individual commitments

### Stevens — individual disposition changes; county position unchanged

**Disposition:** Vacate both federal judgments and remand under ordinary summary-judgment procedure. Reject personal immunity disposing of the entire supported affirmative-interference theory; do not find Gonzalez liable or make every version of nonenforcement unconstitutional.

**Sufficient ground:** Existing law sufficiently distinguished culpable affirmative destruction of an otherwise available protective opportunity from a general failure to provide police services. Freeman supplies the direct supervisory-public-protection application; Ross and Wood supply adopted affirmative-interference and personal-notice rules. On the supported favorable account, a senior officer's knowing, arbitrary use of rank to stop a willing officer's immediate intervention against an appreciated serious danger cannot be dismissed categorically as outside that principle merely because the eventual victims' identities were unknown.

**Justice-specific change and adversarial test:** Stevens's Pinder dissent join found a sufficiently specific deprivation principle even in the earlier assurance-induced withdrawal of private protection. His Lewis immunity-only position remains different: sources available for that rapid pursuit did not adequately identify its particular wrong. The original Saenz immunity assessment treated extension to a supervisory public-investigation command as the notice gap. Freeman supplies exactly that missing mechanism. Its amendment posture limits the result but does not erase the contemporaneously articulated principle, especially beside Wood and Ross. Gregory's lawful arrest and preserved private choices do not establish that the alleged command here left Martinez's protective opportunity intact. Stevens would not require the additional matching fact of a named threatened person where the allegedly appreciated immediate danger extends to road users.

**Limits:** Actual knowing danger, an available and lawful protective intervention, arbitrary culpable suppression, causal increased vulnerability and county policy remain required. Mere suspicion, an ordinary discretionary investigation choice or negligence may not establish those predicates. The later alcohol test does not prove earlier awareness. Genuinely supported factual or legal defenses remain available; no automatic discovery, trial or damages follows.

### O'Connor — dispositions unchanged; grounds revised

**Disposition:** Affirm both defendants on the existing nonprotection ground; independently affirm Gonzalez on particularized conduct-time immunity.

**Sufficient notice ground:** Freeman defeats a categorical public/private distinction, but it did not sufficiently establish the further application to this noncustodial patrol decision concerning a suspected intoxicated driver and later unidentified road users. The established rescue and transportation cases involve an already identified protective situation that official action directly removes. The legal uncertainty concerns whether this canceled proposed investigation is such a deprivation, not whether authorities must contain identical facts.

**Adversarial test and own positions:** Her Pinder Court opinion and Stemler Court opinion preserve the distinction between an actual attributable deprivation and omission of public service. Freeman is the strongest contrary source because a chief blocked other officers; she gives that distinction genuine weight. Its identified assailant, specific threatened family and officers already trying to stop that conduct, together with its reservation of ultimate sufficiency, leave the degree of constitutional participation material. Wood and Ross establish their bounded wrongs; Gregory confirms that an official act affecting protection is not invariably affirmative endangerment. The fractured lower Pinder is considered as contrary reasoning about assurance/omission, not as a universal 1995 immunity decision. Her conclusion does not depend on circuit nonadoption alone.

**Join limits:** She joins no universal known-victim requirement, no categorical immunity for public-protection obstruction and no factual finding negating plaintiffs' awareness inferences. Her county judgment still rests on absence of the federal deprivation on this record, not personal immunity.

### Scalia — dispositions unchanged; grounds revised

**Disposition:** Affirm both defendants on the particular nonprotection ground, and affirm Gonzalez independently on notice.

**Sufficient notice ground:** The preconduct authorities support defined affirmative official injuries, including destruction of private transportation, rescue obstruction and the possible suppression of specific police protection. They do not sufficiently establish the asserted constitutional tort at the actual scope of a superior's instruction not to begin this discretionary investigation. A foreseeable danger and an arbitrary reason do not independently establish the State's causal deprivation.

**Adversarial test and own positions:** His Lewis separate position requires support for the particular right; his Pinder and Stemler joins preserve recognized affirmative injury rather than a categorical custody rule. Freeman is not dismissed by its pleading posture or public setting: it materially refutes an absolute nonprotection label for every command. But its unresolved boundary, and Gregory's insistence on an independently attributable affirmative act, prevent it from supplying sufficiently clear coverage of this account. Wood's conditional immunity ruling is substantial authority at its actual removal-of-protection scope; its mere existence does not supply a general constitutional supervision of nonarrest decisions. He does not treat the split in lower Pinder as conclusive.

**Join limits:** No open-ended conscience formulation is adopted as a substitute for defining the right, and no requirement of an identical earlier accident is imposed. Actual direct intentional assaults or proved rescue blockades remain distinct.

### Kennedy — dispositions unchanged; grounds revised

**Disposition:** Affirm both defendants on nonprotection and Gonzalez independently on qualified immunity.

**Sufficient notice ground:** The law did not make sufficiently apparent that this supervisory choice concerning an uninitiated public investigation was a constitutional deprivation, even on the supported favorable account. The conclusion rests on the materially different causal/protective setting, not on the public character of Martinez's service alone.

**Adversarial test and own positions:** His Pinder majority join requires actual attribution and particularized notice; his Lewis Court opinion requires the real setting rather than a universal intent standard. Freeman's chief-command analogy makes plaintiffs' notice argument substantially stronger. He nevertheless distinguishes its official interference with specific protection against an identified threatening assailant from general suppression of a proposed investigation before any victim or protective encounter is identified. The distinction is functional rather than a requirement to know names. Wood and Ross involve concrete protection removed; Gregory illustrates why causal opportunity and intervening private choices matter. He does not infer equivalent private choices here, but concludes that the combined sources had not sufficiently resolved this extension.

**Join limits:** No rapid-pursuit harmful-purpose standard is mechanically applied. He accepts the favorable awareness and arbitrary-motive inferences for legal review and makes no finding that the decision was reasonable under state law. County affirmance remains merits-based.

### Souter — individual disposition changes; county position unchanged

**Disposition:** Vacate both federal judgments and remand; reject immunity that disposes of the whole supported affirmative-interference theory.

**Sufficient ground:** A sufficiently culpable official act that actually eliminates otherwise available protection against an appreciated serious and immediate danger was distinguishable from nonprotection before June 1995. Freeman expressly applies that distinction to a superior's obstruction of other officers, and Wood and Ross reject immunity on appropriately particularized affirmative-deprivation accounts. The source combination permits application without transforming every police omission into a constitutional wrong.

**Justice-specific change and adversarial test:** His Pinder dissent join supports actual protective displacement; his Uhlrig join preserves notice where broad reorganization and later clinical choices leave personal constitutional attribution unsettled. The earlier Saenz commitment identified public-investigation attribution as an unaddressed extension. Freeman materially answers that source premise. Its ultimate reservation and Piotrowski's qualified discussion do not make the precise affirmative mechanism unknowable, and Gregory's distinct causal findings provide limits rather than a contrary rule permitting arbitrary commands. He considers the lack of a specifically identified victim a substantial factual distinction, but not one that defeats notice of the qualifying affirmative wrong on this allegedly conscious exposure of others to immediate danger.

**Limits:** He requires supported actual availability, causal displacement and culpability, not the mere utterance of a command or the eventual collision. The ordinary remand can resolve other properly presented grounds without supplying new evidence or a guaranteed trial.

### Thomas — dispositions unchanged; grounds revised

**Disposition:** Affirm both defendants on nonprotection and affirm Gonzalez independently on immunity.

**Sufficient notice ground:** The sources do not sufficiently establish the particular substantive constitutional claim arising from this supervisory noninvestigation decision. The recognized affirmative acts and Freeman's possible command-interference claim remain materially different in the degree and kind of state-caused exposure established or assumed.

**Adversarial test and own positions:** His Pinder and Stemler joins and Lewis separate join require a defined right and attributable government act. He acknowledges Freeman as a public-officer analogue; it cannot be confined to private aid or discarded because it allowed amendment. Its express unresolved boundary, however, is significant where the claimed action is a choice not to begin ordinary enforcement against a suspected offender. Ross and Wood have concrete protective circumstances the State itself removed. Gregory reinforces the need to distinguish such removal from lawful police choices preceding private danger. Neither Fifth Circuit nonadoption nor lower Pinder's nine-vote judgment independently decides his position.

**Join limits:** No universal custody or names requirement, no general permission to obstruct rescue, and no bare reliance on factual differences unconnected to the asserted right. He does not join a broad conscience framework or a merits finding against the plaintiffs' factual inferences.

### Ginsburg — individual disposition changes; county position unchanged

**Disposition:** Vacate both federal judgments and remand. Do not allow qualified immunity to foreclose the supported sufficiently culpable affirmative-interference theory in its entirety.

**Sufficient ground:** By June 1995, the combined authorities sufficiently identified the qualifying wrong as an official's culpable use of authority actually to suppress otherwise available protection and thereby create or enhance an appreciated serious danger. Freeman identifies that wrong in the exact institutional mechanism of a chief directing other officers not to act. Wood and Ross supply affirmative-deprivation and immunity applications; Elder and Anderson require the combined landscape rather than a factual twin.

**Justice-specific change and adversarial test:** Her Pinder dissent found sufficient notice for actual protective displacement on much earlier sources; her Elder opinion requires considering this newly supplied, substantially closer authority. The first handoff's assertion that public-investigation attribution remained unsupported is materially incomplete after Freeman. The strongest defense is the combination of unidentified road users, an investigation not yet commenced and the older cases' concrete victims or rescues. For her, those differences require proof of actual appreciated danger and causal loss, not automatic immunity from the supported qualifying account. Gregory supplies no contrary command-blocking rule and reserved private choices cannot be imported. Piotrowski's and Freeman's procedural reservations limit their holdings without eliminating notice supplied by their clear distinction and the adopted rules in Wood and Ross.

**Limits:** No retrospective use of the .21 test, no inference of constitutional intent solely from crude words, no duty to arrest every suspected driver, and no presumption that investigation would have prevented the accident. County policy/custom and causal responsibility must be established separately. Ordinary summary-judgment defenses remain available.

### Breyer — individual disposition changes; county position unchanged

**Disposition:** Vacate both federal judgments and remand at the bounded scope. Reject a categorical personal-immunity termination of the supported affirmative-interference theory.

**Sufficient ground:** The preconduct sources, taken at their actual scope, adequately mark the distinction between declining to provide protection and deliberately using authority to disable an actually available protective response against appreciated serious danger. Freeman gives the directly relevant public-supervisory example; Wood and Ross establish that sufficiently particularized affirmative interference can defeat immunity even without a matching prior incident.

**Justice-specific change and adversarial test:** His Pinder dissent join supports the actual-protection-loss theory; his Uhlrig Court opinion requires precise attribution and notice, while his Lewis concurrence permits immunity-first sequencing when that defense genuinely resolves the case. The missing mechanism in the original Saenz notice assessment is now supplied. The strongest defense remains ordinary supervisory discretion and the uncertain causal value of investigation. That warrants retaining fact-specific deprivation and causation requirements, not using source uncertainty about every possible application to defeat the core qualifying claim. Gregory demonstrates the need for those requirements but does not resolve an arbitrary command that prevents another officer from exercising an actually available intervention.

**Limits:** No numerical deliberation threshold, automatic deliberate-indifference rule, general constitutional policing protocol or guaranteed trial. Less culpable or noncausal conduct can fail on the existing record under ordinary standards. The remand does not presume that the most inculpatory inference is ultimately proved.
