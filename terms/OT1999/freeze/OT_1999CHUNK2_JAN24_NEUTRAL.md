### Issue 2: Section 5 effects

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Simulated *Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board; Price v. Bossier Parish School Board*, 520 U.S. 471 (1997), Question 1, holds that a § 2 violation does not independently establish prohibited § 5 effect and that § 5 effect compares the proposed change with the jurisdiction's lawful operative baseline for retrogression.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** **Relationship between § 5 effect and § 2.** Section 5 effect remains a retrogression inquiry distinct from § 2's results standard; possible § 2 liability does not substitute for the § 5 effects comparison.

* **Material Other Authority:** Time-eligible authorities are used only to maintain the distinction between retrogression and a separate dilution claim.

### Issue 3: Section 5 discriminatory purpose

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Reno v. Bossier Parish School Board; Price v. Bossier Parish School Board, decided May 12, 1997, holds that § 5’s purpose clause reaches a covered voting change adopted with an unconstitutional purpose to dilute voting opportunity on account of race even when the intended injury is not retrogression. The same decision preserves the jurisdiction’s existing burden to establish absence of prohibited purpose.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Purpose is determined from the full contextual record, including impact, history, sequence, departures, contemporaneous statements, rejected alternatives, and pretext. Dilution evidence is relevant but not conclusive.
* **Other Governing Law:** Voting Rights Act § 5; Arlington Heights; Beer; Pleasant Grove; Richmond.
* **Material Other Authority:** The 1997 mandate vacated preclearance for reconsideration under the broad purpose rule without finding actual purpose.
* **Open Point:** The scope of prohibited purpose is settled. The record application and any lawful mixed-motive or same-decision consequence remain to be resolved.
### Issue 4: Same-decision showing

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The 1997 Bossier Parish decision expressly adopted no same-decision defense or new causation rule. Arlington Heights and Mt. Healthy supply time-eligible mixed-motive principles, but their transfer into § 5 must respect the jurisdiction’s statutory burden to establish entitlement to preclearance.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Where governing law permits a same-decision showing, the decisionmaker must prove that independently sufficient lawful reasons would have produced the identical decision without the prohibited consideration. A conceivable lawful explanation is insufficient.
* **Other Governing Law:** Voting Rights Act § 5; Arlington Heights; Mt. Healthy.
