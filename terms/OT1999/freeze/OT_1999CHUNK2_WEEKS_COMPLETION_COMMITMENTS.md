## Shared legal and record boundaries

The granted question is the response to the life-or-death inquiry. The earlier parole inquiry is context only; expert assistance, guilt, a separate statutory-factor instruction entitlement, and an execution or stay question are not added. The transcript's renewed request and objection preserve this response challenge; acceptance or use of the original charge does not supply waiver. No Justice treats the research limits as litigant default.

All eight conclude that assignment 44 fairly presented the same federal response claim: its factual account identified the later question, requested clarification, and refusal, and it incorporated assignment 41's effective-mitigation argument with explicit federal constitutional grounds. This is more than facts from which a different federal theory could be devised. The state court expressly separated assignments it would not consider from the group including 44 that it considered and rejected as meritless; its concluding treatment of jury inquiries confirms that distinction. The claim therefore received merits adjudication. There is no established independent state procedural rejection of this claim and no uncompleted ordinary state-review step. The later untimely collateral proceeding does not require repetitive presentation of a claim already presented on direct review. This conclusion establishes neither general waiver of a preserved State defense nor an exception to Picard, Harless, Harris, Coleman, Gray, or the enacted exhaustion conditions.

All eight reject the proposition that a short or unexplained merits disposition automatically escapes §2254(d). The February 1997 filing does not fall within Lindh or Carriger's pre-enactment premise. Section 2254(d)(1) is a restriction additional to constitutional error: a writ requires a decision contrary to, or involving an unreasonable application of, clearly established Supreme Court federal law. Its alternatives are not collapsed into a de novo error test. The claim's presentation of those alternatives makes their application part of deciding entitlement to habeas relief; no separate holding about forfeiture of a challenge to the statute's applicability is needed. No §2254(d)(2) factual-unreasonableness or §2254(e) hearing entitlement is established by competing interpretations of the undisputed instructions and inquiry.

For the substantive constitutional rule, Lockett and Eddings protect effective mitigating use without requiring favorable weight. Penry and simulated Graham require an available route for supported independent culpability mitigation. Boyde supplies reasonable-likelihood review of the entire charge and trial, not a mere possibility test or a requirement to prove a juror's actual thoughts. Bollenbach supports attention to an expressed difficulty, but its federal direct-review setting and erroneous supplementary presumption do not themselves create a constitutional command to rephrase every accurate instruction. Buchanan's current holding preserves an adequate post-aggravator life route and does not make advocacy or admission a cure for restrictive judicial guidance.

Instruction 2's second paragraph expressly permits death after the necessary aggravator and directs life if all the evidence makes death unjustified. B1 defines mitigation broadly, requires consideration of supported mitigation, and leaves weight to the jury. The jury nevertheless asked which of the two legal alternatives controlled after an aggravator; the judge directed it to the particular paragraph without restating the life option. Both sides' arguments, further deliberation, the vileness verdict's mitigation recital and the poll are relevant context, not conclusive findings of comprehension. No one infers a fixed lunch interruption, the jury's reason for omitting future dangerousness, or an understanding from its tears.
