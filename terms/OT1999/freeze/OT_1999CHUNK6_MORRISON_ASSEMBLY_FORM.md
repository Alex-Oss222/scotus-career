# Morrison / Brzonkala — form before assignment count

The consolidated dockets issue in two signed plenary Opinions of the Court. The enacted private-assault action presents distinct Commerce and section 5 questions with different five-Justice coalitions, and only the Chief joins both. One opinion states the section 5 holding, the statutory scope and the judgment: section 5 sustains the action as a corrective substitute remedy for the documented, systematic discriminatory denial of protection, under validity conditions that are not plaintiff elements, preserving the state-action boundary, Florida Prepaid's section 5 holding and the unpresented color-of-law/severability limits. The other states the Commerce holding, applying the existing class/connection/fit architecture and all materially supported market, necessary-regime and externality paths; in it, its author and three joining Justices dissent from the section 5 holding and the judgment.

Stone-Zsela's approved Commerce ground coincides with O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. His approved section 5 rule coincides with the bounded section 5 ground of Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: the State's discriminatory denial is the constitutional injury, and the substitute remedy is valid only on a materially adequate basis to find the denial systematic, coverage within the affected class, a remedy that meaningfully addresses the deprivation and congruent and proportional reach. No substantive Stone-Zsela narrowing, alternative ground or fallback is used. Each five may join its own opinion, and the Chief, the only member of both, assigns both.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer also share one Commerce proposal that expressly would displace the connection-before-magnitude prerequisite where it defeats their rational substantial-class-effects rationale; they state it in one concurrence. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas share one section 5 objection, stated as a dissent within the Commerce opinion. Neither a historical Thomas original-understanding writing nor a historical legislative-process alternative is added without a frozen current commitment.

McIver has no Record and contributes no assignment count. These form, scope and coalition choices precede the count or selection of an author.
