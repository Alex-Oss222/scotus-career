## Actual record and independent components

The defendants received ten joint ordinary challenges and one alternate-only challenge. Gilbert expressed prosecution-favoring views; the uncontested appellate ruling establishes error in denying the cause challenge. After two unsuccessful objections Martinez-Salazar removed Gilbert peremptorily. The defendants exhausted their allotment, asked for no additional strike, identified no other juror they would have removed and made no impartiality challenge to the seated jury. Counsel's final lack of objection does not erase the earlier cause objection. There is no finding of deliberate judicial manipulation. The cause error is accepted, not approved.

**Constitutional impartiality:** The juror was removed and an impartial jury sat. Ross's controlling distinction defeats the asserted Sixth Amendment injury on these facts. This is not a biased juror's actual participation or an independent discriminatory-selection injury.

**Federal entitlement:** The better reading of Rule 24 grants ten opportunities for peremptory exclusion, all received and exercised here, rather than ten exclusions after every disputed cause ruling has first been correctly resolved. Using one of the allotted strikes to secure removal fulfills the strike's function. The rule contains no automatic replenishment provision. Its separate permission for extra challenges in a multiple-defendant case is not a duty to restore one.

The strongest contrary reading treats for-cause removals as logically prior to the entitlement: a peremptory is valuable precisely because it permits rejection without legal cause, and forcing its expenditure on an excusable juror consumes a strategic opportunity. Ross's footnote 4 leaves room for that construction, and Stevens's joined Ross dissent identifies real compositional and strategic costs. But the prescribed numerical entitlement and this actual record do not establish a separately denied exercise. There was no additional requested strike refused, no identified further desired exclusion and no rule conditioning preservation on corrective use. The limited conclusion does not treat impartiality alone as answering the rule question.

**Curative-use obligation:** Reject the Government's proposed general federal obligation to spend a peremptory to cure a preserved cause error. Neither Rule 24(b) nor Ross imposes that requirement here. The present voluntary procedural choice after an adverse ruling is legally distinct from a compulsory exhaustion rule. Do not infer that leaving a biased juror seated would either necessarily preserve or necessarily waive every appellate claim; that different case is not decided.

**Due process and remedy:** Since the prescribed entitlement was not denied on this record, the Fifth Amendment theory based on deprivation of that entitlement fails. The Court need not choose between automatic reversal and harmless-error review for an actual Rule 24 deprivation. Swain's impairment formulation cannot settle whether impairment occurred. Do not announce that every rule error is harmless if the ultimate jury is impartial, or overrule every peremptory-error remedy. Reverse the Ninth Circuit's lost-peremptory ground and remand for any properly remaining proceedings; no new trial follows from this cause error as cured here.
