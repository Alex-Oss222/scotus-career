## Castillo v. United States

Source: U.S. Reports 530 U.S. 120, 122–124, record recitation; underlying Fifth Circuit judgments at 91 F.3d 699 and 179 F.3d 321. The jury instruction required knowing use or carriage of a firearm during and in relation to a crime of violence. The sentencing judge found machineguns, silencers and handgrenades and imposed thirty-year terms. The first appeal required reconsideration under Bailey's active-employment construction; the Fifth Circuit treated weapon type as a sentencing factor and allowed the judge to reimpose thirty years upon the requisite finding. The District Court did so, and the 1999 Fifth Circuit judgment affirmed. No separate jury determination of the aggravating weapon type is established. The exact indictment wording is not supplied by this extraction. The governing offense text is the pre-1998 version of section 924(c), not the restructured 1998 version.

## Sims v. Apfel

Source: U.S. Reports 530 U.S. 103, 105–107, procedural recitation; regulations in 20 C.F.R. parts 404 and 416 (1999), and Form HA-520 described in the record. Sims applied for Title II and Title XVI benefits, received an adverse ALJ ruling, and through counsel requested Appeals Council review by letter. The Council denied review. The District Court rejected her three challenges. The Fifth Circuit affirmed on the merits the selective-use-of-record challenge. It declined the defective-vocational-hypothetical and failure-to-obtain-consultative-examination issues because she had not specified them to the Council, treating the omission as jurisdictional. She had requested Council review and obtained a final agency decision. No ALJ-stage issue-exhaustion question is presented here.

The regulations permit a request by the one-page HA-520 or another writing. The form provides three lines for the review request and estimates ten minutes for reading instructions, gathering facts and completing it. The ALJ notice says the Council considers the entire decision, including portions with which the claimant agrees, and may review without a request. Briefs to the Council are permitted, not required. The regulations describe an informal, nonadversarial review process; Council review is plenary unless narrowed. The Commissioner seeks issue exhaustion in addition to exhaustion of administrative remedies. These notices and regulatory features must be evaluated on their actual terms, not converted into an extra notice rule by the packet.

## United States v. Hubbell

Source: U.S. Reports 530 U.S. 27, 30–33 and notes 4–6, procedural recitation; D.C. Circuit 167 F.3d 552; District Court 11 F. Supp. 2d 25. Following an earlier guilty plea and cooperation undertaking, an October 1996 subpoena sought eleven broad document categories. On November 19 Hubbell invoked the privilege, then complied under a previously obtained section 6003 order granting statutory immunity. He produced 13,120 pages and answered completeness questions, excepting identified attorney-client and work-product material. The document contents supplied investigative information leading to a ten-count tax, mail-fraud and wire-fraud indictment in April 1998.

The District Court dismissed on immunity grounds and, independently, on the Independent Counsel's authority. The D.C. Circuit rejected the authority ground, which is outside this review. On immunity it vacated and remanded for an inquiry into the Government's prior knowledge of existence, authenticity, possession and control, rather than prior knowledge of document contents. On remand the prosecutor acknowledged inability to meet the circuit's reasonable-particularity standard and made a conditional plea agreement: dismissal unless the ensuing Supreme Court disposition makes it reasonably likely that production immunity is no significant bar; a sufficiently favorable reversal instead permits a guilty plea with no incarceration. The agreement leaves a live controversy. Other defendants' charges were dismissed with prejudice and are outside this matter. No new postdecision independent-source search or evidentiary opportunity is established merely by the earlier circuit remand. The Government's present position disclaims a need to introduce the produced documents or the production act at trial; it does not claim it can establish evidence wholly independent of the immunized production. Distinguish that litigation position from the earlier specific concession about reasonable particularity.

## Harris Trust and Savings Bank v. Salomon Smith Barney Inc.

Source: U.S. Reports 530 U.S. 238, 242–246 and notes 1–3, factual and procedural recitations; Seventh Circuit 184 F.3d 646. APT's investment manager NISA directed purchases of motel interests from Salomon for approximately $21 million. Salomon also provided nondiscretionary brokerage services. The plan trustee and administrator sued after the interests became nearly worthless. Whether they were worthless from the beginning or declined with the market is disputed. The claim seeks rescission, restitution of purchase price with interest, and disgorgement of profits under section 502(a)(3), based on the alleged section 406(a) transaction. No fiduciary status for Salomon is assumed.

The District Court denied Salomon's summary-judgment motion based on categorical nonfiduciary immunity and certified the issue under section 1292(b). The Seventh Circuit reversed and directed summary judgment for Salomon on that legal ground. Party-in-interest status and the applicability of Prohibited Transaction Exemption 75-1 remain disputed and preserved; the categorical legal question assumes a prohibited, nonexempt transaction without finding those predicates. Actual or constructive notice, the appropriate relief on the actual assets or proceeds, and allocation of the purchaser-for-value-without-notice burden have not been finally resolved. Salomon also asserts the plaintiffs changed their argument; the claim throughout remains a section 502(a)(3) claim for equitable relief, and the new articulation focuses on the prohibited transaction as the redressable act rather than treating the counterparty itself as the direct section 406(a) duty bearer. Do not conflate an existing claim with a newly presented argument supporting it.
