## Garner v. Jones — No. 99-137

### Review, governing law and the separate questions

Certiorari reviews the Eleventh Circuit's January 6, 1999 reversal of summary judgment for Board officials and remand. Argument was January 11, 2000. The matter concerns Jones's actual 1995 postponement from the offense-date three-year reconsideration opportunity to August 2003. It does not concern a parole entitlement, his ultimate suitability, the continued validity of his convictions or a damages award. The 1996 policy and custodian's affidavit are evidence of an asserted replacement, not merely counsel's hypothesis; the policy does not require expedited review whenever an inmate asks for it.

The operative standard is **Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration**, present operation effective **Morales, April 25, 1995**, with Cavanaugh's November 30, 1993 original scope retained. Morales Parts II–III have O'Connor, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg's direct joins, together with the Chief Justice's published join. The prisoner must establish the offense-date entitlement, its actual retrospective withdrawal, and its concrete connection to legally available earlier release. He need not prove that a withheld hearing would grant parole. The State must substantiate a substitute it invokes through legal basis, practical availability and timely capacity, while the prisoner retains ultimate constitutional proof. The Court adopted neither a requirement that every substitute be formally enforceable nor a universal probability threshold, mandatory filing procedure, response deadline or presumption transferring ultimate proof.

Jones has shown the relevant initial loss: the actual eight-year deferral removes the three-year occasion at which his still legally available parole could have been considered. His two murders and the initial adverse forecast do not make earlier release legally impossible. What remains disputed is whether the asserted expedited process preserves the meaningful intervening opportunity in practice, and what disposition follows from the way the lower courts treated that evidence.

The former Morales dissent remains material. Kennedy, joined throughout by Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, accepted Cavanaugh's opportunity protection and rejected an actual-release proof requirement. Its sufficient distinction was California's confined, separately reasoned, reviewable prediction, restricted class and three-year ceiling. It did not assume an unproved reopening procedure or legal impossibility of earlier parole. Kennedy accepted bounded testing of an asserted substitute; Scalia, Thomas and Breyer did not adopt an independently reusable enumerated allocation rule. The dissent expressly left defective, perfunctory, categorical and materially different implementations open. Georgia's much longer actual deferral, different legal safeguards and affirmative evidence of a later-review process therefore require fresh application; neither the former dissent nor the former majority mechanically settles this case.

### Supported paths and their limits

**Vacate and remand under the actual Morales rule.** The appellate opinion treats the absence of enforceable safeguards and the discretionary character of policy as grounds to reject the replacement, without applying the governing legal-basis, practical-availability and timely-capacity inquiry to the sworn operational account. The district court used a displaced legal premise. Vacatur can remove the appellate opinion's categorical constitutional determination while returning the actual summary-judgment record for the proper inquiry. Its strongest objection is that the evidence already fails the State's substantiation obligation and the appellate judgment merely reverses defendants' summary judgment. The response supporting vacatur is limited: the existing evidence warrants legal assessment under the correct rule, not a fresh opportunity guaranteed to repair any evidentiary failure. Ordinary summary-judgment procedure governs the record and any further proceedings; the Court does not commission an operational study or presume missing evidence favorable to either side.

**Affirm the reversal of defendants' summary judgment on a confined lost-opportunity ground.** Jones has established the lost occasion. An affidavit asserting an existing process and a policy saying review may occur do not establish as a matter of law how an inmate ordinarily obtains timely consideration of later developments. The existing appellate mandate already returns the case to the district court. The strongest objection is that affirmance could preserve the appellate court's broader rule that discretionary policy can never suffice. An affirmance must expressly reject that rule and leave the effect of a demonstrated materially different replacement to ordinary proceedings. It cannot convert incomplete substantiation at this stage into a finding that no expedited review ever occurs.

**Restore judgment for the officials.** The individualized finding and sworn description are more substantial than the hypothetical advancement rejected in Morales. The competing argument is that, with ultimate constitutional proof on Jones, the existing evidence defeats a claim based only on the interval. That does not justify restoration here: it treats asserted existence as demonstrated practical and timely capacity, or substitutes a prediction of continued unsuitability for the protection Morales recognizes. The present record does not establish an adequate replacement as a matter of law. No Associate commits to that disposition.

## Free v. Abbott Laboratories — No. 99-391

### Review and governing question

The Frees seek reversal of the jurisdictional basis for the final Fifth Circuit judgment entered **June 3, 1999**, reported at 176 F.3d 298 after the January opinion at 164 F.3d 270. Argument was March 27, 2000. The single question assumes that a named plaintiff has a claim within original diversity jurisdiction and that the absent members' claims belong to the same Article III case. This 1993 action uses the then-applicable **$50,000** amount, not the later $75,000 amount. The assumed anchor arose through the lower court's fee-attribution ruling, not an allegation that ordinary individual damages exceeded the threshold.

The operative standard **Supplemental jurisdiction over related state administrative review**, present operation effective **City of Chicago, December 15, 1997**, rejects a requirement of original jurisdiction over every constituent claim in the mixed federal-question setting it decided. Thomas wrote Part I; Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer joined it. Stevens and Ginsburg dissented on antecedent statutory power over the particular administrative appeal. Neither the holding nor that dissent decided this class-action amount question. The Court's express diversity reservation and §1367(b)'s diversity-specific structure require independent analysis.

Section 1367 applies to civil actions commenced on or after December 1, 1990. Subsection (a) covers other claims related to claims within original jurisdiction as part of the same Article III case, including claims involving additional parties. Subsection (b), in diversity-only actions, separately excludes claims by plaintiffs **against** persons joined under Rules 14, 19, 20 or 24, claims by persons proposed as plaintiffs under Rule 19, and claims by persons intervening as plaintiffs under Rule 24 when inconsistent with §1332. Rule 23 is absent. The exclusion concerning Rule 20 defendants is not an exclusion of every Rule 20 plaintiff. Subsection (c)'s discretionary conditions remain separate and are not before the Court for redetermination. The statute's tolling and State-definition provisions create no additional issue here.

Zahn and Snyder retain their pre-enactment jurisdictional rules unless the new grant changes their operation. Finley requires statutory authority for additional parties; §1367 supplies an enacted grant absent there. The dispute is its extent, not the Constitution's capacity to support supplemental jurisdiction or the Court's power to disregard a statutory amount requirement for convenience.

### The two constructions

**Grant reaches the absent members.** The qualifying named claim supplies an original-jurisdiction anchor; the related smaller claims are the other claims described in subsection (a). Congress expressly addressed added parties and specified diversity exclusions without listing Rule 23. The original-jurisdiction proviso cannot require an independent anchor for every supplemental claim. The strongest objection is that §1332's historically claim-specific amount rule enters through the initial civil-action condition before subsection (b) is reached. This construction answers that the inadequate amount of one claimant does not extinguish jurisdiction over another qualifying claim; the statute adds the related claim without aggregating it to create the first claim's amount. A committee report cannot insert an omitted Rule 23 exception into an otherwise operative grant. It need not decide complete-diversity defects, every joinder arrangement, or unrelated claims.

**Zahn remains the rule for these claims.** The original-jurisdiction condition must be read with §1332's settled restriction on diversity actions involving separate plaintiffs' claims. Congress responded to Finley but retained a distinct diversity regime, and the stated legislative history preserving Zahn confirms a reading the introductory condition can bear. Supplemental jurisdiction over nonfederal claims within an action anchored in a federal question does not itself establish that the amount limitation on distinct diversity plaintiffs was displaced. The strongest objection is the broad additional-party sentence and Rule 23's absence from subsection (b). This construction treats subsection (b) as additional protection against enumerated joinder routes, not an exhaustive statement of what constitutes an original diversity action. It must recognize the force of the omission rather than pretend that Rule 23 appears in the text, and must preserve the named plaintiff's qualifying claim rather than declare that the entire action lacks an anchor.

## Powell v. Ridge — April 3, 2000

### Review, governing law and component separation

The supplied merits review follows the December 13 grant and February 28 submission. It reviews 189 F.3d 387, which reversed dismissal of the two counts seeking declaratory and prospective injunctive relief. Count I invokes the Title VI regulation; Count II invokes §1983. There is no damages request. The individual children's alleged comparative funding injury suffices for the presented pleading-stage controversy; statutory enforceability is distinct from Article III standing. State officers sued for prospective relief are not categorically excluded as nonpersons. The municipal plaintiffs' separate standing and final defendant-specific relief need not be decided to answer the granted questions.

Section 601's individual protection, §602's agency rulemaking and compliance mechanisms, and **34 C.F.R. §100.3(b)(2)** perform different work. The regulation prohibits specified discriminatory effects from a recipient's criteria or methods of administering covered benefits. Its validity does not itself establish a private action. Section 601's established intentional-discrimination action does not automatically establish a private regulatory action. Section 1983 requires an individual federal right before its presumption of enforcement and the foreclosure question arise.

The operative **Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983** standard has present operation effective **Bruneau, June 3, 1999** for the Title IX enforcement relationship; Blessing's April 21, 1997 provision-specific right and foreclosure components retain their dates. All eight Associates joined Bruneau's holding that an identified Title IX statutory right is not categorically foreclosed by its administrative funding process or existing implied remedy. Blessing, Stevens for a unanimous Court, requires intended individual benefit, a binding duty and a sufficiently definite entitlement; qualitative rules are not categorically unenforceable, and regulations may elaborate an existing right. Its reasoning does not hold that any valid regulation creates a new individual right. Suter separates right creation, directly implied action, foreclosure and relief; the CAPTA investigation standard similarly shows that a mandatory agency obligation need not create the asserted personal entitlement.

Gebser and Davis concern attribution of intentional discrimination to a recipient. Their expanded attribution routes do not erase discrimination, coverage, causation or remedy requirements and do not decide impact-only private enforcement. **Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX**, present operation effective June 22, 1998, and **Recipient responsibility for peer harassment under Title IX**, effective May 24, 1999, therefore supply analogies and limits, not the missing answer. NCAA v. Smith keeps actual receipt of assistance distinct from economic connection and preserves ordinary pleading questions without guaranteeing amendment.

### Supported paths and strongest objections

**Permit the specified prospective regulatory suit.** The children are the persons protected by §601; §602 expressly delegates implementation of that protection, and the regulation gives an administrable recipient-directed form to their protection from racially unequal program benefits. Cannon's beneficiary-centered implied-action context, the regulatory account recognized in Guardians and Choate, and Wright's acceptance of regulatory elaboration support reading the instruments together. Under §1983, the right must be identified in that statutory/regulatory combination, not in an abstract demand for better school administration. The funding machinery provides no comparable individual remedial proceeding and does not foreclose an established right under Blessing and Bruneau. The strongest objection is that effect discrimination exceeds §601's own intentional-discrimination prohibition and §602 addresses agencies, so the asserted individual right and direct remedy are missing. The affirmative answer must explain why this is authorized implementation of an existing individual protection, rather than allowing any agency to create a private action or any statutory beneficiary to challenge any disparity.

**Reject both impact-only channels.** Section 601's individual right is against intentional discrimination; §602 directs agencies and prescribes their means of compliance. A valid regulation may impose additional administrative conditions without creating the individual statutory right necessary for §1983 or the congressional intent necessary for a directly implied action. The strongest objection is the longstanding integration of the beneficiary-protecting statute with implementing effects regulations, and the real role of regulations in Wright. The negative answer distinguishes elaboration of an already conferred entitlement from creation of a substantively broader entitlement; it cannot rest on the proposition that regulations never matter to right definition. Funding oversight alone is not foreclosure, and the regulation's validity and public enforcement need not be rejected to resolve private enforcement.

**Different channels.** Failure to imply a direct action would not alone defeat §1983, and establishing one sufficient channel could make an alternative unnecessary to the disposition. Each commitment below nevertheless states its position on both presented counts. No Justice treats the implied-action inquiry as the §1983 foreclosure inquiry. An opinion may omit an unnecessary alternate holding at assembly without converting that omission into agreement with the opposite rule.
