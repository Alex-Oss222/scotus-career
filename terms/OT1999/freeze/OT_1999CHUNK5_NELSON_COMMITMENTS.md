## Components, operative ground and alternatives

All eight Associates provisionally would reverse the Federal Circuit's approval of simultaneous addition of Nelson and entry of personal liability, and remand for an opportunity to answer and defend through ordinary proceedings. They would assume without deciding that leave to add him was permissible, rather than invalidate all postjudgment joinder. Ohio Cellular's patent and fee judgments remain undisturbed.

**Common rule:** Permission to add a defendant does not adjudicate the newly asserted personal claim. Before adverse judgment, the added person must receive notice of the personal claim and a meaningful opportunity to respond to it. Rule 15(a)'s liberal amendment principle and its separate response provision work together; a power to adjust response timing does not authorize elimination of the response. The then-operative period was the remaining original period or ten days after service, whichever was longer, unless otherwise ordered. This decision requires a meaningful opportunity, not an inflexible Court-created new deadline.

The corporate litigation and Nelson's testimony addressed patent inequitable conduct and corporate fees. His control of that litigation, financial interest and knowledge of a possible future personal claim do not establish that personal liability was actually tried with his express or implied consent under Rule 15(b). Opposition to a motion asking to add him is not necessarily a chance to answer and defend the claim after addition. Rule 15(c)'s relation-back doctrine supplies no merits judgment. A lawful preclusion contention may narrow issues on remand only after its actual predicates are examined; corporate control does not automatically prove every element of the new obligation.

**Strongest affirmance path:** Nelson directed the litigation, testified about the same conduct, shared the corporation's interests and identified no new defense; a second procedure would be empty. **Controlling objection:** This treats the personal claim as already adjudicated without establishing that it was ever before the court for adjudication against him. Prediction of an adverse result is not the required opportunity to litigate. The letter about corporate liquidation is not a proved fraudulent transfer, and Adams disclaimed veil piercing. No necessity to collect supplies a missing personal obligation.

**Strongest objection to reversal:** Due process permits recognized control and representation relationships to bind some issues and need not require repetitive trials. **Answer and limit:** Reversal grants an opportunity to respond, not an automatic second trial of all facts. Richards and Matsushita preserve recognized binding relationships upon proof, while Zenith distinguishes corporate participation from personal judgment against an unjoined person. The individual must be able to contest the applicability and scope of any proposed preclusion and the personal liability asserted.

## Justice-specific commitments

### Stevens

**Result and ground:** Reverse simultaneous personal judgment; remand for ordinary personal defense, assuming proper addition. His joins in Richards's nonparty-binding rule and Matsushita's constitutional representation component support requiring the actual basis for binding a distinct person. His current-term Weisgram join recognizes that final judgment can follow a fair opportunity; the missing fair opportunity, rather than an automatic retrial entitlement, distinguishes this case.

**Opposing path and barrier:** Actual control may make the evidence and interests overlap, but overlap does not establish consent to adjudicate a claim against the controller. He accepts legitimate issue preclusion on remand, not a categorical immunity for controlling shareholders. Peacock's independent-action jurisdiction rule is distinguishable and not needed for his result; this is an amendment in the existing case, not the new action that rule addresses.

### O'Connor

**Result and ground:** Reverse and remand at the common scope. Her joins in Richards, Matsushita, Peacock and Weisgram distinguish constitutional authority to bind a person, substantive obligation, federal jurisdiction and fair procedure. Rule 15 permits addition but preserves an actual response before judgment.

**Opposing path and barrier:** Nelson's control could justify binding an issue, yet does not itself establish the untried personal claim. She would not join a mandatory new trial or a determination that no lawful theory can ever reach Nelson; the narrower response-and-defense remedy answers the specific procedural error.

### Scalia

**Result and ground:** Reverse simultaneous judgment and remand. His Richards and Matsushita joins supply the due-process boundary; Rule 15(a)'s separate treatment of amendment and response makes liberal amendment an insufficient answer. His Peacock join rejects creation of a new debtor merely from enforcement need, while leaving this case's established jurisdiction distinct.

**Opposing path and barrier:** A no-prejudice characterization cannot erase the rule's response mechanism or establish trial by consent. He would refuse an open-ended procedural balancing rule or a construction automatically importing the Rule 59/60 issue into this grant; the ordinary Rules and meaningful opportunity suffice.

### Kennedy

**Result and ground:** Reverse and remand. His Richards, Matsushita, Peacock and Weisgram joins support preserving real personal defense without manufacturing a new substantive immunity. The timing power in Rule 15(a) can manage proceedings, not turn permission to allege into conclusive liability.

**Opposing path and barrier:** Corporate identity may matter little to the facts of inequitable conduct, but personal liability remains a different legal obligation. The absence of a new defense identified before personal joinder does not prove that an opportunity to answer it would be meaningless. He can join preserved lawful preclusion and ordinary merits resolution without guaranteeing a new trial.

### Souter

**Result and ground:** Reverse approval of personal liability and remand for response and adjudication. His Richards and Matsushita joins reject identical interests as sufficient nonparty binding; his Peacock join preserves the distinction between collecting a debt and creating another debtor. Weisgram confirms the materiality of actual prior opportunity.

**Opposing path and barrier:** A fully controlled corporate defense can support issue preclusion, but here it is being used as a substitute for determining the particular personal claim and scope of binding effect. He would insist that Adams's veil-piercing disclaimer and the absence of a proved new personal claim remain explicit; neither may be silently supplied on appeal.

### Thomas

**Result and ground:** Reverse and remand. His Peacock opinion identifies the independent steps needed to impose another person's debt; his Richards, Matsushita and Weisgram joins require the actual personal and procedural predicates. This case does not call for Peacock's jurisdictional dismissal because the existing patent action is the asserted procedural setting.

**Opposing path and barrier:** A collection emergency or complete shareholder control does not itself establish liability. He joins the rules-based opportunity requirement and its constitutional foundation, not a general ban on alter-ego liability, supplemental proceedings, fee liability for officers or postjudgment amendment.

### Ginsburg

**Result and ground:** Reverse and remand. Her Weisgram opinion links permissible final judgment to an actual fair opportunity, not repeated opportunities whenever a party predicts stronger proof. Here Nelson lacks the first opportunity to answer the personal claim. Her Richards, Matsushita and Peacock joins independently distinguish notice, valid binding effect and new personal obligations.

**Opposing path and barrier:** The argument that nothing different could be said presupposes the liability it seeks to impose. She would preserve ordinary preclusion and procedural management after an answer, but not deem testimony as a corporate witness, opposition to joinder or postjudgment reconsideration an adequate substitute on this established sequence.

### Breyer

**Result and ground:** Reverse and remand at the common scope. His Richards, Matsushita and Weisgram joins support practical fair process rather than formal repetition. Adding Nelson and entering judgment at the same moment prevented the practical response the Rules contemplate; complete corporate control does not cure that identified omission.

**Opposing path and barrier:** Efficient resolution and overlap could make a later trial unnecessary, but cannot justify assuming away legal or factual defenses before they may be presented. He joins a bounded remand allowing ordinary lawful disposition, not an automatic restart of corporate litigation, mandated evidence hearing or new discovery entitlement.

## Precedent treatment, remedy and limits

Foman's liberal amendment rule is applied to permission to plead, not converted into authority for automatic judgment. Zenith's distinction between corporate participation and an unjoined person's personal liability is applied. Richards and Matsushita retain constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate representation and other recognized relationships on their actual predicates; no general entitlement to relitigate precluded issues is created. Peacock's independent-action jurisdiction holding remains intact but does not compel dismissal of this existing action. Weisgram's power to direct judgment after an actual fair opportunity remains intact; it does not authorize denying that opportunity. Kentucky v. Graham's liability-and-fee relationship is preserved at its actual setting rather than made a universal rule resolving this patent claim.

The personal $178,888.51 fee-and-cost judgment cannot stand in the form imposed. Remand removes that adjudication and permits a proper response to the personal claim; it does not vacate patent invalidity or Ohio Cellular's fee obligation, impose new sanctions, adjudicate fraud, resurrect disclaimed veil piercing or award immunity. No independent reopening rule or service violation is decided, and no recovery against Nelson is guaranteed. A contention requiring a different preserved claim or new liability facts must be established through ordinary proceedings.

The eight provisional positions are compatible with one common rationale; none requires a separate writing merely to restate its limits. No opinion author, full-Court tally or Stone position is assigned here.
