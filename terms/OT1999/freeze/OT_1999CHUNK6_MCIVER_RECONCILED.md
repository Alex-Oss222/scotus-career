## Grounds capable of common adoption

### Confined installation rule — seven direct joins

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer adopt both installation grounds. On this record, attaching the devices externally acquired no concealed information at installation and caused no established meaningful interference with McIver's possession, use or mechanical operation. The fact that the Government occupied space on the vehicle for an investigative purpose is considered, but does not itself meet the meaningful-interference threshold. The rule does not make damage or dispossession indispensable to every seizure.

Katz and the actual Bond and Pinson holdings protect retained information against added examination, including information acquired without a conventional entry. They do not make equipment use, investigative purpose or every physical contact a search. The supported attachment did not examine the contents of a closed effect. Public exposure remains relevant to what information was acquired, not a universal surrender of Fourth Amendment protection for an automobile.

Jacobsen supplies the independent meaningful-possession inquiry, and Soldal prevents disposing of it solely by finding no privacy intrusion. Karo's original installation occurred with consent before the respondents acquired the container. It therefore does not directly decide this nonconsensual attachment to an existing owner's vehicle. Its treatment of an unwanted beeper and rejection of automatic equivalence between technical trespass and meaningful interference nevertheless support the seven's conclusion by analogy. They expressly consider the ownership distinction and find it insufficient on the proved interference here.

Class distinguishes the public VIN from the vehicle interior: entry into the interior was a search even though the VIN lacked privacy, with reasonableness resting on the case's confined circumstances. Cardwell's exterior-examination rationale was a four-Justice plurality and depended on established probable cause; Powell supplied the judgment through a different habeas ground. Neither is adopted as a universal majority rule authorizing exterior manipulation or installation. These express qualifications refine the lower opinion's broad exposition while confirming its result at the supported installation boundary.

McIver conceded noncurtilage status for the open driveway/apron. That concession disposes of the proposed need to decide a residential boundary here; it does not authorize entry onto protected curtilage elsewhere. The ordinary public-road monitoring concession also remains separate. Knotts supplies context for exposed travel, not a new holding about ten days of continuous surveillance, all future technology or information from inside a home.

### Stevens's seizure and remedy position — one direct commitment

Stevens's own Karo separate opinion, 468 U.S. 728–736, treats government installation and use of an electronic device as appropriation of the owner's exclusionary control, independently of damage or loss of driving use. His view there did not depend on original ownership at installation; he regarded later delivery as beginning the interference. The present absence of consent on an already owned effect strengthens the application of that personal position. It also gives him a narrower boundary at which to distinguish Karo's controlling consent-before-transfer result. The former dissent is his own basis, not existing controlling law that the other seven must accept.

His Class joins and actual Soldal and Bond joins reinforce the distinction between exposure, the means used to acquire information and independent possession. He reserves whether the attachment is separately a search because the seizure ground suffices. His broader Karo privacy discussion is not silently converted into a present search holding, and the conceded monitoring issue remains outside independent merits review.

No warrant, consent or established installation exception justifies the seizure on the approved record. Actual Labron/Kilgore rejects an added exigency requirement when its automobile exception applies, but does not automatically authorize a continuing device installation or supply unadjudicated installation probable cause. Stevens does not import Kleinfeld's additional-exigency language as current law. Any genuinely preserved distinct justification is confined to its actual facts and procedural availability; the remand creates none.

Stevens would vacate and remand for lawful review of existing-record consequences. The analysis first identifies evidence actually caused by the unlawful installation and any established independent lawful source. Evidence independently admissible on that basis is not tainted evidence rendered harmless. For constitutionally tainted evidence actually admitted, Chapman puts the burden on the Government to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the guilty verdict. Strong other evidence is relevant but is not by itself the required actual-verdict conclusion.

The strongest objection to remand is Kleinfeld's judgment that extensive independent surveillance made tracking superfluous. Stevens does not disregard that assessment or presume that every observation was tainted. But the lower majority did not decide that alternative, and the truck-warrant ruling removed information derived from the unlawful house entry, not all information potentially caused by tracking. A lawful source independent of the house entry does not necessarily establish independence from the installation. The neutral packet leaves the causal and verdict-effect inquiries materially distinct; no new factual finding resolves them here.

His actual Neder whole-opinion join confirms that an initially unfinished appellate harmlessness inquiry can warrant a bounded remand and that the Government retains its preserved-error burden. Neder's omitted-materiality method and his supplementary equivalent-finding rationale do not become a universal test for Fourth Amendment evidentiary error. Conversely, his Karo objection to unbriefed first-instance reconstruction of a warrant record is not a categorical bar to appellate harmlessness review. Together, these positions support review confined to the actual record and available issues, with no reopened opportunity to supply missing independent-source or harmlessness proof.

If lawful admissibility or constitutional harmlessness is established under the applicable distinct standards, the affected conviction survives this objection. If admitted constitutional taint is not harmless under the Government's burden, the affected conviction must be set aside, with a new trial permitted. This is not a determination of evidentiary insufficiency, automatic suppression of everything, unconditional new-trial order or acquittal.

## Departure and confirmation audit

The seven affirming Associates confirm the lower judgment and Part II-B's outcome for different Justice-specific reasons identified above. Their common formulation is narrower than an unqualified exterior-privacy rule: Class's interior-search holding, Cardwell's plurality status, Karo's original-consent boundary, actual Bond/Pinson information acquisition, and Soldal's independent possession protection remain express. No fictitious change in vehicle location, mechanical condition or monitoring evidence is needed to support those qualifications.

**Historical departure:** Stevens would depart from the lower affirmance, not from an established Supreme Court merits vote. His own Karo exclusion/control position supplies the different legal premise on seizure. At remedy, he does not accept the concurrence's superfluity assessment as resolving the preserved installation-fruit objection: the approved record's house-entry excision addressed a different illegality, and actual Neder/Chapman require the Government's actual-verdict showing for admitted constitutional taint. He supports bounded review of those unresolved existing-record questions while giving the extensive independent surveillance its full force; he does not posit missing factual events or a proven harmful error.

All eight confirm the monitoring-preservation boundary rather than infer a Supreme Court monitoring holding from the lower result. The September 22/23 discrepancy remains unresolved and immaterial here. Two devices were attached to one Toyota; the GPS failed after three days and the Birddog continued during the limited period. Neither speculative mechanical danger nor the later truck warrant supplies an installation finding. Mining and Saenz add no Fourth Amendment coverage rule or criminal proof opportunity.
