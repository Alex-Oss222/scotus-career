## Governing law and the two precise applications

All eight preserve Feres's service-incident bar and Brooks's nonservice boundary. No one overrules Feres within this application grant, makes formal furlough universally necessary for recovery, adopts an all-public-road exemption, or treats every on-base injury as barred. Johnson's rejection of a tortfeasor-status exception remains intact; Stencel begins with a service-incident injury and does not decide this threshold. Brown's separate postdischarge wrong does not turn Richards into a civilian.

Stanley reinforces the distinction between classifying an injury as service-incident and demanding additional proof of actual disruption in the particular suit. Once the former predicate is met, the latter is not an independent escape. Its Part II concerned a Bivens remedy and treated service incidence as already settled; Part I was jurisdictional concerning the dismissed FTCA claim. It does not decide Richards's classification or establish an all-military-property rule. Scalia's footnote 5 expressly preserves his Johnson distinction between statutory authorization and an implied constitutional remedy. His authorship cannot be used to erase that statutory position.

For O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, immediate departure from the military workplace while still within the installation and continuing in nonfurlough active service makes the injury incident to service despite the accepted private purpose. All five theories and their derivative claims are therefore barred. The panel's doubts about particular discipline costs do not defeat that classification.

For Stevens, Scalia, Souter and Ginsburg, the accepted release, private journey, private automobile and ordinary public-road setting place the operation and roadworthy-equipment theories outside that service relationship, even on the added geographic assumption. Their ground is the threshold classification in this combined setting. It is not an extra requirement of proving case-specific discipline interference after finding the injury service-incident, nor a rule making an assigned task indispensable in every Feres case.

All eight separately preserve Shearer's command holding for the actual permission and order allegations. Those claims challenge the Army's authorization of an allegedly unqualified soldier to perform the driving assignment, including an order despite inadequate skill or experience. Ordinary-negligence phrasing and the injured member's private activity do not erase that command challenge. Conversely, the maintenance and knowingly unsafe-road-use allegations are not proved procurement, deployment or resource-allocation challenges merely because the vehicle was military. The disputed classification and actual allegations, not count labels alone, explain the component treatment.

Actual Minns supplies an eight-Associate controlling rule for genuine protected policy discretion under §2680(a), with binding-direction and protected-choice qualifications. Its whole-dismissal judgment had no controlling Feres rationale or complete five-Justice §2680(a) application. It creates neither a general military-family immunity nor an automatic operations exception. No Justice converts the absence of a reproduced directive into proof of protected discretion; no independent §2680(a) disposition is frozen here.
