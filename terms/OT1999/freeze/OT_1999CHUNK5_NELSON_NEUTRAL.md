## Event and question

No. 99-502; §1254(1) review of Ohio Cellular Products Corp. v. Adams USA, Inc., 175 F.3d 1343 (Fed. Cir. April 26, 1999), rehearing denied June 3. Argument March 27, 2000; event April 25, 2000. Granted question: whether due process and the Federal Rules permit adding a corporate officer after judgment and simultaneously imposing personal liability without opportunity to respond. Nelson seeks removal of the personal judgment and ordinary opportunity to answer the claim. All nine Justices were seated at both milestones; no recusal is established; ordinary quorum and majority rules apply.

## Material record and lower reasoning

Ohio Cellular sued Adams USA and Apehead for patent infringement. The patents were held invalid, and that judgment was affirmed. The District Court found the case exceptional for purposes of 35 U.S.C. §285, relying on inequitable conduct during prosecution of the patent. A September 1997 reconsideration ruling followed an evidentiary hearing at which Nelson testified and denied intentional withholding of material prior art. Nelson was president and sole shareholder and had controlled the corporation's litigation, but he was not then a party personally defending against a fee claim.

The corporation's counsel sent an October 13, 1997 letter saying a fee award above a limited amount would be uncollectible because the corporation would be liquidated. The majority linked the letter to Nelson through his control; that inference is not a separate adjudicated fraudulent-transfer event. Fees and costs of $178,888.51 were entered against Ohio Cellular January 20, 1998. On February 3, Adams sought leave to amend its third-party complaint and the judgment to add Nelson personally. On March 25, the District Court granted both amendments. Nelson's motion to alter or amend was denied May 18; he appealed. The federal patent jurisdiction and prior corporate judgment are established; no new independent collection action is the case under review.

The Federal Circuit majority treated the question as discretionary Rule 15 amendment. It relied on the same underlying conduct, Nelson's actual participation and control, near identity of interests, anticipated personal exposure, and lack of specified prejudice or a different defense. It distinguished Kentucky v. Graham's vicarious fee setting, relied on Foman's liberal amendment standard and Fromson, and regarded the omitted personal party status as a formality under these circumstances. It treated the underlying inequitable-conduct determination as unchallenged on appeal. That is not a finding that Nelson admitted personal liability or consented to litigate it as a party.

Judge Newman dissented. She distinguished permission to amend from entry of judgment on the new claim; testimony and control of corporate defense did not provide notice that personal liability was being tried. She emphasized that Adams disclaimed veil piercing and that Nelson's personal tort liability had never been pleaded or litigated. She distinguished Fromson because a pretrial motion there gave the proposed parties direct notice and was denied after false assurances of solvency. She also identified a postjudgment reopening objection and challenged the majority's assumptions that Nelson's personal wrongdoing or comprehensiveness of his own defense had been conceded. These are opposing lower-court legal analyses; none is an entered Supreme conclusion.

## Preservation and boundaries

The lower majority expressly addressed Nelson's nonparty-fee objection, the fairness of postjudgment joinder and notice/prejudice; its footnote 5 linked due process to the Rule 15 inquiry. The dissent squarely addressed personal notice and opportunity to defend. The supported procedural claim concerns the substance of this objection; it need not be transformed into a blanket rule barring all postjudgment amendments. A contention that Nelson forfeited the claim merely by failing to invoke a particular rule number requires examination of what objection was presented, not an inference from the compact brief. The available opinion supports review of the same personal-liability objection and contains no established waiver of the chance to answer after joinder.

Adams's disclaimer of veil piercing must be preserved. The question does not require a ruling that Nelson could never be personally liable under any properly pleaded theory, a new merits review of patent validity, or reopening Ohio Cellular's unchallenged fee obligation. Whether a separate legally founded theory would ultimately succeed is not established by corporate control alone.

## Entering law and rules

The slice supplies Peacock (February 21, 1996), **Ancillary enforcement and a new judgment debtor**: a later independent action imposing another's entire debt needs its own jurisdictional basis, while ordinary enforcement of debtor assets remains distinct. This case involves amendment within existing patent litigation; Peacock does not mechanically dictate jurisdictional dismissal but precludes treating collection need as substantive authority to create a new debtor.

The slice also supplies **Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants** (Richards June 10, 1996; Matsushita February 27, 1996): identical interests alone do not bind an absent person's rights; constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate representation or another recognized relationship requires its actual predicates. A finding of litigation control relevant to issue preclusion is not automatically personal party status or adjudication of a new personal claim. Zenith Radio v. Hazeltine Research, 395 U.S.100 (1969), supplies the distinction between a subsidiary's participation and personal judgment against an unjoined parent; Foman, 371 U.S.178 (1962), supplies liberal amendment subject to delay, bad faith, repeated failure, prejudice and futility, not automatic judgment following amendment. Mullane and the ordinary notice/opportunity principle remain relevant; Kentucky v. Graham concerns the connection between merits liability and a fee award within its actual civil-rights setting.

The then-effective Rule 15(a) allowed amendment by leave or consent after its as-of-right predicates and directed that leave be freely given when justice requires. It separately required a response to an amended pleading within the remaining original period or ten days after service, whichever was longer, unless the court otherwise ordered. That timing power does not expressly authorize dispensing with a meaningful response altogether. Rule 15(b) addresses issues actually tried by express or implied consent and permits conforming amendment even after judgment; knowledge of corporate litigation does not itself establish that Nelson consented to trial of his personal liability. Rule 15(c)'s relation-back conditions address claim/party identity and limitations; they are distinct from adjudication and cannot themselves prove merits liability. Rule 4 service and ordinary defense/answer rights remain applicable to an added defendant according to their actual predicates; this packet does not decide a separate service defect or impose a modern response period.

## Supported alternatives and remedies

Nelson's path: liberal joinder permits assertion of a claim but does not conclusively resolve it. The personal defendant must have notice and opportunity to answer and present defenses before adverse adjudication. His role as witness/controller for the corporation and ability to oppose amendment are not necessarily an opportunity to litigate the personal claim on its merits. The strongest objection is that the same alleged misconduct was fully contested under his direction and no new defense was identified; fair notice and control can sometimes make a separate trial unnecessary.

Adams's path: sustain the amendment and judgment as a narrow application of actual notice, complete control, fully litigated conduct and absence of prejudice; recognize that the same person could not fairly use a corporation to avoid the consequence of his own conduct. The strongest objection is that this assumes the very personal claim never tried and substitutes prediction of liability for procedure. It risks treating separate legal identity as immaterial without proving consent, alter ego or another recognized basis.

A narrower reversal may assume joinder was permissible and remove only simultaneous personal liability, remanding for an answer and ordinary consideration of properly presented claims and defenses. It neither requires a new trial on every corporate issue nor grants Nelson immunity. A supported preclusion argument on remand retains only its lawful scope and cannot be prejudged. Affirmance would preserve the personal fee judgment only on its actual procedural basis, not create a general rule that corporate officers may be made judgment debtors whenever the corporation cannot pay.

No path warrants reopening patent invalidity or Ohio Cellular's original fee judgment, ordering veil piercing despite its disclaimer, imposing new sanctions, inventing fraud or guaranteeing recovery against Nelson. The exact amount is the existing fee-and-cost award, not a new damages calculation.
