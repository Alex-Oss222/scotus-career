## What the historical comparison resolves

The historical dissent expressly accepted higher-grade value as an element, construed basic larceny as included, and proposed a limited defendant election of the higher grade. It acknowledged the resulting asymmetry under Schmuck and treated the request as waiver of notice by indictment. Therefore neither acceptance of the value element nor awareness of Schmuck's reciprocity objection distinguishes the four historical dissenters from their present alternatives. The initial value-only affirmance could not be supported on those premises. The fresh stages have removed it.

The revised four-Justice return has the same direction and principal common-law inclusion position as the dissent, but reserves its affirmative grade-election rule. That remaining difference requires its own justification; it is not concealed by describing the historical disagreement merely as a dispute about whether value is an element. Conversely, the four affirming Associates retain the historical majority's three-element analysis within the charged completed-taking configuration. No presumed present vote follows from either historical group.

## Reconciled result and common propositions

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas affirm. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer vacate the categorical noninclusion disposition and remand within the limits below.** No Associate orders acquittal, entry of a larceny conviction or an automatic new trial. No final Court result is inferred before assembly.

All eight apply Schmuck's comparison of legally construed statutory elements, independently of whether the evidence would permit a rational jury to convict of the lesser and acquit of the greater. Factual overlap alone is insufficient. All eight classify value exceeding **$1,000** as an element of §2113(b)'s ten-year grade; its statutory position, repeated grade structure and increased maximum support that conclusion. The charged first-paragraph §2113(a) offense has no value threshold, so the higher larceny grade is not itself a statutory subset merely because substantial money was alleged or proved.

The indictment alleges approximately $14,000; the trial account describes approximately $16,000. Neither figure becomes an element of the statutory robbery definition. The actual proposed charge used an obsolete $100 threshold. A lawful charge would apply $1,000, but this handoff neither changes the historical request nor treats the stale number alone as dispositive. The original letter's broader bank-larceny theory and the attached higher-grade charge require distinct consideration.

### Four affirmance commitments

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas construe §2113(b) to add intent to steal or purloin and taking plus carrying away. The charged first paragraph of subsection (a) requires knowing conduct satisfying its forcible-or-intimidating taking conditions, without those additional requirements. The entry paragraph's express purpose to commit a felony or larceny is not transplanted into this completed-taking branch. Their culpability construction does not punish innocent accidental taking or treat the separate attempt offense as an automatic answer to this completed offense.

The strongest objection invokes robbery's inherited relation to larceny, the stylistic account of removing feloniously, and the possibility that takes already carries the modest movement requirement. These four answer through the enacted contrasting descriptions and the already culpable conduct specified in subsection (a). Morissette and Staples require appropriate fault, not every common-law incident of an analogous crime. Bell also prevents treating the whole bank statute as an unchanged common-law code. The provisions must be construed, not compared by counting different words; their construction produces the additional requirements.

Those independent intent and movement grounds defeat inclusion of either larceny grade. Higher-grade value separately defeats the requested felony as a statutory subset. They therefore need not decide whether the lower paragraph contains an affirmative upper-value element. They also reject the proposed grade-election route on this record: unilateral notice consent does not itself answer Schmuck's reciprocity requirement or the separate charging safeguards. They do not decide the disputed force evidence, the independent evidentiary entitlement or every possible charging theory.

### Four basic-inclusion and bounded-return commitments

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer construe the charged completed forcible taking to retain theft intent and movement. The common-law relation, statutory setting and asserted stylistic deletion support those legally construed elements; the heading alone is not dispositive. Morissette and the contextual approach of Staples and X-Citement Video support this reading. Bell's broader theft coverage does not require stripping the completed robbery branch of those shared ingredients. Their conclusion is an elements holding, not revival of Schmuck's inherent-relationship test.

They construe the lower paragraph as the basic theft grade, with a one-year maximum when the additional aggravated value element is not established as part of the offense. The Government need not affirmatively prove that the amount could not exceed $1,000. A larger taking does not render the basic theft innocent. The lower-value wording still limits the unaggravated punishment; proving facts about a larger sum does not authorize the higher maximum without the aggravated offense's requirements. This construction answers the strongest textual objection rather than silently deleting the lower-value language.

On those premises basic larceny is included in the charged completed robbery. The additional felony element does not answer whether the actual broader request sought a legally available basic instruction. The letter states larceny as the defense to disputed force or intimidation; its attached deficient higher-grade formulation does not conclusively establish abandonment of every basic option. The categorical Mosley ruling bypassed that scope question. These four would set it aside and permit ordinary review of the request, preservation, threshold correction and the Government's independent rational-jury argument. They create no duty to give every unrequested lesser instruction and no new preservation fact.

Keeble explains the function of an actually included alternative where a jury could convict of the lesser while acquitting of the greater. It does not dispense with either inclusion or evidence. The Government may still prevail on a properly assessed request or evidentiary ground. Proof sufficient to sustain robbery is not itself proof that no rational jury could find only theft. The disputed customer contact and other trial circumstances receive no new credibility finding here. Ordinary remedy and any available error inquiry remain separate from the legal classification.

## The higher-grade election: precise reservation and departure

The four returning Associates **do not adopt an automatic grade-election exception, but they also do not hold that every such election is forbidden**. Their sufficient ground is independently included basic larceny and the unresolved scope of the actual request. A proposed higher-grade route would require its own supported explanation of Schmuck, the full charge and any legally necessary consent or waiver. This clarification prevents the frozen nonadoption from becoming a categorical rejection the sources do not support.

The strongest argument for deciding more is concrete. Carter affirmatively requested a larceny instruction; this is more than an ordinary admission of a historical fact. His indictment actually alleges an amount above the current threshold. Basic and aggravated larceny may reasonably be viewed as grades of the same underlying theft, and the proposed asymmetry could be confined to that relationship. The historical dissent confronted those points and the reciprocity objection. The present record does not permit pretending that these objections disappeared or that the dissent had overlooked felony value.

The changed legal premise supporting **reservation**, rather than rejection, is the actual simulated treatment of aggravated-offense procedure and the Associates' prior bounded mandates. Almendarez-Torres and Jones expressly distinguish factual awareness or admission from the legal charge and other element safeguards. X-Citement Video Part V, joined by all four returning Associates, preserves the actual indictment question despite an existing culpability finding. Their unanimous Castillo join likewise requires lawful treatment of the actual charge, preservation and error consequences after identifying an aggravated element. Those prior commitments make it material to decide what the actual request and indictment legally accomplish before deeming a request alone a complete procedural election.

This is a limited analogy, not a claim that those precedents decided Rule 31(c) election. They do not hold that an affirmative request can never supply valid consent; nor do they establish a missing value allegation here. The full legal charging effect remains open under the chosen offense construction. The return already has an independent basic-offense ground, so these four can preserve the disputed election without either manufacturing a waiver or denying every possible lawful route. Their prior public mandates supply a concrete remedial basis for doing so; a generic preference for caution would not suffice.

No present join rests on a finding that Carter waived a grand jury, waived jury determination, admitted value as an element of a properly charged felony, or obtained a new charge. Factual notice, legal sufficiency of the indictment, consent to an instruction, grand-jury waiver, and jury-element admission remain distinct. Stirone protects the actual grand-jury charge against enlargement, but its prosecution-created amendment setting does not decide this defendant-requested proposal. If the higher-grade route later becomes necessary, an actual legal basis and its supported predicates must be addressed, not inferred from this reservation.
