## Comparator result and governing comparison

Each Associate's independent judgment is confirmed. The historical majority rejected express displacement, preserved independent conflict analysis, and treated the deliberately chosen mix and transition as substantive federal policy. The dissent disputed exclusivity against saved liability and stressed text, federalism, agency procedure and the inadequacy of inferred conflict. These writings confirm a real dispute about the legal force of the demonstrated objectives; they do not supply authors or automatically import every paragraph into the present joins.

The independently selected positions are narrower than wholesale adoption of either historical writing. No Justice is moved by a vote quota or a presumed historical alignment.

The concrete in-world legal baseline matters. Gade's five-Justice result rejects automatic displacement from occupational overlap; its four-Justice physical-impossibility formulation is not controlling. Kennedy's separate position there rejects generalized purpose balancing. Myrick preserves supported actual-conflict analysis despite an express clause, but its regulatory gap proves no affirmative federal policy against installation; Scalia joined only that judgment. Medtronic separately recognizes that damages duties can be requirements, that actual substantive counterparts must be identified, and that formal agency interpretation receives weight only within statutory authority. Shanklin turns on a particular completed federal device-selection arrangement, not every minimum safety standard. Locke's assigned vessel-safety field does not convert every agency choice into an exclusive federal field; Souter and Thomas withheld the reporting extension without an adequate provision-specific basis. Mining and Public Lands Council require the actual delegation and text; neither makes regulatory purpose self-executing preemption. ## Statutory and regulatory qualifications retained

The full operative text and qualifications in the neutral packet and supplement remain incorporated; the following distinctions are mandatory in any explanation using them.

Former §1392(d) concerns a federal standard in effect and the same aspect of performance, permits enforcement of identical standards, and permits higher performance requirements for vehicles or equipment procured for a federal, state or local government's own use. Former §1397(k) preserves common-law liability despite compliance. Neither provision disappears through an abstract uniformity claim; no government-procurement dispute is decided here.

The three restraint options are not interchangeable labels for airbags. S4.1.2.1 provides automatic frontal/angular protection at front outboard seats with separate center/rear belt rules and alternative lateral/rollover arrangements. S4.1.2.2 provides the narrower head-on automatic arrangement with prescribed belts and warning requirements. S4.1.2.3 provides the lap/shoulder-and-warning route with its alternative Type 1/testing conditions, distinct center/other-seat requirements and convertible/open-body qualifications. The automatic amendment concerns passenger-car front outboard positions; retained requirements for other seats and vehicle classes do not become an airbag mandate.

The phase-in uses 10%, 25% and 40% for the successive September-to-August production periods beginning September 1, 1986. The 1984 rule uses the specified preceding three-year production averages; the supplied 1986 text permits that average or current-year annual production. Nonbelt driver protection with automatic right-front protection receives 1.5 credit; the right-front system need not use the same technology. The 1986 separate one-credit rule permits qualifying nonbelt driver protection with manual Type 2 right-front protection. The first-year carry includes qualifying production from September 1985 through August 1986; later carry periods expand through August 1987 and August 1988 only for vehicles not already counted. No double counting, Honda-specific denominator or actual credit allocation is inferred.

Multiple-manufacturer attribution is single attribution: imported vehicles go to the importer and domestic vehicles to the participating marketer, subject to the qualifying written assignment to one manufacturer and Part 585 reporting. The 1986 due-care provision requires the manufacturer to establish no reason to know of nonconformity despite due care; it addresses regulatory noncompliance and establishes neither tort immunity nor Honda's diligence. The 1984 text proposed later action on that subject, rather than already adopting the 1986 provision.

The September 1, 1989 all-new-car automatic-protection rule is subject to S4.1.5. The Secretary must make the determination no later than April 1, 1989 that qualifying belt laws cover at least two-thirds of the combined population of the 50 States and District of Columbia under the most recent Census estimates. Vehicles made on or after that determination may use any of the three options. This is not enactment in two-thirds of the States. Qualifying laws require proper front-seat belt use during forward movement, medical-only waivers, at least a $25 penalty including costs for each violator, permission to mitigate damages for an injured violator, a compliance-encouragement program and effectiveness by September 1, 1989. Existing state permission to mitigate can suffice; permission is not an obligation to reduce damages. Preamble discussion of waivers for substantially compliant pre-August 1, 1984 laws and state evaluation reports is not silently substituted into the operative list. No finding establishes that the trigger occurred.

The program's safety, development, acceptance, innovation, cost and timing reasons coexist with encouragement of nonbelt technology and recognition of airbag benefits. It does not prescribe a numerical technology mix or a minimum number of airbag-free cars. The agency's own response concerning potential liability for deficient systems at 49 FR 29000 is material, rather than merely an attributed comment. Affirmance supporters distinguish that liability from the categorical duty at issue; reversal supporters consider it evidence against inferred exclusivity. Neither reading eliminates the passage.

The source supplement's 1984 heading/body mismatch, 1986 correction, and narrower source limits remain recorded. No adjudication rests on the mismatch. No Honda production, credit, attribution, due-care, test-performance or belt-law finding is supplied. A later proposal requiring such a fact must return to neutral validation and independent modeling.
