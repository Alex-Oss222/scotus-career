## Objective correction and complete lower judgment

The full [Seventh Circuit opinion, 179F.3d546](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/179/179.F3d.546.98-3590.html), was read. The bankruptcy court disallowed the roughly $900,000 state claim including interest; the District Court affirmed. The Seventh Circuit reversed and remanded, upholding the State's claim because the trustee had not carried Illinois's burden of persuasion. Thus the raw packet's statement that the judgment under review shifted the burden to the State is incorrect. The trustee seeks reversal of the Seventh Circuit; the State defends it.

Chandler Enterprises, of which Stoecker was president, acquired a corporate aircraft through retailer JPA and its financing affiliate PLI. Chandler paid an initial $2.4million, obtained possession and brought the plane to Illinois in June1988, then exercised its option and obtained title in September. The lower court rejected the isolated-sale defense under Illinois's treatment of the retailer's title transfer. It rejected the District Court's view that the Tax Injunction Act prevented deciding the predicate tax question, referring to Bankruptcy Code §505(a)(1). Those determinations remain distinct from the granted federal burden question.

The State issued a corporate tax notice and a personal penalty notice under its responsible-officer law. The latter applies to the officer with control, supervision or responsibility for returns/payment who willfully fails to perform the duty. The assessment is prima facie evidence. Under the state rule as construed by the Seventh Circuit, the notice shifts production and persuasion to the officer. The record contained no evidence about Stoecker's tax responsibilities or willfulness. Naming Pluhar as financial officer may satisfy production, the court said, but did not satisfy persuasion. There was no evidence Stoecker saw or relied on counsel's no-tax-due letter. Do not convert that absence into an affirmative historical finding of knowing evasion or exclusive responsibility.

The court separately rejected the proof-of-claim timeliness objection under the governing pre-1994 §726 provisions and denied a subordination theory based on delay attributable to the unreported/unregistered aircraft. The grant concerns whether federal bankruptcy changes the state-law burden, not an unrestricted reopening of all tax, timeliness or subordination rulings. Preserved genuine alternatives retain their actual footing; none is invented here.

## Entering law and opposing paths

Butner, 440U.S.48, supplies the rule that state-created property interests ordinarily retain their incidents in bankruptcy absent a federal rule requiring otherwise. Grogan, 498U.S.279, distinguishes state-law validity of a claim from the separate federal dischargeability inquiry and its burden. These predivergence authorities are not a categorical instruction that every procedural rule must be state law. Sections502 and505 provide claims/tax adjudication authority; Bankruptcy Rule3001(f)'s prima-facie evidentiary treatment of a properly filed claim must be kept apart from the ultimate persuasion burden. Under 28U.S.C.§2075 bankruptcy rules do not abridge, enlarge or modify substantive rights. Express Code allocations control where they apply.

The slice copies Noland/CFI's current restriction against replacing statutory entitlements and distribution choices with generalized equity, and Greenwich Collieries' distinction between persuasion, production, lawful presumptions and separately allocated defenses. Greenwich's APA proponent allocation is not an automatic bankruptcy rule. The Court's own tax-characterization cases concern the federal definition of priority, not a command to shift the proof of state liability; their exact qualification is retained.

* Retain the state burden: persuasion is an incident of the state tax claim, and neither ordinary bankruptcy administration nor Rule3001(f) changes it. Different underlying claims may carry different burdens without denying the Code's distributional equality. Strongest counterargument: once a proof of claim is rebutted in the federal claims-allowance process, a uniform creditor-persuasion rule can be characterized as procedure rather than an alteration of the claim.
* Apply a bankruptcy persuasion rule: federal claims procedure could place ultimate proof on the claimant after the trustee rebuts prima facie evidence, promoting uniform administration and creditor treatment. Strongest counterargument: it needs an actual Code or lawful rule basis sufficient to displace the state entitlement; generalized equality is not an enacted change, and a production presumption does not determine ultimate persuasion.
* A narrow decision can identify the burden source and dispose of this federal objection while leaving express federal departures, proof-of-claim evidentiary consequences, valid assessment defenses and distinct dischargeability rules intact. It should not create a universal tax presumption, negate willfulness as an element, or place every bankruptcy burden on debtors.

Affirmance on burden would leave the Seventh Circuit's existing disposition in force at the granted scope. Reversal would require ordinary reconsideration under the adopted federal burden, not automatic disallowance, a new tax exemption or a finding that the State lacks evidence outside the appellate account. Exact claim amount, payment instructions and priority consequences are not fixed by classification of persuasion alone.
