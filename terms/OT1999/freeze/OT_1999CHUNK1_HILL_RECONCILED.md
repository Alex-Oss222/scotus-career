## Reconciled common propositions and application

All eight Associates reach the federal compact merits. No independent state ground, preservation defect, jurisdictional obstacle or nonparticipation is established. Their agreement extends to each proposition A–D at the level stated here, not merely to a shared judgment.

**A. Express date agreement within counsel's authority.** When defense counsel affirmatively and without an established qualification agrees, before expiration, to a specified trial date outside the applicable Article III(a) period, that scheduling agreement binds the represented prisoner as to the agreed trial date. The compact does not require the prisoner's personal attendance, assent or waiver colloquy for this scheduling act. This allocates the timing of a contested trial to counsel; it does not transfer the accused's choices whether to plead guilty, surrender trial, waive counsel or take an appeal.

**B. This agreement is sufficient.** Counsel's express January 9, 1995 agreement to the May 1 date supplies the required affirmative scheduling assent. Expressly accepting that date and proposing that same date have the same legal effect. No separate reference to Article III, proof of deadline-specific advice or personal authorization is required for this category. Hill's initial Article III request establishes the default timing entitlement, not a personal veto over all subsequent scheduling. His April 17 pretrial motion preserves the legal objection to the agreement's effect; it neither proves invalidity nor restores the dismissal entitlement defeated by the agreement itself. No general rule about withdrawing consent or a later independently grounded request to change the schedule is adopted.

**C. Agreement, continuance and inability tolling remain distinct.** The agreement is not a good-cause finding. A necessary or reasonable continuance under Article III(a) still requires good cause shown in open court with the prisoner or counsel present. Article VI(a) separately requires inability to stand trial, as determined by the court with jurisdiction. Neither is found here as an independent ground for the January-to-May delay. The statute's reference to prisoner or counsel supports counsel's recognized scheduling role, but presence alone supplies neither good cause nor the legal answer about waiver. The proviso governing judicial extensions does not expressly displace ordinary scheduling authority or forbid a represented party's agreement to a particular date. Public purposes do not erase the proviso's requirements; neither do they make all affirmative date agreements ineffective.

**D. Disposition, remedy and subsequent stage.** Reverse the New York Court of Appeals' federal compact ground for dismissing the indictment and remand for proceedings consistent with this agreement's effect, including genuinely remaining, properly available issues. The handoff does not require unconditional reinstatement of the conviction or adjudicate those issues. Article V(c) continues to require dismissal with prejudice when the applicable period has actually been violated without a legally effective agreement or applicable statutory exception. No defense-impairment, bad-faith or harmlessness condition is added. This agreement defeats this dismissal ground; it does not weaken the remedy for an actual violation.

The clock remains Article III(a)'s **180 days**, beginning upon actual receipt of the required notice and final-disposition request by **both** the prosecuting officer and appropriate court. The sending custody was **Ohio**, not federal custody. The accepted 167-day computation is the trial court's finding on January 9, not a new reconstruction; accepted motion tolling establishes no universal automatic-tolling rule. No exact receipt or expiration date is invented. The record does not establish Hill's presence, personal knowledge, assent or objection, or counsel's knowledge of the precise deadline. None is supplied by the legal conclusion that this scheduling agreement binds him.

Silence, an actual qualification or reserved objection to the date, an established personal conflict, coercion, misunderstanding, ineffective assistance, indefinite delay, delay beyond May 1, consent first given after expiration, anti-shuttling and other compact protections remain distinct matters. Mentioning them preserves scope; it creates no mandatory factual exception, colloquy, legal-advice checklist or new burden of proof. No such predicate is found here. Hill's preserved later dismissal motion must not be mislabeled a contemporaneous reservation at the scheduling proceeding. No independent constitutional speedy-trial claim is decided.
