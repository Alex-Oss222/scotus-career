## Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens

### Corrected record and review limits

The judgment below is **162 F.3d 195 (2d Cir. 1998), No. 97-6141**, decided December 7, 1998. The District Court denied the State's motion to dismiss for statutory noncoverage and Eleventh Amendment protection; the State took an immediate immunity appeal, and district proceedings were stayed. The Second Circuit affirmed. It treated the statutory issue as within pendent appellate review. This is not a final fraud judgment.

Former employee Jonathan Stevens alleged that Vermont's environmental agency used advance estimates of federal-project work rather than actual hours, instructed employees to match those estimates in biweekly reports, and thereby obtained and retained excess EPA grant payments. These are complaint allegations accepted for the dismissal motion, not adjudicated fraud. The United States declined to intervene in the district-court suit after extensions, reserved specified monitoring and later-intervention interests, and supported Stevens as amicus on dismissal. It intervened in the **appeal** under 28 U.S.C. §§517 and 2403(a); that act did not take over the underlying relator prosecution.

The supplied grant separately presents Article III relator standing and whether the State is a liable person. Neither adjudicated liability nor any amount follows from resolving those questions. Statutory coverage, Article III standing, State amenability and the Executive's constitutional control remain distinct. No Article II holding should issue from a mere recognition that its concerns exist; identify actual question scope and preservation before reaching it.

### Entering law and opposing paths

Lujan supplies concrete injury, causation and redressability, with litigation-stage proof requirements. Steel Co. bars assuming Article III jurisdiction in order to resolve disputed merits. Laidlaw permits an authorized Treasury penalty to redress a supported personal injury through deterrence, but does not create an injury for an uninjured bounty seeker. Here the actual pecuniary injury alleged belongs to the United States; whether the FCA transfers an enforceable portion of that claim, rather than merely promising compensation for bringing suit, is the live Article III question. Historical informers' actions and ordinary assignment principles may support the transfer analysis, but neither a bounty alone nor the government's presence in an appellate brief establishes standing automatically.

Florida Prepaid and Kimel foreclose an assumption that Article I categorically cannot support unmistakably authorized private State enforcement. Their rule still requires both a lawful underlying obligation/enforcement measure and unmistakable authorization of the State's exposure. College Savings rejects consent inferred merely from ordinary continued activity. No compulsory sequence requiring a constitutional immunity judgment before statutory construction follows from those cases.

The enacted FCA provision uses a person category without a general definition in §3729; §§3730(a) and (b) distinguish Attorney General enforcement and private relator actions. Section 3733's investigative definition expressly includes States **for that section**; its wording is evidence in the interpretive dispute, not a silently transferable definition. The ordinary threefold damages and civil-penalty provision has an actual cooperation exception: disclosure within thirty days of first obtaining the information, full cooperation, and disclosure before the listed proceeding or known investigation allow a court to assess no less than double damages. Do not characterize every FCA recovery as mandatory treble damages. The complete official [1994 statutory text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title31/html/USCODE-1994-title31-subtitleIII-chap37-subchapIII-sec3729.htm) supplies the exception's terms; no amount is before this Court.

**Supported path sustaining the lower judgment:** Treat the relator as enforcing a congressionally assigned portion of the government's concrete financial claim; construe person in its statutory context to include States, using the repeated plaintiff/defendant term, States' acknowledged relator role, the investigation provision, the 1986 enactment context and the federal-fisc protection purpose. Separately analyze whether the federal government's retained control and predominant recovery make the action equivalent to a sovereign action, or whether the statute unmistakably authorizes private enforcement under the effective Article I rule. The strongest objection is that capacity to benefit as a claimant does not establish liability as a sovereign defendant, §3733 limits its own definition, and the relator retains an independent claim and important control when intervention is declined.

**Supported path rejecting State liability:** Recognize relator standing through transfer of the government's claim, but apply the ordinary presumption that person excludes a sovereign unless statutory context sufficiently rebuts it. Private plaintiffs' procedural control, the separate investigative definition and the remedies bear on that reading. The strongest objection is that this may underread the statute's consistent usage and anti-fraud coverage; a purpose to protect the Treasury does not itself answer the statutory question. Statutory exclusion permits a limited disposition without deciding constitutional immunity or claims by the United States itself.

**Distinct threshold path:** If the FCA creates no constitutionally sufficient transfer of the government's claim and no personal injury supports this relator, jurisdictional dismissal precedes coverage. The contrary case is not simply a reward-created injury: the asserted assignment of an existing federal financial claim must be addressed. Do not collapse this alternative into a universal ban on citizen enforcement.

The lower majority relies on the government's interests and retained control to reject immunity, and on statutory context to include States. Judge Weinstein's dissent stresses the relator's independent financial interest, limits on governmental unilateral control and the nondelegability of the United States' immunity exception, with Article II/III concerns discussed separately. The dissent's historical Article I prohibition is displaced by entering simulated law. The majority's statutory and action-identity grounds therefore require independent assessment even though a later immunity premise changed.

## Eisenberg v. Montgomery County Public Schools

### Verified posture, policy and exact appellate remedy

The judgment is **197 F.3d123 (4th Cir.1999), No.98-2503**, decided October6 and filed November19,1999. The appeal originated in denial of a preliminary injunction, but the appellate remedy is not confined to an interim determination. Paragraph27 directs immediate preliminary admission to Rosemary Hills and then a **final injunction requiring reconsideration without race, as of the original application date**. Paragraph28 dispenses with a further evidentiary hearing because the panel considered the decisive facts uncontested. Paragraph29 leaves the other transfer factors undisturbed. That actual final direction must be addressed expressly; neither final merits trial nor an exclusively preliminary mandate may be invented.

The transfer policy proceeds through stability, utilization/enrollment, diversity profile and the transfer reason. School racial percentages are compared with countywide percentages and three-year trends, with four categories and annual reevaluation. The policy normally denies transfers worsening the assigned diversity category, with a unique-personal-hardship exception. Five of nineteen white applicants leaving Glen Haven received hardship exceptions for the relevant year, four for siblings already at the receiving school. These are qualifications in operation, not proof that this applicant qualified. Rosemary Hills uses a lottery if eligible requests exceed places; the racial exclusion could prevent lottery eligibility.

The court reports race as the **sole given reason** for denying Jacob's transfer, not merely one contributing consideration. Glen Haven's white enrollment was24.1%, compared with53.4% countywide, and had fallen from38.9% in1994–95. No desegregation decree or judicial violation finding governed the County; the County asserted voluntary dismantlement and educational integration. Absence of a decree does not establish absence of every constitutionally relevant condition or prove current remedial authority.

The District Court used the then-applicable Blackwelder framework: relative threatened hardships, likelihood of success and public interest. It found the hardship balance slightly favored the County and required a strong likelihood showing, which it found absent. The Fourth Circuit reviewed its legal premise de novo. The appellate court **assumed without deciding** that diversity could be a compelling interest and held the mechanism impermissible racial balancing/not narrowly tailored; do not attribute to it a categorical holding that educational diversity can never qualify.

### Entering law and alternatives

Adarand's common substantial-justification standard, including actual evidence, coverage, burdens and concrete practicable alternatives, governs all government racial classifications. Its controlling five-Justice rule displaces categorical strict scrutiny; the four Justices' different grounds remain published positions, not current law. Hopwood recognizes supported educational benefits at the university level and expressly leaves other educational levels outside that holding's decided scope. Eisenberg presents the application/possible extension to K–12. No invented lower K–12 tier follows. Lesage separates unequal-process injury from entitlement to a seat, compensation and future relief. Slater preserves prospective-standing and cessation requirements. Neither makes a race-free-only counterfactual mandatory where current law permits justified racial consideration.

**Supported vacatur/remand:** The lower court used the displaced scrutiny rule and its race-free-only final direction may prohibit consideration governing law permits. Return the preliminary and any final directions for assessment under the common standard, with an explanation of which actual facts remain material. The strongest objection is that the present record's percentage mechanism and individual's burden may already fail even the governing standard, so changed terminology alone cannot justify erasing supported findings or ensuring another evidentiary opportunity.

**Supported affirmance at an appropriately bounded scope:** On the existing record the County may fail to connect the percentage/trend restrictions and individual denial to supported educational benefits or to justify the burden in view of concrete practicable alternatives. Racial allocation for its own sake fails under Adarand too. This route must distinguish demonstrated failure of fit from an assumption that no decree means no permissible purpose. Any race-free-only final remedy requires a basis tied to the actual unconstitutional application, not a universal ban beyond the case.

**Supported reversal of the appellate relief:** If the actual record suffices under the governing standard and the necessary equitable requirements favor the County, the denial of preliminary relief could stand while final merits remain for normal proceedings. The counterargument is that claimed educational value and numerical imbalance do not themselves demonstrate sufficient justification. This handoff supplies no new factual finding that either objective or fit has been proved.

## Verified record and question

The full [Ninth Circuit opinion, 169 F.3d1200](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/169/169.F3d.1200.98-50282.html), including its note, was read. Ohler was convicted of marijuana importation and possession with intent to distribute, sentenced to thirty months and three years' supervision, with a $200 assessment. The Government sought admission of a 1993 methamphetamine-possession conviction under both Rules404(b) and609(a)(1). The District Court rejected character-use admission and initially reserved impeachment; on the first morning of trial it ruled the conviction admissible to impeach if she testified.

Ohler testified after the prosecution rested, denied knowing marijuana was in her van, and described retrieving the van after it had been taken without permission. She disclosed the prior conviction on direct examination; the prosecutor asked on cross whether it was a felony, which she confirmed; on redirect she distinguished personal-use possession from distribution. The circuit held her direct disclosure waived appellate review and did not decide whether Rule609 balancing was erroneous. The granted question concerns timely objection to a definitive adverse ruling followed by defensive disclosure. That question is not the merits of admissibility and does not itself authorize a new trial.

The lower court relied on Williams and other circuit precedent, reasoning that the 1990 removal of Rule609's cross-examination-only wording recognized a previously permitted practice without deciding its effect on appellate waiver. It distinguished the contrary Fifth Circuit practice as grounded in older precedent, not the 1990 amendment. The fact that the Government moved first did not distinguish its controlling Williams rule. These are lower-court grounds, not an answer already established by the Court.

## Term-effective law

Rule609(a)(1), as amended in1990 and quoted in the lower opinion, permits impeachment of the accused by a qualifying felony only if probative value outweighs prejudicial effect to that accused. The rule's treatment of other witnesses and dishonesty crimes remains distinct. Removing the former cross-examination restriction permits direct disclosure; permission to disclose does not expressly answer waiver.

Rule103's applicable source is Pub.L.93-595 §1, January2,1975, 88Stat1930. Control verified the historical amendment sequence and supplied this objective recitation: original103(a) required an affected substantial right and a timely objection or motion to strike with a specific ground unless apparent; subsections(b),(c),(d) concerned the record of ruling, jury hearing and plain error. The definitive-ruling/no-renewal sentence added by the April17,2000 amendment was effective December1,2000. It was not operative for May22. No present-day wording or later committee discussion is imported. The exact full historical scan was not independently recovered here; the verified enactment/history and actual preserved-objection posture suffice for this issue without supplying a new text.

[Luce, 469U.S.38](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/469/38), was read in full including the Brennan concurrence and footnotes. Its holding requires the defendant to testify to preserve a Rule609 impeachment objection. It rests on knowing the actual testimony, use of the conviction and context for prejudice/harmlessness. It does not expressly decide defensive disclosure by a defendant who does testify. Justice Stevens did not participate in Luce; no join is attributed to him. O'Connor joined the Court. Brennan's concurrence, joined by Marshall, limits the ruling to that factual context and reserves broader constitutional/evidentiary applications; it is prior noncontrolling authority, not an current-Justice commitment.

The slice copies the current Olano distinction between forfeiture and intentional waiver, including plain-error conditions, and Old Chief's handling of contextual evidence choices and prejudice. Neither decides this question. The existing timely objection is not converted into simple failure to object; the issue is whether later voluntary introduction relinquishes the preserved claim. The language of waiver and forfeiture must track the chosen ground.

## Supported opposing paths and remedial limits

* Preserve review: a party who timely objects, receives a definitive adverse ruling and then reduces the damage by revealing the expected impeachment does not thereby invite or consent to the ruling. Here the trial record contains her testimony and actual disclosure, unlike Luce. The strongest contrary argument is that her own introduction deprives the Government of its choice whether to use the conviction and changes the course of trial; the Court must decide whether that uncertainty defeats review or belongs in ordinary prejudice/harmlessness analysis.
* Bar review after introduction: a party normally cannot complain of evidence she herself introduced, and the tactical advantage of direct disclosure can carry the loss of an appellate challenge. The strongest contrary argument is that the adverse ruling already made that tactical choice necessary in practical terms, and the rule should not penalize mitigation while rewarding a needlessly damaging sequence. The actual prosecutor's felony question matters to this application but does not prove every unasked trial contingency.
* A narrow rule may confine preservation to timely objection, a definitive ruling and defensive disclosure; independent affirmative use, express consent, abandonment, tentative rulings and a defendant who never testifies remain distinct. A broad waiver rule must explain its relation to Olano and Rule103, rather than assume that every tactical choice is an intentional surrender of review.

If review is preserved, the Ninth Circuit can decide actual Rule609 admissibility and any harmless-error issue in ordinary proceedings. Preservation alone establishes neither evidentiary error nor reversible prejudice and does not automatically require a new trial. If review is barred, affirmance concerns that ground and does not endorse the unreviewed Rule609 merits.

Thus the raw packet's statement that the judgment under review shifted the burden to the State is incorrect. The trustee seeks reversal of the Seventh Circuit; the State defends it.

Chandler Enterprises, of which Stoecker was president, acquired a corporate aircraft through retailer JPA and its financing affiliate PLI. Chandler paid an initial $2.4million, obtained possession and brought the plane to Illinois in June1988, then exercised its option and obtained title in September. The lower court rejected the isolated-sale defense under Illinois's treatment of the retailer's title transfer. It rejected the District Court's view that the Tax Injunction Act prevented deciding the predicate tax question, referring to Bankruptcy Code §505(a)(1). Those determinations remain distinct from the granted federal burden question.

The State issued a corporate tax notice and a personal penalty notice under its responsible-officer law. The latter applies to the officer with control, supervision or responsibility for returns/payment who willfully fails to perform the duty. The assessment is prima facie evidence. Under the state rule as construed by the Seventh Circuit, the notice shifts production and persuasion to the officer. The record contained no evidence about Stoecker's tax responsibilities or willfulness. Naming Pluhar as financial officer may satisfy production, the court said, but did not satisfy persuasion. There was no evidence Stoecker saw or relied on counsel's no-tax-due letter. Do not convert that absence into an affirmative historical finding of knowing evasion or exclusive responsibility.

The court separately rejected the proof-of-claim timeliness objection under the governing pre-1994 §726 provisions and denied a subordination theory based on delay attributable to the unreported/unregistered aircraft. The grant concerns whether federal bankruptcy changes the state-law burden, not an unrestricted reopening of all tax, timeliness or subordination rulings. Preserved genuine alternatives retain their actual footing; none is invented here.

## Entering law and opposing paths

Butner, 440U.S.48, supplies the rule that state-created property interests ordinarily retain their incidents in bankruptcy absent a federal rule requiring otherwise. Grogan, 498U.S.279, distinguishes state-law validity of a claim from the separate federal dischargeability inquiry and its burden. These predivergence authorities are not a categorical instruction that every procedural rule must be state law. Sections502 and505 provide claims/tax adjudication authority; Bankruptcy Rule3001(f)'s prima-facie evidentiary treatment of a properly filed claim must be kept apart from the ultimate persuasion burden. Under 28U.S.C.§2075 bankruptcy rules do not abridge, enlarge or modify substantive rights. Express Code allocations control where they apply.

The slice copies Noland/CFI's current restriction against replacing statutory entitlements and distribution choices with generalized equity, and Greenwich Collieries' distinction between persuasion, production, lawful presumptions and separately allocated defenses. Greenwich's APA proponent allocation is not an automatic bankruptcy rule. The Court's own tax-characterization cases concern the federal definition of priority, not a command to shift the proof of state liability; their exact qualification is retained.

* Retain the state burden: persuasion is an incident of the state tax claim, and neither ordinary bankruptcy administration nor Rule3001(f) changes it. Different underlying claims may carry different burdens without denying the Code's distributional equality. Strongest counterargument: once a proof of claim is rebutted in the federal claims-allowance process, a uniform creditor-persuasion rule can be characterized as procedure rather than an alteration of the claim.
* Apply a bankruptcy persuasion rule: federal claims procedure could place ultimate proof on the claimant after the trustee rebuts prima facie evidence, promoting uniform administration and creditor treatment. Strongest counterargument: it needs an actual Code or lawful rule basis sufficient to displace the state entitlement; generalized equality is not an enacted change, and a production presumption does not determine ultimate persuasion.
* A narrow decision can identify the burden source and dispose of this federal objection while leaving express federal departures, proof-of-claim evidentiary consequences, valid assessment defenses and distinct dischargeability rules intact. It should not create a universal tax presumption, negate willfulness as an element, or place every bankruptcy burden on debtors.

## Castillo v. United States — No. 99-658 — June 5

### Record and reach

The Fifth Circuit's 1999 judgment, 179 F.3d 321, follows its earlier decision at 91 F.3d 699 and a Bailey remand. The jury was asked whether the defendants knowingly used or carried a firearm during and in relation to the predicate crime of violence. The judge found machineguns, silencers and handgrenades and imposed thirty-year firearm terms. The initial circuit decision treated weapon type as a sentencing factor and permitted reimposition upon the requisite finding; the judge did so and the circuit affirmed. The reviewed question is classification of firearm type under the offense-date, pre-1998 version of §924(c)(1). The post-1998 restructuring is a different text.

The control recitation establishes no separate jury finding of the aggravating weapon type. ### Entering law and opposing paths

* Element path: the text identifies distinct, substantially aggravated weapon offenses; weapon character describes the means of committing the crime and carries exceptionally different punishment. The Court could construe the former statute as an aggravated offense and require indictment and jury determination. Strongest counterargument: a graded penalty following a fully stated firearm offense may describe sentencing consequences, and weapon detail can be treated as traditional sentencing information. The answer must come from this statute's text, structure and historical usage, with avoidance and lenity performing only their lawful roles.
* Sentencing-factor path: the opening use/carry prohibition completes the crime and the subsequent weapon language directs the sentencing judge. ## United States v. Hubbell — No. 99-166 — June 5

### Record and reach

The October 1996 subpoena called for eleven broad document categories. On November 19 Hubbell invoked the privilege, then complied under a §6003 order granting statutory use and derivative-use immunity. He produced 13,120 pages and answered completeness questions, identifying attorney-client/work-product exceptions. The contents supplied information leading to the April 1998 ten-count tax, mail-fraud and wire-fraud indictment. These are supported record recitations; they do not establish a privilege for all voluntarily created contents.

The District Court dismissed on immunity and independently on the Independent Counsel's authority. The D.C. Circuit rejected the latter ground, which is outside review; on immunity it vacated and remanded for examination of prior knowledge of existence, authenticity, possession and control. That court did not require prior knowledge of contents. On remand, the prosecutor acknowledged inability to satisfy the circuit's reasonable-particularity rule. A live controversy remains. Other defendants' dismissals with prejudice are not before this Court.

The Government says it need not introduce either the production act or the documents at trial; it does not claim wholly independent evidence. That position differs from the narrower reasonable-particularity concession. The earlier circuit remand is not an unexercised opportunity automatically available after this decision. Any mandate must take the intervening conditional agreement into account.

### Entering law and opposing paths

The Fifth Amendment protects compelled testimonial self-incrimination. Sections 6002–6003 authorize compelled testimony/information with protection against direct and derivative criminal use, subject to their perjury, false-statement and noncompliance exceptions. Kastigar holds use-and-derivative-use immunity coextensive with the privilege and puts the independent-source burden on the Government. Fisher distinguishes unprivileged voluntarily prepared contents from the potentially testimonial concessions made by production, and recognizes a foregone-conclusion setting where those concessions add no material information. Doe, 465 U.S. 605, protects an incriminating testimonial act of producing sole-proprietor records even where the contents are not privileged. Balsys preserves domestic use-immunity protection; its independent foreign-prosecution result narrows no domestic Kastigar rule.

* Protected production and derivative use: selection, location, assembly and authentication of these broadly described, previously unknown records communicate the producer's knowledge. The use of that knowledge as an investigative lead is derivative use even if the physical papers and production testimony are not trial exhibits. Strongest counterargument: voluntarily created contents remain independently unprivileged, and a production rule should not transform statutory use immunity into immunity for all transactions the papers describe.
* Limited act-use protection: prohibit evidentiary use of implicit existence/possession/authenticity admissions but permit the Government to derive leads from the contents. Strongest counterargument: it must explain how the Government acquired the contents without exploiting the compelled testimonial selection that identified and supplied them; describing an ultimate exhibit as unprivileged does not itself establish an independent source.
* A true foregone conclusion would avoid protected testimonial disclosure to the relevant extent, but ordinary expectations that business records exist are not automatically prior knowledge of these responsive documents. If the factual threshold cannot be met under the adopted rule, the present concessions and agreement govern the case's consequence. Do not order a fresh independent-source search by default.

Keep voluntary contents, genuine foregone conclusions, production by third parties, collective-entity custody and lawful independent evidence distinct. No entire commercial-paper privilege, transactional immunity, expanded Independent Counsel ruling or acquittal of other defendants follows.

Stevens and O'Connor joined the Braswell Court's collective-entity rule, which nonetheless excludes personal evidentiary use of the individual production act; it does not decide an immunized personal subpoena by collapsing act into contents. Stevens's Doe, 487 U.S. 201, dissent treats compelled disclosure of the contents of one's mind as testimonial; O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy joined the Court's no-testimony ruling for a hypothetical bank-consent form, while that majority expressly recognizes the mind-disclosure principle. These are relevant distinctions, not current-case votes. Sources: [Braswell](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/487/99), [Doe (1988)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/487/201), [Doe (1984)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/465/605).

## Harris Trust and Savings Bank v. Salomon Smith Barney — No. 99-579 — June 12

### Record and reach

### Entering law and opposing paths

Section 406(a) directs fiduciaries not to cause specified transactions with parties in interest, subject to §408 and applicable exemptions. Section 502(a)(3) expressly identifies authorized plaintiffs and permits injunctions against violations and other appropriate equitable relief to redress violations or enforce ERISA/plan terms. Defendant status and remedy classification are separate questions. A violation of ERISA or plan terms must exist; general trust-law equity cannot substitute for that predicate.

Mertens excludes ordinary compensatory damages from this provision but expressly reserves its antecedent nonfiduciary-action question. It does not forbid every equitable monetary recovery. Peacock requires an independent statutory/plan violation; veil piercing alone supplies none.

* Eligible transferee: where a fiduciary engages in a prohibited, nonexempt transfer, traditional equitable recovery can reach a nonfiduciary recipient with the legally required notice. Section 502(a)(3)'s plaintiff restrictions do not contain a categorical fiduciary-defendant restriction. Strongest counterargument: §406(a)'s duty is imposed on fiduciaries, so imposing recipient liability risks evading Congress's carefully drawn duty and remedy scheme.
* No statutory claim against this defendant: the provision redresses a violation by the person sued and §406(a) imposes no relevant duty on a nonfiduciary counterparty. Strongest counterargument: it must explain why redressing the fiduciary's completed violation cannot include recognized equitable recovery from a knowing transferee when the enforcement text does not identify a closed defendant class; Mertens left that question open rather than deciding it adversely.
* A narrower decision may reject categorical immunity while leaving party-in-interest status, exemption, knowledge, bona-fide-purchaser protection, specific tracing and the precise form/amount of relief to ordinary proceedings. It must not award the purchase price automatically or label every request called restitution equitable. The original payment and current assets/proceeds determine the remedy inquiry. No finding that the investment was worthless at purchase follows.

## 3. Judgment below and competing reasoning

The state majority treated parental care and upbringing as a fundamental liberty, required a compelling justification tailored to the intrusion, and held these statutes deficient because they permitted any person to seek visitation at any time without a harm threshold, a substantial-relationship requirement or prescribed consideration of parental reasons. It recognized that severing an established substantial third-party relationship could cause severe psychological harm, while finding that the statutes did not require such a showing. It rejected the proposition that a judge's view of a better arrangement alone supplies the required justification. The state dissent agreed that the statutes authorized the petitions.
