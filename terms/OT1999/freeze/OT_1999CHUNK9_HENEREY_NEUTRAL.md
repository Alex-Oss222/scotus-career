## Judgment, parties and posture

The Eighth Circuit's December 29, 1999 judgment, 200 F.3d 1128, No. 98-3439, affirmed Judge Charles A. Shaw's summary judgment for the defendants in the Eastern District of Missouri. Chief Judge Wollman wrote, joined by Richard S. Arnold; District Judge Charles R. Wolle, sitting by designation, dissented. Adam Henerey sued through his father under 42 U.S.C. §1983. Named defendants were the City of St. Charles School District, Principal Jerry Cook, and Assistant Principal David Watson, the individuals in both capacities. The appeal was submitted April 21, 1999. The summary-judgment standard requires favorable treatment of the nonmovant's supported evidence and reasonable inferences; the majority and dissent disagree over its application here.

The lower court did not decide that the condom distribution lacked any expressive content. ## Facts and source status

Henerey was a sophomore seeking election as junior class president in March 1997. Candidacy was open to class members subject to meeting the council adviser and signing a contract to obey school rules. A council member told him flyers and posters needed advance approval. The school approved his campaign slogan. The campaign began April 7. After complaints that his posters covered competitors' posters and demeaned others, adviser Mary Stodden told him all materials required administrative approval. The opinion reports these complaints and the warning; it does not establish that the alleged posting misconduct was the actual ground of disqualification.

On April 10, election day, Henerey distributed condoms attached to campaign stickers in the school halls without seeking specific approval for condoms or indicating that the campaign would concern sex. The majority describes about eleven distributed; the dissent describes an offer of about twenty to requesting students. These accounts need not be converted into a single exact quantity. While Stodden was counting votes, a student complained; she informed Cook, who disqualified Henerey for failure to comply with Rule KJ-R. The subsequent count showed he had a majority. Thus do not write that Cook first learned he had won and then removed him in response to the result.

Other candidates distributed candy, gum or buttons without specific advance approval. The school said candy distribution was a longstanding practice with tacit approval. The majority found no record evidence disputing that practice and therefore no evidence of selective content enforcement. The dissent thought a jury could infer that the safe-sex message, rather than the approval process, caused the sanction. No trial resolved this disagreement. The only stated principal's ground identified by the dissent was failure to obtain approval.

## Rule KJ-R and educational function

The official published opinion quotes only relevant portions of the policy, with omissions. Those quoted portions are script-copied into `preparation/OT_1999CHUNK9_HENEREY_RULE_SOURCE.md`; that extract is not represented as the complete policy. The text requires a principal-approved distribution location; approval by the principal or assistant principal the previous day or earlier; more than one school day for material not readily classified or approved; and an approval stamp. It identifies unacceptable hate/hostility/violence material, pornography, obscenity, material unsuitable for school distribution, libel, commercial promotion, unapproved fundraising, and material the principal concludes would materially disrupt classwork, cause substantial disorder, or invade others' rights. The fundraising clause contains superintendent-or-assistant approval as its own exception. All material outside the unacceptable categories is acceptable. Do not flatten these distinct provisions into one general disruption exception or characterize the policy as guaranteeing a decision in exactly twenty-four hours: the lower court used that shorthand, while the quoted text expressly allows longer for difficult material.

The lower court classified the election as a school-sponsored curricular activity in a nonpublic forum. It relied on faculty supervision, a school-defined campaign period, the eligibility/contract requirements, prior approval, and instruction in leadership and democratic participation. It found no policy or practice opening campaign resources to indiscriminate public discourse. It reasoned that observers could attribute approved campaign material to the school. These are the lower court's legal classifications and record-based inferences, not a universal rule that every student campaign message is the school's own speech.

## Grounds below, including the alternative and dissent

The majority's principal ground was ordinary pedagogical enforcement of a known advance-approval requirement. It accepted tacit approval of conventional candy as a reason that different treatment did not establish content selection, rejected the facial prior-restraint and vagueness objections in this secondary-school setting, and found disqualification a reasonable sanction. Its generality analysis invoked the nature of indecency, vulgarity and disruption; it did not hold every unguided restriction on student speech valid.

The majority separately assumed protected expression and disagreement with its content, and still upheld disqualification. It reasoned that condom distribution could be read as encouraging teenage sexual activity and could carry apparent school approval, whereas candy was a conventional modest gratuity. It invoked maturity, decorum, curriculum, avoiding association with controversial sexual topics, and advance notice allowing accommodation of parents' objections. These are an independent appellate rationale and possible legal inferences, not findings that Henerey's actual message urged irresponsible sex, caused disruption, or produced a documented parental confrontation.

Wolle's dissent argued that a jury could find pretext, selective application, insufficient notice that condoms needed approval when other campaign objects did not, and disproportionate disqualification. Approval of the slogan and differential treatment could support the inference that the safe-sex message was the real cause. He rejected controversy over safe sex as enough by itself to suppress discussion in the campaign. He sought reversal of summary rejection so possible damages against one or more defendants could be adjudicated, not automatic election installation or a damages award.

#### Settle v. Dickson County School Board

**Docket or dockets:** Sixth Circuit No. 93-6207; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 25, 1996.

##### What genuine curricular grounds may limit a religious research topic

**Holding:** A teacher may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns, and a student's religious convictions create no exemption from them. But curricular discretion does not make categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal prohibition on academic religious subjects, a legitimate ground merely because it is described as educational judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The teacher-only submission is not presumed publicly attributable to the school or an open forum. Objective inquiry may be distinguished from devotion or personal testimony. Genuine factual or evaluative mistakes can remain pedagogical judgments, while a legally impermissible categorical religious ban does not become valid as an honest academic mistake. The full policy permits objective, respectful, balanced academic religious treatment without promoting tenets or demeaning belief and accommodates student-initiated belief or nonbelief; neither provision guarantees approval.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal curricular viewpoint neutrality, unrestricted topic choice, religion-based exemption, perfect-accuracy duty or independent Free Exercise, Establishment or equal-protection holding.

##### Whether the summary-judgment record establishes lawful cause or the same-decision defense

**Holding:** Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible pedagogical justification when supported evidence permits a material inference that a student's work was rejected for an impermissible religious reason rather than legitimate application of assignment requirements. Under Mt. Healthy, the student must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating role; defendants may then prove by a preponderance that the same adverse decision would have occurred without that conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Failure to secure permission was supported but its independent causal effect was disputed by the next-day no-penalty alternative, mixed reasons and original-choice answer, which also covered Buddha and Muhammed. Faith alone does not prove inability to research. Familiarity does not prove absence of new inquiry. Source requirements must reflect permitted secondary and outside sources, not a new Bible-only or school-library-only premise. Comparator descriptions are relevant without proving identical circumstances.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No pretext, hostility, final causation, liability or automatic defeat of a proved same-decision defense is found. Waters supplies its shared genuine-ground distinction, not an imported employment investigation code. Graduation proves no universal mootness; actual capacities, municipal attribution, immunity and remedies remain open. Conduct-date immunity notice concerns March–April 1991, not later decisions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated and remanded, 9–0, for ordinary proceedings on the free-speech claim. No compulsory approval, reading, grading, credit, removal of the zero, damages, new discovery procedure or trial; another supported lawful disposition remains possible.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier, 484 U.S. 260:** Applies legitimate pedagogical control to this graded research assignment, preserving genuine method and quality decisions and reasonable academic error. Its public-attribution rationale is not automatically transferred to a teacher-only submission.
- **Tinker v. Des Moines Independent Community School District, 393 U.S. 503:** Preserves student expression without creating unrestricted topic choice or displacing genuine assignment requirements.
- **Epperson v. Arkansas, 393 U.S. 97:** Applies the distinction between curricular authority and constitutional immunity, and between objective academic study of religion and governmental religious instruction. No new religion-clause claim is adjudicated.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District, June 7, 1993; Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia, June 29, 1995; and Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue, December 11, 1995:** Preserve the distinction between genuine program boundaries and religious-viewpoint exclusion in their actual settings. They do not classify this assignment as a forum or establish a right to a particular grade.
- **Lee v. Weisman, June 24, 1992, and Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21, February 12, 1996:** Preserve their limits on official worship and coercive religious participation without creating a blanket prohibition on academic religious subjects.
- **Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994:** Applies the genuine-reliance and causation distinction at its shared scope. Its separate investigation approaches and public-employment balance are not adopted as a classroom test.
- **Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle, 429 U.S. 274:** Applies the claimant's protected-conduct and substantial-or-motivating-factor burden and defendants' separate preponderance same-decision defense. A possible lawful reason is not sufficient proof, and partial improper motivation does not automatically establish entitlement to relief.

No precedent is overruled.

#### Boring v. Buncombe County Board of Education

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1835.
**Decided:** 1999-02-17.

##### Assigned instructional production and the asserted personal speech right

**Holding:** A public-school teacher's selection, production, and direction of material as part of the assigned school instructional program constitute institutional curricular expression subject to the school system's lawful control; the artistic discretion entrusted to the teacher does not, on this claim, create a personal First Amendment right against curricular and related employment control. This rule concerns the assigned school production, and does not authorize punishment of separate citizen speech or an independently unconstitutional classification or exclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's complete opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, supplies five direct rationale votes.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier: applies the distinction between personal student expression and school-sponsored educational activity; its student-speech rule is not converted into an instructor's personal control over institutional content.
- Settle v. Dickson County School Board, June 25, 1996: preserves genuine curricular authority and the prohibition on categorical religious exclusion masquerading as pedagogy; distinguishes the student's limited interest within an assignment from the teacher's asserted interest in administering the program.
- Jeffries v. Harleston, November 17, 1997: distinguishes the unaffiliated citizen address expressly outside assigned official work; its protection, actual-cause rule, and limited mandate remain intact.
- Brewster v. Board of Education, August 17, 1998: distinguishes the particular internal attendance-error report and conduct-date immunity question; no general rule withdrawing protection from workplace reports follows.
- Waters v. Churchill, May 31, 1994: preserves its limited majority propositions on genuine lawful grounds and mistaken accounts of protected employee speech; supplies no personal curricular entitlement or universal investigation procedure.
- Mt. Healthy City School District Board of Education v. Doyle: preserves the claimant's protected-conduct and substantial-or-motivating-factor showing and the employer's separate preponderance same-decision defense; no defense is found proved here.
- Pickering v. Board of Education, Connick v. Myers, and Givhan v. Western Line Consolidated School District: retain contextual public-employee speech protection, including private speech; the Court adopts no categorical workplace or private-concern exclusion.
- Epperson v. Arkansas and Keyishian v. Board of Regents: preserve independently applicable constitutional limits on educational authority; the curricular label does not establish immunity from them.

No precedent is overruled.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The teacher's delegated selection and direction of this school instructional production do not supply the personal speech entitlement claimed against curricular and related employment control. That limited institutional-content rule leaves student expression, separate citizen speech, independent constitutional prohibitions, and other sources of employment protection in place. The decision adopts no universal rule for government employees' assigned speech and no constitutional safe harbor based on any conceivable pedagogical reason.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.
**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

### Assigned school productions and teachers’ personal speech

**Governing question:** Whether a teacher has a personal First Amendment right over an assigned school instructional production.

**Current rule:** Selection, production and direction of material as part of an assigned public-school instructional program are institutional curricular expression subject to the school system’s lawful control. Delegated artistic discretion, public controversy and prior approval do not themselves give the teacher a personal constitutional right against curricular and related employment control or make approval an irrevocable constitutional delegation. A protected personal interest must be established before justification and same-decision rules apply.

### Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe — 2000-06-19

[Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe](../records/santa-fe-independent-school-district-v-doe_merits_2000-06-19.md)

#### The school may not delegate its official religious choice to a student majority

**Holding and operative rule:** A public secondary school may not organize an invocation as a representative component of its own football pregame ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that official component will include religious exercise. The challenged board-created election mechanism violates the Establishment Clause under both its unrestricted and nonsectarian/nonproselytizing versions; relief reaches that mechanism while preserving genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access.

**Authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes for the rule, explanation and limited affirmance. The particular government-created representative religious choice is essential to all seven joins; the Court adopts no independent religious-pressure holding.

**Controlling explanation:** Agostini requires identification of the governmental act and its function, using purpose, effect and institutional relationships as evidence within the modified Lemon inquiry. Here the board creates an invocation or message slot within its own ceremony; the student council conducts a majority election under the principal's advice and direction; the electorate selects a single volunteer; and the speaker must serve the board's prescribed purposes. This is an official representative component, not generally available access for independently chosen private speakers. Lee forbids official school organization of worship, and Ingebretsen explains why student initiation or delivery cannot alone make that exercise private. A majority vote cannot privatize the board's delegated religious choice.

The District stresses secular purposes, student authorship, no required prayer, optional attendance and the possibility of silence or a secular message. Those features do not remove the existing governmental mechanism. The Court assumes no official textual preapproval and no delivered October prayer; it does not rest on a finding that every secular purpose is a sham. The ability to decline use of the delegated religious authority does not transform it into neutral access. Pinette and Bronx Household protect actually private expression, where government has not adopted the devotion as its own event component. Tanford likewise distinguishes official organization from private composition, without making its adult setting identical to this school policy.

The backup's religious-content limits retain the same official mechanism and do not cure it. The Court does not direct officials to edit private religion into acceptable language. Nor does it prohibit every election, invited student speaker or religious message. The existing mechanism suffices to affirm; Lee and Ingebretsen's separate protection against substantial practical religious pressure remains intact without requiring legal compulsion or compulsory attendance. No additional pressure finding, universal facial-challenge rule, new forum entitlement, graduation ruling or damages award is necessary.

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing governmental-act/function inquiry; purpose, effects and entanglement remain evidence within that inquiry, not independent surviving prongs.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues in the form Agostini adopted; the Court revives neither an independent purpose test nor an independent endorsement test.
- **Lee v. Weisman:** Its official-organization rule applies to this school-created invocation mechanism. Its independently sufficient substantial-pressure rule remains, without a compulsory-attendance prerequisite; no new pressure holding is made here.
- **Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District:** Its rejection of automatic insulation by student delivery and its protection of lawful private expression apply. Its provision-sensitive interim ruling is not recast as a universal rule invalidating every future religious message.
- **Tanford v. Brand:** Preserved for its established official devotional program and its distinction between independent wording and private access; the Court does not transplant every feature of university commencement to football.
- **Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette:** Its actual generally available private forum is distinguished from this selective representative event component; religious content and government location remain insufficient by themselves to establish attribution.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its independently organized private use remains protected; the school's own representative ceremony presents a different governmental function.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District:** Its protection of religious viewpoints within genuine comparable private access remains intact; no schoolwide private forum is inferred here.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its independently directed private-expression relationship remains intact; it does not authorize government-organized worship.
- **Chabad of Southern Ohio v. City of Cincinnati:** Its contextual attribution inquiry remains; neither a forum label nor location alone supplies the answer.
- **Strout v. Albanese:** Its distinction between genuine private expression and governmental performance or purchase of a religious function is preserved; no football-specific rule is imported from its tuition setting.

The official character of the challenged representative religious-choice mechanism supplies the constitutional defect even without a delivered October prayer or official editing of its words. Government cannot make its own religious choice private by delegating it to a student majority. Agostini's unified governmental-act/function inquiry governs; no independent purpose or endorsement test returns. Genuinely private religious expression retains its protection. The independent substantial-pressure rules of Lee and Ingebretsen continue without requiring legally compulsory attendance; this decision need not decide that additional ground.

#### Good News/Good Sports Club v. School District of Ladue

**Docket or dockets:** Eighth Circuit No. 93-2148; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 28 F.3d 1501.
**Decided:** December 11, 1995.

##### Religious instruction on an admitted moral-development subject

**Holding:** A school opening an after-school period to comparable private moral- and character-development instruction and activities may not exclude an otherwise eligible club because its approach is religious. The rule applies to this actual forum even if nonpublic; genuine subject, activity, scheduling, supervision, safety and access limits remain permissible when applied without viewpoint discrimination.

##### Establishment consequences of these actual private-use arrangements

**Holding:** Equal access on these forum terms does not establish religion: the forum serves a secular purpose, the expression remains private in context, and access creates neither governmental advancement of religion nor excessive entanglement. Private sponsorship must be real; selection, control, adoption, preference or obscured attribution could alter the conclusion, and neutral availability or a disclaimer alone cannot cure official religious activity.

The 11–15-year-old audience, ongoing extracurricular day and available late bus do not on this record convert the private program into school speech; earlier involvement of a former board member establishes no current control.

### Genuine pedagogy and religious perspectives in graded assignments

**Governing question:** A student’s challenge to rejection of a religious research topic within a teacher-assessed curricular assignment.

**Current rule:** Teachers may enforce genuine topic-approval, research, source and academic-method requirements reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns; religious conviction creates no exemption. But categorical exclusion of an otherwise permissible religious perspective, or a mistaken blanket legal ban on academic religious subjects, is not legitimate pedagogy merely because so labeled. Schools retain authority to select knowledge, method or perspective taught and to distinguish objective inquiry from testimony or devotional advocacy; no universal curricular viewpoint-neutrality rule follows. Summary judgment cannot rest on a merely possible academic justification where supported evidence permits a material inference of impermissible religious causation. The student must establish protected conduct and a substantial or motivating role; defendants may prove by a preponderance that the same decision would have occurred absent it.

**Authority by component:**

- Legitimate-pedagogy boundary and actual causal proof: I. Curricular authority must rest on genuine, legitimate pedagogy and II. The affirmative record leaves a material causal dispute, 1996-06-25; Souter’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Settle, 1996-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Ordinary genuine factual or evaluative errors may remain within pedagogical discretion; the rule creates no classroom forum, automatic topic approval, compelled grade, independent investigative duty or conclusive liability from an impermissible factor where the same-decision defense is proved.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Souter, joined by Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer, would vacate the dismissal and remand. The dissent accepts the school's final authority over instruction but recognizes a limited personal expressive interest against adverse action unrelated to genuine legitimate pedagogy. A teacher need not have a right to insist on a chosen play to object that officials punished an allegedly approved expressive contribution for an impermissible nonpedagogical reason. The dissent would extend Hazelwood's legitimate-pedagogical-relation approach to this claim, informed by Settle's distinction between real educational authority and constitutional immunity. It does not assert that either precedent already decided a teacher's entitlement.

Officials could alter content and take related personnel action reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns. They would not have to satisfy strict scrutiny, prove actual disruption in every case, open a forum, or guarantee Boring's preferred production. But approval, compliance with the requested edits, and punishment for unpopular expression are pleaded; insubordination or a merely conceivable legitimate reason cannot replace an actual supported ground. Waters and Jeffries preserve that genuine-ground and causal distinction. Brewster reinforces attention to the actual employee claim and individualized defenses without treating every internal disagreement as protected.

The dissent would permit ordinary adjudication of protection, actual pedagogical grounds, and cause. Boring would have to show protected conduct was a substantial or motivating factor; defendants could prove by a preponderance that they would have made the same decision without it. Liability, municipal attribution, and individual immunity would remain distinct; immunity notice would be judged as of the 1991–1992 conduct. The proposed remand orders no play, reinstatement, damages, discovery, or trial and permits another supported lawful disposition. These four Justices reject the majority's absence-of-personal-interest conclusion while preserving broad final school control.
