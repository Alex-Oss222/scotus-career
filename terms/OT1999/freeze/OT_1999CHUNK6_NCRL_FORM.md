# North Carolina Right to Life — form before assignment

Before counting, the legal form is one signed plenary Opinion of the Court on the common supported ground: actual pre-enforcement controversy; facial defect in the political-committee definition's incidental-issue-speech coverage; absence of a sufficiently definite electoral boundary in the reviewed expenditure coverage; and bounded affirmance preserving the existing relief within the two granted questions. The opinion distinguishes genuine issue discussion from actual candidate financing and preserves distinct disclosure, contribution and coordination rules. It describes Akins and Maine Right to Life as existing law without adopting their disputed reasoning anew or deciding their reserved questions.

All nine Justices support that complete ground. An unnecessary five-vote lead part affirmatively re-adopting the broader Akins/Maine rationales would add legal work the present coverage defect does not require. The five Associates who endorse those rationales retain them; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not acquire an affirmative join through the common judgment. Their separate concurrence will preserve the speaker-supplied electoral-command position and the narrower candidate-cash/conduit agreement. Stone's current approved sufficiently-definite-boundary position supplies no express-words rule and no join in that distinct concurrence.

One lead can carry all common governing propositions, so divided authorship is unnecessary. No administrative reconstruction, guide-specific classification, direct-corporate-gift judgment or newly compelled factual inquiry is added. The Chief supports the tentative judgment and assigns. Eligible authors before considering counts: all nine sitting Justices; each supports the full common rationale just identified. Count and authorship selection follow separately.
