## O'Connor: concrete changed premise and adversarial test

The decisive changed premise is her actual March 4, 1996 ACT/Pacifica position. She joined Parts I–III, including final removal of a mandatory 6 a.m.–10 p.m. exclusion of protected adult broadcasting. That was an affirmative Supreme Court adjudication in this timeline, not a historical merits vote to be presumed. She accepted substantial child access, supervisory difficulty, rapid tuning and meaningful nighttime availability, yet joined the conclusion that those features did not adequately justify the broad adult exclusion. The rule preserved substantial consideration of legislative predictions, rejected a scientific-certainty requirement and did not rest on operational future devices. It therefore supplies a substantive prior choice about the same means/necessity problem, not a generic assertion that O'Connor favors free speech.

Her June 28, 1996 Denver joins sharpen the relevant distinction. She retained voluntary editorial permissions but joined invalidation of the integral compulsory scheme burdening willing speakers and adult recipients. Private editorial discretion did not exempt Congress's content-selected command from review. Her June 26, 1997 Reno separate position preserved only knowing transmissions to exclusively minor recipients; it protected mixed adult audiences. Her March 31, 1997 Turner dissent reinforces independent scrutiny of the actual legal burden and alternatives even under intermediate review. These positions support the application but are not all newly changed historical premises: ACT's actual adjudication and her full join provide the central difference. Erie supplies no automatic inference because she accepted a differently justified conduct restriction there.

The strongest historical objection remains substantial: §505 is narrower than the ACT broadcast restriction, targets leakage from primarily adult channels, permits complete blocking instead of mandatory time channeling, and protects children whose parents miss notices, do not act or encounter installation problems. Its purpose is not merely to replace informed parental preference. A parent-activated alternative cannot simply be assumed equivalent for every household. Her prior opinions do not logically compel affirmance in the face of those differences.

Her supported present resolution gives those differences their actual weight but concludes they do not establish necessity here. The operator's formal blocking option does not restore daytime access to willing adults where the practical choice is time channeling. The supported household alternative is technically concrete and includes adequate recurring notice; the developed record does not establish that its comparative deficit warrants the broader adult burden. She requires neither protection of every determined minor nor a completed perfect-notice experiment. She refuses an inference from unproved notice or low distribution that the adequately publicized alternative has already failed.

**Historical departure:** O'Connor affirms rather than joining historical reversal because her actual ACT/Pacifica full join already rejects an inadequately justified sixteen-hour adult exclusion despite child-access and supervision concerns; her Denver compulsory-versus-voluntary distinction and Reno mixed-adult-audience protection support applying that prior choice to §505's demonstrated time-channeling burden. This is a concrete change in prior adjudicative law and her own published reasoning. Correcting distribution statistics, describing the same record more accurately, changing authors or seeking a particular vote count supplies no independent departure premise.

She joins only the record-bound comparative-necessity holding. She would refuse categorical invalidation of age screens or blocking, a premise that adequate notice already occurred, an assertion that only parental wishes can ever matter, or a universal local-study requirement. No new intermediate hours, judicial technical program or minor-only branch is supplied.

## Scalia's historical additional ground and present scope

Historically Scalia also proposed an independent Ginzburg/pandering theory, even assuming the programs individually received protection, and answered the objection that the Government had not litigated obscenity. No other Justice joined that separate theory. His present commitment adopts only the historically sufficient principal-dissent ground of compelling interest and adequate tailoring. Absence of party advocacy alone would not explain withholding the additional historical theory, because his historical writing expressly rejected that objection.

The relevant in-world difference is his actual published course treating nonobscene adult expression as protected while scrutinizing the government's chosen means: his ACT dissent, his Denver strict-scrutiny treatment of compelled willing transmission, and especially his current-term Erie Part II join against incidental treatment of communicatively selected adult entertainment. Erie preserves genuinely general conduct regulation; it does not withdraw protection merely because an adult business sells the expression. These positions support the independently frozen selection of demanding scrutiny as a sufficient ground here. They do not establish repudiation or overruling of Ginzburg, foreclose its distinct pandering theory, or announce that advertising is never relevant in a properly presented obscenity case. The unadopted additional ground is not an affirmative merits rejection of that theory.

**Historical departure:** Scalia retains historical reversal and its strict-scrutiny sufficient ground but does not adopt his historical independent pandering theory. His actual ACT/Denver protected-indecency analysis and Erie content-selection join supply the altered prior reasoning supporting this narrower present ground. The reconciliation does not invent an obscenity finding, marketing record or new categorical rule. Any proposal to add that independent theory requires a new explicit Justice-specific commitment and its source basis, not inference from his judgment vote.

## Statutory, evidentiary and remedial boundaries

Section 505, codified at 47 U.S.C. §561, selects sexually explicit adult or other indecent programming on channels primarily dedicated to sexually oriented programming. It requires complete audio-and-video scrambling/blocking against nonsubscriber receipt; until compliance the FCC-selected restricted period applies, leaving 10 p.m.–6 a.m. permitted. Scrambling means rearrangement so the programming cannot be viewed or heard understandably. That definition creates no additional FCC compliance proceeding. The Act's facial-review provision numbered §561 is distinct from this codified section.

Section 504, codified at §560, requires complete audio-and-video blocking upon a cable subscriber's request, without charge to that subscriber. Its channel scope is not independently limited to indecency or sexually oriented channels. Zero subscriber price is not zero equipment/installation cost to the operator. Its request duty and definition remain distinct from §505's default/time rule; compliance with one does not automatically discharge the other. Section 504 took effect February 8, 1996; §505's amendment took effect March 9, 1996. No lower-opinion shorthand changes those dates.

The actual proposed alternative is §504 with adequate notice, not merely statutory text or unassisted parental supervision. Notice identifies the channels, intelligible audio/video bleed, possible child exposure without parental knowledge, free blocking and the telephone request method. Regular notice at reasonable intervals and special mailed notice after a previously blocked channel changes are distinct features. Billing inserts, preview channels and advertisements elsewhere are examples, not cumulative obligatory media. No exact interval, installation deadline, automatic replacement/repair protocol or universal notice receipt is found.

Nonsubscriber reception occurs upon tuning to a scrambled channel; a child may tune without parental permission. The record does not establish arrival regardless of channel selection. The survey's below-0.5-percent figure measures device distributions, with 79 operators surveyed and 62 answering that question, not national requests, fulfilled requests or informed parental decisions. Notice adequacy was unresolved. The 69-percent text and 68-percent footnote remain bounded, inconsistent survey figures rather than a synthesized national rate. Distinct 30–50-percent viewing estimates and operator-specific buy figures retain their own periods and populations.

Potential exposure estimates do not measure actual exposure. Anecdotes show incidents but no population prevalence. The lower court nevertheless found sufficient risk to susceptible minors; evidentiary weaknesses do not erase that conclusion. Assertions of weeks-long fulfillment, device failure and channel changes retain their evidentiary status and establish no universal rate. Neither side requires perfect protection or denies the possibility of some unprotected children under either arrangement.

Analog scrambling, traps, mapped converters and digital systems have different capabilities. A device price is not installed cost: the source's $37 amount is per mechanism plus installation, while the $12–$15 trap figure is separate. Mailing an outside-installed trap does not eliminate labor. The 3%, 6% and 56% calculations are scenarios under stated assumptions, not predictions of post-notice uptake. Competing revenue forecasts have different horizons and uncertain conversion assumptions. No perfect technology, cost-free operator solution, inevitable cancellation or future conversion date is found. The six conclude comparative inadequacy is unproved; Scalia and Breyer find the supported default/activation difference sufficient under the same ultimate burden.

## Relief and circulation test

The six affirmance supporters preserve the declaration and injunction against §505 at the adjudicated scope. Their decision does not abolish §504, immunize obscenity, decide future technical arrangements, award damages or resolve the separate equal-protection and vagueness grounds. No new record or proof-repair proceeding is ordered.

The lower opinion states that it will require Playboy to secure notice through its contractual arrangements; it also states that the court lacks jurisdiction to command nonparty operators directly. That announced direction remains only at its recorded lower-court scope and procedural status. The supplied account does not independently establish a later formal implementing order, completed contract changes or delivered notices. Retention concerns unreviewed collateral lower relief, not adoption of a new controlling notice-power holding. It neither converts the announcement into §504's text nor creates a Supreme Court command to design or implement notices. The historical majority expressly criticized judicial implementation of the alternative while formally affirming the injunction; that criticism is a comparator position, not a newly created in-world event or remedial finding. The present sufficient constitutional ground is failure to justify §505 and does not depend on inventing notice authority. No independent challenge to the contractual direction or its formal implementation is resolved. A new Court order or broader endorsement of judicial design would exceed these commitments and require express clarification with a lawful basis. The source's warning that inadequate notice would undermine the alternative establishes no automatic restoration of §505 or new enforcement procedure.

Scalia and Breyer would reverse the First Amendment invalidation and enforcement injunction and remand for lawful disposition of properly preserved grounds not reached below. They decide those grounds neither way and prescribe no interim relief. Section 505 would operate on its enacted and FCC-specified terms subject to unresolved lawful proceedings, not an order that any particular operator buy equipment or a new compliance finding. Section 504's separate request duty remains. The lower constitutional alternative cannot become a statutory notice command through reversal.

There is no supported intermediate judgment choosing new hours, dividing analog and digital systems into a rewritten constitutional statute, adding mandatory devices or severing an invented adult/minor branch. Accurate common background and the demanding review framework can receive all eight joins. Only the six affirmance supporters join the sufficient failure-of-comparative-justification ground; only Scalia and Breyer join the opposite sufficiency conclusion. Separate-writing functions preserve the stated boundaries; authors and actual joins await circulation.

Any proposed ground requiring guaranteed notice, measured post-notice requests, universal device performance, a new marketing or obscenity finding, specified conversion dates, free operator installation or jurisdiction over all operators must return for the missing neutral source and explicit independent commitment. Those are not predicates of this reconciliation. At the bounded scopes above, no source blocker remains.
