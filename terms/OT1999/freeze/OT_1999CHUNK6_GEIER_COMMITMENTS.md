## Review, components and common boundaries

The writ reviews the D.C. Circuit's affirmance of dismissal of the District of Columbia tort claim alleging that the 1987 Accord should have included a driver's airbag despite compliance with FMVSS 208. No threshold impediment, nonparticipation or independent basis to dispose of the writ is supplied. The question concerns preemption, not a trial determination of defect, causation or damages.

Separate components are (A) express displacement under former 15 U.S.C. §1392(d), read with §1397(k), and (B) independent actual-conflict displacement of the actual no-airbag duty. All eight reject express preemption as an independently sufficient basis for dismissing this saved common-law action. That conclusion does not declare that damages duties can never be safety standards or requirements: Cipollone and Medtronic establish the need for a provision-specific inquiry. Here §1397(k)'s common-law compliance saving language must remain effective alongside §1392(d). The latter retains its same-aspect/in-effect-standard conditions, identical-standard permission and higher government-procurement requirement permission. No separate state procurement or identical-rule dispute is decided.

Myrick forecloses treating an express clause as automatically excluding every independent actual-conflict inquiry. It also forecloses treating federal compliance, regulatory overlap or a regulatory gap as sufficient proof of conflict. An operative regulation and supported substantive federal objective are present here; their consequence for this duty remains disputed. Neither side may treat Gade's four-Justice physical-impossibility formulation as controlling law or turn Myrick into an automatic ceiling for every federally permitted design.

The 1986 reproduction's three restraint options, alternative production denominators, differentiated credits and limited carry-forward rules matter as components of an operative phased program. They establish no Honda-specific credit usage, diligence, production calculation or belt-law-trigger determination. No Justice requires a fixed numerical mix, a minimum number of airbag-free cars or a federal command that this particular Accord omit an airbag. The agency's adopted flexibility, innovation, acceptance and phase-in reasons must be read with its safety objective, recognition of airbag benefits, encouragement of nonbelt systems and express reference to potential deficient-system liability. Its later litigation preference cannot create a purpose absent from the contemporaneous rule.

## Two supported paths and their precise disagreement

**Conflict path — O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer.** The supported contemporaneous purposes give the operative options and phase-in substantive significance. The saving clause does not remove every actual-conflict defense. At the actual pleaded scope, the general airbag duty would withdraw choices deliberately retained while technologies developed and the transition proceeded. The rule therefore bars that duty even though physical compliance with both is possible and no numerical mix is mandated. This ground requires both the identified program and the categorical duty; regulatory compliance or minimum-standard language alone would not suffice.

**Claim-preserving path — Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.** The same regulatory material proves a purposeful performance standard, but not protection of its compliance choices from the common-law liability expressly saved. The safety, airbag-benefit and liability passages qualify the inferred exclusivity. No conflicting installation command, protected fixed mix or sufficiently demonstrated exclusive choice defeats this claim. Their common ground is this statute and record, not a universal ban on obstacle preemption; Souter and Thomas's earlier broader reservations do not become the shared holding.

The disagreement concerns the legally protected scope of the demonstrated objective, not whether the rule existed, whether it had meaningful purposes, whether damages influence conduct, or whether all common-law claims must survive. The two groups may share the rejection of express preemption without agreeing on the judgment. Four Associates support affirmance and four support reversal; this is not a Court judgment or an instruction concerning Stone.

## Remedy, circulation limits and source sufficiency

Affirmance supporters sustain only the dismissal of the presented no-airbag duty on the stated conflict ground. Reversal supporters remove that preemption barrier and return the actual claim to ordinary state-law adjudication. Neither path determines defect, causation, injury prevention, damages or regulatory noncompliance; neither orders invalidation of FMVSS 208 or new agency proceedings. A hypothetical manufacturing flaw, vehicle-specific malfunction or omitted alternate pleading is not added to the case.

No supported basis requires another appellate evidentiary program before deciding this legal preemption question. If later circulation proposes a ground depending on Honda's production, used credits, contract attribution, diligence, a regulatory test result or occurrence of the belt-law determination, it must return for the missing exact source rather than infer the fact. These matters are not predicates of the commitments recorded here.

All eight may join an accurate account of the federal rule, original statutory text, procedural posture and retained source limits. Each current judgment and substantive join must retain the Justice-specific limits above. Actual-conflict agreement may not be converted into a generic presumption favoring agencies, and claim-preserving agreement may not be converted into an absolute physical-impossibility rule. No further substantive source blocker is identified for these bounded commitments.
