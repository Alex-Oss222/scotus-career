## Scope and component positions

All eight Associates participate. These are eight independent commitments, not a completed Court judgment. No author is selected and no Stone vote is supplied.

1. **Threshold:** Each Associate reaches the two granted questions. NCRL's intended continuing speech, actual cessation, the penal statutory language and the Board's actual response establish a concrete pre-enforcement controversy. This is not Akins's disputed claim to third-party information. The State's litigation assurance supplies neither a binding limiting construction nor a reason to require the proposed certification to the Board. The supported record permits refusal of abstention without a general rule against abstention in every speech case.
2. **Political-committee definition:** Each Associate would affirm the facial invalidation of section 163-278.6(14). Its combined primary-or-incidental-purpose and election-influence coverage subjects genuine independent issue discussion to the full committee regime without a determinate electoral-financing trigger. This substantial defect appears in the definition itself and in the described administration; it does not depend on guessing the second guide's exact words. The sufficient ground is that coverage defect, not a universal organizational exemption whenever electioneering is not a group's major purpose.
3. **Expenditure coverage within the second granted question:** Each Associate would sustain relief against treating genuine independent issue advocacy as a prohibited expenditure merely because it seeks to influence an election. Section 163-278.6(16)'s general political-purpose language, as used by sections 163-269 and 163-278.19, does not supply an adequate boundary on this record. The first guide's accidentally enclosed endorsement sheet cannot be attributed to the second guide or to every NCRL activity. No Associate finds the exact second guide lawful or unlawful under an unproduced text.
4. **Judgment and remedy:** Affirm the Fourth Circuit's judgment insofar as it invalidates the committee definition and protects independent issue advocacy from the challenged expenditure coverage. The formal judgment is confined to the two granted questions. Leave the separate corporate direct-contribution, session-contribution/solicitation and donor-identification portions of the lower judgment undisturbed, without adjudicating their merits anew. Preserve the existing relief within the reviewed scope; do not replace the statute, compel new rulemaking, order registration or reporting, impose liability, or direct a new factual study. No construction remand is necessary to decide the supported legal defect. This disposition does not independently approve the breadth of an ungranted corporate-direct-gift ruling or announce facial invalidity of every corporate regulation.

## Justice-specific commitments

### Stevens

**Commitment:** Reach the merits and affirm at components 2–4. Join the common coverage-defect rationale. Reject an organization-wide major-purpose immunity for actual covered candidate financing. Preserve Maine Right to Life's limited contextual express-advocacy construction and decline to adopt an exclusive electoral vocabulary.

**Sufficient ground and authorities:** His Maine Right to Life Court opinion (September 14, 1998, Parts II–III) permits only an unmistakable electoral direction under cumulative restrictions, leaving constitutional vagueness open. His Akins Part III join (June 25, 1997) requires actual covered financing rather than an organizational label. His Clifton join (September 14, 1998) protects ordinary candidate inquiry at its statutory and review-specific scope. Those positions together distinguish ascertainable financing from a law extending full committee duties to independent policy discussion merely because it might influence an election.

**Two paths:** A narrow construction preserving actual financing is a lawful objective and preferable to a blanket exemption; its strongest case is Buckley's avoidance construction. Here the statute separately reaches incidental purpose and election influence, and no binding construction supplies the missing restrictions. An affirmance based on that defect survives the State's valid-application objection because it preserves the legitimacy of actual candidate-financing regulation while addressing the substantial issue-speech sweep. A fresh merits remand would have no identified unresolved legal task necessary to this result.

**Join limits:** No categorical constitutional approval of every contextual test, no invalidation of tailored disclosure by analogy to an individual leaflet, and no new direct-gift exemption. Day's nonprofit application supports keeping distinct corporate-financing predicates separate; it is not needed as a new independent holding here.

### O'Connor

**Commitment:** Same disposition; full agreement with the coverage-defect rationale and rejection of categorical organizational immunity. Join the statement that Maine Right to Life permits limited context at its existing statutory scope without deciding its reserved constitutional question.

**Sufficient ground and authorities:** Her Akins Part III join requires transactions, actual candidate connection and applicable exclusions; her Maine Right to Life merits join accepts tightly bounded context. Her Russell Court opinion (April 28, 1999) and Shrink Court opinion (January 24, 2000) require the government to establish a fitted regulatory predicate, while preserving the contribution/expenditure distinction. Those contribution holdings cannot justify replacing a speech-coverage boundary with mere electoral influence.

**Two paths:** Sustain a properly confined organizational reporting duty for genuinely covered financing, versus invalidate the present definition's indiscriminate reach. The strongest objection to invalidation is the importance and lower burden of disclosure compared with a spending ban. That objection does not answer the full continuing committee burdens and criminal sanctions triggered by incidental issue advocacy. The narrower construction alternative lacks the authoritative and textual predicates that supported the federal construction in Maine Right to Life.

**Join limits:** No rule making all reporting the equivalent of a prohibition, no permanent safe harbor for every predominantly issue-oriented entity, no automatic invalidation of valid ordinary reports under other authority, and no relaxation of genuine candidate-financing requirements.

### Scalia

**Commitment:** Reach the merits and affirm at components 2–4. Join the present statute's coverage-defect ground. Maintain the express-terms limitation stated in the Maine Right to Life dissent; do not join an affirmative endorsement of its majority's contextual construction.

**Sufficient ground and authorities:** His Maine Right to Life dissent join (September 14, 1998) insists that the speaker supply the electoral command. Ordinary context may explain explicit words or identify the candidate; external circumstances may not supply an absent electoral direction. His Clifton join protects genuine independent candidate inquiry. His Day joins (May 15, 1995) preserve actual nonprofit independent expression and materially distinct contribution channels. His Akins dissent was jurisdictional and did not decide its major-purpose issue; it supplies no merits commitment here.

**Two paths:** A Buckley narrowing construction could preserve a text fairly limited to express electoral advocacy. Against that path, the present definition deliberately includes incidental election influence and the State has not supplied a binding confinement; deleting those features is legislation. Facial relief is justified by the resulting broad reach into issue advocacy rather than by a demand for one canonical slogan. A narrower guide-specific disposition would avoid some breadth, but its exact text is absent and it would leave the definition's demonstrated uncertainty intact.

**Join limits:** He can join a proposition that no actual candidate cash transfer or genuine conduit gains immunity merely from the organization's issue purpose, without endorsing Akins's broader disputed treatment of an association's own expression. He can join an accurate description of Maine Right to Life as controlling existing law, but not its disputed affirmative merits reasoning. No broad anonymous-organizational-disclosure theory is inferred from McIntyre, in which he dissented.

### Kennedy

**Commitment:** Reach the merits and affirm at components 2–4. Join the common defect and the rejection of immunity for actual candidate cash or genuine conduits. Withhold assent to a broader rule treating an association's own expression as regulable simply because there has been cooperation; maintain his express-terms boundary.

**Sufficient ground and authorities:** His Akins concurrence in part and dissent in part (June 25, 1997) distinguishes candidate cash and conduits from the association's expression. His Maine Right to Life separate opinion (September 14, 1998) requires an electoral command supplied by the communication while allowing ordinary context to explain it. His Clifton join protects actual information gathering. His Shrink dissent (January 24, 2000) preserves fitted contribution regulation without accepting a loose analogy to an actual expressive burden.

**Two paths:** Preserve reporting on proven candidate transfers, versus protect NCRL from full committee obligations based on the mere tendency of issue speech to affect voters. Both principles can coexist, and the latter resolves this case. The State's strongest argument is that disguised electoral messages defeat a literal words list. Kennedy does not demand such a list, but requires explicit electoral direction; external events cannot substitute for it. Recasting this statutory definition as a tailored rule would not answer its independent coverage of issue advocacy.

**Join limits:** Judgment agreement does not supply a join in the broader Akins subpart or Maine contextual rationale. He may join a shared opinion limited to the present substantial defect and separately explain those boundaries. No generally applicable constitutional ruling on every coordination or donor rule is necessary.

### Souter

**Commitment:** Reach and affirm at components 2–4; join the defect rationale, the rejection of an organization-wide exemption for otherwise genuinely covered candidate financing, and the preservation of Maine Right to Life's cumulative construction.

**Sufficient ground and authorities:** His Akins Court opinion (June 25, 1997, Parts III–IV) makes actual transactions and separate exclusions central. His Clifton Court opinion (September 14, 1998) refuses to turn candidate inquiry into a campaign service by assumption. His Maine Right to Life merits join permits limited context only where independent adjudication establishes the cumulative requirements. None supplies a license to criminalize speech by its incidental influence.

**Two paths:** A genuine-financing inquiry might defeat NCRL's broadest organizational claim, but no supported NCRL candidate transfer or coordination finding establishes that path here. The contrary concern is that categorical invalidation could disable ordinary campaign reporting. The judgment answers only the supplied definition and leaves distinct lawful candidate-financing predicates intact. The current statutory defect is sufficient; a remand solely to investigate the unproduced guide would confuse an individual application with the facial coverage claim.

**Join limits:** No inference that NCRLPAC's conduct is NCRL's, no importation of federal thresholds or exclusions into North Carolina law, and no constitutional endorsement of a future contextual rule. Preserve the separately reserved vagueness issue in Maine Right to Life.

### Thomas

**Commitment:** Reach and affirm at components 2–4. Join the common statutory coverage defect; adhere to the express-terms approach in the Maine Right to Life dissent. Do not use his Akins standing dissent to decide a merits issue it expressly left undecided.

**Sufficient ground and authorities:** His Maine Right to Life dissent join (September 14, 1998) protects speakers against an electoral direction supplied by regulators. His Clifton join protects independent inquiry; his Day joins preserve meaningful association and fact-dependent nonprofit independent expression. His McIntyre judgment concurrence (April 19, 1995) protects anonymous political pamphlets on its distinct ground but does not itself decide organizational finance disclosure.

**Two paths:** A clear express-advocacy construction could preserve a lawful electoral-financing rule; the alternative is facial relief against this definition's substantial issue-speech reach. The strongest objection to relief is that some covered committees actually finance candidates. Those lawful subjects do not eliminate the express incidental-influence sweep and penal uncertainty for independent speakers. A legislature could define a proper financing subject; the Court need not supply a replacement statute.

**Join limits:** No exclusive list of permitted slogans, no automatic immunity for candidate cash or genuine conduits, no claim that all disclosure is forbidden, and no join in constitutional approval of a contextual theory that allows external events to generate the absent electoral message.

### Ginsburg

**Commitment:** Reach and affirm at components 2–4; join the present-defect rationale, the rejection of categorical major-purpose immunity and the bounded Maine Right to Life proposition.

**Sufficient ground and authorities:** Her Akins Parts III–IV joins (June 25, 1997) preserve real financing coverage and actual exclusions. Her Maine Right to Life merits join (September 14, 1998) supports restrictive context without constitutional prejudgment. Her limited Clifton join preserves the declaration in that private-petitioner review without disabling specifically authorized safeguards. Her McIntyre join expressly leaves organizational reporting distinct.

**Two paths:** Preserve candidate-financing disclosure with adequately defined coverage, versus protect issue discussion from the present comprehensive duties. The strongest objection to invalidation is the public's legitimate interest in identifying electoral financing. That interest does not establish which transactions the vague definition reaches, and the actual independent expression at issue cannot be converted into a candidate service from subject matter alone. The facial defect is settled without inventing either a guide-specific finding or an exemption for all nonprofit activity.

**Join limits:** Preserve specifically fitted contact or reporting safeguards where actual law and facts support them; reject a general constitutional prohibition on such safeguards. No new ruling concerning the ungranted session and donor components.

### Breyer

**Commitment:** Reach and affirm at components 2–4; join the common defect, the transaction-based rejection of a major-purpose immunity, and the existing cumulative contextual construction with its reserved questions.

**Sufficient ground and authorities:** His Akins merits join (June 25, 1997), Maine Right to Life merits join and Clifton join (September 14, 1998) require attention to the actual regulatory subject and record. His Russell and Shrink joins (April 28, 1999; January 24, 2000) recognize practical campaign-finance needs while requiring a supported fit and meaningful association. That position supports tailored reporting but not full committee burdens founded only on incidental influence.

**Two paths:** A modest, clearly triggered electoral disclosure duty may advance legitimate interests; the present definition instead exposes ordinary issue discussion to comprehensive duties and penalties. The strongest counterargument is that a flexible context inquiry better prevents evasion. The Maine cumulative restrictions answer that concern without abandoning an ascertainable electoral direction. North Carolina's general influence formula contains no comparable confinement, and its actual official treatment reinforces the defect. An open-ended administrative remand would supply no defined necessary task.

**Join limits:** Do not describe every administrative burden as suppression, convert the existing federal construction into a state statute, or approve every contextual rule constitutionally. Reserve real campaign services and different, properly supported disclosure applications.
