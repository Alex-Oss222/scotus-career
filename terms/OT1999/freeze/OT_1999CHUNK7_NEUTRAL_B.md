## Castillo v. United States — No. 99-658 — June 5

### Record and reach

The Fifth Circuit's 1999 judgment, 179 F.3d 321, follows its earlier decision at 91 F.3d 699 and a Bailey remand. The jury was asked whether the defendants knowingly used or carried a firearm during and in relation to the predicate crime of violence. The judge found machineguns, silencers and handgrenades and imposed thirty-year firearm terms. The initial circuit decision treated weapon type as a sentencing factor and permitted reimposition upon the requisite finding; the judge did so and the circuit affirmed. The reviewed question is classification of firearm type under the offense-date, pre-1998 version of §924(c)(1). The post-1998 restructuring is a different text.

The control recitation establishes no separate jury finding of the aggravating weapon type. It does not supply the indictment's exact words, every defendant's instructions or preserved harmless-error argument. Do not find an indictment omission merely from the absence of a jury finding. Classification and the invalidity of relying solely on the judge may be addressed without deciding every question concerning further proceedings. Any definitive count-specific retrial, acquittal or sentence-cap direction needs the actual charge, verdict and applicable error rules; an ordinary remand can preserve those matters without fabricating their resolution.

### Entering law and opposing paths

The preamendment provision connects using or carrying a firearm during and in relation to a federal crime of violence or drug-trafficking crime with a five-year consecutive term; the same statutory sentence specifies ten years for designated short-barreled firearms and thirty years for a machinegun, destructive device or equipped silencer/muffler. The enacted classification must be assessed in its offense-date text, not from its later formatting. The quoted statutory passage should be copied from the source record if reproduced in a Record.

Gaudin supplies jury proof beyond a reasonable doubt once a fact is an offense element. Simulated Almendarez-Torres holds the specified aggravated conviction an element of the higher unlawful-reentry offense; simulated Jones, March 24, 1999, applies statutory aggravated-element and charging principles to carjacking injury/death. Neither announces a universal constitutional rule for all maximum-increasing facts. McMillan's unchanged-maximum mandatory-minimum holding survives; Witte/Watts retain ordinary sentencing within the conviction's authorization. Those distinctions must remain separate.

* Element path: the text identifies distinct, substantially aggravated weapon offenses; weapon character describes the means of committing the crime and carries exceptionally different punishment. The Court could construe the former statute as an aggravated offense and require indictment and jury determination. Strongest counterargument: a graded penalty following a fully stated firearm offense may describe sentencing consequences, and weapon detail can be treated as traditional sentencing information. The answer must come from this statute's text, structure and historical usage, with avoidance and lenity performing only their lawful roles.
* Sentencing-factor path: the opening use/carry prohibition completes the crime and the subsequent weapon language directs the sentencing judge. Strongest counterargument: neither the simulated recidivism case nor Jones supplies a historical sentencing-factor exception; their aggravated-element treatment makes reliance on a generic legislative label or long sentence alone inadequate. McMillan does not resolve classification of these differentiated statutory punishments.
* A narrower statutory disposition can reserve the general constitutional maximum-fact question, the 1998 version, judicial selection within a verdict-authorized range, and any unresolved remedy classification. No automatic retrial or fixed aggregate sentence follows solely from choosing element status.

### Justice-specific source anchors, without vote assignments

Scalia authored simulated Gaudin and the controlling Almendarez-Torres/Jones constructions. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg joined both aggravated-element constructions; Thomas joined Jones; all Associates joined Gaudin. Those are actual positions and their exact scope is copied in the slice. O'Connor's predivergence McMillan opinion supplies the distinction between a minimum within an unchanged maximum and an element; it is not a holding on this former federal weapon statute. Kennedy, O'Connor and Breyer must apply the binding simulated constructions despite not joining Jones's controlling coalition; their absence from that coalition does not establish a position on the weapon statute. An asserted Justice-specific broader constitutional departure would require the relevant prior writing, rather than an inference from membership or a prepared later case.

**Readiness:** Ready for the granted statutory classification and bounded ordinary-remand choices. Exact indictment and error/remedy predicates remain necessary only if modeling proposes a more specific remedial command.

## Sims v. Apfel — No. 98-9537 — June 5

### Record and reach

Sims sought Title II and XVI benefits, obtained an adverse ALJ determination, and through counsel requested Appeals Council review by letter. Review was denied. The District Court rejected three challenges. The Fifth Circuit decided the selective-use-of-record issue adversely on its merits but treated omission from the Council request of the vocational-hypothetical and consultative-examination issues as a jurisdictional bar. Council review was requested and the agency decision is final. No ALJ-stage issue-exhaustion question is presented. Review here does not establish disability or the merits of either unreviewed issue.

The applicable 1999 regulations permit Form HA-520 or another writing. The form supplies three lines and estimates ten minutes to read instructions, gather facts and complete it. The ALJ notice says the Council examines the entire decision, including favorable portions, and may undertake review itself. A brief is permitted, not mandatory. The described administrative process is informal and nonadversarial; review is plenary unless limited. These are existing regulatory and notice features, not a newly fashioned judicial notice requirement. The absence of an express statutory or regulatory issue-exhaustion requirement is distinct from the existing requirement to complete the review route.

### Entering law and opposing paths

Section 405(g)'s final-decision review mechanism, §405(a)'s regulatory authority and the actual review rules govern. Darby's APA superior-appeal rule does not itself decide a different issue-exhaustion question under Social Security's separate review system. McCarthy supplies the need to respect legislative commands and to assess actual institutional interests where judicial discretion remains. Its former BOP procedure and later PLRA transition must not be imported here. The current-term Illinois Council ruling concerns channeling and meaningful review, not a command that a Council request identify every legal issue; the slice includes its controlling and separate positions.

* No judicial forfeiture at this Council stage: the enacted and regulatory scheme gives the agency an obligation and opportunity to review the whole claim, while permissive briefing and the notice/form reasonably invite a short request. A claimant who follows that route should not lose judicial review by omitting particular objections. Strongest counterargument: the agency needs a fair first opportunity to correct the particular error, especially where counsel can readily identify it; nonadversarial proceedings still depend on claimants supplying relevant information.
* Judicial issue exhaustion: preserve the ordinary administrative objection rule so that the Council can correct the ALJ's alleged errors before a court intervenes. Strongest counterargument: this analogy to adversarial litigation must be justified against the agency's actual allocation of responsibility and lack of an express requirement; a jurisdictional label cannot substitute for a statutory basis.
* A narrower no-forfeiture decision could rest on the existing notice and optional presentation rules without deciding every nonadversarial agency system. A broader stage-specific decision could rest on the Council's responsibility for the whole claim. They are distinct rationales, not automatically a single controlling rule. A contrary disposition must address whether the rule is jurisdictional or a prudential forfeiture rule and any legally supported exception.

The decision can leave open ALJ-stage preservation, a claimant who never seeks Council review, intentional waiver, materially new evidence, and a valid later regulation. Deliberate sandbagging is not found here. A reversal on exhaustion returns the two unreviewed issues for ordinary judicial consideration, not an award of benefits or an automatic evidentiary rehearing.

### Justice-specific source anchors, without vote assignments

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter joined simulated McCarthy's statutory-design and institutional-discretion rationale; Scalia and Thomas joined its result only because the process could not award the requested compensation. The difference is explicit in Standards and Tests. All then-serving Justices joined Darby's APA rule. Current-term Illinois Council supplies the recent positions of all eight Associates on the relationship between prescribed administrative review and practical access to court, including narrower statutory positions; its operative provisions are copied. Ginsburg and Breyer's later arrival on the Court does not warrant assigning them a Darby or McCarthy join. None of these sources already establishes this case's answer.

**Readiness:** Ready for independent modeling, preserving the distinction between Council-stage issue exhaustion and merits/ALJ-stage questions.

## United States v. Hubbell — No. 99-166 — June 5

### Record and reach

The October 1996 subpoena called for eleven broad document categories. On November 19 Hubbell invoked the privilege, then complied under a §6003 order granting statutory use and derivative-use immunity. He produced 13,120 pages and answered completeness questions, identifying attorney-client/work-product exceptions. The contents supplied information leading to the April 1998 ten-count tax, mail-fraud and wire-fraud indictment. These are supported record recitations; they do not establish a privilege for all voluntarily created contents.

The District Court dismissed on immunity and independently on the Independent Counsel's authority. The D.C. Circuit rejected the latter ground, which is outside review; on immunity it vacated and remanded for examination of prior knowledge of existence, authenticity, possession and control. That court did not require prior knowledge of contents. On remand, the prosecutor acknowledged inability to satisfy the circuit's reasonable-particularity rule. A conditional agreement provides for dismissal unless this review makes it reasonably likely that production immunity is no significant bar; a sufficiently favorable reversal instead permits a guilty plea without incarceration. A live controversy remains. Other defendants' dismissals with prejudice are not before this Court.

The Government says it need not introduce either the production act or the documents at trial; it does not claim wholly independent evidence. That position differs from the narrower reasonable-particularity concession. The earlier circuit remand is not an unexercised opportunity automatically available after this decision. Any mandate must take the intervening conditional agreement into account.

### Entering law and opposing paths

The Fifth Amendment protects compelled testimonial self-incrimination. Sections 6002–6003 authorize compelled testimony/information with protection against direct and derivative criminal use, subject to their perjury, false-statement and noncompliance exceptions. Kastigar holds use-and-derivative-use immunity coextensive with the privilege and puts the independent-source burden on the Government. Fisher distinguishes unprivileged voluntarily prepared contents from the potentially testimonial concessions made by production, and recognizes a foregone-conclusion setting where those concessions add no material information. Doe, 465 U.S. 605, protects an incriminating testimonial act of producing sole-proprietor records even where the contents are not privileged. Balsys preserves domestic use-immunity protection; its independent foreign-prosecution result narrows no domestic Kastigar rule.

* Protected production and derivative use: selection, location, assembly and authentication of these broadly described, previously unknown records communicate the producer's knowledge. The use of that knowledge as an investigative lead is derivative use even if the physical papers and production testimony are not trial exhibits. Strongest counterargument: voluntarily created contents remain independently unprivileged, and a production rule should not transform statutory use immunity into immunity for all transactions the papers describe.
* Limited act-use protection: prohibit evidentiary use of implicit existence/possession/authenticity admissions but permit the Government to derive leads from the contents. Strongest counterargument: it must explain how the Government acquired the contents without exploiting the compelled testimonial selection that identified and supplied them; describing an ultimate exhibit as unprivileged does not itself establish an independent source.
* A true foregone conclusion would avoid protected testimonial disclosure to the relevant extent, but ordinary expectations that business records exist are not automatically prior knowledge of these responsive documents. If the factual threshold cannot be met under the adopted rule, the present concessions and agreement govern the case's consequence. Do not order a fresh independent-source search by default.

Keep voluntary contents, genuine foregone conclusions, production by third parties, collective-entity custody and lawful independent evidence distinct. No entire commercial-paper privilege, transactional immunity, expanded Independent Counsel ruling or acquittal of other defendants follows.

### Justice-specific source anchors, without vote assignments

Kennedy's Braswell dissent, joined by Scalia, treats the individual's compelled testimonial production as analytically distinct from ownership of corporate records and accepts statutory use immunity as a way to compel it. Stevens and O'Connor joined the Braswell Court's collective-entity rule, which nonetheless excludes personal evidentiary use of the individual production act; it does not decide an immunized personal subpoena by collapsing act into contents. Stevens's Doe, 487 U.S. 201, dissent treats compelled disclosure of the contents of one's mind as testimonial; O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy joined the Court's no-testimony ruling for a hypothetical bank-consent form, while that majority expressly recognizes the mind-disclosure principle. These are relevant distinctions, not current-case votes. Sources: [Braswell](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/487/99), [Doe (1988)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/487/201), [Doe (1984)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/465/605).

The Balsys controlling rule was joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; their actual rule preserves domestic immunity. No separate Ginsburg or Breyer view on this subpoena is inferred from their nonjoin in that foreign-prosecution case. A proposed broader originalist privilege or other Justice-specific enlargement needs its own prior writing; the existing record is sufficient to model the ordinary act/derivative-use dispute without inventing such a position.

**Readiness:** Ready for independent modeling with the conditional agreement and present source concession included. The source does not support another evidentiary round merely because the original circuit disposition used the word remand.

## Mixon v. Ohio — lower No. 98-3368 — June 5

### Verified lower record, correction and limits

The entire published Sixth Circuit opinion, 193 F.3d 389, was recovered and read from [the reporter copy](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/193/193.F3d.389.98-3368.html), saved as [source text](../sources/chunk7-neutral-b/mixon-sixth-circuit.txt). It reviews Rule 12(c) judgments on pleadings. The surviving plaintiffs are Black mothers Mixon (Cleveland) and Thomas (Garfield Heights), and the NAACP; other appellants voluntarily dismissed. The court did not dispute the organization's standing. The school district includes Cleveland, Bratenahl, Linndale, Newburgh Heights and part of Garfield Heights.

H.B.269 applies to districts that are or have been under the defined federal supervision order. After release, Cleveland's mayor selects nine board members from at least eighteen nominees supplied by the statutory panel. Three panel members must reside in the district outside Cleveland, and one appointed board member must do so. Nominees must live in the district and cannot hold elected office; four board members must have the defined expertise. Specified higher-education presidents serve as nonvoting ex officio members. The mayor's CEO appointment power is unilateral for thirty months and thereafter requires board concurrence. These constraints matter to the claim that Cleveland voters alone directly elect the board: they elect the mayor, while the board is genuinely appointed through the described process.

The statute requires a districtwide referendum in the first even-numbered year at least four years after the board assumes control. A choice to restore elections leads to a seven-member elected board at the next regular odd-year election; a choice to retain appointment leads to new appointments the following July. On the published chronology, control returned September 9, 1998 and the court described November 2002/2003 as the earliest expected referendum/restored election. Those dates are the lower court's statutory application, not a later event found to have happened. The supplied packet's simple permanent replacement description is incomplete.

The circuit dismissed Ohio's direct state-law and equal-protection claims on immunity grounds; it allowed the VRA claim against Ohio, and treated the mayor as municipal, not an arm of the State. It affirmed remaining rulings. The opinion rejects geographic differential treatment, licensing objections and the extraterritorial-franchise challenge. Its §2 disposition rests on categorical noncoverage of appointive offices, drawing on Chisom's appointment discussion and several circuits' decisions. Ohio is not a §5 covered jurisdiction. Footnote 12 says the complaint cited §1971 and the court treated the voting claim under the substantive VRA provisions; do not import its private-§1971 statement into the Court's holding because that question is not granted.

**Additional primary record:** The complete District Court opinion, Spivey v. Ohio, 999 F.Supp.987 (N.D.Ohio March 6, 1998), was read in its [published opinion copy](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148102add7b0493447ce95), not the site's optional summary. It expressly uses the eight-count Mixon complaint as the reference for both consolidated actions. Its VRA discussion recites that the complaint alleges discriminatory impact on African-Americans. After describing African-American Citizens' challenge to dilution of Black voters' local influence through statewide appointment, the District Court calls this allegation somewhat analogous, and rejects it on categorical appointive-office noncoverage as well as its §1971 private-action rationale. Coupled with the circuit's express treatment of the claim under §2 and adjudication of categorical coverage, this establishes an actually pleaded racial-effects claim that reached appellate decision. It is more than a record of universally lost voting alone. It does not establish the truth of the alleged effect, specific demographic ratios, liability or every detailed factual allegation. The complete complaint is not a necessary prerequisite to reviewing that categorical legal ground; it remains relevant to more specific sufficiency or liability findings.

**Correction and remaining distinction:** Neither published opinion establishes that a distinct intentional racial-discrimination claim was pleaded and adjudicated. The original packet's phrase that the local record reports rejection of the purpose claim is unsupported at that specificity. The geographical, licensing and extraterritorial constitutional rulings do not become a racial-purpose adjudication. Any affirmative purpose finding or a remand of a particular constitutional purpose claim requires its actual allegation and preservation. Absence from the published recitation is not proof that no such pleading exists.

### Entering law and opposing paths

The slice copies Holder's structural-coverage rule and grounded-comparison requirement, Valdespino's narrow functional district-capacity modification, and Presley's distinction between ordinary authority reallocations and an extreme transfer effectively replacing elective office. Chisom supplies §2's application to elective judicial office and its discussion of appointment; it does not automatically decide whether elimination of an existing election can itself be a discriminatory voting practice under the materially different simulated Holder rule. Section 2 requires protected voters' unequal political opportunity caused by the challenged practice under the statutory totality; it creates no right to proportional winners. The former electoral system can supply an objective institutional comparator without becoming a perpetual command to retain elections.

* Categorical statutory noncoverage: once the office is appointive no election for that office exists for §2 to regulate; the statute's nomination/election language and Chisom's institutional-choice discussion support this path. Strongest counterargument: a challenged change that withdraws an existing election is itself a voting practice, and categorical immunity for it would evade scrutiny of unequal racial opportunity produced by the transition. Holder permits structural inquiry without requiring one uniquely ideal institutional design.
* Coverage with independent injury requirements: a direct elected-to-appointed restructuring can be tested against a practical, legally grounded benchmark, but universal loss alone proves no racial inequality. Strongest counterargument: comparing an appointed body with the old election risks turning every institutional choice into a compulsory election; this must be answered by preserving race-specific injury, causation, totality and genuine institutional interests.
* If the circuit's categorical rationale is rejected, the existing pleaded and appealed racial-impact claim supplies a vehicle for a bounded legal remand. The Court may direct application of the correct coverage/comparator rule while leaving exact pleading sufficiency, causation, unequal racial opportunity, totality and any preserved independent ground to ordinary proceedings. The published recitation does not establish all those elements. No violation, obligatory new evidentiary proceeding or restoration of the old board follows merely from reversal of categorical noncoverage.

Sailors permits genuinely appointive nonlegislative school boards. Holt distinguishes municipal nonresidents subject to some extraterritorial authority from residents selectively excluded from elections within the voting unit, as in Kramer and Hadley. The mayor-selection objection is not automatically an election for board members. Conversely, the absence of an independent right to elect an office does not license intentional racial discrimination: Arlington Heights/Feeney purpose and causation requirements remain applicable to an actual preserved claim. A rational-basis explanation for geographic institutional choice does not answer a proved prohibited purpose. The current-term Rice decision addresses a racial restriction on an existing public electorate, not an appointment requirement. Olech does not erase the distinct racial-purpose predicates. Ohio's direct constitutional immunity and the municipal defendant's amenability remain separate.

### Justice-specific source anchors, without vote assignments

Holder directly establishes Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg's joins in structural coverage, grounded comparison and proof; O'Connor joined coverage while rejecting that benchmark and liability; Kennedy reserved coverage and disputed the comparison; Scalia and Thomas rejected dilution coverage. Valdespino supplies Breyer's join in the rigorously proved functional opportunity route, with O'Connor/Kennedy adhering to a numerical route and Scalia/Thomas maintaining their distinct coverage view. These public positions require independent application, not automatic repetition. The current-term Rice and Olech opinions and separate positions add each participating Associate's relevant electorate and equal-protection commitments. None eliminates the actual pleading and causal-proof requirements here.

**Readiness:** Ready to model the legal coverage, comparator, genuine-appointment, defendant-immunity and bounded remand alternatives on the pleaded and appealed racial-impact claim. Any definitive purpose merits, race-specific liability finding, detailed pleading-sufficiency determination or board-restoration remedy requires its additional factual/preservation predicates. A conditional legal analysis does not itself enter a remand or establish the missing facts.

## Harris Trust and Savings Bank v. Salomon Smith Barney — No. 99-579 — June 12

### Record and reach

NISA, APT's investment manager, directed roughly $21 million in purchases of motel interests from Salomon, which also supplied nondiscretionary brokerage services. The plan trustee and administrator sued after the interests became nearly worthless. Whether they were worthless initially or declined with the market is disputed. They request rescission, restitution of price with interest, and disgorgement under §502(a)(3) for an alleged §406(a) prohibited transaction. The case assumes no Salomon fiduciary status.

The District Court denied a categorical nonfiduciary-immunity summary-judgment motion and certified under §1292(b); the Seventh Circuit, 184 F.3d 646, reversed and directed judgment on that legal ground. Thus the Court reviews defendant eligibility under the existing claim, not a final liability finding. Party-in-interest status, PTE75-1 and prohibited/nonexempt transaction predicates remain disputed. Actual or constructive notice, available relief against actual property/proceeds, and the allocation of the purchaser-for-value-without-notice burden remain undecided. The plaintiffs' new legal articulation of the existing equitable claim is not automatically a new claim; Salomon's preservation objection must address that distinction.

### Entering law and opposing paths

Section 406(a) directs fiduciaries not to cause specified transactions with parties in interest, subject to §408 and applicable exemptions. Section 502(a)(3) expressly identifies authorized plaintiffs and permits injunctions against violations and other appropriate equitable relief to redress violations or enforce ERISA/plan terms. Defendant status and remedy classification are separate questions. A violation of ERISA or plan terms must exist; general trust-law equity cannot substitute for that predicate.

Mertens excludes ordinary compensatory damages from this provision but expressly reserves its antecedent nonfiduciary-action question. It does not forbid every equitable monetary recovery. Varity permits appropriate individual equitable redress where the statutory predicates exist and ordinarily treats adequate alternative relief as a reason equity is unnecessary; it does not convert every recipient into a fiduciary. Peacock requires an independent statutory/plan violation; veil piercing alone supplies none.

* Eligible transferee: where a fiduciary engages in a prohibited, nonexempt transfer, traditional equitable recovery can reach a nonfiduciary recipient with the legally required notice. Section 502(a)(3)'s plaintiff restrictions do not contain a categorical fiduciary-defendant restriction. Strongest counterargument: §406(a)'s duty is imposed on fiduciaries, so imposing recipient liability risks evading Congress's carefully drawn duty and remedy scheme.
* No statutory claim against this defendant: the provision redresses a violation by the person sued and §406(a) imposes no relevant duty on a nonfiduciary counterparty. Strongest counterargument: it must explain why redressing the fiduciary's completed violation cannot include recognized equitable recovery from a knowing transferee when the enforcement text does not identify a closed defendant class; Mertens left that question open rather than deciding it adversely.
* A narrower decision may reject categorical immunity while leaving party-in-interest status, exemption, knowledge, bona-fide-purchaser protection, specific tracing and the precise form/amount of relief to ordinary proceedings. It must not award the purchase price automatically or label every request called restitution equitable. The original payment and current assets/proceeds determine the remedy inquiry. No finding that the investment was worthless at purchase follows.

### Justice-specific source anchors, without vote assignments

Scalia authored Mertens; Kennedy, Souter and Thomas joined its traditional-remedy limitation. Souter authored the simulated Varity holding, joined in its §502(a)(3) portion by Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined Thomas's Peacock statutory-violation requirement. Those are specific existing joins, not a resolution of the reserved transferee question. All must keep Mertens's expressly reserved cause-of-action issue separate from its controlling compensation limit. A proposed additional burden rule, categorical tracing rule or broader recipient duty requires support beyond the legal question presented.

**Readiness:** Ready for independent modeling of the categorical legal question and ordinary remand. Liability, exemption, notice, purchaser defense allocation and actual equitable recovery remain open on their actual record predicates.
