## Component I — preference and permitted use

**Rule supported by all eight:** The Secretary may define grazing preference as priority attached to base property and separately express authorized forage in permitted use, rather than freezing an immutable animal-unit quantity into the preference definition. The actual system must continue adequately safeguarding recognized grazing privileges so far as consistent with the Act and must honor the Act's separate valid-rights, renewal and water protections. The challenged definitional separation does not itself eliminate those safeguards.

**Support and counterargument:** Taylor §2 expressly authorizes range protection and administration; §3 leaves stock numbers and seasons to management, couples renewal preference with discretion and denies that a grazing permit itself conveys an estate in the land. The new definition retains priority; permitted use continues to state allocation, including suspended use within its actual regulatory scope. Those connected provisions defeat the claim that prior regulatory wording made quantity permanently unalterable.

The challengers' strongest path is that a nominal priority emptied of forage would violate the statutory safeguard. That objection is legally sound as a limit on implementation, but is not established by splitting the definitions. The agency's representation that existing preference and suspended-use priority remain is relevant to operation; it cannot authorize a later inconsistent deprivation. Rejecting the facial statutory challenge does not presume every future reduction valid or convert the no-estate clause into absence of all enforceable rights.

**Operative qualifications:** Permitted use is specified for preference holders and encompasses authorized and suspended use, with the actual ephemeral/annual-rangeland exceptions retained. Changes require monitoring, field observations, ecological inventory or other acceptable supporting data. Ordinary reductions require consultation and a documented agreement or proposed decision. Immediate protection for conditions such as drought, fire, flood, infestation or imminent significant resource damage follows the separate consultation-or-reasonable-attempt route; final effective action remains appealable under its stay procedure. These provisions answer the facial assertion of unbounded quantity control, without deciding a future APA or application claim. No new universal process or factual proof requirement is added by this decision.

## Component II — deletion of the commercial-livestock-business qualification

**Rule supported by all eight:** Section 3 does not impose engagement in a commercial livestock business as a universal eligibility requirement. The Secretary may remove that regulatory condition while retaining the Act's stock-owner eligibility, applicable regulatory qualifications and distinct statutory preferences. Commercial engagement in the identified preference category cannot be converted into a prerequisite governing every otherwise eligible stock owner.

**Support and counterargument:** Congress distinguishes the authorized stock-owner class from preference for nearby landowners engaged in the business, bona fide occupants or settlers, and owners of water or water rights as necessary for use of their property. Giving those separate words their separate work permits qualified noncommercial stock owners. It does not validate grazing permits for every organization owning no animals or the conservation-use rule already left outside review.

The strongest contrary path treats actual grazing and the Act's livestock purpose as incompatible with removal of business engagement. Actual grazing and commercial occupation are not identical conditions. The regular use and nonuse rules retain concrete grazing requirements, and no contrary no-stock entitlement is created by deleting only the commercial condition.

The officer may, after two consecutive grazing fee years without substantial authorized use or failure to maintain or use water base property, and after the specified consultation with the permittee and recorded lienholder, cancel the unused amount. Cancellation is discretionary, not automatic. Temporary nonuse remains subject to plan/rangeland-health conformity, reasons and annual approval, for at most three consecutive years; temporarily available forage may be allocated to others. These limits are distinct from the unreviewed conservation-use provision. The listed qualification exceptions for acquired lands and other specified routes retain their own scope; no actual entitlement under an exception is found.

## Component III — prospective title to permanent cooperative improvements

**Rule supported by all eight:** Taylor §§2 and 4 permit the Secretary to specify federal title for future permanent range improvements authorized through cooperative agreements after August 21, 1995, subject to valid existing rights and the rule's contribution protections. Section 4 protects improvements previously constructed and privately owned; it does not require private ownership of every improvement developed under a future cooperative arrangement.

**Support and counterargument:** Section 2 expressly authorizes cooperative agreements, and §4 distinguishes construction under a permit from approved cooperative arrangements. Its reasonable-value provision applies to use of improvements constructed and owned by a prior occupant. Prospective federal title leaves that protection with its actual predicate; it does not confiscate a prior occupant's improvement. State partition-fence requirements likewise remain in force.

The strongest contrary path is that the rule extinguishes the investment protection §4 recognizes by requiring new permanent developments to take federal title. The answer is the distinction between protecting existing ownership and specifying ownership at the beginning of a future authorized arrangement. Public ownership of the land alone would not defeat a separate vested private right; the rule expressly preserves such rights. The Court need not assume that every future agreement will be fair or valid to reject the facial claim of mandatory private title.

All new permanent water developments must proceed through cooperative agreements. The agreement specifies cost or labor allocation; BLM must document contributed funds, labor and materials for the credits specified in §§4120.3-5 and 4120.3-6(c). Work itself confers no exclusive use of the improvement or affected land. Nonstructural improvements belong to the United States; authorized removable livestock-handling and temporary structural improvements may remain privately titled under the separate permit rule. Temporary shared use retains the stated reasonable compensation and cost-allocation procedures. These are retained terms, not an award in this case. The decision adjudicates neither existing-title extinguishment nor an actual taking, compensation amount, final permit reopening or reliance injury.

Mining's express mining-permit correction power is not imported into the Taylor Act. Its temporal distinction illustrates why a rule about future improvements is not a title transfer merely because an applicant already owns base property, but supplies no permission to impose a genuine past-conduct disability or disregard existing rights. Saenz adds no rule governing these components.

## Compatible propositions, scope and disposition

Each of the eight adopts each complete component rule above, its controlling statutory explanation and its stated limits. The reasoning forms a common proposition-level coalition on all three components; no author is assigned. The commercial-qualification ruling does not depend on validating conservation use. The title ruling does not depend on an unrestricted no-estate proposition. The preference ruling does not depend on finding every future allocation reasonable.

Affirm the Tenth Circuit on the three presented statutory-authority questions. Leave its unpresented conservation-use and arbitrary-and-capricious dispositions at their existing scope. No agency remand, new rule, individual permit, hearing, quantity guarantee, property transfer or compensation award follows. The bounded statutory grounds do not require facts the packet leaves unproved, and no source gap is treated as litigant failure.
