Fit governs this assignment. All six eligible Justices already support the common present-offense rule and bounded remedy; no necessary join depends on changing an Associate's ground or resolving a remaining formulation objection. The Chief's expressly permitted present-offense wording is compatible with the five reconciled Associate commitments. An expansion narrative would add no truthful legal work.

Stone-Zsela is selected for the single lead. His case-specific position sharply separates three matters the opinion must keep distinct: constitutional classification newly adopted here, the jury and proof consequences of that classification, and the remaining state charging and further-proceeding questions. His Almendarez-Torres concurrence previously stated the maximum-based constitutional premise, and his Jones position preserved it without representing the statutory judgment as a constitutional holding. His present position expressly identifies the Sixth Amendment, its application through the Fourteenth, and the Fourteenth Amendment's reasonable-doubt protection, while withholding a state charging determination. Writing the common narrower rule himself gives a concrete advantage in carrying that precise doctrinal transition and the admission-as-element condition through to the limited Count 18 mandate without treating his broader individual position as Court law. This is selective doctrinal leadership grounded in his stated legal work, not the case's importance or his office.

Souter is the strongest alternative. His Jones concurrence supplies the exact present-offense functional formulation and already distinguishes ordinary sentencing, prior status and other systems. He can faithfully write the complete opinion, including its state-charging reservation; no ability or stylistic objection makes him ineligible. On the present inputs he offers no additional necessary accommodation or coalition-preservation advantage over Stone's expressly compatible formulation. Stone's count of nine, compared with Souter's ten, further supports selecting Stone where both can carry the same majority, but the count neither supplies the constitutional ground nor changes the opinion's form or scope. Stevens, Scalia and Ginsburg also support the common rule; Thomas's broader new account can coexist with it but performs its additional work in a separate concurrence. All remain eligible, and no broader personal view is used to exclude them.

The single-author form remains the form fixed before counting. The six full joins are checked against the actual final rule and remedy, rather than inferred from authorship. No historical author or join is imported.
