These are five-to-three non-Stone commitments, not a completed nine-Justice judgment. On the reversing path, all five join this operative rule: where the offense-date statute already admits the witness's testimony but makes conviction on that testimony alone legally insufficient unless its age or timely-report exception applies, a later law cannot retrospectively eliminate the additional proof requirement. The ruling does not freeze ordinary competence or admissibility law, establish a constitutional corroboration requirement independent of the earlier statute, or turn every evidentiary disadvantage into an Ex Post Facto violation.

The earlier exception covered a complainant younger than fourteen; the reporting alternative required a report to another person within six months. The amendment, effective September 1, 1993, expanded the age exception to persons younger than eighteen and the reporting period to one year. Counts 7–10 concern offenses from June 1992 through July 1993, when the complainant was fourteen or fifteen. Her March 1995 report meets neither reporting period. The age-exception amendment therefore removes the operative former condition on these counts.
