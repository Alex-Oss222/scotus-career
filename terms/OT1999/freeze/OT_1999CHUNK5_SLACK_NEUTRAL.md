## 1. Channel, questions and participation

The immediate judgment under review is the Ninth Circuit's July 7, 1998 order denying a certificate of probable cause, not a federal merits judgment rejecting Slack's underlying constitutional claims. The District Court's March 31, 1998 disposition supplies the underlying procedural ruling. The supplied question concerns whether a renewed habeas application after an exhaustion dismissal without prejudice is second or successive, including claims omitted from the first filing. The certificate amendments' temporal reach and the standard for an appeal from a procedural dismissal are also presented. Preserve these as distinct questions; neither certificate review nor classification of the petition itself awards collateral relief.

All nine Justices in the frozen entering state are seated: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No case-specific nonparticipation is supplied. Ordinary appellate majority rules apply, subject to final participation validation at assembly.

The existing simulated Hohn holding establishes statutory certiorari review of a judicial certificate denial whether the circuit correctly or incorrectly required a certificate. It expressly reserves Hohn's own transition question. Consequently, a historical brief's suggestion that Hohn already resolved post-enactment certificate timing cannot be treated as a controlling in-world holding. Felker's separate prohibition on review of an actual circuit successive-application authorization decision is not automatically a bar to this certificate-denial review.

## 2. Supported record and distinct procedural components

Slack was convicted in Nevada of second-degree murder with use of a deadly weapon and sentenced to consecutive life terms with the possibility of parole. This is a noncapital case. The Nevada Supreme Court dismissed his direct appeal on November 5, 1991. Disputed accounts about the killing and the merits of the constitutional claims remain allegations or lower-court matters, not findings of this Court.

1. **First federal proceeding.** Slack filed pro se on November 27, 1991, raising evidentiary sufficiency, sexual-relationship evidence, reasonable-doubt instructions and premeditation instructions. He then sought to hold the proceeding in abeyance so that he could present additional unexhausted issues in state court, including trial counsel's handling of a witness's agreement and accomplice instructions and alleged nondisclosure about fingerprints. On February 19, 1992, after a telephonic hearing and with neither party objecting, the District Court dismissed the action without prejudice and allowed an application to renew after exhaustion of all state remedies. The minutes supply no prior federal merits determination. Do not transform this record into an adjudication of the claims or presume deliberate abandonment of them. It is more precise to describe the actual exhaustion dismissal than to assume that every later claim was already within the initial petition.
2. **State return and renewed federal proceeding.** Slack pursued state postconviction relief beginning in July 1992, and returned to federal court in 1995. The District Court and petitioner's principal brief identify commencement on March 27, 1995; the Joint Appendix identifies the renewed petition as filed May 30, 1995. The latter is the reproduced filing date; the former is the stated commencement date. Both precede AEDPA. No choice between those dates is needed to decide the enactment boundary. Do not use the supplemental brief's isolated May 20 wording as an independently verified filing date.
3. **Counsel and amendment.** Counsel was appointed February 13, 1997. The order instructed counsel to explore potential grounds, advise Slack to raise all possible grounds, and amend. The counseled third amended petition was filed December 24, 1997. It presented seven numbered grounds, with component grounds preserved below.
4. **Abuse order and response.** The March 1998 order, dated March 10 in the reproduced order and identified as March 11 in the Joint Appendix contents, found that the State had adequately pleaded abuse as to grounds 2, 4(c), 5, 6 and 7. It directed a cause-and-prejudice showing or a fundamental-miscarriage-of-justice showing within fifteen days. Slack sought reconsideration rather than supplying the requested cause-and-prejudice demonstration, disputing the premise that the renewed filing was successive. The order's own text and the later dismissal, not an inconsistent brief reference to March 13, should control the exact dating if material.
5. **March 31 dismissal.** The District Court applied Farmer v. McDaniel and Rule 9(b), holding that a prior merits determination was unnecessary for abuse based on omitted claims; it found the State's pleading sufficient and Slack's response inadequate to show cause and prejudice. It dismissed grounds 2, 4(c), 5, 6 and 7 with prejudice as abusive. Separately it found ground 3 unexhausted because the direct appeal had presented a state-law objection to the sexual evidence without alerting the state court to the asserted Fifth/Fourteenth Amendment due-process theory. It dismissed the action without prejudice on that exhaustion ground. These are distinct dispositions, not a single all-claims merits dismissal.
6. **Existing remedial directions.** The District Court allowed Slack to seek reopening within thirty days with a personally signed abandonment of ground 3, or to pursue state exhaustion; it also discussed commencing a new federal action under AEDPA, permissive merits denial of unexhausted claims, and limitations consequences. Those are descriptions of the lower order, not this Court's adoption of its limitations analysis or a new reopening period. The review of the abuse classification does not silently resolve every exhaustion or timeliness issue.
7. **Appeal and certification.** Slack noticed appeal April 29, 1998 and applied for a certificate of probable cause May 11. The District Court's denial was dated May 11 and filed May 12. The District Court applied Barefoot: ultimate merits success was unnecessary, but a substantial federal-right showing and issues debatable among reasonable jurists were required. It concluded that the proposed appeal raised no substantial issue. The Ninth Circuit's July 7 order denied the request without a stated substantive explanation. Slack's district certificate request disputed the successive classification, the abuse determination absent deliberate withholding, and the nonexhaustion treatment of ground 3. The later Supreme review questions and the appellate remedy must retain their actual scope; do not assume that every underlying constitutional claim has been adjudicated or that a certificate already exists.

## 3. Law entering the questions

Read this packet with `../entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK5_SLACK.md`, `../entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK5_SLACK_PROCEDURE.md`, and the frozen entering neutral state. Those are reading copies of identified governing authorities; the April 3 baseline requires the actual-record refresh stated above.

- **Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments.** Present operation: Carriger, April 16, 1998, for its specified capital extension; Lindh, June 23, 1997, for its noncapital application-date construction. Lindh excludes the chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended section 2254(d), from a noncapital application filed before April 24, 1996. It preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and Felker's successive-proceeding treatment. It is not an adjudication that every later appellate event or every amendment follows one date. The section 2253 transition question is still open in the supplied law. The State's proposed claim-by-claim application-date rule is an argument to test against that law, not an enacted rule.
- **Habeas and section 2255 appeals and appellate certificates.** Present operation: Peguero, March 2, 1999; Hohn and the April 24, 1996 enactment retain their specified roles. Hohn makes a judicial certificate denial reviewable and reserves its disputed certificate-transition issue; vacatur does not itself issue a certificate. Peguero addresses an actually issued issue-specific certificate and does not dispense with the conditions for proper issuance here, where none has issued.
- **Enacted section 2253.** Paragraph (a) makes a district final habeas or section 2255 order reviewable in the appropriate court of appeals. Paragraph (b) supplies no appeal from the defined federal criminal removal-warrant or detention-pending-removal proceedings. Paragraph (c)(1) requires a circuit justice's or judge's certificate for the applicant's appeal from final state-process habeas orders or section 2255 orders. Paragraph (c)(2) requires a substantial showing of denial of a constitutional right; paragraph (c)(3) requires the qualifying issues to be identified. Do not omit issue identification or turn constitutional showing into a final merits adjudication. Under the enacted Rule 22, district or circuit judges may issue a certificate; a notice without an express request serves as a request to the circuit judges, and a State or its representative needs no certificate. Complete enacted text is mechanically copied in the source excerpt named below. Former certificate-of-probable-cause practice and Barefoot's substantial federal showing are established by the lower filings and the preserved Lonchar discussion; the statutory transition remains contested.
- **Second or successive state-custody habeas applications.** Present operation: Stewart, May 18, 1998. Its seven-Justice merits rule covers a Ford claim actually presented, dismissed as premature and expressly preserved; it does not already settle every exhaustion dismissal, every new claim, or every nonmerits dismissal. Its nine-Justice threshold rule distinguishes classification outside authorization from an actual authorization determination. The narrow rule and its limits must both be applied.
- **Pre-enactment rule and statutory predicates.** Rule 9(b) permits dismissal of repeated grounds when there was a prior merits determination, or of new grounds when their earlier omission constituted abuse. The parties dispute whether the latter clause dispenses with a prior merits prerequisite in this exhaustion-return setting. Former section 2244(b) addresses a subsequent application after federal denial following a factual or legal merits hearing and requires an unadjudicated ground plus no deliberate withholding or other abuse for its exception. These provisions are separately reproduced from the original brief's statutory appendix; neither may be silently collapsed into the other. Rule 9(a) separately permits dismissal for prejudicial delay unless reasonable diligence could not have disclosed the grounds before the prejudice arose.
- **Lonchar, April 1, 1996.** Its unanimous first holding rejects dismissal of a first personal petition through free-floating equitable disapproval instead of governing statutory or Rule predicates. It preserves exhaustion, structured abuse rules, source-authorized sanctions, and unpresented fraud/inherent-power questions. It does not establish an entitlement to a merits writ, hearing, or unlimited serial filings. Its preservation of lawful predicates informs both sides' classification arguments.
- **AEDPA section 2244(b).** If applicable and triggered, repeated claims must be dismissed. New claims require a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court, or both previously undiscoverable facts despite due diligence and facts that, proved and viewed with all the evidence, clearly and convincingly establish that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would find guilt. Circuit advance authorization requires a three-judge panel, prima facie showing and action within thirty days; the grant/denial is unreviewable by appeal, rehearing or certiorari. The District Court must independently dismiss unless the conditions are established. These stringent consequences make the classification question material; they do not decide its answer.

Rose v. Lundy supplies the pre-divergence exhaustion background; Barefoot supplies the substantial-showing/debatable-issue certificate background; Sanders and McCleskey supply the competing abuse arguments. Treat the briefs' references to later historical Supreme precedents only through their actual adopted simulated scope. In particular, the historical versions of Hohn, Stewart, Lonchar, Lindh or Felker do not enlarge the supplied in-world holdings.

## 4. Supported litigation positions and material disagreements

**Petitioner.** A without-prejudice exhaustion dismissal before federal adjudication should not impose successive-petition preclusion on the renewed filing, even when it adds claims. Rose's complete-exhaustion procedure, former section 2244(b), the opening phrase of Rule 9(b), the judgment-based history of abuse, and the predictable procedures required by Lonchar support that argument. Newly discovered or newly refined claims during state exhaustion should not turn compliance with the exhaustion rule into automatic forfeiture. Lawful exhaustion requirements and case-management measures remain available. His principal brief seeks vacatur of the certificate denial and remand, not release or a determination that the underlying claims are meritorious.

On transition, petitioner argues that the pre-enactment application remains one pending case through amendment and appeal, that Lindh controls, and that the State forfeited reliance on AEDPA by not invoking it below and by affirmatively disclaiming it in its principal brief. In the alternative he argues that AEDPA retains Barefoot's ability to review debatable procedural errors. His December supplemental brief endorses the Morris approach requiring a credible procedural-error showing and a substantial allegation of constitutional deprivation. His January reply resists requiring proof of underlying merits where the procedural dismissal prevented development; he argues that the constitutional allegations and debatable barrier suffice at this stage. Thus the compact packet's two-part formula must not be attributed to him as an unqualified concession or treated as already governing law.

**Respondents.** Rule 9(b)'s new-ground clause does not expressly require a prior merits determination; Sanders recognized an abuse inquiry following a nonmerits denial, and McCleskey supplies the State's pleading burden followed by cause/prejudice or the miscarriage-of-justice route. Prior omission of record-apparent claims can be abusive notwithstanding the first dismissal. Respondents argue that serial incomplete filings burden state and federal courts and that Slack returned with unexhausted claims despite the exhaustion purpose of the prior dismissal. Those advocacy assertions are not findings of intentional disobedience, deliberate withholding or misconduct by this Court.

Respondents initially argued that pre-AEDPA law governed. Their supplemental position is that section 2253 is triggered by initiating an appeal after enactment, while the new claims first raised in December 1997 should receive AEDPA treatment under section 2244. Their reply disclaims the proposition that merely amending a pleading creates an entirely new case; it instead relies on when individual claims first appeared, and says the claims are successive because omitted from the 1991 proceeding. They deny waiver because they characterize sections 2244(b) and 2253(c) as jurisdictional. They contend that the change from federal to constitutional rights bars certification of purely procedural questions, and oppose looking beyond the District Court's procedural order to the petition's constitutional claims. Do not recast their argument as agreement with a two-part certificate test.

**Amicus forks disclosed by the parties' supplemental filings.** Those filings identify the United States' position as favoring post-enactment appellate-certificate treatment, permitting review of procedural barriers to constitutional claims, and requiring attention to the underlying constitutional claim; they identify state amici's categorical objection to procedural certificates and CJLF's rejection of that categorical position. These descriptions are attributable accounts in the parties' briefs, not a claim that this validator read the amicus originals. No additional amicus concession or exact formulation is inferred. A model that makes an amicus-specific formulation decisive must obtain its original or a separately verified neutral extraction.

## 5. Supported alternatives, sequencing and remedy limits

- Resolve the certificate regime and the State's waiver/jurisdiction objection on their own legal premises. Available arguments include retaining former CPC treatment for the pending case, applying the new certificate regime to the post-enactment appellate step, and the separate claim-date theory for amendments. Choosing appellate timing does not itself require changing the law governing district merits adjudication.
- Under applicable certificate law, distinguish a substantial/debatable constitutional claim, a substantial/debatable procedural barrier, and success on the underlying claim. Supported paths include the categorical no-procedural-certificate reading; a Barefoot-style procedural route for constitutional claims; and a two-part threshold requiring sufficient constitutional and procedural showings. A plain procedural bar can defeat certification without adjudicating every underlying claim; rejection of the procedural bar does not automatically establish the constitutional showing.
- For successive classification, compare a confined exhaustion-return rule, including newly asserted claims, with respondents' construction allowing abuse for omitted grounds despite the nonmerits first dismissal. Stewart supplies an analogy with express limits, not an already decided universal rule. Sanders's pleading-deficiency context, Rose's exhaustion direction and the statutory/rule text require their own treatment.
- If classification or the certificate standard was erroneous, vacatur/remand for the proper appellate determination is available. The lower court may still need to determine whether particular constitutional claims meet the certificate requirement. The record does not justify an automatic certificate for every ground, a constitutional violation finding, an evidentiary hearing, a new trial, release, or collateral relief.
- If the denial rests on a lawful certificate or abuse ground, affirmance remains a supported path. Any narrower ground must address its preservation and the actual record rather than inventing default from source gaps.
- Removal of the five abuse dismissals leaves the separately preserved exhaustion objections and ground 3's actual procedural posture to lawful handling. Neither a fresh limitations period, an unlimited right to amend, immunity from lawful orders, nor automatic merits review follows. A source-authorized sanction requires its own predicates; the State's concern about serial litigation is not itself a finding that those predicates were met here.
