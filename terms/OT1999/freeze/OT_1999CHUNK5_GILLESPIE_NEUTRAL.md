## Statutory text, qualifications and sources

Section 922(g)(9), effective September 30, 1996, covers a person convicted of a misdemeanor crime of domestic violence. Its separate conduct branches prohibit shipment or transportation in interstate or foreign commerce; possession in or affecting commerce; and receipt of a firearm or ammunition previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. The present dispute concerns possession. Section 922(d)(9) separately prohibits sale or disposition with knowledge or reasonable cause to believe the recipient has a qualifying conviction. Its knowledge term is not by itself a complete interpretation of the separate possession offense's culpability requirements. No criminal notice or mental-state defense is decided here.

Section 921(a)(33) requires a federal or state misdemeanor with an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed within the defined domestic relationship: current or former spouse, parent or guardian; shared child; current or former cohabitation as spouse, parent or guardian; or similarly situated person. Counsel or knowing and intelligent waiver is required. If the jurisdiction entitled the person to a jury trial, there must have been a jury trial or a knowing and intelligent waiver by plea or otherwise. This condition creates no new jury entitlement.

A conviction does not count if expunged or set aside, or if the offense was pardoned or civil rights were restored where the jurisdiction provides for their loss for that offense, subject to the statute's express reservation for a pardon, expungement or restoration that expressly prohibits firearm shipment, transportation, possession or receipt. The reservation does not separately repeat set-aside. No interpretive enlargement or individual eligibility finding is needed here. The official 1998 text begins subsection (a)(33)(A) with a cross-reference to subparagraph (C), while its listed safeguards are in (B); this packet preserves that source feature and invents no missing (C) exception. Qualification is undisputed in this case.

Section 925(a)(1) ordinarily exempts specified government-channel firearms and ammunition transactions and possession, but expressly excepts §§922(d)(9), 922(g)(9), and restrictions concerning §922(p) firearms from that exemption. Its channels include items imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for use of the United States and its departments/agencies or a State and its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. Public employment is not an exemption for every privately owned weapon. Section 925(c) provides discretionary relief upon the statutory public-safety/public-interest showing and review of an actual denial; its existence proves no funded process, denial, jurisdiction or relief entitlement here. No such application is supplied. Section 922(d)'s existing exception for specified licensees permitted to continue under §925(b), or recipients of §925(c) relief, remains distinct and supplies no officer exemption.

## Lower reasoning and controlling changes in this timeline

The Seventh Circuit sustained commerce authority because prior interstate movement satisfied the statutory jurisdictional element; it treated one historical journey as constitutionally sufficient. It rejected commandeering because the rule regulates individuals, federal officials enforce it, and state or local employers choose their staffing responses. On the arms issue it rejected any personal right independent of a substantial relationship to militia effectiveness, while recognizing standing. The district court had alternatively reasoned that the law survived even strict scrutiny because of its qualifying-conviction predicate and domestic-violence prevention objective. The appellate militia premise and automatic commerce-nexus premise cannot govern under the present simulated baseline.

The entering register **Personal arms protection, incorporation and necessary acquisition**, effective through Love (December 11, 1995) and Hale (February 12, 1996), recognizes personal invocation and keeps coverage separate from regulation. Wright (October 5, 1998) supplies the directly controlling **Core-Preservation Review**: Stage 1 coverage; Stage 2 practical-burden justification, including strict scrutiny of core deprivation, intermediate scrutiny of substantial regulation, deferential reasonableness for genuinely incidental burdens, and an independent boundary against abolishing meaningful ordinary defensive home possession. The complete standards and their qualifications must be taken from the slice rather than abbreviated into a general balancing formula.

Fraternal Order of Police (October 7, 1998) unanimously applied that framework to this qualifying disability and the official-use exclusion. It treated the restriction as substantial regulation of particular persons, upheld the important objective of preventing armed recurrence, and found substantial fit in the adjudicated violent-conduct predicate, domestic relationships, conviction safeguards and statutory limits. Work-related possession remains protected at Stage 1; a personal right does not itself require an official-use exception. It reserved genuinely different private-possession, classification, safeguard and restoration applications. Gillespie's supplied application falls within the same official-use setting; the mere individual rather than associational form does not itself distinguish the controlling rule. No hypothetical home record may be created to obtain a different standard.

The register **Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects** retains Lopez's sequence: identify the enacted class and a supported interstate mechanism before aggregation; statutory nexus words and remote movement alone do not establish constitutional eligibility. Hiley (October 9, 1998) specifically upheld §922(g)(9)'s recipient-possession class as fitted to prevent evasion or impairment of §922(d)(9)'s valid prohibited-recipient supply restriction under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. That mechanism is distinct from the separately necessary enacted nexus. Later Griffin/Langley (October 29, 1998) preserves the distinction and rejects adding an individual substantial/direct-effect element to the separate felon provision; do not transfer every §922(g)(1) holding automatically to (g)(9). Hale and Kirk concern the different machinegun stock and do not replace Hiley's specific recipient/supply ground.

Hiley also held that the possession/supply restriction does not commandeer a sheriff merely by affecting staffing: it commands no termination, legislation, prosecution, background checks or appropriations. New York and Printz retain their actual simulated limits; no need exists to extend their permissible-assistance holdings where the challenged statute imposes no state administrative assignment. Garcia's generally applicable-regulation principle remains, but valid commerce authority alone does not automatically resolve every Tenth Amendment objection.
