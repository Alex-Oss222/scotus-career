# Shrink Missouri: fresh neutral validation

Status: sufficient for independent modeling of the challenged statewide contribution limit. The packet's categorical evaluation of adequacy is not a neutral fact; the competing evidence below must travel with the handoff.

Reviewed Shrink Section I in `runtime/OT_1999CHUNK2_NEUTRAL.md`, the complete `preparation/OT_1999CHUNK2_SHRINK_NEUTRAL_FACTS_ONLY.md`, and `entering-law/OT_1999CHUNK2_SHRINK_FOCUSED.md`. The sanitized factual handoff supplies source pinpoints and identifies the character of the evidence. It is not a complete campaign-finance dataset or proof of causal effects.

The presented limit for the statewide auditor election was $1,075, derived from a $1,000 January 1, 1995 base with biennial inflation adjustment rounded to the nearest $25. The PAC gave $1,025 in 1997 and another $50 in 1998 and wished to give more. Fredman asserted inability to mount an effective campaign. Other office categories are not independently presented for adjudication.

The State relied on the Goode affidavit and accounts linking sizeable contributions to official choices and financial relationships. Preserve the distinctions among reported contribution amounts, allegations of influence, the inference drawn from those relationships, and an actual guilty plea concerning misuse of state property. Neither the reported $420,000 PAC contribution nor the plea alone establishes a judicial finding of a completed quid pro quo in every described episode. Actual corruption and its appearance are legally distinct supporting interests; vote equalization is not a substitute.

The district court found that candidates could raise enough money for effective campaigns. The seventy-four-percent vote for Proposition A supports the political context but does not itself decide constitutional validity. The figure that 97.62 percent of contributors to the 1994 auditor campaigns gave no more than $2,000 is a count of contributors, not the percentage of all contributed dollars below that amount and not direct proof about this $1,075 ceiling.

The contrary statewide evidence is material: total spending across five offices declined from about $21.599 million to $9.337 million, primary spending from about $14.249 million to $1.625 million, contested primaries from ten of ten to one of ten, and primary candidates from thirty-two to eleven. The comparison supports an argument about practical suppression; it does not by itself prove that the contribution rule caused every change. Nor may the evidence be omitted merely because the district court reached a contrary ultimate finding.

Actual Russell supplies a close-fit inquiry tied to corruption or its appearance and preservation of meaningful political association and effective campaigning. It rejected particular very low Arkansas limits while sustaining other specified provisions on their record. It establishes no universal constitutional dollar minimum or automatic safe harbor obtained by comparing nominal amounts. The model must examine the scale of the election, available fundraising, cumulative restrictions, indexing, evidence of need and actual burdens. Contributions and independent expenditures retain their distinct treatment; the Colorado Republican rule about actual independence does not decide a direct candidate-contribution cap.

The record supports competing assessments of evidentiary sufficiency and practical burden without inventing either a requirement of proved completed bribes or a rule that a declaration alone always suffices. The source-backed findings, contrary figures and their limits are sufficient for that legal assessment. Any invalidation must identify its presented category and ground; any validation must explain how meaningful participation survives the actual evidence. Refresh effective earlier current-term law before freezing commitments.
