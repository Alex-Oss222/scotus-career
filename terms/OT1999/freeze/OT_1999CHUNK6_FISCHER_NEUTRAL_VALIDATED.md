Review is by certiorari of the Eleventh Circuit's judgment sustaining the conviction against the federal-benefits objection. The granted question is whether the Medicare payments received by West Volusia Hospital Authority satisfy 18 U.S.C. §666(b). ## Record and scope validated from the clean objective extraction

Fischer was president and part owner of Quality Medical Consultants, a billing-audit business. In 1993 he obtained a $1.2 million loan from the Authority, which operated two participating hospitals and received $10–15 million in Medicare payments that year. The investigation concerned diverted loan proceeds, speculative investment losses and a $10,000 kickback to the Authority's chief financial officer. The jury convicted on thirteen counts, resulting in sixty-five months' imprisonment, three years' supervised release and $1.2 million restitution. Those particulars describe the lower case; the present grant does not independently reopen every count, element or sentencing determination.

The amount received and Medicare's status as a federal assistance program are undisputed. The legal dispute concerns whether the hospital receives its benefits rather than only payments for services benefiting patients. The $10,000 annual institutional-benefits threshold is different from the $5,000 property/transaction requirement. Section 666(c) separately excludes bona fide salary, wages, fees, other compensation and ordinary-course paid/reimbursed expenses. Neither that exclusion nor the enacted agent, property and corrupt-intent elements may disappear through an expansive account of federal purpose.

The Medicare program requires licensing, staff, quality assurance, records and facilities standards and supports treatment through payment arrangements addressing operating costs, education, capital and liquidity. Some arrangements permit periodic or advance payments and special treatment of particular providers. These are features of the program, not proof that this Authority received every payment category. The available record recitation does not establish its Part A/Part B allocation or receipt of a particular special subsidy. A holding dependent on a specific special payment would require that actual evidence; the case may instead be analyzed at the proved ordinary program-participation level. Nothing establishes litigant default from this source limit.

The official 1999 §§413.60 and 413.64 were read completely. They distinguish estimated interim payments and final cost settlement, requested accelerated payment under its stated conditions, and elective periodic interim payments subject to qualifications and recoupment. Those are contextual descriptions of the program near decision, not a finding that this hospital received any special arrangement in 1993. The 1999 text includes later amendments; a holding requiring a particular 1993 regulatory entitlement needs that version rather than silently backdating this edition. The objective record's ordinary program participation remains the supported factual basis here.

## Entering law and genuinely opposing paths

Salinas is current simulated law, unanimously stated by Thomas on December 2, 1997. It rejects a requirement that the presented bribe demonstrably affect federal dollars while preserving all enacted offense elements and §666(c). It assumed qualifying benefits and did not hold that every federal receipt is a benefit. The original packet's reference to historical Salinas as a comparator does not displace the controlling simulated decision. Good Samaritan and Regions Hospital describe lawful Medicare reimbursement systems and their respective cost/finality rules; they do not decide §666's criminal benefits classification or create deference to a prosecutor's construction of it.

**Government path:** The enacted phrase covers benefits under a federal program involving grants, contracts, insurance and other assistance. A hospital that participates under continuing federal conditions and receives payments sustaining the availability and operation of covered care can itself receive program assistance even though patients are also beneficiaries and payment relates to services. Assistance need not be a charitable gift, net profit or reimbursement untied to actual costs. The strongest objection is that every federal purchase advances a federal purpose and supports a vendor's operations; those ordinary incidents alone cannot supply a principled boundary for a criminal statute.

**Fischer path:** Medicare primarily insures eligible patients, and ordinary payment for their treatment compensates a provider rather than conferring on it the program benefit §666(b) requires. The express ordinary-course compensation and reimbursement protection in §666(c), read with the benefits language, reinforces a distinction between genuine institutional assistance and normal service receipts. Statutory breadth and residual lenity may matter if text and structure leave a real penal ambiguity. The strongest objection is that insurance and contracts expressly appear in §666(b), patient benefit need not exclude provider benefit, and a categorical purchase-of-services exclusion could erase genuine assistance administered through regulated providers.

A narrower path examines the substance of the actual program and the provider's role, distinguishing a recipient of institutional assistance from an ordinary supplier, without declaring all federal contractors covered or imposing an unstated requirement of traceable profit. Its strongest challenge is administrability and fair notice: program conditions common to ordinary purchasing cannot alone become an undefined criminal factor test. A further narrowing based solely on special subsidies is unavailable without proof that this Authority received them.

Section 666(c) must be interpreted in its own terms. It can be argued either as preserving lawful compensation transactions from criminalization or as also illuminating what receipts are ordinary payments rather than benefits. It does not automatically transform the alleged kickback or diversion into bona fide compensation, and it cannot be treated as irrelevant merely because the dispute appears under subsection (b). The independent model must identify its adopted function and legal consequences.

### Federal-program bribery without traced federal-funds effect

**Governing question:** Whether §666 requires a demonstrable effect of the bribe on federal funds.

**Current rule:** Section 666 requires no proof that the bribe demonstrably affected federal funds. Its actual agency and corrupt-intent requirements, acceptance of value to influence or reward, covered entity business or transactions worth at least $5,000, and qualifying federal benefits exceeding $10,000 in the relevant one-year period remain necessary. The $5,000 threshold concerns the covered business, transaction or series rather than automatically the bribe. Section 666(c) preserves bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expense payments.

**Authority by component:**

- Federal bribery — no demonstrable-effects requirement: Salinas v. United States, 1997-12-02; Thomas for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 1997-12-02.

**Open question:** A distinct statutory connection between the bribe and expenditure of federal funds, outer constitutional applications, the status of every federal receipt as a benefit, and intangible-benefit coverage remain unresolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Salinas v. United States — Federal bribery and RICO conspiracy elements.

### Federal bribery and RICO conspiracy elements

#### Salinas v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-738.
**Decided:** December 2, 1997.

##### Whether §666 requires a demonstrable effect on federal funds

**Holding:** Section 666 does not add proof of a demonstrable effect on federal funds to its actual institutional, transactional and culpability requirements. The Government must still prove agency, corrupt acceptance of a thing of value to influence or reward, covered entity business or transactions valued at least $5,000, and qualifying federal benefits exceeding $10,000 in the relevant year; §666(c)'s exception for bona fide compensation and ordinary-course expenses remains effective.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** The corrupt contact-visit arrangement in the federally connected jail permits rejection of traced federal dollars as an additional element. The $5,000 threshold concerns the business, transaction or series, not necessarily the value of the bribe, watches or truck.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A distinct statutory requirement connecting the bribe to expenditure of federal funds and the statute's outer constitutional reach are separately reserved; neither reservation establishes a nexus test or burden. Qualifying-benefits status is assumed here, not established for every federal receipt. The decision determines no precise annual amount or intangible-benefit coverage and does not reopen valuation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The two bribery convictions are affirmed on this statutory question without independent sentencing review or a new remedial remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Wells distinguishes genuine elements from additions without itself construing §666. Ratzlaf preserves contextual interpretation and residual lenity; neither entails the proposed demonstrable-effects condition.

##### Whether a RICO conspirator must personally commit or promise two predicate acts

**Holding:** Section 1962(d) requires knowing agreement to advance an undertaking that would satisfy the substantive enterprise offense if carried out, without requiring each conspirator personally to commit or agree to commit two predicates. The agreement must concern an unlawful object encompassing the substantive elements, including Reves's direction requirement; association, awareness, friendship or employment alone is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reves's substantive requirement does not automatically require personal direction of the enterprise by every conspirator. Rejecting the personal-two-act instruction objection approves neither the entire charge nor an independent personal-direction instruction. General sufficiency, harmlessness, aiding-and-abetting liability and a general overt-act rule are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The conspiracy conviction is affirmed on the presented question, leaving the distinct §1962(c) acquittal undisturbed and ordering no new trial or special remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shabani supports the distinction between agreement and personal performance within its own statutory scope. Reves remains the substantive enterprise rule and had left conspiracy open; its substantive object is preserved without transferring every performance requirement to each conspirator. Wells and Ratzlaf preserve actual statutory conditions, contextual meaning and residual lenity.
