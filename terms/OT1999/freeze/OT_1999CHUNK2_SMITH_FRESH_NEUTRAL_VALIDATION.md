## The actual procedure and individual record

Under the described Wende procedure counsel reviews the record, supplies a factual and procedural account with record citations, confirms the review and communication with the client, provides a copy and notice of the opportunity to submit a personal brief, and asks the court to review the entire record. Counsel does not withdraw and remains available for further briefing. The appellate court reviews the entire record even without a personal submission and orders briefing if it finds an arguable issue. It may affirm upon finding the appeal frivolous. The procedure's failure expressly to identify arguable issues or declare frivolousness is part of the dispute, not proof that the appeal proceeds with no lawyer at all.

The appellate-project consultation described in the source is not established as a uniform mandatory statewide safeguard. In Robbins's case, counsel did consult the Second District project and obtained permission to file the Wende brief. Preserve that actual fact without generalizing it to every appeal.

Robbins represented himself at trial. His own appellate submission raised insufficiency and Brady claims, which the appellate court found unsupported. The federal district court later identified arguable issues concerning law-library access and refusal to permit withdrawal of the trial waiver of counsel. It did not decide their probability of appellate success. Identification of an arguable issue is not a finding of a meritorious ground requiring reversal, deficient appellate performance, or outcome prejudice.

The district court treated departure from Anders as requiring presumed prejudice and directed a new appeal within thirty days or release. The Ninth Circuit treated Anders as the exclusive procedure, sustained the identification of the two arguable issues, and did not decide whether procedural departure alone would suffice without an arguable issue. It also remanded eleven trial-error claims whose disposition could affect the need for a new appeal. Those claims remain distinct from the procedural and appellate-assistance questions here.

## Questions to model independently

The State's strongest account combines retained counsel, counsel's factual work, client notice and submission, independent whole-record review, and further briefing if an issue appears. The opposing account questions whether judicial review and a neutral narrative adequately substitute for advocacy identifying possible grounds when counsel regards an appeal as frivolous. The constitutional question is whether this alternative supplies the protection required by Douglas, Anders, McCoy, Evitts and Penson, rather than whether it follows Anders word for word.

Procedure-wide validity and the quality of this lawyer's performance are separable. A conclusion accepting the procedure does not resolve whether counsel unreasonably overlooked a nonfrivolous issue in this case. Conversely identification of an issue does not itself establish that the whole procedure is unconstitutional. The Cronic/Penson theory of presumed prejudice requires an identified deprivation of the relevant assistance, with the retained-counsel facts addressed. The ordinary Strickland route requires separate performance and prejudice analysis. Actual Fretwell preserves ordinary reasonable-probability analysis and does not add a general freestanding unfairness requirement. Godinez does not decide the merits of the law-library or withdrawal-of-waiver issues merely because the original trial waiver occurred.
