## Common operative framework and separate components

All eight Associates adhere to the RFRA burden sequence adopted in Clearwater and retained in Swanner and American Life League: the claimant establishes substantial burden; the Government then bears production and persuasion that this application furthers a compelling interest through the least restrictive means. Their disagreement concerns whether the undisputed proposal and the tax authorities supply a sufficient legal resolution now. No Justice treats the word taxation, commercial participation, or Smith's constitutional neutral-law rule as an exemption from RFRA. No Justice decides federal RFRA's constitutionality, which was uncontested below and reserved by Boerne.

The following components remain distinct:

1. **RFRA and collection.** The present assessment and collection demand, including Adams's demand for nonmilitary designation or nonliability pending accommodation. The conceded interest in collecting taxes does not itself concede the least-restrictive-means question.
2. **RFRA and the failure-to-file addition.** The additional burden associated with timely ascertainment and filing, not simply the amount of underlying revenue. Section 6651(a)(1)'s exception requires both reasonable cause and absence of willful neglect. Boyle's ordinary-care rule and its reservations concerning genuine objective inability remain relevant. Religious sincerity alone does not establish either objective inability or statutory qualification.
3. **RFRA and the estimated-tax addition.** Section 6654(e)(3)(A) concerns casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstances making imposition against equity and good conscience, to the extent determined by the Secretary. It is not a general reasonable-cause exception. The separate subsection (B) requires retirement after age 62 or disability in the relevant taxable year or its predecessor, together with reasonable cause and absence of willful neglect; it is not claimed to apply here. The amount-and-duration structure of §6654(a) makes the Government's interest in timely receipt analytically relevant, but does not decide RFRA by its label.
4. **Ungranted independent grounds.** The lower court's statutory conclusions and its note-10 constitutional determination are left undisturbed at their own scope. A failure to establish a Code waiver does not dispose of the separate RFRA defense. Conversely, vacating an RFRA determination does not establish statutory eligibility or a constitutional violation.

All eight can assume the substantial-burden threshold for the present legal decision. Neither the existence of sincere belief alone nor a judicial judgment about Quaker theology is used as a substitute for that threshold. The five supporting remand do not foreclose an ordinary determination of any genuinely disputed threshold issue on remand; they do not require redundant evidence of an undisputed religious objection.

## Enhanced comparison: collection

**A. State arm and local policy identity.** The Pennsylvania DA office is not an arm of the Commonwealth for these office training, supervision and administrative policies. Pennsylvania's county-office classification must be read with the constitutional home-rule/optional-government exception and Philadelphia's consolidation, and with Specter and Duggan. Chalfin's City-officer result does not turn all of Chief Justice Bell's plurality reasoning into a holding. Section 205(a)(4)'s supplied repeal annotation is not resolved beyond what the office-structure question requires. No all-functions classification of prosecutors or all-officials classification of Pennsylvania government is adopted.

**B. Omission liability and causation.** A constitutional injury is necessary but insufficient. Carter must establish an attributable local policy or custom, a deliberately indifferent training or supervision omission, and the requisite causal connection between that particular omission and his constitutional injury. A failure to train must represent a conscious municipal choice in the face of actual or constructive notice of the relevant constitutional danger; negligence, an employee's isolated error, general supervisory responsibility and the hindsight claim that additional training might have helped are insufficient. The identified deficiency must be closely related to and actually cause the deprivation; mere employment and a bare opportunity for intervention are not enough.

Canton permits notice from a sufficiently established pattern or from a sufficiently obvious need to address a clear constitutional duty in recurring circumstances that policymakers know employees will face. It does not invariably require a prior identical incident. Perjury's obvious wrongfulness does not itself show an obvious need for specialized training against it. A known pattern of fabrication, use of false testimony or related misconduct could establish a materially different omission claim. Carter's conviction, later reversal, the witness's account and Ryan's conviction do not themselves establish notice, a policy, or causation. A policy concerning prosecutors also does not make police their subordinates: any theory premised on failure to train or control police requires legally and factually supported authority and causal connection. The disputed exculpatory-evidence allegation must be assessed as the alleged conduct and constitutional duty actually presented, not treated as proved or assimilated automatically to the perjury theory.

Walker may help explain how notice and causation operate, but its three-part formulation is not independently adopted as a new Supreme Court test. The elements are not finally established because the lower court characterized some Walker factors as readily satisfied. Their factual application remains open.

**C. Pleading, distinct defenses and remedy.** Rule 8 does not require Carter to plead unknown internal training policies with heightened particularity before discovery. The described allegations of a policy-related pattern, knowledge and omission suffice to avoid dismissal on the categorical grounds decided below. Their truth, specificity after discovery and legal sufficiency under an appropriate evidentiary record remain contestable. The City can obtain ordinary discovery limits and summary judgment if the required deprivation, policy, fault or causal showing fails. The Court neither orders discovery of an unlimited scope nor guarantees a trial, damages, a new prosecution or particular employment practices.

Municipal responsibility cannot borrow an officer's personal absolute or qualified immunity. Conversely, an allegedly deliberately indifferent municipal policy does not automatically defeat an individual's conduct-date qualified immunity. Buckley and Kalina require a functional inquiry: genuine advocacy retains its protection despite alleged dishonesty, while distinct investigative, administrative and personal factual-oath acts have the protection their actual function warrants. There is no dishonesty exception to advocacy immunity. These propositions prevent confusion on remand; the grant does not require a new disposition of every personal claim, a finding that every challenged act was administrative, or a ruling on individual-immunity pleading.

## Proposed general-program rule supported by five Associates

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer would hold that a public university may require a generally applicable student contribution to a broad program of independently controlled student expression serving its educational function when eligibility, allocation and review preserve viewpoint neutrality and do not attribute agreement or membership to dissenting students or burden their independent expression. Within such a program, the First Amendment does not require an individual ideological veto or refund merely because a recipient espouses a view the payer opposes. This permission concerns the defined educational private-expression program; it does not authorize compelled support for an official orthodoxy, a selected ideological campaign masquerading as a forum, or every privately organized advocacy expense wherever incurred.

Compelled financing implicates expressive interests even without personal attribution. Abood, Keller and Lehnert therefore identify a real burden, not a disposable analogy. Their justification and chargeability inquiry arose from an institution appointed to perform a bounded representative function, with other ideological expenditures outside that function. Here the University's common function is to maintain opportunities for independent and competing student expression, rather than to appoint one ideological representative for all students. Protecting that function through viewpoint neutrality does not require a student to adopt any group's belief. A rule permitting each payer to exclude disfavored views from this common program would change the program's basis from equal access into selective support by ideological preference. No empirical finding that opt-outs are administratively impossible is required or made.

This is an additional application of common-function analysis, not an assertion that Glickman already approved ideological funding. Glickman's actual holding depended on nonideological generic promotion within collective economic regulation. The different justification here is the educational private-expression forum itself, with neutrality securing its breadth. The limiting question is whether the expenditure belongs to that actual program under lawful educational, financial and accountability criteria, not whether the content agrees with the University's teaching or the majority's beliefs. A general educational label, shared benefit, absence of attribution, voluntary university enrollment or democratic authorization alone is insufficient.

Rosenberger informs the distinction between independently edited student speech and official advocacy, and forbids viewpoint exclusion within its actual publication benefit. Its express reservation of compulsory fees means that this case must decide the objection on its own; neither a previously established opt-out nor an automatic answer for every subsidy follows. The five do not require matching dollars for every viewpoint, universal funding of every organization, or expenditure unconstrained by reasonable program scope, financial responsibility, fraud prevention and demonstrated provision of the funded activity. A student's right to argue against recipients and to form or support competing groups remains intact.

On the stipulated ordinary allocation facts, ideological character alone cannot sustain the Seventh Circuit's categorical liability rule. The five would set that rule and the injunction resting on it aside and remand for the distinct referendum and any genuinely preserved issue about the program's actual scope or administration. No categorical approval of all off-campus activity is given: location alone does not make an educational activity ineligible, but a transfer to an external campaign does not become part of a student-expression program merely because the recipient calls it educational. Any necessary characterization must rest on the actual record and preserved claim, not an invented finding that every expenditure is educationally necessary or wholly unrelated.

## Supported alternatives and their strongest objections

**Require acquired source significance for product design.** Product design ordinarily constitutes the goods' appearance or utility that purchasers seek, rather than a separate sign designating origin. Even an arbitrary-looking combination can be attractive or recognizable as a kind of product without immediately indicating one producer. A claimant must therefore establish secondary meaning: the claimed design's primary significance to relevant consumers is to identify source, rather than the product itself. Once that condition is met, nonfunctionality and likelihood of confusion remain independently necessary.

The strongest objection is that the statute lists broad symbols/devices/combinations without distinguishing design from packaging, while *Two Pesos* already rejected a universal acquired-meaning condition. The answer is a source-function distinction, not removal of design from statutory eligibility. *Qualitex* already demonstrates that a category may be eligible but require acquired significance. Restaurant presentation and packaging can operate as source presentation apart from the goods' own desired design; *Two Pesos* left this different category open. No decision about every borderline package/product hybrid is necessary for these garments.

Functionality and confusion alone do not answer the source issue. Nonfunctional ornamental designs remain part of the product consumers may desire, and copying can reproduce that appeal without appropriating an inherently source-identifying signal. Confusion arising from the total circumstances cannot supply protectable distinctiveness if the asserted product feature has not become a source indicator. This is not a finding that these garments are functional, that all copying is innocent, or that copyright and state law offer no remedy.

**Permit inherent product-design distinctiveness on a demanding case-specific showing.** Samara's strongest position combines the broad statutory terms, the specifically articulated combination, favorable Rule 50 treatment of the jury's verdict, and an injunction narrowed to preserve ordinary design competition. A design might be so unusual that it immediately identifies source, and a rule demanding acquired meaning can expose an emerging producer to copying before recognition develops.

The objection that defeats this path for the commitments below is that a legal standard based on unusual appearance and designer intent does not reliably distinguish source signaling from the desired product design. Making the standard demanding does not identify the missing legal criterion; the source association must be demonstrated. *Two Pesos* accepted inherent distinctiveness of restaurant presentation for its limited question and did not adjudicate the category presented here. The cost of delayed trademark protection cannot make the statutory source function disappear.

**Reject this record without deciding every product design.** Judge Newman's view offers a narrower route: ordinary features and varying combinations cannot reasonably sustain this product-line claim even if rare inherently distinctive designs exist. This path respects Rule 50 only if it identifies legal insufficiency, not a judicial preference about aesthetics. All eight Associates prefer answering the presented category question, because merely tightening the present fact application would leave the recurrent product/source confusion in the legal standard. The categorical prerequisite also avoids an appellate retrial of how distinctive these garments look.

**Reject both impact-only channels.** Section 601's individual right is against intentional discrimination; §602 directs agencies and prescribes their means of compliance. The strongest objection is the longstanding integration of the beneficiary-protecting statute with implementing effects regulations, and the real role of regulations in Wright. **Different channels.** Failure to imply a direct action would not alone defeat §1983, and establishing one sufficient channel could make an alternative unnecessary to the disposition. Each commitment below nevertheless states its position on both presented counts. No Justice treats the implied-action inquiry as the §1983 foreclosure inquiry. An opinion may omit an unnecessary alternate holding at assembly without converting that omission into agreement with the opposite rule.

## Threshold paths and supported conclusions

**Continuing reviewability.** The City seeks first federal review of an adverse state judgment that has removed two ordinance provisions and disabled their enforcement. The injury is the present legal effect of that judgment, not an abstract desire to defend legislation or a conjecture that Pap's will reopen. *ASARCO* recognizes that a state judgment can supply concrete injury to the party first invoking federal judicial power where actual adversity and tangible legal consequences remain. The corporate respondent still exists and defends its judgment. The federal invalidation's continuing scope distinguishes an individual prospective employment action in federal court after the employee leaves covered work. The judgment can be reversed on its federal ground without deciding that Pap's would now have standing to commence a new federal action.

The strongest objection is that a continuing adverse judgment alone cannot preserve every case after the underlying dispute ends. *ASARCO* itself distinguishes moot appeals and requires a genuine controversy; *Arizonans* and *Laidlaw* reject fees, generalized interests and speculative recurrence. Here the state judgment actually severs the challenged law rather than merely protecting the closed premises from a prospective enforcement threat. On the reviewability path, that concrete municipal disability and the parties' adverse claim to the continuing judgment supply the requisite stake. The path neither invents a reopening plan nor declares every facial constitutional judgment permanently reviewable regardless of its effects.

**Mootness.** Pap's closure and sale, its representation that it retains no interest in any nude-dancing establishment, and the owner's retirement can establish the end of its prospective personal controversy. Mere corporate survival does not prove realistic recurrence. Fees alone do not supply a merits case. On this path, *ASARCO*'s original-standing rule does not extend to a business controversy that later ceased; the City's broad enforcement interest cannot substitute for a currently affected opposing claimant. *Laidlaw*'s demanding defendant-cessation rule does not automatically burden a plaintiff who leaves the regulated activity while the defendant keeps defending the law.

The strongest objection is the continuing legal injury to the City from a state, rather than federal, judgment. This path must answer that injury and cannot simply copy the federal-court dismissal disposition in *Arizonans*. State courts are not bound by Article III; a moot federal appeal does not authorize a command that Pennsylvania dismiss its action for lack of federal jurisdiction. *ASARCO*'s state-court discussion supports dismissal of federal review without erasing an otherwise competent state adjudication; any requested vacatur requires its own sufficient equitable basis under *Bancorp*.
