# United States v. Morrison / Brzonkala v. Morrison — fresh neutral validation

## Record, enacted remedy and review scope

Brzonkala alleged a September 1994 sexual assault by two fellow Virginia Tech students, emotional injury, treatment and withdrawal from the university. These are complaint allegations. The district court found a sufficiently alleged statutory claim against the assailants but dismissed it on constitutional grounds. The separate Title IX litigation against the university is outside the granted questions. The civil cause of action's constitutional validity under the Commerce Clause and Fourteenth Amendment §5 is presented; neither the alleged assault nor damages is adjudicated here.

Section 13981(b) declares a right against crimes of violence motivated by gender. Subsection (c) creates liability against the person committing the violence, including one acting under color of state law, with compensatory and punitive damages, injunctive and declaratory relief, and other appropriate relief. Gender motivation requires conduct because of or on the basis of gender and at least partly due to gender animus. The violence definition covers felony-against-person conduct and felony-against-property conduct involving a serious physical-injury risk within the referenced state/federal offenses, and includes conduct that would qualify but for the relationship of the actor and victim. No prior criminal complaint, prosecution or conviction is required. Gender motivation must be established by a preponderance; random violence unrelated to gender is excluded. State and federal courts have concurrent jurisdiction. The action does not confer federal jurisdiction over the listed state-law divorce, alimony, marital-property or child-custody decrees.

Congress documented effects on travel, employment, participation in economic activity, productivity, medical expense and interstate goods, as well as discriminatory treatment in state justice systems. Those are legislative findings, not adjudicated facts concerning every assailant or state system. The defendant here is an alleged private assailant. The remedy contains no case-specific interstate-commerce element and is not limited to particular States, an established state denial in the claimant's case, or defendants carrying out discriminatory state enforcement. The material question is what constitutional mechanism supports that enacted class and remedy, not whether the injury is grave or Congress compiled many pages.

## Actual law entering the two questions

Lopez requires identification of the enacted class and a constitutionally adequate interstate mechanism before class-wide magnitude can do its work. Economic/non-economic and traditional-state-subject labels are not categorical switches. Occurrence of similar harm nationally is not by itself that externality. Condon's actual information market likewise supplies no generic national-effects route.

For §5, the relevant constitutional guarantee includes equal state protection, while the Civil Rights Cases (1883) and United States v. Harris, 106 U.S. 629 (1883), supply the historical state-action baseline. That Harris is distinct from the simulated 1995 commerce case named in the entering-law slice. ## Strong opposing paths under the supplied record

**Commerce path supporting the remedy:** Congress can characterize gender-motivated violence as a pervasive barrier to women's participation in interstate labor, education, travel and service markets, not merely isolated criminal incidents. A national civil remedy can reduce that barrier and respond to failures of protection across States. Under Home Builders, proponents may attempt to identify an interstate spillover that individual States cannot internalize, or a necessary role within a valid interstate violence-related regime. The strongest objections are that the enacted class is gender-motivated violence rather than interference directed at a specified interstate market, that ordinary downstream costs are the very mechanism Lopez limits, and that neither nationwide prevalence nor the statute's placement in a comprehensive Act proves a qualifying spillover or anti-evasion necessity. A court cannot invent missing facts or recast the statutory class around the strongest economic example.

**Commerce path opposing the remedy:** The class and mechanism remain private gender-motivated violence, with effects mediated through injury, employment, health and consumption; the specified elements neither direct the violence at a commerce object nor connect it to an identified interstate scheme. The extensive findings may establish magnitude without curing that antecedent gap. The strongest contrary argument is that formal classification can obscure direct barriers and cross-border burdens that Congress reasonably identified, and that simulated law expressly leaves noneconomic conduct regulable under supported mechanisms. The inquiry must therefore examine those mechanisms and the findings' actual content rather than announce a categorical noneconomic exclusion.

**Section 5 path supporting the remedy:** Proven discriminatory state underenforcement can deny equal protection. A federal civil avenue can provide a substitute for biased remedies and prevent that constitutional harm without requiring each victim first to endure and prove a completed state violation. The breadth and persistence of the documented problem, burdens of proving state discrimination, and a civil remedy's relation to inaccessible enforcement support congressional preventive latitude. The strongest objection is that the action runs against the assailant for the private violence itself, whether or not state conduct is unlawful or deficient, and therefore may regulate the underlying private wrong rather than remedy the state constitutional wrong.

**Section 5 path opposing the remedy:** The State/private distinction remains substantive. A nationwide action against private perpetrators without any actual relation to state denial cannot be justified merely by findings that some state systems discriminate. Boerne's latitude within enforcement does not eliminate the right being enforced. The strongest contrary argument is that a requirement of individualized state failure would undercut prophylaxis and misread Boerne and Florida Prepaid; a constitutional remedy may reach conduct that is not itself unconstitutional. The model must explain why the defendant, trigger and practical remedial relationship do or do not fit the guarantee, rather than simply demand a per-case violation.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime, or when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

## Standards and Tests selection: Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

### Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement

**Governing question:** The scope of Congress's preventive and remedial power under Fourteenth Amendment §5.

**Current rule:** Congress may prevent or remedy constitutional injuries through legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently hold unconstitutional, but its means must be congruent and proportional to the injury addressed. Section 5 does not authorize replacement of the substantive constitutional right with a different standard. Examine the guarantee, threatened injury and practical statutory design for a supported remedial relationship; formal findings, a prescribed quantity of evidence, proof of a violation in every jurisdiction or individualized violation findings for every prohibition are not mandatory. Geographic limits and expiration provisions may illuminate fit but are not indispensable. Alleged customer loss from a State competitor’s misdescription of its own product does not establish a constitutional entitlement to prospective customers or truthful competitive self-advertising. The statutory tort and economic injury alone do not identify a constitutional deprivation to which the asserted §5 remedy is fitted. Established business assets, goodwill and legal claims are not categorically excluded from constitutional property. This limit concerns the missing constitutional connection, not a requirement that every prohibited act itself violate the Constitution. Patents are protected property, and Congress may prevent their unconstitutional deprivation through a congruent and proportional remedy subjecting States to private infringement actions: exclusive federal infringement jurisdiction and uncertain alternative State remedies support the judgment that immunity could leave unconstitutional deprivations without adequate redress. Ordinary infringement without a constitutional violation does not defeat that remedy, and its validity does not require each plaintiff to prove a constitutional violation; national uniformity and equal competition alone would not supply Section 5 power.

- Enforcement boundary and method: City of Boerne, City of Boerne v. Flores — I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right, Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-25.

### Commerce power and school-zone firearm possession

#### United States v. Lopez

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1260.
**Decided:** April 26, 1995.

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Holding:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the regulated statutory class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Connection before magnitude: aggregation may measure the effects of a class already connected to interstate commerce; it may not manufacture the missing constitutional connection. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market, but generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The original §922(q)(1)(A) school-zone possession prohibition fails at the connection stage: it regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime, and the chain from local firearm possession through crime costs, impaired education and reduced productivity to the national economy cannot supply that connection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court's sequence is: (1) identify the regulated statutory class; (2) identify the asserted Commerce connection or anchor; (3) identify the interstate mechanism that connects that class to commerce among the several States; (4) only after such a constitutionally sufficient connection exists may aggregation and substantiality determine the magnitude of the class-wide effect; (5) Congress receives substantial latitude over factual prediction and regulatory design once it acts inside the enumerated power; (6) there must be a reasonable, judicially identifiable fit between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked; and (7) a proposed theory must leave an enforceable boundary between the Commerce power and a general police power, so generalized downstream consequences cannot themselves supply the missing connection. The Court independently determines whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally adequate. The fit requirement is not strict scrutiny or a least-restrictive-means test, and the boundary inquiry is a diagnostic of the asserted mechanism, not an independent balancing factor. The rule is not an economic/noneconomic categorical switch, a traditional-governmental-function immunity, a mandatory-legislative-findings rule, a closed list of metaphysical Commerce categories, or a rule that every individual instance must substantially affect interstate commerce.

**Material application:** The regulated class is knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. It is not paid firearm delivery, interstate trafficking, interstate shipment or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that the handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs and need not dispute every empirical step of the education-and-productivity chain; neither theory explains why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States, and neither leaves an enforceable stopping point.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: preserves aggregation at its economic-class scope; the school-possession/productivity chain does not extend that holding to this stand-alone offense.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: preserves Congress’s regulation of an economic class without requiring each instance to have a substantial effect; its regulated commercial setting remains material.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguishes actual interstate commercial operation from remote-object history; its statutory breadth and reserved constitutional ceiling remain intact.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserves broad review of congressional commercial regulation without treating deference as a grant of general police power or imposing a universal formal-findings requirement.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: distinguishes compelled state administration from direct regulation of private persons; neither its anti-compulsion rule nor the traditional local subject alone decides this case.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: remains undisturbed in its generally applicable regulation of state activity; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No categorical exclusion of noneconomic intrastate conduct, reserved State subject area, universal formal-findings requirement or rule that only conduct physically crossing State lines is regulable is adopted. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism, but they cannot create the constitutional connection. No particular form of jurisdictional element is held automatically sufficient. The holding creates no Second Amendment rule and decides no amended possession statute, separate discharge offense or materially different federal firearms offense containing a commerce element.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Holding:** Congress may sometimes include intrastate conduct where inclusion is genuinely necessary and proper to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid interstate regulatory regime. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime. It cannot save original §922(q)(1)(A): the Government identifies no national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends on criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools, and the enacted offense does not define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court recognizes this route without fully defining it; fuller definition of its requirements is reserved for a case that actually presents it. Lopez does not formulate a separate interstate-externality doctrine: whether materially different intrastate conduct imposing direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize may be reached remains reserved for a later case actually presenting it.

**Later-authority backlinks:** United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996, Eighth Circuit No. 91-3830, supplies the first operational rule for the reserved anti-evasion/material-impairment route: Congress may include possession within §922(o)'s direct prohibition of additions to the post-May 19, 1986 civilian machinegun market when excluding it would materially impair enforcement of that valid transfer/stock regime; see Constitutional Structure — Federal regulation of economic activity. National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt, June 8, 1998, Parts II-B and III, resolves the interstate-externality reservation: the regulated class must generate a materially supported substantial cross-border effect that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition must reasonably fit that effect. Part II-A preserves the anti-evasion/material-impairment route; Part III requires material support for either mechanism.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Holding:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this charge. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. The stipulated facts that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas and that Lopez allegedly expected $40 for delivery do not become statutory elements. Because no commerce element is judicially inserted and the constitutional ground requires affirmance of dismissal under the enacted prohibition, the Court does not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than an indictment allegation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: preserves federalism-sensitive construction of ambiguous firearms language, without authorizing addition of an element absent from this unambiguous provision.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserves its clarity principle within permissible statutory interpretation; it supplies no power to rewrite this offense.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguishes interpretation of an enacted knowledge term from adding a new jurisdictional condition; its separate rules for defending a favorable judgment remain intact.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against substituting trial proof for a necessary indictment allegation; the renewed objection remains conditional and is not decided after affirmance on another sufficient ground.
- Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: supplies the grand-jury allegation boundary invoked against curing omission of a necessary offense allegation through later proof; that conditional indictment objection is left unresolved because the commerce ground is sufficient.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's reversal of the original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate are affirmed. No prosecution reinstatement, new commerce allegation, sentencing decision or merits remand is ordered. Supervised-release, special-assessment and conditional indictment objections are unnecessary to decide.

### Commerce authority over intrastate endangered-species takings

#### National Association of Home Builders v. Babbitt

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 96-5354; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1998-06-08.

##### I. The enacted taking class is not made economic by the projects' commercial features

**Holding:** For this challenge to section 9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of the reviewed development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas: five Justices. Ginsburg and Breyer do not join this part, although they join the remaining holdings and the judgment.

##### II. Two supported interstate mechanisms can authorize regulation of noneconomic intrastate conduct

**Holding:** Congress may regulate noneconomic intrastate conduct when its inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. Congress may also regulate such conduct when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover.

The second route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A's regime rule is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices. Part II-B's independent externality rule and its complete limits are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices. Ginsburg and Breyer join both rules as the appropriate inquiry given the Court's independently controlling Part I, while preserving their disagreement with Part I.

##### III. The Government must identify and support the mechanism; legislative latitude does not supply missing facts

**Holding:** The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. Courts independently determine whether the asserted mechanism is constitutionally eligible; Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive and empirical questions, magnitude and practical regulatory design once an eligible mechanism is identified, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** For the regime route, the Government must identify the valid interstate regulation and explain with support why excluding the challenged class would permit evasion or materially impair it. For the externality route, it must support each part of the cross-border mechanism and fit described in Holding II. General claims that endangered species are valuable, that people travel to see wildlife, or that research might produce future commercial uses do not perform that work by themselves. No universal formal-legislative-findings requirement, defendant-specific congressional-findings requirement, clear-and-convincing burden or numerical benchmark is imposed. The obligation concerns support for the invoked mechanism; it does not presume that every individual act must cross a state boundary or produce a separately substantial effect.

##### IV. The judgment must be vacated for application of the two routes to the existing record

**Holding:** Where the judgments sustaining these challenged applications have not adequately determined whether the existing legislative and adjudicative record supports either eligible interstate mechanism, the proper disposition is to vacate and remand for that inquiry. The lower courts must identify the mechanism actually asserted and determine whether the record materially supports its required connection and reasonable fit; neither categorical invalidation nor judicial invention of missing facts is permitted.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer: five Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Sweet Home requires significant habitat modification or degradation that actually kills or injures protected wildlife by significantly impairing essential behavior, including breeding, feeding or sheltering. Proximate causation and ordinary foreseeability remain required; speculative future harm or bare but-for linkage does not suffice. An omission requires an independently existing legal duty, and this decision creates no general habitat-maintenance duty. No actual injury, liability or permit entitlement is newly found. Otherwise lawful development alone is not an exemption. Section 10(a)(1)(B) permission remains confined to taking incidental to, rather than the purpose of, otherwise lawful activity. A conservation plan must state likely impacts, minimization and mitigation measures and funding, alternatives considered and reasons rejected, and other necessary appropriate measures. After public comment the Secretary must find incidental taking, maximum-practicable minimization and mitigation, adequate funding, no appreciable reduction in survival-and-recovery likelihood in the wild, fulfillment of other required measures and receipt of assurances. Those findings require issuance with necessary or appropriate conditions and reporting; noncompliance requires revocation. The substation's existing permit decides no entitlement concerning the separate intersection/corridor controversy.

The distinct threatened-species protections, section 7 agency and incidental-take provisions, scientific permissions, experimental-population provisions and enforcement channels receive no new application here. Sweet Home's reserved section 6(g)(2) question remains reserved; deciding its terms or application would require the operative official text and applicable State-agreement record. The Court inserts no commercial element into section 9 and announces no categorical immunity for traditional State subjects.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacated and remanded, 5–4; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer support the disposition. The existing legislative and adjudicative record must be examined for material support of either eligible mechanism; neither categorical invalidation nor judicial invention of missing facts is permitted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **United States v. Lopez, April 26, 1995:** Applied and developed. Its connection-before-magnitude sequence, enacted-class fit, independent judicial eligibility inquiry and no-insertion rule remain controlling. The Court resolves its open question concerning a materially supported substantial interstate externality; generalized downstream effects remain insufficient.
- **United States v. Hale, February 12, 1996:** Applied as the first route for necessary inclusion of local conduct in a valid interstate regime. Its anti-evasion and material-impairment requirement is preserved; statutory comprehensiveness alone does not establish it.
- **United States v. Rybar, November 24, 1997:** Preserved as an application of Hale to the demonstrated relationship between possession and prohibited machinegun stock; that relationship is not inferred for the ESA.
- **United States v. Kirk, February 14, 1998:** Preserved for the same fitted regime relationship and the absence of any judicially added interstate element; no machinegun premise is transferred to species protection without support.
- **Wickard v. Filburn:** Preserved within its properly defined economic-class scope. Aggregation measures effects after a sufficient connection; it does not manufacture the connection missing from a noneconomic taking class.
- **Perez v. United States:** Preserved for class-wide assessment of connected economic conduct without requiring every act independently to cross a state boundary.
- **Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Association:** Preserved for regulation of economic activity for environmental purposes; distinguished from treating economic features of particular projects as the enacted taking class.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon, June 29, 1995:** Applied and left intact as statutory law governing habitat-caused actual injury, ordinary causation and conditional permissions. It decided no universal Commerce authority over intrastate takings.
- **American Life League v. Reno, November 13, 1995:** Preserved for intentional conduct whose object and operation directly interfere with an actual interstate service market; that distinct mechanism does not establish all environmental applications.
- **United States v. Robinson, March 23, 1998:** Preserved for direct interference with operating commercial assets; the relevant market relationship remains necessary, rather than generalized consequences of local conduct.
- **United States v. Hinds, April 6, 1998:** Applied for the independent work of the enacted predicate and the insufficiency of an owner's economic associations by themselves. Its property-use construction does not insert a use element into the ESA.
- **United States v. Bird, April 13, 1998:** Preserved for direct interference with an actual service market. Its conduct-and-purpose mechanism remains distinct from the two noneconomic-conduct routes addressed here.

Sweet Home's habitat-injury and conditional-permission holdings remain at Environmental Law, Endangered wildlife and habitat-caused injury. The present entry concerns only the Commerce Clause applications.

### Gillespie v. City of Indianapolis — 2000-04-17

[Record](../records/gillespie-v-city-of-indianapolis_merits_2000-04-17.md)

#### Judgment & Remedy

The Seventh Circuit judgment is affirmed, 9–0, on all three granted questions. Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Dismissal stands on the governing constitutional grounds, without adoption of a militia-only right or an automatic constitutional rule based on past interstate movement. The judgment orders no discharge, reinstatement, reassignment, damages, restoration procedure or new hearing. The Police Department and Chief Zunk remain dismissed in their redundant capacities.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. No separate writing is filed.

#### Holdings

**Common authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's opinion is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt the following holdings, their qualifications and the confined affirmance.

#### The personal arms right permits this qualifying official-use disability

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Amendment protects individuals independently of organized-militia participation, including protected keeping and bearing for work, but section 922(g)(9)'s qualifying domestic-violence disability satisfies intermediate scrutiny on this developed official-use record. The adjudicated violent-conduct predicate, defined domestic relationship, conviction safeguards and statutory limits substantially connect this substantial regulation of particular persons to preventing armed recurrence; the personal right does not require an official-use exemption.

**Controlling explanation:** Love identifies the personal holder; Hale separates constitutional coverage from permissible regulation. Wright requires justification measured by the restriction's practical burden, and the October 7, 1998 Fraternal Order of Police decision applies that framework to this very official-use disability. Intermediate scrutiny requires an important objective, a substantial relationship to it, and a restriction that does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate protected activity.

Preventing armed recurrence is important. Gillespie's accepted qualifying conviction involves adjudicated violence against his former wife, not an accusation or unrelated misdemeanor. The statutory offense definition, counsel and jury-where-entitled safeguards and conviction-relief qualifications constrain the class. A service weapon remains available to its possessor; long service and governmental use do not remove the risk identified by that conduct.

The disability seriously affects armed employment and is not incidental paperwork. But an individual plaintiff does not create a different constitutional application merely by bringing the same official-use claim personally. No defect in classification or safeguards, ordinary home-possession claim or actual restoration denial is presented. The controlling FOP application supplies the justification that the lower court's militia theory omitted, so affirmance does not require another hearing to repeat an already resolved inquiry. There is no unconditional constitutional entitlement to this armed public job.

The Court does not permit disarmament for every misdemeanor or enlarge the statute to accusations or unrelated conduct. Wright's full practical-burden inquiry and independent prohibition of abolishing meaningful ordinary defensive home possession remain intact for genuinely different applications. This holding resolves no public-carry licensing, training or recertification question.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Section 921(a)(33) requires a federal or state misdemeanor with an element of use or attempted use of physical force, or threatened use of a deadly weapon, committed by a current or former spouse, parent or guardian; a person sharing a child; a current or former cohabitant as spouse, parent or guardian; or a similarly situated person. Counsel or knowing and intelligent waiver is required. Where the jurisdiction grants a jury entitlement, trial or knowing and intelligent waiver by plea or otherwise is required; no new entitlement is created.

A conviction does not count if expunged or set aside, pardoned, or subject to restored civil rights where the jurisdiction provides for their loss for that offense, subject to the express reservation for a pardon, expungement or restoration that expressly prohibits firearm shipment, transportation, possession or receipt. The reservation does not separately repeat set-aside; the Court does not enlarge it. The official text's reference in subsection (a)(33)(A) to subparagraph (C), where the listed safeguards appear in (B), supplies no invented exception; qualification is undisputed here.

Section 925(a)(1)'s governmental channels concern items imported for, sold or shipped to, or issued for use of the United States or its departments or agencies, or a State or its departments, agencies or political subdivisions. It expressly excepts sections 922(d)(9), 922(g)(9) and restrictions concerning section 922(p) firearms. It is not an exemption for every private firearm of a public employee. Section 922(d)'s separate exception for section 925(b) licensees or recipients of section 925(c) relief supplies no general officer exemption. Section 925(b) concerns specified importers, manufacturers, dealers or collectors under indictment for a crime punishable by more than one year, continuing under an existing license with timely renewal where required, during indictment and until conviction becomes final. Section 925(c)'s discretionary public-safety/public-interest relief and review of an actual denial establish no funded process, actual denial, judicial jurisdiction or relief entitlement here.

#### The recipient restriction fits the interstate supply regime

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(g)(9) may reach Gillespie's possession because the qualifying-recipient restriction is a fitted anti-evasion and anti-impairment component of the valid national restriction on supplying firearms and ammunition to that class, and the enacted commerce connection is accepted in this civil application. The Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses support that inclusion; the applicable statutory nexus remains separately necessary and must be proved in an actual prosecution.

**Controlling explanation:** Lopez requires an identifiable interstate mechanism before aggregate economic effects can support congressional authority. Hiley supplies that mechanism for this recipient class, and the January 18, 2000 FOP decision reaffirms it. Excluding the complementary acquisition and retention of weapons by prohibited recipients would materially impair the supply prohibition. Hale and Kirk preserve that necessary-inclusion method without establishing a general federal possession power.

The constitutional connection therefore rests neither on generalized domestic-violence costs nor on the firearm's past journey alone. The accepted prior interstate movement addresses the civil case's statutory premise; it does not replace the constitutional mechanism or supply an unproved element in another case. The objection that possession is local disregards its fitted relationship to the valid supply restriction. Conversely, statutory jurisdictional words cannot by themselves establish that every application is constitutional.

Section 922(g) retains separate branches for interstate or foreign shipment or transportation, possession in or affecting commerce, and receipt of an item previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Section 922(d)(9)'s knowledge-or-reasonable-cause supply requirement is not imported as the complete culpability rule for section 922(g)(9). This civil judgment decides no criminal notice, mental-state or proof question and inserts no missing nexus.

#### A staffing consequence is not a federal administrative command

**Holding and operative rule:** A valid federal firearm disability does not commandeer a municipality merely because its own job requirements make an affected employee ineligible for an armed position. This application of section 922(g)(9) directly restricts possession and commands no termination, legislation, conviction investigation, prosecution, firearm seizure, background-check administration, appropriation or other administration of a federal program.

**Controlling explanation:** Hiley and the January 18 FOP decision distinguish the loss of a lawful staffing option from compulsory government of others. Indianapolis requires every officer to carry a firearm. Federal law restricts Gillespie's possession; the City independently chooses its employment requirements and its response within the law. The serious employment consequence establishes injury but does not establish the asserted federal command.

New York preserves the prohibition of compulsory state legislation or compulsory ownership and liability. Garcia and South Carolina v. Baker preserve valid direct regulation of state activity, and Condon confirms that compliance work does not itself turn direct regulation into administration of a federal program against others. Printz/Mack addresses an affirmative command for confined temporary records assistance; its limited permission and reserved fiscal objections need not be extended here because this statute assigns no such task.

Commerce authority and the structural inquiry remain distinct. The staffing theory fails because the claimed administrative command is absent, not because every valid federal law necessarily defeats every Tenth Amendment objection. The Court decides neither an actual order requiring local enforcement nor whether state law required retention in a different position.

**Questions not reached:** The ungranted equality claim, abandoned fundamental-employment theory and waived official-use felon/misdemeanant comparison remain outside review. No home-possession application, restoration denial, conviction-validity or classification dispute, independent state employment claim or criminal charge is adjudicated.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Love is applied to personal invocation; its recognition of the holder supplies no immunity from justified regulation.
- Hale is applied to coverage distinct from regulation and to the necessary-inclusion method; its machinegun holdings do not replace the particular recipient/supply analysis.
- Wright is applied through FOP's specific substantial-regulation holding; its full practical-burden inquiry, home boundary and reserved applications remain unchanged, and it creates no automatic-remand rule.

### United States v. Lopez, No. 93-1260

Merits decision, April 26, 1995

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 8, 1994; decided April 26, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, reviewing 2 F.3d 1342 (September 15, 1993). The question is whether the original Gun-Free School Zones Act’s possession prohibition can sustain this prosecution under Congress’s commerce power.

On March 10, 1992, Alfonso Lopez, a twelfth-grade student, brought an unloaded .38 handgun and five bullets to Edison High School in San Antonio. The lower opinion reports a proposed $40 delivery for a gang conflict. The parties stipulated that an ATF agent would testify that the handgun was manufactured outside Texas. Neither circumstance supplies an element absent from the statute or an allegation absent from the indictment. State charges were dropped after federal prosecution began. Following a stipulated bench trial, the district court imposed six months’ imprisonment and two years’ supervised release. The Fifth Circuit reversed and directed dismissal, which the district court subsequently entered. The unresolved appellate objections concerned supervised release and the special assessment.

The charged original 18 U.S.C. §922(q)(1)(A) forbids an individual knowingly to possess a firearm at a place the individual knows, or has reasonable cause to believe, is a school zone. Sections 921(a)(25)–(26) include the grounds of a public, parochial or private elementary or secondary school, as determined under state law, and the area within 1,000 feet of those grounds. The possession subsection has no commerce element.

Its seven exceptions cover possession: on private property outside school grounds; under a license from the State in which the zone is located or its political subdivision, where the licensing law requires prior law-enforcement verification that the individual qualifies; of an unloaded firearm in a locked container or a locked firearms rack on a motor vehicle; for use in a program approved by a school in the zone; in accordance with a contract between such a school and the individual or the individual’s employer; by an officer acting in an official law-enforcement capacity; and of an unloaded firearm while crossing school premises to reach public or private lands open to hunting, with school authorities’ permission to enter. The lower court found none applicable. That is a finding about this prosecution, distinct from the statutory exceptions themselves.

The separate original §922(q)(2) forbids knowingly, or with reckless disregard for another’s safety, discharging or attempting to discharge a firearm at a place the person knows is a school zone. Its exceptions cover private property outside school grounds, participation in a school-approved program, the school-contract situation, and official law-enforcement action. Those four exceptions are distinct from the seven possession exceptions; discharge liability is not decided. Original §922(q)(3) preserves state and local laws establishing gun-free school zones.

Original §924(a)(4) authorizes imprisonment up to five years, a fine not exceeding $5,000, or both; imprisonment may not run concurrently with imprisonment imposed under another provision. Except for the authorized maximum, it treats the offense as a misdemeanor for purposes of other law. The Court does not apply a later fine provision to this charge, decide the reserved sentencing objections, or adjudicate an amended possession statute.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms the Fifth Circuit’s judgment, 5–4. The original school-zone possession charge cannot be sustained under Congress’s commerce power on the theory offered by the United States, and the dismissal remains in force.

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original §922(q)(1)(A) conviction and dismissal mandate | Affirm, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Leave the conviction reversed and the ensuing indictment dismissal in place. No new sentencing determination or merits remand is ordered. |

The four dissenters would reverse the constitutional dismissal and remand for the unresolved supervised-release and special-assessment objections. They do not approve every component of the sentence. The Court’s judgment makes those objections and the conditional indictment-nexus alternative unnecessary; it neither reinstates the prosecution nor supplies a new commerce allegation.

| Dissent | Breyer | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | Would reverse and remand | Rational substantial-class-effects ground sustaining the original offense without an added commerce element; unresolved sentencing objections remain for appellate consideration. |

The Commerce Clause architecture stated below is the reasoning of the Court, joined in full by the five-Justice majority.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The Commerce Clause requires a constitutionally identifiable connection before aggregate magnitude can establish federal authority

**Controlling proposition:** When Congress regulates conduct that is neither interstate commerce itself nor a channel, instrumentality, person, or thing in interstate commerce, the Court must identify the constitutionally relevant regulated class and the interstate mechanism connecting that class to commerce among the several States before aggregation and substantiality may perform constitutional work. Congress may regulate a properly defined class of economic conduct materially participating in or affecting an actual interstate market and may include intrastate conduct genuinely necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regulatory regime. Generalized downstream consequences through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, or public expenditure cannot themselves supply the missing constitutional connection.

**Authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela’s opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas: five direct votes for the proposition and judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Federal commerce power is broad but enumerated. The Court does not begin by asking whether the regulated problem is nationally important or whether Congress rationally believed that its consequences eventually influence the economy. It begins by identifying what Congress actually regulated and why that class falls within commerce among the several States.

The Constitution does not reserve permanent substantive subject areas such as education, crime, firearms, or family regulation exclusively to the States. A valid federal commerce power does not disappear because a State also possesses police authority over the subject. Garcia therefore remains undisturbed, and the Court does not revive a traditional-governmental-function immunity.

The converse is equally important. National importance does not itself create an enumerated power. Congress may not convert a general social problem into commerce merely by tracing its aggregate consequences through expenditures, insurance, education, employment, consumption, or productivity.

The analysis proceeds in an ordered sequence.

First, identify the regulated statutory class. Here Congress criminalized knowing firearm possession in a defined school zone. The class is not paid firearm delivery, interstate firearm trafficking, interstate shipment, or participation in the commercial firearms market merely because the record contains evidence that Lopez expected $40 for delivery or that his handgun had previously been manufactured outside Texas.

Second, identify the asserted commerce connection. Established federal authority includes commerce itself and its channels; instrumentalities, persons, and things genuinely in interstate commerce; and properly defined economic classes materially participating in or affecting actual interstate markets. Congress may also, through the Necessary and Proper Clause, reach intrastate conduct whose inclusion is genuinely necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regulatory regime.

Third, identify the mechanism connecting the class to commerce. The inquiry is not controlled by formal labels such as direct or indirect. It asks what makes the regulated class part of, an obstruction to, an evasion of, or a material impairment of interstate commerce.

Fourth, only after such a connection exists may aggregation and substantiality establish the magnitude of the class-wide relationship. Aggregation measures the effects of a constitutionally connected class; it does not manufacture a connection that otherwise does not exist.

Fifth, Congress receives substantial latitude over factual magnitude, prediction, and regulatory design once it acts inside a valid enumerated power. The Court independently determines the constitutional adequacy of the asserted interstate mechanism and does not substitute its own policy judgment for Congress’s once that boundary has been satisfied.

The original possession prohibition fails at the connection stage. It regulates no interstate transaction, channel, instrumentality, economic class, or identified anti-evasion component of a valid interstate regulatory regime.

The Government’s first theory runs from firearm possession to violent crime, from violent crime to economic costs, and from those costs to insurance, travel, spending, and national economic consequences. The Court accepts that violent crime carries economic costs. Those costs do not identify why this statutory class constitutes commerce among the States. Virtually every serious local harm eventually produces expenditures or changes economic behavior.

The Government’s second theory runs from firearms in schools, to fear and educational disruption, to diminished educational attainment, to lower workforce productivity, and finally to the national economy. The Court again need not dispute every empirical step. The constitutional defect is that this reasoning supplies no enforceable stopping point. Health, crime, family conditions, housing, education, public safety, and innumerable other subjects affect productivity. If that eventual consequence establishes the missing Commerce connection, enumeration no longer supplies a judicially enforceable limit.

The Court therefore adopts connection before magnitude. The question whether Congress has identified a constitutionally eligible interstate mechanism precedes the question whether the regulated class substantially affects commerce.

Wickard v. Filburn remains controlling. Homegrown wheat has an identifiable relationship to the wheat market because self-production substitutes for market acquisition and therefore participates in the market’s supply-and-demand mechanism. Aggregation establishes the class-wide magnitude of an already identified economic relationship.

Perez v. United States likewise remains controlling at its economic-class scope. Congress need not prove that each individual instance independently has a substantial interstate effect when the class itself falls within a constitutionally valid commercial regulation.

Neither precedent establishes that every local activity becomes commerce because its aggregate social consequences eventually alter national economic output.

The Court also adopts no universal requirement of formal congressional findings. Findings may support legislative facts concerning an identified interstate mechanism. Their absence is not automatically fatal, and their presence is not automatically sufficient. A legislative record supports a constitutional connection; it does not create one.

The Court further requires a reasonable relationship between the class Congress actually regulated and the mechanism invoked to sustain it. This is not strict scrutiny and does not require the least restrictive means. It prevents the constitutional mechanism from becoming a nominal justification for a statutory class whose actual reach is unrelated to that mechanism.

Finally, the Court asks whether the Government’s proposed theory leaves a judicially enforceable constitutional boundary. This is not an independent balancing factor. It tests whether the asserted mechanism actually distinguishes regulation of commerce among the States from the aggregate economic residue of ordinary local life.

The Government’s theory does not.

Original §922(q)(1)(A) therefore exceeds Congress’s Commerce Clause authority as charged and applied here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Wickard v. Filburn, 317 U.S. 111: reaffirmed at its economic-class scope. Aggregation remains available once an identifiable interstate market mechanism exists.
- Perez v. United States, 402 U.S. 146: reaffirmed. Congress may regulate an economic class without requiring proof that every individual instance independently has a substantial effect.
- Allied-Bruce Terminix Cos. v. Dobson, January 18, 1995: distinguished. Actual multistate commercial operations fall within a materially different category from the stand-alone local possession offense here; Allied-Bruce did not settle the constitutional ceiling.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264: preserved insofar as Congress receives broad latitude within a valid commerce power. Judicial deference concerning legislative judgment does not transfer to Congress final authority to define the constitutional boundary of its own enumerated power.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528: preserved. The Court does not restore a traditional-governmental-function immunity.
- New York v. United States, June 19, 1992: remains distinct. This case concerns the extent of Congress’s affirmative commerce authority over a private individual, not compelled State administration.

##### Intrastate conduct necessary to a valid interstate regulatory regime remains potentially regulable

**Controlling proposition:** Nothing in the Court’s substantial-effects holding prevents Congress from including intrastate conduct where the inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of an otherwise valid federal regulation of interstate commerce. That authority derives from the Commerce Clause together with the Necessary and Proper Clause and requires an identifiable relationship between the local class and the valid interstate regime.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela’s opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court does not convert the word noneconomic into a categorical constitutional exclusion. Conduct that is not itself a commercial transaction may nevertheless be regulable when it directly attacks an instrumentality, obstructs interstate commerce, or must be included to keep a valid interstate regulatory regime from being circumvented or materially impaired.

That principle cannot save §922(q)(1)(A) on this record. The Government identifies no particular national firearms-market prohibition whose operation materially depends upon criminalizing all possession within 1,000 feet of schools. Nor does the enacted possession offense define its class around concealment, retention, acquisition, trafficking, or another mechanism by which school-zone possession undermines a larger federal market regime.

The Court therefore recognizes the legal route but leaves fuller definition of its requirements for a case that actually presents the issue.

The Court likewise does not decide a materially different theory involving direct and substantial interstate spillovers that individual States cannot practically confine or internalize. No such theory is necessary to resolve school-zone possession, and the Court will not decide a future environmental, public-health, or comparable externality case in advance.

##### The original possession text cannot be saved by adding a new commerce element

**Controlling proposition:** The Court will not insert an unexpressed commerce element into original §922(q)(1)(A) to sustain this prosecution. Constitutional avoidance permits a fair narrowing construction of enacted language; it does not permit the judiciary to enact a different federal offense. Neither the firearm’s prior manufacture outside Texas nor Lopez’s alleged paid delivery becomes a statutory element merely because either fact might provide a stronger federal connection.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela’s opinion of the Court, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas.

**Controlling explanation:** Original §922(q)(1)(A) identifies possession, culpability, and location, subject to its express exceptions. It contains no commerce phrase capable of being interpreted into the connection the Government now needs.

United States v. Bass and Gregory v. Ashcroft support federalism-sensitive interpretation where enacted language admits a narrowing construction. They do not authorize the Court to add omitted statutory language.

United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc. concerned the reach of an enacted knowledge requirement. It does not authorize creation of a new jurisdictional element where Congress enacted none.

The stipulated interstate manufacture of the firearm cannot substitute for an element of the offense. Nor can the alleged $40 delivery transform the charged class into paid trafficking.

Because the Court refuses to insert a new nexus element, it need not decide the separate grand-jury question that would arise if such an element were judicially created and then supported only by trial evidence rather than indictment allegation.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Bass, 404 U.S. 336: applied as a principle of federalism-sensitive statutory interpretation; does not authorize insertion of absent language.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452: preserved. Clear-statement principles operate through permissible statutory readings.
- United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc., November 29, 1994: distinguished. Interpretation of an enacted mens rea term differs from creation of a new commerce condition.
- Stirone v. United States, 361 U.S. 212, and Russell v. United States, 369 U.S. 749: the conditional grand-jury issue is reserved because no commerce element is judicially inserted.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Wickard and Perez are reaffirmed at their economic-class scope; neither is overruled, and aggregation remains available once an identifiable interstate market mechanism exists. Allied-Bruce is distinguished: actual multistate commercial operations fall within a materially different category, and that decision did not settle the constitutional ceiling. Hodel’s latitude for Congress within a valid commerce power is preserved, but deference concerning legislative judgment does not give Congress final authority to define the constitutional boundary of its own enumerated power. Garcia is preserved, and no traditional-governmental-function immunity is restored; New York remains distinct as a decision about compelled State administration. Bass and Gregory operate through permissible statutory readings and do not authorize insertion of absent language; X-Citement Video is distinguished as interpretation of an enacted mens rea term. Stirone and Russell are not applied because the conditional grand-jury question is reserved. No precedent is overruled.

Effective April 26, 1995, the Court’s opinion establishes the controlling Commerce Clause architecture.

The original school-zone possession offense cannot be sustained by the generalized chain from local firearm possession through crime, education, productivity, or other downstream social consequences to the national economy.

The decision establishes that constitutional connection precedes aggregate magnitude.

It preserves Wickard and Perez for genuinely economic classes; preserves broad congressional authority over interstate commerce, channels, instrumentalities, and actual interstate markets; preserves Necessary and Proper authority to include intrastate conduct genuinely necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid interstate regime; and leaves materially different interstate mechanisms for future adjudication.

The decision adopts no general formal-findings requirement.

It adopts no traditional-governmental-function immunity.

It adopts no categorical rule that noneconomic intrastate conduct is beyond federal power.

It adopts no Second Amendment rule.

It does not adjudicate an amended school-zone statute, the separate discharge prohibition, or a hypothetical federal firearms offense containing a materially different commerce element.

Future Commerce Clause cases must begin with the regulated class and the asserted interstate mechanism rather than with the national importance of the problem.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela writes for the Court.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justices Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, dissents. The dissent would sustain the original possession prohibition because Congress could rationally determine that the class of firearms possession near schools substantially affects national commerce through violent crime, disrupted education, workforce preparation, travel, and related economic effects. It emphasizes class-wide rather than individual effects and substantial judicial deference to Congress’s empirical judgment. In its view, Wickard and Perez require attention to the class rather than treating one defendant’s effect as independently substantial. The dissent retains judicial review, a substantial class relationship and reasonable means, rejects a universal formal-findings prerequisite, and does not equate every imaginable consequence with a substantial commercial effect.

The dissent does not defend the statute merely because safe schools are desirable, and it does not rely on the handgun’s remote interstate manufacture as the constitutional basis. Its disagreement is principally one of decisional sequence and constitutional sufficiency: the dissent permits class-wide substantial-effects reasoning to establish the commerce connection, while the Court requires the connection to be identified before aggregation and downstream magnitude may perform that work.

The dissent reads the original offense without a new nexus element; on that reading the indictment omits no required commerce allegation. It would reverse the Fifth Circuit’s constitutional dismissal and remand for resolution of the remaining supervised-release and special-assessment objections, not approve the entire sentence. Its application is noncontrolling.

### City of Boerne v. Flores, No. 95-2074

Merits decision, June 25, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 19, 1997. On writ of certiorari to the Fifth Circuit, reviewing an interlocutory constitutional ruling accepted under 28 U.S.C. §1292(b). The question is whether Congress's Fourteenth Amendment §5 power authorizes RFRA's application to States and local governments in this permit dispute.

St. Peter Catholic Church sought a permit to enlarge its building. The City denied the permit under a historic-preservation ordinance it asserted covered the church. The Archbishop invoked RFRA. The district court ruled on February 1, 1995 that Congress lacked the asserted §5 authority; the Fifth Circuit reversed on January 23, 1996 for application of RFRA. The City seeks dismissal of the statutory claim. The question presented concerns congressional power, not the particular permit denial's burden or justification.

All nine Justices participated in the argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg.
**Opposing:** O'Connor, Souter and Breyer.

O'Connor and Breyer would obtain focused briefing and reargument before a final merits judgment. Souter would do the same and, reargument being refused, would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. None of these three votes is a vote to uphold RFRA. On remand the RFRA claim against the City must be dismissed. Separately pleaded and preserved Free Exercise, speech, property and state-law claims remain subject to their own analysis. No permit, exemption or damages award is ordered.

The Court declines reargument and decides only the presented State and local Section 5 question.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg | Reversal and remand | Entire opinion; enforcement power, RFRA application, prior-law effect and remedy |
| Concurrence | Stevens | No other Justice | Additional ground for reversal | Religious-preference Establishment Clause objection only |
| Concurrence | Scalia | No other Justice | Joins reversal and the Court's rationale | Separate defense of Smith; no additional Court holding |
| Dissent | O'Connor | No other Justice | Would order reargument | Smith critique; conditional §5 agreement only if Smith is correct |
| Dissent | Souter | No other Justice | Would order reargument; failing that, dismiss the writ | Reserves Smith and RFRA validity |
| Dissent | Breyer | No other Justice | Would order reargument | Reserves §5 validity even assuming Smith; does not adopt all of O'Connor's assertions |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Authority for all holdings:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. Six Justices directly adopt the following rules, limits and remedy. The opinion does not contain a separate historical reconstruction of the Fourteenth Amendment's adoption.

##### I. Section 5 permits prevention and remedy, not replacement of a constitutional right

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may enforce the Fourteenth Amendment through preventive and remedial legislation reaching some conduct that a court would not independently find unconstitutional. Its means must be congruent and proportional to the constitutional injury it seeks to prevent or remedy; the enforcement power does not permit Congress to replace the judicially enforceable content of the substantive right with a different constitutional standard.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 5 gives Congress substantial choice in making constitutional protection effective. South Carolina v. Katzenbach and Katzenbach v. Morgan recognize preventive measures that extend beyond adjudicated violations; City of Rome likewise preserves effective enforcement in its setting. Their breadth does not erase the distinction between enforcing a right and changing it. The Court examines the constitutional guarantee, the threatened violation and the practical statutory design. It asks whether the remedy bears a supportable substantive relationship to that injury, not whether Congress compiled a prescribed quantity or form of evidence. Congress need not prove a violation in every affected jurisdiction, make formal findings, or require individualized proof for each prohibited act. Geographic limits and expiration provisions can illuminate fit but are not indispensable conditions. The contrary position correctly emphasizes that prevention would be ineffective if confined to conduct already adjudged unconstitutional. The Court accepts that premise; what it rejects is an enforcement power without a limiting connection to the right being enforced. The independent Fifteenth Amendment power remains governed by its existing precedents and is not further resolved here.

##### II. RFRA's universal State and local command exceeds that enforcement power

**Holding and operative rule:** RFRA's general application to State and local laws cannot be sustained under §5 because it substitutes its substantial-burden, compelling-interest and least-restrictive-means standard across the field for the constitutional boundary retained in Smith, without the required enforcement relationship. The Fifth Circuit's contrary power ruling is reversed, and the RFRA claim against the City must be dismissed.

**Controlling explanation:** Smith remains the constitutional rule for genuinely neutral and generally applicable laws. Lukumi independently protects against objective religious targeting and substantial secular underinclusion; RFRA does not merely enforce those limits. RFRA demands a compelling governmental interest pursued by the least restrictive means whenever covered government action substantially burdens religious exercise, even under neutral and generally applicable laws. Its State and local coverage extends across subjects, governmental functions and rules without a limiting constitutional class, administrative setting or supported remedial problem that fits that universal substitution. The objection concerns the relation between its broad command and the constitutional wrong, not a numerical deficiency in legislative examples. Congress's disagreement with Smith cannot itself authorize rewriting the guarantee through §5. Broad preventive legislation remains possible when appropriately fitted to constitutional injury. The Court does not reconsider Smith, decide the permit denial's neutrality, motive, substantial burden or justification, or deny every other available source of congressional authority to protect religious exercise. Federal RFRA remains outside this case, including its rule concerning later federal enactments. No general hearing requirement or substitute exemption code is created.

##### III. The reserved power question limits future statutory applications without reopening completed judgments

**Holding and operative rule:** Church of Scientology and Swanner did not decide the constitutional power question and therefore do not establish §5 authority for RFRA's universal State and local application. Their prospective statutory applications cannot govern future State and local RFRA claims resting only on that power after this decision, but their completed judgments retain finality and their independent grounds and statutory reasoning retain force within a valid field of operation.

**Controlling explanation:** Both decisions expressly reserved RFRA's constitutionality while applying its claimant-specific statutory standard. Resolving that reservation does not overrule a constitutional holding that neither decision made. The superior constitutional allocation now prevents continued reliance on §5 alone for the universal State and local command. Concrete reliance on final judgments is protected by leaving them intact; general reliance on a future unauthorized federal claim cannot supply missing legislative power. The Court orders no blanket reopening, retrospective recovery or reconsideration of independent constitutional or state-law grounds. Distinct transaction or remedial reliance claims require their own lawful source and are not presumed. Agostini retains Lemon as the governing Establishment Clause test and updates it into one inquiry with three routes: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation receives the attribution-and-function inquiry. Purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within that inquiry, not freestanding prongs or an alternative test. Its overruling of Aguilar's categorical onsite rule and displacement of Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale concern a different Establishment Clause setting and supply no RFRA power ruling. Plyler's distinction between continuing decree terms and underlying federal rights likewise does not authorize Congress to redefine those rights. The remand leaves every separately pleaded and preserved claim for its own analysis and decides no permit entitlement.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Smith remains controlling for genuinely neutral and generally applicable laws; the Court neither overrules it nor decides its correctness anew.
- Lukumi preserves the separate constitutional rules for objective religious targeting, substantial secular underinclusion and demanding justification. Boerne's actual conduct is not classified under those rules here.
- South Carolina v. Katzenbach retains its recognition of effective preventive enforcement in its constitutional setting; its remedies do not establish an unlimited power to redefine rights.
- Katzenbach v. Morgan retains broad preventive authority tied to enforcement, without authorizing replacement of the substantive guarantee.
- City of Rome remains intact within its Fifteenth Amendment setting; no further independent Fifteenth Amendment rule is announced.
- Church of Scientology's constitutional reservation is resolved for §5-based universal State and local application; completed judgments and independent grounds remain intact, and its statutory reasoning survives where validly operative.
- Swanner receives the same prospective limitation; its independent and reserved claims are not extinguished.
- Agostini updates the governing Lemon test into the three-route inquiry described above, overrules Aguilar's categorical treatment of the safeguarded onsite public remedial service and displaces Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale. Ball's completed judgment and unaddressed holdings remain intact; Agostini supplies no RFRA source-of-power ruling.
- Plyler retains its rule for conditional termination under the original PLRA; its prospective-remedy reasoning does not settle congressional authority to alter the constitutional right.

#### Law After Decision

RFRA's universal State and local application cannot rest on §5. Congress retains preventive and remedial power with a congruent and proportional relation to constitutional injury; no universal requirement of formal findings, geographic limits, sunsets or individualized violations is adopted. Smith and Lukumi continue to define the relevant constitutional baseline. Federal RFRA, final judgments and independently available claims remain undisturbed. The earlier RFRA applications are limited prospectively in the invalid field, rather than erased or converted into prior constitutional holdings.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stevens concurs and adds an Establishment Clause ground. RFRA gives religiously motivated claimants a statutory exemption argument unavailable to atheists or agnostics asserting otherwise comparable secular interests. Applying Wallace's neutrality principle, he regards that preference for religion over irreligion as an additional constitutional defect. He does not assume that this church automatically qualifies for an exemption, condemn every religious accommodation, or decide federal RFRA's disposition in this State and local case. No other Justice joins this ground.

Scalia concurs in the Court's full operative reasoning and separately defends Smith. He rejects the claim that historical practice establishes the general judicial exemption regime proposed by the dissent. Protecting religious exercise from discriminatory treatment does not make courts general administrators of exemptions from neutral, generally applicable law. His additional historical defense is his own; the Court's enforcement holding does not depend on it.

O'Connor dissents from immediate decision and would order focused briefing and reargument on Smith, the neutral-law burden question, Sherbert and Yoder. She considers Smith wrong and would restore compelling-interest review of substantial burdens from neutral laws. She agrees Congress cannot redefine constitutional rights and conditionally accepts RFRA's §5 invalidity if Smith is correct. That conditional view does not supply a present merits vote: she would first reconsider the baseline. She grants no permit relief and does not now uphold RFRA.

Souter dissents. His doubts about Smith warrant properly focused briefing before either endorsing or rejecting it in a case whose §5 question depends on the constitutional baseline. He would order reargument; because the Court declines, he would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted. He does not accept RFRA's invalidity even on an assumed Smith baseline, affirm the Fifth Circuit or hold RFRA valid.

Breyer dissents and would obtain reargument on the constitutional baseline before deciding Congress's power. He shares the need to reconsider Smith but does not adopt every historical assertion in O'Connor's critique or her conditional conclusion that RFRA exceeds §5 if Smith is correct. He reserves RFRA's validity under either baseline and does not claim Congress may simply redefine rights.

#### Source Notes

The interlocutory posture and January 23, 1996 judgment are reported in Flores v. City of Boerne, 73 F.3d 1352. RFRA, 42 U.S.C. §§2000bb–2000bb-4, supplies the statutory standard. [The public recitations at 521 U.S. 507](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/95-2074.ZS.html) support the permit background, identifiers and argument date. The power ruling depends on the statute's operation and constitutional scope, not a new empirical inventory of legislative violations. The permit dispute's factual merits remain unresolved.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Fifth Circuit for dismissal of the RFRA claim against the City. Independently pleaded and preserved Free Exercise, speech, property or state-law claims retain their own legal analysis. The Court determines no right to a building permit, exemption, damages or new evidentiary hearing. Completed judgments in earlier cases and federal RFRA are not reopened.

§ 13981. Civil rights 
(a) Purpose 
Pursuant to the affirmative power of Congress 
to enact this part under section 5 of the Four-
teenth Amendment to the Constitution, as well 
as under section 8 of Article I of the Constitu-
tion, it is the purpose of this part to protect the 
civil rights of victims of gender motivated vio-
lence and to promote public safety, health, and 
activities affecting interstate commerce by es-
tablishing a Federal civil rights cause of action 
for victims of crimes of violence motivated by 
gender. 
(b) Right to be free from crimes of violence 
All persons within the United States shall 
have the right to be free from crimes of violence 
motivated by gender (as defined in subsection 
(d) of this section). 
(c) Cause of action 
A person (including a person who acts under 
color of any statute, ordinance, regulation, cus-
tom, or usage of any State) who commits a 
crime of violence motivated by gender and thus 
deprives another of the right declared in sub-
section (b) of this section shall be liable to the 
party injured, in an action for the recovery of 
compensatory and punitive damages, injunctive 
and declaratory relief, and such other relief as a 
court may deem appropriate. 
(d) Definitions 
For purposes of this section— 
(1) the term ‘‘crime of violence motivated by 
gender’’ means a crime of violence committed 
because of gender or on the basis of gender, 
and due, at least in part, to an animus based 
on the victim’s gender; and 
(2) the term ‘‘crime of violence’’ means— 1 
(A) an act or series of acts that would con-
stitute a felony against the person or that 
would constitute a felony against property if 
the conduct presents a serious risk of phys-
ical injury to another, and that would come 
within the meaning of State or Federal of-
fenses described in section 16 of title 18, 
whether or not those acts have actually re-
sulted in criminal charges, prosecution, or 
conviction and whether or not those acts 
were committed in the special maritime, 
territorial, or prison jurisdiction of the 
United States; and 
(B) includes an act or series of acts that 
would constitute a felony described in sub-
paragraph (A) but for the relationship be-
tween the person who takes such action and 
the individual against whom such action is 
taken. 
(e) Limitation and procedures 
(1) Limitation 
Nothing in this section entitles a person to 
a cause of action under subsection (c) of this 
section for random acts of violence unrelated 
to gender or for acts that cannot be dem-
onstrated, by a preponderance of the evidence, 
to be motivated by gender (within the mean-
ing of subsection (d) of this section). 
(2) No prior criminal action 
Nothing in this section requires a prior 
criminal complaint, prosecution, or conviction 
to establish the elements of a cause of action 
under subsection (c) of this section. 
(3) Concurrent jurisdiction 
The Federal and State courts shall have con-
current jurisdiction over actions brought pur-
suant to this part. 
(4) Supplemental jurisdiction 
Neither section 1367 of title 28 nor subsection 
(c) of this section shall be construed, by rea-
son of a claim arising under such subsection, 
to confer on the courts of the United States 
jurisdiction over any State law claim seeking 
the establishment of a divorce, alimony, equi-
table distribution of marital property, or child 
custody decree.
