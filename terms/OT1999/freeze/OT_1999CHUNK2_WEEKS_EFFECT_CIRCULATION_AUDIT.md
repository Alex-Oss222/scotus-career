## Formulation sustained, with its exact limits

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer can support vacatur of the reviewed denial on the response claim and the following confined return:

1. The constitutional error determination and rejection of the actual presentation/default and old-rule barriers remain settled. The receiving court may not reconsider them without restriction.
2. The receiving court independently applies both enacted Section 2254(d)(1) alternatives to the November 4, 1994 state merits adjudication using the appropriate earlier Supreme Court law and state record. Constitutional error alone establishes neither alternative. Denial remains available if neither is satisfied.
3. If statutory eligibility is established, the receiving court must apply the governing harmlessness standard, Brecht's general rule, and perform a substantive whole-record inquiry into the error's causal relationship to the actual sentence. That rule places on the State the burden of establishing beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the sentence; a generic harmlessness label does not replace that work.
4. Brecht's general rule already governs preserved constitutional trial error properly reached on federal habeas, as O'Neal, Roy and Coleman applied it; the return refers to that governing standard without restating it, and the rule already reaches this error. No historical Brecht substantial-effect default is adopted. The return does not adopt any separate writing's contrary preference or authorize free choice between standards.
5. There is no present finding of harmlessness or harmfulness and no present writ or sentence vacatur. An otherwise available violation that warrants relief under the governing effect standard requires conditional penalty relief, with a reasonable prospective opportunity for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital disposition. Guilt and unrelated claims remain undisturbed. No fixed deadline, hearing, new-evidence order, release, execution authority or stay follows.

The statutory issue is a genuine unresolved prerequisite that can end the claim. No accepted lower-court causal-effect determination supports immediate relief. Those features give the return legal work independent of any desired vote count. The reference to the governing harmlessness standard is not a bare direction to apply an unspecified rule, because Brecht's general rule already controls.

The neutral law supplement's demand to identify the standard, burden and causal task is met by Brecht's rule, which places noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt on the State and requires whole-record review. It supplies no holding requiring this Court to apply that rule to the record before the independent statutory condition is resolved. This proposed mandate leaves application of the governing standard as remaining legal work; it does not purport to decide effect now. The receiving court must apply that rule before adjudicating effect and provide a legal basis for its conclusion.

The frozen reference to the governing harmlessness standard identifies Brecht's general rule and authorizes no default to a different standard. The proposed formulation makes explicit that application remains for the receiving court if the statutory inquiry permits relief, and it refers to that standard without restating it. Section 8 permits this operative formulation where each Justice's own sources support it. The compatible joins below are identified inferences from those sources, not historical conference facts or claims that identical wording previously received assent.

## No synthetic substantial-effect rule

Stevens and Kennedy's genuine-grave-doubt rule and Scalia and Thomas's prisoner-borne rule cannot be merged by describing both as substantial-and-injurious-effect review. In an evenly balanced case the former prevents harmlessness while the latter denies relief. Burden and uncertainty allocation are operative parts of the rule, not removable qualifications. Agreement that convincingly established substantial injury warrants relief in an otherwise available case does not supply the rule for the disputed cases or authorize importing one allocation into the other's join.

The three remanding Chapman proponents do not accept a substantial-effect threshold; State-borne noncontribution beyond reasonable doubt is the inquiry Brecht's general rule requires. O'Connor supports it only conditionally while voting to affirm on two independent grounds. These conditional positions are not remand votes, and combining the incompatible substantial-effect approaches cannot displace Brecht's rule. The supplied eight-Associate commitments contain four conditional statements of Brecht's rule and four conditional substantial-influence positions adhering to Brecht dissents, with the latter divided on uncertainty. This audit has no basis to supply a ninth vote.

Nor could a Marks synthesis supply a different rule. Only Stevens among the four substantial-influence proponents supports the actual vacatur/remand. The other three affirm on independently sufficient grounds. Their unneeded conditional views are not a set of narrower judgment-supporting rationales for this remand. Direct doctrinal joins can sometimes cross judgment coalitions, as Coleman Part II demonstrates, but there must still be agreement on the same operative proposition. There is none here on any alternative effect standard, burden and uncertainty rule.

There is a supported narrower methodological agreement: identifying Boyde error is distinct from determining causal effect; the latter requires substantive consideration of the appropriate whole record under Brecht's standard, burden and uncertainty treatment, without indispensable words or a separate heading. Coleman Part III already supplies that principle through the eight Associates' direct joins. Brecht's general rule supplies the standard that principle serves.
