## Formulation sustained, with its exact limits

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer can support vacatur of the reviewed denial on the response claim and the following confined return:

1. The constitutional error determination and rejection of the actual presentation/default and old-rule barriers remain settled. The receiving court may not reconsider them without restriction.
2. The receiving court independently applies both enacted Section 2254(d)(1) alternatives to the November 4, 1994 state merits adjudication using the appropriate earlier Supreme Court law and state record. Constitutional error alone establishes neither alternative. Denial remains available if neither is satisfied.
3. If statutory eligibility is established, the receiving court must resolve the still-open question of the governing error-effect standard under applicable law and perform a substantive whole-record inquiry into the error's causal relationship to the actual sentence. Its selected rule must identify the burden and treatment of uncertainty; a generic harmlessness label does not do that work.
4. This Court does not now decide whether Chapman extends to this jury-response category. Brecht, O'Neal, Roy and Coleman retain their defined holdings. No blanket historical Brecht default or automatic Chapman extension is adopted. The reservation does not reject either proposed approach on its merits or authorize free choice between standards without legal justification.
5. There is no present finding of harmlessness or harmfulness and no present writ or sentence vacatur. An otherwise available violation that warrants relief under the lawfully resolved effect standard requires conditional penalty relief, with a reasonable prospective opportunity for constitutionally adequate sentencing or a lawful noncapital disposition. Guilt and unrelated claims remain undisturbed. No fixed deadline, hearing, new-evidence order, release, execution authority or stay follows.

The statutory issue is a genuine unresolved prerequisite that can end the claim. No accepted lower-court causal-effect determination supports immediate relief. Those features give the reservation legal work independent of any desired vote count. A bare direction to apply an unspecified standard, while implying that one already controls, would not suffice.

The neutral law supplement's demand to identify the standard, burden and causal task bars that silent ambiguity. It supplies no holding requiring this Court to settle every contingent effect question before the independent statutory condition is resolved. This proposed mandate expressly identifies selection of the still-unsettled standard as remaining legal work; it does not purport to apply a complete effect rule now. The receiving court must resolve that question before adjudicating effect, preserving the defined existing holdings and providing a legal basis for its conclusion.

The frozen reference to the standard actually adopted by the eventual Court does not itself select a standard or authorize a default when none is adopted. The proposed instruction changes that part of the remand expressly: selection and application remain for the receiving court if the statutory inquiry permits relief. Section 8 permits this materially different operative formulation where each Justice's own sources support it. The compatible joins below are identified inferences from those sources, not historical conference facts or claims that identical wording previously received assent.

For Stevens, the corresponding preference is a presently specified substantial-influence/grave-doubt rule. The revised formulation answers the join barrier by adopting neither competing rule and requiring actual resolution of the legal question before effect is adjudicated. ## No synthetic substantial-effect rule

Stevens and Kennedy's genuine-grave-doubt rule and Scalia and Thomas's prisoner-borne rule cannot be merged by describing both as substantial-and-injurious-effect review. In an evenly balanced case the former prevents harmlessness while the latter denies relief. Burden and uncertainty allocation are operative parts of the rule, not removable qualifications. Agreement that convincingly established substantial injury warrants relief in an otherwise available case does not supply the rule for the disputed cases or authorize importing one allocation into the other's join.

The three remanding Chapman proponents do not accept a substantial-effect threshold; State-borne noncontribution beyond reasonable doubt is their proposed inquiry. O'Connor supports it only conditionally while voting to affirm on two independent grounds. Neither counting these conditional positions as remand votes nor combining the incompatible substantial-effect approaches establishes a common controlling effect rule. The supplied eight-Associate commitments contain four conditional Chapman supporters and four conditional substantial-influence supporters, with the latter divided on uncertainty. This audit has no basis to supply a ninth vote.

Nor does a Marks synthesis repair the problem. Only Stevens among the four substantial-influence proponents supports the actual vacatur/remand. The other three affirm on independently sufficient grounds. Their unneeded conditional views are not a set of narrower judgment-supporting rationales for this remand. Direct doctrinal joins can sometimes cross judgment coalitions, as Coleman Part II demonstrates, but there must still be agreement on the same operative proposition. There is none here on a complete effect standard, burden and uncertainty rule.

There is a supported narrower methodological agreement: identifying Boyde error is distinct from determining causal effect; the latter requires substantive consideration of the appropriate whole record under the lawfully selected standard, burden and uncertainty treatment, without indispensable words or a separate heading. Coleman Part III already supplies that principle through the eight Associates' direct joins. Preserving that principle does not select the still-unresolved standard for this category.
