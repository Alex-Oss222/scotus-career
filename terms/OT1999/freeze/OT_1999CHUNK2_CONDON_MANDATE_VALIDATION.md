# Condon — narrow no-Stone mandate validation

**Event:** Reno v. Condon, No. 98-1464; January 12, 2000.
**Result:** The proposed direction to dissolve the permanent injunction and enter judgment for the United States **on the facial Commerce Clause and structural federalism challenge**, following reversal and remand, is compatible with all eight frozen Associate commitments. No narrower disposition or new unresolved Associate commitment is required.

## Scope and materials

This audit received only the proposed public mandate and the already authorized neutral record, frozen two-case Associate commitments and frozen reconciliation. It reread Condon's posture, C-a through C-c, individual join boundaries, reconciled remedy and statutory timing limits. No Stone supplement, control material or current Decision Record was read. No new source, fact, historical comparison, vote or assignment was introduced. The event-time research cutoff remains January 12, 2000.

## Basis

The established injunction is the permanent restraint against DPPA enforcement against South Carolina and its officers accompanying the summary judgment under review. The neutral record identifies the federalism determination as its basis; no distinct independently sustained ground for that injunction is established. All eight Associates have already rejected the challenged Commerce Clause and structural grounds on the merits, found the record sufficient for those questions, and joined C-c's conclusion that those grounds cannot sustain either the summary judgment or the permanent injunction.

The more explicit directions implement that completed merits resolution. Dissolving this injunction removes relief resting on the rejected facial challenge. Entering judgment for the United States on the two identified grounds gives the lower court the consequence of the common holdings; it does not enter judgment on every conceivable DPPA claim or enforcement issue. An open-ended factual remand on those already decided grounds is unnecessary. The reservation for any distinct issue properly remaining preserves the frozen scope without asserting that such an issue actually exists.

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer each join this implementation of C-a, C-b and C-c. None of their frozen objections requires preserving the injunction on the rejected grounds, awaiting new facts about those grounds, or withholding judgment on the facial challenge. Their distinct privacy, speech, state-assistance and treasury-liability positions remain outside this mandate.

## Limits carried by the proposed language

The approval is of the identified injunction and the two resolved facial grounds. It is not approval of a general declaration that every DPPA application, remedy or later amendment is constitutional. Section 350's actual staggered temporal provisions remain effective; dissolution neither accelerates a statutory duty nor establishes a legislative-session fact, expired deadline or present South Carolina opt-in obligation. No statutory violation, sanction, funding-condition judgment or disclosure-specific liability is decided. The mandate does not adjudicate or extinguish a distinct issue properly remaining below.

These are the limits the proposed mandate already states. No new conditional vote, changed substantive ground or historical departure results. The text is a permissible specification of the frozen remedy, not a new adjudication. This validation performs no Stone-compatibility or final Record/public-render audit and represents no Git action.
