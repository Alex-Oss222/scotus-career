## Paul P. v. Verniero — December 6, 1999

**Correct entering premise.** Doe v. SEPTA assumed an interest and rejected that particular internal-use claim; it created neither a general constitutional information right nor a seven-factor formula. Sheets recognized a confined substantive protection for unjustified official dissemination of intimate marital and family information. W.P. rests separately on New Jersey's substantive disclosure limits and requires ordinary advance review and State persuasion; its general plan-proof quantum remains unresolved. The present address-privacy question is therefore an extension question, not automatic application of an existing universal right.

**P1, limited substantive protection.** The State's compelled collection and deliberate dissemination of compiled, nonpublic current home-address information implicates a limited personal privacy interest in the exposure of the home and family to outside contact. It is not a secrecy entitlement in public conviction facts, every identifying datum, every address available elsewhere, or all government-held information. The interest does not bar public-safety notification, but requires a sufficient governmental connection between the actual information, recipient audience and protective purpose. Public availability bears on the interest; mere theoretical inspectability does not establish prior dissemination.

**P2, notification and safeguards.** New Jersey has a substantial protective interest in communicating accurate risk information to persons with a concrete safety need. Properly tailored Tier Two and Tier Three notification can serve that interest. Recognition of that objective alone does not justify unnecessary dissemination or redistribution of nonpublic residential information. Safeguards must reasonably serve the system's actual protective function and constrain unnecessary redisclosure; no perfect-containment guarantee, universal least-restrictive-means rule, prescribed assurance form, particular new proof quantum or seven-factor constitutional checklist is adopted. The existing W.P. requirements remain independently controlling at their actual scope.

**P3, disposition.** Affirm the Third Circuit's limited recognition of the residential privacy interest and public-safety objective, as confined by P1–P2, and retain its remand to consider adequate safeguards. Do not prohibit all notification, adjudicate unproved leakage or misconduct, prescribe a statewide distribution system, or reopen W.P.'s punishment and procedural judgments.

Civil Rule 62(d), as in force here, permits an appeal stay by an approved supersedeas bond, and Rule 62(g) preserves appellate stay authority. The existence and terms of an actual applicable stay must be established; an unresolved appeal is not by itself an invented stay, and a reservation of merits is not an execution order. No new stay, accelerated mandate, payment date, interest determination or security condition is added.
