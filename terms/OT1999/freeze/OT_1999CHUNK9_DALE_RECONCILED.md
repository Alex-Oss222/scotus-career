## Historical source verification and surviving premises

The [Cornell syllabus](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-699.ZS.html) identifies all three writings. The separate [Court opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-699.ZO.html), [Stevens dissent](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-699.ZD.html) and [Souter dissent](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/99-699.ZD1.html) were retrieved separately. The complete [official reporter, 530 U.S. 640-702](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-530/pdf/USREPORTS-530-640.pdf), including every writing, appendix and footnote, was read. Historical disposition: reverse and remand, 5-4; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas joined the Court; Stevens dissented with Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Souter separately dissented with Ginsburg and Breyer. This historical topology is evidence, not an assignment instruction.

The historical Court accepted protected instruction through adult example, deferred to the organization's account of its values and expressive impairment, and independently concluded that this adult leader's compelled inclusion materially impaired those values. It expressly rejected exemption by mere assertion, required neither a founding purpose of advocating this particular position nor unanimous agreement, and distinguished a leader publicly identified with the contrary position from a member who disagrees with policy. Roberts supplied the compelling-interest/less-restrictive-means formulation; O'Brien's incidental-regulation approach did not govern this direct interference. The asserted state interests did not justify this intrusion. See 530 U.S. 648-660.

Stevens's dissent did more than favor equal access in an abstract balance. It required an actually adopted, unequivocally advocated position and a demonstrated serious burden on the association's collective expression; neither some expressive activity nor an exclusion policy establishes the necessary connection. It distinguished an expressive-association defense from a speaker's control over a particular expressive presentation. It separately rejected compelled speech here because Dale had not proposed contrary instruction and his inclusion did not carry the attributed message. It acknowledged both a group's right to remain silent and the possibility that a person could publicly epitomize a position incompatible with a group's advocacy, while finding that premise unproved here. See 530 U.S. 678-698, especially 687-689, 695 n.21 and 696. Its intimate-association rejection appears at 698 n.26.

Souter's additional dissent expressly required a clear position advocated over time through the organization's usual channels. It denied that favorable social change or the popularity of a view could determine constitutional protection. An individual who publicly epitomizes a contrary position could present a materially different leadership case; neither the protected status nor this record automatically establishes that hypothesis. Ginsburg and Breyer joined these qualifications. See 530 U.S. 700-702, including the footnote distinguishing a bona fide direct-speech claim.

The current Hurley rule protects actual expressive composition while reserving other disputes and identity-based access without a comparably material expressive burden. It does not abolish the dissenters' distinct inquiry into an association's actual sustained advocacy. Its rejection of a single parade theme does not preclude asking whether BSA advocates the particular position offered to justify this exclusion. Current Jones likewise does not decide that evidentiary question: transferring official-nominee selection power to outsiders is an established injury different from determining what this assistant scoutmaster's retention communicates. Souter's and Breyer's Jones joins therefore do not compel reversal here. Stevens's and Ginsburg's Jones public-primary distinction neither denies private expressive rights nor proves that this particular inclusion impairs them. Kennedy's voluntary-development distinction supports the reversal path only after the expressive connection has been established. No changed legal, factual, procedural or remedial premise supports changing any Associate's historical judgment.

The disagreement concerns the identified expression, this adult role's effect on it and the resulting justification. These columns state the judgment on the presented adult-leadership application, not a conclusion about all BSA membership.

## Grounds, join barriers and separate-writing willingness

**O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas:** Join a common opinion protecting this adult instructional appointment on the demonstrated educational undertaking, supported organizational position and Dale's publicly identified contrary position. Deference to an association's values and assessment of impairment is compatible with independent First Amendment review and the express rule that merely asserting an exclusion does not establish immunity. They do not require BSA to announce an anti-homosexuality lesson in every handbook, abandon broad recruitment, enforce unanimity or permit courts to redefine its values because they seem inconsistent. The sex-education referral policy and absence of planned contrary instruction do not eliminate teaching by adult example. The holding must still explain this particular role and evidence; it cannot treat every customer's identity as a message. Their application rejects O'Brien intermediate scrutiny for the direct burden found here and concludes that the State's access interest does not justify that severe interference. Ordinary public-accommodations law remains enforceable.

O'Connor's [Roberts concurrence, 468 U.S. 631-640](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-468/pdf/USREPORTS-468-609.pdf), was read in full. It locates quiet persuasion, youth instruction and morally directed outdoor training within expression, distinguishes commercial association, and criticizes burdensome proof of a membership-message connection. Her historical full Dale join shows that she can accept the common material-impairment formulation as applied on this record; it does not establish a requirement for a separate commercial-association holding here. Scalia and Thomas have no additional independently necessary ground in the authorized inputs. Kennedy may explain voluntary organizational development and Dale's own freedom to speak without adding either approval of BSA's beliefs or a claim that every regulated relationship is constitutionally immune. All four are willing to join a complete common opinion meeting these limits. O'Connor or Kennedy may write if needed to preserve a stated qualification; no extra writing is inevitable.

**Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer:** Join a substantive affirmance position applying Roberts, Rotary and New York State Club Association to require actual expressive impairment. Their strongest opposing case is genuine education by chosen adult example, which they accept as a protected mode of expression; they reject the inference that this instructor's identity necessarily changes the group's actual message. General moral language, the ordinary program, referral of sexual instruction, diverse sponsors and the policy statements considered in their actual sequence do not establish the requisite unequivocal advocacy and impairment. The 1978 exclusion statement is relevant but is not self-proving expression; later statements cannot simply be treated as contemporaneous explanations for the 1990 expulsion. Conversely, do not call all later statements litigation inventions: the 1991 statement predates this suit. Stevens's historical note recognizes that later action could bear differently on prospective relief than on the legality of the completed expulsion (674 n.6); none of these Justices finds the supplied later materials sufficient to change this disposition.

They separately preserve BSA's freedom not to host contrary teaching or a debate it does not choose. Dale's advocacy elsewhere supplies no right to disregard legitimate Scouting instructions. The record does not establish such disobedience or a planned contrary lesson. Hurley's identified bannered contingent cannot simply be equated with this person's membership. Their position does not make public visibility irrelevant in every case: an established conflict between a group's actual advocacy and a leader who publicly epitomizes an opposing position could be constitutionally material. A single newspaper account and status designation do not establish that conflict for them here. No additional deposition or transcript fact is adopted as a new neutral premise.

The four accept the concrete antidiscrimination interest for the burden actually established. They do not hold that equality invariably defeats a serious expressive burden. Stevens is willing to emphasize the real harms of exclusion; Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer require that social approval of one position not be the constitutional test. They may join a common substantive dissent carrying that distinction, and Souter is willing to explain it separately if needed. Historical dual joinders do not automatically assign a second writing or its author. None will join automatic deference to a litigating characterization, a general license to exclude, or a requirement that an excluded person conceal identity. Their rejection of intimate association rests on the actual size, openness, selectivity and relationships, not solely on the statutory public-accommodation label.

## Procedure, remedy and unresolved scope

No threshold dismissal, default, waiver or participation defect is established. New Jersey controls its own statutory coverage, including the distinctly-private, religious-educational-facility and parental/in-loco-parentis exceptions and adult leadership as a privilege or advantage; those state-law conclusions do not control the federal constitutional burden. Public assistance and the federal charter do not independently establish state action. The dismissed duplicative common-law claim is not revived.

The reversal four would reverse the state judgment on this compelled adult-leadership application and remand consistently with the constitutional protection, without deciding an unpresented category of youth members, employees, customers or every future adult applicant. They need not decide intimate association. The affirmance four would affirm rejection of the federal defenses and permit the existing state remand, without awarding a damages amount. Neither group orders a new trial merely because researchers lack a document. Neither invents a membership-only compromise, which the packet identifies as disputed and unsupported as a ready remedy. No broader remedial settlement or final damages determination is supplied.
