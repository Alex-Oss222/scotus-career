## Fixed neutral premises and separate questions

The Pizza Hut judgments entered January 22, 1993 supply one qualifying strike from their common transaction. Domino's had a January 7 guilty verdict and eighteen-year recommendation, but judgment and sentence entered February 18. The Kayani guilt verdict was January 28, penalty inquiry January 29, and death recommendation January 30. The capital judge imposed sentence April 2, retaining the power to choose life, after Domino's judgment. Virginia's construction that the unentered Domino's verdict did not yet count as a conviction under section 53.1-151(B1) is accepted as state law. The federal significance of the timing remains the question.

Section (B1) concerns three qualifying separate offenses outside one act, transaction or scheme. The distinct (B2) drug rule's intervening-liberty conditions are not imported into (B1). The first-life minimum-service provisions establish neither a general life-without-parole rule nor an independent permanent-ineligibility ground here. The 1988 robbery, other pending charges and later convictions do not become additional completed strikes at the penalty deliberations.

Rule 3A:15(b) permits a timely postverdict motion for trial error or insufficiency; subsection (c) distinguishes acquittal for insufficiency from a new trial for other error. That legal power existed. No particular pending motion, meritorious defect or judicial finding that every possible defect was extinguished is established. Entry is not assumed purely ministerial, and the existence of the power does not establish an actual defect. Later entry does not retroactively prove the complete absence of an earlier substantive contingency.

The prosecutor used the Domino's conduct, prior trial and false denial as future-danger evidence. That use did not require an entered judgment. The jury expressly asked about parole, the defense sought an adequate response, and the court told the jury not to concern itself with what happened afterward. The exact exchange did not include a precisely drafted three-strikes instruction; the express three-strikes theory is identified at April judicial sentencing. State merits treatment prevents manufacturing a new default merely from those differences. A distinct claim that Virginia arbitrarily manipulated state law retains its own identity and preservation questions and is not silently adjudicated here.

The separate decisional inquiries are: (1) the constitutional eligibility trigger and appropriate point in the capital process; (2) if triggered, whether an effective opportunity conveyed accurate legal information; (3) whether section 2254(d)(1) permits relief on the November 1994 adjudication; (4) any necessary causal-effect inquiry under an actually supported standard; and (5) conditional sentence-only relief if every required predicate is met. No earlier stage automatically supplies the later one.

## Governing authority and common limits

Simmons's controlling due-process rule requires an effective opportunity, by accurate defense presentation or adequate instruction, to explain ordinary-parole ineligibility when the State makes future danger an operative reason for death and the only lawful noncapital alternative excludes ordinary parole. It is grounded in Gardner's meaningful opportunity to answer sentencing information and Skipper's effective rebuttal of future danger. Ramos permits truthful release-related information and does not require a guarantee against clemency or new legislation. Actual prison dangerousness remains available to the State. Simmons reserved disputed eligibility and announced no general right to predicted release information.

O'Dell's present-law old-rule and separate watershed holdings remove a categorical Teague obstacle to the confined protection; they do not establish the underlying eligibility, violation, effect or statutory conditions. Allridge distinguishes legal exclusion of ordinary parole from a prediction that a parole-eligible person will not be released. Neither has already decided the effect of a separate guilty verdict awaiting judgment. Their later dates are preserved when evaluating section 2254(d)(1).

Terry Williams's controlling common statutory rules require separate consideration of contrary-to and unreasonable-application review, holdings in force at the state merits decision, and objective legal unreasonableness rather than a poll of judges. A general holding can govern; a state disagreement with a federal court is not by itself unreasonable. Neither four-Justice exclusive allocation between the clauses is controlling. Weeks independently requires statutory eligibility even after constitutional error is found. Undisputed dates and disagreement about their legal effect establish no section (d)(2) factual error or automatic section (e)(2) hearing entitlement.

The defense's statement that Ramdass would never leave prison must be considered but is not automatically accurate legal explanation. The jury's question records a question, not a subjective finding about its eventual vote. Counsel's later unsworn reports of unnamed jurors supply no such finding. Even if the threshold is met, the whole actual opportunity must be examined. A no-parole instruction cannot be ordered on the premise that a parole-eligible alternative already excluded ordinary parole.

## Alternative-path comparison and final independent commitments

The supplemental neutral validation confirms four distinct propositions in the lower opinion: the legal-ineligibility threshold defeats the constitutional claim; practical ineligibility would independently fail section 2254(d)(1) as an extension not clearly established; Teague independently bars that proposed extension; and disagreement over Virginia's legal definition is not an unreasonable determination of fact or an independent federal custody violation. The commitments below assess those grounds separately. The actual appellate passages contain no reasonable-jurist poll; no disposition rests on correcting one. Four Associates would affirm on the stated no-error and independent statutory grounds. Four would vacate and remand the granted claim on the legal errors identified below, while agreeing that the state-law disagreement is not itself a section (d)(2) factual ground. No Associate finds present entitlement to a writ on the supplied record.

**Path A: affirm on the jury-stage legal trigger, with independent statutory denial.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas select this path. At the penalty deliberation the separate Domino's judgment had not entered, so Virginia law did not yet exclude ordinary parole from the noncapital alternative then presented to that jury. A later conviction expected to produce a statutory disability differs from clemency or legislation altering a disability already legally attached. Rule 3A:15's retained authority confirms that entry follows a judicial process, without establishing an actual pending defect. The State could use the admitted robbery conduct as future-danger evidence without representing that the judgment had entered. Its evidentiary use does not supply the missing legal strike or prevent the State from identifying its actual status. Later entry before the judge's sentencing does not retroactively make the earlier jury-stage alternative legally ineligible.

The strongest objection is that the jury was selecting a sentence that could ultimately be imposed only after an already-returned guilty verdict became a judgment, and the actual final sentencing occurred after entry. The strict-trigger response is that Simmons governs the lawful alternative on which the jury acts; it does not require the jury to predict the disposition of a distinct criminal proceeding, however strong the prediction. A truthful conditional statement might be permitted, but present law does not require it as equivalent to an instruction that ordinary parole is already unavailable. This resolves the granted jury-information claim without deciding every possible independent claim about the capital judge's later proceedings.

For these four, an independent section 2254(d)(1) ground also supports denial. Simmons's actual ineligibility trigger, Gardner's adverse-information setting, Skipper's prison-conduct evidence and Ramos's state-law latitude made Virginia's distinction at least an objectively reasonable application of the holdings in force on November 4, 1994. The rationale is substantive comparison with those holdings, not a judge count or a demand for identical facts. Their independent ground would survive their assumption that a present direct-review court might adopt a wider truthful-information protection. No Teague holding or causal-effect conclusion is necessary to either complete affirmance ground.

**Path B: reject conclusive entry-only foreclosure and return for the fixed-alternative and independent habeas inquiries.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer select this path. The controlling protection concerns an effective opportunity to answer the State's future-danger case with an accurate account of the sentence actually available. A separate guilty verdict, its recommendation, and its entry before capital judicial sentencing can present a different question from a forecast that a parole board will never release a legally eligible prisoner. State law controls when the third strike formally attaches; that does not by itself settle every federal question about truthful explanation during a capital process whose final sentence follows entry.

These four would not require a false statement that Ramdass was already legally ineligible on January 29. They would reject an absolute rule making absence of entry by that date conclusive without considering whether the separate proceeding's substantive adjudication was complete and the exact legal consequence at final sentencing. A supported case in which only entry remained may require a truthful account of the conditional legal consequence; mere expectation of conviction, confidence in guilt, or a favorable release prediction would not. This formulation leaves its crucial factual predicate unresolved here. It does not call Domino's entry ministerial, presume that all substantive grounds had failed, or infer a pending ground from Rule 3A:15 alone.

The strongest objection is that this approach extends Simmons beyond its fixed existing ineligibility premise and gives trial courts a new practical-certainty inquiry. These four would answer through the distinction between a completed substantive adjudication followed by entry and prediction of a future adjudication or parole discretion. The supported chronology and the State's use of the very robbery make that distinction material enough to examine; they do not prove the distinction satisfied. The relief would be a bounded legal return, not a new broad prediction entitlement or an immediate writ. If the record cannot establish the proposed completed-adjudication premise, the theory cannot establish a violation by assumption.

Section 2254(d)(1) remains separate for this group. Their Terry Williams position favors applying a prescribed governing protection independently under the contrary-to clause; the common Williams holdings additionally forbid excluding a general rule simply because the facts differ. But neither position makes the general right to fair sentencing sufficient for every new procedural demand. The actual appellate alternative treats the practical/legal distinction as establishing an extension outside clearly established law without resolving the asserted completed-adjudication distinction. For these four, that categorical classification is insufficient: Simmons, Gardner and Skipper must be compared with the precise protected function and supported record, rather than treating the word practical as the answer. A completed substantive adjudication whose remaining step is entry may present an application of the existing protection; an expectation that substantive adjudication will produce a conviction presents the materially different extension.

These four would vacate that independent statutory ground and require consideration of the actual supported claim under the two clauses and the November 1994 law. They do not find either clause satisfied now. An accurate statement of the general statute is not enough if the decisive classification assumes away a material distinction, but the receiving court may still conclude on an adequately supported analysis that the State neither contradicted nor unreasonably applied a governing holding. They accept that reasonable applications can differ, that some applications genuinely demand new law, and that no universal refusal-to-extend rule was resolved in Williams. This is a legal ground for a bounded return, not a finding that constitutional error automatically opens the statutory gateway or that the appellate court used a judicial-poll standard.

The same four separately reject the lower court's conclusive Teague-extension classification without treating statutory eligibility and retroactivity as interchangeable. O'Dell now establishes the old-rule status of the confined effective-response protection; it does not establish every practical-certainty proposal as old. The lower court must first identify which protection the supported facts actually invoke. A claim within the already dictated fixed-alternative protection receives O'Dell's treatment; a materially new entitlement concerning expected future adjudication requires its own finality and exception analysis. Simmons preceded the November 1994 state merits judgment, so no exact later finality date is needed merely to establish its availability then. If a different proposed rule makes the finality date or an exception material, those predicates must be established rather than assumed. These four adopt no new Teague exception and do not use O'Dell as a postcutoff constitutional holding for section 2254(d)(1).

All eight agree with the appellate court's distinct classification of Virginia's conviction definition as a legal question. No Justice supports the District Court's reported section (d)(2) route merely because that state-law construction is disputed. The Path B federal inquiry leaves the state definition intact and concerns the opportunity to communicate an accurate conditional consequence, not a power to count an unentered verdict as an already entered conviction. Section 2254(a) remains the separate federal custody requirement; a state-law disagreement alone supplies no writ.
