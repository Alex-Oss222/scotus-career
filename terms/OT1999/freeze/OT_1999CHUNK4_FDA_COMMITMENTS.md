## Entering law and operative statutory boundaries

**Governing statutory date.** Pub. L. 105-115 section 422 leaves the question of tobacco authority unaffected by that Act and its amendments, and separately requires whatever authority exists to be exercised under the FDCA as it stood on **November 20, 1997**. Neither sentence ratifies either party's interpretation. The Act's general February 19, 1998 effective date, with section 501's exceptions, does not displace that express earlier baseline. This is pre-cutoff background legislation, not a new enactment produced by this proceeding.

Section 406's new section 393 mission is a real mandatory provision with its own qualifications, but it is not part of that earlier tobacco regime. Its invocation by the lower majority cannot supply an applicable tobacco command. The new section 360bbb-2 classification-request/default procedure likewise supplies no operative tobacco route. The following analysis uses only the corrected earlier paragraphs; it does not treat the complete 1998 Code as governing tobacco law.

The following existing rules govern the statutory inquiry:

- **An agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision**, effective *Boston & Maine*, March 25, 1992: the actual agency ground may be recognized, but missing reasons, findings and policies cannot be supplied by counsel or the Court.
- **Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change**, with the relevant *Good Samaritan* component effective June 7, 1993 and present interpretive-change wording from *Smiley*, June 3, 1996: a changed agency view is not categorically invalid; actual delegation, explanation and legitimate reliance remain material. No antecedent-conduct issue is decided merely by applying this rule.
- **Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing**, effective *MCI*, June 17, 1994: an agency cannot remove the essential operation of a statutory command through a bounded modification power. This is not a universal major-questions rule, an economic-importance veto or a prohibition of all consequential regulation.
- **Delegated Customs regulations in tariff litigation**, effective *Haggar*, April 21, 1999: traditional statutory construction comes before deference; a reasonable formal regulation within genuine delegated ambiguity receives Chevron review. Rulemaking alone proves neither delegation nor validity.

*Sweet Home* distinguishes actual statutory coverage, permissible delegated construction and concrete application. *Iowa Utilities Board* separately examines general implementing authority and particular mandatory limits. *Medtronic* requires the actual federal counterpart, different/additional state duty and relevant subject relation before §360k preemption; FDA coverage does not automatically eliminate every state youth-access rule. Current *Locke* likewise rests on its particular national/local statutory allocation, not overlap or a generalized preference for national uniformity.

The relevant statutory propositions are not interchangeable:

1. Section 321(g)(1)(C) covers nonfood articles intended to affect bodily structure or function, separately from subsection (B)'s disease-use category. Its food-claim and dietary-supplement qualifications remain in their stated settings. Section 321(h)'s body-effect alternative includes components and accessories, but excludes an article achieving its primary intended purpose through chemical action or metabolism. Nicotine's chemical action does not by itself decide the claimed classification of the delivery component. The record's deliberate dose design and manufacturer knowledge present an intended-use argument distinct from therapeutic advertising and mere foreseeable effects.
2. Section 353(g) assigns the lead FDA component by the combination's primary mode of action and permits use of other FDA resources for adequate safety, effectiveness or substantial-equivalence review. Administrative assignment is not a textual exemption from otherwise applicable drug requirements. For a combination whose primary mode is a drug, paragraph (1)(A) places primary jurisdiction with the drug-review personnel; paragraph (2) permits other resources without changing that command. The earlier paragraph (4) market-clearance references remain, but no disposition here rests on a biological-product licensing question.
3. Sections 331(a) and 352(j) prohibit interstate introduction of a product misbranded because it is dangerous under the labeled dosage, manner, frequency or duration. Section 352(f)(1)'s directions requirement carries a mandatory regulatory exemption when that requirement is unnecessary for public-health protection; the exemption is not from subsection (f)(2)'s separate warnings requirement. Existing tobacco warnings do not by themselves establish compliance with every FDCA requirement.
4. Sections 331(d) and 355(a) independently require effective approval for introduction of a new drug, while §355(d) specifies the findings and opportunity for hearing governing refusal. The earlier subsection (d) retains its notice and hearing, refusal grounds, approval direction and substantial-evidence definition; the 1997 added single-investigation-plus-confirmatory-evidence sentence is not applied. No fixed minimum number of studies is inferred merely from its removal. This handoff determines no particular new-drug application, transition classification or exemption.
5. Section 360c uses graduated controls and asks about the intended users, labeled conditions and probable health benefit from use against probable injury or illness from use. It does not say that every risky device is immediately banned. The agency's withdrawal/substitute-risk explanation is disputed as a permissible benefit from use; it is not an enacted exception. The earlier section 360c(a)(3)(B) permits other valid scientific evidence on its stated findings, while the new postmarket and least-burdensome effectiveness-review additions do not govern. The earlier section 360c(f) reclassification petition remains available under its criteria and process; the new 30-day de novo request does not. The earlier section 360c(i) substantial-equivalence rule remains, without the new proposed-labeling constraint on that review, the new expedited/postmarket provisions or new guidance requirement. None of those deleted additions establishes a tobacco-intent rule, an exemption, or a shortcut around independent safety duties.
6. Section 360j(e) permits conditions on device sale, distribution or use when harmful potential or collateral measures mean reasonable assurance of safety/effectiveness otherwise cannot be achieved. Training/facility restrictions require their own safe-use determination; lack of board certification alone cannot exclude use. It does not expressly exempt a product from §§352 or 360h. Its advertising reach remains a separate disputed question.
7. Section 360f permits banned-device rulemaking on its stated risk/deception findings, with its labeling-correction and special immediate-effect conditions. Section 360h(e), separately, commands cessation of distribution and notification after the specified reasonable-probability finding of serious adverse consequences or death; it requires a prompt hearing opportunity and vacatur if the grounds are inadequate. Recall follows a further determination. Individuals' devices cannot be recalled, and the greater-risk exception concerns recall from user facilities; it is not a universal exception to cessation of distribution. No present ban, recall or finding under those procedures is ordered here.
8. The cigarette and smokeless-tobacco enactments specifically regulate labeling, advertising, reporting and youth access. Sections 1331/1334 and 4406 retain their actual subject limits. Failed expansion bills, congressional silence and FDA's former statements are not enacted prohibitions or automatic ratification. Their interpretive significance must be tied to the scheme Congress actually enacted. Section 422 adds an express reservation of the disputed authority question, so neither the 1997 mission nor Congress's enactment of modernization legislation confirms an exclusion or a delegation.

## Competing sufficient paths

**Authority exists; reverse the categorical invalidation and remand.** Nicotine deliberately supplied for pharmacological effects fits the structure/function language, and an intended delivery component can fit the device definition. Therapeutic promotional claims are not the only permissible evidence of intent. The unchanged broad coverage provisions and the earlier differentiated regulatory tools permit the agency's coverage construction; at minimum the choice is reasonable within the actual delegated ambiguity. Later statutes regulating tobacco labeling and access can coexist with FDCA coverage without an implied wholesale exemption.

The strongest objection is that the agency's accepted danger findings trigger obligations inconsistent with its chosen continuing-market scheme: a restriction power does not dispense with misbranding, approval or cessation commands. This path answers by keeping authority and lawful exercise separate. It does not approve a relative-risk exception to every command or guarantee continued marketing under this regulation. Classification, the actual applicability of each mandatory provision, its required finding and the legality of the agency's chosen method must receive the appropriate adjudication. An obligation to remove a dangerous covered product, if established, would not by itself show that the product is outside a protective statute. This distinction does not depend on the excluded 1997 flexibility: the earlier definition, graduated class criteria, qualified alternative scientific-evidence route and restricted-device power remain, subject to the earlier mandatory commands. Section 422 preserves that construction question without answering it. The later labeling/access statutes do not expressly command that all tobacco remain lawfully marketed regardless of other law.

**Authority does not extend to this claimed regime; affirm.** Definitions are read within the FDCA's operating requirements and Congress's subsequent tobacco-specific enactments. The agency proposes to treat products it regards as inherently dangerous in customary use as drug/device combinations while retaining their general market through restrictions. The Act's misbranding, safety, approval and cessation structure does not confer that dispensing choice; the cumulative tobacco legislation supplies additional enacted context for concluding that this general scheme is outside the authority claimed. That conclusion rests on the earlier misbranding, approval, class criteria and conditional cessation commands, not the inapplicable new mission. Section 422 neither confirms the conclusion nor creates a new exclusion.

The strongest objection is that broad structure/function text, deliberate nicotine design and Congress's failure to enact an express tobacco exemption defeat the exclusion. This path cannot answer by calling the issue economically important or by converting every later labeling law into a repeal. It reads the definitions in the integrated statutory setting: customarily marketed tobacco and the continuing-market regulatory choice presented cannot be fitted to the FDCA's actual protections simply by selecting one permissive device provision. It does not reject all nontherapeutic drug coverage, every combination product, or products sold with therapeutic claims. Nor does it decide that every dangerous device must be banned without findings and process.

**A narrower defect in particular restrictions.** This is an available remedial consequence of accepting authority, but does not independently resolve the supplied general coverage question on the present source record. No Associate commits to adjudicating a specific advertising exemption, combination-product approval, constitutional claim or preservation fact not established here. A regulation-specific decision would require its actual legal and procedural predicates.

## Proposition and remedial compatibility

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support reversal on statutory coverage and permissible delegated construction; their agreement does not approve every method of device regulation. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support affirmance on the integrated statutory-authority ground; their agreement does not adopt every statement in the lower opinion. These are independent Associate commitments, not a final Court disposition.

All eight preserve traditional statutory construction before deference; lawful interpretive change is not categorically barred. None adopts a freestanding major-questions test, automatic legislative ratification, universal preemption of state tobacco restrictions, or a judicial command to ban/recall tobacco. Intended use is not reduced to therapeutic claims throughout the FDCA. No new criminal liability, retrospective damages, regulatory exemption or compelled rulemaking is established.

If affirmance controls, the regulations' invalidation for want of the claimed authority stands within the presented customary-marketing scope; the district court's vacated advertising rationale does not become law. If reversal controls, the categorical authority ground is removed and unresolved regulatory-method, advertising and constitutional issues return for ordinary adjudication, with actual stays and unreviewed matters handled according to their existing procedural footing. Neither path authorizes unconditional reinstatement of every restriction or substitutes a judicial health program.
