## A. Material facts, claims, and procedural sequence

The following account distinguishes the lower opinion's record recitations and judgments from the claims Williams seeks to prove. The lower court's legal conclusions are not present Supreme Court law.

### Background and the limits of the innocence showing

The prosecution concerned the February 1993 robbery, sexual assault, killing of Morris and Mary Keller, and destruction of their property. Williams and Jeffrey Alan Cruse participated. The lower opinion reports that Williams testified at his January 1994 trial and admitted armed robbery, at least participation as an accomplice in the rape, and shooting Mr. Keller in the head. Williams denied personally raping Mrs. Keller, firing the later shots, and initiating some other acts attributed to him. Cruse supplied much of the evidence Williams disputed. The jury convicted Williams of capital murder and recommended death based on future dangerousness and vileness; the trial court imposed death. The Virginia Supreme Court affirmed. [L ¶¶1–11.]

The lower court relied on Williams's admissions, medical evidence that each gunshot to Mr. Keller contributed to death, and semen evidence attributable to Williams when rejecting a clear-and-convincing innocence showing. It applied Virginia law permitting capital-murder liability where participants directly inflict fatal injuries and where the immediate murderer is an accomplice in the underlying rape. Those are the lower court's evidentiary and state-law reasons, not an independent resolution of a disputed hearing claim. The sanitized source-fact handoff records Williams's concession that he cannot meet §2254(e)(2)(B). This conceded statutory condition must not be inferred merely from missing research material. [L ¶¶21, 27–28; P, sanitized facts.]

### State and federal proceedings

Williams sought state habeas relief and requested discovery, an evidentiary hearing, and expert assistance. The Virginia Supreme Court denied the motions and petition, describing the claims before it as meritless without explaining its reasoning. That general procedural account does not establish that every subsequently discovered federal claim was actually presented to and adjudicated by the state court. [L ¶12.]

Williams filed his federal application after AEDPA's April 24, 1996 effective date. The district court initially granted a hearing on the juror-nondisclosure, prosecutorial-nondisclosure, and alleged informal plea-agreement claims, while refusing a hearing on the psychiatric-report claim. The Fourth Circuit stayed the hearing and required reconsideration under amended §2254(e); the district court then denied the hearing and dismissed the petition. The August 1999 appeal affirmed. The three claims discussed in the appellate opinion's hearing analysis are the report, juror, and prosecutor claims; the plea-agreement claim is separately resolved on its merits. These two lists of three must not be confused. [L ¶13 and parts II–III; P, sanitized facts supplies the initial order's claim allocation.]

### Other lower-court claims and their distinct grounds

1. **Alleged informal plea agreement with Cruse.** Cruse initially agreed to disclose his knowledge in exchange for the Commonwealth not seeking death; the Commonwealth revoked that agreement after discovering that he had concealed his own participation in the rape. [L ¶9.] Williams separately alleged an undisclosed agreement at the time of trial. The Virginia Supreme Court rejected that claim on the merits. Woodson and Cruse's lawyer, Donald Blessing, supplied affidavits denying an agreement at trial; Blessing's additional federal affidavit denied both formal and informal agreements or understandings. The Fourth Circuit credited that unrefuted account under §2254(d), found no reason for another hearing on that merits claim, and alternatively found no Brady materiality because of Williams's admissions. This is not a fourth application of the appellate §2254(e)(2) hearing bar. [L ¶¶33–40.]
2. **Presence and confrontation comment.** Williams challenged Woodson's cross-examination drawing attention to Williams's opportunity to hear every prior witness before testifying. The Fourth Circuit expressly did not decide constitutional error. It found any error harmless because impeachment would affect Williams's exculpatory account without weakening his inculpatory admissions, using the historical substantial-and-injurious-effect formulation. It alternatively concluded that the requested rule would be new under Teague and was not dictated by Griffin or Brooks. Neither alternative may be imported as current Supreme Court law; the scope limits of the entering Portuondo and collateral-error law are stated below. [L ¶¶41–44.]
3. **Federal expert assistance and confidentiality.** Williams challenged the requirement that he demonstrate the need for confidentiality in an adversary proceeding before making an ex parte request under 21 U.S.C. §848(q)(9). He refused that proceeding, and the lower courts denied assistance for failure to establish reasonable necessity. The appellate court rejected the access-to-courts objection at the scope of requiring a hearing, leaving what information was disclosed to district-court discretion. The statute permits authorization upon reasonable necessity for guilt or sentence representation; if authorized, payment must follow under paragraph (10). No ex parte proceeding, communication, or request may be considered without the proper confidentiality showing; any such proceeding, communication, or request must be transcribed and included in the appellate record. The statute's conditions and the lower court's particular interpretation remain distinct. [L ¶¶45–50 and note 2.]
4. **Short filing period.** Williams challenged use of the 180-day period associated with chapter 154 instead of the one-year period for non-opt-in states, asserting that Virginia had not been recognized as qualified. The Fourth Circuit did not decide qualification or validate the shorter period. It found any error harmless because the case remained pending for a year and a half, Williams never moved to amend with a newly developed claim, and his request for five additional months identified no claim to add. The reproduced opinion labels §2263 with the wrong title number; this packet refers to the chapter 154 provision without adopting that transcription error. [L ¶¶51–52.]

These other rulings define what the lower judgment contains. Nothing in the supplied review question authorizes automatic reopening of their independent merits or procedural dispositions. No earlier Supreme Court petition treatment is used to settle their scope.

### Statutory components

The exact §2254(e)(2) text quoted in the sanitized lower source follows by mechanical extraction. Its opening condition, the alternatives in (A), and the cumulative requirement in (B) are separate parts of the enactment.

> (2) If the applicant has failed to develop the factual basis of a claim in State court proceedings, the court shall not hold an evidentiary hearing on the claim unless the applicant shows that --
> (A) the claim relies on -(i) a new rule of constitutional law, made retroactive to cases on collateral review by the Supreme Court, that was previously unavailable; or
> (ii) a factual predicate that could not have been previously discovered through the exercise of due diligence; and
> (B) the facts underlying the claim would be sufficient to establish by clear and convincing evidence that but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found the applicant guilty of the underlying offense.

If the opening condition applies, either (A)(i) or (A)(ii) must be satisfied, and (B) must also be satisfied. The unavailable-new-rule alternative is not omitted merely because the present dispute concerns factual investigation. The concession concerning (B) means that an applicant-side failure within the opening condition would prohibit these hearings; it does not decide whether that condition is met. If the opening condition is not met, §2254(e)(2) supplies no prohibition, but other applicable hearing and habeas conditions remain.

The entering register's **State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254**, effective from AEDPA §104 on April 24, 1996, independently preserves: §2254(d)'s merits-relief restrictions for state-adjudicated claims; §2254(e)(1)'s presumption of correctness for state factual determinations, rebuttable by clear and convincing evidence; and §2254(e)(2)'s cumulative hearing conditions. No comprehensive Supreme Court construction of the contested phrases or their general interaction has been supplied. A hearing and an operative writ are different remedies.

The register's **Exhaustion and merits denial under amended §2254**, effective April 24, 1996, permits relief when state remedies have been exhausted, no available corrective process exists, or circumstances make it ineffective. It permits, but does not require, merits denial despite nonexhaustion; express counsel waiver is required for State waiver. These provisions do not silently answer an independent procedural-default question.

### The important prior-law distinction

**Keeney v. Tamayo-Reyes (May 4, 1992), as decided in the entering law,** retained Townsend's fifth mandatory-hearing circumstance and its deliberate-bypass qualification for material facts inadequately developed on a properly presented claim. Ordinary counsel negligence without deliberate bypass does not bar the necessary hearing under that former-law rule. O'Connor's Court opinion was joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, and Kennedy. Its limited remedy concerned the explanation of the manslaughter mental-state element; supported findings and other denials were undisturbed.

That holding distinguishes Coleman's independent adequate-state-ground default rule, which remains operative. It does not revive Fay's displaced claim-default rule, excuse every new unexhausted claim, or determine the later statute's meaning. AEDPA supplies an additional constraint where applicable; entering law expressly leaves the meaning of failure to develop unresolved. A construction requiring fault through deficient diligence, a continuity argument favoring the former deliberate-bypass rule, and the interaction between statutory hearing limits and claim default must therefore be considered as distinct legal questions. No Justice's answer is supplied here.

The register's **Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments** rests on Lindh (June 23, 1997) and its identified Carriger extension (April 16, 1998). Those decisions preserve specified pre-enactment applications and their exact limits; they do not exempt this post-enactment federal application or settle all of §2254(e)(2)'s construction. Felker's successive-petition, original-habeas, and temporal holdings likewise do not turn this ordinary appellate hearing question into a successive-petition or original-writ case.

## C. Lower reasoning and the strongest competing positions

**The lower court did recognize a diligence threshold.** It did not construe §2254(e)(2) to forbid every hearing merely because the state record was incomplete. Relying on Cardwell and Wright, it required reasonable ignorance and requests tied with particularity to an identified claim. It treated accessible public divorce records, the nonspecific investigation request, and failure to find the psychiatric report in its court file as deficiencies attributable to Williams. It then found the statutory exceptions unsatisfied, including the innocence condition. [L ¶¶16–28.]

**Its alternative cause-and-prejudice ground must remain visible.** Even assuming the amended hearing prohibition did not apply, the court invoked historical Keeney, McCleskey, and Carrier. It found no objective external impediment because Williams failed to develop reasonably available facts and adequately justify discovery. It found no prejudice based on the strength of the admissions and its assessment of the jury allegations. [L ¶¶29–32.] That is an actual alternative rationale of the judgment under review. The historical Keeney hearing premise differs from the controlling entering Keeney rule; it cannot be reinstated merely by quoting the lower court. Any genuinely independent state claim default must be identified on its own legal and factual predicate rather than inferred from this difference.

**Petitioner's supported position.** Williams argued that he had no way to know the specific facts and sought state discovery, a hearing, and investigative assistance that were denied. The strongest claim-specific argument is that reasonable efforts depend on the information available then: absence of a disclosed relationship may leave no meaningful lead to the public divorce file, whereas known references to an existing psychiatric report may call for further action. State or witness concealment, if established, may matter both to attribution and to cause, but allegations of concealment do not prove all elements of relief. [L ¶22; P.]

**Respondent's supported position.** Public availability and information already in counsel's possession may establish avoidable underdevelopment. A general request for an investigator does not immunize unexplained failure to pursue an identifiable lead. Treating conclusory requests as diligence would deprive the statutory prohibition of practical operation. Even if the prohibition is removed, independent claim-default or merits reasons may leave no entitlement to a hearing or writ. The lower court's actual alternative grounds must be assessed under entering law, not silently discarded. [L ¶¶23–32, 38–40.]

**Analytical alternatives, not invented filings.** The statutory text permits debate about whether failure requires lack of diligence or some greater degree of fault; about whether Townsend's deliberate-bypass treatment carries into the new trigger; and about the contrary reading that incompleteness itself activates the conditions. The last reading must answer the applicant-attribution language and the distinction between ordinary hearing availability and the exceptional route through (A) and (B). The continuity reading must answer why Congress enacted a new hearing restriction with an express due-diligence predicate. The diligence reading must identify reasonable efforts without making success or every theoretically possible search the measure. These are options for independent analysis, not statements that any party brief made arguments not supplied here.

## D. Lawful paths and remedies

- **Sustain the prohibition claim by claim:** If the adopted statutory construction attributes the underdevelopment to Williams on a component, the conceded failure of (B) supplies a hearing bar on that component. Findings and circumstances relevant to the report cannot automatically be imputed to the jury relationships.
- **Reject the prohibition on one or more components:** If reasonable efforts or another adopted construction mean Williams did not fail to develop that component, the statutory bar is absent. The Court may resolve application on supported facts or remand for the necessary remaining evaluation. This does not itself establish juror bias, prosecutorial misconduct, Brady materiality, or a right to a writ.
- **Address the alternative ground:** A remand or reversal of the statutory rationale must account for the lower court's alternative ground. Entering Keeney prevents use of ordinary negligence alone as the former-law hearing disqualification for a properly presented claim; Coleman can still govern a genuinely established independent state claim default. If the supplied record does not establish the necessary distinct predicate or resolve cause, prejudice, or the ordinary hearing criteria, those matters may require appropriately bounded further proceedings.
- **Preserve the judgment's independent scope:** The plea-agreement merits determination, presence-comment claim, assistance ruling, and filing-period ruling do not automatically fall with a conclusion about one of the three hearing components. A disposition must state which part of the Fourth Circuit judgment is affected and which is outside the review question or rests on an undisturbed independent basis.
- **Maintain the hearing/writ distinction:** Neither rejection of §2254(e)(2)'s prohibition nor the inadequacy of a lower rationale orders release, acquittal, sentence vacatur, a new trial, or a hearing on every habeas claim. Any order for factual development must identify its claim and remaining legal conditions.
