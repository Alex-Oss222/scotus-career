## Counting rule and scope

Count signed lead opinions after plenary merits review, including an Opinion of the Court in whole or in part or an opinion announcing the judgment, once for each Justice delivering any part of that opinion. Count Associate-assigned opinions too. A single lead divided into several parts by the same author counts once. Exclude separate writings, unsigned per curiam opinions, summary dispositions, procedural certifications, Section 10 explanations, and matters without completed Records. A seriatim writing counts only if it actually meets the lead-opinion rule; no existing Record in this baseline requires that classification.

The recount takes authors directly from each Record?s Opinion Topology or opinion table. Assignment statements supply form and review-stage context; their reported earlier tallies are not used. Cases are ordered by event date and then caption. Late-completed Ramdass and Apprendi are included at their stated dates for future assignments, without reconsidering any prior assignment.

## Independent parser comparison and limitations

The independently extracted 91 event/author pairs agree exactly with [chunk8_tools.py](../runtime/chunk8_tools.py) `counts` output, including the two June 26 cutoffs. No existing Record supplies an unresolved count classification. The machine-readable companion is [OT_1999CHUNK9_ASSIGNMENT_BASELINE.json](OT_1999CHUNK9_ASSIGNMENT_BASELINE.json).

The existing helper deduplicates authors per Record, rather than per distinct lead opinion. That matches every existing Record but would undercount a future Record containing two distinct counted leads delivered by the same Justice. Its broad lead-row recognition scans internal tables as well as Public Projection; a future internal table quoting historical or superseded lead authors could also contaminate the result. Its event-name merits/plenary filter does not by itself prove the review stage. For new Records, verify actual opinion form, review stage, and author rows; do not count a decree merely because its event description mentions an earlier merits decision. No such parser mismatch occurs in this baseline.

## Required checker and prohibited Git operations

[tools/check_term.py](../../../tools/check_term.py) has standard-library imports only. It invokes `git cat-file` through `git_object_exists` for commit-reference existence. It has no flag to suppress that call, so a direct unwrapped run would breach the no-Git instruction whenever it finds a qualifying reference.

Its Python subprocess dependencies are [holdings_volumes.py](../../../tools/holdings_volumes.py) in `check` mode and [split_chunk.py](../../../tools/split_chunk.py) in `--check` or `--parse-only` modes. These modes are read-only, use standard-library imports, and invoke neither Git nor the locked top-level directory. Checker traversal is confined to the selected term plus explicitly named state paths. No checker or dependency traverses the repository root recursively.

The existing chunk8 helper already replaces `git_object_exists` with an in-memory bypass and prints a clear `NOT PERFORMED` history-verification notice. The root operator is preparing a chunk9 copy with a hard subprocess Git guard and the correct opening-ledger reference. Running that adapted checker preserves the other deterministic checks without changing tools. Treat its generic success line as a result for the non-Git checks only: commit-reference existence remains unverified. Use `python -B` or the helper?s `sys.dont_write_bytecode` setting to avoid tool-directory bytecode writes.

No Git command, locked-directory read, or change outside the authorized OT1999 subtree was made for this baseline.
