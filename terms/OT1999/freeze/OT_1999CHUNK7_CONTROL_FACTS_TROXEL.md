Granville and Brad Troxel were unmarried parents of two daughters. After their separation, the children visited their paternal grandparents through their father. He died in May 1993. The mother continued some visitation and then sought to limit it. The grandparents petitioned in December 1993 for two overnight weekends each month and two summer weeks. Granville proposed one day per month without overnight stays, and family holiday participation. The trial court ordered one weekend monthly, one summer week, and four hours on each grandparent's birthday. The dispute was over amount and conditions, not a complete refusal of contact. The mother later married and her husband adopted the children. No allegation or finding of parental unfitness is identified in the published recitation. This absence is not an independent present adjudication of every parental-capacity question.

The Court of Appeals reversed and dismissed on its construction requiring a pending custody action. The Washington Supreme Court rejected that construction but affirmed dismissal on federal constitutional grounds in the consolidated lower proceeding. Only the Troxel/Granville visitation dispute is presented here. The state court's facial construction and reasoning remain distinct from the concrete trial application; no federal disposition is supplied.

The trial court's written findings, reproduced in the published recitation from Appendix page 70a, described the grandparents' large local loving family and opportunities involving cousins and music; they identified benefit from quality time balanced with the children's nuclear family, including the mother's and stepfather's other children. These findings did not identify parental unfitness or an actual harm from the mother's proposed schedule. The oral ruling invoked ordinarily beneficial grandparent contact unless the grandparents' lifestyle would adversely affect the children; the court said it had not been shown that visitation was contrary to their interests. A further oral explanation relied on the judge's favorable childhood summer experiences with grandparents. These are descriptions of the trial judge's stated reasons, not this packet's constitutional evaluation. ## Former statute as reproduced in the source

The following entire subsection is copied mechanically from the saved source's quotation of former RCW 26.10.160(3) (1994). It includes the changed-circumstances qualification. It is authenticated here as a published quotation, not independently checked session-law text:

> Any person may petition the court for visitation rights at any time including, but not limited to, custody proceedings.  The court may order visitation rights for any person when visitation may serve the best interest of the child whether or not there has been any change of circumstances.
