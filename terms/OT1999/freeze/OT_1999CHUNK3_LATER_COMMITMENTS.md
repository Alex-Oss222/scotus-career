## Village of Willowbrook v. Olech — No. 98-1288

**Cutoff and reach:** February 23, 2000. Review of a Seventh Circuit reversal of Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal. Reach the presented equal-protection pleading question; no threshold defect is supplied. The demand was allegedly 33 feet against 15 feet for materially similar owners, followed after about three months by acceptance of 15 feet. The other affected owners mean that the convenient class-of-one description is not a finding that only one person was affected. Intentional deprivation and reckless disregard are alternative allegations; only the sufficient intentional-treatment allegation supports the commitments below.

**Propositions offered for joinder.** O1: An individual may state an equal-protection claim by alleging intentional differential treatment from materially similarly situated persons without a rational basis; membership in a separately identified class is unnecessary. O2: A separate allegation or showing of subjective ill will is not required for that objectively irrational intentional-treatment claim. O3: This complaint suffices; affirm the judgment returning it for ordinary proceedings. Material comparability, intentional conduct, absence of a rational basis, liability and damages remain to be established. O1–O2 do not make negligence, ordinary mistakes, a disagreement over a discretionary judgment, or unequal treatment supported by a material distinction unconstitutional.

**Competing lawful paths.** The objective path follows the individual protection recognized in Sioux City Bridge and Allegheny Pittsburgh, with Beach's conceivable legitimate grounds and Heller's relationship to the actual disadvantage. Its strongest objection is federal constitutional review of every municipal service dispute. Intentional disparity, materially proper comparators and the absence of rational grounds answer that concern without a new evidence-pleading burden. The narrower ill-will path permits this particular complaint because it additionally alleges retaliation after an earlier successful lawsuit, leaving the objective-only case open. Its strongest objection is that the individual equality principle does not generally depend on proving personal hostility. It is a viable ground for this judgment, but supplies no vote for O2.

**Common statutory baseline.** Section 405(h)'s first sentence binds parties to findings and decision after hearing; its second confines review of findings and decision to that provided; its third restricts §1331/§1346 actions to recover on claims arising under the subchapter. ## United States v. Johnson — No. 98-1696

**Cutoff and reach:** March 1, 2000. Reach the automatic-credit question on certiorari to the Sixth Circuit. The earlier vacatur of two firearm convictions and immediate release after the resulting 51-month lawful term are supplied litigant history; they import no historical Supreme Court construction displacing current law. The remaining drug convictions support the three-year supervision term. No threshold defect is supplied.

**Common statutory propositions.** J1: Under §3624(e), supervised release commences when the person is actually released from imprisonment. Excess incarceration before that date does not automatically commence or reduce the term. The provision about imprisonment in connection with a conviction addresses running/tolling after a term has commenced; even a successful argument about the validity of the vacated convictions would not rewrite the separate commencement command. J2: The statutory community-supervision period and imprisonment serve distinct functions; a day of confinement is not automatically a day of supervision. J3: Reverse the Sixth Circuit's contrary automatic-credit rule and remand consistently with these limits. The district court retains its separately conferred §3583(e) powers.

**Alternatives and their strongest objections.** Constructive release on the date lawful custody should have ended would prevent cumulative restraint after excessive imprisonment; it also treats the phrase imprisonment in connection with a conviction as excluding a vacated conviction. Its decisive difficulty is that no release occurred and the concurrency/tolling text cannot start a term that the commencement sentence has not started. Actual release can leave an individual bearing both excessive custody and a full supervision term; that equitable concern is serious but does not make imprisonment into community supervision or erase Congress's qualified modification powers. The statute supplies discretionary relief, not a court-made automatic credit.

**Statutory completeness and remedy.** All eight join the same sufficient rule and reversal/remand. Section 3624(e) separately permits concurrency with another federal, state or local probation, supervised-release or parole term; stops running during imprisonment connected to a conviction; excepts imprisonment of less than 30 consecutive days from that stop; and contains the fine-installment condition, ordinarily limited to two years absent special circumstances. None creates this automatic precommencement credit. No question about invalid-custody tolling of a term that had already commenced is decided.

Under §3583(e)(1), after one year of supervised release, the court may terminate and discharge if the released person's conduct and the interest of justice warrant it, after considering §3553(a)(1), (a)(2)(B)–(D), (a)(4)–(6) and the prescribed probation-modification procedure. Under (e)(2), before expiration or termination, the court may modify, reduce or enlarge conditions, and may extend a term if less than the authorized maximum was imposed, using the applicable procedures and initial-setting provisions. Condition modification is not immediate term termination. Paragraph (e)(3)'s revocation requires a proved violation by a preponderance and has its distinct offense-class limits; paragraph (e)(4)'s nonworking-hours home restriction is only an alternative to incarceration. Neither is ordered here. Excess custody may inform the authorized exercise of discretion; it requires no particular outcome. No automatic discharge, new imprisonment, damages or retrospective sentencing credit is ordered.

## United States v. Locke / Intertanko v. Locke — Nos. 98-1701 and 98-1706

**Cutoff and reach:** March 6, 2000. Certiorari to the Ninth Circuit in the paired maritime challenges. Both dockets receive the same component disposition. Review concerns particular state tanker rules, not a single indivisible environmental program. The equipment provision WAC 317-21-265 was already invalidated below; no cross-challenge requiring its revival is supplied.

**Common propositions.** L1: Ray's statutory distinction remains: the federally assigned general vessel design, construction, alteration, repair, maintenance, equipment, operation, manning and personnel-qualification subjects under Title II can constitute an occupied national field. A state rule's actual function, not its spill-prevention label or simple overlap, determines inclusion. Section 3703(a)'s mandatory national rulemaking, listed vessel subjects and certification structure supply the source; Gade is not overruled and supplies no physical-impossibility-only immunity. L2: Distinct local port and waterway measures under Title I require their own inquiry into operative federal regulation or an affirmative federal determination that additional regulation is inappropriate. Mere silence or agency preference is insufficient under Ray and Myrick. L3: OPA §2718 preserves its stated additional liabilities, requirements, removal measures, funds and penalties; its saving language is not confined to damages. Its specified limitation on preemptive effects of OPA, the 1851 Act and §9509 does not repeal independent PWSA displacement of general vessel standards. It creates no universal exemption for any rule bearing an environmental purpose.

**Provision dispositions currently supported.** L4: WAC -230's comprehensive extra training, -250's master/deck-officer English and crew/instruction communication standards, and -200's general minimum watch composition/standard fleet bridge-management requirement regulate duties, qualifications, training or manning assigned to the national regime. Reverse their approval at that described scope. Preserve the distinct restricted-visibility watch requirement for its own local-waterway/subject analysis on remand, rather than equating it automatically with the general watch rule. L5: Vacate remaining approvals reached through the erroneous general saving-clause/overlap approach and return genuinely unexamined provisions for actual classification and any necessary conflict analysis. No wholesale invalidity or validity follows.

**Additional reporting component, L6.** The separately frozen LOCKE_REPORTING_LAW and LOCKE_REPORTING_NEUTRAL_READY were read before this commitment. Six Associates would also reverse approval of WAC -130's described worldwide five-year-and-continuing event-reporting demand as a general vessel safety/operating-history requirement within the national maritime scheme. Section 3703's general operational assignment, §6101's delegated selection of reportable casualties and manner, and §6102's federally specified uniform State reporting system together supply the asserted federal basis. This is occupation of that defined general vessel-reporting function, not a finding of physical impossibility or an inference that every unrequired federal report is forbidden. Souter and Thomas would instead vacate approval and remand this component for a demonstrated statutory exclusion or actual conflict, for the reasons below.

Section 6102's State role is preserved: the national arrangement expressly includes state reporting and compilation/submission, with the statutory protection against federal use beyond the State's permitted use when the specified State confidentiality/use restrictions apply. The six-Justice position distinguishes that coordinated role and genuinely local incident reporting from an additional worldwide operating-history condition; it does not hold that all State reports are preempted. No finding establishes whether Washington's system satisfies §6102's particular confidentiality condition.

The federal texts retain their exact qualifications. Section 6101(d)(1) addresses foreign vessels in casualties on United States navigable waters; (d)(2) separately addresses the specified oil-carrying foreign vessels and the seaworthiness/environmental categories in United States jurisdictional waters including the EEZ, subject to generally recognized international-law principles. Its separate nonfatal classification and foreign-passenger provisions do not create unlimited foreign-tanker jurisdiction. **Competing merits propositions.** P1, permitting path: A defendant who elects to testify may be subject to ordinary credibility argument about the opportunity to hear the other testimony; that comment is not constitutionally forbidden merely because the opportunity arose from trial presence. Griffin's prohibition on using silence as evidence of guilt does not make the testimony itself immune from ordinary credibility testing. The present summation, read in context, violates neither the right to attend and testify nor the independent due-process requirement of a fair trial on this presented ground. The State may impose stricter rules of advocacy. P2, prohibiting path: The prosecutor may expose actual inconsistency, changed accounts or concrete fabrication, but may not ask the jury in a generic closing argument to infer dishonesty solely from the accused's protected presence. That argument burdens the exercise of the trial right without differentiating honest from dishonest testimony. The distinct lawful evidence-based attacks in this transcript do not cure its separately general accusation.

**Alternative-path objections.** P1's strongest objection is the practical and often legally required character of the accused's presence: a credibility debit automatically attached to attendance burdens confrontation and testimony together. Its answer is that testimony remains evidence subject to ordinary appraisal, while presence itself is neither treated as substantive guilt nor made a prohibited act. Perry's witness-role rule and Jenkins's distinction between protected silence and permissible credibility material support that boundary; Griffin, Brooks and Rock retain their protections against prohibited penalty or arbitrary exclusion. P2's strongest objection is that opportunity to conform is a genuine credibility circumstance visible to the jury and other witnesses receive no categorical insulation. Its answer is to preserve specific evidence and examination while forbidding a general adverse inference equally applicable to every defendant who attends and testifies. The dispute concerns that legal boundary, not a finding that Agard actually tailored his account.
