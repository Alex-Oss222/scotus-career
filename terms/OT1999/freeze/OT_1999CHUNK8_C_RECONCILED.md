## Apprendi — reconciliation of all eight Associates

### Shared issue, controlling-law distinctions and remedy

The live question is both who must determine a disputed present-offense fact and the burden of proof when that fact is legally necessary to raise the maximum authorized by the pleaded offense. Count 18 ordinarily authorizes ten years; the racial-purpose finding authorizes twenty and produced twelve. The actual plea reserves this challenge. It supplies no admission of the additional racial purpose and no surrender of the reserved jury/proof claim. Aggregate punishment potentially available on other counts cannot supply missing authority for the actual twelve-year Count 18 sentence.

The five reconciled reversal commitments share this sufficient rule: a fact concerning commission or consequences of the present offense that is indispensable to a greater maximum than the offense otherwise validly admitted or found by the jury permits performs the constitutional function of an element. It must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt and determined by the jury unless a valid admission or waiver supplies the corresponding lawful basis. Their common rule does not affirmatively decide a constitutional prior-conviction exception, unchanged-maximum minima, capital eligibility or selection, all Guidelines classifications, or general consecutive-sentencing rules. Broader personal positions stated below are not added together to expand this common ground.

For those five, reverse the judgment insofar as it sustains the enhanced Count 18 term and remand for lawful disposition. On the existing admitted offense, the maximum is ten years. No existing jury or equivalent admitted finding supplies racial purpose; the contested hearing and the lower court's assessment of strong evidence do not cure that absence. The remedy does not undo the pleas, acquit, compel an enhancement jury or new prosecution, decide whether another lawfully authorized proceeding is available, require consecutive restructuring, determine the aggregate sentence, or resolve the distinct Graves Act provision. Its parole consequence must follow the lawful sentence and its own terms. No universal structural-error classification or automatic extension of Neder is adopted.

For the three affirmance commitments, sustain the reviewed judgment against the presented federal allocation/proof objection on a substantive defense of the enacted sentencing classification. Neither historical recidivism precedent, notice alone, a plea waiver fiction nor an alternative aggregate sentence supplies their sufficient ground.

## Dickerson — reconciliation of all eight Associates

### Shared issue, statutory scope and remedy

The interlocutory federal appeal concerns case-in-chief use of a statement excluded under Miranda. The Court has no conviction or sentence to vacate. The United States supports the challenge to section 3501's asserted displacement; Cassell supplies the principal contrary advocacy. District-court findings about custody and warning chronology remain findings, while the circuit's voluntariness conclusion remains identified as its inference. Later proffered material is not a new finding. Neither the circuit's decision to take up the statute nor the independent physical-evidence ruling is an additional merits component.

The six reconciled reversal commitments share constitutional implementation, rejection of the particular statutory substitute and retention of Miranda's core under stare decisis. Miranda permits genuinely effective alternatives; it does not permit a return to retrospective voluntariness alone for the core case-in-chief use. The Constitution does not require an immutable wording formula. Existing exceptions and distinct remedies remain, and an unwarned voluntary statement is not thereby actually compelled for every purpose.

For those six, reverse the reviewed judgment insofar as section 3501 supplies its basis for reversing statement suppression and remand consistently with Miranda. Sections (a) and (b) cannot authorize this use merely because a statement is voluntary. Independent voluntariness review remains. The ruling does not invalidate every part of the statute: subsection (c)'s separate six-hour presentment rule and reasonable transportation/distance proviso, subsection (d)'s voluntary noninterrogation or noncustody situations, and subsection (e)'s broader confession definition retain their distinct operation subject to constitutional requirements. Under (b), the listed factors must be considered; their presence or absence need not be conclusive. No new suppression hearing, reopening order, search ruling, indictment dismissal, damages remedy or release follows.

Scalia and Thomas would affirm against the presented Miranda-displacement objection and apply section 3501 on a sustainable voluntariness determination and its required circumstances. Their view must include the judicial conclusion that categorical exclusion of a voluntary unwarned statement cannot be maintained as an indispensable constitutional command. Congress's statute alone cannot override a constitutional interpretation that they simultaneously accept as controlling. Their objection to that judicial rule and its prior state-court implementation supplies the necessary alternative constitutional premise, not an assertion of supervisory power over state courts. Their proposed change is not entering law.

**Individual comparison:** His Roberson opinion and Elstad dissent favor effective protection of the suspect's choice; his simulated Withrow and Davis joins preserve distinct practical safeguards. He rejects both congressional restoration of voluntariness as sufficient and judicial elimination of Miranda's core, while leaving effective alternatives open. No historical judgment departure; historical quotations of displaced postdivergence law are replaced with actual public law.
