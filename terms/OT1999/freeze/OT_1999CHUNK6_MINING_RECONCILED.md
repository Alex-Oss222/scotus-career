### Necessary statutory terms and temporal qualifications

The ordinary rule in §510(c), 30 U.S.C. §1260(c), concerns a currently violating operation currently owned or controlled by the applicant. Actual control through downstream intermediary entities can satisfy the text; a mere upstream relationship cannot substitute for the statutory direction. A three-year schedule of notices is required, but the permit rule separately reaches a current violation shown by the schedule **or other information**. The three-year schedule is not a general age limit on the violation considered.

The ordinary permit block continues until the applicant supplies proof that the violation **has been corrected or is in the process of being corrected to the satisfaction of the authority having jurisdiction**. Completion is not invariably required. Once ordinary control ends, the separate statutory route requires a demonstrated pattern of willful violations of the chapter, of the requisite nature and duration, with resulting irreparable environmental damage indicating an intent not to comply. Those requirements are cumulative. Mere former ownership or control cannot replace them, but termination of control does not erase a claim that satisfies them or another independently authorized law.

For component B, present application, current control and current violation must be real operative predicates, not labels used to enforce a new disability resting solely on completed conduct. The supported disposition establishes no actual control or violation in a particular permit proceeding. Clear contrary prior official guidance is not established here; that is not a finding that nobody relied on anything. Smiley's reserved clear-guidance case, genuinely new past-conduct obligations, penalties and concrete adjudication-specific finality questions remain distinct. The Court recognizes no general retroactive rulemaking authority and does not backdate the IFR.

The lower opinion's footnote 12 separately permits the temporal application involving a pre-rule violation but a post-rule acquisition of control. That application remains permissible at this temporal level; the present ruling additionally rejects the categorical exclusion of ongoing current conditions merely because acquisition also predates the rule. Neither proposition independently validates every control presumption, determines §2462, or treats all old violations as immune from present eligibility conditions.
