## Entering-law distinctions

Article I, section 10's prohibition applies to state penal legislation. Calder v. Bull, 3 U.S. (3 Dall.) 386 (1798), rejected an Ex Post Facto Clause challenge to a civil probate rehearing; Chase's four-category explanation includes retrospective reduction of the evidence legally required for conviction. That category's authority and limits must be read through the later controlling cases, rather than treating every phrase of an individual seriatim opinion as an unlimited rule.

Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990), allows the later correction of an unauthorized fine while preserving an otherwise lawful conviction and life sentence. Its majority rejects a free-standing rule against every retrospective loss of a substantial procedural protection, reiterates Calder's categories and says a procedural label does not immunize a prohibited change. Its footnote 3 distinguishes new evidentiary rules and invokes Hopt and Thompson v. Missouri; it cannot be read as either a categorical ban on all new evidence or an express decision about this corroboration amendment. Collins overrules Kring and Thompson v. Utah's Ex Post Facto reasoning. Those overruled propositions must not be revived through their quotation in an older case.

Hopt v. Utah, 110 U.S. 574 (1884), permits removing a convicted felon's witness incompetency for a later trial of an earlier offense, while distinguishing changes that lessen the amount or degree of proof essential to conviction. The opinion's separate trial-presence, hearsay, jury-function and confession rulings do not decide Carmell. Thompson v. Missouri, 171 U.S. 380 (1898), permits admitting genuine handwriting exemplars for comparison: the ultimate facts, reasonable-doubt burden and jury's assessment remain. It does not approve a legislature directing a lesser quantum of proof sufficient; its reliance on the older Kring/Thompson v. Utah formulations is qualified by Collins.

### Retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole reconsideration

**Governing question:** Whether a later law may withdraw mandatory discretionary-parole reconsideration for an earlier offender by doubling hearing intervals or permitting individualized postponement for up to three years.

**Current rule:** The Ex Post Facto Clause forbids applying to a pre-enactment offender a later statute that removes every alternate mandatory parole-reconsideration opportunity by doubling the post-denial interval from twelve to twenty-four months, where ordinary parole depends on consideration and no retained ordinary means of obtaining intervening review is established. That binding loss of a meaningful release opportunity makes punishment more onerous despite unchanged initial eligibility, maximum sentence and ultimate release discretion; the prisoner need not prove the omitted hearing would have produced release. Relief restores the consideration opportunity, not an entitlement to parole. An individualized initial prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that prediction do not preserve later mandatory opportunities to present rehabilitation, changed circumstances or a materially mistaken forecast. Where the actual three-year deferral removes meaningful annual consideration while parole is legally available and no ordinary substitute is established, it increases punishment. The prisoner must show the earlier entitlement, retrospective actual withdrawal and a concrete connection to a legally available avenue to earlier release; inconvenience alone is insufficient. Once shown, the State must substantiate a substitute it invokes through its legal basis, practical availability and timely capacity to provide that consideration. The prisoner retains the ultimate burden of proving an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

### Retrospective loss of parole consideration

#### California Department of Corrections v. Morales

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-1462.
**Decided:** April 25, 1995.

##### The initial prediction does not preserve the meaningful later opportunity lost here

**Holding:** Applying the 1981 California hearing-deferral provision to Morales’s 1980 offense violates the Ex Post Facto Clause because the actual three-year deferral withdraws offense-date mandatory annual consideration during a period when parole is legally available, without an established ordinary substitute for the meaningful intervening opportunities. The separately reasoned prediction of continued unsuitability and review of that initial prediction do not preserve consideration of later rehabilitation, changed circumstances, or a materially mistaken forecast; relief restores consideration, not release.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: applies its opportunity rule to this reserved individualized setting; its original holding and rejection of proof of the omitted hearing’s outcome remain in force.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): applies protection of a legally available avenue to reduced custody and the distinction between guaranteed benefits and discretionary alternatives; it supplies no right to parole.
- Miller v. Florida, 482 U.S. 423 (1987): applies scrutiny of binding adverse operation despite unchanged outer limits and a possibly identical result.
- Collins v. Youngblood, 497 U.S. 37 (1990): preserves the punishment boundary; no free-standing rule against every retrospective procedural disadvantage is adopted.
- In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985): accepts its separate-reasons construction, permitted factual overlap and initial review; its state-court federal characterization does not displace Cavanaugh or establish ordinary later reopening.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The 1981 amendment retains annual review as the default. It permits a next hearing no later than three years after denial only for a prisoner convicted, in the same or different proceedings, of more than one offense involving the taking of a life, when the Board finds that parole cannot reasonably be expected at a hearing during the intervening years and states its bases. Jackson requires reasons separately directed to postponement, permits their supporting facts to overlap the present-unsuitability reasons, and establishes review of the initial choice. It establishes no generally available later reopening procedure. The rule concerns Morales's actual maximum deferral, not a proved cohort-wide practice; his eligibility arose during the postponed interval.

##### The claimant proves meaningful loss; the State substantiates a substitute it invokes

**Holding:** A prisoner challenging retrospective withdrawal of a parole-consideration opportunity must show the earlier legal entitlement, its retrospective actual withdrawal, and a concrete connection to a legally available avenue to earlier release; inconvenience alone is insufficient, and proof that the omitted hearing would actually have produced parole is unnecessary. After that showing, the State must substantiate any replacement mechanism it invokes by identifying its legal basis and establishing its practical availability and timely capacity to provide the relevant consideration; the prisoner retains the ultimate burden of establishing an unconstitutional increase in punishment.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts II–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cavanaugh v. Roller, November 30, 1993: preserves meaningful opportunity loss and the absence of an actual-release prerequisite; this decision expressly clarifies substantiation of an asserted replacement.
- Weaver v. Graham, 450 U.S. 24 (1981): applies its refusal to assume discretionary alternatives compensate for guaranteed benefits; it did not itself prescribe this parole evidentiary direction.
- In re Jackson, 39 Cal.3d 464 (1985): confines review and conceivable advancement to what the opinion establishes; initial review does not become later reconsideration.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns Morales’s demonstrated meaningful loss under the challenged 1981 provision. It does not invalidate every individualized postponement, require an interim hearing in every possible individualized scheme, or constitutionalize every scheduling detail. Maximum deferral is permission, not a statutory command or a proved near-uniform cohort practice. The Court neither requires wholly different facts for the two decisions nor treats the initial forecast as unreviewable.

No universal probability threshold, general presumption transferring constitutional proof to the State, numerical response deadline, mandatory filing procedure, suitability finding, release date, damages award, or right to favorable parole is adopted. Adequacy of a demonstrated materially different arrangement, later amendments and their transition or mootness effects are not decided. The false-information and sentence claims are not reopened. Unresolved credit and matrix calculations establish neither release entitlement nor legal impossibility of earlier release.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Ninth Circuit's hearing-frequency judgment is affirmed, 5–4, preserving its remand for offense-date annual consideration. Lawful suitability standards and release discretion remain; no parole, release date or damages are ordered. Rejected false-information and sentence claims remain undisturbed.

### Garner v. Jones — 2000-03-28

[Record](../records/garner-v-jones_merits_2000-03-28.md)

#### Event and question

Garner v. Jones, No. 99-137. Merits decision, March 28, 2000. Argued January 11, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit, reviewing Jones v. Garner, 164 F.3d 589, which reversed summary judgment for the parole officials and remanded. The question is whether Georgia may apply Jones's eight-year reconsideration deferral retrospectively in place of his offense-date three-year opportunity, given the asserted expedited-review mechanism.

Jones received life sentences for murders committed in 1974 and, after escape, 1982. The offense-date rule supplied initial consideration after seven years and reconsideration after denial at three-year intervals. The later rule permits intervals no longer than eight years. Following consideration in 1989, 1992 and 1995, the Board denied parole in 1995 and set reconsideration for August 2003, citing the nature and circumstances of the offenses and multiple offenses. Those facts establish neither suitability for release nor legal impossibility of earlier parole.

The Board's 1996 policy permits a deferral up to eight years when parole cannot reasonably be expected in the intervening years. Eligible life-sentence cases, including cases involving parole later revoked, may receive expedited review when changed circumstances or new information warrant earlier consideration. Members must indicate the interval when voting to deny parole. A custodian's affidavit describes full individual review, an individualized prediction and an existing expedited procedure. The regulation separately requires reasons for denial while protecting confidential sources and potentially discouraging diagnostic opinions.

**Authority:** Both holdings are supplied by Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. Seven directly join this application of controlling Morales. Stevens and Ginsburg agree with the governing opportunity protection but do not join the vacatur disposition or its justification.

#### Jones has shown the meaningful reconsideration opportunity withdrawn

**Holding:** Jones's actual eight-year deferral retrospectively removed the offense-date three-year occasion for consideration while parole remained legally available, satisfying Morales's initial showing of a meaningful lost opportunity. He need not prove that the omitted occasion would have produced release; the original prediction of continued unsuitability does not itself preserve consideration of later rehabilitation, changed circumstances or a materially mistaken prediction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Morales governs this withdrawal. That decision requires an earlier legal entitlement, its actual retrospective removal, and a concrete connection to legally available earlier release; the prisoner retains ultimate constitutional proof. Cavanaugh protects the meaningful consideration opportunity, not a promise of favorable parole. Weaver explains why a legally available avenue to reduced custody can matter despite discretion over the outcome, and Miller rejects unchanged outer sentencing limits as a complete answer. Collins confines the inquiry to punishment rather than every procedural disadvantage. Jones's multiple murders, escape and repeated adverse assessments bear on suitability, but do not establish that earlier release was legally impossible. The Court does not decide suitability, impose an actual-release probability requirement or invalidate every longer interval. It applies Morales to the established individual withdrawal and turns separately to the asserted substitute.

#### The asserted expedited procedure requires assessment under the actual substitute rule

**Holding:** A written discretionary policy and sworn description of an existing expedited-review procedure must be assessed for legal basis, practical availability and timely capacity to provide the consideration withdrawn; neither the discretionary wording alone nor an assertion that the procedure exists decides its adequacy. Because the appellate court rejected the arrangement on a broader categorical premise and the district court applied a different Morales premise, the judgment must be vacated for the proper inquiry under ordinary procedure.

**Controlling explanation:** Morales requires the State to substantiate a replacement it invokes without shifting the prisoner's ultimate burden. Georgia supplies a policy and actual affidavit evidence, which materially differ from an advocate's hypothetical power to advance review. Courts must consider that evidence and supported practice. But an assertion that expedited review exists does not itself establish an ordinarily usable route and timely consideration as a matter of law. Conversely, the policy's permission to advance review does not establish that such a route is impossible simply because it is discretionary or lacks an invariably enforceable hearing demand. The Court rejects both shortcuts. The remand preserves Jones's established initial showing and does not replace it with a requirement to prove likely release. It guarantees no opportunity to cure every evidentiary failure, orders no administrative study or automatic discovery, and prescribes no filing form or response deadline. If an adequate substitute is not established, the relief restores timely consideration under lawful rules while leaving the Board's lawful suitability judgment intact.

#### Precedent treatment

- California Department of Corrections v. Morales governs the actual opportunity and substitute inquiries. Its original authority, burden allocation and consideration-only remedy remain; this decision addresses the materially different Georgia arrangement it left open.
- Cavanaugh v. Roller retains protection against the binding loss of a meaningful avenue to earlier release without proof of the omitted hearing's result.
- Weaver v. Graham supplies the distinction between a legally available opportunity and a guaranteed favorable outcome; theoretical discretion does not establish a compensating substitute.
- Miller v. Florida supplies scrutiny of binding adverse operation despite unchanged sentence ceilings or a potentially identical outcome.
- Collins v. Youngblood preserves the punishment boundary and supplies no categorical exemption for parole procedure.

#### Resulting law and limits

Morales continues to govern retrospective withdrawal of mandatory parole consideration. Actual discretionary practices may establish a materially adequate replacement, but their practical availability and timely capacity require support. The Court establishes neither a general significant-risk substitute for that inquiry nor a rule invalidating every discretionary or longer reconsideration arrangement.

#### Source and record limits

[Jones v. Garner, 164 F.3d 589](https://static.case.law/f3d/164/html/0589-01.html) supplies the lower judgment, record history and policy account. The custodian's affidavit and Board instruments appear in the [record and briefs](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0323). The affidavit establishes an assertion of an existing procedure, not an adjudicated finding that the procedure is ordinarily available and timely. No request by Jones for expedited review, rejection of such a request, new rehabilitation finding or statewide operational rate is established. The policy permits expedited review; it does not guarantee a hearing on every request or a response within a fixed period.
