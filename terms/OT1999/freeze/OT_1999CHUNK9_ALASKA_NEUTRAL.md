This provision formally completes that pleading step and supplies no fresh merits reopening.

B. The proposed intersovereign offshore boundary is fixed by Exhibit A, from Point Hope in the Chukchi Sea to the United States-Canada border in the Beaufort Sea. The parties' memorandum states that they agreed to extend the continuous line westward to Point Hope using the resolved issues and uncontroverted principles. It identifies Harrison Bay and the Arco pier as unexcepted Master recommendations. These are representations supporting the joint proposal, not newly adjudicated historic-title findings. The 1997 decision supplies the governing normal baseline and rejects an entitlement based on Dinkum Sands; the proposal cannot restore that entitlement.

The United States would have exclusive exploration and natural-resource rights seaward of the line against Alaska and those claiming under it, with a reciprocal noninterference injunction. The exception preserves Alaska's section 8(g), 43 U.S.C. 1337(g), revenue rights and Paragraph C's lease-administration rules. Alaska would have the corresponding rights shoreward against the United States and those claiming under it, except within the Petroleum Reserve and Arctic Refuge, subject to 43 U.S.C. 1313 and Paragraph C. The reciprocal injunction has that same qualified scope.

Section 1313 preserves lawfully and expressly acquired federal parcels and their accretions, resources and improvements; lands lawfully held under state law; lands expressly retained or ceded at statehood, excluding a merely general marginal-sea retention; lands acquired in a proprietary capacity by eminent domain, purchase, cession, gift or otherwise; lands filled, built up or reclaimed for federal use; existing federal rights in lands actually occupied under claim of right; lands or interests held for tribes, bands, groups or individual Indians; and structures or improvements constructed under the federal navigational servitude. No new parcel-specific exception finding is requested.

Section 1337(g) concerns federal leasing and coastal-state revenue sharing, including its qualified Alaska treatment for tracts partly within the three-mile band, escrow treatment during boundary disputes, later adjustment at settlement, and specified interest and payment rules. It does not give Alaska the seaward title the proposed decree otherwise denies. The decree preserves the section's operation without calculating a dollar award or adjudicating every statutory application. Its exact text is supplied so that the preservation is not mistaken for an unqualified 27-percent award over all offshore revenues.

Section 1301(b) authorizes an offshore boundary fixed by coordinates in a final Supreme Court decree to remain immobilized despite subsequent coastal change. Exhibit A uses NAD 83/WGS 84 as authoritative. NAD 27 coordinates are conversion aids, not equal alternative authority. The schedule is preserved in the original PDF; no coordinate should be retyped or silently changed from OCR. The proposal does not ask the Court to conduct a new survey.

C. Three distinct lease categories govern accounting and administration. For leases subject to section 7 agreements, escrow funds are to be distributed under those agreements within 180 days after decree entry, and their applicable terms govern administration. Existing leases outside section 7 agreements but intersected by the new fixed line remain administered by their original lessor, who keeps all past and future lease revenues; after expiration, relinquishment or termination, Paragraph B governs the area's exploration and exploitation rights. The fixing does not change distribution from former leases in that category that expired before decree entry. Where an existing lease both falls under a section 7 agreement and is intersected by the line, the agreement controls escrow distribution and administration; Paragraph B governs the area after expiration, relinquishment or termination. Fixing the line does not change distribution from former intersected agreement leases that expired before entry.

Paragraph C(2) independently preserves each sovereign's rights and obligations toward lessees and third parties, whether arising under section 7 agreements or otherwise. Present or future unitization, operating, enhanced-recovery, commingling and similar agreements are also preserved. No damages award, lease cancellation or new third-party obligation is proposed by implication.

The Petroleum Reserve's coastal boundary runs from the western bank of the Colville River along the highest highwater mark westward, closing the small lagoons and estuaries named in the proposed text, following the ocean side of barrier islands and sandspits within three miles and the Plover Islands, to Icy Cape. F. These two reservation coastal boundaries remain ambulatory and migrate with relevant physical features. That is distinct from the fixed federal-state offshore line. The parties may resolve resulting disputes through negotiation, alternative dispute resolution, or retained jurisdiction, and may jointly submit a boundary agreement for a supplemental decree. G. Jurisdiction would be retained to effectuate and supplement the decree and the parties' rights; in other respects it would be final.

#### United States v. Alaska

**Docket or dockets:** No. 84, Original.
**Decided:** June 19, 1997.

##### The normal baseline governs without a proved general ten-mile island-fringe rule

**Holding:** Alaska's statutory three-mile submerged-land grant is measured from the Convention's normal low-water baseline, including applicable river and bay closing rules and separate belts of qualifying islands. The evidence establishes no firm and continuing general federal rule converting all waters behind fringing islands with entrances under ten miles into inland waters; the earlier Mississippi Sound judgment does not preclude that conclusion.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts I–II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Article 4 permits but does not require straight baselines; the United States made no selection here. The international twelve-mile territorial sea does not enlarge the statutory three-mile State grant. Alaska asserted general practice rather than specific historic title to Stefansson Sound; no different proved inland-water claim or general power to contract existing state territory is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** California (1965) supplies Convention definitions. The 1985 Alabama/Mississippi decision established particular historic title, not a universal fringe rule; preclusion fails even assuming its availability, without expanding Mendoza or creating an original-action exception. Nome’s artificial-extension rule and the later Alabama/Mississippi and Massachusetts decrees retain their actual geographic scopes.

##### Dinkum Sands’ frequent seasonal submergence defeats island status

**Holding:** A naturally formed feature must ordinarily remain above the agreed mean-high-water datum to qualify as an island under Convention Article 10(1). Dinkum Sands' frequent seasonal submergence below that datum defeats island status and a separate three-mile grant; isolated emergence does not create alternating island entitlement, and abnormal temporary inundation of an otherwise enduring island remains distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts I–II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No percentage-of-days test is adopted. Natural accretion or erosion of an established island is distinct. Article 11 supplies no low-tide-elevation entitlement at the reported distances exceeding three miles from mainland and nearby islands.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Borax and the California decree support the approximately nineteen-year mean-high-water datum, not an absolute physical-permanence condition. Measurements and the survey support the formation’s frequent submergence despite occasional emergence or maps derived from earlier observations.

##### The Petroleum Reserve instruments satisfy separate clear-inclusion and title-defeat requirements

**Holding:** Federal retention requires clear inclusion of the submerged lands and clear intent to defeat future state title for an appropriate public purpose; geography or general purpose alone does not suffice. The 1923 Petroleum Reserve order's boundary and petroleum function, together with Congress's specific Statehood Act treatment of that reserve, satisfy both requirements and retain the disputed submerged estate, under the same demanding inquiry for equal-footing and statutory territorial-sea lands.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts III–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Material application:** The 1923 order’s ocean-side barrier boundary, small-lagoon closures and express three-mile limit identify the estate. Ownership of the submerged petroleum deposits serves the national-defense purpose. Statehood Act §11(b)’s specific recognition of the reserve, read with that estate and function, confirms retained ownership; legislative jurisdiction alone would not establish title.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 1313(a) is not diluted by a general federal-grant presumption. Congressional confirmation makes it unnecessary to decide independent Pickett Act authority; no broad Executive retention power is announced.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Shively preserves congressional retention for appropriate public purposes. Utah Division and Montana require distinct clear inclusion and title-defeating intent; their insufficient reservoir-control and unnecessary-riverbed showings differ from this defined petroleum estate.

##### The legally operative prestatehood wildlife segregation retained the described submerged lands

**Holding:** The 1957 application and legally operative temporary-segregation regulation set the specifically described submerged lands apart for the proposed wildlife refuge before statehood. Statehood Act §6(e)'s exclusion of lands withdrawn or otherwise set apart as wildlife refuges therefore retained those lands, although the application was not a completed final reservation; lands outside its boundaries or lacking comparable legal effect are not decided.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Parts III–IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices.

**Material application:** The 1957 application’s extreme-low-water boundary, coastal features and identified aquatic habitat establish inclusion. The regulation immediately barred settlement, location, sale, selection, entry, lease and other disposals, including mining and mineral leasing, only insofar as the proposed reservation would prevent them; administrative jurisdiction remained unchanged. Those conditions nevertheless gave the specific segregation present legal effect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 6(e) supplies title-defeating intent, separately from §6(m)’s incorporation of the Submerged Lands Act. Neither general ownership nor wildlife purpose alone suffices. The 1960 reservation receives no retroactive effect; the 1980 enlargement does not set the original boundary. Scalia and Thomas do not join the geographic determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Alaska’s baseline, Dinkum and Petroleum Reserve exceptions are overruled; the United States’ original Range exception is sustained. The parties must propose a conforming implementing decree; jurisdiction is retained and each bears its own costs. No final coordinate schedule, new survey, damages or oil-accounting award is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Utah Division and Montana’s distinct inclusion and retention requirements govern. The statutory wildlife exclusion reaches this legally effective temporary segregation without treating every pending application as a completed reservation.

### Ordinary high-water emergence for natural island status

**Governing question:** Whether a naturally formed coastal feature qualifies as an island for a separate submerged-land belt.

**Current rule:** Convention Article 10(1) requires a naturally formed feature ordinarily to remain above the agreed mean-high-water datum. Frequent ordinary seasonal submergence defeats island status; intermittent emergence does not create alternating island entitlement. Abnormal temporary inundation of an otherwise enduring island is different. Mean high water, averaged over approximately nineteen years, is the datum, not absolute permanence or an adopted percentage-of-days test.

**Authority by component:**
- Island definition and seasonal boundary: United States v. Alaska, United States v. Alaska — II. Dinkum Sands does not satisfy the island definition, O'Connor Parts I–II, all nine, 1997-06-19.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Alaska, 1997-06-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** Natural accretion or erosion of an established island and independently qualifying low-tide elevations retain their distinct questions. This rule does not enlarge the statutory three-mile State grant to the international twelve-mile territorial sea.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Alaska (1997-06-19) — Property and Economic Rights / Submerged lands and fixed coastal baselines.

### Coordinate fixation of state-federal submerged-land boundaries

**Governing question:** Whether a state-federal submerged-land boundary fixed by coordinates in a final Supreme Court decree moves with the coast.

**Current rule:** Under 43 U.S.C. §1301(b), a state-federal boundary fixed by coordinates in a final decree of this Court remains immobilized at the decreed coordinates and does not move with later coastline change. The decree’s complete coordinate schedule, including its datum, zones, precision, sequence, segments and arcs, controls; an illustrative map does not replace it.

**Authority by component:**

- Coordinate-fixation consequence and schedule priority: Canonical Decision Record `united_states_v_louisiana_supplemental_decree_1998-10-13.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / The complete coordinates fix and immobilize the boundary, 1998-10-13.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-10-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule does not allocate an unpresented State’s territory, decide reserved property interests or change the entitlement used to select the decreed line.

D. 
The Coastal Boundary of the National Petroleum 
Reserve-Alaska 
The coastal boundary of the National Petroleum 
Reserve-Alaska is a continuous line, as described in 
Executive Order No. 3739-A (1923), that begins at the 
western bank of the Colville River and follows the 
highest highwater mark westerly, extending across the 
entrances 
of small lagoons, including Peard Bay, 
Wainwright Inlet, the Kuk River, Kugrua Bay and 
River, and other small bays and river estuaries, and 
following the ocean side of barrier islands and sandspits 
within three miles of shore and the ocean side of the 
Plover Islands, to the northwestern extremity of Icy 
Cape, approximately 70° 21’ N., 161° 46’ W. 
E. 
The Coastal Boundary of the Arctic National 
Wildlife Refuge 
The coastal boundary of the Arctic National Wildlife 
Refuge is a continuous line, as described in Public Land 
Order No. 2214, 25 Fed. Reg. 12,598 (1960), that begins 
at the intersection of the International Boundary line 
between the State of Alaska and Yukon Territory, 
Canada, with the line of extreme low water of the 
Arctic Ocean in the vicinity of Monument 
1 of said 
International Boundary line, and follows the line of ex- 
treme low water westerly, extending across the en- 
trances of lagoons such that all offshore bars, reefs and 
islands, and lagoons that separate them from the 
mainland, are part of the Refuge, to Brownlow Point, at 
approximately 70° 10° N., 145 51° W.

F. 
Resolution of Disputes Respecting the Coastal 
Boundaries of the National Petroleum Reserve- 
Alaska and the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge 
The coastal boundaries of the National Petroleum 
Reserve-Alaska and the Arctic National Wildlife Re- 
fuge are ambulatory and will therefore migrate as a 
result of changes in relevant physical features. 
The 
United States and the State of Alaska may resolve 
disputes arising from those changes through negotia- 
tion, through alternative methods of dispute resolution, 
or through invocation of this Court’s retained juris- 
diction in accordance with Paragraph G. 
The United 
States and the State of Alaska may jointly submit to 
this Court, for entry as a supplement to this Decree, 
any agreement respecting the location of the coastal 
boundary of the National Petroleum Reserve-Alaska or 
of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge. 
G. 
Retention of Jurisdiction 
The Court retains jurisdiction to entertain such fur- 
ther proceedings, enter such orders, and issue such 
writs as from time to time may be determined neces- 
sary or advisable to effectuate and supplement this 
Decree and the rights of the respective parties. 
In all 
other respects, this Decree is final.
