## Component boundaries and common legal terms

**Paid failure-to-pay addition, section 6651(a)(2).** Browne concerns timely returns with incomplete payment, not Adams's section 6651(a)(1) filing failure. Section 6651(a)(2) ordinarily adds 0.5 percent for a month or fraction, up to 25 percent, subject to its amount rules and applicable statutory qualifications, including the separately triggered rate increase in subsection (d). No amount or rate application is adjudicated. The exception requires reasonable cause and absence of willful neglect. The regulation's payment inquiry concerns ordinary business care and prudence despite inability to pay or undue hardship; the referenced hardship is substantial financial loss, not mere inconvenience. Sincerity neither supplies that Code exception nor eliminates RFRA's independent inquiry. The regulation's specific extension presumptions are not a general religious waiver or an established fact here.

**Paid estimated-tax addition, section 6654.** Amount and period of installment underpayment remain separate from annual-return collection delay. The underpayment period ends at the statutory endpoint or the earlier payment of the relevant portion; later levy cannot be treated as payment on an earlier installment date. All applicable computation rules and exceptions remain, without a computation or finding of eligibility. Section 6654(e)(3)(A) prohibits the addition to the extent the Secretary determines casualty, disaster or other unusual circumstances make its imposition against equity and good conscience. Section 6654(e)(3)(B) separately requires retirement after age 62 or disability in the relevant taxable year or its predecessor, and reasonable cause without willful neglect. Retirement in 1988 does not establish that 1993–95 exception. Neither is section 6651's exception.

**Interest.** Section 6601 concerns unpaid principal over the legally prescribed period and retains its own qualifications; subsection (h) excludes failure to pay estimated tax. No combined interest/addition calculation is approved. Section 6404(e)(1)'s original ministerial-act provision governs the 1993–95 liabilities: no significant aspect of the delay may be attributable to the taxpayer, and the relevant error or delay must follow written IRS contact about the deficiency or payment. The 1996 managerial-act expansion applies to taxable years beginning after July 30, 1996, not these years. Ministerial action is procedural or mechanical after prerequisites are satisfied, not a discretionary collection-priority or legal decision. Alleged avoidable delay is not a finding that these conditions are met. Section 6404(f)'s erroneous-written-advice rule separately requires reasonable reliance on a response to a specific written request and adequate, accurate taxpayer information; no such entitlement is established.

The separate 1996 review enactment applies to requests after July 30, 1996. Its Tax Court route requires the incorporated claimant eligibility and filing within 180 days after mailing of the final refusal, provides abuse-of-discretion review and permits an abatement order; it does not textually require an ordinary deficiency petition. The 1998 renumbering to section 6404(i), including section 3309's required July 22 presidential designation, is verified. No later numbering or post-event forum decision is used. The operative request cannot be dated merely by a form's signature. The May 1997 refusal and the parties' competing descriptions remain; no Tax Court filing, tolling, transfer entitlement or restarted deadline is supplied. The later 1998 suspension and disaster provisions do not automatically reach these liabilities.

**Prospective relief and forum.** The full official 1998 sections 1346 and 2201, with amendment notes, close the former retrieval gap; that gap is not a reason to remand. Section 1346(a)(1)'s ordinary recovery jurisdiction for wrongfully collected taxes, penalties and sums and section 7422's refund-claim prerequisite remain distinct from discretionary interest-abatement review and prospective relief. Section 2201 requires an actual controversy within jurisdiction and contains the Federal-tax restriction with its enacted exceptions; no applicable exception is established by these ordinary income-tax allegations. Section 7421's assessment-and-collection restriction and its qualifications require their own consideration. RFRA's appropriate-relief provision does not automatically resolve either restriction, sovereign immunity or the proper channel. The present remand rests on the actual preserved interaction and claim classification, not absent statutory words or a determination that every statutory issue is Article III jurisdiction.

The ordinary paid-addition refund branch has no independently established jurisdictional defect in this packet. This is not a finding that every unproved prerequisite is met or a new enlargement of refund jurisdiction. If a material actual defect later appears, the affected merits commitments must return to fresh modeling. Interest and prospective relief have present threshold-remand commitments only; none of the eight supplies a conditional interest merits vote or an exclusive-forum holding.

RFRA requires claimants to establish substantial burden, followed by Government production and persuasion that the actual burden advances a compelling interest through the least restrictive means. Substantial burden may be assumed for this pleadings review without final finding. Adequate existing facts and legal reasons may suffice; no universal empirical study, hearing, claimant feasibility burden, governmental duty to disprove every imaginable alternative, or negotiation requirement follows. The Code's separate exceptions cannot be merged or presumed comparable. Federal RFRA's constitutional validity and independent constitutional and Code merits outside the grant remain at their actual scope.

### Justice Stevens

- **Section 6651(a)(2): affirm the RFRA rejection.** His Adams dissent accepts the statutory burdens but permits an adequate legal showing without imagined cost findings; his Clearwater join confirms the framework. Here the accepted proposal leaves payment unperformed until the Government initiates compulsory collection. Available assets and cooperation do not equal timely payment. That legal difference supports the ordinary payment-enforcement consequence even for the narrower alleged post-contact period; he does not find delay unavoidable. The strongest contrary path is vacatur because Adams requires actual means scrutiny and needless delay might matter. He rejects that path for this RFRA claim because the proposal itself substitutes enforced seizure for the taxpayer's payment duty, while preserving separate Code or calculation issues.
- **Section 6654: affirm independently.** His Adams installment analysis supplies the distinct timely-revenue ground. Later collection does not furnish missed installments when due. The strongest remand argument is the need for an independent means inquiry; he finds the actual amount-and-period function and proposal legally sufficient, without treating every annual collection date as an installment finding.
- **Interest: vacate the merits determination for claim classification and the preserved forum inquiry.** Compensation for unpaid time might support merits; a specific review channel might support dismissal. Neither is a present join because discretionary abatement and the independent RFRA refund theory were not differentiated. His Steel Co. statutory-ground position and Ruhrgas join support a confined procedural disposition, not a universal constitutional sequence.
- **Prospective relief: vacate the merits determination for actual threshold adjudication.** Neither RFRA coverage nor simplicity of an asserted bar dispenses with identifying the precise remedy and statutory interaction. No levy, injunction or declaration is approved.
- **Join boundary and reconciliation:** He may join a common threshold explanation and a separate application-specific affirmance of the paid additions. His X-Citement Video join permits a record-supported alternative ground supporting the existing judgment. He refuses a tax-wide RFRA exception, a filing rationale, a spending-earmark rationale, invented administrative cost or adjudication of ungranted claims. No commitment changed; history has no individual merits position to override this analysis.
