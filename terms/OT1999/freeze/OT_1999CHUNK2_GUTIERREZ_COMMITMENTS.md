## Competing paths and sufficient ground

**Candidate-vote reading:** Section 1422 jointly elects Governor and Lieutenant Governor by a single vote applicable to both offices. The majority and runoff sentences concern the votes received by candidates for those offices. A ballot cast in the general election with no gubernatorial selection is not a vote cast in that contest. The reference to persons qualified to vote for the Legislature defines the eligible electorate; it does not deem every person's participation in a different race a vote against every gubernatorial slate. The general-election sentence sets the occasion for this election, not a different denominator. Because the winning slate received a majority of the actual gubernatorial votes, the supplied statutory basis for a runoff fails.

The strongest contrary argument is that Congress deliberately required a majority of participating qualified voters and provided a runoff when none receives that majority. But the enacted phrase is votes cast, read with the single joint gubernatorial vote and candidates receiving votes, rather than ballots deposited or persons participating. Including abstentions from this contest would enlarge the denominator through choices in unrelated contests without a textual command to do so. This reading gives the runoff clause full work when no slate receives a majority of actual gubernatorial votes.

**All-ballot reading:** The qualified-people and general-election language can be urged to demand a majority of everyone participating. Its serious purpose argument is that a runoff secures a broader electoral mandate. Its barrier is the paragraph's office-specific description of the vote and the absence of a command treating a blank gubernatorial choice as a vote for this election. A policy favoring a broader mandate cannot supply that missing command. No ambiguity about invalid candidate votes or fraud is presented.
