## Alternatives and remedy

Sustaining mootness would be lawful if the certificate or another supported change actually eliminated the prospective competitive disadvantage with the assurance required by ordinary cessation doctrine. The existing eligibility benefit is the strongest argument for that path. It is insufficient here because federal acceptance and validity remain substantively unsettled while the established bidding exposure continues; a label alone cannot carry the government's cessation showing.

Reversal, rather than a purely discretionary GVR or a vacatur merely to request more facts, follows because the admitted federal reservations already defeat the stated mootness basis. No independent factfinding order is needed to decide that limited question. Ordinary further proceedings may address genuinely unresolved matters under the existing mandate. Missing research originals do not establish that a litigant omitted required proof or defaulted.

Reversal removes mootness as the ground for ending this action and vacating the favorable district judgment. It does not affirm the district judgment's constitutional reasoning, settle the disputed scope of its relief, or automatically reinstate every provision of its decree. Remaining preserved issues and the decree's lawful effect remain for the lower court under ordinary procedure and the actual 1995 mandate. No new complaint, injunction, award, damages remedy, program redesign or special evidentiary procedure is ordered.
