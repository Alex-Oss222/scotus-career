## Added premise and its legal effect

The official recitation establishes that the Commission already certified the Gutierrez slate. It reports 24,250 Gutierrez votes, 21,200 Ada votes, 1,294 write-ins, 1,313 ballots without a gubernatorial selection and 609 ballots marked for both slates, out of 48,666 general-election ballots. The reported Commission calculation excluded the 1,313 blanks and found 51.21 percent. That calculation leaves the 609 double-marked ballots in its denominator; this is the Commission's treatment, not a newly adjudicated rule approving double votes or defining all invalid ballots.

The source addition does not change the previously supplied office-specific-majority premise or the selected construction. It clarifies implementation: certification is an existing act. Reversal of the Ninth Circuit's all-ballot denominator ruling removes the stated statutory reason for upsetting that act and demanding a runoff. Neither this Court nor the remand court is directed to perform a fictitious first certification. The bounded remand gives effect to the corrected denominator in relation to the actual existing certificate, without declaring an unproved final officeholding event or resolving an independent ballot-validity claim not presented.
