## Rule, exceptions and application boundaries

The six-Associate rule is conditional. Constitutionally ineffective assistance may supply cause for another claim's default, but the independent constitutional assistance claim ordinarily must first be presented to the State and remains subject to an actual adequate and independent state procedural bar. If such a bar applies, cause and actual prejudice for that default may excuse it; the established fundamental-miscarriage route remains distinct. Merely exhausting state remedies through an untimely filing supplies no exemption. The rule is not an absolute prohibition on every defaulted cause claim, a new constitutional right to collateral counsel, or a compulsory order for deciding every nonjurisdictional habeas defense.

Carrier supplies the constitutional threshold and state-presentation requirement; Coleman supplies the adequate/independent-ground principle and its recognized exceptions. O'Sullivan confirms that exhausted and unbarred are different statuses at its actual former-law scope. Gray keeps related claims distinct; Lambrix and Trest preserve nonjurisdictional default and lawful sequencing. Schlup's reliable-new-evidence, whole-record probability gateway remains separate from trial-record legal sufficiency and ordinary prejudice. The Court does not find that gateway satisfied here or require it where another route remains available.

Historical Stewart v. LaGrand is not imported as an in-world nested-default holding. The six-Associate conclusion is supported by the authorized Carrier/Coleman principles and independent model. The comparator's alternative discussion of LaGrand's precedential characterization adds no missing current-law premise. No later procedural-default exception is supplied.
