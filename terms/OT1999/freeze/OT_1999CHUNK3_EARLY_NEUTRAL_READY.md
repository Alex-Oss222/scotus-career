## Validated neutral supplements and competing paths

### Weisgram v. Marley Co.

The live question is appellate remedial authority after exclusion, not whether Rule 702 imposes reliable-foundation screening. One path treats Rule 50's sufficiency inquiry as an inquiry into legally admissible evidence and permits final judgment after a fully contested proffer fails, subject to the verdict winner's opportunity to present preserved new-trial grounds. Its strongest objection is that appellate exclusion after a favorable admission ruling may deprive a litigant of a fair opportunity to supply an alternative foundation or trial presentation. The competing path returns the new-trial question for trial-court consideration when a genuine preserved ground, blocked proffer, legal change or other procedural unfairness requires it. Its strongest objection is that it cannot become an automatic right to replace failed proof after ordinary appellate correction.

Joiner distinguishes evidentiary review from ultimate causation; Kumho sustained the resulting summary judgment on its reconsidered proffer. Neither by itself decides Rule 50(d)'s appellate trial-judgment setting. Kennedy's Joiner rule and Breyer's Kumho rule provide common legal premises; Stevens's reservation of application in those cases identifies a need to examine the procedural opportunity rather than assume the merits of a new expert presentation. The source addendum establishes the preserved Rule 50(a)/(b) motions and Rule 59 alternative, the applicable ten-day rule text, and Neely's existing appellate authority and opportunities to assert new-trial grounds. The grant excludes renewed expert-admissibility and state-law-sufficiency review. The reported rehearing grounds identify no specific additional proffer or independent new-trial ground; that positive procedural recitation supports the narrow question without inferring default from missing research. State Farm's appeal dismissal is preserved; no disposition may revive it.

### Hunt-Wesson, Inc. v. Franchise Tax Board

The constitutional exclusion of the nonunitary receipts is the supplied premise; the question is whether California's deduction adjustment allocates costs or indirectly taxes those receipts. The challenge's strongest path treats an offset measured dollar-for-dollar by exempt receipts, without relation to associated expense, as enlargement of the taxable base across Allied-Signal's boundary. Its counterargument is that exempt income may properly bear a reasonable share of fungible borrowing costs. California's strongest path treats the provision as reasonable expense allocation rather than a tax on exempt gross income; tracing each borrowed dollar is not necessarily required. Its counterargument is the need to explain the actual formula's relationship to deductible expense, especially if the same expense and business produce a different taxable base solely because exempt receipts increase.

Do not convert this inquiry into an unqualified State burden to trace every transaction. Allied-Signal retains the taxpayer's clear-and-cogent burden and separates nexus from fair apportionment. Kennedy's operational-function opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia and Souter among the current Court, supplies the controlling boundary; O'Connor and Thomas's different prior application positions do not remove it. The source addendum and copied provisions establish the staged section 24344 calculation, its distinct section 24402 exception for dividends from already-taxed income, and the dispute between the forms' exclusion of nonunitary interest and California's all-interest statutory construction. Those details preclude describing a flat denial of every interest deduction. No refund amount or factual tracing determination is established.

### Baral v. United States

The question must include the statutory deemed date, not only the packet's false binary of physical remittance versus late return. One path gives §§6513(b)(1) and (2) their specific payment dates for withholding and estimated income tax, then independently applies §6511(b)(2)'s amount window. Its counterargument is whether a remittance is truly a payment before liability is formally reported or assessed. The competing payment/deposit path invokes the legal character of the remittance, distinguishing a deposit awaiting determination from payment of a tax; its counterargument is that Congress may itself designate these particular remittances as paid without assessment. A general deposit argument cannot erase a specific statutory rule if the remittance falls within it.

Lundy's return/claim/window distinction and Brockamp's independently sufficient amount limitation matter; neither supplies an automatic rule for every remittance or a universal jurisdictional label. O'Connor's Lundy reasoning and Breyer's Brockamp reasoning identify the separate statutory operations. The source addendum establishes $4,104 withheld in 1988, $1,100 estimated tax remitted in January 1989, the April 15, 1989 ordinary due date, an August 15 extension, the June 1, 1993 return/claim, July 19 assessment, and a $1,175 requested credit. Section 6511(a) timeliness is undisputed. The three-years-plus-four-months amount window starts February 1, 1990. The §6513 estimated-payment date disregards that filing extension; the extension still enlarges the lookback. Rosenman's suspense-account deposit is a materially different supported prior arrangement, not an assessment prerequisite for all income tax. No incapacity or special-period claim is invented.

### Rotella v. Wood

Klehr's reservation is decisive for framing: it did **not** settle the injury-and-pattern-discovery question. One path begins limitations no later than discovery of an actionable injury, leaving the plaintiff to investigate its legal pattern and use diligent-concealment tolling when supported. Its strongest counterargument is that RICO requires a pattern and a claimant may know of harm before there is a legally actionable RICO claim. The competing path delays accrual until discovery of the injury and sufficient pattern, avoiding a period running before knowledge of the essential claim; its strongest objection is indefinite extension of a four-year period, departure from the Clayton Act analogy, and revival of old injury through later predicates.

Keep three different questions separate: initial accrual, a genuinely new injury directly caused by a later act, and tolling for concealment despite reasonable diligence. Zenith's actually unprovable damages qualification is not a generic delayed-investigation rule. Breyer's Klehr treatment, joined on its principal holdings by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies a date-eligible common boundary while expressly preserving the live trigger question. Steamfitters supplies direct-causation context, not a new limitations bar. The sanitized record establishes the concession that injury was known and all claim elements were complete by 1986, alleged pattern discovery in 1994 and filing in 1997. A claim arising before the pattern exists is therefore not this application. The ten-year predicate interval is not a ten-year civil filing period. The parties' presentation supports deciding the injury-and-pattern discovery issue without choosing universal injury-occurrence versus injury-discovery accrual. No tolling award or later-injury finding follows from a preserved doctrinal possibility alone.

### Rice v. Cayetano

Do not treat “not present here” in the packet's suggested political-status formulation as a neutral finding. One path treats ancestry as a racial proxy excluding citizens from an election of public state officials; Shaw preserves rather than rejects that independent injury, and the Fifteenth Amendment must be addressed on its own terms. Its counterargument is the asserted beneficiary-community and indigenous-trust relationship. The competing path treats the limited electorate as political participation in administration of a special indigenous trust, drawing a bounded analogy to Mancari and any established federal relationship; its counterargument is that a state public office and an ancestry class are not necessarily a tribe's internal electorate or a federally grounded membership classification.

The source addendum supplies the actual ancestry definitions, OHA's public powers and distinct sources of funds. The electorate is the broader Hawaiian class, not only the one-half-blood native-Hawaiian class; the Admission Act trust has five purposes rather than a single exclusive beneficiary. The special-purpose-district argument is also preserved as a separate legal analogy, requiring examination of OHA's actual function and the distinction between a limited franchise and a racial restriction. Neither a benign purpose nor the word ancestry answers classification. Adarand's common standard does not displace a distinct voting prohibition. Rayphand's expressly protected territorial settlement is not a general exception for a State's electorate. Prior Adarand/Hopwood positions distinguish justified racial measures from personal exclusion; the source-specific analysis may differ without treating those precedents as nonexistent. The separate sovereignty election and validity of the underlying programs/trusts are not before the Court; no source finding establishes federal tribal recognition.

### Roe v. Flores-Ortega

Smith v. Robbins is effective law. It permits equivalent safeguards, requires a meaningful advocate-assisted screening function, and restores that missing process without a showing that an omitted appeal probably would succeed. It preserves ordinary Strickland for deficient performance within a provided function. Peguero separately distinguishes a mandatory warning breach, causal loss of an appeal and appellate success. Neither answers every consultation duty after a plea.

One path applies contextual professional reasonableness to a defendant who gave no clear instruction: the plea, retained appeal rights, possible issues, expressed interest and actual consultation matter. Its counterargument is that the appeal choice is personal and a client cannot make it without advice. The competing path requires a regular consultation procedure or affirmative informed decision before counsel permits the appeal period to lapse; its counterargument is Strickland's rejection of mechanical performance rules and the possibility that the actual record already shows informed inaction. Both must distinguish an instruction to appeal, an instruction not to appeal, adequate consultation followed by silence, and no consultation. Do not assume silence is waiver.

For prejudice, actual loss caused by deficient assistance is distinct from probable appellate success and from the loss of an assistance function after an appeal exists. Smith's prior published disagreement between Stevens's functional account and Thomas's individual-performance account, and Peguero's common causation distinction, supply actual judicial sources for this fork. Hill's scheduling rule cannot decide the appeal choice. The source addendum establishes the **July 27, 1995 federal filing**, so Lindh's pre-AEDPA noncapital rule applies; Weeks's post-AEDPA gate cannot be imported. The limited federal hearing found no proven promise, no consent to nonfiling, little or no understanding of appeal and an actual conversation. It did not determine the full contents of consultation, an informed declination or probable appellate merit. The later attempted notice is evidence in its actual context, not an automatic answer to causation. Teague was a ground below and remains distinct from both performance and prejudice. The absence of an asserted express waiver and the guilty plea's actual terms preclude inventing a complete appeal waiver or agreed precise sentence.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

#### Lindh v. Murphy

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6298.

**Decided:** 1997-06-23.

##### The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application

**Holding:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments, including amended §2254(d), do not govern Lindh's noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The application-date construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case rule and the successive-petition provisions governed by Felker; it does not exempt every pending proceeding from every AEDPA provision.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer. Six Justices directly adopt the statutory rule, its limits and reversal with remand.

**Material application:** The express pending-case direction for chapter 154 in §107(c), contrasted with its omission for chapter 153 enacted alongside it, supplies the temporal construction. Lindh filed his noncapital application on July 9, 1992.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary statutory construction may establish temporal direction without a prescribed formula. No universal substantive/procedural classification, permanent constitutional entitlement to filing-date judicial rules, chapter 154 State-qualification ruling, or decision on confrontation, bias, harmlessness, exhaustion or entitlement to a writ is made.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit is reversed and must consider the appeal under pre-AEDPA habeas law; no writ is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Landgraf supplies the statutory-direction inquiry and Rivers its provision-specific operation. Felker is distinguished because its authorization-review proceedings began after enactment and its other Title I timing questions were reserved; its successive-petition and retained-original-authority holdings remain.
