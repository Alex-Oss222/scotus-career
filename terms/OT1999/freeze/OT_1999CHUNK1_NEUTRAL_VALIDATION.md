## Mandatory Paul P. law qualification

The packet's references to Doe v. SEPTA and a controlling informational-privacy rule are overbroad if read as recognition of a general constitutional right. Doe assumes without deciding a constitutional interest against unjustified acquisition and internal use of intimate medical information, then rejects the particular claim on its confined benefits-administration facts. Kennedy, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer joined that assumed-right application. It expressly neither recognizes nor rejects a general medical-information right, adopts the lower court's seven-factor formula, nor requires universal indispensability. Public dissemination is not indispensable to every possible claim, but broader internal use remains unresolved.

W.P.'s eight-Associate advance-review and State-persuasion ground rests on New Jersey's substantive restrictions on disclosure, not a general federal informational-privacy right. Its risk-classification clear-and-convincing rule has five direct supporters; the plan-specific rationale has four, while a five-Justice judgment preserves that injunction in that litigation. Do not manufacture a generally controlling plan-quantum rule. Sheets supplies a confined constitutional rule for intentional unjustified official public dissemination of objectively intimate marital and family information; its extension to nonpublic home addresses requires its own analysis. These qualifications restore the authority actually published and supply no proposed current-case vote.
