SUGGESTION OF MOOTNESS 


Pursuant to Rule 21 of the Rules of this Court, Re- 
spondent Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc. 
(“Laidlaw”) respectfully suggests that this case has be- 
come moot and that the Writ of Certiorari should be 
dismissed. This issue of mootness is in addition to the 
substantive issues of standing and mootness already be- 
fore the Court. Petitioners raised the specific issue in 
their brief of whether closure of Respondent's facility 
moots the case. The Petitioners did so, however, with- 
out support in the record, thereby precipitating this sub- 
mission in order to provide the Court with additional 
facts necessary for an evaluation of this issue. Respond- 
ent further respectfully requests that the Court suspend 
the remainder of the briefing schedule pending the Court's 
disposition of this Suggestion.

BACKGROUND 


The questions presented in this case pertain to the issue 
of whether a citizens group has standing to bring a citi- 
zens suit under Section 505 of the Clean Water Act, 33 
U.S.C. 1365(a), and whether that suit becomes moot 
because the remedy sought—civil penalties—will not re- 
dress the alleged injury pursuant to this Court's decision 
in Steel Company vy. Citizens for a Better Environment, 
523 U.S. 83 (1998). The question of a citizen plaintiff's 
entitlement to attorneys’ fees or litigation costs in a moot 
case is also before this Court. 


These questions arise out of a citizens suit filed by 
Petitioner’ Friends of the Earth (“FOE”) on June 12, 
1992 against Respondent Laidlaw claiming injury caused 
by Laidlaw’s alleged ongoing violations of a National 
Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (“NPDES”) per- 
mit that regulated wastewaster discharges from an in- 
cinerator at Laidlaw’s Roebuck, South Carolina facility. 
The district court found that, although Laidlaw had made 
good faith efforts to achieve compliance prior to the filing 
of the complaint, and that no harm to the environment 
had been caused by the permit violations, Laidlaw should 
pay a statutory penalty of $405,000.00 for NPDES per- 
mit violations occurring mostly before and, in a few 
minor circumstances, after the institution of the lawsuit. 
Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Servy- 
ices (TOC), Inc., 956 F. Supp. 588, 610 (D.S.C. 1997) 
(J.A. 180-181). Injunctive relief was denied in its en- 
tirety. Jd. at 611 (J.A. 183). On appeal, FOE aban- 
doned its request for injunctive relief and sought only an

ARGUMENT 
Permanent Closure of the Facility Has Mooted this Case 


A case is moot, and cannot be maintained, when 
““there is no reasonable expectation that the wrong will 
be repeated.’ Gwaltney of Smithfield, Ltd. v. Chesapeake 
Bay Foundation, Inc., 484 U.S. 49, 66 (1987), quoting 
United States v. W. T. Grant Co., 345 US. 629, 633 
(1953). 


Respondent's permanent closure of the Roebuck facility 
at issue in this case rendered the case moot because no 
actual “case or controversy” continued to exist between 
the parties. Article III standing requires both an “injury 
in fact—an invasion of legally-protected interest which is 
(a) concrete and particularized . . . and (b) actual or 
imminent, not conjectural or hypothetical,” and that the 
injury is capable of being “redressed by a favorable deci- 


4 


sion.” See Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife, 504 U.S. 555, 
560-61 (1992) (internal quotations omitted). A plain- 
tiff must be able to establish the standing requisites of 
injury in fact, causation, and redressability at each stage 
of the litigation. Jd. Here, the alleged injury that was 
the subject of the Petitioner's complaint—ongoing and 
future violations of Respondent's NPDES permit due to 
incinerator wastewater discharges at the Roebuck facility 
—no longer exists, and cannot reasonably be expected to 
recur. The discharge has stopped entirely and the facility 
is closed. The injury is no longer “actual or imminent.” 
See Los Angeles v. Lyons, 461 U.S. 95, 101-02 (1983) 
(“past exposure to illegal conduct does not in itself show 
a present case or controversy regarding injunctive relief 
. . . if unaccompanied by any continuing, present adverse 
effects”), citing O'Shea v. Littleton, 414 U.S. 488, 495- 
96 (1974). 


Petitioners attempt to address the issue of whether the 
facility closure moots this case by making three unsup- 
ported and illogical arguments in their brief on the merits. 
First, Petitioners assert that since the NPDES permit is 
still in effect, Laidlaw “may discharge in the future when- 
ever it cares to do so.” (Brief For Petitioners, p. 8, n.7). 
As clearly demonstrated by the Affidavit of the Roebuck 
facility's Environmental Manager, Charisse G. McLeod, 
Petitioners’ assertion is incorrect—the incinerator that 
generated the wastewater discharged under the NPDES 
permit has been removed and discarded. In fact, the en- 
tire facility is closed and most of the equipment has been 
dismantled and sold or discarded. (McLeod Affidavit). 


Second, Petitioners imply that Laidlaw’s retention of its 
NPDES permit indicates Laidlaw’s intent to engage in 
future NPDES discharges. (Brief For Petitioners, p. 8, 
n.7). As shown by Ms. McLeod's affidavit, however, the 


5 


NPDES permit was kept in effect to make the Roebuck 
site more attractive to a prospective purchaser, not in con- 
templation of any future NPDES discharges by Laidlaw. 
Moreover, Ms. McLeod's affidavit references and incor- 
porates a Consent Agreement, dated June 2, 1997, en- 
tered into between Laidlaw and the South Carolina De- 
partment of Health and Environmental Control (DHEC) 
that reflects, in pertinent part, that Laidlaw shut down 
the incinerator for economic reasons and that any viola- 
tion of the Agreement would be deemed a violation of 
state law. 


Third, Petitioners assert that even the permanent clo- 
sure of the facility would not moot the case because they 
have members affected by alleged, but unidentified, Safety 
Kleen/Laidlaw NPDES discharges at other affiliated cor- 
porations. (See Brief For Petitioners, p. 8, n.7). This as- 
sertion borders on the frivolous. No facts are provided in 
support of this allegation. More fundamentally, the case 
before this Court involves past NPDES permit violations 
only at Respondent's facility located in Roebuck, South 
Carolina. Respondent Laidlaw Environmental Services 
(TOC), Inc. has no other facilities. Understandably, Pe- 
titioners cite no authority for the proposition that moot- 
ness can be avoided by claiming a possible deterrent effect 
that penalties imposed on one company might have on 


another company not in the lawsuit and not alleged to be 
in violation of any permit. 


Laidlaw invites the Court's attention to the position 
taken in this case by the United States as amicus curiae, 
at page 15 of its brief, that “a defendant's voluntary ces- 
sation of permit violations does not moot a citizens suit 
unless the defendant demonstrates that the permit viola- 
tions will not recur.” (Emphasis added). The permanent 
closure of the facility, as described in Ms. McLeod's affi- 


6 


davit, demonstrates just that. In fact, the United States, 
in taking the same basic position in its brief opposing 
certiorari in another case, predicted this exact scenario, 
and a corresponding finding of mootness: 


In the case of Clean Water Act citizens suits, the 
commencement of compliance, even if accompanied 
by the initiation of new procedures or the installa- 
tion of new equipment, would not, as a general mat- 
ter, completely moot a case because, for example, 
the same neglectful behavior that led to the viola- 
tions may still recur in the form of neglectful ad- 
herence to the new procedures or neglectful opera- 
tion and maintenance of the new equipment. Ac- 
cordingly, we submit, a finding of mootness would 
generally be inappropriate absent a showing that the 
prospect of neglectful behavior itself is eliminated 
by, for example, the permanent closing of the facility 
or the permanent cessation of all discharges. See, 
e.g., Pawtuxet Cove Marina, Inc. vy. Ciba-Geiev 
Corp., 807 F.2d 1089 (1st Cir. 1986), cert. denied 
108 S. Ct. 484 (1987) (defendant completed tie-in 
with the municipal treatment facility and ceased dis- 
charging under its NPDES permit). In the absence 
of such a showing, Article III standing would con- 
tinue as well.

Simkins Industries, Inc. v. Sierra Club, No. 88-660 
(October Term, 1988), Brief for the 'Inited States 


as Amicus Curiae, at pages 11-12, n.11. (Emphasis

It is clear that the facts of this case satisfy even the 
Solicitor General's stringent standard for establishing 
mootness. Here, Laidlaw permanently closed, dismantled, 
and put the Roebuck facility up for sale for economic 
reasons. Laidlaw’s consideration of this course of action 
was reported in the South Carolina press as early as Jan- 
uary 28, 1997. (See “Laidlaw Mulling Options,” attached 


> 


to this submission). By June 2, 1997—when Laidlaw 
and DHEC executed a Consent Agreement—the final de- 
cision had been made. These facts make it patently clear 
that the closure decision was not a ruse to avoid this 
Court's review of the Fourth Circuit’s July 16, 1998 
decision. 


CONCLUSION 


This case became moot prior to the grant of the Peti- 
tion for Writ of Certiorari and, accordingly, the Writ 
should be dismissed, and the Fourth Circuit decision al- 
lowed to stand, because the Court had “no constitutional 
power to decide the merits” when it acted on the petition. 
U.S. Vancorp v. Bonner Mall Partnership, 513 U.S. 18, 
27 (1994). The Respondent further requests that this 
Court suspend the remainder of the briefing schedule in 
this case pending its disposition of this Suggestion of 
Mootness.

LAIDLAW MULLING OPTIONS 


Incinerator talking closure with DHEC 


By GARY HENDERSON 
Staff Writer 


A Laidlaw Environmental Services official said Monday 
the company is considering shutting down its Roebuck 
incinerator because the facility is producing “poor finan- 
cial results.” 


Henry Taylor, vice president of legal and regulatory 
affairs, said the plan to close the incinerator could be 
worked out with state officials by February. 


But it will be much longer before the incinerator ac- 
tually stops operating. 


“It might take a year, but certainly less than two 
years,” Taylor said. 


The decision about the possible shutdown of the in- 
cinerator came during negotiations with §.C. Department 
of Health and Environmental Control over Laidlaw’s 
operating permit, Taylor said. 


Laidlaw is currently permitted to burn 4.5 tons of haz- 
ardous waste per hour year around. During the permit 
process, samples of gases being emitted from a trial burn 
of the incinerator must be submitted to the state’s health 
authorities. 


“There was a dispute about the capacity we could 
burn,” Taylor said. “They wanted to go down to 3.7 tons, 
and they didn’t think we provided quality samples. We 
felt we did.” 


2a 


Taylor said Laidlaw officials then decided to approach 
DHEC about closing the incinerator. 


“I think they were happy to see us come with the idea,” 
Taylor said, “but they were shocked.” 


Taylor said it is difficult to make a profit at the Roe- 
buck facility because there are so many incinerators oper- 
ating in the United States. He said kilns used for making 
cement now burn hazardous materials as fuel, reducing 
the demand for incinerators even further. 


“It is highly competitive,” Taylor said. “There is a 
real over-capacity—more incinerators than there is ma- 
terial to burn.” 


Taylor said if the incinerator closes, Laidlaw would 
still use the Roebuck site as a transfer and holding facility. 


“This would become a collection point for small quan- 
tities that would be combined and shipped to another in- 
cinerator site,” Taylor said. 


Dever Melton, a Roebuck resident who has worked 
with other community activists to get the Laidlaw incin- 
erator shut down, said he wants the entire facility closed. 


“We will not be satisfied until there are no more toxic 
chemicals brought to Roebuck,” Melton said, “We will 
not be satisfied until they stop hauling the stuff down the 
road or bringing it in on the rail cars.” 


Taylor said that if the incinerator is shut down, the 
facility’s work force of 48 employees would probably be 
reduced. 


“We don’t have a feel for that yet,” Taylor said, “We 
will need some different skills from the ones we have now, 
but the people working there will have an opportunity to 
continue working with Laidlaw.” 


Hazardous wastes are shipped to the Spartanburg County 
facility from more than 40 states. Laidlaw, a Canadian- 


3a 


based company, operates collection and transfer sites in 
11 states and in Canada. 


A merger between Laidlaw and Rollins Environmental 
was announced earlier this month, which would give the 
merged company 120 facilities. Rollins Environmental is 
a New Jersey-based hazardous waste management com- 
pany. 


‘We will not be satisfied until there are no more 
toxic chemicals brought to Roebuck.’ 


Dever Melton 


Taylor said the merged company has 120 facilities 


around the country, which will probably be reduced to 
100. 


Explosions have occurred twice at the Roebuck plant 
in the last five months after several types of hazardous 
waste were transferred to a holding tank. 


Officials evacuated eight homes in the area last week 
and advised residents of another 65 homes to stay inside 
after a 500-gallon tank exploded. The explosion emitted 


a large cloud of sulfurous vapors, which the wind blew 
about one-half mile from the site. 


“If we have caused any fears or concern among the 
citizenry, we hope it will vanish,” Taylor said. “We want 


our operation to be one that protects the people and the 
environment. It will continue to be.” 


Taylor said the negotiations with DHEC about the in- 
cinerators will continue. 


“It’s not final, even though we are moving in that di- 
rection,” Taylor said.

to complete construction of the converted treatment ca- 
pacity before the date it surrenders its interim status in- 
cineration capacity and will therefore review the process 
design proposal in a timely manner. The process design 
proposal shall consist of a description of the proposed 
process design and the calculated caacity as derived from 
all or part of the 4.5 tons per hour of incineration ca- 
pacity. The cumulative total of the converted treatment 
capacity, which shall consist of fuels blending, shredding,
