This reply is submitted to address the argument set 
forth at page 3 of Petitioners’ Response Brief that the 
facility closure issue had been faised and briefed at the 
certiorari stage of the litigation and, further, that this 
Court’s granting of certiorari amounted to a determina- 
tion that the closure of the facility did not moot the case.’ 


1 By choosing to address this single argument, Respondent does 
not in any way acknowledge the validity of the Petitioners’ alter- 
native arguments. 


2 


That is an overstatement given the context in which the 
issue arose. 


First, it should be borne in mind that there are several 
variations of “mootness.” In Friends of the Earth v. 
Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc., 149 F.3d 
303 (4th Cir. 1998), the Fourth Circuit reasoned that 
the case had become moot because the sole remedy sought 
by Petitioners on appeal—civil penalties—would not re- 
dress any alleged injuries. /d. at 305. At page 19 of its 
Petition for a Writ of Certiorari, Petitioners raised for the 
first time the issue of mootness that, under United States 
v. W. T. Grant Co., 345 U.S. 629 (1953) and its prog- 
eny, occurs when intervening events or changed circum- 
stances during the pendency of a case establish that there 
is no reasonable expectation that the alleged wrongful 
conduct would recur. This issue was not before the 
Fourth Circuit. Having just raised the issue, the Petition- 
ers then argued that “Laidlaw could easily change its 
practice again at any time in the future.” Petition for 
Certiorari at p. 19. 


Respondent addressed the Petitioners’ assertion at page 
10 of Respondent’s Brief in Opposition, stating that “. . . 
there is no possibility that a wrong will be repeated due 
to the closure of the facility.” Similarly, page 7 of the 
brief filed by Amici Curiae South Carolina Chamber of 
Commerce et al. supporting Respondent, in disputing the 
Petitioners’ assertion, noted that the “. . . facility has been 
in closure since August 1998 pursuant to a Consent Order 
entered into with DHEC.” Neither brief discussed the 
issue in any detail. 


FOE responded with several arguments on the facility 
closure issue, including, inter alia, the following at page 
4 of its Reply Brief: 

3. Laidlaw suggests that the case is moot because 
it has shut down its incinerator since the Court

After Petitioners’ Petition for a Writ of Certiorari was 
granted, essentially the same arguments were repeated at 
page 8, n. 7 of the Brief for Petitioners.* 


At that point, Respondent significantly increased its 
focus on the facility closure issue and obtained additional 
information on the extent of and the circumstances sur- 
rounding the closure. The Respondent’s Suggestion of 
Mootness followed, supported by the Affidavit of Charisse 
G. McLeod, which established that, for economic reasons, 
the Roebuck, South Carolina facility had been perma- 
nently closed, equipment removed and dismantled, and 
the property put up for sale. Stated otherwise, the Re- 
spondent simply addressed the criticism set forth at page 
4 of Petitioners’ Reply Brief regarding the absence of this 
type of information. 


Prior to the filing of the Suggestion of Mootness, the 
facility closure issue had been ancillary to the issues be- 
fore the Fourth Circuit and upon which Petitioners’ Peti- 
tion for a Writ of Certiorari was based. It was not until 
the Suggestion of Mootness was filed that this Court had 
sufficient facts to evaluate this issue. Those facts indicate 
unequivocally that the facility has been permanently
