### Flippo v. West Virginia, No. 98-8770

Summary merits disposition, October 18, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted without oral argument. On writ of certiorari to the Circuit Court of West Virginia, Fayette County, after the State Supreme Court denied discretionary review. The question is whether a general homicide-scene exception permitted the prolonged warrantless evidentiary search of the secured cabin and opening of a closed briefcase.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The petition for certiorari is granted, and the Court summarily reverses by a reasoned per curiam opinion. No individual petition vote is disclosed.

The Circuit Court's judgment cannot rest on a general homicide-scene exception. The state courts may consider preserved, legally supported independent warrant exceptions and harmless error. No acquittal, unconditional suppression, new trial, or resolution of unpresented retrial questions is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

The Court issues a per curiam opinion, joined in full by all nine Justices, unanimously reversing and remanding.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Emergency authority does not become general investigative authority.

Police may enter to render emergency aid, locate victims or a dangerous person, and take supported protective or evidence-preservation measures within their actual justification. Once that justification ends, the occurrence of a homicide does not authorize a prolonged warrantless evidentiary search.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion supplies the controlling rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** Mincey and Thompson reject an exception based on the crime's gravity. Here the cabin had been secured before the extended evidence-gathering search, and the trial court authorized a search of everything at the scene without tying it to an existing emergency. Dickerson likewise confines a lawful initial intrusion to its authorized purpose. The accepted emergency entry therefore cannot sustain the later categorical search. This conclusion preserves properly supported emergency action on its own facts.

##### A closed briefcase requires separate search authority.

Seeing an unlocked but closed briefcase does not expose the photographs and negatives concealed within it. Opening it requires a warrant or a separately established exception.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Hicks distinguishes observation from an additional search. The briefcase's location near the body and unlocked condition did not reveal its contents or create authority to inspect them. The trial court made no findings adequate for this Court to resolve implied consent, standardized inventory, another exigency, or harmlessness. Those preserved questions remain for the state courts; absence of findings does not establish that every alternative fails.

#### Precedent Treatment

Mincey v. Arizona and Thompson v. Louisiana are applied to reject a homicide-scene exception. Minnesota v. Dickerson is applied for the limit imposed by an intrusion's actual authorization. Arizona v. Hicks is applied to distinguish viewing a container from searching concealed contents. Chapman v. California remains the standard for any properly presented harmless constitutional error. No precedent is overruled or broadened into an unconditional suppression rule.

#### Law After Decision

The warrant default and recognized, fact-dependent exceptions remain unchanged. A secured homicide scene and a closed container cannot be searched on a categorical crime-scene rationale; independent justifications and harmlessness retain their separate requirements.

#### Source Notes

The Fayette County suppression order and the state denial of discretionary review establish the route and the grounds addressed below. The published record at 528 U.S. 11 supports the cabin, briefcase, and unresolved-alternative facts. Mincey, 437 U.S. 385; Thompson, 469 U.S. 17; Hicks, 480 U.S. 321; and Chapman, 386 U.S. 18 supply the identified surviving rules.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the state courts for proceedings consistent with the rejection of the categorical rationale, including only preserved and legally supported alternative exceptions and harmlessness. Attenuation and other unpresented remedial questions are not decided.

**End of entry: Flippo v. West Virginia, summary merits disposition, October 18, 1999.**

### Texas v. Lesage, No. 98-1111

Summary merits disposition, November 29, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted without oral argument. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit. The question is what a same-decision showing defeats when a rejected applicant alleges an unconstitutional admissions process, and what distinct retrospective, statutory, and prospective claims remain.

#### Judgment

**Reversed in the stated respect and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The Fifth Circuit's judgment is reversed insofar as it treated the same-decision showing as legally irrelevant to outcome-based liability and relief. It must assess Texas's showing under the school's burden to prove the same result under a specified constitutional process, preserving the genuine-dispute requirements of summary judgment. The entire defense judgment is not reinstated. Claims under §§1981 and 1983 and Title VI, proper defendants, immunity, nominal or declaratory relief, and continuing prospective exposure require separate treatment.

#### Opinion Topology

The Court issues a per curiam opinion, joined in full by all nine Justices, unanimously reversing in the stated respect and remanding.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Same-decision proof limits relief for the rejection without erasing a completed process injury.

Once an unconstitutional admissions process has been established, the school must prove by a preponderance that a specified constitutional process would have produced the same rejection. Success defeats admission and compensation for that denial and its consequences; it does not erase the separately established unequal-process injury.

**Authority:** The unanimous per curiam opinion controls every proposition stated here.

**Controlling explanation:** Hopwood separates unequal opportunity to compete from entitlement to a seat and supplies the school-borne defense. The lawful counterfactual must allow any race use that controlling law permits; it cannot silently become a race-neutral-only requirement. Texas offered academic and qualitative evidence supporting the same rejection, but the Fifth Circuit treated its causal significance as irrelevant instead of testing the specified lawful process and burden. Its legal premise was mistaken. The evidence is not newly declared conclusive, and the Court does not decide whether the challenged program was unconstitutional merely because it considered race.

##### Relief follows the particular injury and lawful remedial channel.

An established unconstitutional process may support nominal or declaratory recognition even if Texas proves the same lawful rejection, but only under an available cause against a proper defendant and subject to immunity. Prospective relief additionally requires concrete continuing or imminent exposure to unequal competition and an equitable basis.

**Controlling explanation:** Carey distinguishes recognition of a completed violation from compensation for proved loss; Northeastern Florida distinguishes unequal competition from guaranteed selection. Sections 1981 and 1983 and Title VI retain their separate requirements. Possible cessation of the practice or abandonment of prospective relief must be resolved, not assumed. No admission, damages amount, immunity waiver, personal liability or broad future-program injunction follows from this decision.

#### Precedent Treatment

Hopwood v. Texas is applied to the specified lawful counterfactual, preponderance defense, independent process injury, source-specific remedies and future-exposure requirement; its racial-classification standard and permitted-use boundaries remain unchanged. Mt. Healthy City Board of Education v. Doyle supplies the causal same-decision distinction through Hopwood. Carey v. Piphus supplies the distinction between compensable harm and nominal recognition. Northeastern Florida Chapter v. Jacksonville supplies the competitive-injury principle without guaranteeing admission.

#### Law After Decision

Existing admissions law remains controlling. A successful same-decision defense defeats relief caused by the lost admission, while an independently proved unconstitutional process and any continuing unequal competition retain their own causes, defenses, standing requirements and remedies. Statistical comparisons alone do not replace the required lawful counterfactual.

#### Source Notes

The judgment below is Lesage v. Texas, 158 F.3d 213. Its treatment of the same-decision evidence, the district-court comparisons, and the pleaded statutory and equitable claims define the remand. Hopwood v. Texas, November 25, 1996, supplies the controlling admissions and remedial rules.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Fifth Circuit for application of the school-borne same-decision burden and for claim-specific proceedings. Prospective relief must be dismissed unless concrete continuing or imminent exposure is established; whether the claim was abandoned or the policy ceased remains for express resolution. No particular relief is awarded.

**End of entry: Texas v. Lesage, summary merits disposition, November 29, 1999.**

### Fiore v. White, No. 98-942

Certification, November 30, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 12, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit. The federal question concerns the validity of Fiore's conviction in light of Commonwealth v. Scarpone's construction of the same Pennsylvania offense. The antecedent question is whether that construction states the statute's meaning when Fiore's conviction became final or changed the law.

#### Public Action

The Court unanimously certifies to the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania whether Commonwealth v. Scarpone stated the correct interpretation of 35 Pa. Stat. §6018.401(a) when Fiore's conviction became final, or instead changed the law. The Court requests the state court's explanation of that temporal meaning, retains jurisdiction, and reserves judgment and further proceedings.

No federal merits judgment or habeas relief is entered. All nine Justices—Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer—support certification and retention of jurisdiction. Neither the Third Circuit's judgment nor the conviction is affirmed or reversed at this event.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Stevens delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The opinion unanimously orders certification and reserves the federal judgment.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### An authoritative state-law answer should precede the dependent federal decision.

In this live federal controversy, the Court may certify whether the State Supreme Court's later construction declared the statute's preexisting meaning or changed it, retain jurisdiction, and reserve the federal judgment. Certification neither creates jurisdiction nor transfers the federal constitutional question to the state court.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls this procedural determination.

**Controlling explanation:** Fiore possessed a permit but was prosecuted on the theory that his operational departures made the facility unpermitted. Scarpone's parallel prosecution produced a later authoritative construction rejecting that statutory theory. Whether that construction describes the law at Fiore's finality determines the premise of his federal claim. Arizonans permits certification to resolve an uncertain state-law question within a live controversy; it does not permit certification to repair absent jurisdiction. Immediate habeas relief would assume the original-meaning answer, while immediate rejection on retroactivity would assume a changed-law premise. Neither is established.

**Questions reserved:** The Court decides no state-law meaning, federal sufficiency, retroactivity, fair-notice issue, or entitlement to habeas relief. Winship and Jackson may bear on a missing-element claim after the answer, but they supply no federal disposition today.

#### Precedent Treatment

Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona is applied to certification within an existing live controversy, preserving its jurisdictional limit. In re Winship and Jackson v. Virginia remain available for the federal proof-of-elements question but are not applied to decide Fiore's conviction. Commonwealth v. Scarpone supplies the state construction whose temporal meaning is certified; no answer about that meaning is presumed.

#### Law After Decision

The Court establishes only this procedural certification and reservation. The elements of the Pennsylvania offense at Fiore's finality and the federal consequence remain unresolved; no new criminal or habeas merits rule takes effect.

#### Source Notes

Fiore v. White, 149 F.3d 221, supplies the federal appellate posture. Commonwealth v. Scarpone, 535 Pa. 273, 634 A.2d 1109, supplies the later state construction. The permit and parallel-prosecution facts explain the certified question without deciding its answer.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Jurisdiction remains in this Court while the certified question is before the Supreme Court of Pennsylvania. After its response, the case will return for consideration under the authoritative state-law premise. No response deadline, release order, remand of the federal action or subsequent decision is entered.

**End of entry: Fiore v. White, certification, November 30, 1999.**

### Paul P. v. Verniero

Merits decision, December 6, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Review of the Third Circuit's judgment, 170 F.3d 396, in No. 97-5791. Certiorari granted October 4, 1999; argument or submission November 15, 1999.

The District Court for the District of New Jersey granted the State defendants summary judgment on the privacy challenge. The Third Circuit affirmed that judgment and remanded for consideration of safeguards and material tendered in motions to supplement. The class consists of Tier Two and Tier Three registrants whose offenses followed enactment of New Jersey's notification law. The questions granted concern constitutional protection for nonpublic home-address and identifying information and the governing balancing and dissemination safeguards. The separate ruling about autonomous family relationships is outside review.

#### Judgment

**Judgment:** The Third Circuit's judgment is affirmed within the questions granted, with its safeguards remand retained. The component positions are distinct:

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Public conviction information and a protective notification purpose

**Holding and operative rule.** This informational-privacy claim does not confer a right to conceal public conviction information. New Jersey may pursue the legitimate and substantial objective of communicating accurate risk information to persons with a concrete safety need, subject to the separate protection and safeguards governing nonpublic residential information.

**Authority.** Part I of Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls both propositions. Their agreement does not extend to every disclosure method or to all of the opinion's later parts.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a public conviction does not become confidential because notification repeats it or causes adverse attention. Paul v. Davis rejects a constitutional privacy entitlement to conceal the public record of an arrest; its distinction likewise forecloses treating public conviction facts themselves as private. That conclusion does not decide the different injury alleged from deliberate dissemination of a nonpublic current residential location. Protecting persons likely to encounter an offender is a legitimate governmental function. The present holding approves that objective, not every audience, datum or later use selected in its name. Recognition of the objective leaves the Court's separate residential-information inquiry necessary.

##### Limited protection for compiled nonpublic residential information

**Holding and operative rule.** When the State compels collection and deliberately disseminates compiled nonpublic current home-address and associated identifying information in a manner exposing the home and household to outside contact, the Fourteenth Amendment protects a limited personal privacy interest against disclosure lacking a sufficient connection among the information, its recipients and the protective purpose. The protection does not extend merely by reason of government possession to public convictions, every identifying datum, every address available elsewhere, or all government-held information; actual public availability matters, but theoretical inspectability alone does not establish prior dissemination.

**Authority.** Part II of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These seven Justices directly adopt this limited extension and its boundaries; no combination of separate rationales is needed.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that exposing a nonpublic household location differs from repeating the public fact of conviction. Sheets v. Salt Lake County protects intimate marital and family information against intentional official dissemination without sufficient governmental justification. Its attention to actual exposure, rather than possession or ownership alone, supports this limited extension to the deliberate dissemination of compiled residential information. Doe v. SEPTA assumed a medical-information interest and rejected a claim arising from confined benefits administration; it neither established a general right nor authorized public dissemination detached from the governmental function. Its reservation leaves this outward disclosure question open. The objection that addresses are often readily found does not answer whether these current residential locations are nonpublic and what exposure the State's compilation and distribution produce. Conversely, privacy does not follow from an individual's preference alone. The Court recognizes no general secrecy entitlement in identifiers, no universal constitutional balancing formula, and no independent constitutional rule for the effort needed to assemble otherwise public scattered data. The interest here rests on nonpublic residential exposure, not embarrassment about a conviction.

##### Notification requires safeguards fitted to its protective function

**Holding and operative rule.** Disclosure of protected residential information through Tier Two or Tier Three notification must reasonably fit the notification's protective function: the actual information and recipient audience must have a sufficient safety connection, and safeguards must constrain unnecessary disclosure and redistribution. The protective objective alone does not establish that every distribution or subsequent use is justified; the District Court must consider the adequacy of safeguards on the actual record, without a universal least-restrictive-means requirement, a guarantee of perfect containment, a prescribed assurance form, or a newly established proof quantum.

**Authority.** Part III of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These seven Justices adopt both the rule and retention of the safeguards remand.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a proper reason to notify one audience is not a sufficient reason to expose the same information to everyone. Sheets requires a governmental justification for the actual disclosure; Doe preserves the distinction between functionally confined use and outward dissemination. Those principles permit practical public-safety notification while requiring attention to recipients, scope, use, confidentiality and redistribution. The danger that information cannot be recalled supports a concrete safeguards inquiry, not a ban on all notification. The motions tendered first to the Court of Appeals belong before the District Court for consideration of the challenged applications and any appropriate response; their contents are not adjudicated facts. The Court does not determine whether particular existing precautions are adequate, whether particular alleged harm occurred, or whether every recipient's later speech is attributable to the State. It prescribes no restriction on independently protected private expression. W.P.'s independent state-entitlement and ordinary advance-process requirements remain at their actual scope; this decision neither enlarges its class nor converts its four-Justice notification-plan proof rationale into a general controlling standard.

##### The notification provisions remain available while application-specific safeguards are examined

**Holding and operative rule.** Recognition of the limited residential interest does not invalidate the notification provisions as a matter of law: properly directed public-safety notification may disclose protected information. The summary judgment rejecting the challenge to those provisions is affirmed, while the distinct remand permits the District Court to examine whether the ways notification is applied provide adequate protection against unnecessary disclosure.

**Authority.** Part IV of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These six Justices supply the controlling legal explanation; Scalia and Thomas add judgment-only votes to make the information-disclosure affirmance 8–1, on their no-established-substantive-right ground. Stone-Zsela joins the substantive protection and safeguards rule but opposes this affirmance and does not join its rationale. The two judgment-only votes add no support to Part IV's reasoning.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the protected interest and the public-safety function can coexist. The challenge to notification as a matter of law cannot succeed merely because a properly selected safety audience receives residential information. The State's substantial protective interest supports that limited legal conclusion; it is not a finding that all present applications are sufficiently confined. The Court of Appeals separately required examination of the information offered about actual distribution and safeguards, so affirmance does not extinguish that inquiry. The objection that summary judgment must be vacated until safeguards are adjudicated treats the permitted notification function and its challenged implementation as inseparable. The six Justices preserve the judgment against the former challenge and the remand concerning the latter. They do not resolve the contents of the sealed motions, approve any unproved dissemination, or bar relief that the safeguards proceedings lawfully establish. No independently sufficient alternative ground, categorical denial of privacy, or generalized seven-factor test is adopted.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Paul v. Davis, 424 U.S. 693 (1976):** Applied to public arrest and conviction information and the boundary against reputation alone; it does not foreclose the distinct nonpublic residential-disclosure interest recognized here.
- **Whalen v. Roe, 429 U.S. 589 (1977):** Its distinction between avoiding disclosure of personal matters and autonomous decisionmaking remains. As developed in Sheets, its confidentiality foundation permits safeguarded governmental information functions; no universal compelling-interest or least-intrusive-means formula is drawn from it.
- **Sheets v. Salt Lake County, April 29, 1996:** Its protection against unjustified intentional official dissemination of intimate marital and family information is extended to the confined residential exposure described here. Its limits concerning actual disclosure, public inspectability, government justification and private speech remain.
- **Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority, May 5, 1997:** Distinguished because its assumed-right holding concerns confined benefits administration. It remains an assumed-right application and supplies neither a general informational-privacy right nor the lower court's seven-factor constitutional formula.
- **W.P. v. Verniero, March 16, 1998:** Preserved at its actual scope. Ordinary Tier Two or Tier Three notification for its pre-enactment-offense class requires meaningful advance review of classification and plan, with State persuasion; risk classification requires clear and convincing proof. Its retained plan injunction binds that litigation, but its plan-specific proof reasoning has only four joins and establishes no general plan-proof quantum. Its state-entitlement basis, civil-notification punishment ruling and reserved emergency question are unchanged. The present post-enactment-offense class gains the substantive protection stated here without reopening or enlarging W.P.'s adjudicated components.

No precedent is overruled. The statutory privacy decisions discussed below are not transformed into constitutional commands; this decision adopts no independent rule that compilation alone makes public information constitutionally private.

#### Law After Decision

As of December 6, 1999, the Fourteenth Amendment supplies a limited protection against unjustified deliberate official dissemination of compiled nonpublic current residential information exposing a home and household to outside contact. This extends Sheets beyond its intimate marital-information setting without establishing a general right to control government-held information. A sufficient connection among information, safety audience and protective function, together with reasonably fitted safeguards against unnecessary disclosure and redistribution, governs the notification at issue.

Public conviction information remains outside this secrecy claim. Properly directed notification remains permissible, and the eight-Justice affirmance of the information-disclosure summary judgment, supported by a six-Justice controlling rationale, coexists with the seven-Justice safeguards remand. Doe retains its assumed-right, confined-use holding; W.P.'s independent state-entitlement and procedural rules retain their existing limits. No general heightened plan-proof standard, automatic secrecy for all compiled public records, liability for private redistribution, or categorical restraint on notification follows.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Parts I–III: public conviction information is unprotected by this claim, the State has a legitimate protective objective, compiled nonpublic residential information carries a limited privacy interest, and disclosure needs safeguards reasonably fitted to its protective function. He dissents from affirming summary judgment insofar as a compelling-interest balance alone defeats the claim concerning compiled nonpublic home-address and identifying information. In his view, the safeguards inquiry determines whether that disclosure is justified and therefore must govern adjudication of the claim itself, not only an ancillary examination of implementation after its rejection. He would vacate that part of the affirmance and remand the claim for decision in the District Court under the fitted-safeguards rule, together with the retained safeguards proceedings. His position requires meaningful controls on recipients, use, scope, confidentiality and redistribution, while allowing targeted notification to persons with an actual safety need. It conceals no public conviction, imposes no injunction or interim restraint, and leaves the ungranted family-relationships ruling undisturbed.

**Justice Scalia, joined by Justice Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They agree with Part I's public-conviction and legitimate-purpose propositions but reject extending substantive due process to this residential-information claim. Their Sheets dissents and Doe concurrences distinguish a supported constitutional source from a desire to restrict the government's handling of information. A home address is not rendered constitutionally intimate simply through compilation, and W.P.'s protection rests on an independent state-created substantive restriction rather than a universal federal information right. They affirm the State's information-disclosure summary judgment because the proposed substantive constitutional right has not been established, without joining the Court's limited-interest and balancing rationale. They would vacate the privacy-right ruling and the safeguard directive insofar as dependent on it, and remand consistently with that rejection while preserving W.P.'s existing independent process requirements. They neither adopt Stone-Zsela's right-based merits remand nor deny every possible constitutional protection of the home.

#### Source Notes

The published lower-court opinion, [Paul P. v. Verniero, 170 F.3d 396](https://www.sillscummis.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/170_F3d_396.pdf), supplies the class, procedural posture, district-court ruling, appellate disposition, and the existence and treatment of the motions to supplement; an [independent reproduction](https://openjurist.org/170/f3d/396/paul-q-v-verniero) supplies the same opinion. The Court's decision rests on the disclosed legal question and the remand's record limits: the sealed motion contents do not establish new findings here, and the actual sufficiency of particular dissemination safeguards remains for the District Court. The grant and argument-or-submission dates identify this Court's calendar; the Third Circuit's own argument occurred July 9, 1998 and its judgment issued March 16, 1999. No later proceeding supplies a factual premise or legal rule for this decision.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Supreme Court merits review is concluded. The case returns through the Third Circuit to the District Court for the safeguards proceedings already directed, including consideration of the material the parties sought to add and any relief its supported findings warrant. The summary-judgment component remains affirmed as stated; the family-relationships ruling remains outside this review. There is no new injunction, interim restraint, filing deadline, presumption of misconduct, or ordered follow-up in this Court.

### Drye v. United States, No. 98-1101

Merits decision, December 7, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 8, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit. The question is whether an outstanding federal tax lien reaches the taxpayer's state-created power to accept an inheritance or redirect it by disclaimer.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

The Eighth Circuit's judgment sustaining the federal tax lien is affirmed. The lien reaches Drye's established power over the inheritance. The judgment determines no valuation, recipient liability, competing creditor priority or additional accounting issue.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Souter delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The opinion unanimously sustains the lien.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Federal lien law reaches the taxpayer's existing power to accept or redirect inherited value.

A legally effective state-created power to accept an inheritance or channel its value to another by disclaimer is a right to property within 26 U.S.C. §6321 on this record. State law defines the control, but federal law determines whether the lien reaches it; state relation back cannot erase control that already existed for federal lien purposes.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, states the unanimous rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** National Bank of Commerce separates the existence of a state-law interest from its federal tax treatment. Drye had the choice to receive the value or direct its destination. The strongest contrary argument—that state law treats a disclaimer as effective from death—describes the local consequence of exercising that choice; it does not negate the choice itself. Irvine similarly rejects using state relation back to control an independently defined federal tax consequence. Its gift-tax timing analysis does not become a lien condition here. Romani requires respect for Congress's specific priority choices and supplies no broad exemption for a disclaimer. The lien therefore reaches this existing control without creating an inheritance interest federal law alone would supply.

**Limits:** The Court does not reach a disclaimer completed before legally cognizable control arises, every prior expectancy, a spendthrift-trust interest, valuation, the recipient's liability, or any competing priority.

#### Precedent Treatment

United States v. National Bank of Commerce is applied for state definition of rights followed by federal lien classification. United States v. Irvine is applied by analogy to the distinction between local disclaimer effects and federal tax consequences; its gift-tax reasonable-time exception is not imported into §6321. United States v. Estate of Romani preserves specific statutory priority protections and is distinguished from the present attachment question. No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

The taxpayer's actual power to accept or redirect inherited property is reachable under §6321 despite the state-law relation-back consequence of disclaimer. State law continues to define the interest, and Congress's specific priority rules remain effective.

#### Source Notes

The Eighth Circuit's judgment and the published record at 528 U.S. 49 support the inheritance-control and lien posture. Section 6321 supplies the lien text; National Bank of Commerce, 472 U.S. 713, supplies the surviving state-right/federal-classification rule.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Eighth Circuit's judgment remains in place. Any necessary accounting remains below under otherwise governing law; the Court orders no new valuation or distribution proceeding and decides no additional liability.

**End of entry: Drye v. United States, merits decision, December 7, 1999.**

### Los Angeles Police Department v. United Reporting Publishing Corp., No. 98-678

Merits decision, December 7, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 13, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 146 F.3d 1133. The questions concern First Amendment review of California's purpose and sales-use conditions on access to current arrest addresses, the publisher's maintained claims of its own injury, and the permissible scope of judgment and relief. Only LAPD appealed the district judgment.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Effect |

| Ninth Circuit judgment | Set aside and remanded, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stevens, Kennedy | Stone-Zsela agrees in this judgment only; the seven do not share a single rationale |

No instruction concerning dissolution or continuation of the existing declaratory and injunctive relief commands five votes. The Court issues neither instruction. The Ninth Circuit addresses the actual decree and the parties properly before it under governing law. The separate reported restraint on the Attorney General is not dissolved through LAPD's appeal. #### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court in Parts I–II; opinion announcing the judgment and plurality in Part III | Breyer | Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg throughout; Stone-Zsela in Part I; Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy in Parts I–II | Announces set-aside and remand; Part III explains the four-Justice open-application position | Part I has nine joins, Part II eight, Part III four |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. A speech-selected access and use condition requires First Amendment review.

When the State makes covered records available but denies access or restricts use because the requester intends truthful, lawful commercial solicitation or the conceded commercial address-distribution activity, the condition is subject to the governing commercial-speech standard. The absence of a general right to government information does not exempt that selection: the State must identify a substantial interest, establish real harm and material advancement, and show reasonable fit.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, is unanimous on this rule. It does not resolve the disputed commercial application.

**Controlling explanation:** Lanphere separates a general demand for records from a condition selecting among intended speech uses. Houchins supplies no universal right to information; it does not answer the different condition question. Central Hudson supplies review of lawful, nonmisleading commercial activity; Edenfield requires real harm and material advancement; Fox requires reasonable fit without demanding the least restrictive means. The lawful, nonmisleading premise accepted below remains operative for that activity and does not require production of each future letter. Privacy is a substantial interest, but its existence does not itself prove the validity of this selected line. No right to creation of lists, universal criminal-file access, protection for deception or coercion, or exemption from neutral confidentiality follows. Profit motive alone does not settle the classification of the Register's editorial or mixed material. The separately described own-direct-sales proposal and independently acquired information are not adjudicated through the accepted premise below.

##### 2. The publisher's own claim is not disposed of by labeling the judgment facial.

A plaintiff that pleaded and maintained injury from a rule applied to its own activity may litigate that application without relying on absent speakers' rights. Commercial overbreadth cannot replace proof of a violation, but the existence of valid regulation against deception, coercion or harassment does not by itself establish the validity of this categorical condition; any relief must fit the established violation and equitable need.

**Authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight votes. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** Lanphere's application-specific adjudication and relief require attention to the actual claimant and burden. Broadrick and Ferber's exceptional facial-overbreadth principles do not eliminate a maintained own-injury claim or establish universal relief for commercial speech. Petitioners acknowledged facial and as-applied pleading; the publisher continued to assert its own denial and actual address-service activity. The Register also contains substantive editorial material. Neither the lower courts' facial description nor a contested cross-petition argument establishes abandonment. These facts require consideration of the actual claims; they do not establish their merits, classify every communication, or prove that the signed decree exceeds lawful bounds.

##### 3. Alternative grounds and distinct claims retain their proper appellate scope.

A record ground may defend an existing favorable judgment without a cross-petition when it does not enlarge that judgment; overturning a distinct adverse judgment or enlarging relief requires the necessary review. The maintained Register theory, separately described direct-sales use, and actually preserved alternative grounds remain for consideration within that distinction, without a present ruling on their merits.

**Authority:** Part II and the same eight-Justice coalition control this rule and reservation.

**Controlling explanation:** X-Citement Video preserves record-based grounds supporting a favorable judgment while respecting the limit on affirmative enlargement. It does not require immediate adjudication of every alternative. The actual relief sought and granted must be identified before that rule is applied here. The Court decides no vagueness, equal-protection, due-process, state-constitutional or separate statutory-entitlement question. The Register theory, subscriber/address-service activity and proposed own-direct-sales use remain distinct; an asserted denial for inability to certify does not establish an agency decision on a completed request limited to qualifying journalism.

**Judgment without a single controlling rationale on the commercial remand.** Five Justices leave the commercial application open, but only four join Part III's explanation. Stone-Zsela's different facial and remedial ground does not form a logical subset of that explanation. O'Connor and Scalia would sustain the commercial application, and Stevens and Kennedy would invalidate it. No unified rationale under Marks is adopted. The common legal standards above control; the plurality's detailed assessment of privacy predictions and proposed treatment of relief do not become holdings.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lanphere & Urbaniak v. Colorado is applied to the speech-selected access/use condition and to application-specific relief; no universal right to government records is added.
- Houchins v. KQED is preserved on the absence of a general access entitlement and distinguished from scrutiny of conditions in an existing disclosure program.
- Central Hudson supplies commercial-speech review; Cincinnati v. Discovery Network supplies the required relation between the selected category and the harm; Edenfield v. Fane supplies real-harm and material-advancement requirements. None decides this disputed application by label alone.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox supplies reasonable fit, preserving the distinction from least-restrictive-means review.
- Broadrick v. Oklahoma and New York v. Ferber retain their facial-overbreadth limits without foreclosing the plaintiff's own claim.
- United States v. X-Citement Video is applied to alternative grounds defending a judgment and the separate requirement for enlarging relief.
- Paul P. v. Verniero supports the limited residential-privacy interest. It does not decide whether California's commercial distinction materially advances that interest or reasonably fits the burden.
- Marks v. United States supplies no unified rationale where these grounds lack the necessary logical relationship. No precedent is overruled.

#### Law After Decision

Government ownership of the information does not end First Amendment review of a speech-selected access/use condition. Lanphere's scrutiny and application-specific remedial limits remain governing law. The publisher's maintained own claims must be treated as such. The commercial classification's validity and the actual relief remain unresolved; neither a universal access entitlement nor facial invalidation follows.

The statute's distinct restrictions remain distinct. Paragraph (3) addresses current addresses and requires a declaration under penalty of perjury of a scholarly, journalistic, political or governmental purpose, or investigation by a qualifying licensed private investigator, plus a separate declaration under penalty of perjury against direct or indirect use to sell products or services. The sales-use prohibition applies to information obtained under that paragraph; a permitted purpose does not exempt the requester from it. Item-specific danger to a person involved in an investigation or to successful completion of that investigation or a related investigation remains a separate limit. Mandatory confidentiality for addresses of victims of crimes under Penal Code §§220, 261, 262, 264, 264.1, 273a, 273d, 273.5, 286, 288, 288a, 289, 422.6, 422.7, 422.75 and 646.9 is not converted into discretionary withholding. Paragraph (2)'s distinct requested victim-name withholding and multi-crime deletion rules do not substitute for that mandatory address rule. The incorporated §841.5 and other source-specific restrictions are neither displaced nor construed here.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Breyer's Part III, joined by Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.** These four would vacate and remand for application of Lanphere to the actual maintained claims. The database theory's evidentiary failure differs from the separate home/privacy justification. Statutory permission to publish does not establish equal practical dissemination, while petitioners' assertions about journalists and resellers are not adjudicated facts. The current-address and statutory-source limits, permitted purposes and actual effects require comparison; if advancement is established, the previously unaddressed fit inquiry remains. A supported narrower decision on the existing record is permissible, with no mandatory new trial, numerical studies, regulation of every independent source, or least-restrictive-means requirement. Ordinary procedural rules govern supplementation; the waived cost challenge is not reopened. The four would reconsider existing relief against the actual claims and decree; that particular instruction lacks a fifth vote.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the Lanphere access/use standard and agrees that the appellate judgment must be set aside and the commercial application left open. He would reverse the facial judgment and vacate the permanent injunction now because lawful safety, privacy, deception, coercion and investigative applications defeat the broad facial judgment. That remedy does not approve the commercial-use classification. A maintained, preserved and proved denial of otherwise available records because of intended truthful, lawful solicitation remains subject to real-harm, material-advancement and reasonable-fit review and may support narrower relief. Neutral nondisclosure, redaction, fees, anti-harassment and deception rules remain available. He leaves discriminatory administration, independently acquired information and properly preserved alternative theories open. He does not join the plurality's reason for vacatur or its proposed treatment of the existing relief.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They accept Lanphere scrutiny but would sustain the confined condition for the conceded commercial address-distribution/solicitation activity. Protection at the point of official disclosure, the limitation to current addresses and information acquired by this statutory route, and retention of public-information and investigative access provide sufficient advancement and fit. Their position adopts no disputed newspaper-frequency finding or unreliable-database prediction. They distinguish Discovery Network's physical equivalence and 44 Liquormart's suppression of price information from protecting another person's address; no comparative numerical study is necessary. They would remove or modify relief dependent solely on the rejected commercial ground, preserve independently supported protection, and leave distinct editorial, statutory-journalism and other claims to their own treatment. They do not direct dismissal of the entire action or a restriction on independently acquired information.

**Stevens, joined by Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** They would affirm the conclusion that the condition is invalid as applied to the adjudicated lawful, nonmisleading address-service activity. Lanphere, Discovery Network and Edenfield require the State to justify this selected line. The permitted nonsales uses, potentially including public dissemination, undermine the asserted reason for withholding the same addresses for protected commercial distribution; the record's unsupported database theory supplies no alternative. They reject the existing justification without demanding regulation of every outside source or proof of every future publication, and do not hold that unwanted mail never affects privacy. Shapero protects the conceded lawful solicitation. Having found advancement insufficient, they do not reach fit. They would confine relief to this application and the proper parties, limiting a decree if it exceeds that supported scope without presuming that it does. They would not reopen those commercial merits or command access contrary to independent safety and confidentiality restrictions.

### Rayphand v. Sablan / Torres v. Sablan, No. 99-475

Merits decision, December 13, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted on full merits briefing November 15, 1999. On direct appeal from the three-judge District Court for the Northern Mariana Islands. The two caption forms identify one appeal. The question is whether the federally approved Covenant may preserve equal island representation in the Commonwealth's general legislative Senate despite the substantial population disparities.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, who would reverse the challenged inequality and remand for a constitutional remedy.

All nine Justices sustain direct appellate jurisdiction. The judgment rejecting the equal-island allocation challenge is affirmed, and the denial of the requested injunction remains in place. No new district map, seat count, special election, ouster of incumbents or invalidation of past legislation is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; Stone-Zsela as to jurisdiction only | Eight-Justice merits affirmance; unanimous jurisdiction | Seven Associates join the whole opinion; Stone-Zsela joins jurisdiction only |
| Dissent in part | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Would reverse the challenged allocation and remand | Merits and remedy; jurisdiction joined |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The direct appeal is within the Court's jurisdiction.

The heard denial of injunctive relief by the required three-judge court in this Commonwealth legislative-apportionment action is reviewable under 28 U.S.C. §1253. Covenant §902's intergovernmental consultation procedure does not displace these residents' judicial remedy for asserted personal vote dilution.

**Authority:** The jurisdiction portion of Kennedy's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support.

**Controlling explanation:** Sections 1821(c) and 1824 of Title 48 apply the relevant federal judicial provisions to the Commonwealth; Calero-Toledo supports treating an autonomous territorial commonwealth's government as a State for the relevant three-judge-court function. Section 2284 applies to this general legislative-apportionment challenge, and the actual panel, hearing and merits denial satisfy the direct-review route. The two Saipan residents allege diminished weight of their own votes. Section 902 establishes political consultation, while §202 expressly preserves judicial consistency review. Neither a new certiorari grant nor an exclusive political-remedy rule is needed.

##### II. The particular federal territorial settlement may preserve one equal-island chamber.

The constitutional requirements for this unincorporated territory permit Congress's express accommodation of equal municipal representation in one chamber alongside a population-based other chamber. This specific protected territorial arrangement does not violate equal protection merely because it would fail the population rule governing both chambers of an ordinary State legislature.

**Authority:** The merits portion of Kennedy's opinion is joined by all seven other Associates. Stone-Zsela dissents from this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court recognizes the severe voting disparity and the Senate's general legislative power. Reynolds and Board of Estimate explain why those facts would defeat ordinary state or local geographical representation, but they did not adjudicate this distinct federal territorial settlement. The territorial constitutional framework, reflected in Verdugo-Urquidez, requires attention to the particular institutional relationship while retaining constitutional protection for residents. Covenant §203(c) expressly requires equal municipal representation. Section 501(a) makes Fourteenth Amendment §1 applicable as if the Commonwealth were a State, to the extent it does not apply of its own force; §501(b) preserves the validity of, and Congress's power to consent to, §203. The statutory terms defeat simple transplantation through §501(a), although Congress's constitutional power still requires independent decision. Here the expressly protected allocation, local self-government and population-based other chamber support the confined accommodation. Section 105 gives mutual consent legal significance but does not by itself let a bargain waive individual rights. Neither the national Senate analogy nor proof that another map is technically possible decides the constitutional question.

**Limits:** The Court holds neither that equal protection is inapplicable in territories nor that every territorial departure survives. An ordinary State or municipality cannot invoke this founding arrangement as permission for the same inequality. No universal historical-bargain exception, numerical deviation threshold, wholesale endorsement of every territorial precedent, or finding that union would otherwise have been impossible is adopted.

#### Precedent Treatment

Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht Leasing Co. is applied by analogy to the autonomous commonwealth and three-judge review channel. Reynolds v. Sims and Board of Estimate v. Morris retain their state/local population-equality force and are distinguished by the particular territorial settlement; they are not overruled. United States v. Verdugo-Urquidez supplies the context-sensitive territorial framework, not a prior holding about this Senate. El Vocero confirms that territory is not an automatic exemption from an applicable constitutional guarantee. Shaw and the Miller group preserve actual unequal-participation claims but do not decide this territorial merits issue. The lower-court Atalig and Wabol analyses are considered within their respective settings, not elevated into prior Supreme Court holdings on this legislature.

#### Law After Decision

The Commonwealth's equal municipal representation in one Senate chamber remains constitutional within the specific federally approved arrangement. The population-based other chamber and protected territorial structure are material to that result. The ordinary state/local population rule and judicial review of territorial constitutional claims remain in force.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents from the merits and remedy while joining jurisdiction. He would apply the individual protection against severely unequal voting weight to this general territorial legislature. Reynolds supplies the distinction between geographical representation and individual political equality; Board of Estimate directs attention to the body's actual governmental powers. Smaller-island protection is substantial, but federal approval and an earlier generation's bargain do not by themselves justify permanently unequal individual votes. The national Senate rests on the Constitution's own allocation, unlike this congressional settlement. Flores de Otero confirms a constitutional foundation for territorial equality, though every territorial setting need not follow identical rules. Stone-Zsela would reverse only the challenged inequality and permit the political branches to choose a lawful design, with the remedial court addressing transition and election timing without indefinitely preserving the violation. He would not dictate a seat total, invalidate unrelated Covenant terms or void past enactments. His proposed remedy would not invalidate autonomous local island institutions.

The judgment below is 95 F. Supp. 2d 1133. The Covenant and the Commonwealth Constitution supply distinct allocation provisions. The reported census figures establish population disparity, not an exact voter-registration ratio. Review proceeds on the stated full-briefing submission schedule. The official jurisdictional recitations support the prior panel and appeal events without supplying a full panel roster or the original convening order.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The District Court's merits judgment and denial of the requested apportionment relief stand. The challenged allocation remains in effect; no special remedial proceeding or election is directed.

### Steamfitters Local Union No. 420 Welfare Fund v. Philip Morris, Inc.

Merits decision, January 10, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted on full merits briefing November 29, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, No. 98-1426, reviewing 171 F.3d 912. The granted questions concern whether the funds' pleaded medical expenditures are sufficiently direct injuries under civil RICO and Clayton Act §4. State-law claims are outside review.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Civil RICO ordinary expenditure and cessation-program theories | Dismissal affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | No federal damages recovery or merits remand on these pleaded theories |
| Clayton Act §4 theory | Dismissal affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Federal antitrust dismissal stands; state claims and hypothetical repleading are not adjudicated |

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Thomas delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. The opinion unanimously affirms both federal dismissals.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority:** Each rule, application and reservation below has direct unanimous support in the opinion of the Court.

##### Civil RICO requires a direct relation, and these ordinary expenditures are too remote.

Section 1964(c) requires factual causation and proximate cause, including a direct relation between the asserted business-or-property injury and the alleged violation. A fund's alleged medical payments arising through participants' smoking, illness and treatment do not satisfy that requirement on this pleaded chain, even when defendants allegedly intended the fund to bear the cost.

**Controlling explanation:** Holmes supplies a contextual inquiry informed by attribution, overlapping recovery and the availability of directly injured plaintiffs; those considerations are not three independent elements. Here the fund suffers a real monetary loss, but only after the participant's conduct and bodily injury generate treatment and payment. Alleged targeting and foreseeable costs do not eliminate those links. Attribution depends on separating loss produced by deception from illness and costs that would have occurred despite accurate information or from other causes. Limited duplication favors the funds, and participants cannot recover bodily-injury damages under RICO, but neither fact makes this expenditure chain direct. McCready involved a reimbursement denial that itself implemented the scheme, rather than a payment arising through another person's illness. The Court accepts the allegations and does not demand a damages model at the pleading stage. Statistical methods can quantify loss; they cannot supply an absent direct relation.

##### The cessation-program theory independently fails on its pleaded chain.

Describing concealment as interference with the funds' cost-control decisions does not make the claimed medical payments direct when loss still depends on different fund action, changed participant behavior, avoided illness and avoided treatment.

**Controlling explanation:** This theory receives separate consideration because the funds identify their own decisionmaking. The alleged loss nevertheless remains downstream healthcare expenditure; no independently pleaded property loss occurs through the fund decision without the intervening illness chain. The Court finds no particular program ineffective and imposes no evidentiary damages burden now. A different direct fraud upon a fund, and whether any amendment could state one, are not presented.

##### The funds' property and legal capacity remain distinct from participants' bodily injuries.

Benefit payments reduce the funds' own property, but their legal form and payment duty neither transfer participants' bodily injuries nor create an exemption from RICO's causal requirements.

**Controlling explanation:** ERISA and the trusts' Taft-Hartley form identify their benefit function and capacity; they establish neither a substantive recovery right against these manufacturers nor a categorical inability to protect their own property. The District Court's no-cognizable-property-injury rationale is not adopted. Entity status does not alter the same directness rule applied to the actual asserted interest.

##### The Clayton Act claim is independently too remote.

Under AGC's antitrust inquiry, these healthcare expenditures following participants' purchases, smoking and illness are not sufficiently direct injuries from the alleged suppression of safer tobacco products.

**Controlling explanation:** The funds are not consumers or competitors in the cigarette market, but those labels are not an exclusive test. Their payments were consequences of illness, not the mechanism implementing the alleged market exclusion as reimbursement denial was in McCready. This directness defect suffices without defining every antitrust-injury boundary. RICO receives no imported antitrust-injury, special racketeering-injury or purchaser-seller requirement.

**Limits:** No exceptionless third-party-payer bar, categorical prohibition of aggregate proof, state-law disposition or ruling on an unpresented amendment is adopted.

#### Precedent Treatment

Holmes v. Securities Investor Protection Corp. is applied without changing its contextual direct-relation requirement or turning its explanatory considerations into separate elements. Associated General Contractors supplies the distinct Clayton Act remoteness inquiry; its antitrust-injury requirement is not imported into RICO. Blue Shield v. McCready is distinguished because its reimbursement denial implemented the scheme itself. Sedima's rejection of special racketeering injury and prior-conviction requirements remains intact; statutory breadth does not dispense with causation. No general entity-property or third-party-injury restriction is added.

#### Law After Decision

The particular federal expenditure and cessation theories remain dismissed for remoteness. Real property loss, capacity to sue and intentional targeting do not alone establish statutory directness. Different directly caused injuries remain governed by their own allegations and sources, and RICO and Clayton Act requirements remain separate.

#### Source Notes

The Third Circuit's opinion, 171 F.3d 912, supplies the pleading-stage causal theories and remoteness grounds. The District Court's no-property-injury rationale, questioned by the Third Circuit, is not adopted. The Court's review proceeds on the stated full-briefing submission schedule.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Third Circuit's dismissal of the two federal claims remains in force. No federal damages or merits remand is ordered. The Court adjudicates neither state-law liability nor hypothetical amendment to allege a different direct injury.

**End of entry: Steamfitters Local Union No. 420 Welfare Fund v. Philip Morris, Inc., merits decision, January 10, 2000.**

### Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents; United States v. Florida Board of Regents, Nos. 98-791 and 98-796

Merits decision, January 11, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 13, 1999. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit. The consolidated question is whether Congress unmistakably authorized these private federal ADEA actions against nonconsenting state employers and had constitutional power to do so. The alternative §5 issue is included but unnecessary to the Court's disposition.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents, No. 98-791 | Reversed and remanded, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Categorical immunity dismissal removed; ordinary ADEA adjudication |
| United States v. Florida Board of Regents, No. 98-796 | Reversed and remanded, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Categorical immunity dismissal removed; ordinary ADEA adjudication |

No age-discrimination violation, employer liability, back pay, liquidated damages, fees or other award is established.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Breyer in full; O'Connor and Scalia as to Part I only | Five-Justice reversal and remand | Seven-Justice statutory holding in Part I; five-Justice Article I and reservation/remand in Parts II–III |
| Dissent from Parts II–III | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Would affirm immunity dismissal | Article I opposition and alternative §5 rejection; O'Connor and Scalia join Part I of the Court's opinion |
| Separate dissent on statutory authorization | Thomas | Kennedy | Would affirm independently on statutory grounds | Rejects Part I's incorporation reading; also joins O'Connor's constitutional dissent |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The enacted provisions unmistakably authorize the private federal action.

Sections 630(b), 626(b)–(c), and the incorporated FLSA provisions, read together, authorize private ADEA actions against state employers in federal court. That authorization establishes neither constitutional power, State consent, discrimination nor entitlement to relief.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion controls with seven Justices' support: Ginsburg, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, and Breyer. Kennedy and Thomas disagree.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 630(b) expressly includes States and agencies. Section 626(b) incorporates specified FLSA powers, remedies and procedures, excluding §216(a), and treats amounts owing from an ADEA violation as unpaid wages for enforcement purposes. Section 216(b) expressly permits actions against public-agency employers in federal or state court, and §203(x) includes state government and agencies. Section 626(c) also provides the aggrieved person's action. Treating its competent-court language as the sole authorization while reducing the incorporation to payment calculations or incidental procedure fails to give the express incorporation of remedies its full operation. Seminole Tribe and Florida Prepaid require clear authorization separately from constitutional amenability; this combined text supplies it.

**Operative qualifications:** Section 626(b) permits liquidated damages only for willful violations. Section 626(c)(1) ends the individual's right to bring the action when the EEOC commences an action enforcing that employee's right. The separately incorporated §216(b) terminates its action/party right upon the Secretary's §217 complaint seeking restraint of delay in the employee's unpaid minimum wages or overtime, or legal or equitable relief for alleged violations of §215(a)(3), the prohibition on retaliation. These distinct provisions are not collapsed into one identical rule, and no such governmental filing is adjudicated here.

##### II. Article I independently sustains this authorized enforcement measure.

Congress may enforce the valid federal age-employment duty against States through this expressly authorized private federal action when both the duty and its enforcement measure fall within Congress's Article I authority. The Eleventh Circuit's categorical immunity dismissal therefore cannot stand.

**Authority:** Parts II–III of Ginsburg's opinion have direct support from Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** Florida Prepaid Part III states the governing Article I enforcement principle, rather than a patent-only permission. EEOC v. Wyoming and Garcia support the commerce basis for regulating covered state employment. Allowing the injured employee to prove the enacted violation and obtain the specified relief directly enforces that valid duty and is an appropriate measure within the present threshold inquiry. Changing the regulated subject from patents to employment does not remove the general principle. The contrary distinction between substantive duty and treasury suit is the constitutional objection rejected by that precedent, not an existing limitation that defeats this action. Alden's separate state-court holding does not require resort there first, and College Savings means continued employment is not constructive consent. The action rests on congressional authority, not a new waiver finding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court reserves the ADEA's §5 validity. Age classifications receive rational-basis constitutional review, while the statute supplies broader protection; this distinction neither establishes a §5 holding nor imposes an individual constitutional-tort prerequisite. Coverage, defenses, proof and award-specific statutory and constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication. No officer action, state exhaustion requirement or universal authorization of every federal remedy is created.

#### Precedent Treatment

Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank, Part III, is applied to the ADEA's authorized employment-enforcement measure; its Article I rule remains controlling beyond its patent application. Pennsylvania v. Union Gas retains the constitutional conclusion extended by that rule. Seminole Tribe's distinction between authorization and power is applied without a categorical Article I prohibition. EEOC v. Wyoming and Garcia supply the valid commerce basis for covered state-employment regulation. Alden preserves its separate competent-state-court duty without becoming an exhaustion condition. College Savings Bank preserves the rejection of consent inferred from ordinary activity. Boerne and Florida Prepaid's §5 holdings remain effective; neither is used to decide the reserved ADEA §5 question. Gregory's rational-basis age rule is not a ceiling on otherwise valid commerce legislation.

#### Law After Decision

The expressly authorized federal ADEA claims against these state employers may proceed under the controlling Article I enforcement rule. Statutory authorization has seven direct supporters and the constitutional power/remand ground has five. No Court holding decides §5 validity or any underlying discrimination or award. Existing consent and proper prospective-officer doctrines retain their distinct predicates.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, dissents from the Article I holding and resulting remand. They distinguish a valid federal employment duty from a private action against a nonconsenting State's treasury and expressly seek reconsideration of Florida Prepaid's Article I rule and the Union Gas power conclusion to the necessary extent. They do not claim that a patent-only limitation already governs lower courts or that ordinary age-employment regulation lies outside commerce.

They also reject §5 as an independent basis for these private remedies. Gregory permits rational age classifications, including imperfect generalizations, whereas the ADEA generally demands more than constitutional rationality. Its bona fide occupational qualification requires reasonable necessity to normal business operation; a reasonable factor other than age does not authorize treatment because of age. Good-cause discipline remains distinct. A protected seniority system must be bona fide and not intended to evade the Act, and cannot permit involuntary age-based retirement of a protected individual. For each benefit or benefit package under a bona fide employee benefit plan, the actual payment made or cost incurred for an older worker must be no less than that for a younger worker, as permissible under 29 C.F.R. §1625.10 as in effect on June 22, 1989; the separate alternative is a voluntary early-retirement incentive plan consistent with the Act's purposes; neither excuses failure to hire, and benefit plans cannot permit involuntary age-based retirement. The employer bears the stated burden under those seniority and benefit provisions. The foreign-workplace defense requires that compliance violate the foreign country's law and supplies no domestic exception. No defense is found established.

The four assess the identified governmental complaints, federal-employment newspaper reports, private-sector findings and California survey, principally concerning public-safety age limits, as insufficiently connected to unconstitutional state practices to support this remedy's breadth. They do not infer constitutional validity from state-law permission. The identified recitations are not an exhaustive congressional record, and the dissent does not conclude that Congress heard no evidence. Boerne requires substantive fit; their objection does not impose formal findings, a number of violations, geographical evidence, a sunset or an individual constitutional-tort prerequisite. Florida Prepaid's preventive property remedy presents, for them, a different protected interest and remedial gap; permitting prevention beyond constitutional violations does not itself settle this statute's fit.

Justice Thomas, joined by Kennedy, additionally dissents from statutory authorization. They distinguish express State-employer coverage from unmistakable authorization of this private federal action. Section 626(c) can be read to supply the ADEA's own cause while §626(b) imports FLSA remedial and procedural incidents. The original 1967 incorporation, the 1974 amendment of §216(b), the later removal of §255's incorporation and the competent-court language leave insufficient clarity in their view. They require no universal single-section formula, but regard this statutory ambiguity as independently sufficient to affirm; their constitutional objections remain alternatives if authorization is assumed.

#### Source Notes

The official 1994 and 1999 United States Code text supplies §§630(b), 626(b)–(c), 216(b), 203(x), and the distinct §623 defenses. Legislative reports and hearings are available here through identified published recitations rather than an exhaustive congressional record. The immunity posture assumes state-arm status and supplies no merits findings.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Both cases return for ordinary adjudication of ADEA coverage, alleged discrimination, employer defenses, proof and legally available remedies. The reversal removes the categorical immunity bar only. No statutory defense is established, no governmental enforcement action is presumed, and no monetary or equitable award is directed.

**End of entry: Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents; United States v. Florida Board of Regents, merits decision, January 11, 2000.**

### New York v. Hill, No. 98-1299

Plenary merits decision, January 11, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 2, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the New York Court of Appeals, 92 N.Y.2d 406, 704 N.E.2d 542 (1998).

The question is whether counsel's express agreement to a specified trial date beyond the Interstate Agreement on Detainers' Article III(a) period binds the represented prisoner without personal assent, and whether this agreement suffices. Hill invoked Article III while imprisoned in Ohio. Counsel agreed on January 9, 1995 to a May 1 trial; the trial court found 167 nonexcludable days had elapsed. Hill moved to dismiss on April 17 before trial. Following his murder and robbery convictions and the Appellate Division's affirmance, the Court of Appeals ordered dismissal, distinguishing acceptance of a prosecutor-proposed date from an affirmative request for inconsistent treatment. New York seeks reversal; remaining preserved issues are not resolved here.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The conviction is reinstated as against the Article III(a)/Article V(c) dismissal ground, and the case is remanded for any preserved state or federal issue the court below did not resolve. This removes the compact ground for dismissal without deciding those other issues or ordering unconditional reinstatement.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice Stevens delivers the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine join every controlling proposition and the bounded remedy. No separate writing issues.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority:** The opinion of the Court supplies nine direct votes for each of the four propositions below at the stated level of generality.

##### Counsel may bind a represented prisoner to an expressly agreed trial date

**Holding and operative rule:** When defense counsel expressly agrees, before expiration of Article III(a)'s period, to a specified trial date beyond that period, the represented prisoner may not obtain dismissal because the agreed date exceeded the deadline, absent a contemporaneous objection or reservation when the date is set, a conflict, or a showing that counsel lacked authority in the particular proceeding. The prisoner need not personally attend, repeat the agreement, or undergo a waiver colloquy for this scheduling act; the rule does not authorize counsel to override an express timely objection.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that choosing the date of a contested trial ordinarily belongs to counsel's management of the case. Taylor v. Illinois supplies the distinction between professional trial management, which binds the represented accused, and basic personal choices that counsel cannot surrender. Jones v. Barnes likewise preserves the accused's ultimate choices while allowing counsel to conduct the litigation. Article III(a) contains no command that the prisoner personally make this scheduling decision. Requiring a personal colloquy for each genuine accommodation would impair counsel's coordination of preparation, witnesses, competing proceedings and the court's calendar.

That classification supplies the authority to agree. The absence of a statutory prohibition, standing alone, does not make every statutory protection waivable. Mezzanatto concerns a particular knowing and voluntary impeachment agreement; it supplies no universal waiver rule. Olano requires attention to the right and distinguishes affirmative relinquishment from mere inaction. Brookhart preserves the accused's basic choice to contest guilt against counsel's contrary surrender; a date agreement does not abandon that defense.

The holding is confined to genuine, unqualified agreement within counsel's authority. It neither permits wholesale surrender of the compact nor approves indefinite or bad-faith delay or delay beyond the agreed date. The agreement here preceded expiration; the effect of consent first given afterward is not presented or decided. Coercion, misunderstanding, ineffective assistance and an actual conflict remain distinct questions on their own predicates. No such predicate is established here, and the Court imposes no new hearing, personal-advice requirement or burden of proof.

##### Counsel's January 9 agreement supplies the required assent

**Holding and operative rule:** Counsel's express January 9, 1995 agreement to the May 1 trial date binds Hill as to that date. Express acceptance has the same effect as proposing the identical date; neither an Article III citation, proof of deadline-specific advice nor separate personal authorization is required for this scheduling decision.

**Controlling explanation:** Hill initiated the Article III process while imprisoned in Ohio. The trial court found that 167 nonexcludable days had elapsed when counsel agreed to the date. The agreement therefore preceded expiration on the accepted computation. Hill's initial request established his entitlement to the statutory timetable; it did not place every later calendar decision beyond counsel's authority.

The Court of Appeals permitted explicit waiver or an affirmative request for treatment inconsistent with the Agreement, but distinguished counsel's acceptance of a date the prosecutor proposed. That distinction cannot govern the legal effect of the same affirmative scheduling choice. Counsel did more than remain silent: he expressly accepted May 1 in court. The rule rests on that act, not an assumed strategy, knowledge of the precise deadline, or Hill's personal attendance or assent.

Hill's April 17 motion was made before trial and preserves his legal challenge to the agreement's effect. It was not a contemporaneous objection or reservation when the date was set, and does not restore a dismissal entitlement defeated by the valid agreement. The Court rejects the motion on that ground, not for failure to preserve it. The Court decides no general rule governing withdrawal of consent or a later request to change the schedule on an independently available ground.

##### A date agreement does not replace the compact's statutory conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** An effective agreement to a particular trial date is distinct from an Article III(a) continuance or Article VI(a) tolling. Article III(a) permits a necessary or reasonable continuance only for good cause shown in open court with the prisoner or counsel present; Article VI(a) separately tolls time while the prisoner is unable to stand trial, as determined by the court with jurisdiction.

**Controlling explanation:** Fex v. Michigan supplies Article III(a)'s commencement rule: for a prisoner serving a term of imprisonment who seeks final disposition of an untried charge supporting a detainer in another participating State, the 180 days begin only upon actual receipt of the required written notice and disposition request by both the prosecuting officer and the appropriate court. Delivery to the custodian alone does not start the period. Its separate continuance and inability provisions remain operative.

The statutory permission for either prisoner or counsel to be present is consistent with counsel's scheduling role. Presence is not itself good cause. The Court finds neither a qualifying continuance nor inability to stand trial as an independent justification for the January-to-May interval. The proviso governing judicial extensions does not displace counsel's ordinary authority to agree to this particular date. The compact's interests in prompt disposition, cooperation and rehabilitation neither eliminate the proviso's conditions nor make every affirmative agreement ineffective.

The accepted exclusions for pending defense motions are part of the trial court's computation in this case; they establish no rule of automatic motion-pendency tolling. The Court supplies no new receipt or expiration date and no grace period. This prisoner-initiated Article III case decides no Article IV(c) question. Other compact protections, including anti-shuttling requirements, retain their own predicates and are not surrendered by this date agreement.

##### The agreement defeats this dismissal ground, while the statutory remedy remains mandatory for an actual violation

**Holding and operative rule:** Article V(c) requires dismissal with prejudice when the applicable trial period is violated without an effective agreement or applicable statutory exception; no additional showing of defense impairment or bad faith, and no harmlessness exception, is imposed. Because Hill's agreement defeats the asserted Article III(a)/Article V(c) ground, the Court reverses the judgment dismissing the indictment, reinstates the conviction as against that ground, and remands for any preserved state or federal issue not resolved below.

**Controlling explanation:** Article V(c)'s mandatory remedy does not answer the antecedent question whether the agreed date can constitute the violation asserted. It cannot do so here. The provision's separate refusal-or-failure-to-accept-temporary-custody ground remains intact and is not presented. When dismissal is required, the detainer based on the dismissed charge also loses force.

Reed v. Farley addresses collateral relief for an assumed compact violation. Its collateral threshold supplies no prejudice prerequisite for this preserved direct-review claim and no independent waiver finding. The Court removes the legal ground on which the Court of Appeals dismissed the indictment; it does not decide other preserved challenges to the conviction or order unconditional reinstatement free of their consideration.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Taylor v. Illinois, 484 U.S. 400, 417–418 (1988):** Its distinction between counsel's trial management and the accused's basic personal choices is applied to the express selection of a trial date. Its particular discovery-misconduct facts are not attributed to Hill.
- **Jones v. Barnes, 463 U.S. 745, 751–754 (1983):** Its division between professional litigation management and the accused's ultimate choices supports counsel's scheduling authority; the accused retains the decisions whether to plead guilty, waive a jury, testify and take an appeal.
- **Brookhart v. Janis, 384 U.S. 1, 7–8 (1966):** Its prohibition on counsel surrendering the practical substance of a not-guilty defense over the accused's express objection remains intact; this date agreement does not make that surrender.
- **Fex v. Michigan, February 23, 1993:** Its actual-receipt trigger, qualified continuance power, separate inability tolling and mandatory dismissal rule remain unchanged. Today's decision adds the effect of an authorized express date agreement; Fex itself did not decide that question.
- **United States v. Olano, April 26, 1993:** Its distinction between intentional relinquishment and inaction, with right-specific waiver requirements, is applied. Its Rule 52(b) prejudice allocation does not govern Hill's preserved motion.
- **Godinez v. Moran, June 24, 1993:** Its separation of competence from informed and voluntary personal waiver remains intact. Neither competence nor representation supplies a finding of Hill's personal assent, and personal plea or counsel-waiver requirements do not govern routine scheduling.
- **Reed v. Farley, June 20, 1994:** Its recognition of the compact as federal law is applied; its statutory collateral-relief threshold is distinguished from direct review. Reed supplies neither a merits waiver nor a defense-impairment requirement here.
- **United States v. Mezzanatto, January 18, 1995:** Its limited enforcement of a knowing and voluntary impeachment agreement is consistent with giving lawful consent its actual effect, but is not expanded into a general statutory-waiver presumption or a rule allocating this decision to counsel.
- **Libretti v. United States, November 7, 1995:** Its limits on consent remain: an agreement cannot enlarge statutory punishment or replace a required substantive predicate. Its particular forfeiture-verdict waiver does not require the prisoner's personal colloquy for this separately classified scheduling act.

#### Law After Decision

Effective January 11, 2000, the compact's existing 180-day framework includes the controlling rule that counsel's authorized, unqualified express agreement before expiration binds the prisoner as to a specified later trial date, with the contemporaneous-objection, reservation, conflict and authority limits stated above. Acceptance and proposal are equivalent for that act. No former holding is overruled. The actual-receipt trigger, statutory continuance and inability conditions, and Article V(c)'s remedy remain in force; no general statutory-waiver doctrine, automatic motion-tolling rule or direct-review prejudice prerequisite is adopted.

#### Source Notes

The compact's statutory provisions appear in [Public Law 91-538, 84 Stat. 1397](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-84/pdf/STATUTE-84-Pg1397.pdf). The reported procedural history, 528 U.S. 110, 111–113, supplies the January 9 agreement, the trial court's 167-day computation and the April 17 pretrial motion. The New York judgments are reported at 244 App. Div. 2d 927, 668 N.Y.S.2d 126 (1997), and 92 N.Y.2d 406, 704 N.E.2d 542 (1998). [Taylor](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-484/pdf/USREPORTS-484-400.pdf), [Jones](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-463/pdf/USREPORTS-463-745.pdf) and [Brookhart](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-384/pdf/USREPORTS-384-1.pdf) supply the counsel/accused distinctions stated above.

The record does not establish Hill's personal attendance, knowledge, assent or contemporaneous objection, counsel's knowledge of the precise deadline, or an independent good-cause finding. The decision does not infer those facts and does not supply exact receipt or expiration dates beyond the trial court's accepted computation.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the New York courts. They must give effect to the binding agreement to the May 1 date and may address any preserved state or federal issue left unresolved below. The Court decides no independent constitutional speedy-trial, ineffective-assistance or anti-shuttling claim and orders no new hearing or deadline. Reinstatement remains limited to removing the Article III(a)/Article V(c) dismissal ground.

**End of entry: New York v. Hill, plenary merits decision, January 11, 2000.**

### Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc., No. 98-822

Merits decision, January 12, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 12, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit. The questions concern standing to seek Clean Water Act civil penalties for violations ongoing when suit began, and whether later compliance and asserted facility closure render the case moot. The July 16, 1998 appellate judgment preceded the later closure evidence.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would direct jurisdictional dismissal.

The Fourth Circuit's appellate vacatur and dismissal direction are set aside. The district penalty judgment returns to ordinary appellate review, subject to the unresolved issues and applicable stay procedures; this disposition does not affirm its merits or amount. No immediate collection order, new injunction, fee award or special stay is entered.

#### Opinion Topology

Justice O'Connor delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Justice Stevens also files a concurrence, joined by no other Justice, supporting the Court's remand and adding a ground for survival of a properly initiated penalty claim despite later cessation. Justice Scalia files a dissent, joined by Justice Thomas, challenging injury and, independently, the factual showing of deterrent redress. They would direct jurisdictional dismissal and do not join the reversal or remand.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority:** The seven Justices joining the opinion of the Court directly support its standing, deterrent-redress and bounded-remand holdings. Stevens's additional penalty-survival theory is not a holding of the Court.

##### Concrete interference with members' own river use supplies injury.

Supported curtailment of identified local recreational and aesthetic use because of reasonable concern about ongoing mercury discharges is a concrete personal injury; Article III does not require separate proof of environmental damage when the asserted injury is this loss of use and enjoyment. General interest in compliance or unexplained subjective concern alone remains insufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Lujan requires personal interests and stage-appropriate support, rather than a generalized desire for lawful government. Curtis identified nearby downstream activities and a present wish to return. His childhood cessation was not caused by pollution; that fact does not erase the distinct present-use assertion. Moore's two visits do not become a history of frequent recreation, but her desired activities likewise must be evaluated accurately. Patterson's earlier local recreation and present curtailment independently connect CLEAN's claim to the river. These accounts differ from indefinite intentions to visit distant places. The later findings of adequate water quality and no demonstrated significant environmental harm deserve weight, but do not conclusively resolve reasonable lost enjoyment during ongoing discharge violations at filing. The Court does not treat a permit breach as injury to everyone downstream, decide every property valuation, or separately establish Sierra Club's standing when injured members of the other organizations support the same joined request.

##### Deterrent penalties can redress supported threatened personal injury.

A statutorily authorized civil penalty payable to the Treasury can provide Article III redress when it is likely to deter continuing or reasonably recurring violations affecting the plaintiff's supported personal interests. Payment to the plaintiff is unnecessary for that preventive effect, but punishment of wholly past conduct without a remaining remedial connection, satisfaction from punishment and fees alone are insufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Steel Co. dismissed a suit filed after cure with no continuing or imminent violation alleged, while expressly leaving deterrent redress available for supported threatened injury. This action began amid mercury exceedances; the record contains fourteen on or after June 12, 1992 and thirteen strictly after that filing date. The penalty can affect compliance with those same obligations and therefore the use interests at risk. Government enforcement and other litigation exposure do not eliminate the supported deterrent effect on this record. The Court does not demand certainty or hold that every public penalty necessarily supplies redress.

##### Later compliance does not automatically establish mootness; the closure evidence must be assessed.

A defendant relying on voluntary cessation must make it absolutely clear that the challenged injurious conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur. Substantial compliance sufficient to defeat an injunction does not automatically satisfy that burden or erase standing that existed at filing.

**Controlling explanation:** The Fourth Circuit treated the remaining Treasury penalty as incapable of affecting a live controversy; that premise conflicts with the supported deterrent connection. Later closure presents a different factual question. The affidavit identifies the last discharge on September 1, 1998, distinct closure-plan approvals and dismantling/sale status as of May 21, 1999. It also admits permit retention. The 1997 agreement required incineration to cease, while allowing specified equivalent treatment capacity subject to approval, permits, reconstruction conditions and cessation of incineration before use. Neither a retained permit nor dismantling alone decides recurrence. The lower court must evaluate those facts and legal restraints without presuming resumed operations or permanent impossibility. An asserted 1999 reporting notice, known through a declaration's database/conversation account, establishes neither resumed mercury discharge nor conclusive mootness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not newly affirm or increase the $405,800 penalty, adjudicate the preserved diligent-prosecution defense, award fees, reopen the unappealed denial of an injunction, or decide the unpresented Article II question. The dispute over whether the liability ruling itself was declaratory relief does not establish a separate declaratory decree in the record. Fees neither cure missing standing nor alone prevent mootness. Broader survival of a properly initiated penalty claim after proven permanent cessation is reserved by the Court.

#### Precedent Treatment

Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife is applied to concrete member-use interests and stage-appropriate proof; generalized environmental concern remains insufficient. Steel Co. is distinguished because violations were ongoing at filing and its expressly retained deterrence principle applies; its completed-injury limit remains intact. United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group preserves the separation between representative enforcement and members' personal stakes. The established voluntary-cessation rule is applied without equating denial of prospective equitable relief with constitutional mootness. Tull v. United States informs Stevens's separate penalty analogy but supplies no additional controlling holding here.

#### Law After Decision

Concrete, supported loss of local use may establish injury even without separately proved environmental damage, and an authorized Treasury penalty may redress continuing or reasonably recurring personal harm through deterrence. Standing at commencement remains distinct from later mootness. Closure must be evaluated under the demanding cessation rule, and penalties for wholly past conduct without a personal remedial connection remain outside this holding. Stevens's broader penalty-survival theory has no other join.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Stevens joins the Court and adds a distinct monetary-remedy ground. In his view, a valid civil-penalty judgment cannot be retroactively invalidated simply because postjudgment closure makes recurrence impossible. He also concludes that a properly initiated civil-penalty claim survives postcomplaint cessation even before judgment: the Clean Water Act attaches monetary liability to violations, and Tull's distinction between monetary penalties and prospective injunctions supports that treatment. Initial standing and the preserved statutory diligent-prosecution bar remain necessary. He does not authorize suit by an uninjured person, rely on fees for a personal stake, finally approve the $405,800 amount or order collection. His broader survival theory has no other join; he supports the Court's sufficient grounds and ordinary appellate return while disagreeing that proof of permanent cessation necessarily defeats this properly initiated penalty claim.

Justice Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissents on two independently sufficient Article III grounds. First, the science-based findings of adequate recreational water quality and no demonstrated adverse environmental effect undermine an objective connection between these exceedances and the asserted use loss. Expressed concern is not self-proving injury. Curtis's childhood cessation for another reason and Moore's limited visits reinforce their objection to treating desired use as established interference, without requiring ecosystem destruction in every case or resting their objection on litigant default.

Second, even assuming injury, the State's penalty, governmental enforcement, later compliance and litigation exposure make the additional federal penalty's likely marginal effect on these members' future harm too speculative. They accept Steel Co.'s rule that Treasury penalties can deter supported threatened injury; they reject this factual showing rather than all private reliance on public penalties. General deterrent purpose is not enough, although certainty is not required. Their concern about private public-law enforcement supplies no separate Article II holding. If initial standing were assumed, they accept that cessation and closure could sensibly receive factual review, but that conditional view supplies no vote for the Court's operative reversal, restoration or remand. They would direct jurisdictional dismissal.

#### Source Notes

The original discharge table, JA 195, establishes fourteen mercury exceedances on or after filing and thirteen strictly after filing. The district opinion's postfiling monitoring/reporting counts conflict: JA 159 states thirteen and ten, while JA 178 states one alleged monitoring violation and no reporting violations. The referenced revised exhibits 77–79 and 97 and the 1995 trial transcript passages at 181–182 and 187 are needed to reconcile that discrepancy; it does not decide the mercury-based standing question. The full member affidavits and Curtis/Moore depositions are not reproduced in the core appendix; their identified published recitations retain both favorable and adverse qualifications. The court's June 10, 1992 approval of the State's decree is distinct from the June 9 filing/settlement chronology, and the District Court expressly declined to find fraud or collusion. Later sworn closure assertions remain distinct from findings made below.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Fourth Circuit must address the remaining appeal and cross-appeal under the holdings stated here, including supported closure and recurrence facts, the preserved diligent-prosecution defense and remaining penalty questions. The district judgment returns to unresolved ordinary appellate status when reversal takes effect through the usual appellate process. No prior mandate, implementing dismissal, execution stay or bond is presumed. Collection remains governed by ordinary mandate and applicable stay rules; the Court orders neither immediate execution nor a new stay. The fee-petition timing stay supplies no independent jurisdiction or fee award.

**End of entry: Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc., merits decision, January 12, 2000.**

### Illinois v. Wardlow, No. 98-1036

Merits decision, January 12, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 2, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Illinois. The question is whether the actual pre-stop record supplied particularized reasonable suspicion after Wardlow looked toward officers and fled in an area associated with narcotics trafficking. The subsequent frisk and bag search are outside the granted question.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas, who would reverse the initial-stop ruling and remand for any properly remaining issues.

The Illinois Supreme Court's judgment sustaining suppression is affirmed because the initial stop lacked reasonable suspicion. The existing suppression judgment remains in force; the Court does not separately decide the justification or scope of the frisk.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer in full; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas as to Part I only | Five-Justice affirmance | All nine join the governing rule in Part I; five join application and remedy in Part II |
| Dissent in part | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Would reverse the initial-stop ruling and remand | Disagrees with Part II; joins Part I's common rule |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Suspicion must remain individualized when flight and location are considered together.

Terry requires objective, particularized reasonable suspicion under the whole circumstances. Flight may contribute to that assessment, and a demonstrated connection between the location and crime may supply context, but location alone is insufficient and neither any flight nor every flight in a broadly described high-crime area creates a universal permission to stop.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion has all nine Justices' direct support as the governing law applied here.

**Controlling explanation:** Terry permits grounded inferences rather than proof of an offense, while Brown v. Texas rejects substituting a neighborhood label for an individualized basis. A refusal to cooperate or departure from police is not automatically criminally suspicious. Ornelas requires independent review of ultimate constitutional sufficiency with respect for historical findings and due weight to supported local inferences; it permits no supplementation of testimony. The later discovery of a firearm cannot justify the earlier stop. This common rule leaves the Justices' disagreement about its application intact.

##### II. This record does not establish the required suspicion.

Wardlow's observed departure and route, considered with the credited narcotics setting and a permissible police-recognition inference, do not supply a sufficiently particularized basis for suspecting him of wrongdoing on this record. The initial stop was therefore unlawful, and the Illinois judgment sustaining suppression is affirmed.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has direct support from Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court credits the state high court's finding about narcotics activity and does not exclude flight from consideration. Nolan saw Wardlow look toward the officers and run through a gangway and alley, but observed no law violation or transaction. His description of customers and lookouts concerned what normally occurred in such areas; it did not place a crowd or such activity beside Wardlow on this occasion. Nolan's uniform is established, while vehicle markings and Wardlow's observation of the whole caravan are not. Even allowing an inference that he recognized police, the flight's significance and the generalized setting remained too ambiguous to connect this person adequately to suspected criminal activity. The contrary reading gives those circumstances more particularizing force than this testimony supports. The Court finds neither an innocent motive nor actual guilt, requires no observed completed offense, and adds no categorical third factor.

**Questions not reached:** The separate safety basis and scope of the frisk and bag search are not adjudicated. A stronger flight-related record remains governed by Terry's existing totality inquiry.

#### Precedent Treatment

Terry v. Ohio is applied without a new categorical flight or corroboration rule. Brown v. Texas preserves the insufficiency of location alone. Ornelas v. United States supplies independent ultimate review and due weight for grounded factual inferences; it is not authority to invent additional observations. Flippo v. West Virginia's separate-authorization principle is consistent with keeping the stop and later search distinct but does not decide the stop's factual sufficiency.

#### Law After Decision

The existing totality standard remains unchanged. This pre-stop combination is insufficient on the supported record; flight remains relevant in other factually supported settings. The decision creates neither a right-to-flee exclusion of all evidentiary weight nor a mandatory third circumstance beyond flight and location.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, agrees with the governing totality rule but finds reasonable suspicion here. Wardlow looked toward the officers and immediately ran through a gangway and alley in the credited narcotics setting. For the dissent, that temporal sequence and evasive route provide the individualized conduct missing from a location-only stop. Police recognition can reasonably be inferred without proving that he noticed every vehicle, and an innocent explanation need not defeat every grounded investigative inference. The dissent assumes neither marked cars nor an actual crowd, transaction, pre-stop firearm or guilty motive; the trial court's racial observation supplies no suspicion. It applies Ornelas's controlling independent review while giving weight to supported local inferences; Scalia retains his prior objection to that review allocation. The four would reverse the initial-stop ruling and remand for any properly remaining issue, without automatically reinstating the conviction or deciding the separate frisk and bag search.

Testimony establishing Nolan's uniform is distinct from an inferred recognition of police, and the general expectation of customers or lookouts is distinct from an observed crowd. The state high court accepted the narcotics-area finding. The firearm was discovered after the stop and cannot supply its predicate.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Illinois Supreme Court's suppression judgment stands. No further ruling on the bag search or frisk is directed by this disposition.
