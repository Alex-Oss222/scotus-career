## Event

Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union, No. 00-1293, 535 U.S. 564. Argued November 28, 2001; decided May 13, 2002. October Term 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Third Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), reviewing 217 F.3d 162. The Third Circuit affirmed the February 1, 1999 preliminary injunction against the Child Online Protection Act on the ground that its community-standards feature independently made the statute facially invalid. The question is whether that feature alone supports the appellate disposition; other grounds for the preliminary injunction were not adjudicated by that rationale.

COPA, 47 U.S.C. §231, reaches knowing commercial World Wide Web communications, made with knowledge of the material's character in interstate or foreign commerce, available to any person under seventeen and containing material harmful to minors. Commercial purpose requires a regular business course with a profit objective and knowing posting or solicitation of the material; actual profit and a sole or principal business are unnecessary. This statutory category does not make protected expression commercial advertising. Harmful material includes obscenity or material satisfying all three requirements: taken as a whole and with respect to minors, prurient appeal or pandering judged by the average person applying contemporary community standards; patently offensive depictions, descriptions or representations of sexual acts or contacts, actual or simulated and including normal or perverted acts, or lewd displays of genitals or post-pubescent female breasts, with respect to minors; and, taken as a whole, lack of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value for minors. The community-standards phrase appears expressly in the prurience clause.

Subsection (a) permits a fine up to $50,000, imprisonment up to six months, or both; intentional violations carry an additional fine up to $50,000 per violation, and the separate civil penalty is also up to $50,000 per violation. For the latter two, each day is a separate violation. Subsection (b)(1)–(3) excludes a telecommunications carrier to the extent it provides telecommunications service, an Internet-access-service provider in that capacity, and an information-location service in that capacity. Subsection (b)(4) separately protects intermediaries transmitting, storing, retrieving, hosting, formatting or translating another's communication without selecting or altering its content; deletion consistent with subsection (c) or §230 does not defeat that paragraph's protection. Subsection (c)(1) supplies a good-faith access-restriction defense through a credit card, debit account, adult access code or adult personal identification number, an age-verifying digital certificate, or other reasonable measures feasible with available technology. Subsection (c)(2) separately protects good-faith implementation or restriction activity against a cause of action only when that activity violates no law carrying a criminal or civil penalty. Subsection (d) separately requires protection against unauthorized access to collected information and prior written or electronic consent for disclosure, from the adult concerned or the parent or guardian of a person under seventeen. Disclosure is permitted when necessary to communicate, to conduct a legitimate related business activity, or under an authorizing court order.

The preliminary findings include speakers' inability to exclude Web visitors geographically and verification burdens affecting expense, anonymity and adult access. Those findings remain part of the inquiry; the Court does not substitute assumed future technology for the existing record.

## Holdings

### Community standards alone do not establish COPA's facial invalidity

**Holding and operative rule.** COPA's use of contemporary community standards does not, standing alone, establish facial invalidity: constitutional review must assess the complete offense, its limiting elements and defenses, and its actual burden on protected expression together. This rejection of automatic invalidity supplies neither a presumption that COPA survives nor a holding that varying local standards are constitutional in every application to the Web.

**Authority.** Part II of Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes for this confined rule and explanation. Part III's vacatur, remand and preservation of the injunction also receive Breyer's join. The Court adopts no national-community construction and uses no narrowest-grounds aggregation.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the First Amendment inquiry concerns the enacted restriction as a whole. Reno protects adult Internet expression against substantially overbroad criminal restrictions; it does not turn every reference to community standards in a different law into an independent constitutional defect. COPA contains commercial-coverage, whole-material, prurience, specified-content and serious-value limits that must do their actual legal work. Miller supplies the obscenity framework, and Pope separates serious value from local acceptance. Their relevance does not establish that material lacking serious value for minors is unprotected for adults.

The challengers' strongest objection rests on the existing finding that Web speakers cannot exclude visitors geographically: a restrictive community may consequently burden expression nationwide. The Court preserves that fact and the verification burdens on cost, anonymity and access. But geographic variation alone does not determine the protected reach of all the statutory requirements operating together. Nor does the presence of limiting elements establish that their combined operation satisfies the Constitution. Substantial overbreadth requires examination of protected expression in relation to the statute's legitimate reach, not isolation of one phrase.

Reno and Playboy continue to require justification of the actual protected-adult-speech burden, including consideration of concrete, plausibly effective, less restrictive means. The statutory commercial-purpose label does not reduce literary or other protected expression to advertising. Each defense, provider exclusion and privacy provision retains its own conditions. Free Speech Coalition distinguishes ordinary construction from judicial addition of a new offense limitation. The Court therefore neither mandates a national standard nor finally approves local standards. The complete constitutional challenge and unresolved tailoring grounds return because the reviewed appellate rationale did not decide them. The preliminary injunction remains while that work proceeds.

**Precedent treatment.** Reno's adult-access holding remains controlling and its categorical extension to any community-standards phrase is rejected; Miller and Pope supply distinct obscenity and objective-value principles; Playboy preserves the governmental justification burden; Free Speech Coalition preserves substantial-overbreadth review and the boundary against judicial rewriting. No precedent is overruled.

FCC, 492 U.S. 115: their treatment of community standards remains available, but does not automatically resolve a restriction on nonobscene adult expression through the Web. Their addressed-distribution settings do not erase the geographic findings here.
- United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc., 529 U.S. 803: applied for the Government's ultimate justification burden and examination of concrete, plausibly effective, less speech-restrictive alternatives; no perfect alternative or prescribed legislative-record format is required.

## Law After Decision

Lower courts must evaluate COPA's actual statutory reach and adult-speech burden as a whole. The presence of community standards alone cannot sustain the reviewed categorical facial rationale, and the presence of other elements alone cannot establish ultimate validity. Neither a national standard nor categorical approval of local standards becomes controlling law. The existing adult-speech, substantial-overbreadth and justification rules remain operative. Full statutory validity, the factual adequacy of verification and the comparative effectiveness of alternative protections remain for the returned proceeding because the appellate rationale did not adjudicate them.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, concurring.** She joins the confined holding because the present showing does not identify sufficient otherwise protected material made unlawful solely through community variation once prurience and serious value are considered. Pope's objective-value inquiry and Sable's distinction between an adult ban and confined protection matter, but the Web's inability to select recipients prevents treating conventional mail and telephone distribution as conclusive. She affirmatively regards a national standard as constitutionally permissible and necessary for reasonable Internet obscenity regulation. Her judgment neither forecloses an adequately supported facial challenge nor denies later as-applied relief.

**Thomas, concurring, joined by Scalia.** The prurience and objective serious-value requirements materially narrow the relevant category compared with the CDA. Miller, Pope, Hamling and Sable support rejecting community variation as a sufficient objection to this statutory category; the existing showing does not establish substantial variation in the covered material. This broader explanation would reject the community-standards objection more fully than the Court's common ground. It does not adopt a national-standard amendment, resolve COPA's other constitutional defects, or treat statutory commercial purpose as permission to suppress protected adult literature. They join continued interim protection while the other injunction grounds are considered.

**Souter, concurring, joined by Kennedy and Ginsburg.** The extent of protected expression cannot be measured until the statute's reach is determined. Commercial coverage, the relevant whole work or communication, the different ages within the under-seventeen category and venue consequences affect the relationship between legitimate regulation and protected expression. Reno, Playboy and Free Speech Coalition require attention to actual adult access, not an assumption that value for minors cures the adult burden. These Justices expressly preserve the community-standards challenge as part of the integrated inquiry: after construction, geographic variation may still prove decisive. Their join does not approve local standards as constitutionally harmless, depend on hypothetical geographic controls, or adopt a national standard.

**Breyer, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins the statutory background and disposition but reads community standards to mean the national adult community. The ordinary phrase in context, its legislative explanation and avoidance of serious constitutional difficulty support that construction in his view. A local standard would give restrictive communities practical control over protected speech distributed nationally. Variation among juries applying one national standard is different from variation among the governing substantive standards themselves. This construction is necessary to his agreement, not a merely optional possibility. It does not resolve the other COPA objections, and he agrees that the injunction should continue.

**Stevens, dissenting.** He would affirm the preliminary injunction on the community-standards ground. His objections in Smith and Sable, together with Reno's adult-access rule, apply to the established inability of national Web speakers to avoid restrictive local audiences. Serious value for minors does not define all expression adults may constitutionally receive. Narrowing the commercial category or the whole-work unit may reduce absolute coverage without curing substantial overbreadth relative to legitimate applications. Verification burdens do not erase that injury. He rejects insertion of a national standard into language with an established community-based meaning. His position preserves the legitimate interest in protecting children and ordinary restrictions on unprotected material; it does not require invalidating every future Internet safeguard.

## Procedure After Action

The Third Circuit must reconsider the injunction dispute under the whole-statute inquiry while the existing preliminary injunction remains effective. Its work includes commercial coverage, content breadth, the whole-work unit, differences among minors' ages, the placement and operation of defenses, criminal consequences, chat rooms, discussion groups, links and foreign access insofar as properly presented, verification expense and anonymity, and concrete less restrictive alternatives. The existing findings remain available; the decision imposes no new technological experiment or automatic reopening of proof. Ordinary mandate procedure governs the return.

## Event

Lapides v. Board of Regents of University System of Georgia, No. 01-298, 535 U.S. 613. Argued February 25, 2002; decided May 13, 2002. October Term 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), reviewing 251 F.3d 1372. The question is whether a State that voluntarily removes an action to federal court relinquishes its federal-forum immunity from state-law claims for which it already permits adjudication in its own courts.

A university professor sued the Board and individual officials in Georgia court, alleging that placement and dissemination of sexual-harassment allegations in his personnel files violated state tort law and his federal rights. Those assertions remain allegations. The Georgia Attorney General removed the action on the asserted federal-question basis and sought dismissal. The district court treated removal as waiver; the Eleventh Circuit rejected that conclusion because state law did not separately authorize the Attorney General to waive immunity.

Georgia's Tort Claims Act waives immunity only to the extent and in the manner the Act provides and only for actions in Georgia courts; it expressly retains immunity in federal courts. The Georgia Constitution requires legislative waiver and preserves federal constitutional immunity. The Attorney General's representation authority supports the State's actual removal, while separate authority to declare an immunity waiver is disputed. The State's §1983 damages-defendant status, the individual defendants' claims and defenses, original federal jurisdiction and supplemental jurisdiction remain distinct from the federal-forum immunity issue.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Holdings

### I. Authorized voluntary removal relinquishes federal-forum immunity for claims the State permits in its own courts

**Holding and operative rule.** A State that authorizes voluntary removal of an action to federal court relinquishes its federal-forum immunity from state-law claims for which it has already waived immunity in its own courts, even though its law expressly reserves federal-forum immunity and does not separately authorize counsel to declare a waiver. That actual choice of federal adjudication has a uniform federal consequence; it does not enlarge substantive liability, surrender unrelated defenses or decide removal of claims for which the State retains immunity in its own courts.

**Authority.** Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, controls both holdings and all stated qualifications by nine direct joins.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a State cannot select a federal tribunal for claims it permits in its own courts and then defeat adjudication solely because the chosen tribunal is federal. Clark, Gunter and Gardner recognize the legal consequences of actual submission to federal adjudication. Removal is a voluntary invocation of that adjudication even when the State originally appeared as a defendant.

Georgia's contrary argument correctly distinguishes authority to remove from separate state-law authority to announce an immunity waiver. The conclusion does not follow. The former authority makes the removal the State's own act, and federal law supplies its forum consequence. Requiring a second internal authorization would permit incompatible positions about the very tribunal the State selected. The clarity inquiry concerns that deliberate litigation act; it does not depend on counsel's motive, a prediction of advantage or an abstract balancing of dignity against injury.

College Savings Bank and Florida Prepaid preserve this actual-consent route while rejecting consent inferred from ordinary regulated commerce. Georgia's state-court tort waiver alone supplies no federal consent. Its later authorized removal supplies the operative act. Schacht kept that removal-waiver question open; Raygor preserved an asserted defense during litigation in a forum the State did not select by removal. Neither requires the incompatible forum positions here.

Ford's authorization rationale cannot control insofar as it would preserve federal-forum immunity after this authorized voluntary invocation. Ford is overruled to that extent. The Court retains the Georgia Act's substantive scope and manner conditions and confines the rule to the state-law claims before it; claims immune in the State's own courts and an otherwise valid federal claim lie outside the question necessary to decide this controversy.

**Precedent treatment.** Clark, Gunter and Gardner are applied to authorized voluntary removal; College Savings Bank and Florida Prepaid retain their distinction between actual submission and constructive commercial consent; Schacht's reserved issue is resolved at this scope; Raygor is distinguished; Ford is overruled only insofar as its contrary authorization rationale applies to this invocation.

### II. Forum waiver leaves the cause of action and jurisdictional inquiries distinct

**Holding and operative rule.** Removal waiver does not make the State a person subject to the pleaded §1983 damages claim or cure a defective cause of action; Will's statutory rule applies independently. The district court must establish original jurisdiction separately and apply the mandatory remand rule if it is absent; when original jurisdiction existed but the federal claims are dismissed, §1367(c)(3) permits discretionary treatment of the remaining state claims rather than requiring either federal retention or return to state court.

**Authority.** The unanimous Court opinion identified above directly adopts these rules and their application to the returned proceeding.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that consent to a forum is not a new cause of action. Will excludes the State from the persons subject to the pleaded §1983 damages action. That defect remains despite removal and waiver. It does not collapse the separate questions whether the complaint supplied original federal jurisdiction when removed and whether the individual defendants face viable claims.

Schacht distinguishes a claim's immunity from the action's otherwise valid federal jurisdiction; Steel Co. likewise keeps jurisdiction separate from the merits of a statutory cause. Failure of a federal claim therefore does not automatically establish that original jurisdiction never existed. The district court must examine the actual jurisdictional anchors. Section 1447(c) governs a case lacking subject-matter jurisdiction. If jurisdiction originally existed and all federal claims are then dismissed, §1367(c)(3) allows the court to decline supplemental jurisdiction. The state claims remain a live controversy, and their lawful retention or remand follows that separate inquiry. The waiver holding neither compels their federal adjudication nor adjudicates tort liability or the individual defendants' defenses.

**Precedent treatment.** Will is applied as an independent statutory defendant rule. Schacht and Steel Co. preserve the distinction between jurisdiction, claim defenses and merits; no failure of the State-directed federal claim automatically determines the remaining action's forum.

## Law After Decision

The open removal-waiver question is now resolved for state-law claims that a State allows against itself in its own courts: authorized voluntary removal relinquishes the federal-forum immunity objection despite an earlier express reservation. The substantive scope and manner of the State's waiver, valid causes of action and independent defenses continue to govern liability. Ford's inconsistent internal-authorization rationale cannot defeat this consequence. Removal of claims for which state-court immunity remains intact and an otherwise valid federal claim are outside this decision's confined question. Existing rules of statutory defendant status, original jurisdiction and discretionary supplemental jurisdiction remain separate and operative.

## Event

Verizon Communications Inc. v. FCC, 535 U.S. 467; consolidated Nos. 00-511, 00-555, 00-587, 00-590 and 00-602. Argued October 10, 2001; decided May 13, 2002. October Term 2001. On writs of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, reviewing 219 F.3d 744 under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1). After the earlier AT&T decision, the court of appeals sustained forward-looking pricing in principle but invalidated the efficient-network assumption in 47 C.F.R. §51.505(b)(1) and the additional combination rules in §51.315(c)–(f). Incumbent carriers challenge the costing authority and methodology; the Commission and competing carriers seek restoration of the invalidated rules.

The Telecommunications Act requires incumbent carriers to furnish authorized unbundled network elements on just, reasonable and nondiscriminatory rates and terms. Section 252(d)(1) requires rates based on the cost of providing the interconnection or element, determined without reference to a rate-of-return or other rate-based proceeding, and permits a reasonable profit. The pricing rule uses total element long-run incremental cost, or TELRIC, plus reasonable forward-looking common costs. Its efficient-network assumption retains existing wire-center locations while using currently available efficient technology and a lowest-cost configuration. This is review of general methodology and duties, without an adjudicated particular rate, complete carrier financial record or specific combination request.

Section 251(c)(3) requires access in a manner allowing requesting carriers to combine elements. Paragraphs (c) and (d) require requested necessary combining functions in their respective settings; the Commission's contemporaneous explanation addresses assistance for entrants lacking the facilities or network information needed to combine. That explanation and the paragraphs' distinct text are construed below. Existing-combination protection under paragraph (b), lawful element selection under §251(d)(2), state implementation and lawful compensation remain separate.

## Holdings

### I. The statute permits forward-looking cost and does not require embedded-cost recovery

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 252(d)(1) permits the FCC to adopt a reasonable forward-looking economic measure of the cost of providing an interconnection or network element; it does not require reimbursement of the incumbent's embedded investment or every historical expenditure. Rates must remain just, reasonable, cost-based and nondiscriminatory, with cost determined without reference to a rate-of-return or other rate-based proceeding; reasonable profit is permitted rather than independently guaranteed.

**Authority.** Part I of Scalia's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. The nine-Justice coalition also directly joins Parts III and IV. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg join Scalia in Part II, giving that part eight votes; Breyer does not join Part II.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the enacted term cost does not select the incumbent's books as the sole lawful measure. AT&T establishes national implementing authority while preserving state rate determinations. Mead requires an actual delegated lawmaking function, and the Act's implementing assignment exercised through binding regulations provides it here. Chevron therefore permits a reasonable choice within the enacted boundaries; expertise alone creates no power.

The direction to avoid a rate-of-return or other rate-based proceeding is inconsistent with the asserted exclusive historical-cost construction. It does not itself forbid every conceivable consideration of historical expenditure under every permissible method. Congress required compensation measured by the cost of providing access in a competitive scheme, not repayment of each prior investment merely because it appears in the incumbent's accounts. The permission to include reasonable profit likewise does not guarantee all sunk costs. The choice among permissible forward-looking methods remains subject to reasoned implementation and the statute's independent constraints.

**Precedent treatment.** AT&T's allocation of implementing authority is applied; Mead and Chevron require actual delegation and a reasonable choice within it. Their statutory-boundary requirements remain unchanged.

### II. The challenged efficient-network methodology is a permissible administrative choice

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 51.505(b)(1)'s use of efficient currently available technology and the lowest-cost network configuration, retaining the incumbent's existing wire-center locations, reasonably implements §252(d)(1). The rule is restored with its incremental-cost and reasonable common-cost components, forward-looking capital cost and economic depreciation, element-specific stand-alone-cost ceiling, aggregate common-cost allocation requirement, excluded-cost categories and cost-study protections; its validity does not establish the lawfulness of every resulting charge.

**Authority.** Part II has the eight direct joins identified above; Breyer dissents from this component alone.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that an economic estimate need not reproduce the incumbent's existing technology to measure the cost of supplying an element. Existing wire centers anchor the network; current technology and configuration identify efficient provision; incremental and common costs, capital cost and depreciation prevent treating access as costless. Hope and Duquesne permit regulatory choices among cost methods rather than guaranteeing one accounting system.

The incumbents and the dissent raise a real concern: low leasing prices may weaken replacement investment and discourage competing facilities. But an alternative competitive policy can be reasonable without making this one unlawful. The Commission may facilitate entry through compensated access within Congress's cost requirements. The actual design and retained state implementation reasonably connect the benchmark to providing service; a hypothesized investment injury does not establish that Congress prohibited that choice. Barnhart permits reasoned implementation within a real delegation, while the requirement of agency reasons remains.

The qualifications matter. An element's incremental cost plus allocated common costs cannot exceed the forward-looking stand-alone cost of an efficient firm supplying only that element, including corporate costs. Total allocations must equal network-wide forward-looking common costs excluding retail costs. Embedded, retail and opportunity costs and revenues subsidizing other services are excluded from this calculation. The incumbent must substantiate each offered rate through a compliant cost study. Rates outside proxy ranges or above proxy ceilings require full and fair application of the methodology in the prescribed proceeding. Proceedings under this section must provide notice, comment opportunity and a written record sufficient for review; the record must include any cost study considered in establishing rates. These rules preserve review of concrete applications; the Court neither orders TELRIC for other statutes nor decides an individual rate.

**Precedent treatment.** AT&T's previously open methodology issue is resolved without changing its allocation of state functions. Hope and Duquesne support methodological latitude and preserve rate-result review. Mead, Chevron and Barnhart are applied to this explained exercise of delegated authority.

### III. The supplied methodology challenge establishes no confiscatory rate result

**Holding and operative rule.** The present facial showing does not establish that TELRIC produces unconstitutional confiscation and does not require invalidating the method or construing cost to mandate historical reimbursement. A supported challenge to an actual rate remains available; rejection of these inevitable-confiscation and arbitrary-switching theories is not an exceptionless jurisdictional bar to every challenge before a rate is fixed.

**Authority.** Part III is joined by all nine Justices. Agreement on this constitutional disposition does not supply Breyer's missing join in Part II.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a possibility of inadequate recovery under a method is not a demonstrated confiscatory rate. Hope and Duquesne ordinarily direct attention to the effect of rates as a whole rather than an entitlement to a chosen formula or recovery of every past expenditure. The incumbent's cost-study process and state rate adjudication remain available, and this record supplies no complete rate and financial showing establishing the asserted unconstitutional result.

The asserted inevitability of confiscation does not overcome that deficiency. Nor does Duquesne's concern about opportunistic switching among methods establish that this rule necessarily effects such a taking. The Court preserves that concern at its actual scope rather than treating every methodological change as harmless. Nothing proved here requires a historical-cost saving construction. Particular statutory calculation errors and actual constitutional rate effects therefore remain reviewable on their proper records.

**Precedent treatment.** Hope and Duquesne are applied to the insufficiency of this facial showing and the distinction between methodology and actual confiscatory effects; neither is converted into immunity for all methodology choices.

### IV-A. Required combination assistance must make authorized access usable

**Holding and operative rule.** Sections 251(c)(3) and 252 permit the compensated connecting functions required by §51.315(c) and (d) when the requester is not reasonably able to combine lawfully obtainable elements in the actual network setting and incumbent assistance is necessary to make access usable. This construction of the regulation together with the contemporaneous order applies to both incumbent-element combinations and connections to requester-held elements; it imposes no independent duty on an able entrant to perform the work and creates no own-facilities prerequisite, numerical cost threshold or new entrant-ability proof burden.

**Authority.** Part IV-A has nine direct joins, as identified in the common authority declaration.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the statutory duty to enable combination can include work without which authorized access would be unusable. AT&T already rejected an unambiguous assignment of every physical task to the entrant, but left these particular duties for examination. The Commission's paragraphs 293–294 supply the further reason: entrants may lack necessary facilities or network information, may combine when reasonably able, and receive incumbent assistance when unable.

The strongest contrary reading treats any feasible request as sufficient to compel incumbent labor. The request trigger and necessary-functions language must instead be read with the agency's original account of assistance. The ability limitation is an interpretation of those materials together, not words expressly printed as an exclusive condition in (c) or (d), and not a justification invented by later advocates. A theoretical ability in some other setting does not establish reasonable ability in the incumbent's actual network. Permission to combine does not become an obligation, and voluntary arrangements remain possible on lawful terms.

Compensation alone would create no authority; usable statutory access supplies the connection. AT&T's independent necessity and impairment requirements still determine which elements must be made available, and its existing-combination rule remains separate. The original order's broad element examples do not restore the vacated list or make technical feasibility the sole access criterion. A requester must identify elements before obtaining them, while the incumbent must cooperate where missing network information prevents complete specification during negotiations or arbitration. That creates neither an automatic default nor an entitlement to unspecified elements. Just, reasonable, nondiscriminatory rates and terms govern the work. The explanation is sufficient for this facial review; actual ability, engineering, compensation and element entitlement remain matters of application.

**Precedent treatment.** AT&T's nonexclusive task allocation and independent element-selection limits are applied; the particular assistance duty left open there is sustained. Mead, Barnhart, Gulf Power and New York v. FERC require actual authority and the agency's own applicable explanation, both present here.

### IV-B. Each regulatory condition and proof requirement retains its distinct scope

**Holding and operative rule.** Paragraph (c) requires both technical feasibility and no impairment of other carriers' unbundled-element access or interconnection, including for nonordinary combinations; paragraph (d) independently requires technical feasibility for connections to requester-held elements, without importing (c)(2)'s separate condition. Under (e) the incumbent proves infeasibility to the state commission for denials under (c)(1) or (d), and under (f) it separately proves (c)(2) impairment to that commission; the clear-and-convincing requirement is confined to the described specific and significant reliability/security objection to requested-point access or interconnection, including the same kind of combination objection through the order's cross-reference.

**Authority.** Part IV-B has nine direct joins, including every stated condition and limit.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the promulgated conjunction in (c) controls despite paragraph 296's disjunctive explanatory sentence. Christensen permits fair interpretation, not amendment of unambiguous terms. Both protections remain, and the surrounding account of reliability and other-carrier access remains intelligible; the wording difference requires neither a new rule nor a further explanation remand. Paragraph (d)'s separate language does not erase statutory nondiscrimination or genuine network protections.

Technical feasibility concerns engineering and operations. Novelty, necessary modification, recoverable cost, and billing or accounting adjustments alone do not establish infeasibility. A site with no possible expansion may be infeasible; where expansion is possible, the requester bears its necessary cost under the governing scheme. Physical and virtual collocation retain their distinct statutory treatment, as do §251(f)'s exemptions, suspensions and modifications. Carriers retain network-management and performance responsibility.

The incumbent possesses the network information needed to substantiate objections, justifying (e)'s and (f)'s separate burdens. Successful connections using substantially similar facilities provide substantial evidence of feasibility and the supported quality level, not a conclusive identity presumption. Paragraph 203 requires the incumbent to establish specific, significant reliability/security impacts to the state commission by clear and convincing evidence in the described requested-point setting. Footnote 622 carries that technical discussion into combinations; applying it to the same objection is a contextual reading, not an assertion that the footnote repeats the evidentiary language. No universal heightened burden is imposed on all infeasibility, other-carrier-impairment or entrant-ability disputes. A concrete supported network defense remains available. These actual conditions and the agency's information-based reasons answer the objection that the rules require unrestricted assembly while forcing incumbents to disprove speculative risks.

**Precedent treatment.** Christensen preserves the operative rule's conjunction. AT&T preserves lawful access limits; Gulf Power and New York v. FERC require agency reasons for the actual burdens rather than judicial substitute policy. No earlier holding is overruled.

## Precedent Treatment

- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board, 525 U.S. 366: applied for national implementation, state determinations and nonexclusive task allocation; its open TELRIC and affirmative-combination questions are resolved at the stated scope. Independent necessity and impairment, existing-combination protection and absence of a freestanding own-facilities prerequisite remain effective; the vacated element list is not restored.
- United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218, and Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: applied to binding implementation of actual delegation within statutory boundaries; no expertise-only source of power is recognized.
- Barnhart v. Walton, 535 U.S. 212: applied to reasonable formal implementation within the actual grant; administrative responsibility or longevity does not alone supply authority.
- FPC v. Hope Natural Gas Co., 320 U.S. 591, and Duquesne Light Co. v. Barasch, 488 U.S. 299: applied for latitude in methodology and attention to actual rate effects; no guarantee of recovery of each expenditure or categorical immunity from confiscation review follows.
- Christensen v. Harris County, 529 U.S. 576: applied to prevent an explanatory disjunction from changing the promulgated conjunctive requirement; fair interpretation remains distinct from amendment.
- National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co., 534 U.S. 327, and New York v. FERC, 535 U.S. 1: applied to separate statutory authority from reasoned exercise of it; the original combination order supplies the needed applicable reasons.
- Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co., 463 U.S. 29: the reasoned-explanation requirement remains; Breyer would apply it to require further consideration of the efficient-network benchmark's investment effects, while the Court finds the existing explanation sufficient.

## Law After Decision

Forward-looking pricing is legally available, and this efficient-technology/existing-wire-center methodology survives the facial statutory and constitutional objections with its actual cost and procedural limits. The Constitution does not require historical-cost reimbursement on this showing. Sections 51.315(c)–(f) are restored as necessary assistance for a requester not reasonably able to combine, subject to lawful element entitlement, separate paragraph conditions, supported network objections and lawful compensation. The Court supplies neither a new element list nor a carrier-specific rate or connection result. State commissions retain their assigned application functions; supported statutory and constitutional challenges to particular outcomes remain available.

## Separate Writings

**Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He accepts forward-looking cost, rejects mandatory recovery of embedded investment and the present confiscation theory, and joins the complete bounded combination holding. His disagreement concerns the adequacy of the explanation for the efficient-network pricing assumption. A compulsory leasing price based on best available technology and lowest-cost configuration can discourage replacement investment and independent facilities construction while the incumbent continues to operate existing assets. State Farm's demand for a reasoned connection between means and statutory ends requires a more convincing account of how this particular benchmark advances durable competition and deregulation. Administrative latitude and a prohibition on traditional rate-based proceedings do not alone answer that concern. He would retain the partial vacatur and return the methodology to the agency, without prescribing an embedded-cost formula or repayment of all sunk costs. Necessary, compensated connection assistance poses a different issue: reasonable-ability limits, genuine network defenses and recoverable connection costs answer the concern about unnecessary incumbent assembly. His agreement on combining does not supply agreement on TELRIC.

The consolidated cases return for implementation of the component judgments. The challenged efficient-network provision and additional combination rules are restored within the Court's construction. State commissions apply actual cost studies, rate limits, lawful element entitlement, assistance need, paragraph-specific engineering conditions and proof duties, and compensation rules to concrete disputes. No new rulemaking, universal hearing or carrier-specific factual finding is ordered to repair this facial judgment. Ordinary mandate procedure applies.

## Event

Voting Integrity Project, Inc. v. Keisling. Submitted on the merits; decided May 13, 2002. October Term 2001. On review of the Ninth Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1), following its July 11, 2001 judgment, 259 F.3d 1169, affirming summary judgment for the state defendants. The Project seeks declaratory and injunctive relief against advance voting in federal elections under Oregon's universal vote-by-mail procedure. The question is whether the federal election-day statutes prohibit advance casting when voting remains open and ballots must be received by election day.

Oregon sends ballots fourteen to twenty days before the election and permits earlier return by mail or deposit. Mailed ballots count only if received by election day. Places of deposit remain open for at least eight hours on election day and until at least 8 p.m. A replacement ballot is available at the county clerk's office or a central location when the mailed ballot is destroyed, spoiled, lost or never received. The undisputed system does not make an earlier federal winner legally final. The challenge concerns advance casting itself, not a particular disputed ballot or receipt after the deadline.

The statutes have distinct subjects. Title 2, §1 ties Senate selection to the regular election preceding the Senator's term expiration at which a Representative is regularly chosen; §7 fixes House elections for the Tuesday after the first Monday in November in even-numbered years. Title 3, §1 fixes presidential-elector appointment for the Tuesday after the first Monday in November in the prescribed fourth-year cycle. The presidential absentee provisions in then-42 U.S.C. §1973aa-1(d) address duly qualified residents absent from their election district or unit on election day, while subsection (g) permits less restrictive practices.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at merits submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Ninth Circuit is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0, on both statutory components.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Ninth Circuit's judgment rejecting the congressional-election and presidential-elector preemption claims stands. Oregon's receipt-by-election-day procedure remains operative against this challenge. The judgment requires no replacement election calendar or restructuring and grants no relief concerning ballots first received after the deadline, a question outside this case.

## Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS and BREYER, JJ., joined.

All nine directly join both holdings. No separate writing is filed.

## Holdings

### I. Advance casting is compatible with congressional election on the federal day

**Holding and operative rule.** Then-operative 2 U.S.C. §§1 and 7 do not preempt advance ballot casting when the State's congressional election remains open through the prescribed day and the final legal selection is completed on that day. Oregon's system, with continuing election-day voting, receipt required by that day and no earlier legally final winner, satisfies the rule; the same protection covers a materially equivalent advance-ballot feature, while a genuinely earlier final selection remains forbidden.

**Authority.** Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this holding and the separate presidential-elector holding below.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that a voter's completed act of casting a ballot is distinct from the State's completed legal selection of an officeholder. Foster supplies that distinction: Louisiana could not finally choose a congressional majority winner in October while leaving no selection for the federal November day. Oregon leaves the election open, accepts votes on the federal day and fixes no earlier winner. Advance transmission and casting therefore do not present Foster's conflict.

The Project's textual objection and Congress's recorded rejection of multiday polling deserve direct consideration. A common election day must retain operative force. But transferring the command for final selection to every preliminary voting act would disregard the difference the statutory term election makes. Congress's separate recognition of absentee participation confirms that election-day language can coexist with earlier voter acts; its presidential provisions do not become an express authorization of every congressional practice. The enacted timing rule, rather than convenience or historical custom alone, decides this case.

The proportion of votes returned early does not independently determine when the election is legally complete. Nor is delaying counting or certification enough to save a process that already made an earlier winner final. Here actual participation continues through election day and the receipt deadline remains that day. Later counting or certification does not extend receipt. The holding permits this advance-casting feature while preserving Congress's common final-selection date.

**Precedent treatment.** Foster v. Love is applied to distinguish preliminary participation from legally consummated congressional selection. Its prohibition of an early final election and its allowance for compatible preliminary proceedings remain controlling; no comprehensive code for all election procedures is created.

### II. Advance casting does not make presidential-elector appointment occur early

**Holding and operative rule.** Then-operative 3 U.S.C. §1 fixes the day on which the State's presidential-elector selection is legally completed, rather than requiring each preliminary ballot act to occur that day. Advance casting under Oregon's election-day participation and receipt conditions does not appoint electors early; the rule does not authorize an earlier legally final appointment or decide ballots first received after the prescribed deadline.

**Authority.** The same nine Justices directly join this independent statutory construction. Foster's congressional holding supplies an analogy, not a prior resolution of §1.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the enacted appointment date governs the State's completed selection. A ballot returned before the date contributes to the selection but does not itself appoint an elector while the election remains open. Reading §1 to forbid every such earlier act would conflict with Congress's separate provision for absentee presidential voting.

Then-42 U.S.C. §1973aa-1(d) protects absentee participation by duly qualified residents absent from their election district or unit on election day, and subsection (g) permits less restrictive practices. Those provisions support harmonizing appointment on a common day with compatible advance participation. They do not expressly authorize every early-voting scheme, govern every federal office, or dispense with the appointment date. The challenged universal procedure is lawful because it preserves election-day completion, not because every voter must independently qualify under the federal absentee requirement.

Foster's distinction helps explain why an earlier act is not necessarily a completed choice, but the separate presidential text performs its own work. Oregon continues receiving votes through the prescribed day and does not consummate appointment earlier. The Project's single-day reading therefore does not establish a statutory conflict. The Court applies the enacted system without replacing state election rules or deciding any independent constitutional claim. Practical access supports this interpretation but creates no exception to the federal command.

**Precedent treatment.** Foster's completion distinction is applied by analogy while its congressional scope remains intact. No prior presidential timing decision is enlarged into a permission for late receipt, early final appointment or replacement of an enacted state scheme.

## Precedent Treatment

Foster v. Love, 522 U.S. 67, is applied to congressional selection and its compatibility with preliminary acts. Its rule against a legally complete election before the federal day remains effective. The present presidential holding independently construes 3 U.S.C. §1; it does not retroactively expand Foster's holding. The established distinction between ordinary application of a state presidential-election scheme and replacing that scheme remains undisturbed. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

The federal election-day provisions permit advance casting within an election that stays open through the prescribed day and is legally completed then, including Oregon's universal mail procedure and materially equivalent advance-ballot features with receipt required by that day. Congressional selection and presidential-elector appointment each receive a direct statutory holding. An earlier legally final winner cannot be saved by postponing announcement or certification. This decision changes no receipt deadline, supplies no permission for ballots first received afterward, and establishes no independent constitutional entitlement to a particular voting method.

## Separate Writings

No separate writing is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The defense summary judgment and Ninth Circuit affirmance remain in effect on both statutory claims. Oregon's described procedure continues, including election-day deposits and receipt by election day. Supreme Court merits review is complete, subject to ordinary mandate procedure; no further election-administration proceeding is ordered.

The undisputed procedure leaves election-day participation available and requires receipt by election day. Post-deadline receipt, specific ballot disputes and independent constitutional claims are outside the question presented.

## Event

**Case:** Alabama v. Shelton, No. 00-1214, 535 U.S. 654.
**Event:** Merits decision.
**Argued:** February 19, 2002.
**Decided:** May 20, 2002.
**Route and posture:** Certiorari to the Supreme Court of Alabama, reviewing its decision affirming the misdemeanor-assault conviction and remaining sentence while directing vacatur of thirty days of suspended jail. Shelton represented himself in district court and at a new circuit-court jury trial. The circuit sentence included the suspended term, two years of unsupervised probation and monetary punishment. The Alabama Supreme Court held that no knowing and intelligent waiver of appointed counsel had been established.
**Question:** Whether an uncounseled misdemeanor conviction without a valid waiver may support a suspended jail term that can be activated for a probation violation.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment of the Supreme Court of Alabama.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Suspended thirty-day jail term | Affirm invalidation, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Suspended jail remains vacated; it may not be activated from this uncounseled conviction. |
| Conviction and independent monetary punishment | Preserve, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | None | Conviction, fine, restitution and costs remain intact, subject to necessary lawful state adjustment. |

The judgment is affirmed. Any necessary state-law adjustment must respect the bar on executing this jail sentence. Alabama's representation that its linked probation falls with the suspended term is distinct from the judgment's express vacatur of jail; no freestanding probation disposition is adjudicated.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirms | Full opinion and all controlling propositions. |

## Holdings

### Counsel when the original judgment authorizes suspended imprisonment

**Controlling proposition:** A State may not impose a suspended jail sentence upon an indigent defendant tried without counsel unless the defendant knowingly, intelligently and voluntarily waived counsel. The constitutional protection attaches to the adjudication authorizing imprisonment for the original offense, including a term whose execution is deferred; it does not require appointment merely because the statute permits imprisonment when the court selects a lawful noncustodial punishment.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for this holding and its stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that deferred execution does not change the offense for which a person is imprisoned. Argersinger v. Hamlin supplies the protection against imprisonment following an uncounseled prosecution; Scott v. Illinois preserves the option of a valid fine-only conviction. Here the circuit court actually selected a jail term. Revocation would execute the assault sentence, rather than punish a new offense established in a new trial.

Nichols v. United States permits the defined later counseled Guidelines use of a valid fine-only conviction, with a genuine opportunity to contest reliability and seek an authorized departure. It supplies no general approval of incarceration for the earlier uncounseled offense and no majority overruling of Baldasar v. Illinois. Gagnon v. Scarpelli addresses the distinct due-process protection at revocation. It does not replace representation when guilt is tried.

Alabama's ordinary revocation process does not retry the assault or permit an attack on the underlying conviction. Counsel at that stage cannot recover the lost investigation, advice, examination of witnesses and trial choices. Administrative expense and the usefulness of conditional sentences do not change the adjudication for which assistance is required. The State may provide counsel or select a lawful noncustodial sentence. The Court declines the late request to require appointment whenever imprisonment is merely statutorily authorized; that broader question was not properly presented.

### Valid waiver, distinct later process and the surviving judgment

**Controlling proposition:** Competence to represent oneself and awareness that counsel may be hired do not establish a knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver of appointed counsel. With no valid waiver established here, the suspended jail term must remain vacated; the conviction and independent fine, restitution and costs remain intact, subject to any necessary state-law adjustment consistent with the bar on activating this jail term from the uncounseled conviction.

**Authority:** The same six Justices join the Court's full rationale; all nine support preservation of the conviction and independent monetary punishment.

**Controlling explanation:** Godinez v. Moran distinguishes competence from an actual informed and voluntary relinquishment of counsel. The state record contains no evidence that Shelton was told counsel would be appointed if he could not afford one. His competent performance and familiarity with paid representation therefore do not cure the waiver deficiency found below.

The imprisonment rule does not foreclose placing an uncounseled defendant on probation with incarceration conditioned on a guilty verdict after a complete counseled trial de novo, the functional equivalent of permissible pretrial probation. Such a trial would adjudicate guilt anew with counsel. No such procedure is established or ordered here. Whether safeguards short of complete retrial could suffice remains open because this record presents only Alabama's insufficient ordinary revocation procedure. That reservation differs from the separate question whether freestanding probation itself requires counsel, which this suspended-sentence arrangement does not present.

The state judgment expressly vacates suspended imprisonment. Alabama separately represents that its linked probation falls with that term; the Court does not turn that representation into a finding of independently enforceable probation. The State's late theory of enforcing independent probation through counseled contempt, limited as proposed to five days' imprisonment or a $100 fine, was not passed upon below and remains for initial state-court consideration. The judgment preserves the conviction and otherwise lawful noncustodial consequences while precluding activation of this jail term on the uncounseled adjudication.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Argersinger v. Hamlin, 407 U.S. 25 (1972):** Applies its counsel-and-imprisonment protection to a judgment selecting imprisonment with execution suspended.
- **Scott v. Illinois, 440 U.S. 367 (1979):** Preserves valid fine-only adjudication and the distinction between a possible statutory penalty and an imprisonment sentence actually imposed.
- **Nichols v. United States, 511 U.S. 738 (1994):** Distinguishes its defined later counseled Guidelines permission from imprisonment for the original uncounseled offense; its limited majority agreement is unchanged.
- **Baldasar v. Illinois, 446 U.S. 222 (1980):** Its fractured authority is not generally overruled by Nichols or this decision; no broader enhancement question is resolved here.
- **Gagnon v. Scarpelli, 411 U.S. 778 (1973):** Preserves the distinct conditional due-process right to counsel at revocation, which does not supply the original trial protection.
- **Godinez v. Moran, 509 U.S. 389 (1993):** Applies the separation between capacity and a knowing, intelligent and voluntary actual waiver.

## Law After Decision

Courts may retain a valid uncounseled conviction and lawful noncustodial punishment, but may not use it to support a suspended imprisonment sentence without the required counsel or valid waiver at the guilt adjudication. Ordinary revocation counsel does not cure that defect. The separate full-counseled-retrial arrangement is not foreclosed; lesser safeguards and freestanding probation retain the precise limits stated above. Nichols's narrow later-use permission and the independent actual-waiver requirement remain in force.

## Separate Writings

Thomas's dissent, joined by Scalia and Kennedy, would reverse only the present invalidation of the suspended jail term. It reads Argersinger and Scott to make actual loss of liberty, rather than a presently unexecuted threat, the constitutional attachment point. Shelton has not been imprisoned; a violation, discretionary revocation and a determination that other sanctions are inadequate would precede incarceration. The dissent would address the adequacy of execution procedures when that issue is presented.

The dissent accepts that competent self-representation is not an informed waiver. It does not approve ordinary revocation as a substitute for a counseled trial. A complete counseled retrial, or possibly other adequate safeguards, could protect against unconstitutional execution, and no such procedure is found to exist here. Nichols's defined fine-only Guidelines permission does not authorize unconditional later imprisonment for this offense. The dissent also questions the asserted equivalence between consensual pretrial diversion and post-trial probation and the inference that existing state appointment practices establish the costs of counsel for every suspended sentence. Practical convenience does not decide the Constitution, but uncertain cost comparisons do not justify moving Scott's boundary. The dissent preserves the conviction and independent monetary punishment and leaves freestanding probation and the late contempt theory undecided.

## Procedure After Action

The Supreme Court of Alabama's judgment is affirmed. The suspended thirty-day jail sentence remains vacated; the conviction and independent monetary punishment remain. Further state proceedings, if necessary to adjust the linked disposition, must comply with the prohibition on activating this jail term from the uncounseled conviction. The late independent-probation-and-contempt theory remains for initial state-court consideration on an appropriate record.

## Event

**Case:** Brown v. Gilmore; reviewed judgment 258 F.3d 265 (4th Cir. 2001).
**Event:** Merits decision following full merits submission.
**Decided:** May 20, 2002.
**Route and posture:** Review of the Fourth Circuit's July 24, 2001 judgment affirming the Eastern District of Virginia's summary judgment rejecting the facial Establishment Clause challenge. The student and parent challengers seek invalidation and an injunction. Virginia had permitted a school minute of silence since 1976; the amendment effective July 1, 2000 made it mandatory statewide.
**Question:** Whether the daily classroom silence required by Virginia Code §22.1-203, allowing meditation, prayer or other silent activity, violates the Establishment Clause.
**Enacted arrangement:** The statute states the objectives of guaranteeing each pupil's free exercise and minimizing Commonwealth pressure to engage in or refrain from religious observation. Every school division must establish one minute of silence daily in each classroom. The responsible teacher must ensure that pupils remain seated and silent and make no distracting display. Each pupil may individually meditate, pray or undertake other silent activity that does not interfere with, distract or impede other pupils' corresponding choice. The Office of the Attorney General must intervene and provide legal defense of the law.
**Record:** Administrative directions prohibit teacher selection or suggestion of religious activity, audible devotion and student religious coercion. These directions describe implementation rather than enacted words. Legislative history and student affidavits concerning religious practices and feared pressure are part of the facial record; a future classroom violation is not an established fact.

## Holdings

### Genuine private choice during the required silent interval

**Controlling proposition:** A daily public-school minute requiring seated silence and no distracting display, while leaving each student genuinely free to pray, reflect or pursue another silent activity without requiring religious expression or disclosure, does not establish religion when the activity does not interfere with others' corresponding choice and the State neither directs devotion nor imposes substantial religious pressure. Virginia's statute and the neutral implementation described here satisfy that rule; permission for private prayer and religious motivation among supporters do not by themselves turn the required silence into official worship.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly support this holding and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Under Lemon as modified by Agostini: specific prohibitions and distinct doctrines govern conduct they resolve, an established permissible relationship governs at its actual scope, and otherwise attribution and function determine whether government exercises religious authority. Purpose, effects and institutional relationships are evidence within that inquiry, not independent fallback prongs.

Chaudhuri permits genuine silence leaving religious thought to the participant. Its university setting does not eliminate scrutiny of compulsory classrooms and younger children. Lee forbids official worship and independently protects against substantial school-created religious pressure; Santa Fe rejects delegation of the school's religious choice even before a prayer is delivered. Good News Club distinguishes private expression from governmental adoption, but its voluntary after-school forum does not decide this compulsory-school question.

Here the teacher maintains silence without selecting a devotional activity, speaker or text. No child must pray, reveal a belief or visibly refuse an identified religious observance. Administrative instructions prohibit teacher recommendations to pray, audible devotion and student coercion; those directions describe operation, not additional statutory language. Affidavits concerning religions that use movement or sound do not establish a command to practice silent prayer or a denominational classification. The law leaves nonreligious thought equally private.

The prayer-related preamble, retained prayer reference, mandatory amendment, legislative statements, accompanying resolution and Attorney General defense duty receive consideration. They do not establish devotional government conduct in this arrangement. The defense duty directs litigation, not belief. Even considering the disputed press quotations, the result is unchanged, so their admissibility need not be decided. A secular objective could not excuse actual official worship. The objection that neutral words conceal religious solicitation therefore requires examination of operation and context; it does not justify treating all required silence as religious observance.

**Limits and application:** The facial challenge fails on this statutory and implementation record. Teacher-directed devotion, religious evaluation of students and substantial pressure to pray or refrain remain subject to review. A completed coercive episode is unnecessary when the official program itself prescribes worship. A particular coercive classroom practice requires facts absent from this facial record; no distinct free-exercise claim or categorical immunity for silence laws is decided.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Lemon v. Kurtzman, 403 U.S. 602 (1971), and Agostini v. Felton, June 23, 1997:** Apply the governing unified inquiry into governmental religious authority and function; the former independent purpose, effect and entanglement prongs do not return.
- **Chaudhuri v. Tennessee, April 27, 1998:** Applies its genuine-silence and private-choice rule while separately examining the compulsory school setting and younger audience.
- **Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992):** Preserves both the independent prohibition on official worship and protection against substantial school-created religious pressure; optionality or silence during an actual prayer would not automatically remove either defect.
- **Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe, 530 U.S. 290 (2000):** Distinguishes an official representative religious-choice mechanism from this private-thought interval; a delivered prayer is not a prerequisite to finding an unlawful official program.
- **Good News Club v. Milford Central School, 533 U.S. 98 (2001):** Uses its distinction between private expression and governmental adoption without transferring its after-school forum result to compulsory classrooms.
- **Wallace v. Jaffree, 472 U.S. 38 (1985):** Retains its significance in identifying religious solicitation from statutory history and context; its independent-purpose method does not displace Agostini's current inquiry. This different statutory and operational record establishes genuine private silence.

## Law After Decision

Genuine school silence leaving thought private is constitutionally permissible under the stated conditions. Religious supporters, an express permission to pray and compulsory silence do not themselves establish official worship. Courts must still examine actual governmental function, attribution and substantial pressure in the school setting. Neither secular objectives nor neutral statutory wording protects actual directed devotion. The decision sustains this facial arrangement without restoring independent Lemon prongs or establishing immunity for later administration.

## Holdings

### I. The quantity omission does not remove subject-matter jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** Omission of the aggravating drug quantity from an indictment charging a federal offense does not withdraw the district court's subject-matter jurisdiction under 18 U.S.C. §3231. Ex parte Bain is overruled only insofar as it treats this indictment defect as jurisdictional; the prohibition on substantive indictment amendment without grand-jury resubmission, except changes of form, remains.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by every other Justice, supplies nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress gives federal district courts authority to adjudicate federal criminal prosecutions. An error in stating the accusation differs from absence of power over that class of case. Bain's broad language arose when habeas review provided the channel for correcting federal criminal error; it does not establish the present jurisdictional consequence. Lamar v. United States and United States v. Williams distinguish the power to adjudicate from the sufficiency of the accusation. The conspiracy charged here is within §3231 even though the superseding indictment lacks the quantity needed for the enhanced maximum.

The grand jury's constitutional function remains. Neither the original indictment nor later judicial findings may replace the operative superseding indictment. Stirone v. United States and Russell v. United States concern preserved charging objections and do not make this forfeited omission jurisdictional. Complete absence of a charging instrument is outside the question presented.

### II. The constitutional error remains subject to distinct forfeited-error requirements

**Controlling proposition:** The enhanced sentences lack the required quantity allegation and jury determination, and the unpreserved objection is reviewed under Rule 52(b), not treated as an intentional waiver or a jurisdictional nullity. Error, plainness, an effect on substantial rights and a serious effect on the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings remain distinct requirements; substantial-rights effect is assumed here without deciding prejudice or structural classification.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II-A's error, forfeiture and separate-requirements analysis. Part II-B's affirmative intervening-law plainness proposition is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia assumes plainness and does not join Part II-B.

**Controlling explanation:** Apprendi requires the fact authorizing a higher maximum to receive element protections. The superseding indictment alleged a detectable amount, the jury was instructed that quantity was immaterial, and no quantity verdict was returned. Judicial preponderance findings, notice of enhanced exposure and stipulations about physical exhibits do not supply either missing constitutional determination or an admission of the aggravated offense.

Olano distinguishes failure to object from intentional relinquishment and makes discretionary correction independent of substantial-rights injury. Johnson applies that distinction to a genuine jury-element omission. Under Johnson's intervening-law rule, the error is plain when controlling law changes settled contrary trial law before direct review ends and the error is clear at appellate consideration. That is the posture here; the Court does not decide initially unsettled trial law. Assuming the third condition permits resolution at the independent fourth condition without declaring the omission harmless, structural or cured. Preserved error retains its distinct inquiry; the Court does not authorize future omission of required allegations or jury findings.

### III. This record does not warrant fourth-condition correction

**Controlling proposition:** The forfeited quantity error does not seriously affect the fairness, integrity or public reputation of these proceedings in the established circumstances: proof that the conspiracy involved at least fifty grams of cocaine base is overwhelming and essentially uncontroverted at that statutory threshold. The enhanced sentences receive no relief on this claim, even assuming an effect on substantial rights; this is a particular appellate-remedy decision, not a substitute jury finding or a universal overwhelming-evidence exception.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by every other Justice, supplies nine direct votes for the rule, application and reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson permits refusal to correct a forfeited element error on the particular evidentiary and procedural circumstances without erasing the right. Here the jury found the conspiracy, both sides report physical seizures totaling approximately 380 grams of cocaine base, and the record gave notice of enhanced exposure. The supported credibility and defendant-specific objections concern substantially larger Guidelines quantities. They do not make the fifty-gram conspiracy threshold genuinely contestable on this record. The original indictment bears on notice only; it is not substituted for the superseding indictment.

The defendants correctly emphasize that neither grand nor petit jury made the required quantity determination. But genuine constitutional error and substantial-rights injury do not alone satisfy Olano's fourth condition. The decisive threshold proof, the actual objections and the intervening change from settled trial law support leaving the sentences undisturbed on this forfeited claim. The Fourth Circuit erred in treating evidentiary strength as irrelevant merely because the omission concerned the grand jury.

The Court makes no personal quantity admission for any respondent and adopts no general rule of conspiracy-quantity attribution. It does not replace the verdict, determine guilt of an aggravated offense anew or establish an innocence prerequisite. Preserved claims, materially contested threshold evidence and different trial-strategy circumstances require their applicable analysis. The remand implements rejection of these vacatur grounds; it does not reopen discretionary correction of the same omissions on the same record.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Bain, 121 U.S. 1 (1887):** Overruled only insofar as the present indictment defect removes jurisdiction; substantive amendment still requires grand-jury resubmission, with changes of form excepted.
- **Lamar v. United States; United States v. Williams:** Apply their distinction between authority over a federal prosecution and a defect in the accusation.
- **Stirone v. United States; Russell v. United States:** Preserve their charging protections for preserved objections; they do not require jurisdictional treatment or automatic correction here.
- **Apprendi v. New Jersey, 530 U.S. 466 (2000):** Applies the genuine element-protection requirement; strong proof and notice do not supply a missing allegation, verdict or valid element admission.
- **United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993):** Applies its separate forfeiture, plainness, substantial-rights and discretionary-correction requirements; the third condition does not decide the fourth.
- **Johnson v. United States, May 12, 1997:** Applies its intervening-law plainness rule and particular fourth-condition refusal of relief without converting either into a universal evidence exception.
- **United States v. Vonn, March 4, 2002:** Its distinct Rule 11 record inquiry and remand do not require a second appellate exercise of discretion in every forfeited-error case.
- **Neder v. United States:** Preserved-error review remains distinct; this decision supplies no replacement for its actual-verdict inquiry.

Forfeited claims remain subject to Rule 52(b)'s distinct requirements. Preserved error and materially different contested records retain their own governing analysis.

## Holdings

### I. Section 1331 remains available for this substantial federal claim

**Controlling proposition:** Assuming without deciding that the determination-review sentence of 47 U.S.C. §252(e)(6) does not cover this enforcement decision, it does not withdraw 28 U.S.C. §1331 jurisdiction over the substantial claim that the later commission order violates the Telecommunications Act or applicable federal rules. A purported federal claim fails this jurisdictional threshold if it clearly appears immaterial and made solely to obtain jurisdiction, or wholly insubstantial and frivolous; this complaint falls within neither exception.

**Authority:** Parts I–III of Thomas's Opinion of the Court are joined by all eight other Justices. Their jurisdictional and officer-relief propositions have nine direct supporters, independent of the disagreement over the form of the mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** Bell v. Hood separates a substantial federal controversy from eventual entitlement to relief. This complaint asserts a federal limitation on the commission's continuing order; it does not become nonfederal simply because agreement interpretation is involved. Whether the asserted private statutory cause ultimately exists remains a merits question, not a reason to deny jurisdiction over this substantial claim.

Section 252(e)(6) contains two different sentences. The first makes FCC substitution under paragraph (5), and judicial review of FCC action, exclusive remedies for a state commission's failure to carry out its section 252 responsibilities in a proceeding or other matter under that section. Paragraph (5) requires FCC preemption and assumption of responsibility within ninety days after notification or notice of that failure. The second sentence permits any party aggrieved by a state commission determination under section 252 to bring an action in an appropriate federal district court to determine whether the agreement or statement meets sections 251 and 252; it does not repeat that exclusivity. This case challenges action, not an established paragraph (5) failure.

Paragraph (e)(4) expressly excludes state-court review of commission approval or rejection of an agreement. Its separate timing rule deems an agreement approved if the commission does not approve or reject it within ninety days after the parties submit a negotiated agreement, or thirty days after they submit an arbitrated agreement. Those provisions do not withdraw ordinary federal-question jurisdiction over the present federal challenge. Paragraph (e)(3) preserves other state-law requirements in review, notwithstanding paragraph (2) and subject to §253. The Court preserves each condition rather than treating the review structure as universally exclusive or every contract dispute as federal. Full coverage of the determination-review sentence is outside the granted alternative question and unnecessary to its answer.

### II. The later administrative order is not a state-court judgment

**Controlling proposition:** The restriction on lower federal appellate review of state-court judgments does not bar this original federal challenge to the later commission action. Actual claim or issue preclusion remains a distinct question for adjudication at its proper scope.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; all nine Justices join.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 1331 does not confer appellate jurisdiction over a state-court judgment. But the complaint attacks the commission's later order, entered after the intervening federal development, rather than seeking appellate reversal of the earlier Maryland judgment. A state administrative order does not become a judicial judgment merely because an earlier related commission decision was reviewed in state court. The Court therefore rejects the categorical state-court-review objection. It neither vacates that prior judgment nor decides the separate preclusive effect of its actual determinations.

### III. Necessary officer-relief alternatives do not sustain categorical dismissal

**Controlling proposition:** A claim to restrain responsible commissioners from continuing enforcement of an order alleged to violate federal law may proceed through the prospective officer-relief route. Private intercarrier financial consequences do not by themselves convert that restraint into retrospective compensation from the State, and the commission's absence does not categorically make such relief impossible.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court's opinion; all nine Justices join this confined rule and its remedial limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The grant concerns the §1331 alternative, but immunity and indispensability were separate grounds for sustaining dismissal. Florida Prepaid permits consideration of a respondent's properly presented unchanged-judgment alternative; resolving those grounds only as necessary does not enlarge the grant to every telecommunications or immunity question.

Ex parte Young permits prospective restraint of connected officers' ongoing federal violations. The threshold examines the allegation and requested relief, not whether Verizon has proved its federal claim. This statutory scheme does not impose a restrictive substitute remedial sequence that the prospective action would evade. Seminole Tribe permits its officer route within enacted remedial limits; Coeur d'Alene preserves the properly limited route without generalized sovereign-interest balancing as a bar. Schacht keeps jurisdiction, defendants and immunity distinct.

Restraint of continued enforcement is prospective even when it changes private carriers' financial positions. Effective restraint may operate against the enforcing commissioners without the commission itself. That conclusion defeats categorical dismissal; it does not decide all other parties or prayers. A declaration affecting accrued private obligations and a direction concerning private-carrier refunds require their own analysis below. No refund entitlement is established. Edelman's boundary against retrospective State compensation and Pennhurst's boundary against federal commands enforcing state law remain.

Direct Commission amenability, regulatory-participation consent, sufficient authorization of direct-State suit and the private statutory cause need not be decided to preserve this officer claim. Florida Prepaid's Article I rule remains available on its own constitutional and unmistakable-authorization predicates; the Court finds none of those predicates here merely from participation. College Savings Bank does not equate continued ordinary commercial activity with consent or determine how this regulatory role should be classified, and Lapides's voluntary-removal circumstances are not present. The lower court must adjudicate federal compliance and tailor any permissible remedy rather than assume the compensation claim succeeds.

### IV. The judgments are vacated and the cases returned

**Controlling proposition:** The Fourth Circuit's judgments in both consolidated dockets are vacated and the cases remanded for adjudication under the jurisdictional and prospective-officer rules stated above. Remaining defendant, claim, preclusion and remedy questions must be addressed at their actual scope; the compensation merits are not resolved by this threshold decision.

## Holdings

### I. The statutory collateral standard and ordinary prejudice requirement

**Controlling proposition:** The July 1, 1997 federal application is governed by amended §2254. **Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion is joined by all nine Justices at this framework level. Stevens does not join the Court's Cronic application or its final rejection of the ordinary tactical claim.

**Controlling explanation:** Lindh makes the application date decisive for the amendment's applicability. ### II. These omissions within actual advocacy do not trigger Cronic's presumption

**Controlling proposition:** Counsel's failure to call additional mitigation witnesses and waiver of closing do not amount to a complete failure of adversarial testing in this sentencing proceeding, where counsel substantively presented mitigation already in evidence, sought mercy, obtained favorable cross-examination and resisted damaging prosecution proof. The challenged choices require Strickland analysis; the conclusion neither excuses abandonment nor eliminates other recognized circumstances in which Cronic presumes prejudice.

**Authority:** Parts II–IV of Breyer's opinion are joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author in that eight-Justice coalition. Stevens dissents from these applications and the judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Cronic addresses a complete loss of the relevant adversarial function, not every failure within representation. Counsel's opening directed the same jury to the guilt-stage experts and mitigating circumstances, invoked military service and remorse, and requested mercy. Cross-examination elicited the Bronze Star and honorable discharge, and objections excluded damaging prosecution material. These acts supplied adversarial sentencing assistance rather than physical presence alone. Cone argues that guilt-stage insanity evidence did not become a sufficient individualized case for life after conviction, and that the isolated acts left the decisive prosecution case unanswered. Those objections concern the quality and adequacy of the assistance actually given. They do not establish the complete failure on which this claimed presumption depends. No numerical tally of acts or universal rule that any activity defeats every Cronic category follows.

**Precedent treatment:** United States v. Cronic, 466 U.S. 648 (1984), is applied to the claimed complete-failure category; its other recognized grounds for presumed prejudice remain. Strickland governs the particular alleged errors within this represented proceeding.

### III. The confined tactical claim fails on a sufficient performance ground

**Controlling proposition:** On the reviewed available-mitigation and closing-argument record, the state court's rejection of deficient performance was not an objectively unreasonable application of Strickland. That sufficient ground defeats this confined claim without an actual-prejudice determination; it does not decide distinct allegations of failure to investigate or develop mitigation, their default treatment, or every remaining sentencing ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The ordinary claim was addressed below and is fairly included in review of the state rejection. Pritchett provided pretrial support for resentencing when one of two aggravators failed and the remaining weighing could not be assumed. It guaranteed no new hearing whenever any aggravator failed. Cone's 1984 direct appeal later distinguished the surviving aggravators and applied harmless error; that later ruling is not counsel's April 1982 knowledge. Counsel's testimony supplied multiple reasons for waiving closing, including existing mitigation and avoiding the particular rebuttal. This independent state-performance assessment does not depend on the district court's erroneous additional prejudice requirement. **Precedent treatment:** Williams and Strickland are applied to an independently sufficient, context-specific performance ground. Cozzolino v. State, 584 S.W.2d 765 (Tenn. 1979), and State v. Cone's remand argument presents those issues but establishes neither cause nor invalidity of the state ground. Lee concerns a different state procedural application and does not resolve Tennessee's. Private initiation, a request for personal reparations and court-like safeguards do not transform the Commission's determination of the regulatory controversy into an independent private judicial suit.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this tribunal rule and propose a different remand. Shared opposition to the existing final dismissal is not agreement on the controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Commission applies the maritime statute, reviews the ALJ and issues its own administrative order through the enacted scheme. Congress's use of complaints and adjudicative safeguards is a method of national regulatory administration. ## Holdings

### I. Actual narrowing and patentability purpose are separate requirements

**Controlling proposition:** An amendment that actually narrows a claim to obtain the patent by satisfying a patentability requirement can create prosecution-history estoppel, including narrowing for a disclosure requirement. Actual narrowing is determined from the claim's legally operative scope before and after amendment in its patent and prosecution context; a voluntary change receives the same substantive inquiry, while a cosmetic or merely explanatory change is not automatically a surrender.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court is joined in full by the other eight participating Justices at the propositions and remand stated here. Existing Warner-Jenkinson propositions not reconsidered remain governed by that decision's own authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Warner-Jenkinson preserves nonliteral protection while enforcing actual claim limitations and prosecution surrender. Exhibit Supply protects what the applicant relinquished to obtain the patent. Neither confines patentability to novelty or prior-art avoidance. A substantive restriction addressing disclosure can therefore matter; describing an objection as formal does not immunize the ensuing narrowing. Conversely, a §112 rejection or changed words do not establish the purpose and legal scope of each resulting limitation.

Moving a restriction from a dependent claim into an independent claim can narrow that independent claim, notwithstanding the restriction's earlier presence elsewhere. The Court rejects the contrary categorical implication. A feature also need not be separately novel or demanded word-for-word by the examiner to contribute to a voluntary patentability narrowing. These conclusions give SMC's combination-purpose argument real force without treating the overall prosecution response as a conclusive answer for every limitation.

**Precedent treatment:** Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co., March 3, 1997, is applied to actual limitation-specific surrender. Exhibit Supply Co. v. Ace Patents Corp., 315 U.S. 126 (1942), protects the actual concession without establishing an irrebuttable bar to all equivalents.

### II. The existing purpose-explanation burden remains distinct

**Controlling proposition:** When required narrowing lacks a disclosed legally sufficient reason, the patentee seeking an equivalent bears the explanatory burden. After fair consideration, an inadequate explanation permits the rebuttable presumption of a patentability-related reason and estoppel at the limitation; a supported nonpatentability explanation must be considered, including in the context of an older prosecution record.

**Controlling explanation:** Warner-Jenkinson already assigns this burden to the applicant, who chose the narrowed claim. It does not demand nonexistent contemporaneous formulations or turn historical silence into misconduct. Nor does it guarantee a hearing, reopening or another opportunity to discharge an existing burden. The Court adds no automatic purpose presumption for every voluntary textual change.

The written Stoll §112 response is established, but does not alone show that both the ring and sleeve terms had one purpose. Carroll's replacement combination was offered against the German reference, but the particular ring amendment still requires analysis. An attorney-error account cannot erase the issued sleeve restriction, although a supported nonpatentability explanation must receive its proper consideration. SMC's characterization of an effective concession and the district court's earlier description of the sleeve's reason as a mystery are not a stipulation that the burden has failed. The accepted record permits the three trigger determinations stated below; an admission is not their prerequisite.

**Precedent treatment:** Warner-Jenkinson's explanation burden and older-record qualification are applied unchanged; no subjective bad-faith condition or automatic second evidentiary opportunity is added.

### III. Qualifying narrowing presumptively surrenders the asserted equivalent, subject to concrete rebuttal

**Controlling proposition:** Once patentability-related narrowing is established, the patentee presumptively surrendered the asserted equivalent at the affected limitation. The patentee may rebut with a concrete, objectively supported basis, anchored in the prosecution, showing that the actual relinquishment did not encompass that variation—for example, that the equivalent was not reasonably foreseeable at the relevant prosecution stage, that the amendment's rationale related only tangentially to it, or that it could not reasonably have been described within the claim at that time.

**Controlling explanation:** A complete bar sacrifices all nonliteral protection beyond the actual concession. Unrestricted flexibility, however, would deny competitors the public boundary produced by amendment. The rebuttable presumption gives each interest legal effect. The accused infringer need not first prove that this very equivalent was surrendered; that would collapse the trigger and scope inquiries. The patentee must establish the concrete reason the variation remains outside the surrender, judged at the amendment rather than through an uncommunicated intention to retain broad rights. The stated grounds are not automatic safe harbors, a closed mechanical list or an unstructured fairness balance.

Objective grounding in the prosecution does not require a universal exclusion of reliable technical or explanatory evidence outside the contemporaneous file. Such evidence may illuminate the claim, prosecution and reason for amendment, particularly in an older record. It cannot contradict a clear public concession, delete a claim term or substitute the inventor's private wishes for the grant. Ordinary procedure governs a proffer's admissibility and any further evidentiary opportunity. Markman permits useful technical explanation while leaving public claim meaning to the court; Warner-Jenkinson requires fair treatment of supported explanations without automatic reopening.

Successful rebuttal removes only this estoppel barrier; technical equivalence and infringement still require proof. Failed rebuttal forecloses that equivalent at the amended limitation. This scope presumption is separate from the existing presumption about an unexplained amendment's purpose.

**Precedent treatment:** Warner-Jenkinson is developed through the explicit rebuttable scope presumption while its purpose burden remains intact. Markman v. Westview Instruments, Inc., April 23, 1996, preserves judicial claim construction and useful technical evidence without dictating every appellate standard or a special hearing. Graver Tank & Manufacturing Co. v. Linde Air Products Co., 339 U.S. 605 (1950), retains protection against insubstantial substitutions within the legal claim boundaries.

### IV-A. The Stoll ring amendment is qualifying patentability narrowing

**Controlling proposition:** Incorporating the pair-of-rings restriction into Stoll's replacement independent claim narrowed that claim and served a patentability purpose in the response to the §112 rejection. Festo's account that the old sealing means already distinguished the references does not defeat that trigger, although it may bear on whether the amendment's rationale encompassed the single-ring equivalent under the separate scope inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** The original independent claim's general sealing means at each end and the replacement's specific ring structure are not interchangeable. The restriction's earlier presence in a dependent claim did not give the old independent claim the same limit. This comparison adopts no general means-plus-function construction and decides no technical equivalence.

The replacement sought allowance after the disclosure rejection, requested reconsideration under §112 and addressed impurity-prevention structure in discussing the references. Even accepting that the older claim already distinguished those references, the applicant chose a more specific description to answer the statutory objection. That is a patentability reason; the examiner need not dictate the exact words. It is unnecessary to decide SMC's additional contention that the rings were independently indispensable to avoiding the art. The asserted earlier distinction remains potentially relevant to the actual extent of surrender, but does not itself rebut the scope presumption.

**Precedent treatment:** Warner-Jenkinson's actual-narrowing and patentability rule is applied to this specific claim and response; its preserved equivalents inquiry supplies the separate work on remand.

### IV-B. The Stoll sleeve amendment is qualifying patentability narrowing

**Controlling proposition:** Adding magnetizable sleeve material to Stoll's replacement independent claim narrowed that claim in the documented patentability response. The earlier proffer of inventor nonintention, lack of independent necessity and attorney mistake does not establish a supported nonpatentability purpose for the attorney's actual submission or permit deletion of the issued restriction; the scope presumption applies to the nonmagnetizable-sleeve equivalent, subject to Festo's separate rebuttal burden.

**Controlling explanation:** The original independent claim lacked this material restriction. The applicant incorporated it from a dependent claim while recasting the rejected claim to obtain allowance. Neither a separate examiner demand nor independent necessity of magnetizability is a condition of the narrowing's legal effect. The sleeve remains distinct from the cylinder tube and the ring limitations.

The Court fairly considers the earlier account that the inventor neither intended nor authorized the restriction and that it was unnecessary. Even accepting the asserted absence of inventor intent, the proffer gives no supported nonpatentability reason for the prosecuting attorney's actual selection; it instead requests that the claim be read without the chosen words. A genuinely supported nonpatentability explanation can matter, including when counsel made the amendment. The asserted mistake here does not displace the objective submission or remove the literal term. No finding of false testimony or disobedient attorney intent follows.

This conclusion rests on the actual response and the legal insufficiency of the contrary grounds. It does not rest on a supposed concession, the district judge's description of the reason as a mystery, or historical silence treated as misconduct. It adds no presumption that every voluntary edit is patentability-related and demands no nonexistent contemporary formula. Rejecting literal deletion still does not conclusively extinguish every sleeve equivalent; that remains the separate scope inquiry.

**Precedent treatment:** Warner-Jenkinson's fair-consideration and public-claim rules are applied together. Its older-record qualification neither makes an explanation automatically sufficient nor guarantees another evidentiary opportunity.

### IV-C. The Carroll ring amendment is qualifying patentability narrowing

**Controlling proposition:** Replacing Carroll's former independent claim with claim 9's express pair-of-rings limitation narrowed the claim as part of the combination submitted to distinguish the German reference and preserve patentability. The feature's alleged presence in the reference and the emphasis on other features do not defeat that trigger, although those particulars may bear on the scope of surrender of the single-ring equivalent.

**Controlling explanation:** Disclosure of rings in the specification did not itself impose the new limitation on the former independent claim. The Court identifies this ring restriction rather than substituting another component's narrowing. The applicant included it in the chosen replacement combination offered against the reference. Independent novelty, indispensability and a word-for-word examiner demand are not prerequisites to the legal effect of that choice.

The verified emphasis on magnets, end members and cushions may show a tangential relationship between the amendment's rationale and the asserted substitute; Festo must establish the relevant scope showing. Its acknowledgment of some unrelated abandonment neither concedes surrender of this equivalent nor conclusively rebuts the presumption. The Court applies the rule to this accepted ring addition and documented response, without declaring that every element of every amended combination necessarily has one purpose. Stoll's disclosure rejection and sleeve explanation are not attributed to Carroll.

**Precedent treatment:** Warner-Jenkinson's limitation-specific surrender rule governs the trigger, while the separate scope presumption preserves the legal relevance of the particular amendment rationale to the accused substitute.

### V. Preservation and the all-elements inquiry retain their own work

**Controlling proposition:** A dispositive preservation ruling must identify the actual defense, procedural act and governing rule for the particular patent and stage. The separate requirement that every material claim element be satisfied remains operative; vacating the complete-bar judgment decides neither that objection nor the legally available infringement and remedy questions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Stoll trial exchanges include counsel's immediate disagreement with an abandonment characterization. They establish neither a general surrender of estoppel nor a Carroll-specific waiver. The separate report that Carroll estoppel was not raised at summary judgment retains its attributed procedural scope. The court of appeals must address a properly presented objection under the applicable rule, without assuming an unestablished procedural omission. It must also address the all-elements objection that the en banc court left unresolved. Legal boundaries come first; genuine remaining infringement facts retain ordinary factfinder treatment. Further district-court work follows only when necessary under the corrected rule and ordinary procedure.

**Precedent treatment:** Warner-Jenkinson's element-specific equivalents rule remains controlling; overall similarity cannot erase a material limitation. Its existing no-bad-intent rule is not reconsidered, and retains that decision's eight-Justice authority.

## Precedent Treatment

Warner-Jenkinson's limitation-specific protection, purpose-explanation burden and contextual older-record qualification remain. This decision adds the rebuttable scope-of-surrender presumption after a qualifying narrowing, without merging the two burdens. Its existing no-bad-intent holding remains at its original authority. Graver Tank preserves nonliteral protection for insubstantial differences within the claim boundaries, and Exhibit Supply protects actual prosecution surrender. Markman preserves judicial construction, including embedded technical-usage questions and useful explanatory evidence, while leaving external infringement facts and unaddressed appellate standards in their ordinary places. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Narrowing for any patentability requirement, including disclosure, may trigger estoppel. The law now expressly presumes surrender of the asserted equivalent after that trigger, while permitting concrete objective rebuttal directed to what the amendment reasonably relinquished. Required-narrowing explanation and scope rebuttal are separate burdens. Every material limitation remains operative; neither rejection of a complete bar nor successful estoppel rebuttal establishes infringement. Older records receive fair treatment without automatic evidentiary reopening. The three disputed amendments meet the trigger; only particular-equivalent scope rebuttal and the surviving ordinary procedural and infringement applications return for decision.

No patent invalidation, infringement judgment, damages award, new hearing or evidentiary reopening is ordered.

## Event

Gisbrecht v. Barnhart, No. 01-131, 535 U.S. 789. Merits decision, May 28, 2002. Argued March 20, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit.

The question is whether 42 U.S.C. §406(b) requires an exclusive lodestar calculation—reasonable hours multiplied by a reasonable rate—or review beginning with a lawful contingent-fee agreement. Gisbrecht, Miller and Sandine obtained benefits after federal court proceedings and each agreed to a fee of 25 percent of past-due benefits. Tim and Ralph Wilborn represented the three petitioners. The district courts awarded less under the lodestar method, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed. Each petitioner also received an EAJA award. Anderson, included in the lower caption, is not a petitioner here.

## Participation

## Judgment & Remedy

## Holdings

### I. Review begins with the lawful contingent agreement

**Controlling proposition:** When §406(b)'s favorable-judgment and attorney-representation conditions are satisfied, the court begins with the lawful contingent-fee agreement and independently determines a reasonable fee for the actual court representation, no more than 25 percent of the statutorily defined past-due benefits. The agreement carries no presumption of reasonableness, the attorney bears the burden of substantiating the requested award, and an exclusive lodestar cannot replace the agreement at the outset merely because it is customary in adversary fee-shifting cases.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, with the author included in that eight-Justice coalition. Scalia does not join this methodology.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 406(b) supervises a claimant-funded representation arrangement; it prescribes a reasonable fee and a ceiling, but no hourly formula. Dague's prohibition of a nonpayment-risk enhancement under its two environmental fee-shifting statutes remains intact. Blanchard likewise separates a private bargain from an adversary's statutory obligation. Neither decides this different compensation mechanism. The Commissioner's concern that accumulated benefits, earnings or dependents can increase a percentage fee without more legal work is substantial. It requires genuine individual review and defeats presumptive payment of the maximum. It does not justify depriving the contingent bargain of a role that the statute leaves it. Beginning with the agreement accounts for the representation obtained and its possibility of no payment; treating the agreement as conclusive would erase judicial protection of the benefits.

**Precedent treatment:** City of Burlington v. Dague, 505 U.S. 557 (1992), and Blanchard v. Bergeron, 489 U.S. 87 (1989), are distinguished by the statutory obligation and payor; their fee-shifting holdings remain in force.

### II. Independent review permits supported reductions, not an automatic hourly substitute

**Controlling proposition:** The court must assess the actual services and results and explain the fee allowed. It may reduce the contractual request for substandard representation, attorney-caused delay inflating accumulated benefits, or compensation disproportionate to the representation; time records and rate information may assist that inquiry, but a high effective hourly rate alone does not establish unreasonableness and no universal multiplier or hourly limit governs.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by the same eight-Justice coalition. Scalia separately agrees that judicial review, substantiation and the statutory ceiling are mandatory, but does not join this agreement-centered implementation.

**Controlling explanation:** The attorney must establish reasonableness even when a signed agreement, success and the numerical ceiling are undisputed. Hours and rates may expose a disproportionate return, while the actual quality and result may justify a reduction. They remain evidence for review of this bargain, not obligatory replacement arithmetic. Contingent economics do not confer a separate right to a risk premium, reimbursement for unrelated losses or an award justified only by saying all Social Security cases are risky. The record's statement that no individual case was argued unusually risky therefore neither proves a reasonable percentage nor requires an hourly ceiling. Each representation must be considered separately. Buckhannon distinguishes fee eligibility from a supported reasonable amount; its different eligibility rule does not displace §406(b)'s favorable-judgment requirement. The error here is the exclusive starting method, so the awards require redetermination rather than automatic enhancement.

**Precedent treatment:** Buckhannon Board & Care Home, Inc. v. West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources, May 29, 2001, supplies only the distinction between eligibility and reasonable amount; its statute-specific eligibility holding is not transferred to §406(b).

### III. Statutory conditions and overlapping EAJA fees remain distinct

**Controlling proposition:** Section 406(b) permits a judicially allowed fee for successful court representation from, and not in addition to, the prescribed past-due benefits, subject to its 25 percent ceiling and applicable statutory payment conditions. EAJA separately permits government-paid fees and other expenses under its own conditions; when counsel receives fees under both provisions for the same work, counsel must refund the smaller fee to the claimant under Public Law 99-80's saving provision.

**Authority:** The opinion's statutory-conditions discussion is adopted by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins its favorable-judgment, court-representation, cap, independent allowance, separate-EAJA and overlapping-fee refund rules. He adds no position on the unpresented individual certification, assessment, benefit-base or criminal-liability applications. These limitations do not withhold his join from Parts I–II or the mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** A contract is not an award. Certification is out of benefits, not an additional government fee. No other fee may be payable or certified for the same court representation except as §406(b) permits, subject to the separate EAJA saving provision. The separate EAJA award neither determines the §406(b) amount nor imports EAJA's rate limitations into it. Actual receipt of overlapping fees for the same work triggers the smaller-fee refund; the comparison concerns fees, not every expense. Schaefer preserves EAJA's independent finality, timing and entitlement rules, and Forney permits review of actual unawarded relief. Neither establishes the method decided today. Administrative representation under §406(a) remains separate; the aggregate administrative-and-court cap question is outside the presented methodology issue.

The applicable benefit-base provision excludes continued benefits under §423(g) or (h) and calculates benefits before an applicable §1320a-6(a) reduction. Its 1994 addition applies to favorable judgments made after 180 days after August 15, 1994. The assessment amendment applies when certification is required after January 31, 2000. Subsection (d) assesses the attorney, permits offset collection, prohibits direct or indirect claimant reimbursement, and permits collection and availability for obligation only to the extent and in the amount provided in advance by appropriations Acts. The pre-2001 rate is 6.3 percent; after 2000 the Commissioner sets the cost-recovery rate, capped at 6.3 percent. Actual dates and inputs govern those applications on remand. Section 406(b)(2) forbids charging, demanding, receiving or collecting more than the court allows, with a maximum $500 fine, one year of imprisonment, or both, subject to the specific EAJA saving rule. The methodology dispute establishes no violation.

**Precedent treatment:** Shalala v. Schaefer, 509 U.S. 292 (1993), and Forney v. Apfel, 524 U.S. 266 (1998), retain their distinct remand, fee and review functions. Martin v. Hadix, June 21, 1999, does not replace these express judgment and certification transitions with its service-date rule.

## Precedent Treatment

Dague's categorical no-risk-enhancement rule remains controlling under its two environmental fee provisions; the different claimant-funded mechanism is distinguished. Blanchard's rule governing an adversary's statutory fee obligation is likewise preserved. Buckhannon's separation of fee eligibility from reasonable amount remains, without importing its eligibility rule into §406(b). Schaefer and Forney retain their remand, finality, EAJA and adverse-relief functions. Martin's particular PLRA service-date rule does not replace §406's own transitions. Mathias v. WorldCom Technologies, Inc., May 20, 2002, supplies no bar to review of these actual unawarded fees merely because the claimants obtained benefits.

## Law After Decision

Courts reviewing claimant-funded fees for successful court representation must begin with a lawful contingent agreement and independently determine a reasonable award within the statutory ceiling. The contract percentage is not presumptively due; supported reductions, time-and-rate evidence and an explained individual decision protect benefits. Neither exclusive lodestar replacement nor automatic maximum payment is the governing rule. Administrative compensation, EAJA eligibility and payment, and the statutory certification and assessment conditions remain separate.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia dissents from Parts I–II and the judgment, while joining the independently stated judicial-review and statutory safeguards. He would value reasonable hours at a reasonable market rate, without a nonpayment-risk multiplier, and affirm these three awards. Section 406(b)'s direction to determine a reasonable fee, in his view, calls for valuation of services rather than enforcement of the contingent percentage. Changing the payor does not change that judicial task, and the percentage maximum remains useful as an additional ceiling. Dague supports this approach without already deciding this statute. He rejects substituting EAJA's limited rate for the relevant market rate.

He objects that the Court combines an agreement made before the result with an after-the-result reasonableness review whose reductions remain less definite than lodestar. Earnings, dependents and delay can increase benefits without corresponding work; attorney-caused delay can also violate the bargain's implicit obligation. The supplied local rates and hours, rejection of risk enhancement and explanations of Miller's objections support affirmance under his method. He accepts independent substantiation, the ceiling and the overlapping-fee refund, without joining the Court's agreement method or finding misconduct.

The Ninth Circuit must return the three reviewed determinations for separate awards using the lawful agreements as the starting point, actual services and results, the attorney's substantiation burden, the statutory ceiling and the applicable payment and overlap rules. Existing supported service findings remain available. Final amounts depend on the actual representation and statutory inputs.

## Holdings

### I. Enforcement jurisdiction and the actual release

**Controlling proposition:** A federal court may enforce a settlement incorporated into its judgment or covered by an express retention of enforcement jurisdiction. That jurisdiction does not itself authorize an injunction against state proceedings or determine the scope of the release; the court must separately satisfy 28 U.S.C. §2283.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight participating Justices, controls each holding here. The shared ground is the statute and this release, not a universal rule allocating every evidence question exclusively to the second forum.

**Controlling explanation:** Kokkonen permits enforcement where the judicial disposition incorporates the obligation or expressly retains jurisdiction, as this disposition does. Prudential therefore has a forum for enforcing the settlement. But Congress separately restricts injunctions against state proceedings to those expressly authorized by Act of Congress, necessary in aid of federal jurisdiction, or necessary to protect or effectuate federal judgments. No Act expressly authorizing this injunction is identified. The two remaining exceptions must each justify the restraint actually imposed; retained jurisdiction cannot do their work.

**Precedent treatment:** Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co., 511 U.S. 375 (1994), is applied to the incorporated release and retained jurisdiction; it creates no independent Anti-Injunction Act exception.

### II. Judgment protection does not turn shared facts into released claims

**Controlling proposition:** A settlement court may enjoin actual prosecution of a released claim or adjudication inconsistent with a matter conclusively resolved, within the release's lawful scope and §2283's judgment-protection exception. It may not prohibit evidence offered for an expressly retained claim merely because the evidence concerns a released transaction; it must identify the protected claim or issue and connect the proposed use of proof to that adjudicative conflict.

**Controlling explanation:** Chick Kam Choo requires identification of the adjudication protected against relitigation. Matsushita gives lawful representative releases their real effect, including claims beyond those tried; it does not erase valid exclusions. Baker distinguishes a judgment's binding effect from the separate demand that another court give its evidence restrictions compulsory operation. The Lowes are bound for two policies, and a request for punitive or other relief may be an indirect attempt to recover on a released transaction. Their excluded-policy label therefore is not conclusive. Equally, the notice expressly allowed policy-specific exclusions, and use of common sales-practice facts does not by itself seek compensation for a released policy or reopen a decided issue. Prudential's concern about circumvention warrants inquiry into the claim and the use of proof, not a categorical prohibition based only on factual association. The present order exceeds that boundary.

**Precedent treatment:** Chick Kam Choo v. Exxon Corp., 486 U.S. 140 (1988), supplies the precise relitigation inquiry. Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367 (1996), preserves lawful release effect and valid exclusions. Baker v. General Motors Corp., 522 U.S. 222 (1998), informs the bounded distinction between adjudicative effect and evidentiary operation; its different binding-party setting remains material.

### III. Administration alone does not establish necessity in aid of jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** Section 2283's aid-of-jurisdiction exception requires necessity to prevent serious impairment of the federal court's ability to administer or dispose of its case. Retained jurisdiction, settlement complexity, shared facts and concern about future claims do not establish that necessity for the breadth of this order; the All Writs Act supplies no exemption from §2283.

**Controlling explanation:** Atlantic Coast Line requires more than federal jurisdiction or ordinary interference with a federal right. Ongoing administration of a nationwide settlement is relevant, but the stated grounds do not show that litigation over the expressly retained policies, or their overlapping proof, seriously impairs that administration. The All Writs Act permits appropriate assistance to existing jurisdiction; it does not enlarge the separate statutory exceptions. This independently defeats the asserted aid-of-jurisdiction justification for the categorical restriction. Any narrower protection must identify the actual conduct, necessity and statutory basis. The sufficient statutory grounds make the Lowes' constitutional notice and vagueness objections unnecessary to decide; the completed settlement approval is not reopened.

**Precedent treatment:** Atlantic Coast Line Railroad Co. v. Brotherhood of Locomotive Engineers, 398 U.S. 281 (1970), is applied to the required serious impairment and necessity, preserving its independent restraint on federal interference.

## Precedent Treatment

Kokkonen's settlement-enforcement jurisdiction remains distinct from permission to enjoin another court. Chick Kam Choo's precise relitigation inquiry and Atlantic Coast Line's necessity rule govern the two live exceptions separately. Matsushita's lawful-release and binding-representation limits remain, including the effect of valid exclusions. Baker's distinction between judgment recognition and evidentiary operation is applied at this release-specific scope; no general witness-access right follows. Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor and Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp. retain their representative-structure requirements; settlement benefits do not replace them, and this decision does not reopen the completed certification or settlement approval. No precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

A settlement court with enforcement jurisdiction must separately establish an Anti-Injunction Act exception for the particular restraint. Policy-specific exclusions retain practical effect: shared facts alone do not convert a preserved claim into a released one. Supported protection against actual released relief or inconsistent adjudication remains available. Ordinary reopening, admissibility, privilege and claim-specific determinations remain with the appropriate court under their governing rules.

## Event

South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection. Merits decision, May 28, 2002. Review of the Third Circuit's December 17, 2001 judgment, 274 F.3d 771; lower-court Nos. 01-2224 and 01-2296. Submitted on full merits papers before May 28, 2002; no oral-argument or exact submission date is specified.

The questions are whether EPA Title VI effects regulations create the asserted individual right under §1983 and whether the pending prospective request must independently receive adjudication under Title VI. Waterfront South residents challenge permitting of a slag-grinding cement facility. Their February 13, 2001 complaint named NJDEP and Commissioner Robert C. Shinn, Jr., and pleaded intentional discrimination and a direct effects theory. St. Lawrence intervened. The April 19 injunction used the direct route; an amendment added §1983, and the May 10 supplemental order continued relief on that ground. The Third Circuit suspended the injunction on June 15 and later rejected its §1983 basis. The amendment did not establish abandonment of the original theories.

## Holdings

### I. The asserted regulation-created effects right is not enforceable under §1983

**Controlling proposition:** Section 601's intentional-discrimination protection and §602's agency instructions do not confer the additional individual effects right asserted here. A regulation may elaborate a statutory entitlement, but cannot create this missing broader right for §1983; Sandoval's separate authorization of direct prospective Title VI enforcement does not alter that rule.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, including the author in that five-Justice coalition. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer disagree. The proposition has direct majority support despite the different formal appellate dispositions; no aggregation of distinct rationales is required.

**Controlling explanation:** Powell resolved the asserted §1983 right independently of the source of a direct statutory action. Sandoval overruled only the direct-action prohibition and expressly preserved the right-creation holding. Blessing requires a particular congressional entitlement, not simply a beneficial program or a mandatory agency command. The residents' regulatory protection may be enforceable through the direct channel without becoming the different right asserted under §1983. Treating every valid implementing regulation as a new individual entitlement would erase that distinction; treating Powell's direct-action prohibition as unchanged would disregard Sandoval. Wright and Wilder preserve regulatory elaboration of entitlements the statutes themselves confer; they do not supply the missing broader effects right. This conclusion does not foreclose an independently conferred statutory right or the original intentional-discrimination claim.

**Precedent treatment:** Powell v. Ridge, April 3, 2000, is applied only at its surviving §1983 holding. Alexander v. Sandoval, April 24, 2001, retains its express limited overruling and separate direct action. Blessing v. Freestone, 520 U.S. 329 (1997), Wright v. Roanoke Redevelopment and Housing Authority, 479 U.S. 418 (1987), and Wilder v. Virginia Hospital Association, 496 U.S. 498 (1990), retain the distinction between identifying and elaborating a statutory right.

### II. The retained prospective Title VI claim must receive its own adjudication

**Controlling proposition:** Title VI beneficiaries may seek prospective compliance by a proper covered defendant with valid, sufficiently definite §602 requirements implementing their statutory discrimination protection. The original permitting claim must be adjudicated through that existing route, applying the actual recipient, program, regulatory duty, prohibited effect, causal relationship and fitted-remedy requirements; disparity alone is not an automatic violation.

**Authority:** Part II is joined by all nine Justices. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas apply Sandoval as controlling precedent while retaining their original interpretive disagreement; they do not join a new §1983 effects right.

**Controlling explanation:** Sandoval authorizes this category of prospective action; its existence is settled law, not a question postponed for remand. The original direct theory was not shown abandoned by adding §1983. Under 40 C.F.R. §7.35(b), the court must identify criteria or methods of administering the covered program that have the prohibited discriminatory effect, or defeat or substantially impair the program's objectives for protected individuals. Section 7.35(c) separately governs site or facility selection under its own purpose-or-effect terms; its distinct claim requires its own pleading and proof and is not established here merely by the separate criteria-or-methods claim. Cumulative effects matter when the actual regulatory duty reaches them, not because courts possess general authority to equalize environmental conditions. The accepted emissions analysis neither establishes nor disproves the discrimination claim. Existing findings retain their ordinary status for review; the remaining factual, regulatory and equitable applications must be decided under the governing rule.

**Precedent treatment:** Sandoval is applied to the retained EPA permitting claim at the reach of its prospective enforcement rule, with valid and definite duties, actual elements and fitted relief; it creates no damages remedy for unintentional effects.

### III. Jurisdiction, proper defendants and interim relief require their distinct inquiries

**Controlling proposition:** A substantial federal controversy supports federal adjudication independently of eventual success. A connected state officer may face prospective relief against an alleged continuing federal violation within the enacted remedial limits, but direct agency amenability, actual officer connection and fitted equitable relief require their own predicates; the original §601 claim retains its intent requirement and actual procedural footing.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the proper-defendant, prospective-remedy and direct-claim application; he agrees in judgment only with further adjudication of the separately pleaded intentional claim and adds no ground on that component.

**Controlling explanation:** Verizon Maryland separates substantial federal jurisdiction, defendant immunity and ultimate entitlement. Young permits prospective relief against an actually connected officer; Commissioner Shinn is named, but naming him does not establish that connection or the scope of relief. NJDEP's direct amenability cannot be assumed from participation or consent not established here. The intentional claim remains distinct from the regulatory effects claim. Once §1983's asserted effects right fails, residents must receive prompt consideration of the retained statutory route and requested protection, with the required merits and equitable findings addressing the threatened injury and lawful limits of an order. The existing injunction was suspended, and vacatur does not revive it. Actual findings receive ordinary review; remand does not direct a new hearing or wholesale reopening.

**Precedent treatment:** Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission of Maryland, May 20, 2002, and Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908), are applied to jurisdiction and prospective connected-officer relief; neither decides this permitting violation or direct agency amenability.

## Precedent Treatment

Powell's regulation-created-right holding remains controlling; its direct-action prohibition remains overruled only to Sandoval's stated extent. Sandoval governs this category of prospective claim and does not supply intent, damages for unintentional effects or a new §1983 right. Blessing, Wright and Wilder retain their particular-right and regulatory-elaboration principles. Verizon Maryland and Young preserve the separation among jurisdiction, defendant, merits and remedy. No further precedent is overruled.

## Law After Decision

Title VI's direct prospective action and §1983's individual-right inquiry remain distinct. This permitting claim may proceed under the established direct route upon the actual regulatory and defendant predicates, while the asserted regulation-created §1983 effects right remains unavailable. Section 601's intentional-discrimination route retains its independent requirement. The legal route is settled; coverage, violation, causation and relief require adjudication on the record.

## Separate Writings

Justice Stevens, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, concurs in the direct-route remand and dissents from Part I. He would reconsider Powell's §1983 holding to the extent it rejects this identified entitlement. The statutory beneficiaries, nondiscrimination protection, express implementation authority and a valid, sufficiently definite implementing requirement can, in his view, define the individual protection that §1983 enforces. Blessing's particular-right inquiry and Wright's and Wilder's recognition of authorized elaboration support that conclusion. He does not treat every regulation or administrative command as a right or establish a general entitlement to equal environmental burdens. Sandoval independently requires consideration of the direct claim, so his §1983 disagreement does not undermine remand, actual causation, proper-defendant or equitable requirements.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the §1983 rejection and direct prospective adjudication but dissents from vacating the appellate judgment in full. He would affirm the rejected §1983 basis and vacate only the foreclosure of the retained Title VI theory. The two channels answer different questions, and Sandoval's direct-action rule leaves Powell's §1983 rule intact. He would require prompt determination of the covered recipient, actual regulatory duty, causal effect, proper prospective defendant and interim-relief requirements. Protection requires a supported lawful order; his disposition would not automatically restore the injunction or decide the permit's validity.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for prompt adjudication of the retained direct prospective claim, the distinct intentional claim and the actual defendant and equitable inquiries. The injunction remains suspended unless lawful preliminary relief is supported and entered. Existing findings receive ordinary review; there is no categorical new-hearing requirement. Regulatory invalidity or an independent permit-law claim not preserved in this appeal is not decided.
