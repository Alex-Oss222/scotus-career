## 3. Current Law

The opening Holdings and Standards and Tests remain authoritative at their published scope, as affected by the following effective current-term law. Exact controlling propositions and precedent treatment remain in the linked public-law sections and authoritative Records.

### Brown v. City of Oneonta — 2001-11-13

Purposeful investigative selection by race is subject to the common substantial-justification standard. Victim-supplied identifying information may justify fitted measures, but its source and the absence of hostility create no categorical exemption. The rule governs the classification and its justification; individual liability and remedies remain subject to their existing requirements.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#brown-v-city-of-oneonta-merits-2001-11-13).

### Nebraska v. Wyoming — 2001-11-13

The complete modified decree governs the parties' allocation, accounting, storage, groundwater treatment, administration and reserved rights. Its express future-effective and modification provisions control their respective subjects. Existing general enforcement and modification law remains; the settled allegations supply no independent new entitlement.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#nebraska-v-wyoming-decree-2001-11-13).

### TRW Inc. v. Andrews — 2001-11-13

The governing version of §1681p has no general discovery extension. Its precise statutory misrepresentation exception remains available when its conditions are met. The Court's ruling does not decide the first-presented actual-loss theory or create a universal accrual rule for federal statutes.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#trw-inc-v-andrews-merits-2001-11-13).

### United States Postal Service v. Gregory — 2001-11-13

A pending contractual grievance creates no categorical statutory bar to considering earlier discipline in later adverse-action review. Adequate independent review remains essential, and the agency's proof burden is separate from judicial review. Bolling's actual adequacy remains undecided by the Court. A completed reversal must receive the effect required by the applicable agreement and review framework through the available procedural mechanisms.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#united-states-postal-service-v-gregory-merits-2001-11-13).

### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Mineta — 2001-11-27

No substantive doctrinal change. The governing racial-classification law remains in force. The lower-court judgment survives without a Supreme Court endorsement of its constitutional rationale.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#adarand-constructors-inc-v-mineta-dig-2001-11-27).

### Chickasaw Nation v. United States — 2001-11-27

Section 2719(d)(1) does not give covered tribal gaming chapter 35's state wagering-tax exemption. Reporting and withholding parity operates within the provision's described subject. Other Code exclusions, the actual tax base and the occupational tax's independent linkage remain governed by their own terms; no general tribal federal-tax immunity is recognized.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#chickasaw-nation-v-united-states-merits-2001-11-27).

### Church of Scientology International v. Behar — 2001-11-27

The First Amendment supplies no automatic subsidiary-meaning immunity for a materially distinct defamatory factual accusation. Context may establish absence of actionable material falsity, and the public-figure burden remains demanding and statement-specific. Protected criticism elsewhere in a publication cannot replace those inquiries. State-law injury, causation and damages rules remain separately operative.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#church-of-scientology-international-v-behar-merits-2001-11-27).

### Correctional Services Corp. v. Malesko — 2001-11-27

A confined federal constitutional damages cause is available against a private corporation exercising federal custody for its own deliberate constitutional misconduct through policy, established practice or authorized decision. Corporate attribution and causation must be proved; ordinary negligence and respondeat superior do not suffice. Meyer's federal-agency bar and existing individual-action rules remain in force.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#correctional-services-corp-v-malesko-merits-2001-11-27).

### J. E. M. Ag Supply, Inc. v. Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc. — 2001-12-10

Plants meeting the utility statute's requirements may receive utility patents alongside the plant-specific regimes. Each right carries its own requirements and exceptions. The certificate amendments and Asgrow's surviving former-law operation retain their actual reach; the decision supplies no generalized saved-seed or research exemption from utility-patent rights.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#j-e-m-ag-supply-inc-v-pioneer-hi-bred-international-inc-merits-2001-12-10).

### Schrader v. Blackwell — 2001-12-10

The official party designation may remain linked to Ohio's recognition system in these circumstances. The holding applies burden-and-fit review to a designation restriction; it supplies no categorical approval of every recognition threshold or prohibition of truthful affiliation.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#schrader-v-blackwell-merits-2001-12-10).

### United States v. Knights — 2001-12-10

The Fourth Amendment permits the defined probation-condition and individualized-suspicion combination despite ordinary investigative purpose. Existing scope, execution and exclusion rules retain their separate operation; the decision creates no general suspicionless-search or consent-waiver rule.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#united-states-v-knights-merits-2001-12-10).

### Dusenbery v. United States — 2002-01-08

A known prisoner must receive notice through a reasonably calculated custodial route. The proved certified-mail and internal-distribution process satisfies that standard without proof of actual receipt. The holding is procedure-based and leaves genuinely unreliable or known-failure situations to their own records.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#dusenbery-v-united-states-merits-2002-01-08).

### Great-West Life & Annuity Insurance Co. v. Knudson — 2002-01-08

Section 502(a)(3) does not authorize this demand for personal payment merely from the reimbursement clause. Genuine property-based equity remains available when its defendant, property and defense requirements are met. ### Mistaken attribution and continuing organizational leadership

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 455(a) does not require disqualification where a judge was mistakenly identified as an association's president in a motion to file an amicus brief, remained on its executive committee, took no part in preparing or approving the submission, and was only vaguely aware of the earlier case. **Authority:** The per curiam opinion is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt the same statutory rule, application and reversal with remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the appearance inquiry must use the full facts. Liteky supplies the objective standard: an informed, detached observer considers the relevant circumstances together, without requiring proof of actual bias. Liljeberg likewise requires an informed assessment and keeps the existence of a violation distinct from the remedy for one. The mistaken presidential designation cannot be treated as true, but the judge's continuing executive-committee membership cannot be disregarded either.

Organizational leadership can connect a person to an institutional position even without personal authorship. That is the defendants' strongest argument. Here, however, the established absence of preparation or approval and the judge's vague awareness materially limit what the observer can attribute to the judge. The circuit's disqualification ruling therefore cannot stand on this ground.

- Liteky v. United States, 510 U.S. 540 (March 7, 1994): applies the controlling cumulative, informed-observer standard. The absence of actual bias does not defeat an appearance claim, and no additional intensity requirement governs this outside affiliation.

### US Airways, Inc. v. Barnett — 2002-04-29

### Reasonable accommodation and undue hardship perform separate statutory work

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee ordinarily satisfies the initial accommodation showing by identifying an accommodation reasonable on its face or in the run of cases, not merely one effective for that employee; once reasonableness is shown, the employer must demonstrate significant difficulty or expense in its particular operations under section 12111(10). A requested exception to a disability-neutral policy is not automatically outside the accommodation duty, and the expectation that another worker may obtain an open position under this noncontractual policy does not by itself make the position unavailable for the statutory inquiry.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that reasonable accommodation may require different treatment to provide an employee a workplace opportunity. Section 12111(9) expressly identifies possible reassignment to a vacant position and adjustment of policies. Its permissive examples do not make every requested preference reasonable, but they foreclose treating uniform enforcement alone as an answer. Nor does this policy's bidding expectation establish an incumbent who must be displaced: Barnett occupies the position, the employer declares it open for bidding, and the policy creates no contractual entitlement.

Reasonable is an independent limit. An effective change may affect fellow employees in ways not captured by the employer's own operational expense. The ordinary first showing and the employer's particular hardship defense therefore do different work. This allocation does not require the employee to disprove every possible business burden. Garrett preserves the enacted distinction between accommodation and the employer's demonstrated significant-difficulty-or-expense defense. The latter considers the accommodation's nature and cost; facility resources, staffing, expenses and operational impact; the entity's overall resources, size and facilities; and workforce functions and the facilities' geographic, administrative and fiscal relationships.

The contrary disability-created-obstacle theory would deny a seniority exception because the allocation applies to all workers. But the present accommodation responds to Barnett's inability to perform his former work, and reassignment and policy adjustment remain statutory possibilities. Reasonableness limits those possibilities without excluding the requested exception at the outset. This conclusion also rejects effectiveness alone as a complete test; the employer's actual seniority system must receive the independent consideration stated next.

**Precedent treatment:** Garrett's separation of statutory conditions is preserved. Sutton, Murphy and Toyota retain their distinct coverage rules; none converts coverage into a right to a particular accommodation.

### An established seniority system ordinarily defeats a conflicting request, subject to special circumstances

**Holding and operative rule:** An employer's showing that a requested reassignment conflicts with an established seniority system ordinarily establishes that the accommodation is unreasonable and warrants summary judgment on that issue, including when management created the system. The employee may defeat that ground by presenting evidence of special circumstances making an exception reasonable in the particular case; the employee bears that showing, and the employer retains the separate undue-hardship defense if reasonableness is established.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that established seniority carries weight beyond its possible financial cost. Objective ordering gives employees expectations of fair treatment, job security and predictable advancement. Requiring the employer in every ordinary case to justify whether those expectations should yield would substitute discretionary accommodation decisions for the consistency on which the system depends. Those benefits can exist under a management-created policy as well as a collective agreement. Hardison supplies an analogy concerning the importance of seniority, not its different statute's hardship threshold or an automatic ADA exemption.

The absence of an express ADA seniority exemption does not eliminate the word reasonable. Conversely, the independent force of reasonable does not make seniority absolute. The employee can show that the actual system weakens the expectation on which the ordinary inference rests. Examples include an employer's frequent exercise of reserved amendment authority, sufficiently reducing expectations of adherence, or existing exceptions whose nature makes one further exception unlikely to matter. The examples are nonexclusive, and no numerical frequency threshold is prescribed. Bare reserved amendment authority does not automatically establish such circumstances; the employee must explain why an exception is reasonable in the actual setting.

The objection that this approach merges reasonableness with hardship overlooks their different subjects. The seniority inquiry concerns the character of the requested preference and employees' expectations; the employer's defense concerns significant difficulty or expense in its operations. The special-circumstances opportunity answers the reason for the ordinary seniority inference rather than requiring the employee to negate every operational burden. It remains a genuine evidentiary opportunity under ordinary summary-judgment principles, even for a bona fide system, not merely a chance to prove that the policy is a sham. Scalia and Thomas's contrary exclusion would foreclose that opportunity; Souter and Ginsburg's ordinary-inquiry approach would give seniority no comparable initial effect. The statute supports the qualified rule adopted here.

**Precedent treatment:** Hardison is used for the bounded seniority analogy; its Title VII setting and hardship threshold remain distinct. No prior ADA holding is overruled: this decision resolves the previously open seniority question under Title I.

### Compaq Computer Corp. v. Commissioner — 2002-05-28

#### I. Correct tax characterization requires consistent profit measurement

**Holding and operative rule:** A court independently reviews a transaction's legal characterization for federal tax purposes while reviewing its subsidiary historical findings for clear error. Where the taxpayer owns the dividend entitlement and bears the foreign tax liability, the pretax calculation includes the gross dividend, including withholding paid on the taxpayer's behalf; a worldwide after-tax calculation must also account for the corresponding legally available United States foreign tax credit.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the tax paid on Compaq's behalf remains part of its gross dividend. Old Colony Trust v. Commissioner treats discharge of the taxpayer's obligation by another as income to the taxpayer; Diedrich v. Commissioner applies the same principle when a recipient pays the transferor's tax. Treasury Regulation §1.901-2(f)(1) identifies the foreign-tax payer by legal liability even when a withholding agent remits payment, and §1.901-2(f)(2)(i) preserves that treatment when another party assumes the liability. Compaq held the dividend entitlement on the record date and bore the Netherlands tax obligation. The withholding therefore did not turn the gross dividend into fictional income.

The Tax Court's calculation subtracted the foreign tax but excluded the corresponding United States credit, yielding an apparent loss of approximately $1.5 million. That calculation measures neither pretax nor worldwide after-tax return. Correctly including the gross dividend and actual transaction charges produces approximately $1.894 million before tax; accounting for the credit and approximately $644,000 in net worldwide tax produces approximately $1.25 million after tax. The Court rejects the Commissioner's inconsistent measure, not every inquiry into a transaction's actual economic operation.

Frank Lyon v. United States separates reviewable legal tax characterization from subsidiary historical facts. The findings about the one-hour meeting, limited investigation, promoter coordination and brief holding period remain intact. Correcting the legal computation does not find that Compaq conducted extensive diligence, and the profit figures do not establish a universal minimum-profit test.

**Precedent treatment:** Old Colony Trust and Diedrich are applied to withholding that discharges the dividend owner's tax liability; Frank Lyon's distinction between historical facts and legal tax characterization is applied without change.

#### II. This actual market transaction has economic substance and sufficient business purpose

**Holding and operative rule:** The established purchase of Royal Dutch American Depository Receipts, acquisition of the dividend entitlement and resale must be recognized under the applicable income, capital-loss and foreign-tax-credit provisions: its actual return, acquired economic rights and market exposure establish economic substance and sufficient business purpose despite its tax objective. Substantive-sham scrutiny remains available, but tax motivation, rapid resale and the reduction of avoidable risk do not by themselves defeat recognition of this demonstrated arrangement.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for the complete bounded holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that these actual economic interests distinguish the arrangement from a tax form without qualifying substance. Gregory v. Helvering requires statutory substance rather than labels; Knetsch v. United States rejects purported borrowing without genuine economic function. Frank Lyon requires recognition of genuine multiparty rights and duties supported by substantive business considerations even when favorable tax treatment helps explain the transaction.

Compaq acquired and disposed of securities through 46 transactions on the New York Stock Exchange, held the dividend entitlement on the record date, and obtained an actual pretax return after transaction charges. Prices could and did move; execution and dividend risks remained. The promoter selected the counterparty, trade sizes and prices, and management undertook only limited investigation. Those facts support the Commissioner's contention that tax consequences drove the arrangement. They do not erase its established return and economic position or transform the trades into a closed market whose prices Compaq controlled. Neither nominal title nor reporting income alone supplies the conclusion.

Winn-Dixie Stores v. Commissioner preserves substantive qualification even when a taxpayer avoids a particular statutory prohibition. Its accepted absence of both nontax business purpose and pretax profit potential differs from this record. The credit scheme does not displace that scrutiny, but scrutiny must give effect to the actual economic rights and legally correct computation. The established facts resolve substance and purpose here; a new purpose trial would identify no remaining material fact necessary to this disposition.

The Court adopts no safe harbor for nominal profits, fabricated trades, circular controlled pricing, nonexistent foreign tax liability or failure of an independent credit limitation. The case presents both substance and sufficient purpose, so it does not require a universal choice among conjunctive, disjunctive or factor-based formulations for records lacking one of those features. The 1997 §901(k) holding-period provision does not govern the 1992 transaction or establish automatic validity for all earlier transactions.

**Precedent treatment:** Gregory and Knetsch retain substantive-sham scrutiny; Frank Lyon is applied to the real economic rights acquired here; Winn-Dixie's distinction between avoiding a particular restriction and substantively qualifying for a tax benefit remains intact.

#### III. Recognition removes the reviewed adjustment and its dependent penalty

**Holding and operative rule:** Within the Commissioner's stipulation that the transaction's reporting is otherwise unchallenged, recognition removes the deficiency and §6662 negligence penalty insofar as they depend on the rejected substantive-sham adjustment, leaving only computation under the applicable statutory requirements. The contingent request to deduct transaction expenses if the transaction is disregarded has no operative work once the transaction is recognized.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion of the Court has nine votes for the deficiency, penalty and bounded computation treatment: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Its concluding treatment of the contingent expense-deduction request has eight opinion votes: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela agrees with that disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** Recognition restores the challenged income, loss and foreign-credit treatment within the reporting dispute the Commissioner has preserved. The sham adjustment therefore supplies no resulting underpayment on which to sustain the dependent negligence penalty. That conclusion does not treat limited investigation as invariably reasonable care. The alternative expense request was conditioned on disregard; recognition removes that condition and makes the alternative unnecessary. Further proceedings implement the applicable statutory computation within the stipulation, rather than reopen the case to search for an unasserted disallowance theory. Independent ownership, reporting and credit-limit objections excluded by the stipulation remain outside the question reviewed, and their statutory requirements remain in force.

**Precedent treatment:** The remedy implements the recognition required by Frank Lyon and the substantive distinction preserved in Winn-Dixie; it adds no separate rule of immunity from negligence penalties or credit limitations.

- Gregory v. Helvering, 293 U.S. 465: applied to retain the requirement of statutory substance; it does not make tax motivation alone disqualifying.
- Knetsch v. United States, 364 U.S. 361: distinguished because this transaction acquired genuine market rights and an actual return; its treatment of economically empty borrowing remains in force.
- Frank Lyon Co. v. United States, 435 U.S. 561, 580–584 & n.16: applied to genuine economic rights despite tax planning and to independent review of legal tax characterization with deference to subsidiary historical findings.
- Old Colony Trust Co. v. Commissioner, 279 U.S. 716, 729: applied because withholding discharges Compaq's own tax liability and therefore forms part of its income.
- Diedrich v. Commissioner, 457 U.S. 191, 199–200: applied for the same liability-discharge principle; payment by another does not remove the economic benefit from the taxpayer's income.
- Winn-Dixie Stores, Inc. v. Commissioner, decided February 20, 2002: distinguished on its accepted absence of both economic predicates; its preservation of substance scrutiny, enacted tax consequences and transaction-specific assessment remains controlling.

From May 28, 2002, profit analysis of a dividend transaction with established ownership and foreign tax liability must treat the gross dividend and the legally available credit consistently. This demonstrated market arrangement satisfies substance and purpose, and its reviewed sham-dependent adjustment and penalty cannot stand. The decision adds that application to the existing substance doctrine without replacing it with a nominal-profit threshold, an ownership-only safe harbor or a universal test for cases with different economic findings. Independent statutory qualification requirements retain their force. The 1997 holding-period rule supplies no governing standard for this 1992 transaction.

### Holmes Group, Inc. v. Vornado Air Circulation Systems, Inc. — 2002-06-03

#### I. A patent counterclaim does not supply the incorporated arising-under basis

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the then-operative 28 U.S.C. §§1295(a)(1) and 1338(a), a patent counterclaim alone, including a compulsory counterclaim, does not make an action arise under patent law when the plaintiff's well-pleaded complaint supplies no patent-law basis. The Federal Circuit therefore lacks the asserted specialized jurisdiction over this nonpatent Rule 54(b) judgment; that conclusion does not eliminate the district court's independent jurisdiction over Holmes's federal trade-dress action.

**Authority:** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, commands seven votes for the rule and application. O'Connor and Ginsburg agree with jurisdictional vacatur on the distinct ground described in their separate writing.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress made the plaintiff's well-pleaded claim the relevant source of patent jurisdiction. Christianson v. Colt identifies the incorporated §1338 basis: patent law must create the claim or supply a substantial question necessarily resolved to obtain the requested relief. Section 1295 refers to that jurisdictional source; its provision for jurisdiction based in whole or in part on §1338 permits qualifying mixed actions without changing the source of the patent claim. The express copyright, mask-work and trademark-only exception preserves regional review where no other §1338(a) claim supplies the specialized basis.

A counterclaim is an affirmative claim, not merely a defense. Cardinal Chemical v. Morton recognizes its independent controversy, but does not decide whether that claim can itself create the specialized appellate allocation over a nonpatent complaint. Extending Christianson's source-of-claim reasoning answers that distinct question. Rule 13's compulsory-claim duty and Rule 54(b)'s partial-final-judgment mechanism do not rewrite the jurisdictional statutes. The coercive action corresponding to Holmes's declaratory trade-dress claim concerns trade dress; a different patent action Vornado could bring does not transform that complaint.

Uniform patent adjudication supports Vornado's competing interpretation, but cannot replace the statutory reference to the well-pleaded claim. The Court rejects that interpretation without requiring a finding that the counterclaim is frivolous or manipulative. The patent counterclaim remains real and pending, yet does not confer this jurisdiction. The rule addresses a counterclaim-only patent basis. No actual or constructive amendment adding a patent claim to Holmes's complaint is presented, and the Court decides no jurisdictional consequence of such an amendment or universal rule fixing appellate jurisdiction forever at the initial filing. Patent infringement, functionality and collateral-estoppel merits remain outside this jurisdictional review.

**Precedent treatment:** Christianson's incorporated source-of-claim analysis is extended to the compulsory-counterclaim question; Cardinal Chemical's independent-counterclaim rule remains intact but supplies no new appellate grant.

#### II. The appeal must be transferred to the Tenth Circuit

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1631 requires a court lacking jurisdiction to transfer an appeal when the receiving court could have heard it when originally noticed and transfer serves the interest of justice; the appeal then proceeds as though noticed in that court on its original notice date. Those conditions are satisfied here, so the Federal Circuit's judgment is vacated and that court must transfer the appeal to the Tenth Circuit.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for this rule, its application and the definite transfer mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that transfer preserves this timely appeal in the court authorized to hear it. The district court entered final judgment on the trade-dress claim under Rule 54(b), expressly finding no just reason for delay. Holmes acknowledges that Vornado timely noticed the appeal. Sections 1291 and 1294(1) provide the regional route from the District of Kansas once the asserted specialized grant is excluded. The Tenth Circuit therefore could have heard the appeal when it was noticed.

Transfer serves the interest of justice because Vornado timely sought review and its erroneous forum choice rested on a substantial existing circuit interpretation. That precedent does not create jurisdiction by reliance, but preserving the timely appeal avoids forfeiture while correcting its destination. The competing allegations of strategic pleading and forum manipulation do not establish that this notice was knowingly baseless or abusive. Holmes itself requests regional proceedings. These supported circumstances warrant transfer, rather than dismissal or another inquiry into already established predicates; they do not make every timely misdirected appeal transferable.

Christianson supplies the procedural course of vacating an appellate merits judgment entered without jurisdiction and directing transfer to the lawful circuit. Rivet requires actual authority before a merits judgment may stand; Caterpillar's preservation of a judgment after an actual jurisdictional cure does not authorize convenience-based retention here. The mandate directs the Federal Circuit's action under §1631; the Supreme Court is not treated as the statutory transferor. The original notice date retains its statutory effect without any need to invent a different filing date. Regional review will address the preserved merits under governing law. Jurisdictional vacatur and transfer do not affirm or reverse the district court's first-claim judgment for the separate conditional order's purposes.

**Precedent treatment:** Christianson's direct-transfer remedy is applied; Rivet's actual-authority requirement is preserved; Caterpillar's actual-cure rule remains confined to a jurisdictional basis that truly arose before trial and judgment.

- Christianson v. Colt Industries Operating Corp., 486 U.S. 800: its well-pleaded-claim analysis is extended to exclude a counterclaim-only patent basis; its transfer remedy is applied, with its actual-amendment reservation retained.
- Cardinal Chemical Co. v. Morton International, Inc., 508 U.S. 83: its independent-counterclaim and continuing-controversy holdings remain intact; they do not establish this specialized jurisdictional source.
- Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana, decided February 24, 1998: its insistence on an actual jurisdictional source is applied without treating an affirmative counterclaim as merely a defense.
- Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis, decided December 10, 1996: distinguished because it preserves a judgment after actual jurisdiction arose; convenience or consent supplies no comparable authority here.
- TrafFix Devices, Inc. v. Marketing Displays, Inc.: its functionality rule is neither reached nor altered; substantive trade-dress law cannot supply missing appellate authority.

A patent counterclaim alone cannot supply the incorporated arising-under basis for the Federal Circuit's patent allocation under the governing statutes, even if compulsory. The rule preserves the independent controversy a counterclaim presents and leaves materially amended plaintiff pleadings to their own jurisdictional record. Here the proper regional forum receives the timely appeal through mandatory transfer after all §1631 conditions have been established; the jurisdictional disposition leaves the merits and conditional district-order triggers for their lawful stages.

Exact public holding, authority, treatment and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK6_PUBLIC_LAW.md#holmes-group-inc-v-vornado-air-circulation-systems-inc-merits-2002-06-03).

### SEC v. Zandford — 2002-06-03

#### I. Sales that execute a concealed appropriation plan are connected with the deception

**Holding and operative rule:** A broker who, with the required scienter and in breach of a duty to customers, secretly sells their entrusted securities for a contemporaneously concealed purpose of appropriating the proceeds commits deception in connection with the sales under §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 when the liquidation is an integral means of executing that plan. No false statement about the securities' value is required, but the actual sales, deceptive conduct and operative connection must be established, together with the applicable interstate-means and Rule 10b-5 conditions; a later independently conceived misuse of proceeds from an honestly completed sale does not satisfy this rule.

**Authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, commands nine votes for this complete statutory rule and its application to the alleged scheme.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that entrusted trading authority can itself be used to execute fraud against the securities' owners. The SEC alleges that Zandford purported to manage the Woods' account for their benefit while selling their securities to obtain proceeds for himself. Those allegations describe a single deceptive course, not an honest sale followed by unrelated theft. Investment discretion permits trades without advance approval; it does not authorize concealed liquidation for personal appropriation. Successful later conversion is not an additional element of the connection once the sale executes the concealed plan.

Superintendent of Insurance v. Bankers Life establishes that fair value in a securities sale does not defeat a claim when the sale and diversion are parts of the fraud. Chiarella supplies the duty that makes the alleged silence deceptive. Santa Fe excludes fiduciary unfairness without deception, while the complaint alleges concealed disloyal use of the very authority entrusted by the owners. O'Hagan links securities use to the consummation of entrusted-source deception; here the victims' own securities sales implement the alleged wrongdoing. Kravitz and Wharf distinguish a knowingly false transactional undertaking from later nonperformance. Their private-action reliance and loss requirements do not become automatic elements of this primary SEC enforcement claim.

Zandford argues that he already controlled the assets and that ordinary market sales remained separate from conversion. That distinction could matter if the evidence showed an honestly completed liquidation followed by independent theft. It cannot justify categorical dismissal of the different integrated scheme alleged. Neither broker status, custody, inferior management, breach alone nor temporal proximity supplies the necessary connection. Disclosed disloyalty without deception likewise does not satisfy this particular rule. Section 10(b)'s interstate condition requires use of interstate-commerce means or instrumentalities, the mails or a national securities-exchange facility. Rule 10b-5's prohibitions on fraudulent schemes, material misstatements or misleading omissions, and fraudulent practices retain their respective terms. The Court independently resolves the statutory connection; no additional controlling-deference holding is needed.

**Precedent treatment:** Bankers Life, Chiarella and Santa Fe are applied; O'Hagan's transaction-connected deception principle is applied to entrusted customer sales; Kravitz and Wharf supply the contemporaneous-false-undertaking distinction without importing private-action elements.

#### II. The categorical dismissal must be reversed while civil proof and relief are reassessed

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the complaint alleges securities sales that execute the concealed appropriation scheme, the Fourth Circuit's categorical connection ruling cannot sustain dismissal of the reviewed §10(b)/Rule 10b-5 claim. The judgment is reversed and the case remanded for application of ordinary civil proof, issue-preclusion and remedial rules under the corrected connection standard; the wire-fraud convictions do not automatically establish every securities element or restore the prior injunction and $343,000 disgorgement.

**Authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion of the Court has the same nine full joins and controls the reversal and bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court decides the legal sufficiency of the alleged relationship between deception and sales. The district court entered summary judgment after relying on thirteen wire-fraud convictions, and the Fourth Circuit separately held that those convictions did not necessarily resolve the legal securities-connection question. The SEC permits Zandford to contest that legal issue here. Rejection of the categorical dismissal therefore does not make the conviction label a substitute for the securities elements.

On remand, the court must determine what the civil record establishes, which factual issues were necessarily decided in the criminal proceeding, and what authorized relief follows. Those are ordinary liability and remedial tasks under the announced rule, not unresolved law concerning whether the pleaded integrated sales can satisfy the connection. The disposition creates no entitlement to a new hearing or unlimited discovery. Section 17(a) sufficiency is outside the SEC's requested review, and the disclaimed excessive-trading theory is not revived. Central Bank's separate private affirmative-assistance action remains intact but supplies no necessary theory for Zandford's alleged primary conduct.

**Precedent treatment:** The remand applies the transaction-connected deception rule without changing the distinct private assistance theory preserved in Central Bank or converting a wire-fraud judgment into a securities adjudication.

- Superintendent of Insurance of New York v. Bankers Life & Casualty Co., 404 U.S. 6: applied because fair-price execution does not remove fraud when the securities sale and diversion form one scheme.
- Chiarella v. United States, 445 U.S. 222: applied to retain the duty needed to make silence deceptive.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462: preserved; fiduciary unfairness alone is insufficient, while the pleaded scheme includes deception.
- United States v. O'Hagan, decided June 25, 1997: its actual securities-use connection is applied to liquidation of customers' own entrusted securities, without making every conversion a securities violation.
- Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. Kravitz, decided November 12, 1998: its knowingly false transactional-undertaking rule supports the distinction from later breach; its private reliance, causation and loss predicates retain their own scope.
- Wharf (Holdings) Ltd. v. United International Holdings, Inc., decided May 21, 2001: applied for contemporaneous deception at the securities transaction, without treating these customers as purchasers of the option at issue there.
- Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., decided April 19, 1994: its confined private affirmative-assistance action remains undisturbed; this claim concerns primary conduct in an SEC action.

Section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 reach actual securities sales that execute a contemporaneous concealed plan to appropriate customer assets when the ordinary duty, scienter, deception, interstate-means and transaction conditions are met. Fair market price and discretionary authority create no categorical exception. The rule excludes a nexus based only on status, custody or independently conceived later theft, and preserves civil proof and remedial requirements. The independent statutory construction supplies the decision without resolving a further claim to controlling deference for the SEC's early adjudications.

### United States v. Mackby — 2002-06-03

#### I. This combined FCA recovery is a covered fine

**Holding and operative rule:** The False Claims Act's fixed civil penalties and treble damages imposed together for Mackby's false Medicare claims constitute an exaction at least partly punitive and therefore subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Their compensatory and regulatory purposes remain relevant to the constitutional assessment but do not defeat coverage of this combined recovery.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies nine votes for the complete coverage holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a civil label cannot exempt a monetary demand that operates partly as punishment. Austin v. United States subjects payments to the sovereign to the Excessive Fines Clause when punishment is at least one function, even when remedial purposes coexist. Here the United States receives fixed sanctions linked to culpable false claims alongside multiplied recovery. The 111 penalties of $5,000 total $555,000, and three times the $58,151.64 overpayment adds $174,454.92, producing the $729,454.92 combined demand. Its operation includes punishment and deterrence.

The Government's strongest contrary argument is that direct overpayment understates fraud's real costs: investigation, delay and difficult-to-measure harms can justify multiplied or liquidated compensation. Bornstein recognizes both recovery and fraud-deterrence functions; Rex Trailer recognizes legitimate compensatory work in liquidated recovery. Those considerations remain relevant, but do not make this combined fixed-penalty and treble-damages obligation solely remedial. Neither the existence of compensatory functions nor the civil classification answers Austin's question.

Hudson addresses a distinct Double Jeopardy classification, including its retained personal-penalty inquiry. It supplies no substitute for the Excessive Fines threshold and no constitutional accounting ceiling here. Nor does Vermont Agency's assignment-standing and sovereign-state-defendant ruling itself supply a categorical punishment holding for every treble award. The present decision addresses the combination imposed on Mackby, not an isolated treble award or a materially different statutory combination. Coverage calls for scrutiny; it does not establish that this amount is excessive or import every criminal-procedure rule.

**Precedent treatment:** Austin's partly punitive coverage rule is applied; Bornstein's and Rex Trailer's compensatory principles remain relevant without negating punishment; Hudson and Vermont Agency retain their distinct constitutional and statutory roles.

#### II. The combined demand must be assessed for gross disproportionality

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Bajakajian, this combined monetary demand violates the Excessive Fines Clause only if grossly disproportionate to the gravity of Mackby's actual offense, assessed with substantial respect for legislative punishment judgments and appropriate attention to culpability, repeated conduct, harm, compensatory functions and sanction severity. The Ninth Circuit's remand for development and application of that inquiry stands, while established FCA liability remains intact and no replacement award or final excessiveness determination is made.

**Authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion of the Court has nine joins for this governing standard, its application and the remand: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The distinct appellate-review paragraph has the narrower authority stated below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the amount must be compared with the gravity of the actual false-claims offense. Bajakajian supplies gross disproportionality rather than strict equivalence and distinguishes statutory authorization from the constitutional limit. Congress's choice of penalties deserves substantial respect, but neither that authorization nor the minimum per-claim amount conclusively resolves the inquiry. The district court must examine the combined demand; the penalty and multiplied recovery need not be assessed as though the other did not exist.

Mackby's clinic furnished therapy, which bears on actual harm. It nevertheless used his physician father's provider number even though the father did not render, refer or supervise the represented services. The false physician-involvement representation supplied reimbursement eligibility and avoided the applicable therapist payment caps. The remand preserves those established liability facts while permitting development of the facts needed to assess culpability, repeated conduct, actual loss, broader harm and the recovery's lawful compensatory functions.

The relevant considerations do not form an exhaustive mandatory checklist, a fixed permissible multiplier, a direct-loss ratio or a government-cost ceiling. The lower opinion's illustrative question about desired deterrence does not impose a separate burden on the Government to prove that this precise sum was indispensable, or require the least amount capable of deterring fraud. Conversely, fraud liability does not authorize any conceivable amount. Bajakajian's application to a reporting offense involving lawful funds does not predetermine the different assessment here. The district court applies the announced rule to a developed record; this remaining work is a proportionality determination under settled law, not a reservation of the governing legal standard.

**Precedent treatment:** Bajakajian's gross-disproportionality rule and legislative respect are applied without transferring its reporting-offense result; Austin's separation between coverage and actual excessiveness remains intact.

#### Part II, review-standard paragraph. Independent legal review preserves supported factual findings

**Holding and operative rule:** An appellate court independently reviews the constitutional determination whether this combined punitive demand is grossly disproportionate, while reviewing supported underlying factual findings for clear error. Independent constitutional review does not authorize inventing an additional offense or substituting unsupported adverse facts for the findings.

**Authority:** The review-standard paragraph of Part II of Breyer's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplying eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela joins Part II's proportionality standard and disposition but not this paragraph.

**Controlling explanation:** Bajakajian separates the constitutional evaluation from the historical facts on which it rests. Deference to supported findings therefore does not turn the Excessive Fines boundary into unrestricted trial-court discretion. The same distinction governs review of the constitutional determination after this monetary-sanction remand; it creates no universal standard for every mixed question.

**Precedent treatment:** Bajakajian's independent constitutional review and clear-error factual review are applied to this combined FCA demand without change to their respective functions.

- Austin v. United States, 509 U.S. 602: its partly punitive coverage rule and distinction between coverage and actual excessiveness are applied to this direct monetary recovery; its disputed historical forfeiture explanation is not made necessary here.
- United States v. Bajakajian, 524 U.S. 321: its gross-disproportionality standard, legislative respect and distinct appellate/factual review rules are applied; its particular reporting-offense application does not determine this fraud sanction.
- United States v. Bornstein, 423 U.S. 303: its recovery and fraud-deterrence functions support examination of the scheme's actual operation rather than treating all multiplied recovery as solely compensation.
- Rex Trailer Co. v. United States, 350 U.S. 148: its compensatory account of liquidated recovery remains relevant but does not eliminate the independent punishment function of this combination.
- Hudson v. United States, decided December 10, 1997: its Double Jeopardy rules, including the retained Halper personal-penalty inquiry, remain distinct and supply no Excessive Fines exemption or universal cost ceiling.
- Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens, 529 U.S. 765: its assigned-claim standing and sovereign-state-defendant holdings are preserved; no separate categorical treble-damages punishment rule is drawn from them.

This combined FCA penalty-and-treble-damages recovery is a covered fine even though it also compensates the Government. Gross disproportionality to the actual offense governs the aggregate assessment, with supported facts and lawful compensatory functions retained. The constitutional evaluation receives independent appellate review and factual findings receive clear-error review. No fixed ratio, cost ceiling, indispensable-deterrence requirement or automatic invalidation follows from coverage. The character of an isolated treble award or materially different combination lies outside the presented question.

### Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Echazabal — 2002-06-10

#### I. The statute permits the bounded own-health qualification defense

**Holding and operative rule:** Under original ADA §12113(a), an employer may establish a job-related qualification standard consistent with business necessity, subject to the statute's reasonable-accommodation condition; subsection (b)'s threat-to-others example does not exhaust that general defense. The EEOC's formally promulgated own-health defense is a permissible exercise of its §12116 delegated authority, rather than a revision of §12111(3)'s statutory definition of a threat to others.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies eight direct votes for the complete statutory/delegated-regulation ground. Stone-Zsela agrees with authorization on the independent statutory ground stated separately.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory provisions do different work. Section 12113(a) establishes a general defense for qualification standards shown to be job-related and consistent with business necessity, where performance cannot be accomplished through reasonable accommodation. Subsection (b) identifies absence of a threat to others as one permissible standard. Treating that example as exhaustive would erase the independent breadth of subsection (a). The statutory definition directed to others retains its terms; the own-health regulation implements the broader defense.

Haggar, Mead and Walton require actual delegated authority and its exercise before a lawful administrative choice receives controlling treatment. Section 12116 expressly assigns Title I rulemaking to the EEOC, and the implementing regulation exercises that authority. The bounded own-health rule reasonably implements the general defense; the statute need not compel it as the only possible implementation.

The contrary reading draws force from Congress's repeated references to danger to others and its rejection of paternalistic exclusion. Those concerns constrain the rule rather than nullify the general defense. Significant occupational risk, individual objective evidence and accommodation limits distinguish the regulation from an employer's generalized judgment about another person's welfare. Employment-safety obligations support that reasonable accommodation of statutory purposes, but the Court decides no actual OSHA liability or freestanding safety exemption. Nor does an employee's willingness to accept danger automatically defeat a properly proved defense.

**Precedent treatment:** Haggar and Mead's actual-delegation requirement and Walton's formal-regulation ground are applied; no new agency-deference principle is adopted.

#### II. The employer must prove the regulation's individual and objective conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** The own-health defense requires the employer to establish a significant risk of substantial harm arising from job performance that cannot be eliminated or reduced by reasonable accommodation, through an individualized assessment of present ability to perform essential functions safely. The assessment must rest on reasonable medical judgment using current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence and must consider the risk's duration, nature and severity, likelihood, and imminence, alongside the statutory job-relatedness and business-necessity requirements.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion has all nine joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the defense concerns demonstrated risk to this worker from this job. Sections 1630.15(b) and 1630.2(r) impose the listed conditions, not a diagnosis-based veto. Bragdon supplies the objective individual method: a physician's sincere conclusion alone is insufficient. Its separate discussion of public-health authorities does not transform Chevron's private physicians into such authorities. A later declaration is not necessarily evidence of later medical knowledge; it may address the knowledge and evidence available when the employment decision occurred.

Coverage, essential-function qualification and the employer's safety defense remain distinct. Sutton, Murphy and Toyota do not turn assumed disability coverage into an adjudication of safety. Describing safety as an essential function cannot bypass the defense's evidentiary conditions, while a properly preserved independent qualification argument is not permanently foreclosed. Barnett's separate accommodation and hardship inquiries create no medical-risk presumption. The rule's accommodation condition remains operative without creating a claim a litigant did not preserve.

**Precedent treatment:** Bragdon's objective method is applied without extending its public-health weighting to private physicians; the coverage/qualification boundaries of Sutton, Murphy and Toyota and Barnett's distinct accommodation inquiry remain intact.

#### III. Reversal returns the unresolved medical-evidence question within the preserved motion

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's categorical invalidation of the own-health defense is reversed, and the case is remanded for consideration of the unresolved challenge to Chevron's medical showing under the regulation and ordinary summary-judgment rules. Disability remains assumed for Chevron's motion, and the remand does not revive an accommodation argument not advanced against that motion, reopen Irwin's separate judgment, or automatically restore Chevron's summary judgment.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's opinion has the same nine full joins as Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** The district court acknowledged a genuine dispute over whether the work posed a heightened danger to Echazabal, but discounted his contrary medical declarations because they were prepared later. The Ninth Circuit did not decide his challenge to that treatment because it rejected the regulation categorically. Its remaining task is to distinguish later medical discoveries from later evaluations of knowledge available at the relevant time, and to apply ordinary review principles to the disputed showing.

Chevron did not contest disability for purposes of its motion. Echazabal did not oppose that motion by arguing that accommodation could or should have been made, although he raised accommodation against Irwin. The statute's accommodation condition therefore cannot be used to manufacture a preserved Chevron claim or require a new accommodation investigation. The Court decides the challenged defense's availability, leaving its actual application and any properly preserved remaining issue for the lower court; it orders no new evidentiary hearing or predetermined employment outcome.

**Precedent treatment:** The objective assessment required by Bragdon governs the remand while the original ADA's distinct coverage and accommodation requirements retain their proper procedural roles.

- Haggar, Mead and Barnhart v. Walton: applied to an actual formal exercise of express delegated rulemaking authority, without allowing expertise alone to create power.
- Bragdon v. Abbott: its objective individual method is applied; its public-health-authority discussion supplies no special weight to private employer physicians.
- Sutton, Murphy and Toyota: their disability-coverage rules remain separate from qualification and safety on this assumed-coverage motion.
- US Airways v. Barnett: its accommodation/hardship distinction remains intact; no seniority presumption is imported into medical risk.

Original ADA Title I permits the EEOC own-health defense under the broader qualification provision, subject to every statutory and regulatory condition stated above. Employers bear the defense burden; diagnosis, professional sincerity and general welfare concerns are insufficient. Regulatory validity does not establish a particular employer's evidence, and it does not broaden the issues preserved on summary judgment.

### Devlin v. Scardelletti — 2002-06-10

#### Limited appellate party status follows this mandatory adjudication of the objector's own rights

**Holding and operative rule:** An unnamed member of a mandatory Rule 23 class who timely objects to approval of a settlement binding his own rights is a party for the limited purpose of appealing that approval without a further intervention prerequisite. The appellate-status dismissal must therefore be reversed and Devlin's preserved objections considered under ordinary appellate rules, without reopening the affirmed intervention denial or deciding the settlement's merits.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for the rule, limits and remand. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the relevant status depends on the particular representative adjudication and appeal. Devlin belongs to the mandatory class, cannot opt out, and has timely objected to the disposition of his own pension rights. Rule 23 makes him part of that binding proceeding. His objection gives the court and settlement proponents an opportunity to address his challenge. Recognizing status for the approval appeal therefore does not confer general control of the class or an appeal on behalf of every member.

Felzen requires an otherwise nonparty shareholder to intervene before appealing settlement of the corporation's derivative claim. Being heard does not alone create party status there. Here the additional decisive relationship is the mandatory adjudication of the member's own rights; Felzen expressly left different personal-claim categories distinct. This decision establishes that distinction rather than pretending Felzen already adopted it. Marino concerned objectors outside the class and retains its ordinary intervention rule for such nonparties.

The dissent argues that represented binding and personal litigation remain different, and that Rule 24 supplies an orderly route to independent appellate status. That concern warrants the rule's limits, but does not require duplicating the timely objection for this specific appeal from disposition of a non-excludable personal claim. Ordinary preservation, standing to appeal, appellate deadlines and management remain necessary. Richards and Matsushita preserve the requirements for valid representative binding; they do not automatically grant every affected person appellate rights. Amchem and Ortiz continue to impose independent certification and representation safeguards.

The late intervention motion does not make the settlement objections late. The actual denial remains undisturbed, and McDonald's recognition of timely postjudgment intervention in its setting does not reopen it. The settlement's validity, pension entitlement, optional-class appeals and unrelated interlocutory orders lie outside the question resolved.

**Precedent treatment:** Felzen and Marino are distinguished at their actual derivative-claim and outsider boundaries; representative-binding and certification precedents remain intact; McDonald supplies no fictional successful intervention here.

- Felzen: its intervention requirement for an otherwise nonparty shareholder appealing a derivative settlement remains controlling; this mandatory adjudication of the member's own rights presents a different category.
- Marino v. Ortiz, 484 U.S. 301: its ordinary intervention rule for outsiders is preserved.
- Richards v. Jefferson County and Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein: valid representative binding remains necessary and does not itself confer universal appellate status.
- Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor and Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp.: certification and representation protections remain independent.
- United Airlines, Inc. v. McDonald, 432 U.S. 385: its timely postjudgment intervention rule does not deem this application timely or reopen its denial.

A timely objector in a mandatory class may appeal approval disposing of his own rights without intervening, solely in that limited capacity and subject to ordinary appellate requirements. Felzen's derivative-shareholder rule remains intact. The decision gives neither general control of representative litigation nor a merits entitlement.

### Franconia Associates v. United States — 2002-06-10

#### I. Repudiation does not compel immediate accrual of a future-performance claim

**Holding and operative rule:** On the assumed undertaking to accept permitted prepayment when a borrower exercises the option, advance statutory repudiation does not inevitably start §2501's six-year period at enactment or implementation. The borrower may effectively elect to treat repudiation as breach; absent that election, the claim accrues when the promised performance is due and wrongly refused or otherwise breached, subject to the actual promise and any legally sufficient excuse for tender.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies nine votes for the rule and reversal of both reviewed contract limitations judgments.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the assumed promise concerns performance when the borrower chooses to prepay, not a warranty that Congress will enact no inconsistent law. Roehm v. Horst permits a promisee to elect an immediate action after anticipatory repudiation without requiring that election. Mobil distinguishes missed performance from anticipatory repudiation of a future governmental process promise. Winstar preserves contractual monetary responsibility while Congress remains free to legislate; the capacity to legislate does not convert every future promise into present performance.

The Government argues that the option was continuously available and that legislation immediately impaired it, making the first possible repudiation suit the first accrual under §2501. That argument confuses an optional election with an unavoidable breach date on the undertaking assumed here. The period runs when this claimant's cause is complete, not merely upon notice that future performance is threatened. Bay Area Laundry supports the distinction between an obligation and a complete enforcement claim, but supplies no ERISA timetable here. No discovery extension or tolling doctrine is adopted.

The Court answers the legal question on the Government's express assumption of the alleged unrestricted-prepayment undertaking for accrual review. Hercules and Winstar still require an actual authorized bargain before ultimate liability. Formation, authority, scope, effective election, due performance, tender or its lawful excuse, refusal, other breach, defenses and the filing interval must be examined claim by claim. The assumption establishes none of those ultimate merits. The alleged Perri request and agency response remain an affidavit whose reconsideration-stage admissibility and effect are unresolved, not a common event for all borrowers. The 1992 extension to later loans does not create a new accrual date for these pre-1979 borrowers. Both categorical contract limitations dismissals are reversed without declaring every claim timely or awarding compensation.

**Precedent treatment:** Roehm's election rule is applied; Mobil's actual/anticipatory distinction and Winstar's separation of sovereign power from contractual responsibility are applied; Hercules's authorized-bargain requirement remains intact; Bay Area Laundry supplies a completion analogy only.

#### II. The reviewed Franconia taking requires its own completed-injury inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** Repudiation of an assumed prepayment promise does not by itself establish that the distinct asserted as-applied taking was complete at ELIHPA's enactment. The Franconia taking limitations disposition is vacated and remanded to identify the protected interest, challenged governmental act, completed injury, sufficiently definitive application where material, and filing interval under the preserved claim, with remaining lawful grounds considered separately.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplying eight direct opinion votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with the vacatur and remand only.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional claim cannot be dated by mechanically adopting the rejected contract-accrual premise. Petitioners describe an as-applied burden on real-property use; the Government describes destruction of a contractual exit option. The actual protected interest, act and completed injury must resolve that distinction. If a pleaded theory alleges an already completed appropriation of a contract right, its completeness and timing require their own analysis; the contract future-performance rule neither postpones every taking nor makes both claims accrue together.

The statute preserves negotiated and conditional routes to prepayment, with distinct sale, continued-use, tenant-protection and budget provisions described above. Their existence does not prove any borrower eligible, but prevents assuming a complete permanent burden for every property upon enactment. Suitum requires a definitive use determination without useless acts that cannot change it. Palazzolo permits omission of materially equivalent applications after a definitive restriction while preserving meaningful different proposals and available exceptions. The record supplies neither universal futility nor universal finality. The Government's assertion that sale procedures were not completed does not establish that every remaining step could change every claimant's burden.

Tahoe-Sierra preserves the contextual inquiry for a properly presented regulatory claim rather than automatic taking from a selected interval. Lujan v. G & G's contract-remedy due-process holding does not independently dispose of this taking theory. The lower court must apply these rules to the preserved Franconia claims; no taking, compensation amount or aggregate finality dismissal is ordered. The distinct January 2, 2002 Grass Valley ruling is outside the reviewed appellate judgments and receives no disposition here.

**Precedent treatment:** Suitum and Palazzolo's definitive-action principles govern their actual functions; Tahoe-Sierra's contextual inquiry remains intact; Lujan v. G & G is not expanded from due process into a categorical takings bar.

- Roehm v. Horst, 178 U.S. 1: ordinary anticipatory-repudiation election is applied without compulsory acceleration.
- Mobil Oil Exploration / Marathon Oil: actual and anticipatory performance remain distinct; its restitution result supplies no automatic recovery here.
- United States v. Winstar Corp. and Hercules Inc. v. United States: actual authorized contractual undertakings govern ultimate liability while sovereign legislative authority remains.
- Bay Area Laundry: completion of an enforcement claim is distinguished from underlying obligation; its ERISA periods are not transplanted.
- TRW and Young: no general discovery extension or stay-based tolling is imported into this accrual question.
- Suitum and Palazzolo: definitive action and genuinely meaningful remaining choices retain their distinct work.
- Tahoe-Sierra: no categorical taking follows from selecting a regulatory interval.
- Lujan v. G & G: availability of a contract remedy for due process does not alone defeat a separate taking claim.

On the future-prepayment undertaking assumed here, §2501 runs from a legally effective election to treat repudiation as breach or from due performance wrongly refused or otherwise breached, rather than categorically from 1988 enactment or implementation. The constitutional claim requires its own completed-injury analysis. These rules remove the reviewed temporal grounds without establishing actual contract formation, liability, finality, taking, timeliness for every borrower or compensation.

### JPMorgan Chase Bank v. Traffic Stream (BVI) Infrastructure Ltd. — 2002-06-10

#### I. The BVI corporation is a subject of the United Kingdom

**Holding and operative rule:** A corporation organized under British Virgin Islands law is a subject of the United Kingdom under 28 U.S.C. §1332(a)(2) because the territory's corporate and governmental authority rests under United Kingdom sovereignty. That established relationship at commencement satisfies the foreign-subject category without treating the territory as an independent foreign state; other statutory jurisdictional requirements remain independently necessary.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt the rule and its reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute's alternative reference to subjects reaches the allegiance of this organized entity to the recognized sovereign whose authority sustains the legal system creating it. The British Virgin Islands is not an independent recognized foreign state. It is a territory under United Kingdom sovereignty, and incorporation under its law therefore establishes the relevant corporate relationship to that sovereign.

Traffic Stream correctly insists that alienage jurisdiction does not cover every foreign person and cannot arise from convenience. But equating subjecthood with full modern citizenship would omit the alternative Congress enacted. Individual immigration, passport and nationality distinctions do not determine the allegiance of a corporation formed under this dependent territorial government. Diplomatic and commercial purposes confirm this reading; they do not independently confer jurisdiction. No universal rule for every associated territory or every natural person's nationality follows.

Caterpillar requires actual jurisdiction rather than finality or consent. Rivet likewise requires the statutory jurisdictional source instead of a convenient substitute. Those requirements are met by the relationship existing when the action began, not by the forum clause, the completed debt litigation or a later nationality enactment. Verizon Maryland preserves the separation between jurisdiction and ultimate relief; its federal-question rule does not supply the alienage basis here.

The sovereign facts are established, so another factual inquiry is unnecessary merely because their legal significance is contested. The erroneous dismissal is reversed and the remaining appellate issues return below. The Court does not decide the financing merits anew, determine another corporation's principal place of business, or resolve the separate reach of §1332(c) to alien corporations. None is needed to answer the category question presented.

**Precedent treatment:** Caterpillar's actual-jurisdiction requirement and Rivet's statutory-source requirement are applied; Verizon Maryland's distinction between forum and ultimate entitlement is preserved without extending its §1331 holding.

- Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis: actual statutory jurisdiction is necessary; litigation completion and convenience do not cure its continuing absence.
- Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana: the governing statutory source must supply adjudicatory power; another convenient basis cannot replace it.
- Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission of Maryland: jurisdiction remains distinct from ultimate merits and relief; its federal-question holding does not determine alienage.

A BVI corporation falls within §1332(a)(2)'s United Kingdom subject category through the established sovereign relationship. The rule does not rest on the territory's separate statehood, personal nationality classifications, consent or retroactive legislation. Other diversity requirements and materially different territorial arrangements remain governed by their own predicates.

### McKune v. Lile — 2002-06-10

#### I. The required disclosures present a real, unprotected risk of incrimination

**Holding and operative rule:** A prisoner retains the privilege against required disclosures that create genuine additional criminal exposure notwithstanding conviction, prior testimony or a claim of innocence. Qualified confidentiality and past nonprosecution do not remove that exposure; if immunity replaces the privilege, it must adequately protect against use and derivative use of compelled statements.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies five votes for both holdings and the affirmance. The dissenters also recognize the incriminating risk but disagree with the compulsion conclusion below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Lile's required account of other sexual conduct presents a genuine risk concerning uncharged offenses and possible perjury. Mitchell preserves the privilege where further admissions can incriminate and confines waiver to its proper scope. Reiner establishes that professed innocence does not forfeit a supported privilege. Neither his conviction nor his earlier account makes every requested disclosure harmless.

The program supplies no use or derivative-use immunity. Its confidentiality protections contain exceptions, and the State's reported history of nonprosecution is not a legal prohibition against future use. Kastigar identifies the protection adequate to replace the privilege; it does not confer that protection here. The State may choose an adequately protective arrangement, but the Court neither grants immunity nor adjudicates hypothetical protected disclosures. Because the challenged condition threatens concrete consequences now, prospective adjudication does not require waiting for a later prosecution and evidentiary use.

**Precedent treatment:** Mitchell's retained-privilege and limited-waiver rule and Reiner's no-innocence-forfeiture rule are applied; Kastigar governs sufficient immunity without creating it in this case.

#### II. This linked package of consequences compels unprotected admissions

**Holding and operative rule:** The State may not automatically impose this material security-area transfer and combined confinement restrictions solely because a prisoner refuses disclosures carrying real criminal exposure without adequate protection against criminal use. The prospective restraint is affirmed at that scope, leaving voluntary treatment, sufficiently protected disclosures and independently justified classifications available without creating permanent rights to preferred housing or privileges.

**Authority:** Breyer's opinion commands the same five-Justice coalition identified above. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from the compulsion determination and resulting affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the automatic Level III-to-Level I reduction and transfer, considered together, impose substantial pressure to surrender the privilege. The policy materially reduces property, activities, visitation, earnings and expenditures and moves Lile to more restricted surroundings because he declines unprotected admissions. The unchanged sentence and credits limit the burden but do not make the combined condition voluntary. No finding of inevitable assault or particular increased physical danger is needed or made.

Murphy distinguishes ordinary reporting from a threatened penalty for invoking the privilege. Garrity, Spevack, Gardner and Turley establish in their employment, licensing and contracting settings that governmental control cannot exact incriminating statements through substantial penalties. Their principle survives imprisonment, while the setting informs its application. Woodard involved an optional interview without this automatic penalty; Baxter permitted a supported civil inference, not silence automatically sufficient for punishment. Mitchell's sentencing-inference rule is not extended by pretending this program adds sentence days.

Kansas argues that treatment requires candor and prisoners lack entitlement to preferred conditions. Candor may be a genuine clinical condition of participation. It does not independently justify this substantial automatic pressure toward prosecution exposure. Turner recognizes legitimate administrative and rehabilitative interests; rational purpose alone does not answer an established privilege penalty. Sandin addresses a different due-process inquiry and does not replace the Fifth Amendment standard. Its retained substantive-constraint rule, as well as the proposed hardship test, leaves this independent right to be evaluated on its own terms.

The injunction removes the coercive condition rather than prescribing treatment or a permanent classification. Kansas may revise disclosure requirements, protect against criminal use, offer voluntary treatment or act on independent classification grounds. The Court orders no immunity grant, damages or sentence reduction, decides no universal rule for minor incentives, and does not resolve the separate testing claim or future evidentiary use of compelled answers.

**Precedent treatment:** Murphy and the governmental-penalty cases are applied to the actual combined burden; Woodard and Baxter are distinguished; Mitchell, Turner and Sandin retain their distinct functions rather than supplying categorical treatment exceptions.

- Mitchell v. United States: retained privilege and limited waiver govern genuine further exposure; its sentencing-inference rule is not converted into a treatment-program rule.
- Ohio v. Reiner: a claim of innocence does not extinguish supported incriminating risk.
- Kastigar v. United States: adequate use and derivative-use protection may replace the privilege; no immunity is conferred here.
- Minnesota v. Murphy; Garrity v. New Jersey; Spevack v. Klein; Gardner v. Broderick; Lefkowitz v. Turley: substantial governmental penalties for protected silence supply the governing prohibition in their respective settings.
- Ohio Adult Parole Authority v. Woodard: optional participation without an automatic penalty is distinguished.
- Baxter v. Palmigiano: a supported disciplinary inference is distinguished from automatic punishment on silence alone.
- Sandin v. Conner: the existing substantive-constraint and meaningful-deprivation due-process rule remains intact; the dissenting hardship proposal does not control this case.
- Turner v. Safley: legitimate prison purposes remain relevant but do not alone dispose of an independent privilege penalty.

Real incriminating exposure continues during imprisonment. This automatic combination of transfer and material restrictions cannot be used to extract unprotected disclosures, even without extra sentence days or a separate entitlement to preferred conditions. Treatment candor, independent classification, protected disclosures and genuinely voluntary programs remain available. Neither the majority nor the opposing severity grounds establishes a universal rule for every minor incentive or prison condition.

### National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan — 2002-06-10

#### I. Related timely acts do not revive completed discrete claims

**Holding and operative rule:** A known discrete discriminatory or retaliatory act must be charged within its own §706(e)(1) period, subject to independently established equitable grounds; a related later act or the present effect of the earlier act does not restart that period. The ordinary period is 180 days, extended where qualifying state or local proceedings were initially instituted to 300 days or thirty days after notice of their termination, whichever is earlier; the distinct statutory seniority-system triggers remain intact.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. Parts I and III command nine votes; Part II commands six, as specified below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that each completed discharge, promotion refusal, discipline or other discrete decision is its own employment practice for this filing question. Evans distinguishes an old act's continuing effects from a current violation. Ricks locates the relevant discriminatory decision rather than its later consequences. A common supervisor, motive or connection to timely conduct does not change when a completed practice occurred.

Morgan's broader related-act theory would make the filing command ineffective whenever an employee alleges another similar decision. The statute instead supplies particular periods and particular qualifications. Section 706(e)(2) separately treats a seniority system adopted for an intentionally discriminatory purpose: the practice occurs upon adoption, when an individual becomes subject to it, or when application injures the aggrieved person. That amendment supersedes Lorance's former seniority timing rule at its stated scope; it does not create a general related-act exception. Section 706(g) bars backpay liability beginning more than two years before the charge was filed with the Commission; that ceiling limits relief on a timely practice and revives no stale discrete claim.

The charge here was filed February 27, 1995, giving the agreed May 3, 1994 start of the 300-day window; no dispute invokes the earlier thirty-day alternative. The March 8 amendment concerns the March termination. Known discrete acts outside that period cannot support independent liability merely through relationship to later acts. This record does not require deciding whether occurrence or reasonable discovery governs a concealed or undiscovered discrete act. TRW's different statutory text and exception do not decide that distinct Title VII issue, and no discovery exception is adopted here.

**Precedent treatment:** Evans and Ricks govern completed acts and present effects; Lorance is subject to the enacted seniority amendment; TRW remains confined to its own statutory construction.

#### II. One continuing hostile environment may include its earlier components

**Holding and operative rule:** A hostile-environment charge encompasses the whole connected unlawful practice when at least one act contributing to that same environment occurs within the applicable filing period, including earlier constituent conduct relevant to liability. Earlier actionability or knowledge alone does not sever that practice, but a later unrelated incident, a different environment or an intervening change ending the earlier practice does not revive it; discrete decisions retain their separate clocks.

**Authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplying six votes. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy dissent from this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory unit is the hostile environment, not every utterance viewed alone. Harris supplies the totality inquiry into objectively and subjectively abusive conditions without requiring psychological injury. Oncale requires conduct because of protected status and assessment of severity or pervasiveness in context. An individual constituent incident need not alone establish that collective wrong. When conduct contributing to the same environment occurs within the window, the charged practice occurs there even if it began and became actionable earlier.

Amtrak and the partial dissent emphasize that an employee could have sued after recognizing an already actionable environment. That opportunity does not end a practice that continues. The statute measures occurrence of the charged wrong; it does not require the employee to split the same conditions at the window's first day. Rotella's rejection of delayed pattern discovery for a complete old injury addresses a different question. This rule identifies the continuing practice rather than adding a discovery extension.

The connection must be established, not assumed from the allegation list. The kind and persistence of conduct, participants and a material interruption inform whether one environment continued; these are contextual considerations rather than a mandatory exclusive checklist. An empty interval is not automatically dispositive, but a change ending the environment prevents a later incident from reviving it. A discrete personnel decision does not become timely merely because it also supplies evidence of hostility.

The claim remains subject to proof of the substantive environment, protected-status causation and employer attribution under the governing actual-aid rules. The two-year backpay ceiling and traditional equitable defenses remain distinct. On remand the courts must determine continuity and the evidentiary consequences under this rule; the present record does not establish that every alleged event belonged to one practice.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris and Oncale supply the collective substantive practice; Rotella is distinguished; the existing Andrade, Faragher and Harrison attribution rules remain separate and unchanged.

#### III. Equity, background evidence and trial review retain independent work

**Holding and operative rule:** Waiver, estoppel and equitable tolling require their established grounds, and a prejudicial-delay defense requires lack of diligence and resulting prejudice; related acts or an internal complaint alone establish neither. Older conduct may be admissible background evidence without creating independent liability for a stale discrete claim, and the appellate court must address the limitations ruling's actual evidentiary and instructional consequences and preserved independent objections before determining the proper further proceedings.

**Authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. Their common agreement requires further appellate work; the three Part II dissenters would also reject the connected-practice liability rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Zipes treats timely charging as subject to waiver, estoppel and equitable tolling rather than as an inflexible jurisdictional barrier. Robbins & Myers does not let a collateral grievance automatically extend the filing period. Edelman permits verification of an otherwise sufficient timely submission; it does not make untimely initiation timely. Young's tolling for a legal disability supports no universal extension here. Each actual equitable ground must be proved.

Amtrak may assert a traditional delay defense upon proof of deficient diligence and prejudice. The earlier internal complaint does not establish either element by itself. The precise prejudice, remedy for proved delay, and application of that defense against the EEOC are not decided in this private action. Nor does the filing rule create a general employer defense based on preventive policies and employee nonuse, an issue the existing attribution decisions leave open.

The district court's partial summary judgment preceded a November 19, 1998 defense verdict. The Ninth Circuit vacated both judgments and ordered a new trial without resolving separate evidentiary objections. That verdict cannot automatically establish harmlessness because Morgan challenges the limitation of environment evidence and related instructions. Conversely, rejecting discrete revival proves no independent evidentiary error. The judgment is affirmed as to the connected-environment route, reversed as to stale discrete revival, and remanded to apply both rules and resolve preserved trial objections. Neither automatic restoration of the defense verdict nor an unconditional new trial for stale discrete claims follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Zipes and Robbins & Myers retain their distinct equitable and grievance rules; Edelman's timely-initiation condition and Young's actual-disability rationale remain intact.

- Evans; Ricks: current effects do not restart the clock for a completed discriminatory decision.
- Lorance: the 1991 seniority-system amendment supplies adoption, subjection and injury triggers at its stated scope.
- Harris; Oncale: the collective, contextual and protected-status nature of hostile conditions defines the relevant practice.
- Rotella: delayed recognition of an old complete injury differs from occurrence of a continuing practice.
- Zipes; Robbins & Myers: actual equitable grounds survive, but a collateral grievance supplies no automatic extension.
- TRW: its text-specific discovery construction supplies no general Title VII rule.
- Edelman: later verification preserves timely initiation; it does not revive stale acts.
- Young: actual legal-disability tolling is not a general fairness extension.
- Andrade; Faragher; Harrison: employer attribution remains governed by the actual-aid framework; no general preventive-policy/nonuse defense is adopted.

Known discrete acts retain separate filing periods despite related timely conduct. One hostile environment is timely as a practice when a contributing act falls within the window, subject to actual continuity, substantive proof, existing attribution law and distinct remedial limits. The two-year backpay ceiling does not revive stale discrete liability. Traditional equity and background evidence retain their separate functions. Concealed-act discovery timing remains undecided because the record presents known discrimination.

### United States v. Bollin — 2002-06-10

#### I. Section 853(e) does not authorize restraint merely because property may later qualify as a substitute

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 853(e), as incorporated by §982, authorizes preservation of the property identified by its reference to subsection (a); it does not authorize restraint of untainted potential substitute property merely because §853(p) may later require its forfeiture. A continuing restraint based solely on that enlargement must be vacated even when the request for release arises after conviction.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion of the Court is supported by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. That common coalition controls both holdings and the complete statutory remedy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that eventual forfeiture and present restraint require their respective statutory authority. Section 853(e) expressly identifies subsection-(a) property. Section 853(p) separately reaches other property of the defendant, up to the qualifying value, when the defendant's act or omission makes covered property unavailable because it cannot be found despite due diligence, was transferred or sold to or deposited with a third party, was placed beyond the court's jurisdiction, substantially diminished in value, or was commingled so that division cannot be accomplished without difficulty. Potential satisfaction of those conditions does not convert substitute property into subsection-(a) property.

The government invokes mandatory substitution, dissipation risk, §853(o)'s liberal-construction direction and the lower court's analogy to Billman's RICO restraint rule. Those arguments explain the preservation objective but do not change the property Congress designated. Liberal construction implements the enacted grants; it does not erase the distinction between the property presently restrainable and property reachable through a later substitution order. The possibility of dissipation cannot supply an omitted power.

Libretti requires statutory authority independently of agreement and distinguishes the actual forfeiture categories. Monsanto sustains restraint of tainted forfeitable property; Caplin & Drysdale rejects a counsel-fee exemption for such property. Neither establishes authority over this untainted IRA merely because it may become a substitute.

The continuing order invokes §853(e). The $1.2 million verdict and notice of possible substitution are not an order forfeiting the IRA, and the government has not sought the required amendment. Because the statutory source fails, the present denial of release cannot stand. The postconviction timing of the request does not repair that defect. The broader appellate counsel-of-choice question is unnecessary to resolve the challenged restraint and is not reached.

**Precedent treatment:** Libretti's statutory-boundary principle is applied; Monsanto and Caplin & Drysdale retain their tainted-property holdings and are distinguished from the missing substitute-restraint authority.

#### II. Release from this restraint leaves distinct lawful postconviction powers under their own conditions

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Circuit's asset-restraint ruling is reversed, and the release motion is remanded with instructions to vacate the IRA restraint insofar as it rests solely on §853(e) and potential substitute status; the IRA must be released from that restraint unless the government obtains a distinct legally authorized postconviction order satisfying its actual predicates. Georgia garnishment protection cannot defeat authorized federal forfeiture, but federal supremacy does not supply missing federal restraint authority.

**Authority:** Part II of the same unanimous opinion controls the confined remedy and the federal-state distinction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a new statutory label cannot preserve the invalid order. Section 853(g) operates after entry of an order declaring property forfeited and protects the property actually ordered forfeited. The existing money judgment is not the absent order against the IRA. Section 853(p) remains available under its own unavailability and value conditions; this decision neither supplies those findings nor relieves the government of obtaining the necessary order.

Georgia's garnishment provisions protect the account before payment and ordinarily protect paid benefits except for the garnishable quarter, subject to any greater exemption. Those State rules cannot defeat a valid federal forfeiture. But supremacy resolves conflict with authorized federal action; it does not authorize federal action Congress withheld.

Section 982(b)(2) presents a separate, unpreserved issue. Its intermediary restriction concerns a defendant who handled but did not retain the laundered property, with an exception when the offense conduct involved three or more separate transactions totaling at least $100,000 in any twelve-month period. Gormley did not raise that claim in the district court. Its factual predicates and any lawful consideration in later substitute proceedings are not decided here; the reservation creates no independent entitlement to reopen an issue.

The court must remove the unlawful basis now, rather than continue it on the possibility of eventual forfeiture. Any distinct postconviction order must stand on its own authority and actual conditions. The result grants relief from this restraint, not a constitutional exemption or an adjudication that precisely $80,000 must be paid to appellate counsel. The convictions and all other judgment components outside the granted asset question remain intact.

**Precedent treatment:** Libretti's separation of statutory authority from procedural or consensual sufficiency is preserved in the remedy; Monsanto and Caplin & Drysdale remain available within their actual tainted-property scope.

- Libretti v. United States, decided November 7, 1995, Part I-B: applied to require the enacted property category and statutory predicates independently of agreement, conviction or a broad forfeiture objective; its remaining waiver and procedural holdings are unchanged.
- United States v. Monsanto, 491 U.S. 600: distinguished because it addresses restraint of tainted forfeitable property and does not enlarge §853(e) to potential substitute assets.
- Caplin & Drysdale, Chartered v. United States, 491 U.S. 617: preserved for the absence of a counsel-fee exemption for tainted forfeitable property; it supplies no missing restraint authority.
- The Fourth Circuit's Billman analogy does not justify extending §853(e) beyond its subsection-(a) reference. The Court resolves the statute governing this restraint without adjudicating a separate RICO order.

Section 853(e) cannot restrain an untainted potential substitute asset solely because §853(p) may later make it forfeitable. The rule governs a continuing §853(e) order as well as its original statutory basis; a request made after conviction does not eliminate the property limitation. Actual substitute forfeiture and protection of property already ordered forfeited remain governed separately by §§853(p) and 853(g). State exemption law cannot defeat authorized federal forfeiture, and federal supremacy cannot create an omitted grant. The present IRA restraint must be removed absent a distinct valid order; the broader appellate-counsel claim and the unpreserved intermediary issue remain outside the holding.

### United States v. Faasse — 2002-06-10

#### I. The established cross-border support duty supplies the commerce connection

**Holding and operative rule:** Congress may prohibit willful post-enactment nonpayment of a legally fixed past-due support obligation when the obligor and child reside in different States, because the prohibition protects the actual interstate payment obligation against obstruction. Under the original §228, the obligation must have remained unpaid longer than one year or exceed $5,000; these alternatives do not replace the different-State relationship, the operative State-law duty or willfulness.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices, controls the rule, its application and affirmance. The common coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this conviction falls within United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder, which sustains the original §228 prohibition for a legally fixed cross-border payment obligation. A Michigan court fixed Faasse's support duty; he resided in California while his child resided in Michigan. The federal prohibition protects that established interstate monetary obligation against willful obstruction.

Faasse argues that nonpayment concerns a local family duty and requires neither travel nor a completed commercial transaction. Mussari/Schroeder rejected that argument for this statutory relationship. A law protecting payment against deliberate withholding need not require an earlier completed transfer. The Court adds no travel, flight, bank-instrument or evasion-motive element. The State's creation of the obligation does not erase its interstate payment character, and federal enforcement supplies no authority to create or modify the support duty.

Lopez requires a genuine commerce connection before aggregate magnitude can matter; Morrison rejects reliance on general social and economic consequences to supply a missing connection. The Court relies on the payment relationship itself, not downstream poverty, public-assistance costs or family-policy importance. Those limits retain their force.

The original Act governs the conviction. Its willfulness and alternative delinquency conditions remain necessary; financial hardship and culpable nonpayment remain distinct under the applicable law. Earlier arrears may establish past-due status, but completed failures before October 25, 1992 cannot themselves constitute the federal crime. Faasse's plea and the established residence relationship permit resolution of this power challenge. The State order's validity and the separate restitution calculation are outside the question reviewed. The conviction and restitution judgment therefore remain intact against the assigned constitutional objection.

**Precedent treatment:** Mussari/Schroeder is applied directly to the same original-Act interstate obligation; Lopez and Morrison retain their connection-before-aggregation restrictions. No precedent is overruled.

- United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder, decided June 30, 1997: applied directly; its interstate-payment ground, actual offense conditions, post-enactment limit and preservation of State authority remain controlling.
- United States v. Lopez, decided April 26, 1995: applied to require the actual interstate mechanism before aggregation; general downstream consequences cannot supply a missing connection.
- United States v. Morrison: preserved for the rejection of unconnected social-cost aggregation; the established cross-border payment duty supplies the distinct sufficient ground here.

The original §228 prohibition remains valid for willful post-enactment default on an operative, legally fixed support obligation owed across State lines and satisfying either statutory delinquency threshold. The different-State relationship supplies the constitutional connection without an added travel, completed-transfer, bank-instrument or evasion-motive requirement. State authority over creation and modification of the underlying duty remains intact. This decision applies the existing rule and does not enlarge it to intrastate support or authorize punishment absent the actual elements.

### United States v. Farmer — 2002-06-10

#### I. This supported trial-counsel claim requires prompt adversary review

**Holding and operative rule:** A criminal defendant who makes a substantial, specific showing that assets held in continuing civil seizure may be untainted and are needed to retain trial counsel is entitled to a prompt, focused adversary hearing on the disputed taint and actual financial need. The denial is immediately reviewable under the collateral-order rule when postponement until the end of trial would irretrievably lose the asserted counsel opportunity; Farmer's sworn submissions and the identified legitimate-property claim satisfy the hearing threshold.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion of the Court has nine direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Their agreement covers reviewability, the threshold, its application and entitlement to the hearing; their explanatory and burden differences are stated separately below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the preliminary seizure determination cannot conclusively settle this later, supported request. Civil seizures began in July 1998. Farmer filed a claim and a $5,000 cost bond, yet no civil forfeiture action preceded the May 2000 indictment. He then sought a hearing before trial. His affidavit and sworn answers supported the magistrate's effective-indigency determination. Counsel's affidavit identified agents' reported recognition of legitimate merchandise. That account supplies a particular supported assertion, not a final determination of title or lack of taint.

The government disputes Farmer's other resources and relies on the warrants. At appellate argument it accepted a hearing if he sufficiently established inability to retain counsel. The sworn record satisfies that initial requirement. The government remains free to contest the actual resources and assets at the hearing. Requiring conclusive proof before permitting the procedure needed to test those matters would confuse entitlement to process with entitlement to release.

Monsanto expressly reserved the adversary-hearing question; its approval of probable-cause restraint of tainted property does not answer this one. Caplin & Drysdale supplies no right to spend proved forfeitable proceeds, but a disputed asset does not become proved proceeds merely because it was seized. A focused post-seizure hearing preserves that distinction without trying the criminal charges. Immediate review protects the time-sensitive counsel opportunity that ordinary final review could no longer restore.

**Precedent treatment:** Monsanto's express process reservation is resolved for this supported post-seizure counsel claim; its substantive tainted-property ruling and Caplin & Drysdale's fee-exemption ruling remain intact.

#### II. The required process reflects the actual interest, error risk and bounded government burden

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Mathews, the substantial and time-sensitive interest in retaining trial counsel with legitimate funds, the concrete risk of error in continued custody and the value of focused adversary testing require the hearing on this record, while the government's forfeiture interest permits a substantial initial showing and a hearing confined to the disputed property and need. Neither every seizure nor an unsupported request triggers an asset hearing.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion of the Court has eight direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia joins the operative hearing rule on his distinct post-seizure ground, not this Mathews explanation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the requested hearing has practical value proportionate to its confined burden. Mathews directs attention to the private interest, the risk of erroneous deprivation and probable value of additional procedure, and the government's interests and burdens. The ability to use legitimate assets for a defense can be lost before a later forfeiture judgment supplies an answer. Here the concrete property assertion, sworn need evidence and prolonged custody without a civil forfeiture action establish more than a speculative desire for additional process.

Adversary testing can identify other resources and examine whether the particular needed assets are supported by probable cause of taint. Warrants supported the seizures but did not resolve those disputed matters conclusively. Good preserves due process as an independent protection even when a seizure satisfies the Fourth Amendment. Its ordinary pre-seizure protection for civil real property does not automatically govern movable property or establish a new pre-seizure rule here; its independence principle nevertheless defeats treating the warrant as a complete procedural answer.

The government has a legitimate interest in preserving forfeitable property and avoiding a premature trial of the indictment. A substantial initial showing screens unsupported demands. Limiting the hearing to the particular assets and actual need protects that interest while providing usable review. This conclusion determines why a hearing is required; it does not itself determine how the separate ultimate release burden must be allocated.

**Precedent treatment:** Mathews is applied to this disputed post-seizure setting; Good's independent-process principle is applied without extending its real-property pre-seizure rule to all assets.

#### III. Farmer bears the confined release burden at the hearing

**Holding and operative rule:** At the limited hearing Farmer must establish by a preponderance of the evidence that the particular assets needed for counsel are untainted and were seized without probable cause supporting continued forfeiture custody, and that he actually needs those same funds to retain counsel; the government may contest his other resources and present probable cause of taint. If he establishes that showing, the court must permit access to the legitimate funds required for counsel unless an independently established lawful basis prevents release; if he does not, this counsel-access claim supplies no release.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's opinion of the Court has eight direct joins: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela opposes the allocation of the taint burden to Farmer and does not join this part. His agreement that need must be proved by a preponderance does not become a join in the complete allocation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court adopts the Fourth Circuit's confined standard for the relief Farmer requests. The substantial initial showing opens the hearing; it is not the final release showing. Farmer seeks access to particular assets for a particular defense need and must establish both their legitimacy and that need by the stated standard. The government may rebut his financial showing and offer probable cause connecting the assets to forfeiture. The warrant is evidence of the initial basis, not a conclusive answer at the adversary hearing.

The competing allocation would require the government to justify continued restraint once taint is materially contested, leaving only need to Farmer. The Court instead retains the movant's burden for this limited counsel-access claim. A substantial threshold and a usable hearing prevent the owner from having to prove the entire case before obtaining process; they do not require shifting the ultimate burden for this requested release. The inquiry remains about the disputed assets and resources, not proof of criminal guilt or final adjudication of the entire forfeiture.

Success requires access to the legitimate amount actually needed unless a separate lawful basis for custody is established. Failure defeats release on this counsel claim. The ultimate burden in any forfeiture proceeding is unchanged. Living expenses, interest on seized funds and final forfeiture merits are outside the reviewed counsel question. Those limits do not diminish the definite hearing entitlement or the conditional relief the governing standard requires.

**Precedent treatment:** Monsanto and Caplin & Drysdale remain controlling for genuinely tainted property; neither had decided this hearing allocation. The Fourth Circuit's release standard is adopted here, not described as an already established Supreme Court test.

- Mathews v. Eldridge, 424 U.S. 319: applied by the eight-Justice Part II coalition to the actual private interest, error risk, value of focused testing and government burden.
- United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, decided December 13, 1993: its independent-process principle is applied; its ordinary prior-hearing rule remains confined to civil real property with its existing exigency qualification, and supplies no universal pre-seizure rule for movable assets.
- United States v. Monsanto, 491 U.S. 600: its reserved adversary-hearing issue is resolved at this supported post-seizure scope; lawful probable-cause restraint of tainted property remains available.
- Caplin & Drysdale, Chartered v. United States, 491 U.S. 617: its rejection of a counsel-fee exemption for tainted forfeitable property remains intact; it does not turn a contested seizure into a final taint adjudication.

A supported, specific claim that continuing civil seizure prevents use of potentially legitimate assets for trial counsel triggers a prompt focused hearing. The initial substantial showing remains distinct from the preponderance showing for release. The defendant bears the confined burden on both legitimacy and actual need, subject to the government's opportunity to contest resources and show probable cause of taint. Successful proof requires access to the legitimate funds needed unless an independently established lawful ground prevents release. This rule does not shift the ultimate forfeiture burden or convert every seizure into a counsel violation. The hearing entitlement has nine direct votes, the Mathews explanation eight votes excluding Scalia, and the complete release allocation eight votes excluding Stone-Zsela.

### Barnes v. Gorman — 2002-06-17

#### I. The incorporated remedies exclude punitive damages under both statutory channels

**Holding and operative rule:** Punitive damages are unavailable in these private actions under Rehabilitation Act §504 and ADA Title II because their incorporated Title VI remedial scheme authorizes appropriate compensation and equitable correction, but not the additional punitive exposure at issue. The punitive component must be eliminated while the $1,034,817.33 compensatory judgment, unreviewed liability determination and independently available relief remain intact.

**Authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supplying six votes for the statutory rule and remedy. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer agree with reversal solely on a distinct municipal-defendant ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the enacted cross-references carry the same remedial limit into both claims. Section 794a(a)(2) adopts Title VI remedies for §504; §12133 supplies Title II's remedies through §794a. The separate federal-employment route in §794a(a)(1) does not govern. Title II covers public entities without regard to funding, but Congress expressly chose the incorporated remedy. The Court need not pretend every Title II defendant accepted a federal grant to give that choice effect.

Franklin establishes appropriate compensation for intentional discrimination and rejects a categorical funding-notice objection to making good that injury. It leaves punitive relief unresolved. Pennhurst's recipient-notice principle reinforces the distinction between responsibility to repair a known violation and exceptional punishment of indeterminate magnitude. Contractual remedial expectations inform that particular distinction: compensation ordinarily responds to breach, while punishment requires a further basis. The incorporated scheme supplies no sufficient basis for that additional exposure. This reasoning neither converts every statutory civil-rights action into a literal private contract nor makes a contract-law catalogue govern every monetary remedy.

Gorman relies on Franklin's presumption of appropriate relief absent congressional limitation and the traditional punitive response to aggravated rights violations. But appropriateness requires attention to the remedial arrangement Congress selected; acknowledgment of an action does not automatically authorize every sanction. Grave injury and culpability do not supply the missing punitive authorization. Compensation for proved loss remains available.

Kolstad interprets expressly authorized employment punishment; the 1991 employment provisions do not directly prohibit or authorize punishment in these different channels. Searles preserves punishment in an existing §1983 action against a distinct statutory injury restriction. Great-West requires examination of the authorized remedy, without transferring ERISA's equitable-only language here. None establishes this punitive remedy. Because the statutory ground disposes of the award, the Court does not reach the unpreserved Newport alternative. The appellate punitive-review remand is displaced; there is no need to decide culpability or amount for a legally unavailable remedy.

**Precedent treatment:** Franklin's compensatory holding and appropriate-remedy principle are reaffirmed; Pennhurst's notice principle informs exceptional punishment without barring compensation; Kolstad, Searles and Great-West retain their distinct statutory settings. No precedent is overruled.

- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools: appropriate compensation for intentional discrimination remains available; its reserved punitive question is resolved only for the incorporated channels here.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman: notice of the funding obligation informs punitive exposure without eliminating compensatory redress.
- Kolstad v. American Dental Association: expressly authorized employment punishment has distinct eligibility and attribution rules; those rules do not authorize punishment here.
- Searles v. Van Bebber: preserving §1983 punitive relief against the PLRA restriction does not create that remedy in every statutory action.
- Great-West Life & Annuity Insurance Co. v. Knudson: remedy-specific statutory construction is preserved without importing ERISA's equitable-only text.
- Newport v. Fact Concerts: the three-Justice concurrence applies its municipal-punishment principle; the Court does not reach that alternative.

Punitive damages are unavailable under the Title VI remedies incorporated for these §504 and Title II private actions. Appropriate compensation and equitable correction remain governed by their own requirements. Title II's broader substantive coverage does not change its express remedial incorporation. The decision supplies neither a universal contract-only remedial code nor a change to distinct statutory punitive authority, attorney-fee authority or recipient attribution.

### Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of New York, Inc. v. Village of Stratton — 2002-06-17

#### I. This broad advance-identification and permit prerequisite fails the required fit

**Holding and operative rule:** The Village may not apply §§116.01 and 116.03's advance identifying registration and permit prerequisite, or its dependent §116.04 carry/display enforcement, to the reviewed ordinary noncommercial religious and political canvassing and handbill distribution, because the objective burdens on anonymous and spontaneous advocacy lack adequate fit to the asserted fraud, crime and privacy interests even under the Village's less demanding standard. Remand must prevent that enforcement while preserving the unchallenged §116.07 resident-exclusion mechanism, actual owner refusal and ordinary fraud and trespass laws; materially different commercial, fundraising and financing-disclosure applications are outside the question decided.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplying eight votes for the complete rule, explanation and remedy. Thomas agrees with the judgment on his distinct method and does not join the common rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that routine, free issuance does not eliminate compulsory public identification and advance official permission before ordinary advocacy. Talley and McIntyre protect anonymous advocacy; Buckley examines when, to whom and why identity is exposed. Seeing an advocate's face does not reveal her legal name and residential history. The registration requirement also prevents spontaneous speech. These objective burdens exist independently of any speaker's religious or patriotic unwillingness to obey an otherwise lawful rule.

Martin and Schneider preserve direct dissemination to willing listeners while permitting control of actual trespass and fraud. The Village offers real commercial-scam evidence, concern for older residents, limited police resources and investigative benefits. The Court does not require proof of complete uselessness or a prior local crime wave. Those considerations nevertheless fail to justify licensing every peaceful noncommercial advocate, including speakers seeking neither sales nor contributions. A permit does not assure genuine identity or prevent wrongdoing, and the possibility of some investigative assistance does not establish sufficient fit across this broad speech class.

Resident choice directly protects household privacy. Owners may decline contact or use the unchallenged registered-and-posted exclusion procedure. A permit does not make an unwanted visitor welcome, and barring an unlicensed advocate does not implement a willing listener's preference. Fraud and trespass controls target misconduct without this general prerequisite.

Thomas v. Chicago Park District sustains fitted management of competing uses of common public space, not every nondiscretionary permit. No comparable coordination function appears here. Buckley's distinct initiative-administration and financial-reporting questions likewise do not supply that function. City of Ladue reinforces the importance of a distinctive expressive channel; alternative means do not erase its burden.

Assuming content neutrality and the Village's less demanding means-end test, the prerequisite fails. No universal scrutiny tier or prohibition of every license is needed. The Question 2 grant does not call for independent free-exercise, hybrid-rights or vagueness holdings. Relief addresses the reviewed prerequisite as construed below, without enlarging willing-listener access into entry against an owner's objection.

**Precedent treatment:** Talley, McIntyre, Martin and Schneider protect anonymity and direct advocacy with their lawful-enforcement limits; Buckley's exposure inquiry is applied while its different administrative settings remain distinct; Thomas and City of Ladue retain their context-specific functions.

- Talley v. California; McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission: anonymous individual advocacy is protected; actual burden and context matter, without a requirement to prove personal threats.
- Martin v. City of Struthers; Schneider v. State: direct dissemination and willing listeners remain protected alongside valid fraud and trespass controls.
- Buckley v. American Constitutional Law Foundation: the timing and accessibility of compelled identification matter; initiative administration and financing disclosure retain their distinct predicates.
- Thomas v. Chicago Park District: objective, bounded public-space coordination is distinguished from a general license for household advocacy.
- City of Ladue v. Gilleo: the value of a distinctive expressive medium is preserved; signs and canvassing are not treated as identical settings.

The broad advance-registration and permit system cannot govern the reviewed ordinary noncommercial religious and political advocacy. Free, routine issuance reduces some burdens but does not establish adequate fit for compulsory public identification and loss of spontaneous access to willing listeners. The rule preserves resident-controlled exclusion and targeted enforcement, and establishes neither a universal ban on permits nor an independent religious-exercise rule.

### United States Postal Service v. Gregory — 2001-11-13

**Thomas, concurring.** Thomas joins the limited statutory opinion at the scope shown in the table, but would also decide statutory sufficiency because the Postal Service's reliance on Bolling makes that ground part of the presented dispute. Her remand rests on the Board's actual reopening power, Gregory's election of Board review of the removal, the reservation of Bolling's adequacy, and rejection of an absolute prohibition on agency consideration of pending discipline. They therefore support removal of this punitive component, preserve compensation and seek no punitive-culpability or amount-review remand.

Published authors and joins: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK6_PUBLIC_LAW.md#barnes-v-gorman-merits-2002-06-17).

### Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of New York, Inc. v. Village of Stratton — 2002-06-17

Thomas concurs in the judgment. His McIntyre concurrence grounds anonymous advocacy in the historical protection of anonymous pamphleteering; his Buckley concurrence applies strict scrutiny to direct burdens on speech. The advance personal registration and permission required here directly burden that protected activity and are not narrowly fitted to the asserted interests. Actual fraud, trespass and resident-controlled exclusion provide materially more targeted means than licensing all peaceful religious and political advocacy. He also agrees that the ordinance fails the Court's less demanding fit inquiry, but that alternative conclusion does not replace his strict-scrutiny framework or make him a full joiner in the Court's method. His Thomas v. Chicago Park District join preserves genuinely fitted shared-space administration. The result rests on the objective speech burden, not a speaker's subjective religious or patriotic refusal to comply, and creates no independent free-exercise holding or immunity from lawful owner exclusion.
