## 3. Current Law

The opening Holdings and Standards and Tests remain authoritative at their published scope, as affected by the following effective current-term law. Exact controlling propositions and precedent treatment remain in the linked public-law sections and authoritative Records.

### Brown v. City of Oneonta — 2001-11-13

Purposeful investigative selection by race is subject to the common substantial-justification standard. Victim-supplied identifying information may justify fitted measures, but its source and the absence of hostility create no categorical exemption. The rule governs the classification and its justification; individual liability and remedies remain subject to their existing requirements.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#brown-v-city-of-oneonta-merits-2001-11-13).

### Nebraska v. Wyoming — 2001-11-13

The complete modified decree governs the parties' allocation, accounting, storage, groundwater treatment, administration and reserved rights. Its express future-effective and modification provisions control their respective subjects. Existing general enforcement and modification law remains; the settled allegations supply no independent new entitlement.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#nebraska-v-wyoming-decree-2001-11-13).

### TRW Inc. v. Andrews — 2001-11-13

The governing version of §1681p has no general discovery extension. Its precise statutory misrepresentation exception remains available when its conditions are met. The Court's ruling does not decide the first-presented actual-loss theory or create a universal accrual rule for federal statutes.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#trw-inc-v-andrews-merits-2001-11-13).

### United States Postal Service v. Gregory — 2001-11-13

A pending contractual grievance creates no categorical statutory bar to considering earlier discipline in later adverse-action review. Adequate independent review remains essential, and the agency's proof burden is separate from judicial review. Bolling's actual adequacy remains undecided by the Court. A completed reversal must receive the effect required by the applicable agreement and review framework through the available procedural mechanisms.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#united-states-postal-service-v-gregory-merits-2001-11-13).

### Adarand Constructors, Inc. v. Mineta — 2001-11-27

No substantive doctrinal change. The governing racial-classification law remains in force. The lower-court judgment survives without a Supreme Court endorsement of its constitutional rationale.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#adarand-constructors-inc-v-mineta-dig-2001-11-27).

### Chickasaw Nation v. United States — 2001-11-27

Section 2719(d)(1) does not give covered tribal gaming chapter 35's state wagering-tax exemption. Reporting and withholding parity operates within the provision's described subject. Other Code exclusions, the actual tax base and the occupational tax's independent linkage remain governed by their own terms; no general tribal federal-tax immunity is recognized.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#chickasaw-nation-v-united-states-merits-2001-11-27).

### Church of Scientology International v. Behar — 2001-11-27

The First Amendment supplies no automatic subsidiary-meaning immunity for a materially distinct defamatory factual accusation. Context may establish absence of actionable material falsity, and the public-figure burden remains demanding and statement-specific. Protected criticism elsewhere in a publication cannot replace those inquiries. State-law injury, causation and damages rules remain separately operative.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#church-of-scientology-international-v-behar-merits-2001-11-27).

### Correctional Services Corp. v. Malesko — 2001-11-27

A confined federal constitutional damages cause is available against a private corporation exercising federal custody for its own deliberate constitutional misconduct through policy, established practice or authorized decision. Corporate attribution and causation must be proved; ordinary negligence and respondeat superior do not suffice. Meyer's federal-agency bar and existing individual-action rules remain in force.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#correctional-services-corp-v-malesko-merits-2001-11-27).

### J. E. M. Ag Supply, Inc. v. Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc. — 2001-12-10

Plants meeting the utility statute's requirements may receive utility patents alongside the plant-specific regimes. Each right carries its own requirements and exceptions. The certificate amendments and Asgrow's surviving former-law operation retain their actual reach; the decision supplies no generalized saved-seed or research exemption from utility-patent rights.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#j-e-m-ag-supply-inc-v-pioneer-hi-bred-international-inc-merits-2001-12-10).

### Schrader v. Blackwell — 2001-12-10

The official party designation may remain linked to Ohio's recognition system in these circumstances. The holding applies burden-and-fit review to a designation restriction; it supplies no categorical approval of every recognition threshold or prohibition of truthful affiliation.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#schrader-v-blackwell-merits-2001-12-10).

### United States v. Knights — 2001-12-10

The Fourth Amendment permits the defined probation-condition and individualized-suspicion combination despite ordinary investigative purpose. Existing scope, execution and exclusion rules retain their separate operation; the decision creates no general suspicionless-search or consent-waiver rule.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#united-states-v-knights-merits-2001-12-10).

### Dusenbery v. United States — 2002-01-08

A known prisoner must receive notice through a reasonably calculated custodial route. The proved certified-mail and internal-distribution process satisfies that standard without proof of actual receipt. The holding is procedure-based and leaves genuinely unreliable or known-failure situations to their own records.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#dusenbery-v-united-states-merits-2002-01-08).

### Great-West Life & Annuity Insurance Co. v. Knudson — 2002-01-08

Section 502(a)(3) does not authorize this demand for personal payment merely from the reimbursement clause. Genuine property-based equity remains available when its defendant, property and defense requirements are met. A different claim against another holder requires its own proper pleading, jurisdiction, process and equitable basis; the present judgment decides no such claim.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#great-west-life-annuity-insurance-co-v-knudson-merits-2002-01-08).

### Searles v. Van Bebber — 2002-01-08

Original section 1997e(e) bars the named distress compensation absent physical injury while preserving nominal recognition and punitive relief under their own predicates. Established liability and punitive culpability survive a separate damages-calculation defect. The lawful punitive amount and consequential fee decision remain ordinary remedial work under those settled rules.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#searles-v-van-bebber-merits-2002-01-08).

### Toyota Motor Manufacturing, Kentucky, Inc. v. Williams — 2002-01-08

The original ADA now requires the stated severe restriction in central daily-life manual tasks and permanent or long-term impact, assessed individually under the existing underlying-impairment mitigation rule. Occupational categories alone do not establish disability; workplace and nonwork functional evidence remain relevant. The lower courts apply those settled rules to the reviewed partial judgment, with other claims retaining their existing procedural status.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#toyota-motor-manufacturing-kentucky-inc-v-williams-merits-2002-01-08).

### Chao v. Mallard Bay Drilling, Inc. — 2002-01-09

OSHA retains authority over these working conditions because Coast Guard potential authority was not exercised through pertinent standards or comprehensive regulation. Work on a barge in Louisiana falls within §4(a), subject independently to any displacement actually established under §4(b)(1). Actual overlap, rather than vessel status alone, governs other settings.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#chao-v-mallard-bay-drilling-inc-merits-2002-01-09).

### Kelly v. South Carolina — 2002-01-09

Effective January 9, 2002, the combined future-danger presentation triggers Simmons despite its concurrent character and prison-adaptability uses, and the actual defense argument and ordinary-meaning charge are constitutionally inadequate. The existing contextual trigger, effective alternative means of explanation and State-burden direct-review harmlessness standard remain in force. The danger and adequacy determinations bind the remand; the state court must perform the distinct error-effect inquiry. No controlling rationale requires fixed words or treats every omission as structural error.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK2_PUBLIC_LAW.md#kelly-v-south-carolina-merits-2002-01-09).

### Riley v. St. Luke's Episcopal Hospital — 2002-01-09

The FCA's bounded assigned-claim mechanism may constitutionally continue after initial governmental declination. Its private claimant does not occupy a continuing federal office, and the actual retained government powers, with their distinct statutory conditions, are sufficient for the arrangement presented. These Article II conclusions are separate from assigned-injury standing and statutory defendant coverage. They authorize no general private executive office and do not decide an actual judicial override of a supported government request absent from this record.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK2_PUBLIC_LAW.md#riley-v-st-lukes-episcopal-hospital-merits-2002-01-09).

### EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. — 2002-01-15

The FAA supplies no bar for this excluded employment agreement. Independently, an employee's private forum promise does not remove Congress's individual-remedy categories from the EEOC's own enforcement action, even if the promise binds the employee under an independent source. Liability and every actual award remain governed by their statutory predicates, mitigation rules and nonduplication limits. Concrete adjudication, release, settlement and satisfaction questions await a record establishing them.

Exact holdings, proposition-level authority, precedent treatment, limits and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK2_PUBLIC_LAW.md#eeoc-v-waffle-house-inc-merits-2002-01-15).

### In re Cybernetic Services, Inc. — 2002-01-15

A nonownership patent security interest otherwise perfected under the governing California law needs no additional section 261 filing to prevail against this section 544(a)(1) trustee. Transaction coverage and protected-transferee status remain independent federal inquiries. ### TRW Inc. v. Andrews — 2001-11-13

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** Stone-Zsela joins the Court's statutory ruling and judgment. Properly preserved questions remain subject to ordinary adjudication. The newly presented theory receives no affirmative permission to reopen merely because the Court leaves it undecided.

Traditional fraud/concealment protection and the latent-injury setting remain separate rules. They object that leaving the general background uncertain invites uncertainty under other statutes. Their agreement with reversal and remand does not establish that every disclosure completes every FCRA claim or decide the newly raised actual-loss theory.

### United States Postal Service v. Gregory — 2001-11-13

**Thomas, concurring.** Thomas joins the limited statutory opinion at the scope shown in the table, but would also decide statutory sufficiency because the Postal Service's reliance on Bolling makes that ground part of the presented dispute. Under Bolling, full fresh review applies if written notice, a recorded action, or an opportunity to challenge above the original imposing authority was absent. If all three exist, the employee may show clear error on the earlier record. Thomas considers that framework consistent with §7503's protections for minor discipline and its absence of a direct Board appeal. The statute does not require retrying the facts of every earlier action merely because it bears on a later penalty. The agency must establish the earlier action, while the appealed charge and penalty retain their own proof requirements. The contractual and Board routes protect different rights; one route's conclusion is not automatically a prerequisite for the other. He accepts remand concerning the overturned warning without commanding reopening. His affirmative statutory view neither becomes the Court's holding nor resolves every constitutional application.

**Ginsburg, concurring in the judgment.** Ginsburg questions whether restricted clear-error review can satisfy the statutory preponderance requirement when the earlier merits remain unadjudicated. The opportunity to contest a recorded action does not itself prove adequate review. She therefore withholds assent to the Court's independent-review rationale, without holding Bolling invalid or requiring fresh review of every final disciplinary action. Her remand rests on the Board's actual reopening power, Gregory's election of Board review of the removal, the reservation of Bolling's adequacy, and rejection of an absolute prohibition on agency consideration of pending discipline. Reopening provides a route to request consideration of the changed disciplinary record, not guaranteed relief. A refusal to address a material invalidated predicate could present an arbitrary-and-capricious question. The removal election does not abandon earlier grievances, establish personal control over union arbitration, or substitute for adequate Board process. Her distinction between final discipline and an action still being challenged remains a noncontrolling qualification.

These are published noncontrolling positions, with the scope and joins shown in the [public opinion account](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK1_PUBLIC_LAW.md#united-states-postal-service-v-gregory-merits-2001-11-13).

### Kelly v. South Carolina — 2002-01-09

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by no other Justice.** He joins Part I in full. **Justice Scalia, dissenting, joined by no other Justice.** He would affirm against this federal claim because the Constitution does not supply this further parole-information obligation. Gardner's unavailable adverse material and Skipper's excluded rebuttal evidence do not establish a general duty to explain parole law. **Justice Kennedy, dissenting, joined by no other Justice.** He accepts Simmons and Shafer's contextual rule but finds the trigger absent here. **Justice Thomas, dissenting, joined by no other Justice.** Assuming the sentencing alternatives and future-danger predicate, he finds the actual information adequate. If cable-modem access is cable service, subsection (d)'s formula controls; if it is telecommunications service, subsection (e)'s formula controls. The service supplied by an Internet service provider and the transmission carrying customers to that provider are distinct classification questions. The Commission's treatment of one does not necessarily resolve the other. Permian Basin's demand for a reasoned rate method supports returning that choice to the agency. Probable ultimate jurisdiction and policy favoring broadband deployment cannot supply the missing statutory explanation. This position rejects categorical noncoverage but withholds approval of the existing unexplained agency determination.

### Kansas v. Crane — 2002-01-22

Scalia, joined by Thomas, agrees that due process does not require complete inability to control conduct. An additional independent control finding is not constitutionally required. The new serious-difficulty formulation, in his view, gives uncertain guidance and turns descriptions of an individual's condition into a universal extra element. This position neither treats a label or danger alone as sufficient nor allows a high burden to create a missing substantive predicate. It preserves Foucha's ended-predicate rule and Hendricks and Seling's separate review of actual punitive operation. He would reverse the complete-incapacity rule and remand under the statute's genuine condition-and-causal-danger requirements.

### Lee v. Kemna — 2002-01-22

Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, would affirm because Rule 24.10 retained a substantive function apart from the written-motion form. Earlier alibi descriptions and earlier attendance did not necessarily establish what the witnesses would then say, whether they could be obtained within a reasonable time, or the current diligence and other facts needed to evaluate the delay. A focused contemporaneous presentation also creates a reviewable record. It does not hold that every formally regular rule is adequate in every application, and relies on no finding about why the witnesses left or whether their alibi was true.

Stevens concurs in Parts II and III and dissents from Part I's uniform direction. ### Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co. — 2002-02-19

**Stevens, joined by O'Connor and Breyer, dissenting.** The dissent would reverse and remand for application of an inclusive succession rule. Congress allocated existing health-benefit obligations across continuing business relationships. Reading the successor sentence in that integrated structure avoids the anomaly of reaching a related person's successor while exempting the closer direct successor that continued the coal business and assumed its agreement. The contextual treatment of successors in 1 U.S.C. sections 1 and 5, together with the labor-law succession principles illustrated by Wiley, Golden State Bottling and Fall River Dyeing, supports that reading; those authorities do not establish liability for every solvent purchaser.

The dissent recognizes that a differentiated list and a separate prospective transfer provision could reflect compromise. Its answer is that the entire succession structure supplies the context for that list, and the Court's separation defeats the allocation Congress made. Legislative explanations corroborate that construction rather than replacing the text. The dissent does not treat a bargaining discount as a proved fact, impose unrestricted federal common-law liability, or decide the constitutional question. Preserved successor-status and other application issues remain for the proper tribunal on remand.

These published positions are noncontrolling at the stated scope. [Public opinion account](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK2_PUBLIC_LAW.md#barnhart-v-sigmon-coal-co-merits-2002-02-19).

### Romeu v. Cohen — 2002-02-19

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The four would affirm both equality dismissals. Residence ordinarily identifies membership in a State's electorate. Congress and New York may accommodate former residents outside the domestic political system without extending the accommodation to people who establish membership in another domestic polity. Burdick, Timmons and Schrader permit meaningful electoral categories and require identification of the relevant electorate before the severity of a lost opportunity determines scrutiny. Saenz concerns equal citizenship in the new State, not a continuing former-state vote.

The dissent acknowledges that Puerto Rico's local elections and Resident Commissioner do not replace presidential participation. That absence makes the exclusion consequential but does not establish continuing membership in New York's electorate. Domestic political membership and incremental overseas accommodation provide a rational basis for the distinction, without making territorial power unreviewable or every residence rule valid. UOCAVA is a federal floor, so New York could extend eligibility; that permission does not establish a constitutional duty to do so. The proposed decree's precision does not cure the majority's antecedent membership error. The four join the prospective-review and federal-floor conclusions and the rejection of independent permanent-membership, travel, Article IV and general due-process entitlements.

Stone-Zsela joins the equality rules and prospective relief but does not reach the independent entitlement theories rejected by the eight Associates. That join limit supplies no contrary entitlement rule.

These published positions are noncontrolling at the stated scope. [Public opinion account](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK2_PUBLIC_LAW.md#romeu-v-cohen-merits-2002-02-19).

### Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer — 2002-02-20

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor and Scalia, dissenting.** The dissent would affirm the resources-first requirement and return the matter for a calculation using supported income and resource figures. Section (e)(2)(C) directs an increased resource allowance when the community spouse's protected income is inadequate. Predicting income from resources legally attributable to that spouse differs from predicting another person's optional payment. Section (d)(1)(B)'s posteligibility transfer does not add a prerequisite that the spouse depend on that payment before receiving the resource protection; denying eligibility can prevent the projected arrangement from beginning.

The dissent recognizes that (b)(1) is not a reciprocal nonattribution rule and that (b)(2) expressly addresses posteligibility attribution. Its point is the scheme's separation of ownership, resource protection and voluntary support, not an invented symmetric prohibition. The Court's answer that every method forecasts future circumstances fails to explain why Congress's resource protection may depend first on another person's voluntary transfer. A future payment might not occur or might diminish; those are objections to the legal method, not findings about Irene's later conduct. Administrative flexibility, conservation of funds and expertise cannot supply the missing compulsory transfer or displace the specific statutory command. The agency's changed view is not invalid merely because it changed, but its reasoning must answer this objection. The dissent asserts no constitutional right to preserve assets and would award no unsupported dollar amount.

These published positions are noncontrolling at the stated scope. [Public opinion account](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK2_PUBLIC_LAW.md#wisconsin-department-of-health-and-family-services-v-blumer-merits-2002-02-20).
