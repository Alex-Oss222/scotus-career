## 39. United States v. Vonn

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-03-04, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the appellate judgment and remand for application of Rule 52(b) to the relevant full record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The court of appeals must assess each required plain-error element, including whether the omission affected substantial rights and whether correction is justified. Do not conclusively reinstate the convictions or order new pleas without that application.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Unpreserved plea error and the relevant record

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Rule 11 objection was first presented on appeal; the alternative withdrawal motion did not preserve this distinct omission. Reach the applicable review rule and evidentiary scope.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an omitted trial-counsel warning receives ordinary forfeiture review and whether prior judicial advice is relevant to the defendant’s substantial-rights showing.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Olano to Rule 11; reject the proposed special exception. Clarify the permitted record without changing the judge’s plea-taking obligation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A defendant who forfeits a Rule 11 objection must satisfy Rule 52(b). The reviewing court may consider relevant information throughout the existing record when determining the omission’s effect, including prior rights advisements. Rule 11(h) does not silently displace Rule 52(b). The omission remains an error; evidence of actual understanding bears on its consequences.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs direct review of forfeited Rule 11 errors. It does not convert intentional waiver into reviewable forfeiture, decide a collateral claim, or establish that every type of constitutional plea defect has the same consequences.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The general rule and the plea rule can operate together. A special harmlessness instruction protects against automatic reversal but says nothing sufficient to exempt silent litigants from the otherwise applicable allocation of burdens. Full-record review addresses actual effect more accurately than a transcript boundary that ignores known relevant advice. Relevance is not conclusiveness: timing, content, and demonstrated comprehension still matter. The procedural rules’ text and Olano’s allocation of burdens are decisive; record-wide consideration is a functional consequence of testing actual effect under that rule. Stone reaffirms the difference between harmless error preserved below and plain error forfeited below, rather than converting his position on ordinary constitutional harmlessness into an exception for Rule 11 omissions. The court must determine error, plainness, effect on substantial rights, and the separate basis for discretionary correction. Prior advice is relevant evidence of understanding, not a conclusive presumption, and the judge’s plea colloquy duty remains mandatory.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The record-specific substantial-rights and discretionary findings belong on remand because the appellate court applied a different framework; the trial-court and appellate functions remain distinct.

## 40. Delaney v. DeTella +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-03-04, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of qualified immunity on the assumed favorable facts, with factual sufficiency disputes outside the interlocutory review.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the case for proof of each defendant’s knowledge, response, causation, and compensable injury. Do not award damages or establish that every defendant participated merely because the common policy existed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Conditions of confinement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The merits question can be resolved as a legal sufficiency issue on the favorable facts; those facts must not be converted into findings.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether knowing and unjustified maintenance of this prolonged near-total exercise deprivation is actionable punishment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Farmer to the particular conditions, without creating strict liability or a fixed nationwide exercise entitlement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A prolonged denial of meaningful exercise to a prisoner confined in a space inadequate for it violates the Eighth Amendment when it creates a substantial health risk that the responsible official actually appreciates and unreasonably disregards. The assumed six-month deprivation and complaints suffice to require adjudication under that rule; a lockdown label does not itself supply an adequate response.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule reaches responsible officials with actual knowledge and an unreasonable response. Short restrictions, genuine unavoidable security necessities, and reasonable alternative exercise or protective measures require their own facts.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Duration and confinement make this more than inconvenience. Farmer permits the inference that repeated complaints and the obvious conditions disclosed a serious risk, while allowing defendants to contest knowledge and responsibility at trial. The constitutional inquiry evaluates the actual response to the known risk, not compliance with a state regulation alone. Farmer’s two requirements are decisive: a serious health risk and knowing unreasonable disregard, proved through direct or circumstantial evidence. Lockdown necessity is relevant to the response; it is not a categorical exception authorizing six months of complete deprivation. Stone applies the existing culpability standard rather than imposing strict liability, weekly-hour minima, or a universally least-restrictive prison regimen. The assumed facts permit a legal sufficiency determination; disputed knowledge and each official’s responsibility remain for the factfinder.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: Clearly established law and personal immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The officials ask for final protection from damages on the assumed facts, so conduct-date clarity must be resolved separately.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether reasonable officials in 1996 had fair notice of the prohibition in the circumstances assumed.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing objective immunity framework retained in Saucier, using Farmer and pertinent law predating the spring 1996 conduct. No later decision supplies retrospective notice.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On facts establishing knowledge of substantial health danger from a six-month denial of meaningful exercise without a reasonable security response, the existing law gave sufficient notice. Qualified immunity therefore does not require summary judgment for these officials on that assumed record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion is limited by the facts necessary to establish notice and responsibility. It does not deny immunity for materially different conditions or for an official lacking the knowledge or authority required for liability.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The right must be described concretely, but officials need not receive a prior judgment involving the identical cell dimensions and number of days. The preconduct cases and Farmer identify both the protected need and the culpable disregard. Facts disproving those predicates may defeat liability independently of immunity. French and Davenport gave particular notice before the challenged lockdown, including Davenport’s treatment of this segregation unit; an identical cell-and-duration case is unnecessary. Stone reaffirms the existing notice framework for this disposition because it resolves the claim without reopening the broader immunity doctrine. His proposed authoritative-reliance replacement has not become law through Saucier and is unnecessary to deny immunity on these assumed facts. The holding guides analogous complete deprivations while preserving genuinely different security needs and available exercise alternatives.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual disputed knowledge and role of each officer require fact-finding and are reserved on that ground.

## 41. Ruiz v. United States (Ruiz v. Johnson) +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the appellate judgment requiring application of the PLRA and remand for adequate findings; reject automatic wholesale termination.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court must identify each live federal violation and connect each retained obligation to need, narrowness and intrusiveness, resolving the preserved cross-appeal issues. Retain or tailor relief satisfying §3626(b)(3), and terminate obligations that do not. Any interim protection must rest on applicable statutory or equitable authority and supported current danger, not a suspension of the PLRA by fiat.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: New prospective standards do not erase independent adjudication

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Both stated grounds maintained the injunction and are reviewable. The constitutional objections to applying the termination law are necessary to the disposition.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Are the termination provisions constitutionally inapplicable or barred by the State’s earlier consent?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Rouse, Benjamin, Gavin and Hadix on conditional prospective termination; preserve Plyler’s original-text scope and distinguish Miller’s automatic-stay question.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Congress may require ongoing prison relief to satisfy current statutory criteria without reopening accrued damages or commanding a judicial finding of lawful conditions. Consent to the earlier decree does not estop invocation of a later governing statute merely because the State previously promised compliance. The asserted general finality and due-process objections fail.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This governs prospective prison obligations and leaves the court responsible for actual federal rights, current facts and statutory exceptions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Controlling precedent, the statute’s structure and the prospective character of the remedy decide this issue. Rouse and Hadix already reject an irrevocable entitlement to every prospective consent-decree term. Their rule, rather than an extension of Miller’s stay holding, governs termination. A consent decree remains enforceable while operative, but consent does not freeze the governing prospective law. Texas’s changed legal argument alone establishes neither misleading conduct sufficient for estoppel nor compliance with federal rights. Benjamin requires a genuine opportunity for necessary current proof; Gavin and Hadix require the statutory inquiry actually to occur. They do not presume an evidentiary refusal or command a hearing in every case. The State’s motion is not evidence that prisoners are safe. A concrete denial of time or ability to present the statutory case requires its own review; it is not established merely by the enacted criteria.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Accrued damages and a proved inability to obtain meaningful adjudication are not established by this record.

### Issue 2: Retention requires findings tied to actual violations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The alternative findings are part of the refusal to dissolve, but the appellate court cannot supply missing trial-level connections.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do generalized findings of unconstitutional prison conditions sustain all remaining terms of the decree?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply §3626(b)(3) and the existing Eighth Amendment framework without creating a per se solitary-confinement rule.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The movant must first establish the applicable termination ground; under §3626(b)(2), original substantive findings may suffice without ritual statutory language. Where amended §3626(b)(3) governs retention, each retained obligation, or coherently explained group, requires written, record-based findings of a current and ongoing federal-right violation, necessity, narrowness and least intrusive effective correction. Generalized findings do not establish those connections for every decree term. The incomplete findings require remand rather than automatic extinction of all protection.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The inquiry covers force, safety, segregation and preserved medical-care disputes under their respective legal elements. An institution-wide measure can remain necessary if the proved violation and practical remedy are institution-wide.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory requirements are mandatory; contextual factual judgment determines whether they are met. That combination gives the enacted conditions decisive weight and uses functional assessment to identify an effective remedy. Extreme isolation may establish grave injury on adequate evidence, especially for vulnerable prisoners, but the label alone proves neither all liability elements nor the need for every old term. Conversely, calling a remedy intrusive cannot justify leaving an ongoing violation uncorrected where less intrusive measures are ineffective. Stone decides the legal standard completely and leaves only its record-dependent application below.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A categorical rule governing all segregation, individual damages and findings on undeveloped conditions are outside this review.

## 42. Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-03-19, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment rejecting the automatic additional-leave entitlement.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny relief on the asserted categorical theory. Preserve the legal possibility of relief for a demonstrated notice-related interference, but do not invent or reopen a factually unsupported alternative claim in this record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Regulatory designation and statutory relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The automatic regulation is necessary to the claim as presented. Its validity can be resolved on statutory grounds without deciding whether every form of individualized notice is required or constitutional.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether failure to designate otherwise qualifying leave necessarily entitles the employee to twelve more weeks regardless of impairment or harm.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply delegated-power and statutory-remedy limits. Do not propose eliminating deference or weakening the underlying leave right.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Secretary may not make failure to designate leave an automatic entitlement to an additional twelve weeks when the employee has received the qualifying leave and the omission caused no demonstrated impairment of statutory rights. The challenged categorical consequence conflicts with the statute’s defined entitlement and remedial conditions. Notice violations remain actionable where the applicable law and record establish interference and a basis for relief.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule addresses this mandatory additional entitlement, not every notice regulation, equitable remedy, or employer practice. An employee who would have structured leave differently and lost protected opportunities presents a different application requiring proof.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Notice can determine whether an employee can use the right effectively. That supports an enforceable implementation measure, but it does not erase the distinction between failing to provide information and causing the loss of protected leave. The categorical regulation directs relief even when the omission could not change the employee’s position. Administrative simplicity cannot supply the missing statutory authorization. The FMLA’s entitlement and remedial conditions carry decisive weight. Mead distinguishes delegated legal force from mere expertise, but even a formal regulation cannot contradict the statutory scheme. Stone distinguishes an enforceable designation duty from the automatic consequence challenged here: useful notice does not make twelve additional weeks legally necessary when no protected choice or benefit was lost. This rejects a categorical remedial rule, not ordinary agency discretion to implement notice or equitable relief for a demonstrated interference. No new constitutional balancing test is needed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Notice duties and remedies other than the challenged automatic additional-leave consequence fall outside the reviewed regulatory provision; their general validity and exact content are not decided.

## 43. Edelman v. Lynchburg College

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-03-19, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment declaring the relation-back regulation invalid and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow a timely charge to receive a later verification that relates back. Require the lower court to decide whether the original letter was a charge and to address other preserved defenses; award no discrimination relief at this stage.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Verification and timely initiation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The appellate rule supplies a dispositive barrier. Resolve its validity while leaving the independently disputed character of the letter to the proper tribunal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress required the oath itself within the filing period or permitted a later cure of a timely charge.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the statute’s distinct requirements and accept the procedural regulation as lawful. This result rests on statutory construction without requiring a new general deference rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A charge timely submitted under Title VII may be verified after the filing period through the EEOC’s relation-back procedure. The oath remains mandatory, but the statute does not make it a condition that must be fulfilled at the first timely submission. The later verification can cure that defect without converting a document that otherwise is not a charge into one.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects timely initiation followed by actual verification. It does not excuse the filing deadline, eliminate verification, establish the sufficiency of every intake document, or compel an employer to answer an unverified merits accusation contrary to governing protections.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Prompt initiation protects against staleness; verification provides a meaningful assurance that the accusation is adopted under oath. Those functions can coexist without an unstated simultaneity rule. The regulation preserves both rather than adding substantive liability. The district court’s separate concern about the letter cannot be resolved simply by declaring that curable defects never matter. The separate statutory provisions for initiation and verification carry decisive weight. Becker supplies a useful but bounded analogy: an expressly permitted cure completes an existing timely filing instead of creating a late one. It does not itself authorize every cure, so the Title VII text and the EEOC’s procedural authority still do the legal work here. Stone sustains the relation-back regulation on that independent reading without resolving every degree of administrative deference. Whether the original letter otherwise constituted a charge is a distinct preserved question the limited review of verification did not decide.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether this letter qualifies as a charge needs the preserved lower-court analysis and is reserved on that record-dependent ground.
