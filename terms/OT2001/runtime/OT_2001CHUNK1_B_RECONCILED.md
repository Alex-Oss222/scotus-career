## 9. United States v. Knights - reconciled

### Result, proposition and remedy

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer vote to reverse the purpose-based suppression judgment and remand. All eight join this precise rule: the warrantless residence search of this probationer is reasonable because he was subject to the known express search condition, the officer had individualized reasonable suspicion of criminal activity during probation, and the search fell within the condition's lawful scope. The combination sufficiently reduces the protected privacy interest and supplies a justification for dispensing with probable cause and a warrant. An ordinary criminal-investigation purpose does not independently defeat that justification. The opinion does not rest on complete consent or forfeiture of all Fourth Amendment protection.

Griffin supports the lawful supervisory relationship and diminished privacy, while leaving other constitutional justifications possible. Whren supports objective assessment after authority for the particular intrusion has been established; it does not itself supply residential search authority. Edmond and Ferguson preserve their purpose inquiry in the actual suspicionless and special-needs settings. Ornelas governs review of ultimate suspicion under its factual/legal allocation. Scott addresses exclusion after an assumed unconstitutional supervisory search, so its different remedy rule remains fully operative: there is no established violation to which that rule must attach here.

The strongest contrary contention is that Griffin permits only a special-needs supervisory search and that a residence otherwise requires probable cause and a warrant. It fails because Griffin did not make its permitted rationale exclusive and because the actual condition and individualized suspicion materially change this search's justification. The Court's rule cannot be reduced to reasonable suspicion alone or to the search condition alone. Whether a condition permits a suspicionless search or establishes full consent is genuinely outside the necessary question, because individualized suspicion supplies the sufficient ground.

### Final judgment and common propositions

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer all reverse Williams's partial summary judgment and remand. All eight directly join the following propositions, including their qualifications:

1. Under the original ADA, substantial limitation in performing manual tasks requires an impairment that prevents or severely restricts the individual from performing tasks central to most people's daily lives. The impairment's impact must be permanent or long term. The assessment remains individual: diagnosis alone is insufficient, and the nature, severity and duration of this person's underlying limitation matter. The rule demands a considerable functional limitation, not complete inability or loss of virtually every manual ability; it sets no numerical duration threshold.
2. A working-specific class-of-jobs formula cannot establish substantial limitation in the distinct major life activity of performing manual tasks. Workplace evidence remains relevant insofar as it establishes limitation of the general manual functions the activity encompasses. Inability to perform repetitive overhead work or specialized assembly tasks for prolonged periods is not, simply because those tasks form an occupational class, sufficient to establish this disability. Ordinary household and self-care evidence is also relevant and cannot be disregarded. Ability to perform some such tasks is not a categorical defense; their actual extent and the other relevant manual limitations must be considered under the stated demanding standard.
3. Sutton and Murphy supply the controlling mitigation rule: assess the individual's underlying substantial limitation without treating beneficial correction or medication as eliminating it. Apply the manual-task threshold to that legally defined condition. Individual proof remains necessary. Coverage is distinct from qualification, accommodation and safety. The Court does not revive historical Sutton's actual-treated-state construction or historical Kirkingburg's compensation-for-impairment method.
4. Williams's appellate partial judgment cannot stand because the occupational-task shortcut does not establish the required substantial limitation as a matter of law, and the appellate treatment disregarded relevant nonwork function. The lower courts must reassess that judgment under the corrected manual-task rule, the controlling mitigation baseline and ordinary summary-judgment standards. The scope of review independently prevents reinstatement of Toyota's separate summary judgment. Neither party receives a final Supreme Court disability determination.

The daily-life formulation is specific to the manual-task activity being asserted. It does not amend Bragdon by restricting every possible major life activity to something performed each day or by excluding reproduction. Likewise, permanent or long-term impact describes the functional impact required here, not a demand that a medical diagnosis itself be incurable. A claimant need not stop treatment to establish the underlying limitation. The original statute and contemporaneous rules govern; later amendments do not enter.

### Why the provisional broader formulation changes

The original and scope-refreshed commitments correctly rejected an automatic job-class finding, diagnosis-only coverage, complete incapacity and a categorical ban on workplace evidence. Those points survive. They did not, however, supply a concrete Justice-specific reason to replace the historical severe-restriction, central-daily-task and permanent-or-long-term criteria with a general inquiry into any important manual function and duration as only an unconstrained consideration.

The changed mitigation baseline matters to what functional condition is assessed. It removes reliance on the historical Sutton proposition that successful correction defeats actual-disability coverage. It also prevents reliance on that decision's population estimate as a reason to reconstruct the rejected mitigation rule. But the meaning of substantial limitation in a particular major life activity is a separate statutory question. Nothing in simulated Sutton or Murphy holds that a minor or short-lived limitation is substantial, that any important work task defines the manual-task activity, or that an enduring-impact requirement is forbidden. Bragdon's individual substantiality holding rejects complete inability while preserving a meaningful threshold. Simulated Kirkingburg supplies no disability-method holding that can be cited for this purpose; the manual-task rule must rest on the original statute and the relevant contemporaneous regulatory guidance, not a nonexistent simulated proposition.

The historical opinion supplies independent grounds that remain available: substantial denotes a considerable limitation; major distinguishes general manual abilities central to ordinary life from isolated specialized tasks; the Act's common disability definition is not limited to employment; and the contemporaneous duration guidance addresses expected duration and permanent or long-term impact. The historical comparator shows all eight Associates accepting those grounds, including the two historical Sutton dissenters. None of the inherited public positions supplies a contrary manual-task or duration holding. Accordingly, a different mitigation precedent alone does not justify the unanimous broader departure in the provisional handoff. Reconciliation corrects that unsupported extension while preserving the genuinely changed governing mitigation rule.

The formal judgment also changes from provisional vacatur to reversal of the employee's partial judgment, followed by remand. The judgment is erroneous under the rule applied by all eight. No changed record, remedial entitlement or review scope requires departure from the historical reversal form. This is not reinstatement of the employer's judgment and not a determination that Williams can never satisfy the Act. ## 14. Great-West Life & Annuity Insurance Co. v. Knudson - reconciled

### Actual disagreement and propositions

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas affirm the judgment rejecting this federal remedial demand. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate and remand the remedial rejection so that the asserted restitution claim is considered without the rejected categorical bar. All eight reach the question, preserve the distinction between the actual defendants and other holders, and retain ordinary proof and defenses. No final reimbursement entitlement or amount is found.

The affirming four jointly adopt these propositions:

1. Section 502(a)(3) requires an appropriate traditionally equitable remedy. A demand for personal payment of a past-due contractual debt does not become equitable merely because the complaint requests an injunction, specific performance or restitution. An injunction remains subject to equity's remedial limits. The ordinary rule does not deny the distinct exceptional uses of specific performance to prevent otherwise inadequately remediable future losses; none supplies this demand for an accrued sum.
2. Restitution includes legal and equitable forms. A constructive trust or equitable lien ordinarily restores specifically identifiable property or proceeds to which the claimant asserts an equitable entitlement from the defendant who holds them; ordinary liability for a benefit conferred is different. This case concerns the latter demand because the settlement property was paid to the separate trust and attorney, rather than held by the named beneficiaries in the manner the proposed property remedy requires. This proposition is not a universal tracing formula for every equitable remedy. An accounting for profits has distinct predicates and is not this claim.
3. The trust-beneficiary analogy does not itself supply personal collection from other assets. A traditional charge against a beneficiary's interest in a trust is distinct from the personal money judgment sought here. Mertens prevents treating the whole former remedial jurisdiction of trust courts as statutory authorization.
4. The specific federal claim therefore fails. The alternative construction of the actual plan terms need not be decided to sustain this judgment. A different claim against another property holder requires its own preserved pleading, jurisdiction, process and equitable predicates; none is adjudicated here.

The remanding four jointly adopt a different sufficient ground: the asserted recovery measured by the beneficiary's gain, for restoration of benefits out of a third-party recovery, should be treated as the traditionally recognized restitution category for purposes of allowing this claim to proceed. Mertens's requirement that the category be typically available in equity does not mean that the category must have been exclusive to equity, and it does not make lack of direct possession dispositive of every restorative theory. This clears the categorical statutory-remedy objection; it does not establish actual enrichment, ultimate plan entitlement, amount or authority over a nonparty's property. The lower court must then address the plan-construction ground and necessary remedial predicates. The majority's distinction is not answered merely by calling all money equitable; the remanding group relies on the alleged gain-restoration character, unlike Mertens's loss compensation.

The government's trust-beneficiary/advance argument supports that restorative characterization but cannot itself create a new property fact or direct a remedy against a person absent from the case. The remanding group need not find that the Knudsons directly possess the settlement fund; doing so would contradict the supplied record. Their disagreement is the statutory significance of that fact, not the fact's existence.

## 15. Searles v. Van Bebber - reconciled

### Judgment components and common rule

The fee award receives the consequential reconsideration required by the revised damages judgment; no independent fee doctrine is adopted.

All eight directly join each of these propositions:

1. Original section 1997e(e) bars recovery for mental or emotional injury suffered in custody without the required prior physical-injury showing. The constitutional source of the violated right does not exempt the actual distress compensation awarded here. Describing the right itself as valuable cannot sustain the compensatory amount under Carey and Stachura.
2. Nominal relief recognizes an established violation and is not compensation for the barred injury category. On the preserved violation and absence of compensable injury in this case, one dollar is required.
3. Punitive relief punishes culpable conduct and does not compensate distress; section 1997e(e) therefore does not categorically bar it. Smith v. Wade requires evil motive or intent, or reckless or callous indifference to federal rights. This is a distinct remedial predicate, not a universal demand that punitive fault exceed every substantive liability standard. Punitive damages remain discretionary even when their culpability prerequisite is established.
4. The punitive amount cannot stand because its instruction expressly relied on the invalid compensatory award. Redetermination removes that premise without retrying the separately established findings. A positive punitive award is not automatically compelled. Punishment cannot be used to reproduce barred compensation under a different label.
5. The preserved objection that this application denies court access or due process fails at this actual scope: the underlying right remains enforceable through the distinct nominal and punitive avenues. No categorical ruling on every possible application of the statute follows.

The all-damages reading fails to respect the injury named by the statute. The opposing compensation argument properly distinguishes the constitutional wrong from distress, but that distinction supports nominal recognition and appropriate punishment rather than preserving this jury's expressly emotional-injury award. Harmlessness cannot preserve an amount expressly tied to the invalid compensatory premise.
