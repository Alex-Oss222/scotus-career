## 44. Zahrey v. Coffey

**Existing action and next event:** Review of the Second Circuit's July 20, 2000 judgment, 221 F.3d 342, reversing dismissal of the claim against Martin E. Coffey. The next event is the supplied merits submission.

**Source and supported propositions:** The [Second Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/221/f3d/342) supplies the complaint's allegations, its qualified-immunity posture, the concessions defining review, and the competing causation arguments. It is a published judicial account of allegations, not findings that fabrication occurred. The relevant discussion and footnotes were read. No assertion is made that the petition, every merits filing or the underlying testimony was independently recovered.

The complaint alleges Coffey joined other officials in procuring false accusations from Lisa Rivera and Sidney Quick, followed by indictment, arrest, bail revocation and eight months of detention before acquittal. Coffey's alleged direct participation occurred in 1996. The complaint also contains an account under which he was initially misled by other officers; liability cannot rest on innocent receipt of false information. The live favorable account must establish his own deliberate fabrication, material causal use and resulting unjustified liberty deprivation.

Coffey concedes investigative capacity only for this appeal and reserves actual functional classification below. Zahrey does not contest absolute immunity for Coffey's advocacy before the grand jury and at the bail hearing. The litigation challenges antecedent investigative conduct; a result allowing it to proceed cannot silently abolish advocacy immunity. A truthful basis independently sufficient for the same detention may defeat material causation. The claim is not complete merely when a false document is created and put away.

**Entering law:** Buckley excludes ordinary investigative fabrication but does not decide the constitutional injury. Fletcher preserves advocacy and separates personal evidence from advocacy; it does not adopt a dishonesty exception. Albright has no majority rationale and does not settle this deliberate-fabrication claim. Clanton applies Franks and Malley to material warrant and detention deception but expressly reserves a general fabrication rule and the distinct coerced-confession branch. Crawford-El preserves ordinary proof and case management. Malesko concerns a new corporate defendant, not the individual federal-officer action litigated here.

**Notice sources:** Mooney, Pyle and Napue concern deliberate adjudicative falsehood; Franks and Malley concern material deception producing seizure; White v. Frank, 855 F.2d 956, and Jones v. City of Chicago, 856 F.2d 985, provide pre-1996 circuit applications. Buckley's 1993 opinions address function rather than conclusively establishing the merits. The adverse Seventh Circuit decision on remand, 20 F.3d 789 (1994), and Rhodes, 939 F.Supp. 1256 (1995), are material notice objections. Their existence is neither mechanically dispositive nor immaterial. Later Fletcher, Clanton, Lanier and 1997 circuit decisions supply no retrospective notice.

**Bounded review:** The granted formulation is a due-process claim and qualified immunity. Although the lower opinion quotes Fourth Amendment allegations, the commitments do not independently adjudicate a new Fourth Amendment merits claim. The specific-source issue is addressed as an objection to the presented theory. No new individual-officer damages category is separately requested or created.

**Readiness:** Ready for independent provisional commitments on the supported favorable allegations, subject to the earlier-effective-law refresh. No disputed functional fact is assumed in Coffey's favor or against him beyond his limited appellate concession.

**Existing action and next event:** Review of the Ninth Circuit's July 20, 2001 judgment, 257 F.3d 1004, No. 99-56682. The next event is the supplied full-merits submission.

**Source and supported propositions:** The [Ninth Circuit opinion](https://openjurist.org/257/f3d/1004/leonard-milstein-v-stephen-l-cooley) was read through its operative analysis and all notes. Its account of the second amended complaint establishes seven distinct challenged functions, with allegations accepted only for dismissal review:

| Component | Supported act and procedural limitation |
|---|---|
| Investigative fabrication | Recruiting Gutierrez to recant his defense testimony and falsely accuse Milstein, before the grand jury, arrest and asserted probable cause. |
| Crime report | Filing the report as complaining witnesses or purported crime victims. The complaint does not say the report was sworn; its claimed evidentiary function remains distinct from a legal submission. |
| Investigation | Investigating the purported offense between the crime report and grand-jury proceedings. |
| Indictment advocacy | Pressing for an indictment while misleading the grand jury about their asserted neutrality; the pleaded function at issue is obtaining charges, not an independently established investigative-grand-jury act. |
| Information and warrant submission | Directing an investigator to sign a criminal complaint and reprosecuting by information. No personal oath by the prosecutors or the investigator is alleged in this complaint. Do not transform the signature into Fletcher's personal sworn certification. |
| Counsel motion | Appearing in court to oppose reappointment of defense counsel. |
| Press statements | Statements after conviction and its reversal. Absence of absolute immunity does not make defamation alone a constitutional claim. |

The conviction was reversed for insufficient evidence. That fact does not prove deliberate falsehood or establish every constitutional liability element. The Ninth Circuit reserved qualified immunity for the remanded claims. The selected merits scope concerns absolute immunity; it supplies no vehicle for deciding that separate defense without its proper record.

**Entering law and alternatives:** Buckley and Fletcher require functional separation. The proposed broader knowing-material-falsehood exception would change the eight-Associate advocacy rule in Fletcher, rather than merely apply it. Its strongest justification is that purposeful creation of materially false evidence lacks legitimate advocacy value; its strongest objection is that the proposed mental-state inquiry makes immunity from suit turn on litigation over precisely the allegations Imbler protects against. Ordinary discovery protection under Crawford-El is not a substitute for absolute immunity. An intent allegation alone does not change an act's function. These alternatives will be tested separately for every Associate.

**Readiness:** Ready for independent provisional commitments, preserving all seven components and the unresolved constitutional elements and personal defenses. No source gap requires a factual assumption determining the immunity classification.

## 48. Hoffman Plastic Compounds v. NLRB

**Existing action and next event:** Review of the D.C. Circuit's January 16, 2001 en banc judgment, 237 F.3d 639, enforcing the limited Board remedy. The next event is merits decision on March 27, 2002. The statutory remedy, rather than the established unlawful discharge, is reviewed.

**Source:** The [Board's December 2001 merits brief](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/hoffman-plastic-v-nlrb-brief-merits) was retrieved and read in full, including all 27 footnotes. The extracted text is retained in the task's temporary source copy. Its quotations and citations to the Board and appellate record supply the bounded historical recitations below under Engine section 14; arguments about legal effect and incentives remain advocacy. Control located the collection item micro_IA40385014_0462. No original-document absence is asserted.

**Supported record:** Castro was hired in May 1988 and discriminatorily laid off on January 31, 1989. The Board found that Hoffman inspected apparently genuine documents, attempted to comply with the verification regime, did not know Castro lacked authorization and would not have hired him if it had known. These are Board findings reported with citations, not statutory commands. Review of the January 1992 unfair-labor-practice finding was waived. On June 14, 1993, the final compliance-hearing day, Castro disclosed use of another person's birth certificate. The Board denied reinstatement and ended backpay on that discovery date. Its award was $66,951 plus interest.

The attempted March 10, 1989 reinstatement offer did not terminate backpay because the Board found it insufficiently specific, unequivocal and unconditional; that finding was not challenged below. Mitigation was found on the basis of interim work. No finding establishes that Castro used additional false documents to obtain that work. The federal brief's suggestion that some jobs might fall outside the verification rules is an alternative legal explanation, not a finding about each job.

**Statutory precision:** NLRA section 10(c) authorizes remedial affirmative action including reinstatement with or without backpay, within the Act's policies; its actual-discharge-for-cause limit remains distinct. IRCA's section 1324a(a)(1) prohibits knowing unauthorized hiring and separately hiring without required verification; subsection (a)(2) prohibits continued employment with knowledge of unauthorized status. Subsection (b) supplies document verification. Subsection (a)(3)'s good-faith verification defense concerns the employer's own liability, not an affirmative authorization for the worker. Under section (h)(3), employment authorization depends on permanent residence or authorization under the Act or by the Attorney General. Section 1546(b) of title 18 prohibits the described knowing misuse of identification documents to satisfy verification.

The civil document penalties in section 1324c were enacted in 1990 and do not apply retroactively to Castro's 1988 document use. They cannot be cited as a penalty governing that act. No criminal conviction or immigration order against Castro is found here. The question is whether the immigration rules limit the labor remedy, not whether all unlawful employment has identical penalties.

**Entering law and live alternatives:** ABF leaves reviewable remedial discretion rather than automatic forfeiture for hearing falsehood; it preserves separate challenges to a defeated remedial predicate. McKennon separates discrimination liability from subsequently discovered sufficient grounds for discharge, ordinarily ending prospective relief and limiting backpay at discovery. Sure-Tan requires attention to legal work availability and unlawful reentry in its departed-worker record. Southern Steamship disallows a labor remedy incompatible with another federal prohibition. Neither ABF nor McKennon settles this immigration question merely by analogy. Neither the existence of IRCA sanctions nor an asserted policy of deterrence mechanically establishes a backpay ban.

**Bounded paths:** Enforce the limited award as a monetary remedy for the distinct labor violation, without reinstatement or postdiscovery accrual; or deny this category of backpay for unperformed unauthorized work while preserving the violation and other lawful relief. The record supplies no independent defect requiring recalculation or renewed mitigation findings. Earned wages, another employment statute's remedies, and knowingly unlawful hiring by a different employer are outside the necessary holding.

**Readiness:** Ready for independent provisional commitments on those two statutory paths, subject to the earlier-effective-law refresh. The preserved calculation and mitigation findings supply no reason for a source-based stop or invented record request.
