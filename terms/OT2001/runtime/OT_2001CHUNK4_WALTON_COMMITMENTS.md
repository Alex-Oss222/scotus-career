## Supported record and distinct decision components

Walton applied for both disability insurance benefits and supplemental security income on April 12, 1995. His amended onset date was October 31, 1994. He began part-time work in May 1995 and full-time work on December 10, 1995. The first administrative ruling after the July 1996 hearing awarded a closed period; the final ruling after administrative remand found substantial gainful activity in October 1995 and denied initial entitlement because the inability did not continue for twelve months. The Fourth Circuit affirmed the October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity determination. That factual component is not recalculated, and neither the full-time December date nor later regulatory earnings thresholds replace it. Continuing impairment is undisputed; medical recovery is not a finding.

The reviewed legal components are:

1. **Duration:** Whether the twelve-month branch of the adult disability definitions requires the impairment to disable the claimant from substantial gainful activity for that period, rather than requiring only a lasting impairment accompanied by a shorter inability.
2. **Prediction and initial entitlement:** Whether an earlier expectation of twelve-month inability conclusively establishes entitlement when actual substantial gainful activity within that period is known before the favorable disability determination.
3. **Title II trial work:** Whether the claimed trial-work protection prevents use of that work to decide initial disability and entitlement, although the functional duration condition is not otherwise satisfied on the actual predecision record.
4. **Interpretive authority and timing:** Whether the operative agency interpretation is lawful under the statute and regulations, distinguishing its merits from an asserted entitlement to controlling deference and from retroactive application of the August 2000 regulation.

The circuit supplied independent legal grounds: its asserted unambiguous impairment-only construction and, alternatively, its conclusion that the agency's construction would be unreasonable even if interpretation were permissible. Reversal must reject both. It is insufficient to invoke agency expertise, deny deference without construing the statute, or resolve only the grammar while leaving the trial-work ground intact.

## Competing paths and their decisive objections

### W1 — functional duration, actual predecision evidence and no initial trial-work entitlement

The disability definition addresses an inability to work caused by a medically determinable impairment. On the twelve-month branch, the impairment must remain disabling for twelve continuous months or be expected to do so. A lasting diagnosis with a shorter functional inability does not alone meet that branch. The expected-duration language permits a favorable determination before twelve months elapse; it does not require the agency to disregard actual substantial work within the period, known before its favorable determination. The rule therefore imposes no universal twelve-month wait for adjudication and does not exclude an impairment expected to result in death under the separate statutory alternative.

Section 422(c) protects services during a qualifying trial-work period against use in determining whether an established disability ceased during that period. It presupposes entitlement and does not itself create the initial disability condition. Walton cannot use the disputed trial-work status to exclude the very evidence relevant to whether that initial condition was satisfied. The five-month waiting period governs when ordinary Title II payment can begin after disability and the other conditions are met; passing five months does not establish twelve-month disability. Entitlement is not equated with the date a benefit check or notice happens to be mailed. The narrower point is that an earlier unadjudicated prediction does not conclusively establish the disputed initial duration condition in the face of actual contrary work evidence already available when eligibility is decided.

The strongest objection is the grammar: the relative clause follows impairment, and an impairment, rather than inability to work, causes death. W1 does not require reading the statute as saying that gainful activity causes death. It reads impairment in the definition as the impairment producing the described inability; the durability of that disabling condition, rather than the existence of a condition detached from its functional consequence, is the relevant unit. The death alternative remains separate. That reading gives the definition an operative work-disability function without making every persisting medical diagnosis a basis for benefits after only brief inability.

The strongest structural objection is that sections 423(a)(1) and 422(c)(3) speak of entitlement, not a prior award, and that similarly situated claimants may receive decisions at different times. W1 accepts that an agency cannot freely add a payment or mailing condition. It distinguishes deciding initial qualification on known facts from ending an entitlement already recognized on an adequate prospective basis. The provisions permitting an expected duration cannot sensibly make a prediction permanently decisive even when contrary actual duration is known before the initial determination. The agency may make an early favorable determination where the available evidence supports the required expectation; its later cessation and trial-work obligations then retain their own legal predicates. The present holding does not decide every consequence of a favorable pre-return award, agency reopening or a changed expectation after that award.

### W2 — impairment-only duration and accrued trial-work entitlement

Affirmance could read the relative clause as imposing duration exclusively on impairment. Once inability due to that qualifying impairment, the ordinary waiting period and the remaining entitlement conditions are met, trial work could begin without an adjudication. The October work would then be excluded from the cessation assessment during the protected period. This path gives direct effect to the nearest referent and the statute's entitlement language, avoids converting administrative speed into a statutory condition, and promotes attempted work.

Its principal difficulty is the connection between the defined disability and functional inability: a chronic impairment plus short inability could produce the claimed entitlement although actual work ability returns before the functional period ever satisfies the duration branch. It also uses trial-work cessation protection to establish the initial entitlement on which that protection depends. The eight provisional commitments below reject W2 for these reasons, rather than on an invented medical recovery or a belief that an award's date appears expressly in the disability definition.

### W3 — functional duration but conclusively accrued expected-duration entitlement

A divided resolution could accept functional duration yet affirm trial-work protection because a supported earlier expectation established entitlement once the waiting period ended. The strongest argument is that Congress expressly permits expected duration and does not say an erroneous forecast retrospectively erases benefits. The answer under W1 is that the initial determination may use actual facts already known: expected duration is a basis for timely adjudication on incomplete experience, not an instruction to disregard completed contradictory experience. No authenticated final pre-return award fixes Walton's initial status, and the first later closed-period ruling did not survive administrative review. This is a legal assessment of the stated sequence, not an invented finding that his original medical expectation was unreasonable.

W3's possible force in a materially different case with a favorable determination before substantial work is reserved. The present commitments do not turn that reservation into a holding that every previously awarded claimant necessarily receives every asserted trial-work protection.

## Agency instruments, timing and the sufficient common rationale

Sections 405(a) and 1383(d)(1) supply actual rulemaking and proof-administration authority for the two programs. They require consistency with the benefit provisions; program complexity alone is no delegation. SSR 82-52 and the March 6, 1995 proposal supplied the interpretation used in the challenged adjudication as recited in the lower opinion. Section 402.35(b)(1) establishes the adopted Ruling's binding operation within the agency. It does not by itself establish interpretive force binding a reviewing court under Mead. A proposal is not a final legislative rule.

The trial-work amendment effective August 10, 2000 is not applied retroactively as a new condition governing Walton's 1995 work or the earlier adjudication. Its later formal expression is consistent with the antecedent interpretation identified in the lower judgment, but that consistency does not itself prove every procedural requirement for promulgation. Smiley permits qualified interpretive use of a later authoritative construction without backdating it; the present result need not enlarge that permission because W1 independently resolves the statutory question at the adjudicated scope. No clear contrary agency promise or individual reliance finding is supplied or needed for this independent ground.

The eight Associates accept W1's statutory construction and record application as independently sufficient to reverse. Their support is for the actual duration and initial-entitlement rule, not merely for a result that could be sustained if some unproved source of delegated authority later appeared. The agency's consistent functional construction reinforces the reading through its reasons. The handoff neither categorically denies Chevron treatment to all Social Security Rulings or benefit adjudications nor holds that section 405(a) and internal binding effect automatically make every interpretation controlling. A broad classification of that mechanism is unnecessary to this shared sufficient ground. Scalia preserves his different Mead position but can join the independent rule and application; O'Connor's Edelman concurrence does not prevent an independent construction when she finds that construction persuasive on a different statutory text.

Regulatory construction is distinct. Sections 404.1505 and 404.1509 describe the impairment-caused inability and duration. Their use of impairment does not require treating every lasting impairment as a qualifying disability despite restored substantial work capacity. The responsible agency's integrated reading is consistent with those provisions. Auer does not supply power to amend clear text, and the present interpretation does not use Auer to retroactively insert the 2000 amendment. The statutory holding is sufficient without treating a proposal, internal ruling or counsel's submission as an amendment of a governing regulation.

## Exact common propositions and qualifications for later assembly

The eight Associates directly support all three substantive propositions below at the same scope. Their separate reservations about the deference mechanism supply no additional holding and are not combined into one through judgment arithmetic.

1. **Adult duration.** On the twelve-month branch of sections 423(d)(1)(A) and 1382c(a)(3)(A), the disabling inability to perform substantial gainful activity caused by the medically determinable impairment must have lasted or be expected to last continuously for at least twelve months. Persistence of the impairment alone is insufficient when the actual functional inability lasts less. The expected-death alternative is retained; the separate statutory-blindness provision in section 423(d)(1)(B) and the SSI child provision in section 1382c(a)(3)(C) are not adjudicated. Walton's adult claim supplies no basis to transplant their different criteria.

2. **Actual predecision work and expected duration.** Where the claimant actually demonstrates substantial gainful activity within twelve months of onset before a favorable disability determination, the initial eligibility decision may consider that work in determining whether the required disabling duration was satisfied. An earlier expectation is not conclusively controlling against actual evidence known before that determination. A claimant whose supported expected duration reaches the statutory period can receive a favorable prospective decision without waiting for twelve months to elapse. The rule does not adjudicate reopening, reliance or cessation after a favorable pre-return determination, the separate status of an unsuccessful work attempt, or medical recovery.

3. **Title II trial work in this initial-entitlement posture.** Section 422(c)'s protection against treating services as showing cessation during a qualifying trial-work period does not establish the initial entitlement the period presupposes. Walton's October work therefore remains available for the initial-duration determination; satisfaction of the ordinary five-month waiting period cannot supply the missing duration condition. This is not a general rule that entitlement always begins only on the date an award is issued. Title II's period begins with entitlement under the governing provisions, and its other start and end conditions remain operative. No Title II trial-work or five-month payment rule is imposed on SSI merely because the adult duration definitions share the operative language.

The following supplied qualifications remain distinct and should not be replaced with a vague reference to an unspecified exception:

- Ordinary section 423(a)(1) entitlement requires insured status, an application, age below retirement age and disability. The ordinary five consecutive calendar-month waiting period requires disability throughout, subject to its statutory start limits. Section 423(a)(1)(ii)'s separate route for qualifying prior entitlement or a prior disability period ending within sixty months remains unadjudicated; there is no premise that Walton qualifies for it.
- Under section 422(c), services include gainful or remunerative activity and activity the Commissioner determines is normally performed for remuneration or gain; the concept is not identical to substantial gainful activity. During the qualifying period, services are excluded for the statutory cessation determination. The period ends at the earlier of the ninth service month in sixty consecutive months or the month disability ceases as determined after applying that service exclusion. The service months need not be consecutive. No new nine-month calculation is made here.
- The statute prohibits another trial-work period in the same disability period and preserves its historical commencement limit and distinct child-benefit and surviving-spouse commencement terms. The supplied later regulation's application-month limit, waiting-period work exclusion, within-twelve-month/predecision-work exclusion, benefit-category limits and termination on other evidence of ended disability remain distinct provisions. The decision does not apply new 2001 or 2002 service thresholds to the affirmed October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding.
- The current regulation's use of services during a completed trial period to evaluate disability afterward does not authorize using those services to establish cessation during a protected period. The present holding instead concerns whether initial qualifying entitlement was established in the first place.
- The statutory expected-death alternative avoids the twelve-month duration branch; it is not narrowed to a requirement that death actually occur. No expected-death finding is made for Walton.

## Formal judgment position, independent grounds and readiness

All eight Associates support reversal of the Fourth Circuit insofar as it rejected the Commissioner's functional-duration and initial trial-work interpretation and directed relief on that legal basis. The ordinary remand implements reinstatement of the agency denial at the reviewed scope. The circuit's distinct affirmance that Walton performed substantial gainful activity in October 1995 remains undisturbed. No new medical hearing, compelled regulation, benefit amount, recovery finding, charge of claimant fault or new filing obligation follows.

The independent statutory construction answers the circuit's first ground: its impairment-only reading is not the correct reading of the combined definition and entitlement provisions. It also defeats the circuit's alternative reasonableness ground: the agency's treatment of known actual work and of trial work's cessation function preserves, rather than contradicts, the statutory scheme. The expected-duration language, the five-month waiting period and entitlement terminology retain the distinct work explained above; no prior-award phrase is invented in the statutory text. The judgment therefore does not rest merely on refusing to endorse the circuit's language while leaving its independent basis intact.

The supplemental handoff is complete at this scope. No remaining source gap prevents the common statutory disposition. A proposed broader holding classifying all Social Security Rulings, validating every procedural aspect of the 2000 rule, adjudicating an awarded claimant's different rights or deciding an unsuccessful-work-attempt issue would exceed the supported model and require separate source support and joins. The parent retains the final chronological dependency check before assembly. The earlier five-case lane A freeze remains unchanged.
