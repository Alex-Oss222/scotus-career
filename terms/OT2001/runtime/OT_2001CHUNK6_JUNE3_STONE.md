## 83. Holmes Group, Inc. v. Vornado Air Circulation Systems, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Federal Circuit’s judgment because the patent counterclaim does not supply the required arising-under basis.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for disposition consistent with the proper regional appellate route and any applicable transfer authority. Preserve the distinction between appellate allocation and the district court’s jurisdiction over Holmes’s federal trade-dress action. Do not decide patent infringement, functionality, or collateral estoppel on the merits.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Follow the enacted jurisdictional source

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide specialized appellate jurisdiction before any trade-dress merits. Reach the effect of the patent counterclaim because it is the asserted jurisdictional anchor.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a compulsory counterclaim changes a nonpatent complaint into an action arising under patent law for the relevant jurisdictional statutes.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing complaint-based approach to arising-under jurisdiction and propose its explicit application to this specialized statutory allocation.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A patent counterclaim, including a compulsory one, does not make an action arise under patent law when the plaintiff’s well-pleaded complaint supplies no patent-law basis. The Federal Circuit therefore lacks the specialized appellate jurisdiction asserted in this case.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule addresses jurisdiction grounded solely in a counterclaim. It does not eliminate district-court jurisdiction supplied by other federal claims or determine the effect of a materially amended complaint asserting patent rights.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Sections 1338 and 1295 use the established arising-under formulation; Christianson and the well-pleaded-complaint rule give that language a source-of-claim meaning. Stone reaffirms that allocation and applies it to a compulsory patent counterclaim. Statutory text and inherited jurisdictional usage carry decisive weight over a functional preference for uniform patent adjudication. Rules requiring a counterclaim to be pleaded do not convert it into the plaintiff's cause of action. The complaint supplies a federal trade-dress controversy, so the defect concerns specialized appellate allocation rather than an absence of all district-court jurisdiction. TrafFix's substantive functionality holding does not create appellate authority to reach those merits. The Federal Circuit judgment must be vacated and the proper regional route used; an amended patent complaint would require a different jurisdictional record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The trade-dress merits, patent merits, and materially changed pleading configurations are outside the jurisdictional question presented.


## 81. SEC v. Zandford

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit’s categorical dismissal and remand for adjudication of the SEC’s claim under an integrated deceptive-sale analysis.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the SEC to prove that the securities sales were undertaken as part of the concealed appropriation scheme. Reassess the summary-judgment record and criminal issue preclusion rather than assuming every civil element is established. No new civil penalty, disgorgement amount, or injunction is fixed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The transaction as an instrument of the deception

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory connection issue necessary to resolve the dismissal, while distinguishing pleading sufficiency from judgment on proof.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether secret liquidation of entrusted securities to steal the proceeds can constitute deception in connection with the sales.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply O’Hagan’s governing relationship between entrusted authority, deception, and trading; propose its direct application to this integrated broker scheme.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A broker commits fraud in connection with securities sales when, while purporting to act for the account holders, he secretly liquidates their securities as part of a plan to appropriate the proceeds. The provision does not require a false statement about the securities’ value, but requires a demonstrated connection between deceptive conduct and the transactions themselves.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the alleged planned sales from this discretionary account. It excludes liability based solely on a broker’s status or on a later independent misuse of money from a completed nondeceptive transaction.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 10(b)'s transaction connection, applied through O'Hagan's account of concealed misuse of entrusted authority, is decisive. The broker allegedly used discretionary sale authority as the instrument for stealing the clients' assets; liquidation and appropriation formed one deceptive course rather than an innocent sale followed by an unrelated theft. Stone extends O'Hagan's reasoning to that setting without creating a federal remedy for every fiduciary breach. The combination uses statutory text and the scheme's actual operation, with the required securities connection limiting the functional analysis. Authority to select investments for clients is not authority to sell secretly for oneself. This resolves the categorical dismissal ground; the scope of the criminal judgment's preclusion and the civil summary-judgment record still determine which facts and remedies have been established.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Remaining proof, issue preclusion, and the proper civil remedies depend on record matters beyond the categorical question decided.


## 82. United States v. Mackby +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the ruling that the combined statutory penalties and treble damages are subject to the Excessive Fines Clause, with remand for proportionality review.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the established False Claims Act liability. Require assessment of the aggregate punitive extraction under Bajakajian, taking account of actual loss, culpability, repeated conduct, lawful compensatory functions, and the sanction’s severity. Do not set a new award or presume that the existing one is excessive.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Constitutional scrutiny of a mixed-purpose recovery

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only whether the constitutional protection applies and the consequence of that answer. The question does not reopen liability or demand a final excessiveness ruling.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a civil enforcement recovery containing fixed penalties and multiplied damages can avoid the Excessive Fines Clause because it also compensates the government.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Austin and Bajakajian to the monetary scheme before the Court, without replacing their gross-disproportionality standard.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** False Claims Act civil penalties and treble damages imposed together constitute an exaction at least partly punitive and therefore fall within the Excessive Fines Clause. Their compensatory and regulatory purposes remain relevant to proportionality but do not defeat coverage.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs the combined recovery imposed for Mackby’s false Medicare claims. It requires scrutiny rather than invalidation and does not establish a fixed permissible multiplier or a separate criminal classification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Austin's partly punitive criterion and Bajakajian's distinct excessiveness inquiry govern. The label civil cannot remove a sovereign monetary exaction from scrutiny when fixed penalties and multiplied recovery perform punitive work. The actual function of this combined award is decisive on coverage; compensation for difficult-to-measure injury remains relevant when assessing gross disproportionality. The judgment's severity, repeated knowing claims, actual loss and culpability must be assessed together, rather than assuming either that treatment provided defeats fraud or that fraud validates any amount. This applies the existing constitutional standard to a new statutory recovery and creates no fixed permissible multiplier. Remand is warranted because the assigned question concerns coverage and the trial court has not performed the resulting proportionality inquiry, not because Stone leaves the governing rule unanswered.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The final proportionality determination requires findings and assessment not resolved by the assigned coverage question. Other constitutional classifications and unchallenged liability issues lie outside it.
