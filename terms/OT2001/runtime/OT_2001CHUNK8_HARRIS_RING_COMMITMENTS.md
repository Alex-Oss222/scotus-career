## Harris — separable components and supported paths

### H1. Construction precedes constitutional classification

The amended provision is best read to express a basic firearm offense with graduated penalty conditions, rather than to reproduce the former statute's aggravated firearm-type offenses. The completed conduct clause and ensuing minimum-term directions support that reading. Castillo construed a materially different text; its unanimity does not supply a construction of this amendment. The strongest competing element reading is substantial: defined intimidating conduct is a familiar culpability distinction, and a mandatory two-year increase is consequential; Jones establishes that placement in a penalty clause is not conclusive.

For the five Associates accepting Angle, the sentencing-condition construction cannot dispense with constitutional element protections. It gives the enacted words their natural structural meaning while applying the independent constitutional constraint to the legal work brandishing performs. It does not attribute an affirmative congressional decision to dispense with a jury or proof beyond a reasonable doubt, matters on which the text is silent. Their constitutional conclusion does not depend on construing every penalty condition as a separate statutory offense. A statutory-element concurrence remains a lawful narrower alternative, but no row below needs to adopt it to reach relief.

O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer also choose the sentencing-condition construction. They would preserve that classification's ordinary sentencing consequences only through express reconsideration of Angle's contrary constitutional floor holding. They cannot describe McMillan's displaced distinction as the law of the Court entering this case.

### H2. Mandatory floor, federal charge, waiver and proof

Angle I-A directly covers a fact raising the legally binding floor from five to seven years despite an unchanged maximum. Its rule does not depend on whether the constraint originates in a statute or the mandatory Guidelines. Angle I-B separately supplies applicable federal grand-jury protection. The indictment omitted brandishing; the trial determination established the basic carrying offense. No valid admission of brandishing as an aggravated element is supplied. The lawful bench-trial waiver permits the judge to determine an element; it does not enlarge the charge or substitute preponderance for reasonable doubt. There is no independent entitlement to a second jury solely because the factfinder was a judge after that valid waiver.

The strongest contrary merits path is the published Angle dissent: a legislature may guide sentencing within an outer range and need not make each direction an offense element. That path would sustain judicial brandishing findings under McMillan's former minimum distinction. Its immediate obstacle is an exact contrary controlling precedent, not an unresolved minimum question. A vote on that ground must therefore expressly maintain the dissent and seek reconsideration of Angle. A seven-year term's abstract availability within the outer range is not a factual or remedial answer to the sentence actually imposed.

### H3. Error review and actual relief

The preserved objection asserted a statutory element and demanded charging and trial proof; the constitutional Apprendi argument was added on appeal. The record does not establish an intentional relinquishment of the constitutional claim. The five relief votes can assume forfeiture of the constitutional theory and resolve the stricter Rule 52(b) requirements, without falsely finding that every theory was preserved or transferring the ordinary prejudice burden to the Government.

1. **Error:** Applying Angle to this pending direct review, the uncharged brandishing fact was used to compel the aggravated minimum without an element determination beyond a reasonable doubt. The constitutional error is real but nonjurisdictional under Cotton.
2. **Plainness:** McMillan supplied settled contrary minimum law at sentencing; Angle changes that law during direct review. Johnson's controlling review-time rule makes the covered error clear now. This is its settled-law/intervening-law setting, not a new rule for all initially unsettled questions. Scalia's row distinguishes applying that binding rule where necessary to relief from his earlier refusals to endorse an unnecessary affirmative timing holding.
3. **Substantial rights:** Harris carries the ordinary burden through the actual sentencing record: the judge described the contested brandishing issue as close, resolved it under a lower standard, and imposed the minimum that §2K2.4 made the guideline sentence. The judge's favorable view of seven years is adverse evidence and is considered. It does not eliminate the reasonable probability of a different lawful sentencing outcome where the judge expressly declined to select that term independent of brandishing. The conclusion is a concrete showing of effect, not presumed prejudice from a changed range or the Government's failure to prove harmlessness.
4. **Discretionary correction:** Additional imprisonment compelled by a genuinely contested aggravator resolved under the lower standard, with an objection demanding charging and trial proof and no unaffected sentence independently selected, seriously impairs the fairness and integrity of this sentencing proceeding. This separate conclusion does not follow merely because the third condition is satisfied. Johnson and Cotton involved their particular clear or essentially uncontested threshold records; their denial of correction is not transferred to this disputed brandishing record. Neither actual innocence nor a second unrelated institutional wrong is required.

The strongest no-relief argument is that the judge considered seven years appropriate and could impose it through a lawful departure. The answer is limited: possible lawful future action is not an entered alternative sentence, and the express refusal to commit to it matters. No row treats reversal as proof that only five years is lawful. Relief vacates the firearm sentence and the judgment affirming that sentence, preserves the basic conviction, and remands for lawful sentencing under the existing charge and applicable law. The mandate creates neither authority for a new aggravated trial nor a fixed replacement term. The circuit's independent evidentiary determinations are not reopened beyond what the granted charging-and-proof issue requires.

## Ring — separable components and supported paths

### R1. Capital eligibility and Walton

The verdict established felony murder, not the additional statutory eligibility fact necessary to permit death. The question is the fact that first authorizes death, not the final choice between lawful punishments. Apprendi reserved this capital question and Angle did not resolve it. The six Sixth Amendment supporters below would now resolve it expressly: a present-offense aggravator necessary for death eligibility must be found by a jury beyond a reasonable doubt, absent a valid element admission or lawful jury waiver. Neither is supplied here. They would overrule Walton only insofar as it permits the judge to make that necessary eligibility finding in place of the jury.

The strongest competing retention path invokes Walton's direct approval, the distinctive constitutional demand for capital narrowing, the State's established procedure and the judicial-selection decisions. It cannot be dismissed as though Walton were already gone. The response is that narrowing identifies the permissible capital class; it does not require that the judge rather than the jury find the fact defining membership. The State's authority to make death a possible penalty for the general offense does not eliminate its enacted additional prerequisite. Judicial selection after valid eligibility, consideration of mitigation, and weighing are distinct. The Sixth Amendment holding neither constitutionalizes every selection fact nor incorporates the federal grand-jury clause against Arizona.

The new Sixth Amendment proponents acknowledge the State's procedural reliance and the need for lawful transition. Their conclusion is that Apprendi's actual-authorization principle and Angle's rejection of formal punishment labels undermine Walton's contrary allocation rationale at this precise factfinding boundary. This is an express doctrinal decision now, not retroactive attribution of the holding to either earlier case. Existing constraints on lawful further capital proceedings remain; the Court creates no new procedural vehicle.

### R2. Harmlessness and the bounded mandate

Arizona actually raises harmlessness from the robbery verdict. The premise cannot be accepted merely by renaming robbery as pecuniary gain. A jury element omission is distinct from appellate review of an invalid selection factor; Clemons-type reweighing cannot supply a missing eligibility verdict. The proposed ordinary remand must examine the actual instructions, necessary findings, Arizona's aggravator definition and record conflicts. The State bears the preserved-error burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the omission did not contribute to the actual result. Under the qualified Neder method, necessary equivalent findings suffice but are not the exclusive possible basis; additional existing-record inferences must remain reliably connected to the actual verdict, and new credibility findings or sufficient evidence for a hypothetical verdict do not suffice.

The six Sixth Amendment proponents support reversing the legal rejection of the jury claim, vacating the Arizona judgment insofar as it affirms death, and remanding for the proper error-review disposition. They do not make an unsupported conclusive harmlessness finding here. Nor do they order unconditional sentence relief without addressing the raised review argument: if the State fails the applicable harmlessness standard, the death sentence must be set aside; a legally sufficient harmlessness determination may sustain it. Scalia and Ginsburg would confine the harmlessness inquiry to genuine equivalent jury findings, preserving their published Neder distinction. Their judgment agreement does not supply votes for the broader inference method.

This mandate preserves the felony-murder conviction. It does not order a new guilt trial, automatically impose life, direct an unauthorized capital jury, supply an aggravator finding or itself select death. The absence of the complete actual charge in this bounded packet prevents a conclusive harmlessness determination, but does not prevent the appellate remand to perform that ordinary record inquiry.

### R3. Sixth and Eighth Amendment alternatives

The allocation rule proposed for the Court concerns the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments. Eighth Amendment narrowing and individualized-selection principles are relevant context but do not automatically require a jury on every sentencing issue. Atkins's categorical disability rule decides no allocation question. A separate Eighth Amendment jury-selection theory must have its own Justice-specific support and must confront Harris v. Alabama and the selection precedents; it cannot be counted as a Sixth Amendment join or used to expand the grant into a general jury-sentencing holding.

Breyer's two paths are specifically tested below. The independent provisional conclusion retains his prior judicial-selection position and does not attribute a new Eighth Amendment jury requirement to him merely from his general capital-reliability concerns. That conclusion is subject to the separate historical-reconciliation stage, not to hidden reliance on the present case's historical writing.
