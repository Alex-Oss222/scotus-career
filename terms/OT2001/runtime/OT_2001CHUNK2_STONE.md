## 17. Kelly v. South Carolina

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-09, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the state judgment insofar as it sustains the death sentence without the required parole-ineligibility instruction; remand for a new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the guilt judgment. Require accurate explanation of life without parole when the governing conditions are met. On this presentation, do not treat the omitted information as harmless merely because substantial aggravating evidence existed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The jury’s actual choice determines the information it needs

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the penalty instruction under Simmons and direct-review harmlessness; do not reopen guilt.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the State can avoid Simmons through a character label or an earlier statutory stage.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Simmons and apply Shafer’s staged-sentencing holding; determine from the full presentation that future danger was placed in issue and that the omission was harmful under Chapman.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When the State’s evidence or argument materially places future dangerousness in issue, the defendant is entitled to an accurate and effective explanation that the only nondeath sentence the jury may select at the punishment stage is life without parole. Character terminology does not defeat the right when the presentation conveys a prospective threat. A different sentencing possibility before the required aggravating finding does not alter the jury’s later binary choice.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the actual death-selection stage and does not require a particular script or an instruction in every capital proceeding.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Evidence of escape and further violence invites jurors to consider what the defendant may do after conviction. The legal question cannot turn on whether the prosecutor uses a designated phrase. Nor can the State answer the jury’s punishment-stage concern by identifying an option that no longer exists once the necessary finding has been made. Accurate information about the nondeath alternative addresses the premise on which the State seeks death. The record does not permit dismissal of that omission as harmless solely from the strength of aggravation. Simmons and Shafer carry decisive precedential weight. Characterizing evidence as character evidence cannot erase its ordinary prospective implication: escape planning and violence while confined support the very inference the legal parole information answers. On this record, exclusion of that answer leaves reasonable doubt whether the misleading sentencing premise contributed to death; the State therefore fails Chapman. That is a record-specific harmlessness judgment, not structural error or a categorical command for every capital case. Guilt remains undisturbed because the defect concerns the selection of punishment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other conviction issues and sentencing arrangements without the Simmons conditions remain outside the ruling.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the rejection of the Take Care and Appointments Clause challenges and remand for ordinary adjudication of the false-claims action.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the action to proceed subject to the government’s actual statutory powers and the relator’s statutory limitations. Resolve no fraud liability, damages, or future government dismissal motion now.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A limited assigned claim does not create a separate executive office

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply the governing standing holding, then independently decide the two Article II questions.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether nonintervened prosecution under this statutory structure violates executive control or appointment requirements.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Propose a new resolution of constitutional questions expressly left open in Vermont Agency; do not attribute the answer to that earlier case.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A private relator’s prosecution of the False Claims Act’s partially assigned pecuniary claim, within the statute’s retained government intervention, dismissal, settlement, and protective powers, does not violate the Take Care Clause merely because the government initially declines intervention. The relator’s case-specific private litigating role is not a continuing federal office requiring appointment under the Appointments Clause. The conclusion depends on the actual bounded role and statutory controls, not a general power to transfer executive functions to private persons.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding permits this civil enforcement mechanism. It does not authorize private criminal prosecution, rulemaking, or unrestricted exercise of federal coercive powers.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defendants correctly identify consequences for federal interests and litigation policy. The structure must therefore be examined in detail rather than justified solely by historical labels. Here the relator pursues an assigned financial claim through a court, while the government retains identified means of protecting its interests. That differs from giving a private actor a standing governmental office or general executive authority. Nonintervention allows the private litigant to bear the case initially; it does not necessarily terminate the executive’s statutory powers. Neither constitutional clause makes the assignment reasoning irrelevant, but neither is conclusively answered by standing alone. Constitutional structure carries decisive weight, informed by the early qui tam practice rather than displaced by it. Vermont Agency is reaffirmed for assignment-based standing and expressly not extended into a holding it reserved under Article II. The relator holds no continuing statutory office and has no general prosecutorial, rulemaking, or executive power. The retained intervention, dismissal, settlement, and protective controls keep this financial litigation from disabling the Executive’s constitutional responsibility. This resolves both appointment and Take Care objections without importing Morrison’s independent criminal office as the same institution.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific disputes over a later dismissal, intervention, settlement, or exercise of an ungranted sovereign power require their own record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Return for further proceedings consistent with the lawful stop determination. Preserve any separate issue concerning consent or search scope actually left open; use no post-stop discovery to justify the earlier seizure.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The relationship among facts matters

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the ultimate reasonable-suspicion question on the supported historical findings.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the combined observations justify the stop despite possible innocent explanations.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Ornelas and Wardlow’s contextual principles without weakening independent judicial review.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Reasonable suspicion depends on the objective totality of circumstances known before the stop. A court may not discard each supported observation merely because it admits an innocent explanation. It must assess whether the observations and their rational relationship produce a particularized basis for suspecting wrongdoing. Training and experience receive weight when they explain that relationship; they do not replace judicial evaluation or validate irrelevant facts.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule permits cumulative inference without treating mere numerical accumulation or an officer’s unexplained intuition as sufficient.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The route, timing, and sensor context make the vehicle observations more informative than they would be on an ordinary road without that background. The record supplies a connected inference rather than a requirement to find each action independently criminal. Arvizu’s concern about profiles remains valid: the court must ask how the observations bear on this vehicle and this suspected activity. Independent review can credit the combination here while rejecting the notion that any series of innocent acts necessarily warrants a stop. Cortez and Terry’s totality standard, reaffirmed with Ornelas’s independent legal review, is decisive. The observations gain meaning from their relationship: a remote bypass route, timing around the checkpoint shift, sensor information, and unusual vehicle behavior support an inference particular to this trip. Ordinary innocent acts do not become unlawful, and their sheer number is not the test. Stone decides reasonable suspicion on the accepted pre-stop findings because the record permits the legal application; another remand merely to redo that legal comparison is unnecessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Separate consent or search-scope disputes, if preserved, remain distinct.

* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The FAA does not govern this employment contract under Circuit City and therefore supplies no federal policy ground for restricting the EEOC’s relief. Independently, an employee’s private agreement does not bind the nonparty EEOC or eliminate Congress’s victim-specific remedies in its own enforcement action. Liability and relief still require their statutory showings, with actual overlapping satisfaction accounted for when proved.

First Options reinforces that a contract does not bind a nonassenting agency, but the statutory authority is decisive. Compensation serves public enforcement as well as the injured employee; the label victim-specific does not transfer control of the action to Baker. Courts can prevent actual duplicate recovery without stripping authorized remedies in advance. These two sufficient statutory grounds answer the presented question, while the employment agreement’s validity under another source remains outside it.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the rejection of the facial challenge to the ordinance’s neutral coordination structure.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the ordinance operative while preserving properly presented claims of discriminatory application or use of criteria outside the authorized objective grounds. Do not impose every content-censorship procedure on ordinary park scheduling.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Safeguards should address the system’s actual discretionary risk

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the facial structure; do not decide unsupported allegations about particular officials’ motives.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the neutral permit scheme requires the full censorship safeguards.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Forsyth’s demand for constrained discretion and distinguish the separate censorship setting.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A genuinely content-neutral public-park permit system may use objective logistical criteria, a definite decision period, written reasons, and available administrative and judicial review without adopting every procedure required for content censorship. Its validity depends on real limits on discretion and timely access to review. Facial validity does not authorize viewpoint discrimination, unexplained exceptions, or audience-hostility burdens.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers this coordination structure and does not immunize its administration or all permit ordinances.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The challengers identify a real risk: administrative control can suppress expression as effectively as an explicit censor. The answer is to examine what officials may decide and how quickly their decision can be challenged. The ordinance’s enumerated grounds and timetable confine ordinary park management rather than authorize approval of messages. Requiring a government-filed judicial proceeding for each scheduling denial would address a different problem. Forsyth remains the controlling warning against an ordinance that permits officials to price or condition expression through unbounded or content-linked discretion. Stone distinguishes Freedman’s content-censorship system and reaffirms Forsyth’s constraint on discretionary burdens. The ordinance’s objective grounds, 28-day decision period, reasons, and review are the relevant safeguards; content neutrality alone would not save unbounded discretion. Its structure, tested against the practical risk of suppression, carries decisive weight. No general safe harbor protects discriminatory administration, and no ruling on whether Freedman requires a prompt final judicial determination is needed after its applicability is rejected.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A preserved claim of discriminatory administration requires its own factual adjudication.

* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the statutory coverage and ordinary displacement analysis; adopt a new patent-collateral holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A security interest that does not transfer patent ownership need not be recorded with the Patent and Trademark Office merely to obtain priority over a bankruptcy trustee exercising hypothetical lien-creditor powers, where the interest is otherwise perfected under applicable state commercial law. Keeping those categories distinct avoids inventing a federal filing obligation from the general federal character of the property. Section 261’s ownership-transfer language and the trustee’s lien-creditor status provide independent textual grounds. Waterman’s legal distinction between a transfer of title and a lesser interest is applied, not overruled; its title-transferring mortgage is materially different from this security interest. The trustee is also not a subsequent purchaser or mortgagee within the recording protection. Historical legal meaning assists the text and carries no independent power to create federal perfection requirements. State commercial law therefore operates alongside patent law without changing the patent monopoly.

The federal court must adjudicate the continuance claim under the due-process rule against arbitrary denial of a meaningful opportunity to present a defense, considering the actual trial circumstances and any lawful remaining habeas restriction. The state appellate court did not adjudicate that federal merits claim, so §2254(d) does not attach merely to its procedural ruling. No release is ordered before violation and entitlement to relief are determined.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Adequacy requires a defensible application of the rule

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the adequacy issue before reaching the defense claim or an excuse for a valid default.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these written-motion and affidavit omissions adequately bar review on this record.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing default framework and adopt a narrow as-applied adequacy holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A state’s legitimate written-motion and affidavit requirements do not supply an adequate ground barring federal review when their application to an unexpected midtrial witness disappearance is exorbitant in the particular circumstances, the material reasons and anticipated testimony are substantially before the court, and the actual denial rests on other considerations. This is an exceptional application ruling, not a general dispensation from procedural compliance.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not treat every oral request as sufficient or every alternative trial reason as waiver of a formal requirement. The record must establish the exceptional mismatch.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Procedural rules serve decisions, including decisions refusing delay. Their general legitimacy does not explain why an additional piece of paper was essential after an unforeseen absence where the court already possessed the substance needed to consider the request. The State’s strongest point is that diligence and likely availability matter; those questions should be assessed against the actual record. The appropriate disposition permits federal examination without predetermining whether denying the requested delay violated the Constitution. The adequate-and-independent-ground doctrine carries decisive weight, including its requirement of a legitimate application, not only a facially legitimate rule. This is an as-applied exception to enforcement of an exorbitant formal demand, not replacement of Coleman or a free-floating hardship balance. Lee’s sworn explanation, subpoenas, prior disclosures, and short requested delay substantially supplied the information the rules seek; requiring a contemporaneous sworn account of an unexplained disappearance would demand what the emergency made unavailable. Because the state ground is inadequate, cause and prejudice are not a substitute threshold he must additionally satisfy. The remaining merits and relief inquiry is separate and must be resolved under its own legal burdens.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The due-process claim requires adjudication of the trial circumstances and disputed significance of the missing witnesses on a record the federal courts did not reach. The adequacy holding neither finds the alibi true nor resolves any distinct factual allegation about who caused the absence; those matters cannot be assumed in ordering ultimate habeas relief.

## 25. Kansas v. Crane

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-22, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Kansas Supreme Court’s complete-inability rule and remand for application of a serious functional impairment requirement; do not reinstate Crane’s commitment automatically.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require a finding of serious difficulty controlling the dangerous conduct, substantially connected to the qualifying mental impairment, under Kansas’s beyond-a-reasonable-doubt commitment burden. Determine whether the existing verdict and record lawfully establish that requirement; if not, the present order cannot stand, and any new proceeding requires independent lawful authority. Any properly preserved claim of retrospective punitive operation must be assessed under Hendricks and Seling on Crane’s actual program and circumstances.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A control test cannot cure independently unlawful punishment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Kansas judgment rests on the asserted need for complete inability to control behavior. That legal requirement is properly before the Court. Hendricks does not furnish a categorical alternative ground invalidating this later commitment without the required factual predicates.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether due process requires complete inability to control conduct and, if not, what functional impairment must distinguish this commitment from detention for ordinary criminal propensity.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Distinguish Hendricks’s as-applied punishment holding; propose a substantive due-process rule requiring serious impairment connected to the dangerous conduct, without requiring total incapacity. Apply Seling’s punishment framework only to an actually presented operational claim.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Due process does not require proof that a person lacks all control. It requires a serious present functional impairment producing serious difficulty controlling the dangerous conduct and substantially connected to the demonstrated danger that authorizes this form of civil custody. A diagnosis or a prediction of recidivism alone is insufficient. Kansas’s statutory beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden remains controlling; no lesser constitutional minimum reduces it. A verdict framed solely in terms of statutory diagnosis and likely repetition cannot be treated as necessarily resolving the required functional impairment.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule reaches the lack-of-control question in otherwise lawful civil commitment and is not confined to one diagnostic label. It neither validates punitive retrospective custody nor imports a complete treatment, review, or alternative-placement code into this grant.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Complete incapacity is an unrealistic boundary: serious impairment can make dangerous conduct exceptionally difficult to control while leaving some ability to choose. Eliminating impairment altogether would authorize confinement for predicted criminal propensity after punishment, which does not establish an independent civil basis. A functional standard is therefore decisive, grounded in liberty’s distinction between civil incapacity and ordinary culpable choice; clinical evidence informs the legal finding without making diagnosis conclusive. Hendricks is reaffirmed for its actual punishment holding and distinguished because its initial program facts are not findings about Crane. Seling requires the same separation of lawful civil purpose, actual operation, and retrospective effect. The State must establish the newly articulated control requirement under its own greater statutory burden. This answers the constitutional question the Kansas court used without either blessing the program generally or inventing a broader commitment regime.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The precise effect of Crane’s verdict and evidence requires application below. A facial redesign of civil commitment, new periodic-review procedures, and treatment or alternative-placement requirements not presented by the grant are not adjudicated; a preserved punishment challenge requires Crane-specific operational facts.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the appellate disposition on the questions presented: require rule-of-reason treatment of the challenged tie, preserve reassignment and vacatur of the remedy, and decline a categorical rule requiring all liability findings to be vacated.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** On remand, require the new judge to revisit any retained finding whose material factual or credibility basis cannot be separated from the impartiality violation. Preserve genuinely independent determinations and conduct new lawful remedial proceedings. No additional liability ruling on unpresented antitrust components is proposed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Evaluate the actual competitive consequences of integration

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved tying question without treating every monopolization finding as reopened.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this software combination warrants per se condemnation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the limits on categorical antitrust analysis; propose a narrow software-combination application.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The challenged integration of browser and operating-system functions must be evaluated under the rule of reason where the record presents material competitive and product-integration questions not reliably resolved by the existing per se tying category. The inquiry must examine product demand, coercion, power, foreclosure, and supported efficiencies. Integration is neither automatic justification nor immunity.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns this category and record; it does not invalidate all per se tying rules or resolve every exclusionary practice in the case.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The decisive concern is the reliability of the categorical inference. A product combination may change functionality while distribution restrictions may independently suppress rivals. An inquiry capable of distinguishing those effects provides a better legal basis for liability than either immediate condemnation or deference to the producer’s label. The government retains a full opportunity to prove anticompetitive harm, and Microsoft must substantiate the explanations material to its defense. Stone distinguishes Jefferson Parish’s per se inference for the product integration actually presented, rather than overruling tying law. California Dental’s insistence on an economically reliable basis for abbreviated condemnation is reaffirmed. The statutory competitive inquiry carries decisive weight: plaintiffs must establish anticompetitive effect, supported efficiencies must be considered, and any renewed condemnation must explain the resulting comparison. This defined exception to per se treatment is justified by the unreliable inference for the integrated platform, not by a general judicial preference for balancing.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unpresented monopoly-maintenance and attempted-monopolization issues are not separately decided.

### Issue 2: Correct the appearance violation without inventing a universal vacatur rule

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Treat the secret interviews under the ordinary objective standard, then examine the dependence of each challenged component.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the violation necessarily requires vacating all liability as well as the remedy.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply §455(a), Liteky’s distinction between ordinary adjudication and extrajudicial conduct, and Liljeberg’s established vacatur factors.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Undisclosed press contacts during pending litigation that reasonably call impartiality into question require disqualification without proof of actual bias. The consequences for prior civil judgments require a remedial inquiry into the violation and the affected determinations. A new judge must reconsider findings materially dependent on compromised factfinding or discretionary judgment; independently sustainable determinations need not be erased solely because a violation occurred.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Independent legal review cannot be used to declare a genuinely tainted credibility judgment harmless by assumption. The decision supplies neither automatic total vacatur nor a blanket preservation rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Public confidence depends on impartial adjudication, including the opportunity to expose disqualifying conduct while the case is pending. An embargo on publication does not remove that concern. Still, the remedy should identify what must be redone to restore lawful adjudication. Reassignment and a new remedy are necessary here; broader liability reconsideration follows where the record cannot support meaningful separation. This approach protects both the litigants’ right to an impartial tribunal and the validity of determinations shown to have an independent foundation. Section 455(a) supplies disqualification; Liljeberg supplies the remedial factors of injustice to these parties, injustice in other cases, and public confidence. Stone applies that existing multifactor inquiry rather than inventing a solely factual-taint test. Liteky’s distinction between ordinary judicial rulings and disqualifying extrajudicial conduct reinforces the violation. Remedial factors are decisive for scope: secret interviews threaten confidence, but need not erase independently reviewable determinations. Reassignment and a fresh remedy are required; retained liability must be reconsidered wherever meaningful independence cannot be established.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The effect of a particular disputed liability finding remains subject to the directed remand inquiry.

Reading maintenance to include every momentary exchange would erase a limiting term and turn ordinary participation into institutional record custody. Preserving the later-retention question respects the same distinction in the other direction. The statutory maintenance requirement carries decisive weight; the classroom’s function confirms what temporary handling means in this context. Student grading is not recordkeeping on behalf of the institution merely because a teacher directs it. Stone assumes a cause of action only for this adverse merits resolution because the federal claim is substantial enough to support jurisdiction and no affirmative relief requires recognizing an action. ## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Equal treatment within an extended former-resident electorate

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The challenged exclusion personally denies the requested ballot, and relief through New York’s existing electors can redress it. The recurring prospective eligibility claim survives the completed election.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether living in Puerto Rico alone sufficiently justifies excluding a former New York resident from the absentee eligibility retained by comparable former residents abroad.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend Anderson–Burdick electoral equal protection to New York’s selective preservation of a former-resident presidential franchise. This is a proposed extension of the protected electoral interest, not an already decided entitlement of every nonresident. Apply the severe-burden branch once the claimant establishes comparable former-resident eligibility and exclusion from presidential voting. Reaffirm the State-based electoral allocation; distinguish McDonald, Saenz and Harris v. Rosario at their respective holdings.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A former resident otherwise qualified for New York’s extended presidential electorate may challenge the destination-based exclusion under electoral equal protection. The claimant must establish that eligibility comparison and the complete presidential-voting exclusion. Once established, New York must justify this severe restriction by a compelling electoral interest and narrow tailoring. The interests advanced for denying Romeu a ballot because he resides and votes locally in Puerto Rico do not satisfy that standard. Territorial residence acquires no suspect status, and Congress’s minimum-coverage definition remains undecided.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The extension reaches this State’s existing former-resident presidential program and the demonstrated complete exclusion of an otherwise comparable applicant. It does not establish permanent state membership for all emigrants, require every voting convenience, or subject every residence rule to strict scrutiny. Neutral eligibility, authentication and prevention of a second presidential ballot remain permissible.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The decisive method is Anderson–Burdick’s severe-burden scrutiny, following an express extension of the electoral interest it protects. Article II’s structure limits the remedy to New York’s electors; it does not itself decide the equality claim. Stone rejects the premise that the State may answer every exclusion within an existing former-resident electorate merely by observing that it need never have retained those voters. Equal protection reaches the selective distribution of that electoral opportunity. Comparable former residence supplies the connection to New York; the challenged destination rule supplies the distinction. McDonald is distinguishable because its record did not establish exclusion from voting itself. Here the claim concerns loss of presidential participation, not a preferred absentee procedure. Local voting and election of a Resident Commissioner are real political participation, but concern different offices. Preventing duplicate presidential votes can be achieved by a rule directed to that duplication. An otherwise eligible former resident in Puerto Rico has no second presidential ballot to surrender. Ordinary identification and prior-residence requirements remain, so the geographic exclusion is not a narrowly tailored response to registration administration. Incremental protection of citizens lacking any domestic vote is a legitimate legislative aim, but under the selected severe-burden standard it does not justify denying this distinct presidential opportunity to a comparable former resident. The concern about unequal rights among Puerto Rico’s residents, including advantages associated with the means to establish mainland residence, is substantial. Nevertheless, preserving the existing former-state connection while extending the same eligibility to Romeu is narrower than either allocating territorial electors or creating a franchise for persons with no such connection. Those broader questions are reserved. Stone does not also announce that the distinction fails rational-basis review: under that different test the challenger ordinarily bears the burden and conceivable legitimate purposes suffice. The government’s justification burden here follows the expressly identified electoral rule, not a heightened form of ordinary territorial-classification review. Harris v. Rosario’s congressional benefits holding therefore need not be overruled. Prospective inclusion protects existing voters’ reliance and requires no reopening of completed elections. UOCAVA’s overseas minimum prevents New York from equalizing by withdrawing the federally protected ballots; it does not forbid broader state eligibility. An injunction against the responsible state official, with ordinary eligibility facts determined below, supplies complete relief without invalidating the federal definition or ordering any other State to change its program.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Territorial statehood, congressional representation, a territorial electoral allocation and any future amendment of the extended absentee scheme consistent with federal requirements are outside the question. The alternative travel, Article IV privileges-and-immunities and due-process theories need not be reached because equality supplies complete relief from the challenged exclusion.
