## 111. BE&K Construction Co. v. NLRB

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment enforcing liability under the Board’s unsuccessful-plus-retaliatory-purpose standard and remand.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the dependent litigation-based order to the extent it rests on that standard. Permit only a properly preserved, legally sufficient reconsideration under a narrower rule. Do not declare all underlying claims reasonable, award BE&K damages, or disturb independent valid court sanctions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Losing a reasonable lawsuit does not by itself forfeit petition protection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The legal standard is sufficiently clear for review without reconstructing every claim in the earlier litigation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether failure plus retaliatory purpose is enough to impose an unfair-labor-practice sanction on reasonably based litigation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reconcile Bill Johnson’s Restaurants with petition protection and the NLRA’s unfair-labor-practice provisions. Treat its discussion of completed unsuccessful suits as insufficient to decide this reasonable-suit question; use constitutional avoidance to reject the Board’s broader construction without importing all antitrust sham doctrine.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The NLRA does not authorize liability for a reasonably based lawsuit merely because it failed and was pursued with a retaliatory purpose. An unsuccessful result is not objective baselessness, and the Board’s contrary standard cannot sustain this order.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding leaves genuinely sham proceedings, independently unlawful objectives, applicable litigation sanctions, and materially different abuse patterns to their own rules.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Judicial resolution often identifies which reasonable legal position prevails only after the parties incur costs. If loss and hostility alone made suit unlawful, the right to present an arguable claim would depend on success and the claimant’s goodwill toward the opponent. The strongest labor concern is the use of legal costs to suppress organizing. That concern justifies regulation of abuse supported by the required objective foundation; it does not eliminate the petitioning interest in a claim with a reasonable basis. The statute can be read coherently without imposing the Board’s entire class of liability, so a broader constitutional ruling is unnecessary. The decisive ground is the limiting construction of the statute; the serious petitioning burden explains why that construction is required. The cease-and-desist, notice and fee obligations depend on the rejected standard and must fall to that extent. Whether a particular claim lacked an objective basis or sought an independently unlawful end was not established by the mere fact of defeat, so any permitted reconsideration must make those distinct determinations.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The precise standard for serial abuse, fraud, and an independently unlawful objective is not resolved by this record.


## 114. Trans Union Corp. v. Federal Trade Commission +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the D.C. Circuit’s denial of relief from the FTC order on the granted First Amendment challenge.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the restriction on the identified nonconsensual sale of credit-derived targeted lists. Do not expand the order to independently collected public information, consented uses, or statutory exceptions, and do not adjudicate unpreserved alternative claims.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Commercial use of confidential financial records receives scrutiny and can be limited

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The actual activity is the sale of consumer targeting information drawn from confidential credit records. The corporation’s status neither eliminates speech review nor settles its outcome.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the statutory use restriction satisfies the governing commercial-speech standard in this application.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply U.S. West, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission and the existing commercial-speech framework, extending the supported confidentiality analysis to this external credit-data sale.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The FCRA restriction on these nonconsensual credit-derived marketing lists satisfies the First Amendment because it directly protects confidential financial attributes and reasonably limits their repurposing while retaining consent and defined legitimate credit uses.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding is tied to source, informational content, and use. It does not create a general ban on commercial databases or reduce editorial reporting to commercial speech because its publisher earns revenue.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The protected interest is control against an unrelated commercial disclosure of information entrusted to a credit system. A list of people selected for a financial characteristic can disclose that characteristic without reproducing the underlying account. Restricting that sale directly addresses the asserted privacy harm. Written consent provides a route for the consumer to authorize the use; a firm-credit-offer exception has a distinct credit function and statutory conditions, so it does not necessarily reproduce the same uncontrolled repurposing. The government must establish those distinctions in operation, which the reviewed record supports. A less burdensome opt-out is not constitutionally required merely because it increases the seller’s response rate. Central Hudson’s commercial-speech standard, as applied in U.S. West, carries decisive weight. The protected product communicates consumer attributes for commercial targeting, so its classification does not rest merely on Trans Union’s corporate identity or receipt of payment. The statutory exceptions preserve consented or credit-related uses without destroying the restriction’s fit to unrelated marketing; reasonably fitted affirmative consent does not become unconstitutional because opt-out would be cheaper. This is an extension of the privacy application, not a general information-ownership rule. Lorillard’s actual-fit requirement is reaffirmed: the distinction is that these limits directly restrict the nonconsensual disclosure causing the privacy injury while preserving authorized uses, whereas an expansive geographic advertising ban burdened adult communication far beyond the supported youth-protection fit.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Public-concern publication, independently acquired information, final data ownership, and unpreserved challenges to other agency treatment remain outside the holding.


## 115. United States v. Ruiz

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the vacatur and limited sentencing remand on the ground that the demanded blanket waiver was an unconstitutional condition, while narrowing the Ninth Circuit’s disclosure rule to information materially important to investigation, advice or the plea decision.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** On remand the sentencing court must address the loss of the fast-track recommendation caused by refusal of the unlawful condition and exercise its existing remedial and sentencing authority on that corrected basis. It may not treat refusal of that waiver as a lawful reason to deny consideration. No automatic two-level reduction, compelled plea agreement, plea withdrawal or damages follows; the actual effect and lawful corrective relief must be determined. Ruiz’s conviction remains undisturbed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A sentence appeal requires a connected legal injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The judge understood departure discretion; that alternative jurisdictional theory is unavailable on the stated record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §3742 permits examination of the claimed unconstitutional plea condition as a source of sentence illegality.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the statutory legal-error route without creating general appellate review of discretionary leniency.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 3742(a)(1) permits review of the asserted unlawful sentencing condition. The record’s withdrawal of the fast-track offer because Ruiz rejected the waiver supplies the relevant connection to her requested sentencing relief; a merely discretionary refusal to depart would not. The constitutional condition must be adjudicated even though the judge understood ordinary departure discretion.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not make every declined bargain or discretionary refusal appealable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The relevant injury is denial of the bargaining opportunity on an unlawful condition, not an established suppression of a particular item or an entitlement to a particular sentence. Ruiz refused the waiver, the prosecutor withdrew the offer for that reason, and she sought the associated sentencing treatment after her unconditional plea. That sequence connects the legal objection to the sentence sufficiently for §3742 review. It does not establish what recommendation would have been made without the condition or compel the sentencing judge to accept it. The statutory distinction between legal error and discretionary leniency is decisive; the remand applies the former without authorizing general review of the latter.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general expansion of statutory sentence jurisdiction or guaranteed plea bargain is adopted.

### Issue 2: Material favorable information must be usable before the binding choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The existing trial-disclosure cases do not resolve this pre-plea timing and waiver question. A prospective duty and relief from a completed plea or sentence require distinct inquiries.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a blanket waiver can authorize withholding known favorable information materially important to advice and a plea decision.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend the favorable-evidence principle to the constitutional function of an informed, counseled plea, while preserving source-specific limits and retrospective remedy requirements.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Before obtaining a binding plea, the prosecution must make timely disclosure of favorable information known to its case team that can materially assist investigation, advice, or the plea decision, including materially significant impeachment and affirmative-defense evidence. A blanket prospective waiver cannot excuse that duty.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This is not complete discovery, a guarantee of perfect knowledge, or a requirement to finish all trial preparation before any negotiation. Genuine privilege and safety concerns require protective arrangements compatible with meaningful decision-making.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A witness’s known inability to support the accusation can matter before trial precisely because it changes whether counsel should recommend surrendering trial. Categorizing that information as impeachment does not make its significance disappear at bargaining. The same is true of a supported affirmative defense. The government’s resource and witness concerns are substantial and justify tailored protection, sequencing, and judicial review; they do not justify a categorical entitlement to conceal material favorable information. Prospective disclosure is measured by practical usefulness, while relief from a completed plea or sentence requires the governing causal and materiality showing. United States v. Mezzanatto remains intact. Its defendant knowingly accepted a defined consequence for using his own inconsistent account at trial. It did not approve every prospective waiver of every institutional protection. A blanket surrender of unknown favorable information materially necessary to decide whether to plead differs because the withheld information can prevent informed assessment of the very decision demanded. This distinction preserves limited, knowing waivers and rejects categorical invalidity merely because an agreement is negotiated under pressure. The decisive principle is meaningful exercise of the informed, counseled plea choice; the practical operation of the information duty explains its timing. The agreement’s promise to disclose evidence establishing factual innocence is insufficient because known information undermining the central witness or supporting an affirmative defense can materially affect competent advice without conclusively proving innocence. The rule concerns material usefulness of information already known after reasonable team inquiry, not a duty to create new evidence. The actual waiver is invalid to that defined extent; no unknown suppressed item is presumed and no completed plea is set aside without its own causal showing.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The record establishes no specific suppressed item, and the Court does not decide that Ruiz’s plea was involuntary or that every limited informed waiver is invalid.


## 116. Kirk v. Louisiana

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the state appellate judgment and remand for the omitted home-entry analysis.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require a record-based determination of exigent circumstances and, if needed, the causal and independent-source issues for the challenged evidence. Order no acquittal, automatic suppression of every item, reinstatement of the conviction, or new factual finding from this Court.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Probable cause does not supply permission to enter a home

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The appellate court’s legal premise is deficient even if an exception might ultimately sustain the entry.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether probable cause alone can support the warrantless entry and conviction-sustaining judgment.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Payton v. New York without creating a new exception or prejudging the unresolved factual ground.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Nonconsensual entry into a home requires a warrant or a valid exception in addition to probable cause. A judgment that omits that inquiry cannot be sustained on probable cause alone.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding leaves ordinary exigent-circumstances doctrine and genuinely independent evidence rules intact.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Probable cause answers whether officers have an adequate basis to believe a crime or evidence exists. A home-entry rule answers whether they may cross the protected boundary without judicial authorization. Treating the first answer as the second erases the warrant requirement where it has its central operation. The destruction concern must be evaluated under the actual circumstances, and the later warrant must be assessed for genuine independence if the first entry was unlawful. Those are necessary lower-court determinations, not reasons to retain an incorrect categorical rule. Payton’s categorical home-entry boundary is decisive; the fact-dependent exigency exception remains real but must be established on the actual record. McArthur is distinguished because restraining entry temporarily to preserve evidence while promptly obtaining a warrant is not itself authorization to enter and search. The narrow legal correction is justified by the state court’s omission of the exception question, which prevents this Court from replacing it with an invented factual finding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The existence of exigency, the independence of the later warrant, and ultimate suppression and conviction consequences remain unresolved.
