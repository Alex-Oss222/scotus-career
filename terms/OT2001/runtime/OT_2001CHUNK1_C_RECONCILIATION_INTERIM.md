## 10. J. E. M. Ag Supply v. Pioneer Hi-Bred — final non-Stone conclusions for later freeze

**Disposition.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg affirm rejection of the categorical statutory-ineligibility defense. Stevens and Breyer reverse and remand because the plant-specific enactments supply the exclusive statutory regime for the plant varieties they cover. Breyer's provisional affirmance changes after full comparator and entering-law review. The other seven dispositions remain unchanged.

### Governing propositions and joins

| Proposition or ground | Full support at this level | Contrary position or limit |
|---|---|---|
| A newly developed plant is not categorically excluded from section 101 because the PPA or PVPA also covers its subject matter; an applicant must satisfy the utility-patent statute's independent requirements. | O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Stevens and Breyer reject this result for the covered plant categories, preserving Chakrabarty's microorganism holding. |
| The PPA's limited reproduction right and relaxed description rule, and the PVPA's distinct eligibility requirements and exceptions, regulate the rights each statute supplies; they do not create a general immunity from an independently valid utility patent. Different eligibility and disclosure requirements leave work for both regimes. | Same six | Stevens and Breyer regard this as bypassing the particular congressional allocation, including saved-seed and research protections. |
| Neither plant-specific statute expressly makes its protection exclusive. Their enactment and the PPA's recodification do not establish an irreconcilable conflict requiring exclusion from section 101. | Same six | The dissenters treat the PPA's actual amendment of the predecessor patent provision as deliberate specific treatment, not merely an implied repeal of unrelated earlier legislation. |
| The present judgment resolves the categorical defense; ordinary novelty, nonobviousness, disclosure and other patent requirements remain independently operative. | All eight accept the distinction, but only the six apply it to sustain utility-patent eligibility for these plants. | This shared limit is not a vote for the six-Justice eligibility proposition. |

**Affirmance explanation.** Chakrabarty's broad reading of manufacture and composition of matter rejects a living-subject exclusion and treats human invention, rather than life alone, as material. The PPA accommodated obstacles to plant protection through a limited right and a relaxed description requirement. It does not say that an invention meeting the general statute's stricter conditions loses that protection. The PVPA likewise confers a distinct right under distinct eligibility and disclosure requirements. Its own saved-seed and research limits remain effective within that regime; they do not purport to negate every separate federal patent right. The strongest contrary argument is that overlap reduces the practical value of those limits. The six answer that different proof requirements and different exclusionary rights permit both enactments to operate, and overlap does not itself establish exclusivity. This reasoning does not depend on treating administrative practice or congressional silence as binding statutory interpretation.

**Dissent explanation.** Stevens and Breyer accept that Chakrabarty permits a human-made microorganism outside the specialized plant categories. They reject extending that answer to the very subject Congress addressed separately. The 1930 PPA expressly amended the general patent provision, imposed the asexual-reproduction condition, and restricted the right to that form of reproduction; the PVPA later supplied protection for seed reproduction with saved-seed and research exceptions. On their reading, the general utility route would bypass those particular decisions. Recodification did not undo them. The different eligibility thresholds do not explain why Congress would protect saved seed and research only for varieties lacking the utility statute's additional qualifications. Their conclusion concerns the statutory plant categories and does not exclude all living inventions or independently qualifying processes.

**Affirmance grounds.** The government demonstrated a regular route directed to the identified prisoner at the correct institution, including more than postal delivery to a government address. Mullane and Mennonite require reasonable efforts to communicate, not proof that every effort succeeded. Missing old logs do not establish an unreasonable procedure. A feasible improvement does not, by itself, make the earlier procedure constitutionally inadequate. The contrary argument correctly emphasizes custody, but the four consider the described institutional route sufficient without a universally required verification mechanism. The other attempted addresses supply no independent cure for a deficient prison route.

**Vacatur grounds.** The confined owner cannot supervise or replace the government's intermediary; the government controls the relevant location and the final transmission. Mullane's practical inquiry asks whether the route to that person was reasonably reliable in light of feasible alternatives. The evidence of delivery between employees does not adequately establish the final inmate-directed safeguard. The four require a constitutionally sufficient custodial route rather than a receipt guarantee. Their position does not depend merely on a denial of receipt or destroyed records. They would set aside the judgment sustaining this notice and return the matter for the proper consequences of the notice defect, not treat it as proof of ownership or automatic entitlement to the property.

## 16. Chao v. Mallard Bay Drilling — final non-Stone conclusions for later freeze

**Disposition.** All eight Associates reverse the Fifth Circuit and remand for proceedings consistent with rejection of both the displacement and workplace objections. All eight join the same grounds at the same level of generality. The historical participating Associates reached the same result; Scalia's added simulated participation rests on the authorized baseline and his own sources.

### Complete propositions, application and limits

1. Under section 4(b)(1), another federal agency displaces OSHA only through actual exercise of its statutory power to prescribe or enforce occupational-safety or health standards for the relevant working conditions. Mere possession of jurisdiction is insufficient. The exercise may consist of pertinent specific regulations or actual comprehensive regulation of the relevant category; word-for-word duplication of an OSHA rule is unnecessary. Minimal regulation of some unrelated vessel matters does not establish whole-vessel or industry-wide displacement.

2. The Coast Guard had not actually regulated the cited evacuation and emergency-response working conditions of this uninspected inland drilling barge through pertinent rules or a comprehensive regime. General maritime responsibility and the casualty investigation do not supply that missing exercise. The statute therefore leaves OSHA authority over these conditions. The result does not require choosing between the hazard-based and area-based descriptions of working conditions: no comprehensive or pertinent actual regulation has been established under either description. This is a bounded resolution on the supplied regulatory setting, not a holding that all uninspected-vessel conditions belong to OSHA.

3. The employment aboard the barge within Louisiana occurred in a workplace in a State under section 4(a). Anchoring in navigable waters does not remove it from the statute's geographic coverage; no general maritime exception appears in that text. The geographic question is independent of the separate other-agency displacement provision.

The principal contrary argument seeks coherent maritime control from the Coast Guard's broad responsibilities. It cannot replace Congress's actual-exercise requirement or convert unrelated regulation into comprehensive occupational-safety coverage. Recognizing genuine comprehensive regulation preserves coordination without erasing the statutory condition. The receiving court implements the rejection of both objections in the actual administrative posture. The citation merits were not challenged; the remand supplies no reopening of those merits or newly prescribed hearing.
