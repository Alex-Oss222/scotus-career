# Romeu v. Cohen ? neutral reach and remedy compatibility question

## Existing record and scope

The claim concerns recurring prospective eligibility for New York's presidential absentee ballot. Romeu formerly resided in New York and later resided and registered to vote in Puerto Rico. The challenged federal and state classifications are distinct. UOCAVA requires absentee eligibility for its qualifying overseas class but does not prohibit broader state eligibility. New York Election Law section 11-200(1) additionally excludes the relevant domestic territorial residence, registration, and voting. The case seeks participation through New York's existing electors; the completed 2000 election is not reopened. No new factual or procedural premise is proposed.

## Alternative formulation for compatibility review

Consider a judgment reversing dismissal of the state equal-protection claim and remanding for prospective relief against the responsible New York official. The rule would extend Anderson?Burdick review to a former resident who establishes ordinary eligibility comparable to the former residents retained in New York's extended presidential electorate and complete exclusion from presidential voting. That demonstrated exclusion triggers the severe-burden branch: a compelling electoral interest and narrow tailoring. Territorial residence itself does not become a suspect classification. State relief would prohibit denial of a presidential absentee ballot solely because the otherwise qualified applicant resides and votes locally in Puerto Rico, severing the challenged outside-the-United-States and territorial-registration/voting qualifications as applied. Ordinary former-residence, age, registration, authentication, and no-second-presidential-vote requirements remain enforceable. Ordinary eligibility facts are determined below.

That complete state remedy supplies the requested ballot opportunity while retaining the federal minimum. On that formulation, assess whether the federal equality claim must still receive an affirmative constitutional disposition or instead may be left undecided because no additional relief is necessary. Assess separately whether alternative independent travel, Article IV privileges-and-immunities, and due-process theories must be decided or may be left unadjudicated because the state equality ground completely supports the requested prospective relief. The formulation does not declare the federal definition valid, invalidate it, award territorial electors, or establish permanent former-state electoral membership.

## Questions for the scoped audit

Using each Justice's own date-eligible grounds, assess the reach, remedy, and opinion-join consequences of the formulation. Distinguish willingness to join sufficient state relief from continuing a separate published position that would also invalidate the federal exclusion or reject an independent entitlement. Preserve any actual refusal to join; do not infer agreement from availability of fewer holdings. If a judgment component still requires resolution, identify the precise procedural disposition that the record and commitments support. Explain any change to the existing reconciled reach/remedy commitment and whether the resulting opinion still resolves every issue necessary to the relief ordered. No author or assignment is proposed.
