## Controlling scope of the handoff

1. **Review and concrete effect.** Section 1292(a)(1) reaches the actual refusal to dissolve, including its alternative supporting ground. The contingent label does not change that operation. The challenged March framework governed loss of enforceable protections and continuing obligations. June's implementation alone establishes neither acquiescence nor mootness. This conclusion does not assert that no later filing exists, assume a separate June appeal, or guarantee a particular reopening procedure.

2. **Distinct statutory predicates.** The elapsed-period route under section 3626(b)(1)(A)(iii) remains separate from (b)(2)'s movant-established absence of substantive original need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings. Sufficient original findings require no formula and defeat that latter predicate, not necessarily the former route. Both routes retain amended (b)(3)'s written, record-based current-and-ongoing-violation, necessity, no-broader-than-necessary, narrowness and least-intrusive-means requirements. The earlier disjunction is not substituted. The preserved question about a violation excluded solely by the conjunction is not decided without its factual predicate.

3. **Constitutional and estoppel grounds.** Enacted prospective standards leave actual legal and factual adjudication to the courts. The consent decree supplies no perpetual entitlement to unchanged future enforcement. The court's meaningful-adjudication and necessary-proof obligations survive; the Fifth Circuit's unqualified due-process language is not adopted. Nor is a prospective injunction deprived of every form of finality simply because it can be modified. Historical Plaut descriptions are not imported over the actual entering law. Consent and compromise alone do not estop the statutory motion; no independent promise, contract, accrued claim or statutory waiver is invented or categorically foreclosed.

4. **What the findings defect establishes.** The identified defect was the missing connection between retained obligations and statutory remedial criteria, not merely absent statutory phrases. Findings of injury do not automatically preserve all old duties; missing remedial findings do not negate injury or command wholesale termination without the appropriate assessment. A real reason may support several duties without redundant headings. A remedy can exceed a minimum conduct command when the complete statutory findings justify it. The reported hearing limit alone does not establish denial of material necessary proof.

5. **Medical cross-appeal.** March reports the prisoners' challenge to the adverse medical and psychiatric finding but supplies no separate reasoned factual resolution. Affirming the selected legal judgment does not manufacture such a resolution, endorse every trial finding, extinguish a still-proper issue by inference, or find adequate care everywhere. Estelle and Farmer retain their actual-knowledge, ordinary-circumstantial-proof, unreasonable-disregard and reasonable-response distinctions. No new statewide medical-liability finding is made. Any properly preserved issue actually remaining for decision must travel through an available ordinary procedure; this is not a finding that a specific appeal or motion remains pending. An independent adjudication of the medical evidence or reversal of June would require the additional source and scope inquiry identified in the independent freeze.

6. **Completed implementation and mandate.** June's action is procedural history, not an additional granted merits judgment. There is no duplicate findings command, new ninety-day clock, invented stay, automatic restoration of old obligations, revival of the withdrawn Rule 60(b)(5) motion, approval of a proposed settlement or future end date, mandatory new evidentiary hearing, or closure of the class action. Section 3626(a)'s new-relief conditions remain distinct from (b)'s retention inquiry and (e)'s stay rules. Any further work depends on an actual remaining issue and lawful procedural authority.
