# OT2001 chunk 8 scoped approved supplement

## 112. Harris v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the sentence-sustaining judgment, vacate the seven-year firearm sentence, and remand for lawful resentencing. Preserve the basic firearm conviction. Relief is warranted under Rule 52(b) even assuming forfeiture of the constitutional theory; no structural-error rule is necessary.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Do not treat the uncharged preponderance finding as authority for a mandatory seven-year floor. Begin with the five-year statutory minimum and its applicable guideline consequence; a different sentence requires an independently lawful basis and reasons. The judge’s statement that seven years might again be appropriate is not an already imposed alternative sentence. Any further aggravated proceeding requires independent charging, waiver, procedural and double-jeopardy authority; this mandate supplies none automatically and promises no particular new term.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The amended statute uses a sentencing condition

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory question should be answered before using avoidance as a substitute for a constitutional decision.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the amended text makes brandishing a separate statutory element.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Conclude that the current structure treats brandishing as a mandatory sentencing condition, while distinguishing the older statute in Castillo v. United States.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The amended §924(c)(1)(A) sets out the basic offense and then assigns graduated mandatory minimums, including the brandishing condition. That construction does not determine whether the Constitution requires treating the condition as an element.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion addresses this amended structure, not the former firearm-type provision or every aggravated criminal statute.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The provision’s operative division and graduated sentencing language are substantial evidence of Congress’s chosen mechanism. Brandishing describes culpable conduct and creates serious additional punishment, which makes the alternative reading plausible, but the better statutory account is a mandatory sentencing condition. Avoidance cannot be used to claim the constitutional issue has disappeared while retaining that condition’s full effect. Text and statutory arrangement decide this construction; avoidance does not license rewriting an otherwise ascertainable sentencing provision solely to evade the constitutional question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The distinct discharge condition is not independently adjudicated on an absent record.

### Issue 2: Compulsory additional punishment requires element protections

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The issue lies beyond Apprendi’s holding and squarely implicates McMillan v. Pennsylvania.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a present-offense fact that raises the legally mandatory minimum may be found under ordinary sentencing procedures.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend the jury and reasonable-doubt rule to mandatory-minimum aggravators and expressly overrule McMillan v. Pennsylvania to that extent.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A present-offense fact necessary to raise the mandatory minimum must receive the charging protections applicable to the prosecution and be validly admitted or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the proper guilt factfinder. Judicial sentencing labels do not excuse those requirements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns facts changing the legally required punishment range, not facts used to select a discretionary sentence within the range already authorized. This case supplies no constitutional prior-conviction exception.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Before the brandishing finding, the law permits a sentence beginning at five years. After it, the law forbids a sentence below seven. The finding therefore changes the lawful judgment the court may enter, even though the higher maximum remains available in both situations. The government’s reliance on possible punishment overlooks that mandatory change. The jury guarantee and reasonable-doubt standard constrain facts legally necessary to aggravated punishment, not only facts attached to one endpoint. Reliance on the previous rule warrants honest treatment of pending and final cases under their own doctrines, rather than continued deprivation here. A jury waiver does not transform an ordinary sentencing finding into a beyond-reasonable-doubt adjudication of the uncharged condition. The decisive constitutional method is the allocation of authority over facts that change the legally permitted range. Functional comparison of the two endpoints exposes the defect in McMillan’s distinction; general opposition to mandatory sentences does not supply the holding. This rule must govern beyond Harris because treating the same legal constraint differently by its label would preserve the evasion. Harris’s jury waiver changes the proper finder, not the required burden or the indictment’s scope.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Collateral retroactivity, every sentencing factor, and a wholesale redesign of the federal sentencing system are not decided.

### Issue 3: The error warrants correction even under the forfeiture standard

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory-element objection was made at sentencing; the constitutional extension was added on appeal. Assume without deciding that the latter was forfeited and apply Rule 52(b), placing the ordinary prejudice burden on Harris. This route resolves the sentence without inventing either an unrecorded constitutional objection or an intentional waiver.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether all four plain-error requirements warrant sentence relief notwithstanding the judge’s expressed view that seven years was appropriate.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Olano and Johnson, distinguish Johnson’s particular refusal of discretionary correction, and retain Neder’s separate rule for preserved error. Neither the new constitutional rule nor the indictment omission makes reversal automatic.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The use of the uncharged, preponderance-found aggravator is error under the constitutional rule adopted here, and Johnson makes that error plain on this pending direct review despite McMillan’s earlier contrary rule. Harris also carries his burden of showing an effect on substantial rights: the contested finding supplied the compulsory seven-year sentence and its guideline consequence, while the record supplies no independently selected alternative sentence. On this record, enforcing that additional mandatory punishment without the required adjudication seriously impairs the fairness and integrity of sentencing, warranting discretionary correction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This is a case-specific application of all four conditions. It neither presumes prejudice from every range error nor treats any constitutional sentencing error as structural, and it does not hold that seven years could never be imposed through lawful proceedings.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The actual sentencing record is decisive. Harris contested brandishing, the judge treated it as close under a preponderance standard, and the verdict on carrying did not resolve the distinct purpose-to-intimidate question beyond a reasonable doubt. The resulting minimum supplied the sentence under §2K2.4. The judge’s belief that seven years was appropriate is contrary evidence that must be taken seriously, but he declined to commit to that term without the enhancement; a possible future departure is not an unaffected alternative judgment. Taken together, the disputed aggravator, the proof standard actually used and the direct operation of the minimum establish outcome-affecting prejudice, rather than merely the abstract loss of discretion. This conclusion is an inference from this record, not a burden shifted to the Government to disprove prejudice. The fourth condition independently warrants correction because the judgment enforces additional compulsory imprisonment resting on a genuinely contested fact never adjudicated with the protections it requires. Johnson’s clear, essentially uncontested materiality setting is materially different. Neder supplies no automatic harmlessness answer: a carrying verdict and a close preponderance finding do not establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt, and clear-error affirmance is not the missing criminal adjudication. No appellate finding that Harris did or did not brandish is made. Deciding the error’s consequence now permits the ordinary sentencing court to perform the remaining lawful work without reopening the valid conviction or inventing authority for an enhancement trial.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the statutory objection alone also preserved the constitutional theory need not be decided because the more demanding forfeiture inquiry is satisfied. A genuinely independent lawful sentence, an admitted aggravator, other records and collateral retroactivity remain outside this holding.


## 113. Ring v. Arizona

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the ruling that judge-only death-eligibility factfinding is constitutional, vacate the appellate affirmance of the death sentence, and remand for the State’s actual harmless-error argument under the governing standard. Preserve the felony-murder conviction.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require an initial determination whether the State establishes harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt under the qualified Neder v. United States framework on the actual record. Arizona’s claim that pecuniary gain was implicit in the robbery verdict is not sufficient merely as a label; assess the elements, actual instructions and findings, and qualifying evidence without resolving new credibility disputes. If the State fails, vacate the death sentence and conduct only sentencing proceedings independently authorized by state law and consistent with constitutional and double-jeopardy limits. If the State carries the burden, a consistent sentence judgment may be reinstated. Create no new Arizona procedure, automatic enhancement trial, acquittal, or release.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Death eligibility is a legal authorization question

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The granted claim concerns the additional fact without which death cannot be imposed. It does not require resolving every aspect of capital selection.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether that eligibility fact must be determined by a jury beyond a reasonable doubt.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend the principle of Apprendi v. New Jersey to the expressly reserved capital setting and overrule Walton v. Arizona insofar as it allows a judge alone to establish the necessary aggravating fact.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A statutory aggravating fact necessary to authorize death must be validly admitted or found by a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Calling the fact a sentencing consideration cannot place it outside the constitutional guilt-finding protections.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs eligibility facts. It does not decide that every mitigating fact, moral evaluation, or final selection decision is an element, or apply the federal grand-jury guarantee to state prosecutions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The verdict authorizes the punishment attached to felony murder without additional findings. Arizona law requires a further aggravating fact before death becomes lawful, so the fact enlarges the punishment authority in the same constitutionally relevant way as the present-offense fact in Apprendi v. New Jersey. Judicial expertise and a desire for consistency do not supply a power to reassign a constitutional factfinding duty. Walton v. Arizona must be confronted, not pronounced implicitly dead: its classification is incompatible with this extension, and its reliance interest in a procedure cannot outweigh accurate observance of the jury guarantee in the pending case. The robbery verdict does not automatically substitute for an unasked aggravator question. Arizona’s preserved harmlessness argument must nevertheless be resolved under the governing error rule. Stone would not declare every omitted aggravator structural error. The appellate sentence affirmance therefore returns for a defined harmlessness inquiry: robbery and pecuniary-gain aggravation must be compared under their actual elements and verdict, with the qualified role of uncontested evidence and without a substitute appellate factfinding trial. This resolves the legal consequences while leaving the record application initially to the court that did not decide it under the correct constitutional rule. The decisive rule is the jury’s structural authority over eligibility facts. The remedial application extends Neder’s qualified actual-verdict inquiry to this omitted eligibility finding: the State may rely on supported inferences from the verdict and whole record, but not simply the reviewing court’s conviction that the aggravator was probably true. That limited extension is justified because it asks whether the proper factfinder’s actual adjudication leaves the omission harmless without making an appellate tribunal the initial capital-eligibility finder.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ultimate sentencer, complete weighing procedure, collateral retroactivity, and independently authorized renewed capital proceedings are outside the question presented. The State’s preserved harmlessness contention returns under the stated standard because its actual-record application was not decided below under this constitutional rule; no universal structural-error rule is adopted.
