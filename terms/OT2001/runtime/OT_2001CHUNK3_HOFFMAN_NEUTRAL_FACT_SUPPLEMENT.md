# Hoffman Plastic: neutral factual supplement

This supplement clarifies only the underlying hearing evidence and lower-adjudication posture.

At the June 14, 1993 compliance hearing, Castro acknowledged that he lacked work authorization and had obtained the Hoffman job using a friend's birth certificate. The published ALJ factual recital also reports his present-tense testimony about using that certificate and false identification to work in this country. The Board separately recounts his explanation that he borrowed the certificate to secure work. This provides evidence from which continued use may be inferred. The recital does not identify a postdischarge employer, a date on which a document was tendered after discharge, or a particular interim job obtained by presenting false documentation. The scope of that temporal inference is disputed: petitioner reads the testimony as admitting continued use, while respondent disputes that the evidence establishes additional false-document tenders to interim employers. Respondent's suggested alternative explanations for the interim work are possibilities, not agency findings. Sources: Pet. App. 79a-80a, 92a; petitioner reply pp. 14-15; respondent merits p. 41 & n.26.

The ALJ also described Castro's driver's license and social security card as apparently obtained through the certificate. That is the ALJ's expressed inference; the published recital does not reproduce a separate admission establishing that causal chain. Source: Pet. App. 92a.

The Board's finding that the fraudulent certificate was used to obtain employment was not excepted to, and no exception challenged receipt of the evidence. That observation does not specifically resolve whether the evidence establishes a separate false-document tender at a named interim employer. Source: Pet. App. 84a n.10.

The Board found reasonable mitigation efforts through work as a carpenter's helper, gardener, day laborer, and mechanic's assistant, as well as unsuccessful applications to factories. It terminated backpay on June 14, 1993, the date Hoffman discovered Castro's initial fraud and lack of authorization. These determinations do not identify how Castro obtained any particular interim job. Their status as unchallenged remedial determinations must be kept distinct from the parties' dispute over the Board's statutory authority to award backpay. Sources: Pet. App. 85a-86a & n.12; respondent merits pp. 39-41.

### ALJ factual recital, Pet. App. 92a

~~~text
The Respondent discovered at the backpay hearing that 
Castro used fraudulent identification in applying for work 
with the Respondent. Castro used a fraudulent birth certificate 
as identification for immigration purposes.’ Castro testified 
that he used a friend’s birth certificate and that he uses that 
certificate to work in the United States. Castro also had a 
California driver’s license and a social security card evidently 
obtained by use of the fraudulent birth certificate. He testified 
that he uses the false identification in order to work in this 
country. Castro is not authorized to work in this country. The 
Respondent contends that since it did not know that Castro 
was not authorized to work it cannot be now ordered to 
employ him in contradiction of the immigration laws.
~~~

### Board mitigation finding, Pet. App. 86a n.12

~~~text
We additionally have considered and rejected the Respondent's 
argument that Castro is not entitled to backpay because he cannot, 
according to the Respondent, make reasonable efforts to find interim work 
in light of his undocumented status. The record shows that Castro worked 
as a carpenter's helper, gardener, day laborer, and mechanic's assistant 
following his unlawful layoff, and in addition unsuccessfully applied for 
work at certain factories. Thus, we find that Castro satisfied his obligation 
to make reasonable efforts to find interim work following his unlawful 


layoff by the Respondent.
~~~
