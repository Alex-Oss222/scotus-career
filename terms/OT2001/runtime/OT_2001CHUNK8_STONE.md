## 110. United States v. Angle +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-20, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the vacatur of Angle’s sentence, with corrected constitutional instructions for resentencing; vacate the affirmance of Phifer’s sentence and remand for renewed Rule 52(b) analysis under the stated limits on binding ranges and hypothetical stacking. Leave the convictions undisturbed.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** At Angle’s new sentencing, use only a lawfully authorized binding range and supported ordinary findings within it. For Phifer, reject the categorical no-prejudice conclusion based on an assumed 292-month mandatory total and decide the remaining plain-error conditions on the actual record. Do not silently convert the Guidelines into an advisory code, direct a new enhancement jury without legal authority, or impose consecutive terms from this Court.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A mandatory range has legal force despite a larger outer ceiling

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Angle’s sentence is already vacated for inadequate findings, so the governing rule must guide a new hearing. The broader constitutional application was expressly reserved in Apprendi v. New Jersey.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a present-offense fact that enlarges a binding punishment range receives jury and reasonable-doubt protection.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend Apprendi v. New Jersey to these binding Guidelines constraints and expressly overrule McMillan v. Pennsylvania insofar as it permits a present-offense fact found under ordinary sentencing procedures to raise a mandatory floor. Apprendi reserved these applications; this opinion would decide them rather than attribute them to that earlier holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A present-offense fact necessary to increase the legally binding ceiling or floor of punishment must be admitted with the required protections or found beyond a reasonable doubt by the constitutionally proper factfinder. A larger outer statutory ceiling does not make a mandatory inner range discretionary.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Ordinary judicial assessment may select a sentence within the range independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions. The rule addresses binding legal consequences, not every factual consideration affecting the judge’s choice.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The constitutional division turns on who establishes the facts that change legal punishment authority. A mandatory guideline that forbids a lower range or authorizes a higher ceiling because of an additional fact performs that work even though Congress also supplies an outer limit. The best contrary argument invokes the traditional judicial sentencing role. That role remains where the verdict already permits the choice; it does not justify treating a legally binding change as mere advice. The Commission’s institutional validity and the existence of limited departures do not by themselves make the operative range nonbinding. McMillan v. Pennsylvania draws a formal maximum/minimum distinction that cannot account for the legal loss of sentencing freedom caused by a mandatory floor. Stone would expressly reject that distinction to this extent, while preserving judicial assessment within punishment already authorized by the verdict or valid admissions. The constitutional allocation of factfinding is decisive; the functional operation of the Guidelines identifies when that allocation is implicated. This combines a structural jury rule with examination of legal effect, not a balance of efficiency against the jury. A rule reaching the binding floor as well as ceiling is necessary here because Angle’s judicial quantity finding compelled the bottom of his 210-to-262-month range, even though his actual sentence remained below the 240-month statutory ceiling.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Prior convictions, a general advisory-system remedy, and consecutive sentencing that does not depend on the disputed binding-range premise are not decided.

### Issue 2: A hypothetical aggregate cannot replace the actual count judgment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Phifer’s claim is forfeited and requires all applicable Rule 52(b) conditions; the count excess is distinct from the newly resolved Guidelines question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether possible or assumed mandatory stacking automatically defeats prejudice from an excessive count sentence.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Olano’s separate steps and reject an unsupported counterfactual as an automatic prejudice rule.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Phifer’s 292-month drug-count term is erroneous and plainly exceeds the 240 months authorized by his quantity-free verdict. The lower court’s categorical no-prejudice conclusion fails because the assumed mandatory 292-month aggregate depends on the same judicial quantity findings challenged here. A demonstrated lawful inevitable aggregate can be relevant under Olano; unused aggregate statutory exposure by itself is not that demonstration. The remaining substantial-rights and discretionary-correction questions require the actual evidence and lawful range, not the invalid premise.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding rejects the categorical rationale and requires actual review. It does not declare every count error structural, eliminate the defendant’s ordinary prejudice burden, or guarantee a lower aggregate after lawful resentencing.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The imposed judgment contains a 292-month drug term; the hypothetical judgment contains different terms, a different concurrent-or-consecutive structure, and a supposedly mandatory total derived from disputed judicial quantity findings. Those are not interchangeable without analysis. Since the range premise must be reconsidered under the constitutional rule, the appellate inevitability conclusion cannot support affirmance. The lower court must address the actual Rule 52(b) conditions and any properly supported alternative, not presume either automatic correction or automatic harmlessness. The first two Rule 52(b) conditions are thus resolved for the count excess. The remand concerns the uncompleted prejudice and discretionary inquiries, including any properly supported evidence-based alternative; it is not a new opportunity to invoke sixty years of theoretical exposure as an answer. Johnson’s appellate-time plainness rule and Olano’s final fairness requirement remain applicable, while Neder’s preserved-error allocation does not transfer the burden to the Government here.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The proper new total, lawful sentence restructuring, and any authorization for further factfinding remain for the appropriate court under the governing procedural and sentencing law.

## 111. BE&K Construction Co. v. NLRB

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment enforcing liability under the Board’s unsuccessful-plus-retaliatory-purpose standard and remand.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the dependent litigation-based order to the extent it rests on that standard. Permit only a properly preserved, legally sufficient reconsideration under a narrower rule. Do not declare all underlying claims reasonable, award BE&K damages, or disturb independent valid court sanctions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Losing a reasonable lawsuit does not by itself forfeit petition protection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The legal standard is sufficiently clear for review without reconstructing every claim in the earlier litigation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether failure plus retaliatory purpose is enough to impose an unfair-labor-practice sanction on reasonably based litigation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reconcile Bill Johnson’s Restaurants with petition protection and the NLRA’s unfair-labor-practice provisions. Treat its discussion of completed unsuccessful suits as insufficient to decide this reasonable-suit question; use constitutional avoidance to reject the Board’s broader construction without importing all antitrust sham doctrine.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The NLRA does not authorize liability for a reasonably based lawsuit merely because it failed and was pursued with a retaliatory purpose. An unsuccessful result is not objective baselessness, and the Board’s contrary standard cannot sustain this order.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding leaves genuinely sham proceedings, independently unlawful objectives, applicable litigation sanctions, and materially different abuse patterns to their own rules.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Judicial resolution often identifies which reasonable legal position prevails only after the parties incur costs. If loss and hostility alone made suit unlawful, the right to present an arguable claim would depend on success and the claimant’s goodwill toward the opponent. The strongest labor concern is the use of legal costs to suppress organizing. That concern justifies regulation of abuse supported by the required objective foundation; it does not eliminate the petitioning interest in a claim with a reasonable basis. The statute can be read coherently without imposing the Board’s entire class of liability, so a broader constitutional ruling is unnecessary. The decisive ground is the limiting construction of the statute; the serious petitioning burden explains why that construction is required. The cease-and-desist, notice and fee obligations depend on the rejected standard and must fall to that extent. Whether a particular claim lacked an objective basis or sought an independently unlawful end was not established by the mere fact of defeat, so any permitted reconsideration must make those distinct determinations.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The precise standard for serial abuse, fraud, and an independently unlawful objective is not resolved by this record.

## 112. Harris v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the sentence-sustaining judgment, vacate the seven-year firearm sentence, and remand for lawful resentencing. Preserve the basic firearm conviction. Relief is warranted under Rule 52(b) even assuming forfeiture of the constitutional theory; no structural-error rule is necessary.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Do not treat the uncharged preponderance finding as authority for a mandatory seven-year floor. Begin with the five-year statutory minimum and its applicable guideline consequence; a different sentence requires an independently lawful basis and reasons. The judge’s statement that seven years might again be appropriate is not an already imposed alternative sentence. Any further aggravated proceeding requires independent charging, waiver, procedural and double-jeopardy authority; this mandate supplies none automatically and promises no particular new term.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The amended statute uses a sentencing condition

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory question should be answered before using avoidance as a substitute for a constitutional decision.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the amended text makes brandishing a separate statutory element.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Conclude that the current structure treats brandishing as a mandatory sentencing condition, while distinguishing the older statute in Castillo v. United States.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The amended §924(c)(1)(A) sets out the basic offense and then assigns graduated mandatory minimums, including the brandishing condition. That construction does not determine whether the Constitution requires treating the condition as an element.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion addresses this amended structure, not the former firearm-type provision or every aggravated criminal statute.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The provision’s operative division and graduated sentencing language are substantial evidence of Congress’s chosen mechanism. Brandishing describes culpable conduct and creates serious additional punishment, which makes the alternative reading plausible, but the better statutory account is a mandatory sentencing condition. Avoidance cannot be used to claim the constitutional issue has disappeared while retaining that condition’s full effect. Text and statutory arrangement decide this construction; avoidance does not license rewriting an otherwise ascertainable sentencing provision solely to evade the constitutional question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The distinct discharge condition is not independently adjudicated on an absent record.

### Issue 2: Compulsory additional punishment requires element protections

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The issue lies beyond Apprendi’s holding and squarely implicates McMillan v. Pennsylvania.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a present-offense fact that raises the legally mandatory minimum may be found under ordinary sentencing procedures.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend the jury and reasonable-doubt rule to mandatory-minimum aggravators and expressly overrule McMillan v. Pennsylvania to that extent.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A present-offense fact necessary to raise the mandatory minimum must receive the charging protections applicable to the prosecution and be validly admitted or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the proper guilt factfinder. Judicial sentencing labels do not excuse those requirements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns facts changing the legally required punishment range, not facts used to select a discretionary sentence within the range already authorized. This case supplies no constitutional prior-conviction exception.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Before the brandishing finding, the law permits a sentence beginning at five years. After it, the law forbids a sentence below seven. The finding therefore changes the lawful judgment the court may enter, even though the higher maximum remains available in both situations. The government’s reliance on possible punishment overlooks that mandatory change. The jury guarantee and reasonable-doubt standard constrain facts legally necessary to aggravated punishment, not only facts attached to one endpoint. Reliance on the previous rule warrants honest treatment of pending and final cases under their own doctrines, rather than continued deprivation here. A jury waiver does not transform an ordinary sentencing finding into a beyond-reasonable-doubt adjudication of the uncharged condition. The decisive constitutional method is the allocation of authority over facts that change the legally permitted range. Functional comparison of the two endpoints exposes the defect in McMillan’s distinction; general opposition to mandatory sentences does not supply the holding. This rule must govern beyond Harris because treating the same legal constraint differently by its label would preserve the evasion. Harris’s jury waiver changes the proper finder, not the required burden or the indictment’s scope.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Collateral retroactivity, every sentencing factor, and a wholesale redesign of the federal sentencing system are not decided.

### Issue 3: The error warrants correction even under the forfeiture standard

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory-element objection was made at sentencing; the constitutional extension was added on appeal. Assume without deciding that the latter was forfeited and apply Rule 52(b), placing the ordinary prejudice burden on Harris. This route resolves the sentence without inventing either an unrecorded constitutional objection or an intentional waiver.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether all four plain-error requirements warrant sentence relief notwithstanding the judge’s expressed view that seven years was appropriate.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Olano and Johnson, distinguish Johnson’s particular refusal of discretionary correction, and retain Neder’s separate rule for preserved error. Neither the new constitutional rule nor the indictment omission makes reversal automatic.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The use of the uncharged, preponderance-found aggravator is error under the constitutional rule adopted here, and Johnson makes that error plain on this pending direct review despite McMillan’s earlier contrary rule. Harris also carries his burden of showing an effect on substantial rights: the contested finding supplied the compulsory seven-year sentence and its guideline consequence, while the record supplies no independently selected alternative sentence. On this record, enforcing that additional mandatory punishment without the required adjudication seriously impairs the fairness and integrity of sentencing, warranting discretionary correction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This is a case-specific application of all four conditions. It neither presumes prejudice from every range error nor treats any constitutional sentencing error as structural, and it does not hold that seven years could never be imposed through lawful proceedings.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The actual sentencing record is decisive. Harris contested brandishing, the judge treated it as close under a preponderance standard, and the verdict on carrying did not resolve the distinct purpose-to-intimidate question beyond a reasonable doubt. The resulting minimum supplied the sentence under §2K2.4. The judge’s belief that seven years was appropriate is contrary evidence that must be taken seriously, but he declined to commit to that term without the enhancement; a possible future departure is not an unaffected alternative judgment. Taken together, the disputed aggravator, the proof standard actually used and the direct operation of the minimum establish outcome-affecting prejudice, rather than merely the abstract loss of discretion. This conclusion is an inference from this record, not a burden shifted to the Government to disprove prejudice. The fourth condition independently warrants correction because the judgment enforces additional compulsory imprisonment resting on a genuinely contested fact never adjudicated with the protections it requires. Johnson’s clear, essentially uncontested materiality setting is materially different. Neder supplies no automatic harmlessness answer: a carrying verdict and a close preponderance finding do not establish noncontribution beyond a reasonable doubt, and clear-error affirmance is not the missing criminal adjudication. No appellate finding that Harris did or did not brandish is made. Deciding the error’s consequence now permits the ordinary sentencing court to perform the remaining lawful work without reopening the valid conviction or inventing authority for an enhancement trial.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the statutory objection alone also preserved the constitutional theory need not be decided because the more demanding forfeiture inquiry is satisfied. A genuinely independent lawful sentence, an admitted aggravator, other records and collateral retroactivity remain outside this holding.

## 113. Ring v. Arizona

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the ruling that judge-only death-eligibility factfinding is constitutional, vacate the appellate affirmance of the death sentence, and remand for the State’s actual harmless-error argument under the governing standard. Preserve the felony-murder conviction.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require an initial determination whether the State establishes harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt under the qualified Neder v. United States framework on the actual record. Arizona’s claim that pecuniary gain was implicit in the robbery verdict is not sufficient merely as a label; assess the elements, actual instructions and findings, and qualifying evidence without resolving new credibility disputes. If the State fails, vacate the death sentence and conduct only sentencing proceedings independently authorized by state law and consistent with constitutional and double-jeopardy limits. If the State carries the burden, a consistent sentence judgment may be reinstated. Create no new Arizona procedure, automatic enhancement trial, acquittal, or release.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Death eligibility is a legal authorization question

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The granted claim concerns the additional fact without which death cannot be imposed. It does not require resolving every aspect of capital selection.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether that eligibility fact must be determined by a jury beyond a reasonable doubt.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend the principle of Apprendi v. New Jersey to the expressly reserved capital setting and overrule Walton v. Arizona insofar as it allows a judge alone to establish the necessary aggravating fact.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A statutory aggravating fact necessary to authorize death must be validly admitted or found by a jury beyond a reasonable doubt. Calling the fact a sentencing consideration cannot place it outside the constitutional guilt-finding protections.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs eligibility facts. It does not decide that every mitigating fact, moral evaluation, or final selection decision is an element, or apply the federal grand-jury guarantee to state prosecutions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The verdict authorizes the punishment attached to felony murder without additional findings. Arizona law requires a further aggravating fact before death becomes lawful, so the fact enlarges the punishment authority in the same constitutionally relevant way as the present-offense fact in Apprendi v. New Jersey. Judicial expertise and a desire for consistency do not supply a power to reassign a constitutional factfinding duty. Walton v. Arizona must be confronted, not pronounced implicitly dead: its classification is incompatible with this extension, and its reliance interest in a procedure cannot outweigh accurate observance of the jury guarantee in the pending case. The robbery verdict does not automatically substitute for an unasked aggravator question. Arizona’s preserved harmlessness argument must nevertheless be resolved under the governing error rule. Stone would not declare every omitted aggravator structural error. The appellate sentence affirmance therefore returns for a defined harmlessness inquiry: robbery and pecuniary-gain aggravation must be compared under their actual elements and verdict, with the qualified role of uncontested evidence and without a substitute appellate factfinding trial. This resolves the legal consequences while leaving the record application initially to the court that did not decide it under the correct constitutional rule. The decisive rule is the jury’s structural authority over eligibility facts. The remedial application extends Neder’s qualified actual-verdict inquiry to this omitted eligibility finding: the State may rely on supported inferences from the verdict and whole record, but not simply the reviewing court’s conviction that the aggravator was probably true. That limited extension is justified because it asks whether the proper factfinder’s actual adjudication leaves the omission harmless without making an appellate tribunal the initial capital-eligibility finder.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The ultimate sentencer, complete weighing procedure, collateral retroactivity, and independently authorized renewed capital proceedings are outside the question presented. The State’s preserved harmlessness contention returns under the stated standard because its actual-record application was not decided below under this constitutional rule; no universal structural-error rule is adopted.

## 114. Trans Union Corp. v. Federal Trade Commission +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the D.C. Circuit’s denial of relief from the FTC order on the granted First Amendment challenge.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the restriction on the identified nonconsensual sale of credit-derived targeted lists. Do not expand the order to independently collected public information, consented uses, or statutory exceptions, and do not adjudicate unpreserved alternative claims.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Commercial use of confidential financial records receives scrutiny and can be limited

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The actual activity is the sale of consumer targeting information drawn from confidential credit records. The corporation’s status neither eliminates speech review nor settles its outcome.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the statutory use restriction satisfies the governing commercial-speech standard in this application.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply U.S. West, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission and the existing commercial-speech framework, extending the supported confidentiality analysis to this external credit-data sale.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The FCRA restriction on these nonconsensual credit-derived marketing lists satisfies the First Amendment because it directly protects confidential financial attributes and reasonably limits their repurposing while retaining consent and defined legitimate credit uses.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding is tied to source, informational content, and use. It does not create a general ban on commercial databases or reduce editorial reporting to commercial speech because its publisher earns revenue.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The protected interest is control against an unrelated commercial disclosure of information entrusted to a credit system. A list of people selected for a financial characteristic can disclose that characteristic without reproducing the underlying account. Restricting that sale directly addresses the asserted privacy harm. Written consent provides a route for the consumer to authorize the use; a firm-credit-offer exception has a distinct credit function and statutory conditions, so it does not necessarily reproduce the same uncontrolled repurposing. The government must establish those distinctions in operation, which the reviewed record supports. A less burdensome opt-out is not constitutionally required merely because it increases the seller’s response rate. Central Hudson’s commercial-speech standard, as applied in U.S. West, carries decisive weight. The protected product communicates consumer attributes for commercial targeting, so its classification does not rest merely on Trans Union’s corporate identity or receipt of payment. The statutory exceptions preserve consented or credit-related uses without destroying the restriction’s fit to unrelated marketing; reasonably fitted affirmative consent does not become unconstitutional because opt-out would be cheaper. This is an extension of the privacy application, not a general information-ownership rule. Lorillard’s actual-fit requirement is reaffirmed: the distinction is that these limits directly restrict the nonconsensual disclosure causing the privacy injury while preserving authorized uses, whereas an expansive geographic advertising ban burdened adult communication far beyond the supported youth-protection fit.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Public-concern publication, independently acquired information, final data ownership, and unpreserved challenges to other agency treatment remain outside the holding.

## 115. United States v. Ruiz

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the vacatur and limited sentencing remand on the ground that the demanded blanket waiver was an unconstitutional condition, while narrowing the Ninth Circuit’s disclosure rule to information materially important to investigation, advice or the plea decision.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** On remand the sentencing court must address the loss of the fast-track recommendation caused by refusal of the unlawful condition and exercise its existing remedial and sentencing authority on that corrected basis. It may not treat refusal of that waiver as a lawful reason to deny consideration. No automatic two-level reduction, compelled plea agreement, plea withdrawal or damages follows; the actual effect and lawful corrective relief must be determined. Ruiz’s conviction remains undisturbed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A sentence appeal requires a connected legal injury

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The judge understood departure discretion; that alternative jurisdictional theory is unavailable on the stated record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §3742 permits examination of the claimed unconstitutional plea condition as a source of sentence illegality.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the statutory legal-error route without creating general appellate review of discretionary leniency.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 3742(a)(1) permits review of the asserted unlawful sentencing condition. The record’s withdrawal of the fast-track offer because Ruiz rejected the waiver supplies the relevant connection to her requested sentencing relief; a merely discretionary refusal to depart would not. The constitutional condition must be adjudicated even though the judge understood ordinary departure discretion.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not make every declined bargain or discretionary refusal appealable.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The relevant injury is denial of the bargaining opportunity on an unlawful condition, not an established suppression of a particular item or an entitlement to a particular sentence. Ruiz refused the waiver, the prosecutor withdrew the offer for that reason, and she sought the associated sentencing treatment after her unconditional plea. That sequence connects the legal objection to the sentence sufficiently for §3742 review. It does not establish what recommendation would have been made without the condition or compel the sentencing judge to accept it. The statutory distinction between legal error and discretionary leniency is decisive; the remand applies the former without authorizing general review of the latter.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general expansion of statutory sentence jurisdiction or guaranteed plea bargain is adopted.

### Issue 2: Material favorable information must be usable before the binding choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The existing trial-disclosure cases do not resolve this pre-plea timing and waiver question. A prospective duty and relief from a completed plea or sentence require distinct inquiries.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a blanket waiver can authorize withholding known favorable information materially important to advice and a plea decision.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend the favorable-evidence principle to the constitutional function of an informed, counseled plea, while preserving source-specific limits and retrospective remedy requirements.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Before obtaining a binding plea, the prosecution must make timely disclosure of favorable information known to its case team that can materially assist investigation, advice, or the plea decision, including materially significant impeachment and affirmative-defense evidence. A blanket prospective waiver cannot excuse that duty.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This is not complete discovery, a guarantee of perfect knowledge, or a requirement to finish all trial preparation before any negotiation. Genuine privilege and safety concerns require protective arrangements compatible with meaningful decision-making.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A witness’s known inability to support the accusation can matter before trial precisely because it changes whether counsel should recommend surrendering trial. Categorizing that information as impeachment does not make its significance disappear at bargaining. The same is true of a supported affirmative defense. The government’s resource and witness concerns are substantial and justify tailored protection, sequencing, and judicial review; they do not justify a categorical entitlement to conceal material favorable information. Prospective disclosure is measured by practical usefulness, while relief from a completed plea or sentence requires the governing causal and materiality showing. United States v. Mezzanatto remains intact. Its defendant knowingly accepted a defined consequence for using his own inconsistent account at trial. It did not approve every prospective waiver of every institutional protection. A blanket surrender of unknown favorable information materially necessary to decide whether to plead differs because the withheld information can prevent informed assessment of the very decision demanded. This distinction preserves limited, knowing waivers and rejects categorical invalidity merely because an agreement is negotiated under pressure. The decisive principle is meaningful exercise of the informed, counseled plea choice; the practical operation of the information duty explains its timing. The agreement’s promise to disclose evidence establishing factual innocence is insufficient because known information undermining the central witness or supporting an affirmative defense can materially affect competent advice without conclusively proving innocence. The rule concerns material usefulness of information already known after reasonable team inquiry, not a duty to create new evidence. The actual waiver is invalid to that defined extent; no unknown suppressed item is presumed and no completed plea is set aside without its own causal showing.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The record establishes no specific suppressed item, and the Court does not decide that Ruiz’s plea was involuntary or that every limited informed waiver is invalid.

## 116. Kirk v. Louisiana

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-24, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the state appellate judgment and remand for the omitted home-entry analysis.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require a record-based determination of exigent circumstances and, if needed, the causal and independent-source issues for the challenged evidence. Order no acquittal, automatic suppression of every item, reinstatement of the conviction, or new factual finding from this Court.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Probable cause does not supply permission to enter a home

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The appellate court’s legal premise is deficient even if an exception might ultimately sustain the entry.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether probable cause alone can support the warrantless entry and conviction-sustaining judgment.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Payton v. New York without creating a new exception or prejudging the unresolved factual ground.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Nonconsensual entry into a home requires a warrant or a valid exception in addition to probable cause. A judgment that omits that inquiry cannot be sustained on probable cause alone.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding leaves ordinary exigent-circumstances doctrine and genuinely independent evidence rules intact.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Probable cause answers whether officers have an adequate basis to believe a crime or evidence exists. A home-entry rule answers whether they may cross the protected boundary without judicial authorization. Treating the first answer as the second erases the warrant requirement where it has its central operation. The destruction concern must be evaluated under the actual circumstances, and the later warrant must be assessed for genuine independence if the first entry was unlawful. Those are necessary lower-court determinations, not reasons to retain an incorrect categorical rule. Payton’s categorical home-entry boundary is decisive; the fact-dependent exigency exception remains real but must be established on the actual record. McArthur is distinguished because restraining entry temporarily to preserve evidence while promptly obtaining a warrant is not itself authorization to enter and search. The narrow legal correction is justified by the state court’s omission of the exception question, which prevents this Court from replacing it with an invented factual finding.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The existence of exigency, the independence of the later warrant, and ultimate suppression and conviction consequences remain unresolved.

## 117. Zelman v. Simmons-Harris / Hanna Perkins School v. Simmons-Harris / Taylor v. Simmons-Harris

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-27, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the blanket program disposition and remand under the controlling financed-function rule, with a binding rejection of the claim that parental routing alone permits public purchase of integrated devotional instruction.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require prompt, effective relief against any demonstrated public purchase of the integrated religious instructional function, using the existing record where sufficient. Preserve lawful secular and tutorial applications and determine state-law severability. Do not order an all-provider cutoff, school closure, pupil transfer, repayment, or a newly designed voucher scheme.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The function purchased controls the expenditure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The relevant government act is a restricted tuition payment redeemed by a provider, not a parent’s unrestricted private expenditure. The actual use must be proved at the scope necessary for relief.
