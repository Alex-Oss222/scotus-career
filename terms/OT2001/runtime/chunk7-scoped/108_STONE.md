## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Preserved state authority includes lawful local implementation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The appellate ground concerns institutional allocation. Actual safety fit was not adjudicated and is necessary before any individual ordinance can be sustained.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the saving clause prohibits a State from administering its retained safety power through municipalities.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply ordinary statutory construction and the delegation principle recognized in Wisconsin Public Intervenor v. Mortier.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 14501(c)(2)(A) preserves a State’s ability to delegate its motor-vehicle safety authority to local governments. The omission of a second reference to political subdivisions does not by itself withdraw that ordinary means of exercising the reserved state power.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns delegated safety regulation only. It supplies no independent municipal power and no exception for pricing, market entry, or other economic rules merely labeled safety.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress expressly displaced local economic regulation in the broad prohibition. Preserving state safety authority would ordinarily preserve the State’s choices about enforcement and administration; a prohibition on local delegation needs more than the absence of redundant institutional wording in this context. The contrary textual inference deserves consideration but would create a substantial allocation change without language addressing delegation. The deregulatory objective remains protected by testing what a local rule actually does and by the remaining federal supervision. That inquiry must occur on remand, not be assumed in the City’s favor. Text in statutory context is decisive, with Mortier’s account of state delegation resolving the institutional meaning of the preserved power. This combines textual and structural reasoning; federalism does not create an exception beyond the safety authority Congress saved. A rule permitting delegation is necessary because the categorical exclusion caused the judgment, while the ordinance-by-ordinance safety inquiry properly remains because the lower court did not reach it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The validity of each Columbus requirement and any independent federal conflict remain for the lower court.
