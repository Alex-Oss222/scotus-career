# OT_2001CHUNK7

## 98. United States v. Fior D'Italia, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-17, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s judgment rejecting aggregate estimation.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for entry of a judgment consistent with the authorized method and the taxpayer’s accuracy stipulation. Do not enlarge the tax base, impose employee assessments, or award an uncalculated amount.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Reasonable estimation implements an existing tax obligation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The authority issue is cleanly presented because accuracy was expressly withdrawn from dispute.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether assessment of the employer requires a prior assessment of each employee.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply §§6201, 3111 and 3121(q) together. Their assessment and employer-liability terms resolve this question without a presumption of unlimited administrative power.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 6201 permits a reasonable aggregate estimate of an employer’s liability under §§3111 and 3121(q). A prior determination or assessment of every employee’s liability is not a statutory prerequisite. A method remains subject to the substantive tax exclusions and an available challenge to factual accuracy.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule authorizes a method of calculation, not a conclusive presumption that all receipts generate taxable tips at the same rate. It does not change refund jurisdiction or statutory filing limits.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The employer owes the tax because the statute treats qualifying tips as its remuneration, rather than because the government has successfully collected from an employee. Requiring a prior individual assessment inserts a procedural condition Congress did not state. The counterargument identifies real limitations of a rough estimate, but those limitations concern whether the assessment measures the actual statutory base. A supported challenge could require correction for exempt amounts, wage ceilings, or differing tipping patterns. This taxpayer deliberately presented only the legal-power issue and cannot obtain a factual recalculation on hypothetical defects. The enacted allocation of tax liability is decisive; administrative practicality confirms the construction but cannot enlarge the taxable base. A method-specific rule is necessary for future assessments because the asserted legal defect is the absence of an employee-by-employee prerequisite, while the accuracy stipulation makes any wider factual ruling unnecessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The correctness of a different estimate and a supported claim of abusive agency conduct are not decided.
