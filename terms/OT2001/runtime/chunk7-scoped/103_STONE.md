## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Washington Supreme Court on the granted issue: the specific FERPA nondisclosure entitlement is enforceable through §1983 when that provision’s elements and the independently required state-action predicate are met.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Reject categorical elimination of the federal claim. Leave the existing judgment undisturbed on the question accepted for review; do not add damages, decide an ungranted state-action challenge, or reopen the separate state-law components. Ordinary remaining preserved challenges, if any, retain their own scope.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A defined privacy entitlement may use Congress’s existing civil-rights channel

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Assume the lower state-action determination only because review excluded it. Decide neither an implied FERPA action nor general private-university liability.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the specific nondisclosure and consent obligations define an enforceable individual right and whether administration forecloses §1983.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the provision-specific rule of Blessing v. Freestone. Extend it to FERPA’s defined privacy entitlement without replacing it with a general statutory-benefit test.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The specified protection against an institution’s policy or practice of unauthorized disclosure of a student’s covered education records creates an individual federal entitlement. Section 1983 provides an available channel where its independent predicates are satisfied; FERPA’s administrative correction and funding sanctions do not establish incompatible remedial exclusivity.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule preserves the covered-record definition, institutional policy-or-practice limitation, consent allocation, and enacted exceptions. It does not authorize a claim for every administrative defect, every overheard rumor, or every disclosure by a private actor.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The text identifies particular records, particular persons whose consent controls, and circumstances permitting release. Those terms provide a judicially manageable obligation with a direct individual object. An institution-wide policy condition limits the wrong rather than transforming the protected privacy into a governmental interest only. The strongest contrary point is Congress’s centralized funding machinery. That machinery can correct recipient practices, but its presence neither expressly repeals §1983 nor supplies the individual remedial substitute needed to show incompatibility. Treating the absence of an implied statutory action as decisive would disregard that §1983 is itself enacted. The decisive source is FERPA’s own defined nondisclosure obligation, read under Blessing; remedial compatibility follows only after that right is identified. Powell v. Ridge is distinguished because the entitlement asserted here is specified in the statute rather than created solely by regulation. Alexander v. Sandoval addresses a different direct statutory enforcement route and does not eliminate this threshold. The rule must reach institutional practices of disclosure because that is the statutory condition; it need not announce a right to sue for every departure from educational administration.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** State action, any ungranted damages issue, and a case-specific failure to prove an institutional practice are not newly adjudicated here.
