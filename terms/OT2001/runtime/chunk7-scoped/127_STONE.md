## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The governing capacity rule and the information needed for judgment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The filed direct appeal supplies a congressional judgment to examine, but the available account does not establish whether a single-group crossover claim was preserved or whether independent findings defeat it. Those are matters that could change the judgment. Stone resolves the governing legal distinction and states the disposition that follows on each state of the record, without pretending those facts have been found.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the identified numerical-rule discrepancy requires vacatur of the affected part of the judgment, or whether independently sufficient grounds sustain it.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply and reaffirm Valdespino, Growe, Voinovich, and De Grandy. Distinguish a single group's crossover capacity from aggregation of distinct protected groups. No change to entering law is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A preserved § 2 district-formation claim by one protected group cannot be rejected solely for lack of a numerical majority when the admissible record supports dependable ability to elect in a lawful compact district. The claimant must still prove cohesion, relevant ordinary majority-bloc defeat, causal connection, and unequal opportunity under the totality. A reviewing court must preserve any independent sufficient lawful ground; an erroneous numerical screen warrants relief only when material to the disposition. Here the judgment is vacated in the affected part if the numerical screen was material to a preserved claim, and affirmed if an independent ground sustains it.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs preserved single-group claims against this court-drawn congressional plan and comparable claims within Valdespino's existing scope. It neither requires a fixed number of Latino districts nor authorizes statutory liability for mere influence. A court's duty to remedy malapportionment does not exempt its replacement map from that rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 2 protects effective electoral opportunity rather than a favored racial percentage. “Effective opportunity” means a practical ability to elect the voters' demonstrated preferred candidates under the actual electoral structure; it is neither assured victory nor merely persuading an otherwise controlling electorate. “Dependable” requires support adequate to the actual causal prediction, not a mandatory number of studies, fixed winning percentage, or immutable coalition. “Compactness” requires a lawful and geographically workable alternative; broad statewide population totals cannot establish it. “Independent ground” means a finding sufficient under the correct law whose reasoning does not depend on the rejected majority-only premise. A totality conclusion that simply repeats that premise is not independent; a supported finding of genuinely unequal or equal opportunity may be.

  Plaintiffs bear the ordinary preponderance burden on the statutory elements: each required proposition must be more likely true than not. Rigorous proof of functional capacity demands reliable support for dependability, not a heightened proof standard or compulsory study. These distinctions make the requested clarification consequential. The court below invoked numerical exclusivity but also made compactness and totality determinations. Stone cannot assume either that those determinations survive unchanged or that they collapse. Nor can a finding against common cohesion among different minority groups be silently converted into a rejection of one group's dependable outside support. “Preserved error” means a material legal objection properly available for appellate resolution on the claims and record carried forward, not a theory newly constructed from demographic facts. Ordinary procedural rules govern that inquiry and any lawful exception; a mistaken citation alone supplies no missing claim.

  This combines statutory text, controlling precedent, and functional application. Precedent carries decisive legal weight because it already recognizes both capacity routes; district-specific voting and geographic facts determine their application. The remedial distinction is structural: judicial responsibility requires compliance with enacted voting rights while withholding an unsupported choice of racial or partisan outcomes. Stone does not balance away a proved local deprivation through aggregate gains. He also does not treat statutory protection as a command to maximize seats. The present procedural scope follows the missing judgment-material information, not a preference for leaving the legal rule obscure.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The legal entitlement of a combined protected-group coalition, an influence-only claim, and a universal population or statistical formula lie outside the identified single-group question. Separate House and Senate judgments lie outside this appeal. No constitutional deprivation test is imposed as an additional § 2 element. The two-branch disposition in Part B is the approved position; which branch is recorded depends on the opinion below and the record supplied.

## D. OPINION ASSIGNMENT

* **When this note applies:** Each case is run separately. The other Justices' stances are run first, and Stone's approved position is applied afterward. This note is complete in itself and does not depend on any other case file.
* **Counting rule:** The Court has nine Justices. Six make a quorum. Five make a majority. Count Stone as one vote for the test. If at least four of the other eight Justices agree with the test as applied to *Balderas v. Texas*, five Justices agree, and **Stone writes the opinion for the Court and articulates the test in it**. If fewer than four of the other eight agree, this note assigns no opinion, and Stone's approved position stands as his own vote and reasoning. If fewer than six Justices participate, there is no quorum and nothing is assigned.
* **What counts as agreeing with the test in this case:** A Justice agrees if that Justice's stance accepts each of the following. (1) Voting Rights Act claims proceed on their own elements and are not gated by any constitutional test. (2) A single protected group may show capacity to elect by a numerical majority or by dependable crossover support. (3) Relief is limited to the violation proved, independent grounds are preserved, and the State acts first. Agreement with the judgment alone is not agreement with the test.
* **The test, stated in full for this case file:** Government may take account of race or party when it organizes elections. The constitutional injury is what the State made that characteristic do to a person's place in the political community. It is never how heavily the characteristic weighed in the State's decision. A complaint that alleges only motive, predominance, district shape or composition states no claim. Standing is decided first and is not a merits element. The plaintiff must then prove a specific operation on that operation's own elements. For race the operations are Exclude, Mark, Burden and Dilute; a fifth, Segregate, is drafted and reserved. For party they are Foreclose and Entrench. Predominance and shape are evidence and nothing more. Relief removes the proved operation and goes no further, with the State acting first. Voting Rights Act claims proceed on their own elements and are never gated by this test.
* **What Stone articulates in this case if he writes:** This is a statutory case, and no operation is adjudicated. Stone states the test's common rules that bear on it: the independence of Voting Rights Act claims, the State-first remedy rule, and the limit of relief to the violation proved. He states the two *Valdespino* capacity routes and the difference between an erroneous legal screen and an independent ground, and he applies the two-branch disposition in Part B.
