## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A bounded administrative search of a private workplace computer

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The question arises on a developed summary-judgment record. The actual conduct is a public employer’s misconduct investigation, rather than ordinary police evidence collection.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the initial file inventory and three limited follow-up examinations were sufficiently connected to suspected work misuse and proportionate in scope.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply O’Connor v. Ortega’s workplace reasonableness framework and retain the employee’s reasonable expectation of privacy. Ownership and administrative access do not establish a waiver; no new computer exception is created.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An employee’s retained privacy does not preclude a limited, work-connected inspection reasonably initiated to investigate neglect of duty. Follow-up examination is permissible only to the extent justified by the information lawfully obtained and the same employment purpose. These identified inspections satisfy that standard.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the actual inventory and selected program inspections, not blanket copying, unrelated communications, personal tax returns, or later police use of an administrative program.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The inquiry concerns concrete work performance, and the first inspection disclosed program names rather than opening all personal documents. Those discoveries provided a particular reason to confirm suspected private-business software. Approved outside work did not establish permission to use the department’s computer for it. Conversely, an anonymous complaint did not authorize every later search: the limited program-specific scope supplies the operative restraint. Nothing in the accepted record establishes an examination of client returns, so an adverse inference of that examination would invent the decisive fact. The controlling method is the workplace balance as applied to inception and scope, with the concrete employment need and the restrained inspection carrying decisive weight. The result is limited to the searches actually proved because an unperformed examination of return contents would present a materially greater intrusion.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Searching tax-return contents, general employee monitoring, and the independent demotion and salary claims are not within the presented question and established search record.
