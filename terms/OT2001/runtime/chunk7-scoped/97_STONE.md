# OT_2001CHUNK7

## 97. Carey v. Saffold

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-17, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Ninth Circuit’s judgment and remand for a state-law timeliness determination within a functional pendency rule.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Exclude intervals that form part of timely state collateral review. Require a reasoned determination whether this interval qualifies, with certification or other lawful clarification if needed, and then recalculate the federal period. Preserve all other habeas gates; award no writ on the underlying conviction.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The statutory review process remains pending through timely continuation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The case requires interpreting the limitation statute, not deciding a new equitable exception or the habeas merits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a reasonable interval between successive California filings remains pending and whether the merits language conclusively proves reasonableness.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Artuz v. Bennett’s filing-versus-relief distinction and Duncan v. Walker’s state-proceeding limitation; resolve the previously open meaning of pendency during timely continuation of state review.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A properly initiated state collateral review course remains pending during an interval needed for timely pursuit at the next available level. California’s original-petition procedure qualifies when it functions that way. An alternative merits ruling does not conclusively establish timely filing or override an actual state untimeliness determination.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The construction protects timely ordinary continuation, not abandoned proceedings, unreasonable gaps, or every later collateral attack. State timeliness and federal proper-filing questions retain their independent legal content.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reading pending only as a document physically awaiting one judge’s order would make the federal deadline run during the very steps ordinarily necessary to complete state review. Functional continuity avoids that textual and structural mismatch without excusing indefinite delay. The state order’s diligence language prevents treating its merits phrase as dispositive. The remand must identify the actual ground, the applicable California standard, and any supported reason for the interval; it may not presume that four and one-half months is always reasonable or always late. Text and the structure of the State’s review process carry decisive weight; functional treatment of California’s original petitions identifies what constitutes that process. Duncan forecloses treating time in federal court as equivalent, and Artuz does not convert a genuine filing deadline into a mere merits bar. The remand is needed for the ambiguous state order and the actual timeliness of this four-and-one-half-month gap, not to excuse delay because habeas relief is important.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The underlying constitutional claim and an unpresented equitable-tolling claim are outside this decision.
