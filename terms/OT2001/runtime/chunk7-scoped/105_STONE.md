## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A safety label must be tested against the actual accommodation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The evidence has been tried, and the review question is sufficiency and legal instruction rather than a new choice among competing planning preferences.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the supported accommodation verdict can stand despite the Village’s safety defense and the instruction’s placement.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the statutory accommodation duty and the objective-risk method of Bragdon v. Abbott; adapt the defense allocation to the public-entity and housing provisions.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A municipality must reasonably modify its driveway rule when necessary to afford a qualified resident equal housing access unless it establishes an applicable defense. An asserted direct threat requires objective individualized support and reasonable consideration of mitigation. The record supports this verdict, and the safety finding reflected in the verdict renders the identified placement error harmless.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The duty reaches the necessary exception established for this residence. It neither guarantees the most valuable design nor requires a municipality to overlook proved, unmitigable danger.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The relevant comparison is actual usability for this resident, not formal availability of some driveway. Evidence concerning the longer rear route, the physical limitations, and practical design alternatives could support necessity and reasonableness. The Village may protect children and pedestrians, but its conclusion must connect this driveway and driver to a supported risk rather than treating impaired mobility as proof of unsafe driving. Requiring the entity to prove that affirmative justification leaves plaintiffs responsible for their own statutory elements. Because the jury rejected the actual safety defense under the submitted theory, repeating the same dispute solely to relabel it as accommodation would not remedy a consequential error. This is an application of statutory necessity and reasonable modification, with an individualized assessment of the asserted defense. Those enacted requirements and the trial evidence carry decisive weight; a free-floating balance of homeowner preference against municipal convenience would misstate the duty. The limited property-specific judgment follows because the proved disability and route difficulty establish why an exception matters here without establishing a general entitlement to front driveways. PGA TOUR v. Martin confirms that uniform enforcement and institutional judgment do not replace individual statutory assessment, while its Title III fundamental-alteration defense remains distinct from the direct-threat defense presented here.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No general land-use exemption, intent-based holding, or new disposition of the rejected constitutional claims is adopted.
