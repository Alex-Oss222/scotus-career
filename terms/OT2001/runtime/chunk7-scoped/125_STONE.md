## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A smaller racial population share and preserved statutory opportunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The final denial of relief after trial presents whether the statutory electoral injury was proved. The failure of the necessary opposing-bloc condition is sufficient to decide the claim without resolving every disputed demographic or coalition question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does § 2 require invalidation of the changes to Districts 27 and 34 despite supported findings that opposing bloc voting does not ordinarily defeat Black voters' preferred candidates?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply and reaffirm Gingles as modified by Valdespino, together with Voinovich and De Grandy. No new numerical rule or statutory cause of action is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 2 does not freeze a numerical-majority district. A claimant must establish the requisite electoral barrier and unequal opportunity under the governing framework. Where supported district-specific findings establish continuing ability to elect and failure of legally consequential opposing bloc voting, the dilution claim fails despite a substantial reduction in the group's population share. That is the situation found here. Gains elsewhere cannot excuse an established deprivation in the affected electorate.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs this single-group district-dilution claim and analogous changes supported by actual electoral-capacity evidence. It protects effective opportunity below fifty percent without making every sub-fifty district adequate. It gives no right to a racial percentage, preferred incumbent or maximized seat count.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory text protects opportunity, and the causal framework identifies when boundaries deny it. Political cohesion means sufficiently shared candidate preferences to make a group electoral claim meaningful; it is established from voting evidence, not assumed from identity. Dependable crossover means support sufficiently reliable in relevant electoral conditions to provide actual ability to elect, not one unusual victory, guessed turnout or a hope that officeholders will be sympathetic. Opposing bloc voting is legally consequential when it ordinarily defeats the group's candidates of choice after relevant crossover and special circumstances are considered. These are functional inquiries into electoral operation, not a fixed percentage formula.

  Plaintiffs bear the ordinary civil burden of proving the statutory elements by a preponderance of the evidence: the required proposition must be more likely true than not. Valdespino's demand for rigorous support describes the reliability needed to establish dependable capacity, not a new heightened standard of proof or mandatory research protocol. The accepted district-specific evidence supports opportunity in both districts, including the meaningful nomination stage. Its force comes from its relevance and support, not the raw number of elections studied or the racial identity of projected winners. Rule 52(a) gives the trial court's supported evaluation appropriate effect; disagreement with its expert choice is not itself clear error. The statutory framework and controlling precedent carry decisive weight; functional electoral evidence determines their application. The independent totality inquiry remains necessary when all threshold conditions are met, but it cannot cure failure of a necessary causal condition. Nor can proportionality shift the burden or create a defense to actual local dilution.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Distinct-minority-group aggregation is unnecessary because a protected group may receive crossover votes without combining claimant classes, and opportunity was found even without Hispanic support. A statutory influence-only claim is not decided; the independently necessary opposing-bloc showing is absent. No § 5 nonretrogression rule or later amendment is reached.

### Issue 2: Constitutional injury requires the alleged impairment, not racial arithmetic

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Plaintiffs tried intentional impairment and participation-chilling theories. Those theories must receive their own analysis rather than disappear into the statutory result or a finding about the mapmakers' predominant motive.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do the challenged lines establish intentional racial dilution or material interference with political participation on this tried record?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reaffirm the territorial-design boundary of Shaw, Miller and Easley and apply their protection of independent injuries. State the minimum requirements needed to resolve the claims presented. Do not characterize a comprehensive new constitutional voting test as already adopted.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Racial consideration in a common electorate does not itself deprive a voter of equal political membership. Intentional dilution requires discriminatory purpose, material impairment of protected electoral opportunity and causation. A distinct participation claim requires an identified protected activity and a concrete, material impairment attributable to the challenged racial treatment or prohibited purpose; it does not require proof that a candidate already lost. The credited record establishes neither the asserted deliberate dilution nor the predicted participation impairment. The claims therefore fail without granting racial or partisan mapmaking a general immunity.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Voters of every race receive the same protection. A shared territorial electorate means that residents vote within the same geographic constituency without different legal eligibility because of individual race. That description defeats a design-only theory, not an otherwise proved denial of equal access. Effective electoral opportunity and political participation are distinct interests; preservation of the first does not conclusively establish preservation of the second.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** An operation is the governmental rule, practice or boundary arrangement alleged to act on a protected interest. Racial motivation describes why an act occurred; the alleged operation identifies what it did. A design-only complaint identifies composition, shape or predominance without an independent impairment. Material impairment means a practical restriction on the protected ability to participate or elect; a technical change or disappointment about leverage is insufficient, but total exclusion is not required. Discriminatory purpose means action taken at least partly because it would impair racial electoral opportunity or equal participation, not merely awareness of racial effects. Where that purpose is an element, it need not be the sole or predominant motive.

  Plaintiffs retain the preponderance burden on the elements of their claim. The recognized purpose-based causation framework assigns the government the same ordinary civil burden to establish, where applicable, that materially the same decision would have occurred without the prohibited purpose; a bare assertion of politics does not establish that defense. No same-decision finding is needed here because the supported findings do not establish the asserted discriminatory purpose or material injury in the first place. The result does not depend on balancing an established deliberate racial injury against predicted political benefits.

  The alleged chilling effect must be examined as a participation injury, not rejected merely because an election remains winnable. But declining population share alone does not prove reduced organizing, nomination access or participation, and the trial court rejected the asserted impairment. No presumption based on crossing fifty percent overrides that finding. Existing independent-injury doctrine carries decisive legal weight, while supported record findings decide this application. This formulation explains why the claims fail and how the same kinds of claims can succeed on adequate proof; the case supplies no basis to announce every proposed racial or partisan operation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No complete new doctrine for deliberate coalition fragmentation, coordinated racial separation, partisan foreclosure or partisan entrenchment is adopted. Those questions lack the necessary proved and preserved claim here. A freestanding due-process theory cannot be resolved without a developed preserved issue. No new justification rule for an actual racial allocation of candidacy or participation rights is needed because no such separate allocation has been established.

### Issue 3: The Fifteenth Amendment allegation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The complaint expressly invokes the Fifteenth Amendment, and the trial court rejected discriminatory purpose. That independent ground permits resolution without deciding the full constitutional coverage question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can this alleged racial vote dilution establish Fifteenth Amendment relief despite the supported failure to prove purposeful racial abridgment?
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Voinovich's express coverage reservation and its claim-specific failure-of-proof approach.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Assuming the Fifteenth Amendment reaches the dilution theory asserted, the claim fails because the required discriminatory purpose is not established. Affirm denial of this relief without holding that redistricting can never violate the Amendment.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This resolves the pleaded purposeful-abridgment theory. It leaves intact the prohibition against racial exclusion from the public electorate and does not turn § 2's results standard into a constitutional purpose presumption.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The supported findings do not show purposeful racial abridgment. The legal distinction between racial awareness and the prohibited purpose applies here as well. Precedent and the independently dispositive evidentiary failure carry decisive weight; neither the broadest possible coverage rule nor a categorical exclusion of dilution is necessary to the judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether the Fifteenth Amendment reaches every form of vote dilution remains open because the assumed claim fails on an essential element. No independent racial eligibility restriction is presented for decision.

## D. OPINION ASSIGNMENT

* **When this note applies:** Each case is run separately. The other Justices' stances are run first, and Stone's approved position is applied afterward. This note is complete in itself and does not depend on any other case file.
* **Counting rule:** The Court has nine Justices. Six make a quorum. Five make a majority. Count Stone as one vote for the test. If at least four of the other eight Justices agree with the test as applied to *Page v. Bartels*, five Justices agree, and **Stone writes the opinion for the Court and articulates the test in it**. If fewer than four of the other eight agree, this note assigns no opinion, and Stone's approved position stands as his own vote and reasoning. If fewer than six Justices participate, there is no quorum and nothing is assigned.
* **What counts as agreeing with the test in this case:** A Justice agrees if that Justice's stance accepts each of the following. (1) Racial consideration or predominance in a shared district is not itself a constitutional injury. (2) A constitutional claim requires an independently proved deprivation with its own elements. (3) The Section 2 claim is decided on its own statutory elements. Agreement with the judgment alone is not agreement with the test.
* **The test, stated in full for this case file:** Government may take account of race or party when it organizes elections. The constitutional injury is what the State made that characteristic do to a person's place in the political community. It is never how heavily the characteristic weighed in the State's decision. A complaint that alleges only motive, predominance, district shape or composition states no claim. Standing is decided first and is not a merits element. The plaintiff must then prove a specific operation on that operation's own elements. For race the operations are Exclude, Mark, Burden and Dilute; a fifth, Segregate, is drafted and reserved. For party they are Foreclose and Entrench. Predominance and shape are evidence and nothing more. Relief removes the proved operation and goes no further, with the State acting first. Voting Rights Act claims proceed on their own elements and are never gated by this test.
* **What Stone articulates in this case if he writes:** The principle and the difference between racial motivation and the governmental operation. The elements of intentional dilution: purpose, material impairment and causation. The minimum statement of a participation claim in Issue 2. The independence of the Section 2 claim, decided under *Gingles* as modified by *Valdespino*. The rule that voters of every race have the same protection.
