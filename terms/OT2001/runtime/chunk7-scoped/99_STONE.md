# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-17, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Fifth Circuit’s constitutional disposition and remand for the full United States v. Wright application. Reject the claim that the individual right depends on militia membership and reject categorical invalidity merely because an express credible-threat sentence is absent. Do not affirm the complete possession disability without the required justification inquiry.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require examination of the actual civil adjudication and the private-possession burden under strict scrutiny. The Government must establish a compelling protective interest, narrow tailoring, and the absence of a comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative. If that showing is supported, the remaining prosecution may proceed subject to proof of every enacted element; if it fails, dismiss the unconstitutional application. The existing record may suffice, and additional proceedings are permitted only if needed. The mandate neither retries the divorce dispute automatically nor enters a firearm conviction or a finding that every alleged threat occurred.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The complete private-possession disability requires the full core-burden inquiry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Love v. Pepersack settles the individual holder. Review of pretrial dismissal must separately determine whether this civil-order application has the justification required by United States v. Wright; no guilt verdict supplies missing facts.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a complete temporary firearm-possession disability can be justified without an express credible-threat formula and what inquiry this record requires.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Retain strict scrutiny for this complete private-possession disability under Wright’s core-deprivation rationale. Napier now controls the classification of its qualifying civil-order disability under intermediate scrutiny; Stone would overrule that classification to the extent it permits a complete private-possession prohibition to avoid strict scrutiny, consistently with his separate position there. Reaffirm Napier’s individual coverage and conclusion that an adequately adjudicated civil danger predicate can suffice without a criminal conviction. Fraternal Order of Police’s official-use holding does not resolve the private-possession burden.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The absence of an express credible-threat formula does not alone invalidate §922(g)(8)’s alternative force-prohibition predicate. But a complete private-possession disability burdens the core and requires a compelling protective interest, narrow tailoring, and no comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative, established from the actual adjudicative basis and operation. The lower court’s process-and-temporariness rationale does not complete that inquiry; this application must return for it.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The categorical home boundary bars general abolition of ordinary defensive keeping. It does not immunize a genuinely justified individual dangerousness restriction, but an ordinary civil status or unexamined order cannot itself remove a person from the protection. Temporary duration affects tailoring, not whether a total prohibition is merely incidental.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Preventing lethal domestic violence is compelling. The hearing, opportunity to contest a specific threat, express restraint against injury, and Texas requirement of a protective basis are material. They answer why a verbal formula is not constitutionally indispensable, but do not conclusively prove the entire fit of a federal prohibition on all possession. The court must examine whether the civil proceeding actually established the necessary individualized risk and whether its duration and review mechanisms constrain the disability. It must also address supported less restrictive alternatives, such as a narrower conduct or access restriction, rather than assume that any injunction makes complete disarmament necessary. Such alternatives may prove inadequate because continued weapon access preserves the identified danger; that is a justification to assess on this record, not a finding to invent. A federal constitutional inquiry is distinct from an unrestricted collateral retrial of the state order. The proposed remand resolves the governing rule and permits ordinary application without validating every domestic-relations form.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Final constitutional application requires the developed adjudicative and alternatives record identified above. No ex parte order, permanent disability, newly recognized collateral-attack route, or guilt determination is decided.
