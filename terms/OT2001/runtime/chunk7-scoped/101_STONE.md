## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of the challenged proceeds claims under §853(n)(6)(A).
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the forfeiture of the identified proceeds and assets traced to them, while leaving intact the relief already granted for property established to derive from legitimate funds. Do not forfeit an independent lawful share merely because its owner is married to the defendant.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: An innocent spouse’s status does not precede the crime’s proceeds

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The claimants have a concrete property controversy and received the statutory hearing; the dispute is statutory entitlement rather than lack of standing.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does their asserted community interest qualify as a prior vested or superior interest under §853(n)(6)(A)?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the statute’s temporal and ownership conditions; distinguish Bennis, Buena Vista and Lester without weakening their protections in their own settings.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An interest arising only when criminal proceeds are acquired does not precede the offense that generated them and therefore does not satisfy §853(n)(6)(A) on the asserted theory. The separate bona-fide-purchaser protection is not claimed. Assuming community title under state law does not change that federal priority.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns the proved proceeds in dispute. It does not reach a spouse’s independently owned lawful property, an antecedent superior interest or a properly established purchase for value.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Text and statutory structure are decisive. Subsection (n)(6)(A)’s temporal requirement and subsection (c)’s relation-back rule must operate together; otherwise every later gratuitous transfer would nullify the protected-purchaser distinction. Bennis protects an existing lawful interest from another’s misuse, while these claims arise from the proceeds themselves. That material difference justifies distinguishing rather than narrowing away Bennis’s innocent-owner rule. Sympathy for the spouses cannot insert an additional statutory category, and the existing protection of assets traced to legitimate funds shows why provenance must be examined item by item. The statutory answer resolves the presented claim without inventing a procedural defect or ruling on every constitutional forfeiture question.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A bona-fide-purchaser claim and procedural-due-process challenge were not presented; untainted substitute property is not the challenged category.
