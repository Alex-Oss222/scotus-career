## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of the motion to compel under this arbitration clause.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow the underlying contract action to proceed in the proper judicial forum. Award no substantive contract relief. Do not rewrite the clause to supply terms the parties did not adopt or invalidate unrelated contract obligations.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The FAA preserves this general contract defense

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The challenge specifically concerns the arbitration provision; the court can decide its enforceability without deciding the contract merits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do choice-of-law rules and the FAA require enforcement despite the established clause-specific unconscionability?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Casarotto’s equal-footing rule and apply Montana’s existing conflict and general unconscionability doctrines.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Montana’s materially greater interest in the franchise’s local performance and its fundamental general policy against the proved oppressive adhesion term permit application of Montana law. The FAA does not preempt that generally applicable defense. The established one-sided reservation of the drafter’s principal enforcement avenues, together with the nonnegotiated formation and imposed distant forum, sustains denial here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not require identical bilateral remedies, invalidate every form contract or presume oppression from relative size. Each general-law element and the actual carve-outs remain necessary.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** FAA §2’s text carries decisive weight, with the conflict factors and contract operation supplying their native factual inquiries. Enforcement on equal terms means that ordinary unconscionability remains real for arbitration as for other contracts. The hotel’s local operation and obligations support Montana’s materially greater interest despite inaccuracies in the trial court’s incidental residence and signing details. The supported lack of actual negotiation concerns this clause; the existence of later drafter-prepared addenda does not conclusively refute it. Choice retains specified, commercially important court remedies while imposing arbitration on the franchisees’ disputes, not an absolute exemption from every claim. That practical imbalance, evaluated under Montana law, establishes this defense without a new federal reciprocity requirement. An earlier voluntary arbitration demand does not amend the contract.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The underlying franchise breach, damages and enforceability of a materially different negotiated clause are outside this review.
