# OT_2001CHUNK7

## 107. Christopher v. Harbury

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-20, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the D.C. Circuit’s reinstatement of the access count and remand for dismissal of that count, leaving the surviving tort claims for ordinary adjudication.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The complaint, including counsel’s actual clarification identifying an emotional-distress action and a lost emergency injunction, does not identify a distinct presently available access remedy. No further amendment or damages channel is ordered. Any properly sought amendment must satisfy ordinary procedural law on its actual allegations; the mandate supplies no entitlement to relitigate the same deficiency.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A lost judicial opportunity must be identified without demanding proof of a lost trial

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The case concerns a motion to dismiss; plausible factual allegations receive their proper procedural assumption, but missing legal predicates cannot be supplied by severity alone.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What the access count must identify before proceeding and whether the current theory meets that requirement.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Lewis v. Casey’s concrete-injury principle to the asserted retrospective obstruction and state the necessary extension expressly. Do not infer a new damages channel from injury alone.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A backward-looking access claim must identify an arguable underlying judicial action, official obstruction causing loss or material impairment of the opportunity to pursue it, and a remedy for that loss not otherwise obtainable on a surviving claim. Even taking the emotional-distress theory identified by counsel as part of the pleading, Harbury has not identified the necessary distinct remedy.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The claimant need not establish certain success in the underlying action, prove the foreign allegations at pleading, or show that every related tort claim disappeared. She must identify a concrete legal opportunity and nonduplicative loss.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lewis v. Casey’s actual-injury requirement is decisive and extends to a claim about a lost past opportunity. Intentional deception can obstruct access, but the Constitution does not create an additional damages action whenever an official lies about an existing tort. Harbury’s clarification identified an emotional-distress action that might once have supported an emergency injunction. That injunction is now unavailable in either action, while the surviving tort counts remain capable of compensating the consequences alleged; the access count identifies no additional available remedy for a distinct loss. The missing predicate is therefore present even after the clarification, not merely a technical omission that requires another remand to explain the claim. Ordinary notice pleading requires identification, not proof of ultimate success. Deciding this concrete deficiency avoids unnecessary rulings on foreign-affairs authority, a new Bivens channel or immunity and leaves the surviving causes to their own elements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The truth of the torture and concealment allegations, the merits of surviving tort claims, a new Bivens remedy, official immunity and foreign-affairs defenses are not adjudicated. Bivens availability was not contested below and is assumed only to resolve this deficient count.
