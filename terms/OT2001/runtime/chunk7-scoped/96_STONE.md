# OT_2001CHUNK7

## 96. United States v. Drayton

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-17, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit’s suppression judgment on the ground that this coordinated encounter effected an unsupported seizure, while rejecting a categorical warning prerequisite.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve exclusion of the cocaine obtained during the continuous unlawful restraint. Return for the consequences appropriate to Brown’s conditional plea and Drayton’s trial conviction; the suppression decision does not itself order an acquittal. No independent consent holding or new Terry justification is required.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Actual freedom to decline an officer’s request

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The search evidence is challenged through preserved suppression proceedings. The district court’s finding of cooperative behavior must be respected as a factual finding where supported, but does not settle the legal significance of the full coordinated encounter.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a reasonable passenger in this actual coordinated encounter would have felt free to decline the officers’ demands or terminate the exchange.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Bostick’s totality-of-the-circumstances test and Robinette’s rejection of an indispensable refusal warning. Distinguish ordinary questioning by an officer from this coordinated assumption of the passenger compartment.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The officers’ positions, coordinated progression, close approach and announcement of an interdiction operation objectively communicated that the passengers were expected to submit before their journey resumed. Brown and Drayton were seized without an adequate independent justification. The absence of a refusal warning contributes to that conclusion but is neither necessary nor sufficient by itself.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The result rests on the police-created display of control, not on the bus’s ordinary confinement or a rule that all onboard questioning is a seizure. Both passengers were subject to that operation before Brown’s arrest; his arrest intensified the same pressure on Drayton.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bostick requires an objective assessment of communicated freedom, not a search for a physically blocked exit. An officer facing the passengers from the front, with two colleagues moving forward from the rear, occupied the positions ordinarily controlling movement and departure while asking for cooperation in an announced interdiction. The driver’s absence and retention of the tickets supplied context; those travel arrangements alone were not the seizure. Quiet requests, concealed weapons and a passable aisle weigh for the Government, but they do not overcome the combined display of official control in this confined setting. The decisive method is the totality assessment required by precedent, and the decisive fact is the coordinated operation rather than the wording of a single request. No independent break, release or intervening event separated the submission and searches from that restraint. The immediate searches therefore remain its product. This record permits a decision now; another open-ended assessment of the same settled encounter facts is unnecessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent consent untainted by detention and an unpresented Terry justification are outside the accepted question. The holding creates no warning code, damages ruling or categorical prohibition on police questioning aboard buses.
