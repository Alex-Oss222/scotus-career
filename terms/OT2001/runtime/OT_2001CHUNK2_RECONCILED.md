## Kelly v. South Carolina, No. 00-9280

### Components and operative scope

**K1 — Actual sentencing alternative.** Under controlling Shafer, once the jury finds the statutory aggravator its only alternatives are death and life without ordinary parole. The earlier possibility of a judge-imposed term of years does not defeat the conditional Simmons protection. All eight accept that consequence of existing law; Scalia's acceptance of the statutory relationship is not a join to the constitutional rule.

**K2 — Future-danger predicate.** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer find that the combined evidence and argument put future danger in issue. The planned hostage attack, armed escape plan and enduring-danger characterization support a prospective threat inference even though the State also used the material to address character, retribution and prison adaptability. The majority ground does not require expert prediction or a verbal formula and does not make every brutal offense sufficient. A claim that escape alone necessarily implies future violence is unnecessary. Parole ineligibility does not prevent the State from arguing truthful danger inside prison or through escape. The aggravator-use limitation does not eliminate the evidence's potential sentencing-selection message.

Kennedy accepts the contextual test but finds its predicate unmet here: the prosecution's actual use was retribution, character and inability to adapt safely to prison, not a meaningful invocation of future danger from ordinary release. He does not demand the words future dangerousness. He distinguishes evidence capable of a predictive inference from the State's actual invocation of the release-related danger to which Simmons supplies an answer. Scalia rejects this further constitutional parole-information application. Thomas assumes the predicate and resolves adequacy instead.

**K3 — Adequate information.** The same five reversing Justices find the permitted defense descriptions of permanent imprisonment and the judge's ordinary-meaning instruction insufficient on this complete presentation to convey legal ineligibility for ordinary parole. They address the State's real alternative argument, including the absence of a parole inquiry or a direction forbidding parole consideration and the testimony about good-time reduction. Those features do not themselves explain the legally unavailable release mechanism. Rhetorical duration descriptions and an undefined ordinary meaning leave the pertinent legal boundary unclear. No fixed words are required; an effective defense presentation can suffice without a judicial instruction. The right does not depend on a juror question or proof of actual juror confusion. Thomas finds the uncontradicted combined presentation adequate without a prescribed statutory formula. Kennedy and Scalia do not reach adequacy as independent grounds.

**K4 — Consequence and remedy.** The five reversing Justices reverse the judgment rejecting the federal sentencing claim and remand for the State-burden Chapman inquiry on the existing sentencing record. The danger and adequacy issues are settled for that remand. The State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the omission did not contribute to selection of death; if it cannot, a new lawful penalty proceeding is necessary if death is pursued, while a lawful noncapital sentence remains available. The current judgment does not itself vacate the death sentence, order an immediate penalty hearing or substitute life imprisonment. Guilt and independent sentences are unaffected. No structural-error rule, harmlessness finding or harmlessness waiver is inferred. The State's adequate-information argument is a no-violation ground, not an admission about effect.

## Riley v. St. Luke's Episcopal Hospital

### Operative questions and disposition

**R1 — Take Care Clause.** Affirm the en banc judgment insofar as it rejects the presented structural objection to this nonintervened FCA action. A private relator may pursue the partially assigned federal pecuniary claim under this combination of historical claimant status and retained governmental powers. The conclusion does not follow from Article III standing alone, from calling litigation civil, or from the phrase in the name of the United States. The Executive has a meaningful first opportunity to take over, can later seek intervention for good cause, has distinct dismissal and settlement powers, can protect investigation through the qualified discovery stay and can pursue an alternate remedy. These controls, coupled with the relator's limited assigned claim and absence of general sovereign enforcement powers, permit the action without making every litigation decision an executive appointment. This holds the challenged arrangement permissible; it does not equate nonintervention with consent to every later act.

**R2 — Appointments Clause.** The relator pursuing this assigned claim is not the holder of a continuing federal office. The Act creates an entitlement to bring a particular civil action, with a contingent private share and specified party powers; it does not place Riley in an enduring governmental position with jurisdiction, continuing duties and authority to adjudicate, regulate or prosecute a class of public matters. The distinction concerns the function and position, not a salary test, the absence of a governmental job title or the mere fact that no appointment occurred. A limited-duration public office may still require appointment, as Morrison demonstrates. Landry forecloses treating lack of final-decision power as dispositive; it does not turn every authorized civil claimant into an officer. The statute's substantial public consequences are considered and do not erase this claimant/office distinction.

**R3 — Remedy.** Affirm rejection of the two constitutional dismissal grounds and return the underlying action for ordinary proceedings. The Fifth Circuit reversed the district court's dismissal; affirmance preserves that appellate remand. No allegation of health-care fraud is established, no damages or penalty is awarded and no governmental intervention is deemed to have occurred. The action remains subject to all properly applicable statutory and merits requirements. The constitutional defense by the United States is not merits intervention. The judgment does not automatically decide statutory coverage of every named defendant, and in particular does not displace Vermont Agency's rule for a sovereign State defendant.

## United States v. Arvizu

### Components

**A1 — Method.** Reasonable suspicion rests on the objective, particularized inference from all circumstances known before the stop. A circumstance is not excluded simply because it is compatible with innocent conduct or insufficient standing alone. Courts may and must examine whether each proposed inference has factual support and how much weight it actually contributes; totality is not a rule that every asserted fact counts positively. The amended Ninth Circuit opinion acknowledged totality and softened several formulations, but its categorical rejection of the children's posture and waving and its treatment of the observations separately removed their potential contextual connection before assessing the whole.

**A2 — This stop.** The supported combination supplies reasonable suspicion for a brief investigative stop. The sensors tracked a vehicle along a route capable of avoiding the operating checkpoint; the final turn took the last bypass road; the timing coincided with the approach of a staffing transition familiar to the agent; and the vehicle's passenger behavior and the children's raised knees supplied observable, vehicle-specific circumstances consistent with concealed cargo and an attempted performance of normality. The driver slowed and remained stiff without acknowledging the agent while the children persistently waved facing forward. The pattern matters in connection with the route and apparent load, not as an independent greeting obligation. The agent could draw the limited evasion-and-cargo inference without excluding an ordinary family outing or knowing the precise contraband.

The inference is modest but particularized. The road also serves homes, recreation and tourists; a minivan is an ordinary family vehicle; the shift change was about forty-five minutes away; and the agent had local patrol and alien-stop experience but no personal drug stop. Those qualifications reduce weight and remain in the analysis. A similar minivan's drug stop a month earlier cannot taint all minivans. Valid registration to a relative in a broadly described smuggling neighborhood adds little without a connection to that owner or address and is not necessary to the sufficient combination. No traffic violation, erratic driving, contraband observation, knowledge of staffing by Arvizu, or expert drug-smuggling experience is invented. The suspicious pattern need not prove a crime more likely than an innocent explanation, but its actual factual connections must justify more than a hunch.

**A3 — Review.** Under Ornelas the ultimate constitutional sufficiency is independently determined, while supported historical facts and credibility determinations receive clear-error protection and grounded local inferences receive genuine due weight. That weight does not insulate the officer's ultimate conclusion. The material observations permit resolution of sufficiency without a further factfinding remand. All eight join this application of the governing review allocation. Scalia separately preserves his objection to the conceptual compatibility of due weight for an inferential conclusion with genuinely independent ultimate review; he does not withhold the present opinion join.

**A4 — Disposition and separate search.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment resting on unlawful-stop taint and remand for the remaining preserved suppression issues concerning consent. A lawful stop removes that particular taint premise; it does not establish voluntary consent or its scope. ## Components and supported alternative paths

## Final operative paths

**A — serious difficulty, vacate and remand:** Due process does not require total or complete inability to control behavior. It does require proof that the present qualifying condition causes serious difficulty controlling dangerous conduct, sufficient in light of the condition's nature and severity to distinguish the eligible person from a dangerous but ordinary recidivist. **A — exceptional inadequacy:** Vacate the Eighth Circuit judgment treating the continuance-rule omissions as an adequate state ground and remand for consideration of the due-process claim under the remaining applicable habeas law. Rule 24.09 requires a written motion and affidavit unless the adverse party consents to an oral application. Subsection (d)(1)(B) authorizes the community spouse monthly income allowance to be deducted from the institutionalized spouse’s care contribution **only to the extent actually made available to the community spouse**. That transfer remains optional; the statute does not command that every institutionalized spouse make it.

Subsection (e)(2)(C) requires an increased resource allowance when the community spouse establishes the statutory insufficiency under a lawful calculation. The income-first method may forecast the income available after eligibility in making that calculation; it does not transform an anticipated transfer into an actual deduction or a compulsory transfer. The hearing adjustment for exceptional circumstances producing significant financial duress under subsection (e)(2)(B) is distinct from this resource-increase provision. The court-ordered support treatment under subsection (d)(5) is another distinct rule. None may be silently substituted for another’s trigger.

The majority’s explanation reads the framework as protecting a minimum maintenance income while preserving resources for care, rather than guaranteeing the largest possible resource reserve. Some prediction is inherent in either method. The statute allows the anticipated support stream to be considered, and its nonattribution provision does not forbid that consideration. The principal contrary argument is that (e)(2)(C) directs an increase based on resources and that a voluntary future payment is not current income belonging to the community spouse. The majority answers that this pre-eligibility decision evaluates the spouse’s circumstances when eligibility and the support allowance operate, so it can include the contemplated allowance without declaring ownership or payment already accomplished. No additional consent, escrow, or payment guarantee is imposed as a judicial condition of the rule, and no later transfer is assumed as a fact.

**B2 — Agency interpretation.** The administrative history distinguishes the November 1993 instruction requiring income first from the March 3, 1994 clarification permitting reasonable alternatives pending a final rule. The September 7, 2001 proposed rule, 66 Fed. Reg. 46763–46768, remained a proposal at this event. Regional letters discussed in the historical opinion are additional communications, not a basis to erase that chronology. These materials may receive the persuasive weight their analysis earns; they do not bind merely because they state the agency’s preferred policy. The majority independently adopts the statutory construction and gives the agency’s experience respectful consideration. Mead and Christensen therefore do not remove a premise on which this result depends. Good Samaritan confirms that a change in agency position alone does not establish invalidity. No universal administrative-law proposition unnecessary to this statutory case is adopted.

**B3 — Disposition.** Reverse the Wisconsin appellate court’s categorical rejection of income first and remand for the ordinary eligibility and allowance determinations under the permissible method. Do not decide a particular benefits amount or an eligibility date not resolved by the supplied record. The dissenting camp would affirm the requirement of a resources-first recalculation under its reading of (e)(2)(C).

### Selected separate position

Stevens, O’Connor, and Scalia may jointly support a dissent that gives the specific resource-increase command priority over the agency’s broader maintenance policy. ## 32. Winn-Dixie Stores, Inc. v. Commissioner

### Components and final rule

**W1 — Statutory exception and substantive deduction.** Compliance with the four-of-seven exception in 26 U.S.C. §264(c)(1) removes the relevant §264(a)(3) bar on interest associated with the prohibited systematic borrowing pattern; it does not independently establish a deduction under §163 or immunize a transaction lacking economic substance. As accepted below, the relevant pattern satisfied the exception because no part of the annual premium was financed by policy borrowing in four of the first seven years. Compliance is accepted, not independently adjudicated. The distinction is between removing this statutory disallowance and satisfying the separate conditions for the claimed deduction.

Knetsch, 364 U.S. 361, requires examination of the substantive transaction notwithstanding the absence of a particular statutory prohibition; Gregory, 293 U.S. 465, distinguishes transactions meeting statutory substance from arrangements whose claimed tax form supplies no qualifying substance. Actual policies, payments, and contractual obligations do not by themselves establish the substance of the borrowing arrangement. The strongest counterargument invokes Congress’s detailed regulation of insurance borrowing and the current Gitlitz holding’s respect for expressly allowed tax consequences. The answer is that this exception removes one bar without affirmatively suspending the independent background requirements. The Court does not use generalized tax policy to cancel a deduction Congress has actually conferred on a qualifying transaction.

**W2 — Accepted findings and narrow application.** The appeal accepts the findings that the arrangement could not produce a pretax profit and had no nontax business purpose. Interest and fees exceeded the relevant benefits, and the claimed broad employee-insurance arrangement supplied no accepted business need for this borrowing program. Winn-Dixie expressly disclaimed a factual challenge to those findings. On that jointly negative record, the claimed interest and related fees are not deductible merely because the four-of-seven exception is met.

The decision does not choose a universal conjunctive or disjunctive economic-substance test for cases in which profit potential and business purpose point in different directions. That is outside the presented dispute because both relevant findings are accepted. Nor does it hold that every loss-producing investment is a sham or that tax motives alone defeat an otherwise qualifying transaction. A different transaction with an actual pretax return presents a different premise; no contrary outcome is imported from another taxpayer’s case.

**W3 — Disposition.** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit’s judgment sustaining the reviewed 1993 deficiency and disallowance of interest and fees. No new cash-flow trial, premium-timing inquiry, or reconstruction of missing tax facts is required. The lower appellate docket and Tax Court docket are not supplied Supreme Court docket numbers.
