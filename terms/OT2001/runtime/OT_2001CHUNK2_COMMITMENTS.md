## Kelly v. South Carolina

### Questions and sufficient grounds

**K1 — Governing sentencing alternative.** Shafer governs the actual post-aggravator choice between death and life without ordinary parole. The earlier thirty-year judicial alternative does not defeat the conditional Simmons right. This is already controlling law, not a new judgment about every capital sentencing procedure.

**K2 — State-raised future danger.** The escape and hostage evidence and the closing argument about enduring murderous character put future danger in issue. The State may offer this evidence for character, adaptability and retribution, and its attempt to confine a particular reference to the victim and crime is relevant. Those purposes do not exhaust the message of the presentation as a whole. Discussion of a concealed shank used in an escape plan and the prediction implicit in the continuing-character argument supports a prospective threat inference without expert prediction or a prescribed verbal formula. The restriction to character rather than statutory aggravators governs aggravator proof; it does not eliminate the evidence's role in selecting punishment after an aggravator is found. Parole information answers the possible ordinary-release dimension without pretending it prevents danger within prison or escape.

**K3 — Effective information.** The exact combined presentation matters. The defense could describe confinement as a slow death and tell the jury Kelly would never see daylight again; the judge directed the ordinary meaning of life; the jury asked no parole question; and no instruction expressly barred consideration of parole. Those features make the State's adequacy argument substantial. They do not, for the six Justices identified below, communicate the legal fact that ordinary parole is unavailable with sufficient clarity. The judicial explanation leaves the ordinary meaning of a frequently qualified sentence undefined, and the defense's anticipated-life descriptions can be understood as advocacy about the likely experience of imprisonment. Evidence concerning good-time credits addresses a particular reduction mechanism; it does not itself explain ordinary-parole ineligibility. The refusal of the accurate statutory explanation therefore leaves the legal answer unavailable in this presentation. A parole inquiry is evidence relevant to the whole record, not a prerequisite to due process. No prescribed words are required, and an adequate defense explanation without a judicial instruction remains sufficient.

**K4 — Error consequence.** Reverse the judgment rejecting this federal claim and remand for the State-burden Chapman inquiry on the existing sentencing record. K1–K3 are resolved; harmlessness is not. The State's adequacy argument is a no-violation argument, not a concession about error effect. The absence of a parole question and the defense's descriptions remain relevant to effect even though they do not establish adequate legal information. The State must show beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to death selection. If it cannot, death may be pursued only through a new lawful penalty proceeding; a lawful noncapital sentence remains available. Guilt is unaffected. The present judgment does not itself order an immediate penalty hearing or substitute life imprisonment, and no structural-error or general harmlessness-waiver premise is adopted.

### Adversarial comparison and limits

The strongest **no-trigger** path is that the prosecutor presented past brutality and disputed adaptability to prison, expressly disclaimed future danger and confined a particular reference to the victim; parole ineligibility does not rebut prison violence or escape. Its weakness is that the whole presentation includes a proposed future attack, escape planning and enduring dangerous character. The six K2 commitments treat that combination as material prospective evidence, without excluding the State's legitimate prison-danger case. Scalia preserves a distinct constitutional limit; Thomas need not resolve the trigger to affirm.

The strongest **adequacy** path is Thomas's: actual defense presentation may suffice; these jurors received uncontradicted permanent-confinement advocacy, no no-concern instruction and no apparent question. The opposing path cannot simply quote Shafer's result, because its judicial until-death language and supplemental exclusion differ. The six K3 commitments instead find the legal distinction between ordinary life terminology and statutory ineligibility decisive on this actual presentation. They accept the absence of affirmative exclusion as a relevant difference but not as a substitute for effective explanation. Neither path presumes what the jurors actually believed.

The strongest **immediate new-hearing** path relies on the specific prospective threat and refusal of its legal answer. It is not adopted: the actual defense statements, good-time evidence and absence of a question make effect a separate, live inquiry, and no supplied finding or concession eliminates it. The strongest **immediate harmlessness** path treats those features as sufficient proof of no contribution. It too is not adopted: adequacy and effect differ, and this record has not received the requisite Chapman adjudication. The bounded remand resolves the constitutional questions while preserving the State's real burden, not an invented default.

## Riley v. St. Luke's Episcopal Hospital

### Operative questions and disposition

**R1 — Take Care Clause.** Affirm the en banc judgment insofar as it rejects the presented structural objection to this nonintervened FCA action. A private relator may pursue the partially assigned federal pecuniary claim under this combination of historical claimant status and retained governmental powers. The conclusion does not follow from Article III standing alone, from calling litigation civil, or from the phrase in the name of the United States. The Executive has a meaningful first opportunity to take over, can later seek intervention for good cause, has distinct dismissal and settlement powers, can protect investigation through the qualified discovery stay and can pursue an alternate remedy. These controls, coupled with the relator's limited assigned claim and absence of general sovereign enforcement powers, permit the action without making every litigation decision an executive appointment. This holds the challenged arrangement permissible; it does not equate nonintervention with consent to every later act.

**R2 — Appointments Clause.** The relator pursuing this assigned claim is not the holder of a continuing federal office. The Act creates an entitlement to bring a particular civil action, with a contingent private share and specified party powers; it does not place Riley in an enduring governmental position with jurisdiction, continuing duties and authority to adjudicate, regulate or prosecute a class of public matters. The distinction concerns the function and position, not a salary test, the absence of a governmental job title or the mere fact that no appointment occurred. A limited-duration public office may still require appointment, as Morrison demonstrates. Landry forecloses treating lack of final-decision power as dispositive; it does not turn every authorized civil claimant into an officer. The statute's substantial public consequences are considered and do not erase this claimant/office distinction.

**R3 — Remedy.** Affirm rejection of the two constitutional dismissal grounds and return the underlying action for ordinary proceedings. The Fifth Circuit reversed the district court's dismissal; affirmance preserves that appellate remand. No allegation of health-care fraud is established, no damages or penalty is awarded and no governmental intervention is deemed to have occurred. The action remains subject to all properly applicable statutory and merits requirements. The constitutional defense by the United States is not merits intervention. The judgment does not automatically decide statutory coverage of every named defendant, and in particular does not displace Vermont Agency's rule for a sovereign State defendant.

### Actual controls supporting and limiting R1

The majority rationale incorporates these distinct controls, not an unqualified executive power over the relator:

| Provision | Actual authority and qualification |
|---|---|
| Section 3730(b)(1) | A voluntary dismissal requires the court's and Attorney General's written consent and reasons. This does not give the relator a unilateral power to abandon the federal interest. |
| Sections (b)(2)–(4) | The complaint and substantially all material evidence are delivered under seal; at least sixty days permit the initial intervention decision, with good-cause extensions. If the government proceeds, it conducts the action; if it declines, the relator has the statutory right to conduct it. |
| Section (c)(1) | When the government proceeds, it has primary responsibility and is not bound by relator acts; the relator's party status continues subject to the particular subsection (c)(2) limitations. |
| Section (c)(2)(A) | Government dismissal over the relator's objection requires notice of the motion and opportunity for a hearing. The settlement standard from the next subparagraph is not inserted here. No actual refusal of a supported governmental dismissal is presented. |
| Section (c)(2)(B) | Government settlement over objection requires a hearing and a finding that it is fair, adequate and reasonable under all the circumstances. Good cause permits the hearing to be held in camera. |
| Sections (c)(2)(C) and (D) | The government's interference, undue-delay, repetition, irrelevance or harassment showing may support discretionary participation limits; the defendant has the distinct harassment, undue-burden or unnecessary-expense route. These powers do not amount to ordinary at-will removal of a public employee. |
| Section (c)(3) | After declination the relator conducts the suit. The government can request pleadings and deposition transcripts, at its expense, and obtain later intervention on good cause without extinguishing the relator's status and rights. Intervention is not unconditional. |
| Section (c)(4) | Whether or not it proceeds, the government can seek a discovery stay for no more than sixty days through an in-camera showing of interference with an investigation or prosecution arising from the same facts. Extension requires the further reasonable-diligence and interference showing. This is not a power to stay the entire case indefinitely for any policy reason. |
| Section (c)(5) | The government may use an available alternate remedy, including administrative civil penalties, preserving the relator's specified rights. Qualifying final findings or conclusions bind the parties as provided; alternate remedy is not a power to erase the assigned share. |

The significance of these provisions is cumulative but not arithmetic. The government retains actual ways to protect its financial and enforcement interests; the conditions preserve an existing claimant's procedural and financial interests and the court's ordinary adjudicative role. No allegation that Riley frustrated a particular executive decision is supplied. That absence limits an as-applied ruling; it does not excuse review of the statute's facial allocation.

### Competing lawful paths and remedy limits

The **permissible-assignment path** rests on both the claimant's defined private interest and retained executive protections. Its strongest counterargument is not a missing official title but control of litigation that can impose public consequences despite executive declination. The six affirmative commitments answer that objection through the actual structure and limited claim, without treating nonintervention as irrevocable abandonment. Morrison's controls are an instructive comparison, not four mandatory boxes every civil claimant must satisfy. Nor are its criminal powers a reason to ignore Article II in civil cases.

The **public-prosecution path**, adopted by Scalia and Thomas, treats litigation of the retained sovereign claim as significant executive authority in a statutory role continuing through the suit. Its hardest objection is the longstanding assigned-claim tradition and the difference between a case-bound claimant and an office that exists independently of one claim. They regard the retained public claim and statutory enforcement authority as decisive; the other six regard the limited assignee position as outside officeholding and separately test executive interference. Landry rules out a final-decision shortcut for either side. Vermont Agency's Article II reservation prevents either side from describing standing as the answer.

For Scalia and Thomas, the narrow consequence is to set aside permission for this nonintervened relator prosecution, without invalidating section 3730(a), dismissing the federal government's own claims with prejudice or declaring the entire FCA inseverable. Government prosecution remains a workable distinct statutory route. They would return for the procedural consequence of that limitation, including any lawful governmental election, rather than invent an intervention or appointment. Neither would treat notice and hearing as the fair-adequate-reasonable settlement test. No particular immunity, merits defense, remedy amount or limitations consequence is determined.

For the six affirming Justices, complete resolution of the presented structural claims requires no severability ruling because neither component is invalid. The preserved governmental powers are constitutional in this arrangement; their application to an actual refused dismissal, settlement, discovery stay or late-intervention request is absent and is not invented. The clauses remain distinct and no Justice's Article III vote is counted as an Article II vote.

## United States v. Arvizu

### Components

**A1 — Method.** Reasonable suspicion rests on the objective, particularized inference from all circumstances known before the stop. A circumstance is not excluded simply because it is compatible with innocent conduct or insufficient standing alone. Courts may and must examine whether each proposed inference has factual support and how much weight it actually contributes; totality is not a rule that every asserted fact counts positively. The amended Ninth Circuit opinion acknowledged totality and softened several formulations, but its categorical rejection of the children's posture and waving and its treatment of the observations separately removed their potential contextual connection before assessing the whole.

**A2 — This stop.** The supported combination supplies reasonable suspicion for a brief investigative stop. The sensors tracked a vehicle along a route capable of avoiding the operating checkpoint; the final turn took the last bypass road; the timing coincided with the approach of a staffing transition familiar to the agent; and the vehicle's passenger behavior and the children's raised knees supplied observable, vehicle-specific circumstances consistent with concealed cargo and an attempted performance of normality. The driver slowed and remained stiff without acknowledging the agent while the children persistently waved facing forward. The pattern matters in connection with the route and apparent load, not as an independent greeting obligation. The agent could draw the limited evasion-and-cargo inference without excluding an ordinary family outing or knowing the precise contraband.

The inference is modest but particularized. The road also serves homes, recreation and tourists; a minivan is an ordinary family vehicle; the shift change was about forty-five minutes away; and the agent had local patrol and alien-stop experience but no personal drug stop. Those qualifications reduce weight and remain in the analysis. A similar minivan's drug stop a month earlier cannot taint all minivans. Valid registration to a relative in a broadly described smuggling neighborhood adds little without a connection to that owner or address and is not necessary to the sufficient combination. No traffic violation, erratic driving, contraband observation, knowledge of staffing by Arvizu, or expert drug-smuggling experience is invented. The suspicious pattern need not prove a crime more likely than an innocent explanation, but its actual factual connections must justify more than a hunch.

**A3 — Review.** Under Ornelas the ultimate constitutional sufficiency is independently determined, while supported historical facts and credibility determinations receive clear-error protection and grounded local inferences receive genuine due weight. That weight does not insulate the officer's ultimate conclusion. The material observations permit resolution of sufficiency without a further factfinding remand. Scalia preserves his distinct review position below; the other seven directly adopt this component.

**A4 — Disposition and separate search.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment resting on unlawful-stop taint and remand for the remaining preserved suppression issues concerning consent. A lawful stop removes that particular taint premise; it does not establish voluntary consent or its scope. The driver's claim that permission extended only to an exterior look and the disputed gun-hand interaction remain for proper appellate treatment, with the trial court's findings receiving the applicable review. The district court rejected the stop and voluntary-consent challenges, and the conditional plea reserved suppression review. The Court neither finally reinstates the conviction without that review nor orders suppression, a new evidentiary hearing, withdrawal of the plea or acquittal. The remand may adjudicate what is actually preserved, not manufacture new consent issues or decide them from the lawful-stop holding.

### Alternative paths tested

The strongest **insufficiency path** accepts A1 and A3 but finds the total pattern too weak: many lawful travelers use the road; slowing is normal; demeanor can become a theory that every response is suspicious; children wave and use luggage as footrests; a shift forty-five minutes away does not establish deliberate timing; and a relative's neighborhood identifies no crime. This path cannot be dismissed by counting observations. The eight A2 commitments instead find the supported relation of the sensor-tracked bypass route and final turn to the vehicle-specific cargo/posture/waving pattern sufficient for the lower reasonable-suspicion threshold. They do not rely on registration stigma to bridge a missing connection. Wardlow remains binding and is distinguished by its materially thinner observed-conduct record, not silently displaced.

The strongest **method-only remand** would ask the Ninth Circuit to evaluate the whole before resolving sufficiency. That is unnecessary for the stop because the credited pre-stop observations and their lawful ordinary explanations are available, permitting the Court's independent legal determination. A remand remains necessary for the separate consent grounds. Scalia supports the same judgment through his preferred deference and the supported sufficiency of the pattern while withholding A3.

The strongest **complete-restoration path** would reinstate the district court's suppression denial and leave the plea intact as soon as the stop is held lawful. It fails because independent voluntariness and scope were contested and the Ninth Circuit relied on derivative taint. Nothing in A1–A3 supplies consent. No later observation or contraband discovery is used to justify the initial seizure.

## EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc.

### Components and disposition

**W1 — FAA exclusion.** Under the actual Circuit City holding, this employment arbitration agreement is outside the FAA. The exclusion covers commerce-engaged employment beyond transportation occupations. The FAA therefore supplies no authority to restrict the Commission's suit or remedies by enforcement of Baker's agreement. This does not declare the agreement invalid under every source of law, determine Baker's independent state-law contractual duties or establish liability for discrimination. All eight accept this application as governing law; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas preserve their previously published disagreement with Circuit City's construction.

**W2 — Independent statutory action and individual remedies in this case.** The employee's excluded employment agreement does not remove victim-specific relief from the EEOC's own ADA enforcement action. Section 12117(a) gives the Commission the enumerated Title VII enforcement powers, remedies and procedures. Section 706(f)(1) authorizes its civil action against the private respondent after the statutory process, and section 706(g) permits appropriate individual equitable relief. Section 1981a(a)(2) and (d)(1)(B) expressly include the Commission among those authorized to seek the specified damages. The agency sues under that authority, not through an assignment of Baker's contractual claim. Baker did not agree for the agency, and the government made no arbitration agreement. The destination of relief does not change the source of the Commission's cause or make it the contracting employee.

First Options supplies the antecedent-assent principle; it does not by itself decide the statutory remedy question. General Telephone establishes that statutory public enforcement may include relief for identified victims without becoming merely their representative suit. Gilmer preserves both individual arbitration and an independent agency role in its actual securities-registration/ADEA setting; it supplies no rule that classwide or prospective relief is the agency's exclusive available remedy. Congress's later express damages provision reinforces the need to follow the agency's enacted remedy menu. State-law enforceability of Baker's promise cannot, merely by making that promise enforceable against him, turn the Commission into a party or amend its federal remedy authority.

**W2-broader scope.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would also state that the independent-action/nonassent rule does not depend on whether an otherwise valid employee arbitration obligation has a federal or state source: a private forum agreement alone does not eliminate the nonparty Commission's statutory remedies. Scalia and Thomas do not join that proposition insofar as it resolves a hypothetical agreement affirmatively governed by the FAA. They join W2 in this actual excluded-agreement setting, where no competing federal arbitration command applies. This is a scope limitation, not a vote for maintaining any part of the categorical relief bar here.

**W3 — Relief requires its own predicates.** Remove the categorical bar and permit the EEOC to seek the relief its statute authorizes. No remedy is awarded merely because the agency can ask for it. The alleged disability, qualification, discrimination, injury, causation and requested remedy predicates remain to be proved. Toyota's actual disability rules govern any relevant merits inquiry; an on-the-job seizure is not a finding satisfying them. Great-West concerns the different ERISA remedy language and cannot erase express ADA damages authority.

Under section 706(g)(1), the prescribed intentional-discrimination finding permits an injunction and appropriate affirmative action, including hiring or reinstatement, back pay and other equitable relief. Back pay does not accrue earlier than two years before the charge and must be reduced by interim earnings or amounts earnable with reasonable diligence. Section 706(g)(2)(A) separately restricts specified employment and back-pay orders when the employment action rested on another reason. Section 706(g)(2)(B)'s distinct same-action limitation is tied to its own section 703(m) mixed-motive trigger; this decision does not silently make that Title VII trigger an ADA finding or create a new ADA causation rule.

Section 1981a's relief is confined to its specified intentional violations and does not supply disparate-impact damages. Its accommodation provision bars these damages when the covered entity demonstrates good-faith consultation and efforts to identify and make an equally effective reasonable accommodation without undue hardship. Punitive damages require malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and are unavailable against governmental respondents. Compensatory awards under this section exclude back pay, interest on it and other section 706(g) relief. The combined capped categories of future pecuniary and listed nonpecuniary compensatory damages plus punitive damages are subject to employer-size limits: $50,000 for more than 14 and fewer than 101 employees, $100,000 for more than 100 and fewer than 201, $200,000 for more than 200 and fewer than 501, and $300,000 for more than 500, with the statutory twenty-calendar-week/current-or-preceding-year condition. No size finding is supplied here.

Courts must prevent duplicate satisfaction of the same injury. An actual award, settlement, release or payment may raise different remedial or preclusion questions, but none is established in this record. A mere agreement selecting a forum is not an adjudication, release or satisfaction. No universal rule about the effect of completed arbitration or settlement is needed or adopted.

**W4 — Judgment.** Reverse the Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition of victim-specific judicial relief and remand for ordinary liability and remedy proceedings consistent with W1–W3. The permitted public enforcement action continues. The Court orders neither arbitration of the agency's suit, payment, reinstatement, a finding of disability nor a damages amount. Baker's own independent state-law obligations are not adjudicated simply because the EEOC may proceed. There is no need for a remand to determine contractual enforceability as a prerequisite to the agency's statutory remedy menu under W2.

### Adversarial paths

The strongest **derivative-relief path** distinguishes public injunctions from money or reinstatement for Baker, argues that judicial recovery accomplishes what he agreed to arbitrate and invokes equitable appropriateness to respect that bargain. It acknowledges agency nonassent and therefore does not seek to compel the Commission itself to arbitrate. Its defect on this record is that the claimant in court derives authority directly from federal law, whose individual remedies are express, while Circuit City removes the asserted federal arbitration command. The court may decide actual remedial appropriateness on proved facts; it cannot substitute an automatic contract-based exclusion for those predicates. The statutory word may preserves remedial judgment, not a general authority to erase a named remedy category solely because a beneficiary selected a private forum.

The strongest **FAA-only remand** would reverse the federal-arbitration premise but leave the entire remedy question for possible state-law contractual enforceability. All eight reject that as incomplete here: even an enforceable promise by Baker does not create Commission assent or convey control of the statutory public claim. W2 resolves that live independent ground at the actual agreement's scope. Scalia and Thomas's limit concerns a genuinely different federal statutory conflict, not the state-law premise already resolved.

The **independent-action path** must confront settlement and double-recovery objections rather than claim the employee's acts can never matter. W3 does so: an executed release, completed award or satisfaction is not interchangeable with an executory forum promise, and concrete consequences may be addressed when present. The remand awards nothing. Thus no factual premise concerning damages, waiver, arbitration completion or employer size is needed to remove the categorical bar.

## In re Cybernetic Services, Inc.

### Components and judgment

**C1 — Covered transaction and federal displacement.** Section 261's recording protection for assignments, grants and conveyances concerns transfers of patent ownership rights, including an actual title-transferring mortgage. This stipulated collateral security interest transferred no ownership and does not fall within that category. Its state-law perfection therefore does not conflict with the federal ownership-recording rule or intrude on a comprehensive federal system for perfecting every patent lien. The rights transferred, not the instrument's title, control. The decision does not classify every instrument called a security interest, declare that patent mortgages never fall within section 261, or convert all collateral interests into patent licenses for all substantive purposes.

Section 261 makes a covered assignment, grant or conveyance void against a subsequent purchaser or mortgagee for valuable consideration without notice unless recorded within three months of its date or before the subsequent purchase or mortgage. Both recording alternatives and the consideration/no-notice conditions retain their force. The statute does not declare an unrecorded transaction void against everyone or prescribe universal perfection for any encumbrance touching a patent.

Waterman's actual mortgage transferred title and carried ownership consequences; its rights-based distinction supports rather than defeats this nonownership boundary. Littlefield likewise distinguishes the absolute ownership conveyance from retained lesser rights. The national-register policy is substantial, but national patent protection does not itself displace ordinary secured-credit rules. O'Melveny's identified-conflict requirement defeats creation of a separate federal uniformity rule merely because national notice would be convenient. State filing here regulates credit priority and does not create patent-like rights in unpatented subject matter or alter patent validity, duration or exclusive scope. The particular conflict that would justify displacement is absent.

**C2 — Trustee's competing status, an independently sufficient federal ground.** Section 544(a)(1) places the trustee in the position of a hypothetical judicial lien creditor as of commencement, without regard to knowledge and whether or not an actual creditor exists. That status is not section 261's ownership-taking purchaser or title mortgagee. Consequently, even if transaction coverage were assumed, the trustee's preserved status supplies no federal recording protection against this creditor's earlier interest. The statutory independence from knowledge does not turn a judicial lien into ownership acquired for consideration. Section 544(a)(2)'s unsatisfied-execution theory was not preserved below and no exceptional circumstances were identified; purchaser status likewise was not argued below. Those theories are not newly adjudicated. Section 544(a)(3)'s text concerns real property and supplies no automatic patent-purchaser status.

**C3 — California's two independent step-back grounds.** Former California Commercial Code section 9104(a) withdraws Article 9 only to the extent a federal statute governs the rights of parties and affected third parties in the relevant transaction. Because section 261 does not govern this nonownership collateral interest against this judicial lien creditor, that provision does not withdraw state perfection. The state provision must be addressed on its own text; it is not merely renamed federal conflict preemption, and no universal decision about its precise outer reach is necessary.

Former sections 9302(3)(a) and 9302(4) separately govern substitution of an external filing system. Their reference to a national registration or another filing place must concern an adequate system for perfecting the relevant security interest. A national register of patents and ownership transfers, plus discretionary acceptance of collateral documents, does not itself establish a federal perfection system for this nonownership lien. Those provisions therefore do not make the stipulated California filing ineffective or require an additional PTO filing here. This construction gives effect to their transaction-specific perfection function and the Article 9 comments' description of an adequate outside filing system; it does not replace the governing former Article 9 with revised provisions or declare national registration irrelevant when a federal statute actually supplies the applicable security-interest system.

**C4 — Disposition.** Affirm the Ninth Circuit judgment sustaining the BAP and bankruptcy court's priority determination and relief from the automatic stay. The creditors' state filing compliance and its priority in time over the bankruptcy petition are stipulated. With no required federal filing and no applicable state step-back, their perfected interest has priority over the trustee's preserved section 544(a)(1) claim. The bankruptcy court's permission to pursue foreclosure remains in place; the Supreme Court does not itself transfer ownership, decree that foreclosure has occurred, establish a sale price, adjudicate patent validity or direct payment of a specified debt. No characterization remand is necessary because nonownership transfer and state compliance are stipulated rather than inferred.

### Adversarial paths and preserved limits

The **federal-recording path** would treat modern security interests as the functional successors to patent mortgages, read conveyance broadly and identify a single national notice system as essential to reliable patent transfers. Its difficulty is twofold: the present instrument transfers no ownership of the kind Waterman addressed, and the trustee relies on judicial-lien status rather than the statutory purchaser/mortgagee category. Thus transaction coverage alone would not yield this trustee's priority. A policy preference for broader filing cannot fix either statutory mismatch.

The independent **state-step-back path** could accept absence of federal preemption yet read section 9302(3)(a)'s national-registration language to make state filing ineffective. That is a real alternative, not redundant with C1. The eight Justices reject it because its function and subsection (4)'s perfection consequence require an outside system applicable to this security interest, while section 9104(a) withdraws Article 9 only within actual federal governance. An ownership register is not a security-interest perfection system merely because a collateral document may be accepted for recording. The Court need not decide the validity of PTO regulations to recognize that administrative availability is not statutory necessity.

The **characterization-remand path** would be appropriate if the instrument's rights or California filing compliance were unresolved. They are expressly stipulated here. No source-recovery gap authorizes contradicting that judicially reported stipulation or ordering production to recreate a contest the parties did not present.

The copyright comparison supplies no shortcut: the different statute expressly addresses hypothecation, and its own perfection merits are not before this Court. Neither J. E. M.'s coexistence holding nor its dissent decides patent collateral by analogy alone. The adequate conclusion rests on section 261, section 544, governing former California provisions and the actual instrument's stipulated effect.

## Components and supported alternative paths

**T1 — Scope of censorship safeguards.** A genuinely content-neutral system allocating park space and coordinating activities need not adopt Freedman's entire procedure for censorship of expressive content. Narrow, objective grounds, action within the established bounded period, written explanation and an available avenue for judicial review still matter. Freedman's requirement that the censor initiate judicial proceedings and carry the censorship burden is not automatically a requirement for every administrative refusal of a neutral park permit. This conclusion does not exempt a licensing mechanism from review merely because its officials describe it as logistical.

The strongest contrary path applies the full censorship safeguards to any permit prerequisite, because delay can defeat speech regardless of the ground of denial. The pertinent distinction is the official function actually authorized: deciding acceptable ideas poses a different recurring danger from applying definite use, scheduling and safety limits. Existing neutral-permit authority permits the latter subject to meaningful constraints. No ruling is needed that every neutral scheme may dispense with all timing protection, or that an applicant can never challenge actual delay.

**T2 — Standards and discretion in this ordinance.** The two prior-conduct predicates and eleven further grounds confine denial to the stated subjects. The prior-conduct language operates only to the extent permitted by law. The ordinance MAY permit denial on a listed ground; it does not require denial on every occurrence. Permission to excuse an otherwise disqualifying circumstance is not permission to invent an additional ground of denial. The actual grounds concern completion and prescribed submissions, material falsehood, legal capacity, unpaid damage or debt, competing earlier applications and scheduled District programs, designated uses, unreasonable danger, required licenses and legal prohibitions. They are sufficient for the facial standards challenge when applied as park-management criteria, not as authorization to select favored ideas.

The strongest invalidity path treats permissive exceptions, danger assessment and cross-references as open-ended power to discriminate. Forsyth requires real constraints and does not accept presumed goodwill. Here the specified grounds, order of receipt and written explanation allow a court to identify a reason outside the authorized criteria or a discriminatory application. No holding approves hostile-audience pricing, an unpresented fee amount, every financial condition in every application, viewpoint favoritism, or a refusal justified merely by anticipated objection to a message. A materially different implemented discretion remains challengeable.

**T3 — Timing, review and remedy.** The District must act within 14 days, unless it gives written notice extending that period for another 14 days. Denial must state its grounds in writing; where feasible the District must propose measures to cure application defects. When denial rests on a prior competing application, it must suggest other times or places. The applicant has seven days to appeal in writing; the General Superintendent must act within seven days. Common-law certiorari review is available in state court. Together these protections support rejection of the facial challenge without importing every Freedman procedure. There is no established automatic-issuance rule and no supplied adjudication deadline for the reviewing court; neither is invented.

All commitments affirm the Seventh Circuit's facial judgment. No application-specific discrimination finding, damages, injunction against a particular denial or new substitute permit ordinance is entered. The actual constitutionality of delayed, selective or content-based administration remains for a proper claim and record. The disposition does not presume that such an independent claim is pending or establish its elements by allegation alone.

## 28. Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co.

## 29. Romeu v. Cohen

### Distinct questions and supported alternative paths

The recurring prospective eligibility dispute remains reviewable. No 2000 ballot, count or electoral allocation is reopened. Romeu seeks participation in New York's existing presidential electorate through its former-resident absentee rules, not electors for Puerto Rico. The same-date Igartua proposal supplies no entering law.

**R1 — Federal classification.** UOCAVA mandates absentee voting for its covered overseas class; it does not itself forbid New York to extend a state-established nonresident franchise more broadly. The constitutional issue nevertheless includes the federal classification among otherwise qualified former residents: a mandatory protection can distribute electoral opportunity unequally even though it is a floor. That issue must not be disposed of by falsely describing federal law as a command that New York exclude every territorial resident.

**R2 — State exclusion.** New York's rule conditions the overseas route on residence outside the United States and disavowal of voting or registration in any State or United States territory. The latter concerns any domestic elections, not merely casting a second presidential ballot. It independently excludes this former resident. The existing overseas franchise is the relevant comparator; mere authority to insist on ordinary state residence does not alone answer equality within an exception the law actually supplies.

**Membership/accommodation path — affirm R1 and R2.** Bona fide residence may delimit a State's political membership. Overseas preservation is a justified, incremental accommodation to former residents outside the domestic electoral system; a citizen settling in Puerto Rico becomes part of a different domestic polity and retains its civic participation. Territorial residence is not a suspect classification. The distinction is rationally related to that accommodated class and equal treatment of territorial residents, rather than punishment for movement or an assessment of deserving citizens. This path treats the requested former-state membership, not the practical severity of losing a presidential vote by itself, as the threshold distinction from burdens on an existing resident electorate. It does not claim that a local election is another presidential ballot or that every geographic distinction is valid.

**Existing-franchise equality path — reverse R1 and R2.** Once the government retains otherwise qualified former residents as presidential voters, complete exclusion of the comparable former resident who moves to Puerto Rico requires electoral burden-and-fit scrutiny. The injury is exclusion from choosing the same national officers, not merely inconvenience in obtaining an absentee ballot. This complete exclusion is a severe burden within the preserved franchise and requires a narrowly drawn rule advancing a compelling interest. The State and federal classification must respond to that particular participation interest. Local or territorial voting does not supply the missing presidential vote; the state declaration reaches more than duplicate voting. The offered incremental-accommodation and equal-territorial-treatment interests do not satisfy that demanding justification for total exclusion from this existing former-resident presidential franchise. No permanent constitutional membership in every former State is recognized: the defect is unequal treatment within the preserved nonresident mechanism. Article II's allocation to existing state electors remains intact.

The strongest objection to the equality path is that it imports a full voting right before establishing continuing membership and thereby risks compelling any emigrant exception to become universal. The answer is bounded to comparable prior eligibility and the actual former-resident presidential franchise; neutral verification, no duplicate presidential voting, existing qualification rules and lawful prospective organization remain available. The strongest objection to accommodation is the absence of any presidential substitute for either group; its supporters answer that domestic political membership supplies the relevant distinction without pretending that it yields a presidential vote.

**R3 — Alternative constitutional theories.** No Justice finds an independent permanent right under Article IV to carry a former State's ballot, or reads Saenz's equal treatment of new state citizens as a requirement that the former State preserve membership. The right to travel does not itself guarantee retention of every former-state electoral incident. For the equality-path Justices these propositions do not answer the distinct selective-franchise claim; they reject the asserted independent carryover/travel theory while sustaining equality. Due process supplies no separate general entitlement on the supported facts beyond the equality issue adjudicated.

**R4 — Remedies.** Affirmance sustains the reviewed dismissal and preserves the distinction's present operation without deciding power to legislate every imaginable broader franchise. Reversal would set aside dismissal of the federal and state equality claims and remand for prospective equal treatment within the existing former-resident presidential system, with defendants, severability and implementation addressed under their separate authority. Federal minimum obligations remain operative unless and until lawfully conformed to the judgment; the State may not cure its unequal rule simply by denying the federally required overseas vote. No immediate unconditional ballot, new territorial electors, pro rata electoral plan or retrospective count follows. Independent alternative-theory rejection need not be reopened.

## 31. Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer

### Exact statutory relationships and components

**B1 — Resource-hearing methodology.** Section 1396r-5(a)(1) gives this section priority over inconsistent Medicaid provisions; (a)(3) preserves ordinary income/resource determinations and methodology except where the section specifically provides otherwise. The section protects a resource amount and income support through distinct provisions.

Section (b)(1) prevents the COMMUNITY spouse's income from being deemed available to the INSTITUTIONALIZED spouse, except as (b)(2) provides. It is not a reciprocal general prohibition written in the opposite direction. Section (b)(2)'s title/instrument rules expressly govern income attribution for the POSTELIGIBILITY determination under (d), with their trust, joint-payment, instrument and rebutted-ownership qualifications. Section (c)(2) governs resources at initial eligibility, excluding the amount computed under (f)(2)(A); (c)(4) later separates community-spouse resources. These dates and directions cannot be collapsed.

Section (d)(1)(B) permits the community-spouse income allowance to be deducted from the institutionalized spouse's income for care only to the extent that income is actually made available to or for the community spouse. Section (d)(2) measures that allowance by the maintenance minimum less income otherwise available, with the court-order rule of (d)(5) remaining distinct. Section (e)(2)(C) requires a substituted resource allowance when the spouse establishes that the existing allowance, in relation to the income it generates, is inadequate to raise the community spouse's income to the maintenance minimum. The exceptional-circumstances/significant-financial-duress adjustment to the maintenance minimum under (e)(2)(B) is a different provision and remains separate.

**Permissible-income-first path — reverse.** The resource hearing forecasts the community spouse's income after eligibility; it may consider the anticipated community-spouse income allowance authorized by (d) before enlarging protected resources under (e). The posteligibility title-attribution rules do not expressly define all projected income for this separate hearing. Section (a)(3)'s preserved methodology room and the combined income-and-resource protection permit the State's choice. This does not require the institutionalized spouse to transfer income, deem a proposed transfer accomplished, or authorize a (d)(1)(B) payment deduction for money not actually made available. A transfer's optional character does not itself entitle the couple to a larger resource shelter while leaving the authorized income support unused. The claim is to the statute's maintenance protection, not maximal preservation of assets.

**Resources-first path — affirm.** Section (e)(2)(C) speaks to the community spouse's income and the income generated by the resource allowance in determining eligibility. An optional later transfer from the other spouse is not presently that income. The specific resource-protection command qualifies general methodological flexibility. Although (b)(2)'s attribution rules are expressly posteligibility, their careful separation of ownership and transfer reinforces the scheme; it is not necessary to misstate (b)(1) as a reciprocal prohibition. The State cannot defeat the mandatory resource increase by assuming that an optional posteligibility payment has supplied an existing income deficiency.

Each path must answer the other: income-first must preserve actual payment and attribution limits; resources-first must explain why a necessarily prospective resource-income forecast cannot include the very support allowance the same scheme authorizes. Neither may infer Irene's later transfers or invent a consent, escrow, evidentiary-burden or guaranteed-payment condition as a compromise.

**B2 — Agency weight.** The November 1993 memorandum described income-first as required; the March 3, 1994 clarification permitted income-first or another reasonable interpretation pending final regulations. The September 7, 2001 publication is a proposed rule, not an effective final rule. Good Samaritan does not disqualify a considered view just because it changed. Mead and Christensen prevent automatic controlling weight from advisory guidance or a proposal. The policy reasons receive their earned persuasive weight. The final statutory commitments below do not depend on pretending that these documents bind a court or State as enacted law.

**B3 — Remedy.** Reversal rejects the categorical federal prohibition on Wisconsin's method and remands for ordinary application and remaining eligibility calculations; it does not determine a transfer, amount, initial eligibility date or benefit award. Affirmance preserves the resources-first legal constraint and requires recalculation under it, without granting all requested resources or declaring immediate eligibility. Actual payment calculations remain subject to (d)(1)(B), and no unclaimed exceptional-duress or court-order finding is supplied.
