## 80. Compaq Computer Corp. v. Commissioner +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Fifth Circuit’s rejection of the transaction-disregard ground and related penalty.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Recognize the transaction under the applicable income, loss and foreign-tax-credit provisions; remove the deficiency and negligence penalty insofar as they depend on the rejected sham characterization. Leave only computation under those statutory requirements, not a new search for an unasserted disallowance theory.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Tax motivation does not negate proved economic substance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Commissioner’s disregard theory and legal profit computation are squarely presented; no later tax amendment governs the transaction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does this actual ADR arrangement lack economic substance or a sufficient business purpose?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply and reconcile Gregory’s substance principle with Frank Lyon and Old Colony Trust; distinguish a fictitious or economically empty arrangement from these actual transactions.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The identified market purchase, dividend entitlement and resale are not disregarded merely because they were arranged to obtain favorable tax consequences. Foreign tax paid on the taxpayer’s gross dividend remains part of the income analysis; a supposedly neutral profitability calculation cannot subtract that tax while ignoring its corresponding legally available credit. The supported profit and real market dealings establish substance and sufficient purpose here.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the demonstrated arrangement and the statutory consequences actually claimed. It supplies no safe harbor for fabricated trades, circular controlled pricing, nonexistent tax liability or failure of an independent credit limitation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Enacted tax treatment carries decisive weight; the functional substance inquiry determines whether real events qualify for that treatment. A gross dividend does not become fictional because a withholding agent pays part of it to a foreign sovereign on the taxpayer’s behalf. The transactions changed legal rights and occurred in an actual market; minimizing risk is not proof that no real transaction occurred. Stone retains a meaningful sham doctrine but refuses to use a mismatched before-and-after-tax calculation as its premise. A tax motive can explain why a transaction was chosen without erasing an independently real profit. This record resolves both substance and purpose, so selecting a universal formulation for every disputed economic-substance test would decide more than necessary. The consistently calculated pretax profit of approximately $1.894 million matters as an actual return, not a judicial presumption that every nominal profit validates a tax arrangement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unasserted statutory credit defects, later statutory amendments and a universal choice among every circuit’s formulation are outside this disposition.

## 81. SEC v. Zandford

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit’s categorical dismissal and remand for adjudication of the SEC’s claim under an integrated deceptive-sale analysis.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the SEC to prove that the securities sales were undertaken as part of the concealed appropriation scheme. Reassess the summary-judgment record and criminal issue preclusion rather than assuming every civil element is established. No new civil penalty, disgorgement amount, or injunction is fixed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The transaction as an instrument of the deception

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory connection issue necessary to resolve the dismissal, while distinguishing pleading sufficiency from judgment on proof.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether secret liquidation of entrusted securities to steal the proceeds can constitute deception in connection with the sales.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply O’Hagan’s governing relationship between entrusted authority, deception, and trading; propose its direct application to this integrated broker scheme.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A broker commits fraud in connection with securities sales when, while purporting to act for the account holders, he secretly liquidates their securities as part of a plan to appropriate the proceeds. The provision does not require a false statement about the securities’ value, but requires a demonstrated connection between deceptive conduct and the transactions themselves.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the alleged planned sales from this discretionary account. It excludes liability based solely on a broker’s status or on a later independent misuse of money from a completed nondeceptive transaction.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 10(b)'s transaction connection, applied through O'Hagan's account of concealed misuse of entrusted authority, is decisive. The broker allegedly used discretionary sale authority as the instrument for stealing the clients' assets; liquidation and appropriation formed one deceptive course rather than an innocent sale followed by an unrelated theft. Stone extends O'Hagan's reasoning to that setting without creating a federal remedy for every fiduciary breach. The combination uses statutory text and the scheme's actual operation, with the required securities connection limiting the functional analysis. Authority to select investments for clients is not authority to sell secretly for oneself. This resolves the categorical dismissal ground; the scope of the criminal judgment's preclusion and the civil summary-judgment record still determine which facts and remedies have been established.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Remaining proof, issue preclusion, and the proper civil remedies depend on record matters beyond the categorical question decided.

## 82. United States v. Mackby +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the ruling that the combined statutory penalties and treble damages are subject to the Excessive Fines Clause, with remand for proportionality review.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the established False Claims Act liability. Require assessment of the aggregate punitive extraction under Bajakajian, taking account of actual loss, culpability, repeated conduct, lawful compensatory functions, and the sanction’s severity. Do not set a new award or presume that the existing one is excessive.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Constitutional scrutiny of a mixed-purpose recovery

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only whether the constitutional protection applies and the consequence of that answer. The question does not reopen liability or demand a final excessiveness ruling.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a civil enforcement recovery containing fixed penalties and multiplied damages can avoid the Excessive Fines Clause because it also compensates the government.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Austin and Bajakajian to the monetary scheme before the Court, without replacing their gross-disproportionality standard.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** False Claims Act civil penalties and treble damages imposed together constitute an exaction at least partly punitive and therefore fall within the Excessive Fines Clause. Their compensatory and regulatory purposes remain relevant to proportionality but do not defeat coverage.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs the combined recovery imposed for Mackby’s false Medicare claims. It requires scrutiny rather than invalidation and does not establish a fixed permissible multiplier or a separate criminal classification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Austin's partly punitive criterion and Bajakajian's distinct excessiveness inquiry govern. The label civil cannot remove a sovereign monetary exaction from scrutiny when fixed penalties and multiplied recovery perform punitive work. The actual function of this combined award is decisive on coverage; compensation for difficult-to-measure injury remains relevant when assessing gross disproportionality. The judgment's severity, repeated knowing claims, actual loss and culpability must be assessed together, rather than assuming either that treatment provided defeats fraud or that fraud validates any amount. This applies the existing constitutional standard to a new statutory recovery and creates no fixed permissible multiplier. Remand is warranted because the assigned question concerns coverage and the trial court has not performed the resulting proportionality inquiry, not because Stone leaves the governing rule unanswered.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The final proportionality determination requires findings and assessment not resolved by the assigned coverage question. Other constitutional classifications and unchallenged liability issues lie outside it.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Follow the enacted jurisdictional source

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide specialized appellate jurisdiction before any trade-dress merits. Reach the effect of the patent counterclaim because it is the asserted jurisdictional anchor.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a compulsory counterclaim changes a nonpatent complaint into an action arising under patent law for the relevant jurisdictional statutes.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing complaint-based approach to arising-under jurisdiction and propose its explicit application to this specialized statutory allocation.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A patent counterclaim, including a compulsory one, does not make an action arise under patent law when the plaintiff’s well-pleaded complaint supplies no patent-law basis. The Federal Circuit therefore lacks the specialized appellate jurisdiction asserted in this case.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule addresses jurisdiction grounded solely in a counterclaim. It does not eliminate district-court jurisdiction supplied by other federal claims or determine the effect of a materially amended complaint asserting patent rights.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Sections 1338 and 1295 use the established arising-under formulation; Christianson and the well-pleaded-complaint rule give that language a source-of-claim meaning. Stone reaffirms that allocation and applies it to a compulsory patent counterclaim. Statutory text and inherited jurisdictional usage carry decisive weight over a functional preference for uniform patent adjudication. Rules requiring a counterclaim to be pleaded do not convert it into the plaintiff's cause of action. The complaint supplies a federal trade-dress controversy, so the defect concerns specialized appellate allocation rather than an absence of all district-court jurisdiction. TrafFix's substantive functionality holding does not create appellate authority to reach those merits. The Federal Circuit judgment must be vacated and the proper regional route used; an amended patent complaint would require a different jurisdictional record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The trade-dress merits, patent merits, and materially changed pleading configurations are outside the jurisdictional question presented.

Rule 23 makes him part of the mandatory adjudication, and timely objection gives the court and proponents an opportunity to address his challenge; requiring another intervention step for this appeal would disconnect binding party status from review. Amchem and Ortiz retain their independent certification and representation protections. No merits entitlement follows from the appellate route, and no broader rule for optional classes or unrelated orders is needed.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment insofar as it prevents the challenged consequences from being imposed solely for refusal to provide unprotected incriminating admissions.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve prospective protection against the coercive condition. Kansas may provide adequate protection against criminal use, revise the disclosure requirement, or operate voluntary treatment. The injunction does not confer permanent preferred housing or prevent classifications justified on independent grounds. No damages or sentence reduction is ordered.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Treatment without a compelled surrender of the privilege

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach compulsion because the record establishes potential additional criminal exposure and concrete consequences tied to refusal. Do not wait for a prosecution to impose prospective protection against the condition.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the combined consequences may be used to extract unprotected admissions as a condition of treatment participation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the privilege’s continuing protection and propose its unresolved application to this package of prison consequences. Do not import a liberty-interest test as the Fifth Amendment rule.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When the State conditions avoidance of a material security transfer and combined confinement restrictions on disclosures carrying real criminal exposure, it compels self-incrimination unless adequate protection against criminal use removes that exposure. A rehabilitative purpose does not by itself validate the condition.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs this linked package and these unprotected disclosures. It does not prohibit voluntary treatment, require certification of an uncooperative participant, or invalidate an independently justified classification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Fifth Amendment's penalty cases, including Garrity and Minnesota v. Murphy, protect against using governmental control to exact incriminating statements without adequate protection. Mitchell confirms that conviction does not erase exposure from further admissions. Stone applies that source to the combined security transfer and material restrictions actually attached to refusal. Their coercive function is decisive; the absence of additional sentence days is relevant but does not categorically eliminate compulsion. Sandin's different liberty-interest inquiry cannot replace the privilege's standard. Legitimate treatment and classification goals receive real respect, but can be pursued through protected disclosures, voluntary participation or independent security judgments. The relief removes the coercive condition and supplies no permanent right to preferred housing. A rule for every minor incentive would go beyond the concrete package that makes this controversy justiciable.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The legality of hypothetical immunized disclosures, distinct independently justified transfers, or later criminal evidentiary use requires facts or proceedings outside this question.

## 86. Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Echazabal

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical holding that the ADA forbids an own-health qualification defense and remand for application of the regulation’s actual requirements.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit consideration of the defense only through an individualized, objectively supported assessment of significant employment-related risk and available reasonable accommodation. Do not order hiring, discharge, damages, or summary judgment without resolving the properly preserved remaining issues.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Authorized defense with bounded individual proof

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the legal validity of the regulation; leave unresolved medical and accommodation disputes for the appropriate factfinding process.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the statutory qualification provision authorizes a defense directed to significant danger to the particular worker.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Treat the general qualification language and the specified direct-threat example as parts of one statute, with the agency’s actual delegated role and the statute’s anti-stereotyping purpose constraining the defense.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The ADA permits the EEOC to recognize an own-health qualification defense for a significant risk arising from the job where the employer establishes an individualized, objectively supported assessment, business necessity, and the inability of reasonable accommodation to eliminate or adequately reduce the relevant risk. The reference to danger to others does not exhaust the general qualification provision.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Uphold the defense’s legal availability, not Chevron’s factual entitlement to it.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 12113(a)'s general qualification defense, its requirement that performance cannot be accomplished through reasonable accommodation and the nonexclusive wording of subsection (b) must be read together. The specification of threats to others does not itself erase every otherwise authorized qualification standard directed to a grave risk to the worker. Stone gives decisive weight to that statutory scheme. Bragdon requires a concrete, individualized health assessment, while Sutton and Garrett keep coverage, qualification and accommodation separate. The EEOC's regulation deserves substantial persuasive weight because its objective evidence, significant-risk and accommodation conditions implement those limits; Mead requires actual delegated rulemaking authority rather than expertise alone, and that authority is present here. The result also follows from independent construction. No generalized paternalistic veto follows: Chevron must prove the regulation's actual conditions on the medical and accommodation record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The precise degree of established risk, reasonable accommodations, and ultimate summary-judgment entitlement remain on remand. The decision creates no broad power to control an employee’s medical choices.

## 88. National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm in part, reverse in part, and remand: reject revival of untimely discrete acts merely through relation to timely acts, but permit consideration of a single hostile environment with a contributing act inside the charge period.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Classify the challenged practices and identify the timely components on remand. Preserve permissible background evidence and independently established tolling, estoppel, or equitable defenses. Make no finding of unlawful harassment, discriminatory intent, employer attribution, or an amount recoverable.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Different temporal treatment for different statutory wrongs

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the filing rule on the summary-judgment record while reserving disputed facts about continuity and liability.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** How the statutory clock applies to completed decisions and to an environment constituted by a course of conduct.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the charge text to the actual nature of the asserted practice, informed by Harris and Oncale’s harassment standard, and propose the unresolved temporal rule.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A discrete discriminatory or retaliatory decision must be charged within its applicable period, subject to established equitable grounds. A charge concerning one hostile environment is timely for that practice when a contributing act occurs within the period; earlier components of the same environment remain relevant to liability, while unrelated old wrongs do not become timely.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Decide the charge-period treatment of these categories without recognizing a general continuing-violation exception for similar employment decisions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The charge provision measures time from the unlawful practice that occurred. Stone applies that text separately to discrete decisions and to the cumulative wrong recognized in Harris and Oncale. A discharge or refused promotion is ordinarily complete when it happens; similarity to a timely act does not restart its clock. A hostile environment can be constituted by a connected course of conduct, so a timely contributing act permits consideration of the same environment's earlier components. Text determines the unit of liability, while contextual examination determines whether the incidents form that unit; the statutory distinction carries decisive weight. Zipes preserves actual equitable grounds, and old acts may remain background evidence without becoming independently recoverable. The rule answers both temporal questions and leaves factual continuity, attribution and damages for ordinary application.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Distinct successive environments, the facts supporting equitable relief, employer attribution, and damages require their own determinations. Old conduct’s evidentiary relevance does not itself create recovery for an untimely discrete act.

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the accrual rule necessary to assess timeliness, without deciding ultimate contractual liability.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the Government’s advance repudiation necessarily starts the limitations period for a future prepayment-performance claim.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Winstar’s actual-contract approach and ordinary repudiation principles to the alleged promise, separating sovereign legislation from contractual timing.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When the Government promises to accept prepayment upon a borrower’s request, an advance statutory repudiation does not inevitably accrue the contract claim at enactment. The borrower may elect to sue on repudiation, but that option is not a compulsory earlier accrual date. The Government's assumption of the alleged promise for the timing issue permits a legal answer without deciding all liability defenses. Tender, refusal, any earlier election and each agreement's terms remain the facts needed to apply the six-year period, not legal questions left open.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The existence and authority of each promise, earlier elections or breaches, other statutory theories, recoverable losses, and any demand for specific performance remain outside this timing holding.

* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the entitlement to a prompt limited adversary hearing, with modified instructions governing the parties’ burdens.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require Farmer to establish by a preponderance of the evidence actual need for the identified funds to hire counsel and require the government to justify their continued restraint by the applicable probable-cause taint showing or another independently valid basis. Release the needed amount lacking such support. Do not order release of proved forfeitable proceeds or decide criminal guilt.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A supported need-and-legitimacy showing requires usable review

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Farmer’s sworn submissions and the specific report of legitimate seized property satisfy the initial substantial-showing requirement. Delaying review until after trial would defeat the counsel interest.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What hearing and burdens are required to test whether assets needed for defense may lawfully remain seized?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Mathews to this disputed post-seizure setting, distinguish the merits holdings of Monsanto and Caplin, and announce a defined hearing allocation.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A substantial, specific showing that restrained funds may be untainted and necessary for criminal defense triggers a prompt adversary hearing. The defendant must establish financial need by a preponderance of the evidence; once taint is materially contested, the government must justify continued restraint by the applicable probable-cause connection to forfeiture. Unsubstantiated demands for a hearing do not suffice, and probable cause of the offense alone does not establish every asset’s taint.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers a criminal-defense need materially impaired by seizure of allegedly legitimate funds. It does not compel an asset hearing in every case or a full trial of guilt.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mathews balancing carries decisive weight: the defense opportunity is time-sensitive, the concrete dispute creates meaningful error risk, and a focused hearing can reduce that risk without trying the indictment. Monsanto reserves this process question; treating it as already resolved would misstate precedent. The initial threshold respects legitimate enforcement needs but cannot demand conclusive innocence of the assets before allowing access to the evidence. Stone expressly selects the government’s burden to justify continued restraint once the threshold is met, rather than adopting a rule that makes the owner disprove the government’s case through inaccessible information. Need remains the defendant’s burden by a preponderance of the evidence, a fact principally within the defendant’s access. On this record the submissions warrant the hearing, while the ultimate amount and taint remain unproved.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Basic living expenses, interest on seized funds and a complete forfeiture merits judgment lie outside this counsel-focused appeal.

## 93. Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of New York, Inc. v. Village of Stratton

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand with instructions to prevent enforcement of the permit prerequisite against the protected noncommercial advocacy at issue.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Target the identification and prior-permission requirement as applied to the broad noncommercial canvassing category before the Court. Preserve residents’ right to decline visits, ordinary enforcement against fraud and trespass, and distinct commercial or fundraising rules not adjudicated here. No damages finding or entitlement to enter unwilling recipients’ property is ordered.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Require a fitted justification for advance permission

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the legal burden on the speakers’ intended protected activity, using the ordinance as construed below.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the village may impose advance registration and identification across this broad category of religious and political canvassing.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the protection for anonymous individual advocacy and examine the added burdens of prior permission and loss of spontaneity. Distinguish willing-listener access from compelled access to a home.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A municipality may not require advance identification and a permit for ordinary noncommercial religious or political canvassing where its asserted fraud, crime, and privacy interests lack an adequate fit to the full range of protected advocacy burdened. The absence of a fee or official discretion does not remove the constitutional burden.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Resolve this broad permit regime without a universal exemption from every law governing canvassing.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** McIntyre and Talley protect anonymous advocacy; Martin and the canvassing cases preserve the willing listener's choice while recognizing legitimate privacy and anti-fraud regulation. Stone applies that body of law to advance identification and permission for ordinary religious and political visits. The regime's fit to the actual protected activity is decisive: nonfinancial advocacy does not carry the fraud premise of solicitation, a permit without meaningful identity verification does little to identify criminals, and an owner can refuse contact through ordinary property rules. The rule also prohibits spontaneous speech and anonymous outreach before any misconduct. It fails even the intermediate means-end justification advanced to defend it, so no new universal scrutiny tier is required. The conclusion covers the broad noncommercial prerequisite without deciding narrower commercial registration, trespass or financial-disclosure laws.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A narrowly drawn commercial regulation, actual fraud, trespass after an owner’s refusal, and materially different disclosure obligations require separate analysis.

## 94. Barnes v. Gorman

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-17, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the punitive-damages component of the Eighth Circuit judgment.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the exclusion of punitive damages under these two statutory channels. Preserve the compensatory component and all independently available relief. Award no new amount and decide no unpreserved municipal-immunity defense.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Statutory limits on punitive relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The remedial question is properly presented; the existing finding of liability does not answer it. The alternative government-immunity ground need not be reached.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the remedies incorporated by Title II and §504 authorize an additional punitive award.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools as to compensatory relief; distinguish its make-whole rationale from an unresolved punitive remedy under these incorporated Title VI channels. No precedent is overruled.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The remedies incorporated from Title VI authorize compensation and appropriate equitable correction but do not, without a sufficient statutory basis for punitive exposure, authorize punishment beyond making good the discrimination injury. The punitive award cannot stand through these statutes.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule reaches punitive awards under the identified Title II and §504 channels. It leaves damages authorized by a different enactment, ordinary compensation, equitable relief, and attorney-fee authority to their own sources.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The remedial cross-references, read against the traditional distinction between making good an injury and imposing an additional penalty, are decisive. Franklin recognized compensation for the proved discrimination injury; it did not hold that every judicially available sanction automatically follows. Neither the incorporation provisions nor the funding obligation identifies an additional punitive liability of indeterminate magnitude. The recipient-notice principle reinforces that reading, but does not convert every civil-rights remedy into a literal contract remedy or immunize intentional discrimination. Gorman’s grave transport injuries support compensation; their severity does not itself authorize a separate punishment. This provision-specific construction supplies the necessary answer without reaching the alternative municipal-immunity question or diminishing compensatory recovery.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Private actions under different remedial statutes and the unpreserved municipal-immunity alternative fall outside the ground needed here.
