## Rule B objections: unchanged after the proposal

The proposal accepts reasonable as an independent initial condition, respects actual vacancy and qualification, and permits evidence of noncash effects and employee expectations. Those are meaningful statutory limits. They do not answer Rule B's conclusion that requiring the typical employer to litigate the effect of each seniority exception can itself undermine predictable allocation. Under Rule B, the established conflict ordinarily defeats facial reasonableness, with the employee able to establish special circumstances before the employer must demonstrate particular undue hardship. The proposal expressly denies that ordinary inference. A direction to take reliance seriously within ordinary reasonableness and employer hardship does not preserve the proposition it rejects.

His concurrence, 406–408, accepts the presumption and places on Barnett the task of answering the relevant application questions to overcome it. | The proposal avoids effectiveness alone and preserves noncash concerns, but permits an ordinary showing without overcoming the seniority inference. No changed legal, factual or remedial premise answers his accepted presumption. Retain Rule B vacatur and remand. His statement that the circuit properly rejected the employer's absolute rule and reversed summary judgment does not withdraw the seniority standard he expressly joins. |
| Kennedy | His full historical join at 393 and 396–406 gives established seniority expectations an independent ordinary effect, including under an employer-created policy. They do not furnish a new distinction. His current Martin and Olmstead positions preserve actual program and allocation limits without displacing this Title I ground. | Breyer | The explanation at 400–405 distinguishes effects on fellow employees from business hardship and treats objective seniority as a system whose benefits depend on ordinary consistency. The strongest possible reason for these three to reconsider is that the proposed statutory sequence avoids making reasonable redundant while preventing contractual assumptions about a unilateral policy. That argument is already present in the frozen Rule A and in the historical opposing writings. Rule B expressly confronted both points and selected the generalization based on expectations rather than enforceable possession alone. The present proposal supplies no distinct revision that resolves that choice on these Associates' own date-eligible grounds. The audit therefore rejects a changed join rather than treating respectful language about reliance as an accommodation of the operative objection.

## O'Connor's preferred rule and exact mandate inference

Her concurrence at 408–411 contains two distinct source-supported positions: a preferred legal-entitlement/vacancy rule and a willing full join to Rule B to permit an actual statutory majority where her preferred rule cannot do so. The preferred rule remains a real preassembly commitment, not an inference invented from the proposal.

Under that preferred rule, a third party's actual occupancy or enforceable entitlement prevents the position from being vacant. Mere expectation under an unenforceable seniority policy supplies no legal possessor. At 410 she applies the employer's disclaimer and conceded absence of legally enforceable employee rights to this particular opening and treats the requested accommodation as reasonable. She then preserves the employer's burden of particular undue hardship. At 411 she objects that Rule B's special-circumstances stage blurs general reasonableness and particular hardship.

**Preferred formal mandate confirmed, with exact scope:** Affirm the reviewed reversal of policy-based employer summary judgment and return for further proceedings consistent with her legal-entitlement rule, the satisfied initial reasonableness showing on this seniority question, and the employer's actual hardship defense. This is a supported assembly inference from her preferred legal application. It is not an express historical separate affirmance vote: historically she joined the Court's vacatur and its full rule for the reason she states. It does not affirm every Ninth Circuit doctrinal formulation, award the mailroom position, decide separate eligibility predicates or reopen retaliation. The independent cargo and interactive-process grounds retain their existing scope.

The proposed phrase remanding for ordinary reasonableness and particular hardship must not be used to erase her initial-showing conclusion. She has resolved the seniority objection to reasonableness under her preferred approach. Other independent predicates can retain their actual procedural status; that is different from returning this same policy-only question wholly unassessed. Souter and Ginsburg likewise find the initial showing satisfied at 423–424 and would affirm; their explanation must not be weakened to obtain broader agreement.

Her preferred position can therefore support the foregoing confined affirmance judgment alongside Souter and Ginsburg, although their full rationales differ. It does not establish her complete agreement with every ordinary-inquiry sentence, or supply agreement by any Rule B or Rule C Associate. If an opinion attributes a complete initial-showing holding to a Court coalition, each additional Justice must actually adopt that application.

**Conditional Rule B join preserved:** O'Connor may join Rule B in full under the previously frozen condition that her preferred resolution cannot command a majority and her full adoption enables Rule B to become the actual Court's statutory rule, with practical special circumstances and the separate hardship defense intact. The source explains why she accepts that specific alternative and why it often converges with her preferred approach. Her institutional concern does not authorize an unconditional transfer, a different invented framework, or a vote change that still produces no statutory majority. This clean audit has no remaining private position and makes no determination that the condition is met.

## No common mandate from the unchanged positions

Rule B's vacatur returns the reviewed seniority question for a claimant opportunity to show special circumstances under an ordinary presumption. Rule C's reversal rejects the claimed override of this established allocation as outside the statutory obligation. The proposal's affirmance retains the rejection of employer summary judgment without the seniority presumption, and the Rule A application treats the employee's initial showing as satisfied. These are materially different legal instructions and procedural consequences.

The following combinations do not cure the conflict:

- Combining Rule B vacatur with Rule C reversal solely because both disturb the appellate judgment does not supply an agreed instruction concerning whether Barnett may establish an exception. Rule C's express objection to the exception and renewed opportunity prevents that inference.
- Calling the Rule B/C combination a common ordinary unreasonableness proposition does not establish the shared rationale or remedy. Rule C's anterior statutory exclusion and Rule B's rebuttable reasonableness analysis perform different work and have different consequences in this case.
- Combining all preferences for some further proceedings conceals that Rule C permits no further merits opportunity to establish this priority, Rule B requires a distinct special showing, and Rule A would not impose it.
- Leaving the seniority question unanswered while affirming or recognizing independent unreviewed grounds would not resolve the question the Court took up. Stevens preserves those grounds but expressly supports deciding seniority under Rule B.

No new common operative mandate is established by this proposal. That conclusion is confined to the supplied commitments; it does not claim that an actual full Court can never reconsider or reach a judgment. No equal division can be entered from this multiway split, and no historical Chief Justice's vote may fill the missing support.

## Supported further consideration and precise remaining barrier

The genuinely available preexisting accommodation is O'Connor's conditional full Rule B join. Assembly may test that condition against the actual complete set of positions without new invention. No additional Rule B or Rule C change is approved by this audit.

Further section 8 work must identify an actual new operative formulation answering one of the remaining objections, or a material neutral premise independently supported and returned through the required clean stages. Merely renaming the same ordinary inquiry, emphasizing the already known noncontractual policy, or obtaining an author's preference supplies no such basis. The present material also supplies no vote for an evidentiary reopening solely because the operator lacks the complete handbook.

If the complete assembly still lacks a judgment majority, section 8 permits supported further consideration, but this handoff contains no frozen votes for reargument, supplemental briefing, dismissal or another dated procedural action. A possible further-consideration event would need its own neutral procedural question, actual participation and modeled commitments, followed by ordinary reconciliation and assembly. This is permission to evaluate a lawful next step, not an order, a prediction of votes or a terminal resolution. Until an actual supported route is established, the matter remains open.

**Exact operator barrier:** The circulated ordinary-inquiry proposal does not answer the three Rule B Associates' ordinary seniority-inference objection or the two Rule C Associates' disability-created-obstacle objection. O'Connor's preferred affirmance and conditional Rule B alternative retain their stated scope, and no common seniority mandate is supplied by aggregating incompatible vacatur, reversal and affirmance positions. The complete assembly must establish an actual judgment coalition or model a supported further-consideration action; this audit supplies neither a missing vote nor a terminal action.
