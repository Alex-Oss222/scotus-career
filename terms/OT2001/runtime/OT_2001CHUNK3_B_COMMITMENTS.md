## 39. United States v. Vonn — March 4, 2002

### Review scope and proposed controlling rules

The Court may decide the two review rules on certiorari from the Ninth Circuit. The Rule 11(c)(3) omission is an error despite current representation. The question is its appellate consequence, not whether a judge may omit the required advice. Defense silence is forfeiture rather than a proved intentional waiver. The later motion to withdraw on a different ground does not preserve the advice claim. The prosecutor's attempted correction at the second hearing is part of the actual record; it does not establish that Vonn himself sought relief on this ground or that the omission was corrected.

**Component A — review standard.** Seven Associates would hold that Rule 11(h) does not displace Rule 52(b) for an unpreserved claim. Such a defendant ordinarily bears the substantial-rights burden, and correction remains subject to the distinct discretionary condition. The mandatory trial-counsel warning and the general appellate review rule can both operate. No special reasonable-probability formula, constitutional-plea invalidity rule or collateral-review rule is introduced. Stevens would retain the Government's harmlessness burden under Rule 11(h) for this judge-controlled plea procedure.

**Component B — relevant record.** All eight would reject the colloquy-only limit. A reviewing court may consider relevant portions of the official record bearing on the defendant's knowledge and the omission's effect, including the earlier advisements. Such material does not substitute for the judge's required personal colloquy or become conclusive merely because counsel signed a form. No off-record conversation may be invented.

**Component C — application and remedy.** Seven would vacate the appellate judgment and return it for Rule 52(b) review using the relevant official record. Stevens would also return the matter for whole-record review but under Rule 11(h)'s Government burden. No Justice here commits to automatic reinstatement, immediate withdrawal of every plea, a finding of harmlessness, a quantified prejudice test or resolution of the unresolved perjury-warning contention. The Ninth Circuit must apply the correct standard and address other properly remaining issues.

## 40. Delaney v. DeTella — March 4, 2002

### Components and common lawful ground

**Component A — appellate reach.** All eight accept legal review of immunity on the assumed plaintiff-favorable facts. They would reject any attempt to obtain appellate resolution of disputed injury, awareness, causation or the guards' actual authority. Johnson v. Jones and Behrens distinguish that unavailable factual review from the legal issue properly before the Court. The appeal's factual objections may be dismissed or left outside the decision without dismissing the legal immunity issue.

**Component B — constitutional sufficiency.** Seven would hold that the assumed combination of six months without meaningful exercise, extremely cramped confinement, serious physical and psychological effects, actual awareness of the substantial risk and no reasonable response states a violation under Farmer. Thomas accepts that legal consequence under the controlling, unchallenged conditions framework without joining a broader independent constitutional endorsement of every deprivation characterized as harmful. None requires completed grave physical injury before a serious known risk can be unconstitutional. None treats exercise as an absolute entitlement regardless of acute security danger or converts five hours into a nationwide constitutional quota.

**Component C — qualified immunity.** All eight would affirm rejection of the legal immunity defense on the proper assumed account. By 1996, Farmer supplied the actual-awareness and unreasonable-response rule. Davenport concerned the same institution's segregated prisoners and prolonged exercise deprivation; French and Jamison-Bey reinforced the danger, and Anderson v. Romero identified the acute-security-risk qualification. Cases involving shorter restrictions, alternative exercise or a particular dangerous prisoner did not authorize this assumed six-month near-total deprivation with known serious risk. Exact factual identity is unnecessary under Anderson v. Creighton and Elder. Later cases supply no retroactive notice.

**Component D — remedy.** All eight would affirm the denial of summary judgment at this legal scope and return for ordinary proceedings. The judgment establishes no disputed fact, damages entitlement, mandatory trial or collective responsibility. Each defendant may contest actual knowledge, power to respond, reasonable response, injury and causation. A guard without authority or culpable personal involvement may prevail on a proper record; that possibility is not an established defense on this interlocutory appeal. Searles's remedial distinctions remain for any actually presented compensation issue and do not decide this immunity appeal.

## 43. Edelman v. Lynchburg College — March 19, 2002

### Components and proposed common rule

**Component A — verification timing.** All eight would hold that section 706(b)'s oath requirement does not require verification of an otherwise sufficient timely written charge before section 706(e)(1)'s filing period expires. The relation-back regulation validly allows that defect to be cured. It preserves actual verification and timely initiation; it does not make an unverified final charge sufficient indefinitely or extend the deadline for commencing a charge.

**Component B — interpretive authority.** All eight can join a sufficient independent statutory construction: the filing and verification provisions do not expressly make their performance simultaneous, and the procedural regulation gives effect to both requirements. Section 713(a)'s procedural delegation supports that operation. Mead requires an actual delegation and does not make formal labels decisive; the published procedural rule differs from the revisable classification letter there. No resolution of every Chevron question is necessary because the text and established verification practice sustain the rule. Scalia would additionally treat the authoritative procedural implementation as entitled to deference within the delegated ambiguity; that broader premise need not enter a common opinion.

**Component C — judgment and remand.** All eight would vacate the Fourth Circuit's judgment and remand for further proceedings under the valid relation-back rule. This removes the categorical verification ground without deciding whether the November letter actually qualified as a charge. The alternative ground concerning the letter's character and the remaining defenses must receive ordinary resolution. This is no discrimination judgment, tenure order or award of relief. The already existing verification is not imagined anew.
