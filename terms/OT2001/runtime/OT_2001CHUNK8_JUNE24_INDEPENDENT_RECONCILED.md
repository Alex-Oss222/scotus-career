## 111. BE&K Construction Co. v. NLRB

### Reconciliation of judgment and rationale

The historical judgment reversed and remanded unanimously. O'Connor's historical opinion had four present Associates: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Scalia and Thomas additionally supported a prospective objective-baselessness rule. Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, agreed only with a confined rejection of the actual treatment of reasonably based unsuccessful litigation; those four rejected broader implications and the description of Bill Johnson's completed-suit discussion as mere dicta.

The provisional eight-Justice result survives. The provisional suggestion that all eight could share the full constitutional-concern and Bill Johnson's rationale requires qualification. The frozen material identifies no changed premise that would make Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg or Breyer abandon their concrete historical objections. PRE's actual single-suit antitrust rule was already part of the historical setting and remains bounded here. Town & Country's statutory-boundary principle and the administrative remand do not erase their distinct labor-law view. History is strong evidence at this unresolved fork.

**B1 — common operative ground, eight Associates.** Section 8(a)(1) does not authorize this Board standard to condemn a reasonably based but unsuccessful suit merely because it seeks to stop protected union activity or is accompanied by hostility to the union. An unsuccessful outcome and that broad conception of retaliatory motive do not adequately distinguish a genuine attempt to obtain judicial relief from abuse of litigation. The actual standard and dependent enforcement cannot stand. This proposition does not establish that every underlying claim was reasonable or that stronger, different evidence of misuse could never support liability.

**B2 — broader explanation, four Associates.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas support the full statutory-avoidance account: retrospective declarations of illegality burden petitioning; reasonably based unsuccessful claims may present genuine grievances and advance public airing, legal development and peaceful dispute resolution; a broad retaliation category does not remove serious constitutional doubt. Section 8(a)(1)'s general language need not be read to reach that whole class. These four treat Bill Johnson's discussion of completed litigation as not dispositive of this issue, including its characterization as dicta. The other four are not counted in that characterization or in an implication that antitrust and labor-law immunity must be coextensive.

**B2 — limited concurrence, four Associates.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the narrow B1 rejection and the judgment, while preserving Bill Johnson's as meaningful authority for completed retaliatory suits under a properly confined inquiry. They reject the inference that losing a reasonable suit plus union hostility virtually establishes retaliation. They leave room for a suit that would not have been brought but for a purpose to impose process costs regardless of outcome, and for a broader course of antiunion conduct, if a valid rule and actual supported record establish that distinct case. They regard the labor statute's protection against antiunion litigation, the Board's screening role, and its remedial rather than treble-damages setting as legally material differences from antitrust. Their position is not a finding that such an alternative violation occurred here.

**B2 — additional position, Scalia and Thomas.** They favor applying the objective-baselessness-before-subjective-abuse gate to an appropriate ordinary single-suit NLRA case, preserving PRE's category limits. Their institutional ground is that agency motive findings under substantial-evidence review threaten access to an Article III court at least as seriously as a later judicial antitrust action. This explains their support for the broader account and their disagreement with the limited concurrence. The historical writing expressly reserved adoption for an appropriate case; the current narrow disposition likewise does not announce the complete gate as an eight-Justice holding or treat this preference as a decided replacement rule. Ordinary sanctions imposed by courts themselves remain distinct.

## 115. United States v. Ruiz

### Reconciliation and component holdings

The historical judgment reversed unanimously; the seven present Associates other than Thomas joined the Court's rationale, and Thomas concurred in the judgment. That structure is consistent with the provisional commitments and the current simulated plea/disclosure authorities. None supplies a material changed premise requiring a historical outcome departure.

**R1 — jurisdictional determination.** Section 3742(a)(1) permits review of a sentence imposed in violation of law. A mistaken belief that departure authority is absent could qualify, but the supplied judge understood that authority. Mere disagreement with the discretionary refusal supplies no appeal. The asserted constitutional condition nevertheless had to be resolved to determine whether it established the statutory ground; Mine Workers permits that jurisdictional inquiry. Once the constitutional claim fails, section 3742(a)(1) supplies no jurisdiction to review the discretionary refusal on this theory. Do not shorten this to either unlimited appellate jurisdiction or lack of power even to determine the legal claim. Steel Co. is respected because the merits question is necessary to determine the claimed jurisdiction, not advisory resolution on assumed authority.

**R2 — impeachment.** The Constitution does not require advance disclosure of material impeachment information before entry into the plea agreement at issue. The trial-disclosure guarantee, and the practical value of evidence for preparation, do not require complete evidentiary knowledge before a competent defendant makes an actually informed and voluntary choice about trial. A knowing choice requires understanding of the right, charge and relevant consequences in the constitutional sense; it does not require advance knowledge of every witness weakness. This holds for the asserted categorical disclosure condition without finding impeachment unimportant or every actual plea valid.

Seven Associates adopt that stage-specific explanation with the practical considerations in the frozen packet: legitimate early resolution and witness confidentiality, the continuing innocence-disclosure promise and ordinary plea safeguards. Those are legal considerations, not a finding that this case's particular witness faced danger. Kyles's preparation uses remain part of its trial-fairness inquiry, and Vonn's mandatory judicial duty and whole-record rule are not erased.

**R3 — affirmative-defense information.** This is a separate holding, confined to the agreement presented, including the continuing factual-innocence disclosure promise. The Constitution does not require advance provision of the identified affirmative-defense information as a condition of this plea bargaining offer. Most of the R2 reasons apply: informed choice can allocate uncertainty about proof; the timing requirement can impose substantial preparation costs; and the proposed categorical requirement is not entailed by the fair-trial guarantee. The information is not relabeled impeachment, and affirmative defenses are not relabeled factual innocence. No proved concealed dispositive defense, false representation, factual-innocence suppression, defective advice or actual uninformed plea is decided. The ruling is substantive rejection of this asserted categorical duty, not merely a finding that Ruiz failed to identify a hidden item.

**R4 — consequence.** Reverse the Ninth Circuit judgment vacating the sentence on the unconstitutional-condition theory. The rejected offer supplies no enforceable promise or automatic two-level reduction, and Ruiz did not assent to this waiver merely by later pleading guilty without an agreement. Absence of an accepted bargain does not itself defeat every unconstitutional-condition claim; this one fails because the identified condition is not constitutionally invalid at the decided scope. No general innocence prerequisite or duplicative harmlessness hurdle is added.

## 116. Kirk v. Louisiana

### Formal remedy reconciliation

The historical per curiam reversed and remanded because the state appellate court treated probable cause as sufficient, expressly leaving both exigency and the asserted independent-source theory undecided. The provisional handoff proposed vacatur for that same unresolved posture. The current packet identifies no different event, preservation fact, exigency finding or adjudicated suppression consequence. The distinction between reversal and vacatur therefore needs a legal basis beyond a general preference for corrected-rule remands.

The entering Kyllo remand concerned the newly corrected classification of a technological examination as a search, an established absence of an exception for that scan, and a preserved derivative-evidence claim under a conditional plea. Its particular vacatur does not create a universal remedial rule for every incomplete suppression analysis. Kirk instead corrects a direct rejection of Payton's already controlling threshold requirement. McArthur itself reversed its erroneous suppression ground while preserving independent issues. Neither source gives any of these eight Justices a material reason to replace the unchanged comparator's precise reversal here. Accordingly, all eight reconciled commitments are **reverse and remand**, not vacate and remand. The disposition rejects the erroneous legal ground without deciding the remaining conviction outcome.

### Common operative rule and remand

K1–K3 have eight direct Associate joins. Probable cause to arrest or believe evidence is within a home does not itself authorize nonconsensual entry without an appropriate warrant; a valid exception, including probable cause plus exigent circumstances where applicable, must independently justify crossing the threshold. Arrest authority and a search incident to arrest cannot supply the missing home-entry authority. A later warrant does not retroactively authorize that earlier entry.

Reverse the state appellate judgment insofar as it sustains the warrantless entry, arrest and search on probable cause alone, and remand for proceedings consistent with Payton. The receiving court must address the unresolved exigency question on the record and any properly remaining consequence or independent-source issue under its own governing requirements. The officers' stated destruction concern is not an established exigency; nor does the appellate omission establish that exigency was absent. A later warrant is not automatically independent, but a genuinely independent source may affect admissibility without validating the entry. No blanket exclusion or conclusive conviction invalidation follows.
