## Supported facts

- The complaint alleges willful violations of FCRA ?1681e(a) in the disputed disclosures and seeks actual and punitive damages, as well as injunctive relief. The contemporaneous ?1681n provides actual damages and punitive damages the court may allow for willful noncompliance; the 1996 statutory-damages amendment is later than the events and does not govern them.
- The complaint does not allege that TRW materially and willfully misrepresented information that the FCRA required it to disclose to Andrews. No pleaded predicate is established for the express ?1681p misrepresentation exception.
- Andrews first presented in this Court the alternative argument that liability did not arise until actual damages materialized, despite rejection of a general discovery approach. That alternative was not raised or briefed below. The Government did not join Andrews in advancing it. Her position placed the asserted actual damages in May 1995.
- The challenged first two disclosures occurred on July 25 and September 27, 1994; the complaint was filed October 21, 1996. The claims based on the October 28, 1994 and January 3, 1995 disclosures have distinct timeliness and merits posture. The information-accuracy claim resolved by jury and the other defendant/state-law dispositions are outside this review.
