## Entering law and common operative proposition

The opening legal cutoff is June 28, 2001; the event-date cutoff is January 8, 2002. No new effective current-term authority or coordinated same-day sequence is supplied. The Standards and Tests rule titled **Mitigating measures in actual ADA disability**, effective from Sutton and Murphy on June 22, 1999, requires assessment of the underlying impairment without beneficial mitigation eliminating its substantial limitation. Individual proof remains necessary, and qualification, accommodation and safety are distinct. The original ADA governs; no later amendment enters.

Bragdon supplies individualized substantiality and does not restrict major activities categorically to economic or everyday activity. Sutton's and Murphy's occupational-breadth holdings concern the particular working theories and cannot automatically define manual tasks. Their prior separate opinions preserve the distinction between an impairment and a substantial limitation but establish no permanence or near-total-incapacity condition. Kirkingburg's independent safety-qualification principle does not settle this coverage question.

All eight Associates adopt the same bounded proposition: Substantial limitation of manual tasks requires an individualized assessment of important manual functions and the practical breadth, severity and duration of their impairment in the person's life. Work and nonwork evidence can bear on that inquiry. Inability to perform the specialized or repetitive tasks of a particular production assignment does not establish the statutory limitation merely because those tasks can be grouped as a class of jobs. Ability to perform some household activities likewise does not conclusively negate substantial limitations of other important manual functions. The underlying impairment must be assessed under Sutton and Murphy.

These considerations explain application of the substantial-limitation requirement; they create no mandatory checklist, numerical threshold or fixed duration minimum. The proposition requires neither near-total loss of manual capacity nor categorical exclusion of workplace evidence. It does not replace the statutory substantiality threshold with a rule that any important task restriction suffices.

## Two-path test within the corrected question

**Sustain Williams's partial judgment:** The strongest supported argument is that repetitive gripping, sustained movement and overhead use are important manual capacities, and evidence of those limitations does not become legally irrelevant because it comes from work. Ability to perform some self-care does not necessarily contradict substantial limitation. The difficulty is that the appellate judgment used a class-of-job-related-tasks formulation to establish a different major life activity, while the supplied record contains competing accounts of functional breadth. The correct rule does not make that shortcut sufficient to establish Williams's entitlement as a matter of law.

**Vacate Williams's partial judgment and remand:** Toyota's strongest supported argument identifies precisely that legal mismatch. Its proper consequence is reassessment of the employee's favorable partial judgment under the activity-specific inquiry and ordinary summary-judgment standards. The strongest counterargument is that a remand might wrongly discount substantial work-related limitations or imply a more demanding disability threshold. The common rule answers it by keeping that evidence relevant and preserving Sutton, Murphy and Bragdon's individual inquiry without requiring near-total or permanent loss.

**Excluded remedial extension:** Reinstatement of Toyota's separate judgment cannot be justified simply by rejection of Williams's partial judgment. It lies outside the accepted question, and the legal defect in one party's favorable judgment does not itself establish the other party's entitlement. No inference about the merits of Toyota's separate request follows from that scope ruling.

## Exact remedy and procedural limits

Vacate the appellate partial summary judgment in Williams's favor on disability under the manual-tasks theory and remand for reassessment under the corrected activity-specific rule and ordinary summary-judgment standards. The Court neither reinstates Toyota's separate summary judgment nor adjudicates its entitlement to that relief. The scope of review, rather than an inferred merits answer, supplies that independent limit.

The lower courts may conduct ordinary further proceedings as procedurally warranted in the continuing case. This remand does not reopen an independent unreviewed coverage branch or judgment, revive a forfeited theory, confer a new claim, or direct a ruling on qualification, accommodation, safety, liability or remedy. Such matters retain whatever procedural status and independent elements they otherwise have. No mandatory new trial, evidentiary hearing, supplementation or employment relief is ordered. No disability finding is made for either side.
