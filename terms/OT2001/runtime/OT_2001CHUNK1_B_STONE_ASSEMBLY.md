## 9. United States v. Knights

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2001-12-10, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical purpose-based suppression ruling and remand under a condition-and-reasonable-suspicion analysis.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Admit the evidence if no independently preserved defect remains after application of that rule. Preserve any distinct scope or factual issue actually open on the record; do not declare the condition a blanket waiver.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: An objectively justified search does not fail merely because it investigates crime

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach this search’s reasonableness, with reasonable suspicion and the express condition as necessary premises.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether ordinary investigative purpose defeats an otherwise justified probation search.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the governing reasonableness and independent-review principles in a new probation-search combination.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A search of a probationer’s residence within a known, lawful search condition is reasonable when supported by particularized reasonable suspicion of criminal activity under the circumstances presented. Its ordinary criminal-investigative purpose does not independently invalidate it. Probation and notice diminish relevant expectations without extinguishing Fourth Amendment protection or establishing blanket voluntary consent.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding requires the combination present here. It does not authorize suspicionless residential searches or all conditions a sentencing court might impose.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The lower court’s purpose restriction treats a justified investigation as disqualifying even though the condition expressly contemplates law-enforcement officers and objective grounds connect the residence to crime. The constitutional inquiry should examine the actual intrusion and justification. The supervision relationship is relevant to the privacy balance, while individualized suspicion prevents the condition from operating as an unrestricted license. Scott remains meaningful because an unconstitutional search does not become immune from exclusion merely through the officer’s supervisory status. This is a reasonableness balance under the Fourth Amendment, not an inference of blanket contractual consent. The condition, notice, probation relationship, residential intrusion, and individualized suspicion do distinct work; the objectively supported suspicion and limited search authority are decisive in combination. Stone distinguishes Griffin’s special-needs justification without treating it as the exclusive means of sustaining a supervision search, and reaffirms Scott’s exclusion rule for an unconstitutional search. The present combination establishes a defined sufficient ground, not a ruling on suspicionless searches or unbounded conditions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Suspicionless searches, blanket consent, and unpresented conditions are reserved.

## 11. Schrader v. Blackwell +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2001-12-10, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining the categorical exclusion of truthful affiliation information and remand for application of the burden-and-justification rule and appropriately bounded prospective relief.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The recurring election dispute remains reviewable despite completion of the election. Require consideration of a truthful, verifiable affiliation designation that does not falsely imply recognized-party nomination, and tailor prospective relief accordingly. Do not change the completed ballot or confer full party recognition.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The state must justify the information it withholds

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The recurring statutory restriction affects the party and its candidates in elections too short for complete review; the record supports the capable-of-repetition exception applied below. Reach the ballot-label question without ordering a new mootness inquiry merely because the election ended.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether full party recognition can be the categorical prerequisite for any truthful affiliation label.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the governing burden-and-fit framework in a new label-specific setting. Do not treat Timmons as deciding a materially different ballot burden.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Where a candidate independently qualifies for the ballot and establishes a genuine party affiliation, a state must justify withholding all accurate affiliation information by the actual burden and its demonstrated ballot interests. It may distinguish formal recognized-party nomination from affiliation through a neutral designation. Full party recognition is not, by itself, a sufficient explanation for prohibiting every truthful label.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not compel misleading or unverifiable labels, eliminate party-recognition thresholds, or confer other recognized-party privileges.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ohio has a legitimate interest in making the ballot’s official representations accurate. That interest can support reserving a formal nomination label, but the record must explain why no truthful affiliation format would serve it. The candidate has already met the access requirement, so a separate party-wide threshold needs its own connection to the information withheld. Timmons’s recognition of alternate channels of association is relevant to burden but not dispositive. The remand should resolve actual affiliation, wording, and administrative fit rather than assume the only choices are complete party recognition or silence. Anderson–Burdick supplies the burden-sensitive standard; no racial tier or newly created right to the state’s endorsement is necessary. Timmons is distinguished because a ban on fusion nomination does not decide whether a qualified candidate can convey truthful affiliation in a format that distinguishes nomination from association. The rule’s practical operation and the State’s actual accuracy interests carry decisive weight within that received standard. A neutral verification requirement may protect accuracy; an unexplained demand for full party status does not answer the distinct labeling burden.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The exact permissible label and any genuinely disputed verification facts remain for remedial application. Full party recognition and retrospective alteration of the completed election are outside the requested relief.

## 13. Toyota Motor Manufacturing, Kentucky, Inc. v. Williams

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-08, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Sixth Circuit’s partial summary judgment and remand for an individualized manual-activity determination under the governing disability rules.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Assess Williams’s underlying limitations without discounting them through beneficial mitigation. Do not impose a categorical requirement of permanent or nearly total inability, and do not grant Toyota automatic judgment. Keep accommodation, qualification, and other preserved coverage routes separate.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Measure the activity without importing a different activity’s shortcut

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the manual-task standard and summary-judgment error; leave genuinely disputed function and remaining liability elements for remand.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What evidence establishes substantial limitation of manual tasks.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Sutton and Murphy’s mitigation rule and propose an activity-specific clarification. Reject an unsupported categorical severe-and-permanent threshold.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A manual-task disability requires an individualized substantial limitation of important manual functioning, considered in breadth, frequency, duration, and practical effect. Inability to perform a class of occupational tasks is relevant but not conclusive merely because working has its own class-of-jobs inquiry. Important work-related functions cannot be excluded simply because they occur at work. Beneficial mitigation is disregarded under the governing baseline; ability to perform some household tasks does not automatically defeat coverage.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding does not make every task difficulty a disability or treat a diagnosis as sufficient. It permits consideration of expected duration without converting permanence into an indispensable element.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The appellate court moved too quickly from a job-task category to a different statutory activity. Toyota is entitled to correction of that reasoning, but not to a new rule that only the most pervasive daily-life limitations count. Sustained gripping, repetition, and reaching may be important capacities across life even where the person retains other functions. The record must identify the actual limitation and its practical magnitude. That inquiry also prevents accommodation analysis from silently determining coverage and preserves Sutton’s deliberate rejection of counting successful mitigation against the claimant. The statute’s individualized substantial-limitation requirement is decisive; the functional evidence applies that requirement rather than creating a new checklist. Breadth, frequency, duration, and practical effect are relevant considerations, not separately mandatory elements. Stone reaffirms Sutton and Murphy on mitigation and distinguishes the class-of-jobs standard for working from the manual-task activity. He rejects permanence as an absolute prerequisite because expected duration informs substantiality without rewriting substantial limitation as total incapacity. The new articulation is necessary to correct the legal standard; actual disability still requires proof.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Remaining factual disputes, other preserved disability routes, qualification, and accommodation are not finally adjudicated.

## 14. Great-West Life & Annuity Insurance Co. v. Knudson

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-08, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm dismissal of the particular reimbursement claim because it seeks personal payment without establishing the property-and-defendant basis required for the asserted equitable relief.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Award no reimbursement on the present theory. Leave any independently available, properly pleaded claim against an appropriate holder to ordinary procedural rules; do not order amendment, join the trust by implication, or adjudicate absent recipients’ property.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: An equitable remedy requires its own legal and factual basis

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the statutory remedy; do not decide an unnecessary broader reimbursement entitlement or absent-party claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the present demand is a traditional equitable recovery or a personal debt action.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Mertens and Harris Trust while proposing a specific clarification for this reimbursement posture.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A contractual reimbursement clause does not alone make a demand for personal payment appropriate equitable relief. A claimant invoking a constructive trust or equitable lien must identify the property and defendant connection required by that traditional remedy, subject to its defenses. When the present claim seeks satisfaction of a debt from the named defendants rather than recovery of qualifying property held by them, the equitable-relief provision does not authorize it.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding distinguishes remedies rather than categorically excluding monetary equity. It does not foreclose an otherwise valid claim against an appropriate recipient merely because the property originated in a settlement.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mertens makes the statutory category meaningful, while Harris Trust shows that genuine equitable recovery can reach a nonfiduciary recipient. Those propositions coexist only if the remedy’s actual prerequisites matter. Here the beneficiaries’ asserted contractual debt cannot substitute for identifying the property and holder against whom equity operates. The settlement’s distribution therefore matters to relief, not merely to collectability. A court must not cure a mismatch by treating a trust or attorney as if already before it. Traditional remedy classification carries decisive weight because Mertens gives equitable relief that statutory meaning. Stone reaffirms that construction despite his earlier disagreement; neither a changed vote nor a preference for reimbursement supplies a reason to reopen it. Harris Trust is reconciled through identifiable property, a proper recipient, and applicable equitable defenses. The named defendants’ lack of the asserted fund matters to the requested remedy, not to sympathy for either party. This remedy-specific disposition makes a broader ruling on all ERISA reimbursement clauses unnecessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Claims against absent recipients, any new tracing theory, and unnecessary contract interpretation remain undecided.

## 15. Searles v. Van Bebber +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-08, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the compensatory-award vacatur, nominal-damages entitlement, and availability of punitive damages; require one dollar nominal damages, a new determination of punitive amount, and reconsideration of the fee award.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the jury’s constitutional-liability finding, its finding of the culpable state of mind supporting punitive damages, its no-physical-injury finding, and the verdicts for the other officials. Vacate the $3,650 compensatory award and $42,500 punitive amount; retry punitive amount under instructions excluding reliance on the invalid compensation. Reconsider attorney fees in light of the lawful recovery.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A limitation on distress compensation is not immunity from the right

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Liability, punitive culpability, and absence of physical injury have been found by the jury and are preserved. The statutory availability of relief and the infected punitive amount are the reviewed remedial questions.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which damages remain legally available without physical injury.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Harris v. Garner’s confinement-at-filing and mental-or-emotional-injury limitation at its actual scope; resolve the distinct nominal and punitive remedies it did not foreclose.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Section 1997e(e) bars the compensatory award for mental or emotional injury absent its physical-injury prerequisite. It does not eliminate nominal damages for the established constitutional violation or punitive damages based on the separately established culpability. The jury’s punitive culpability finding survives; the amount cannot survive an instruction tying it to the invalid compensatory award. Nominal damages of one dollar and a properly instructed punitive-amount determination are required, followed by fee reconsideration.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns the specified injury category and distinct remedies. It does not presume economic loss, establish punitive culpability from liability alone, or set an automatic punitive-to-nominal ratio.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The enacted restriction identifies an injury category; it does not erase the right or every remedy when no bodily injury occurs. Text and the different legal functions of compensation, nominal recognition, and punitive deterrence are decisive together, with text controlling the limit. Harris v. Garner addresses when the restriction attaches and cannot be expanded into a remedy-wide bar. Carey’s recognition of nominal relief and Smith v. Wade’s distinct punitive culpability rule remain operative. Here the verdict established both liability and punitive culpability, so those questions must not be retried merely because the punitive calculation used invalid compensation. The proper correction is one dollar nominal damages, a lawful punitive amount, and a corresponding fee decision, not a new merits trial or restoration of the existing $42,500.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The amount of punitive damages and the recalculated fee award require further application; unproved economic losses are not part of this recovery.
