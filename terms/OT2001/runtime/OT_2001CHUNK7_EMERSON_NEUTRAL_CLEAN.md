### Issue 1: Individual right and temporary protective-order disability

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Love v. Pepersack recognizes individual protection independent of militia membership. Wright provides graduated review by actual burden. United States v. Napier (June 1, 2001) holds that qualifying civil domestic-violence orders, supported by adjudicated violence and recurrence risk after notice and an opportunity to participate, may justify disarmament without a criminal conviction. Six Justices classify the presented disability as substantial regulation satisfying intermediate scrutiny; Stone joins coverage and the civil predicate but concurs in the judgment under strict scrutiny. Fraternal Order of Police retains its distinct conviction-based, official-use holding.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify covered keeping or bearing first. A core deprivation requires a compelling interest, narrow tailoring, and no comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative. A substantial regulation requires an important objective, a substantial relationship, and no unnecessary or effective elimination of the protected activity. Incidental restrictions receive reasonableness review. The categorical prohibition of a general abolition of meaningful ordinary defensive home possession remains independent; it does not immunize every person or use.
* **Other Governing Law:** Second Amendment; Fifth Amendment due process; 18 U.S.C. §§922(g)(8), 924(a), in their event-date form. Section 922(g)(8)(C) supplies alternative express-danger and express-force-prohibition routes.
* **Material Other Authority:** Texas law governing temporary injunctions matters to what the civil proceeding determined and the available review. The court’s factual account is evidence of the record, not a simulated precedent.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The individual-right and availability-of-a-civil-predicate questions are settled. Napier does not validate allegations alone, ex parte orders or unrelated civil orders, does not invent a fixed duration or restoration procedure, and does not equate its violence-and-recurrence findings with a statutory credible-threat finding. Its controlling intermediate-scrutiny classification must be acknowledged. Emerson’s order and adjudicative basis require their own comparison with Napier.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual Texas proceeding supplies a sufficiently adjudicated protective basis despite the absence of an express credible-threat formula, and whether this application survives the governing scrutiny.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Fifth Circuit treated the right as individual but sustained the statute, relying on notice, hearing, express force restrictions, Texas injunction standards, and the order’s temporary, reviewable character. It refused a collateral retrial of the civil evidentiary dispute. **Emerson:** The order used standard language and made no actual danger finding; a complete possession ban cannot rest on an unproved allegation or an injunction unrelated to firearm risk. **United States:** Congress expressly provided alternative predicates; meaningful judicial process and protection against threatened domestic injury justify temporary disarmament, and absence of particular words does not negate an adjudication. **Limits and alternatives:** The express-finding argument cannot be accepted by replacing the statute’s disjunction with a conjunction. That textual conclusion does not decide the constitutional sufficiency of every order. The separate notice defense requires the actual warning and knowledge record, not presumed knowledge of all law.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Judgment Consequences:**

**Affirmance path:** Sustain this application after identifying adequate individual process and a violence-related judicial basis; permit prosecution, without adjudicating guilt. **Narrow constitutional-remand path:** Reject both a collective-only account and an indispensable verbal finding, but require review of whether the actual proceeding adjudicated a sufficient protective basis. That rule would invalidate unsupported applications without striking the whole statute. **Dismissal path:** If the disability rests solely on formal boilerplate with no adequate protective adjudication, hold the severe deprivation unjustified as applied; relief reaches the remaining count, not the divorce order. A facial invalidation would require a separately defended reason that valid protective applications cannot survive.
