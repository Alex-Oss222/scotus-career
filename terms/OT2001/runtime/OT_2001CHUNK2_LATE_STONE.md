## 28. Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-02-19, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment that the challenged assignments are unauthorized on the asserted asset-successor theory.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Set aside the assignments to the extent they depend solely on that theory. Preserve any independently established statutory or contractual obligation without inventing one, and leave administration of unassigned beneficiaries to the lawful statutory mechanisms.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Financial responsibility must arise from the enacted category

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve statutory authority before reaching constitutional limits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether asset succession alone permits these Coal Act assignments.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt the statutory coverage holding while preserving the limited authority of Eastern Enterprises.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Coal Act does not authorize assignment to an asset purchaser solely because it succeeded to a signatory operator’s business assets when it is neither the signatory nor a statutorily defined related person. The program’s financing objectives cannot add an omitted liable-person category. A purchaser independently within an enacted category, or subject to a separate enforceable obligation, presents a different question.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns the identified assignments and relationship. It does not exempt all asset transactions or invalidate the Coal Act generally.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The government’s strongest concern is practical continuity: employees and assets may remain while the original operator disappears. Congress nevertheless allocated liability through specified legal relationships. Courts can enforce those relationships and examine evasion without replacing them with a general successor doctrine. The differentiated use of successor language reinforces the need for a textual basis rather than a revenue-preserving inference. This statutory disposition also avoids falsely treating Eastern Enterprises as a majority constitutional theory that the governing vote structure did not produce. The enumerated liable-person categories carry decisive weight. The statute expressly reaches successors of specified related persons while omitting a general successor-to-signatory category; that differentiated wording is concrete textual evidence, not a universal presumption that every omission is deliberate. MCI’s delegation boundary and Mead’s distinction between interpretive authority and persuasion foreclose filling this category merely to sustain program revenue. Eastern Enterprises supplies no unnecessary constitutional ground. The holding governs the claimed asset-successor category while preserving actual liability under an independently satisfied statutory relationship.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unnecessary constitutional questions and unproved independent obligations are reserved.

## 30. Igartua De La Rosa v. United States +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment vacating the district court’s territorial-elector order and directing dismissal of the claim to that relief.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No injunction requiring appointment or counting of Puerto Rican presidential electors issues. The judgment does not prohibit a locally authorized nonbinding vote or adjudicate an independently preserved absentee-voting claim through a State.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Equal citizenship does not authorize a judicial electoral allocation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Court can interpret the provisions governing the demanded electors. That concrete legal question is justiciable; the claim fails on its constitutional merits rather than a blanket exemption of territorial government from judicial review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May a federal court compel the appointment and counting of Puerto Rican presidential electors under the present Constitution?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Article II’s allocation and distinguish equality within an existing electoral system from creation of an additional electoral unit.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Article II assigns presidential electors to States, with the District of Columbia separately covered by the Twenty-Third Amendment. Puerto Rico is neither. The requested appointment-and-counting order therefore exceeds judicial remedial authority; citizenship alone supplies no additional allocation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This resolves the territorial presidential-elector claim. It does not deny Puerto Rican citizens equal protection, due process or other applicable rights, and it does not validate every territorial classification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Text and constitutional structure carry decisive weight. The number and source of electors are constitutive rules, not administrative qualifications that a court may equalize by adding a voter. The Twenty-Third Amendment confirms the means by which a non-State electoral unit was expressly included. Political exclusion is serious, but judicial power to remedy a violation must arise from a right the Constitution supplies; the remedy cannot amend the structural rule it is meant to enforce. No premise of racial or cultural inferiority from the Insular Cases is needed or accepted. Stone therefore declines to use those cases as a general immunity for territorial government while also declining to overrule them on questions this judgment does not require. Romeu’s request to use an existing State allocation is materially different. A complete rule on the requested allocation is necessary; broader territorial status and representation are not.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Congressional seats, territorial statehood, the unappealed treaty and UOCAVA rulings, and the local-law validity of Puerto Rico’s voting legislation are outside this review.

## 27. Owasso Independent School District No. I-011 v. Falvo

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-02-19, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment insofar as it treats the temporary peer-graded papers as institutionally maintained education records.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for proceedings consistent with the limited coverage holding. Do not decide whether a teacher’s retained gradebook is protected or whether every FERPA provision supports a private action; address any genuinely distinct surviving claim on its actual record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Temporary classroom handling does not establish statutory maintenance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Assume the disputed private-enforcement route only as needed to resolve the adverse coverage question; do not use the assumption as a holding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the peer-graded papers satisfy the maintenance element.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt the narrow statutory-coverage holding, preserving the separate rights inquiry.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Papers temporarily exchanged and graded by classmates during an instructional exercise are not, on that basis alone, education records maintained by the institution or its recordkeeping agent. The maintenance requirement is distinct from the paper’s relation to a student and from a teacher’s direction of classroom activity. Subsequent retention or entry in a maintained institutional record must be analyzed separately.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding creates no universal central-file requirement and no exemption for all information handled in a classroom. It decides neither gradebook coverage nor general private enforceability.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The privacy concern is intelligible: disclosure can embarrass a student. The enacted definition nevertheless regulates a specified class of records rather than every classroom disclosure of performance. Reading maintenance to include every momentary exchange would erase a limiting term and turn ordinary participation into institutional record custody. Preserving the later-retention question respects the same distinction in the other direction. The Court can resolve this claim without prematurely settling the more general remedial issue scheduled for later consideration. The statutory maintenance requirement carries decisive weight; the classroom’s function confirms what temporary handling means in this context. Student grading is not recordkeeping on behalf of the institution merely because a teacher directs it. Stone assumes a cause of action only for this adverse merits resolution because the federal claim is substantial enough to support jurisdiction and no affirmative relief requires recognizing an action. The omission of a private-enforcement ruling is therefore a sufficient statutory disposition, not an unresolved part of the records question. No later Gonzaga ruling is assumed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Private enforceability and materially different institutional retention practices are reserved.

## 29. Romeu v. Cohen +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the dismissal of Romeu’s equal-protection challenge to New York’s exclusions and remand for prospective relief against the responsible election official. The independent challenge to Congress’s minimum-coverage definition need not be decided to grant that relief.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Enjoin application of New York Election Law §11-200(1)’s outside-the-United-States requirement and territorial-registration or voting bar to deny Romeu a presidential absentee ballot solely because he resides and votes locally in Puerto Rico. Apply ordinary former-residence, age, registration and authentication requirements; prohibit a second presidential vote, not participation in unrelated territorial elections. Determine remaining ordinary eligibility facts below. UOCAVA is a minimum, not a prohibition on broader New York eligibility. Sever the discriminatory state qualifications as applied; create no territorial electors, reopen no completed count and order no change to another State’s program.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Equal treatment within an extended former-resident electorate

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The challenged exclusion personally denies the requested ballot, and relief through New York’s existing electors can redress it. The recurring prospective eligibility claim survives the completed election.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether living in Puerto Rico alone sufficiently justifies excluding a former New York resident from the absentee eligibility retained by comparable former residents abroad.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend Anderson–Burdick electoral equal protection to New York’s selective preservation of a former-resident presidential franchise. This is a proposed extension of the protected electoral interest, not an already decided entitlement of every nonresident. Apply the severe-burden branch once the claimant establishes comparable former-resident eligibility and exclusion from presidential voting. Reaffirm the State-based electoral allocation; distinguish McDonald, Saenz and Harris v. Rosario at their respective holdings.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A former resident otherwise qualified for New York’s extended presidential electorate may challenge the destination-based exclusion under electoral equal protection. The claimant must establish that eligibility comparison and the complete presidential-voting exclusion. Once established, New York must justify this severe restriction by a compelling electoral interest and narrow tailoring. The interests advanced for denying Romeu a ballot because he resides and votes locally in Puerto Rico do not satisfy that standard. Territorial residence acquires no suspect status, and Congress’s minimum-coverage definition remains undecided.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The extension reaches this State’s existing former-resident presidential program and the demonstrated complete exclusion of an otherwise comparable applicant. It does not establish permanent state membership for all emigrants, require every voting convenience, or subject every residence rule to strict scrutiny. Neutral eligibility, authentication and prevention of a second presidential ballot remain permissible.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The decisive method is Anderson–Burdick’s severe-burden scrutiny, following an express extension of the electoral interest it protects. Article II’s structure limits the remedy to New York’s electors; it does not itself decide the equality claim. Stone rejects the premise that the State may answer every exclusion within an existing former-resident electorate merely by observing that it need never have retained those voters. Equal protection reaches the selective distribution of that electoral opportunity. Comparable former residence supplies the connection to New York; the challenged destination rule supplies the distinction. McDonald is distinguishable because its record did not establish exclusion from voting itself. Here the claim concerns loss of presidential participation, not a preferred absentee procedure. Local voting and election of a Resident Commissioner are real political participation, but concern different offices. Preventing duplicate presidential votes can be achieved by a rule directed to that duplication. An otherwise eligible former resident in Puerto Rico has no second presidential ballot to surrender. Ordinary identification and prior-residence requirements remain, so the geographic exclusion is not a narrowly tailored response to registration administration. Incremental protection of citizens lacking any domestic vote is a legitimate legislative aim, but under the selected severe-burden standard it does not justify denying this distinct presidential opportunity to a comparable former resident. The concern about unequal rights among Puerto Rico’s residents, including advantages associated with the means to establish mainland residence, is substantial. Nevertheless, preserving the existing former-state connection while extending the same eligibility to Romeu is narrower than either allocating territorial electors or creating a franchise for persons with no such connection. Those broader questions are reserved. Stone does not also announce that the distinction fails rational-basis review: under that different test the challenger ordinarily bears the burden and conceivable legitimate purposes suffice. The government’s justification burden here follows the expressly identified electoral rule, not a heightened form of ordinary territorial-classification review. Harris v. Rosario’s congressional benefits holding therefore need not be overruled. Prospective inclusion protects existing voters’ reliance and requires no reopening of completed elections. UOCAVA’s overseas minimum prevents New York from equalizing by withdrawing the federally protected ballots; it does not forbid broader state eligibility. An injunction against the responsible state official, with ordinary eligibility facts determined below, supplies complete relief without invalidating the federal definition or ordering any other State to change its program.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Territorial statehood, congressional representation, a territorial electoral allocation and any future amendment of the extended absentee scheme consistent with federal requirements are outside the question. The alternative travel, Article IV privileges-and-immunities and due-process theories need not be reached because equality supplies complete relief from the challenged exclusion.

## 32. Winn-Dixie Stores, Inc. v. Commissioner +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-02-20, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the deduction disallowance on the substantive-sham ground presented.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the reviewed deficiency determination intact. Do not impose penalties, decide other tax years, or disallow economically independent insurance expenditures not included in the judgment.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Statutory exceptions and substantive sham

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The taxpayer accepts the historical findings, so the statutory interaction and characterization are ripe for decision.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether satisfying the four-of-seven exception supplies a deduction when the borrowing exists solely to generate deductions and has no prospect of pretax economic gain.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the surviving distinction between removal of one disallowance and affirmative qualification for a deduction; propose its application to this insurance arrangement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 264(c)(1) does not affirmatively immunize policy borrowing from the substantive-sham doctrine. Where the accepted findings establish both no nontax business purpose and no prospect of pretax profit in the borrowing arrangement, formal satisfaction of that exception does not require the claimed interest deductions.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding reaches the arrangement and findings before the Court. It neither requires every tax-incentivized transaction to be profitable without the incentive nor treats tax motivation alone as disqualifying.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The exception qualifies §264’s particular restriction. Treating it as a complete grant would erase the separate inquiry into what transaction generated the claimed interest. The record supplies a substantive deficit, not merely an unattractive tax result. Judicial characterization remains bounded by the enacted incentive and the economic transaction actually undertaken. The statutory exception removes a particular disallowance; it does not affirmatively define every covered borrowing as deductible interest. Knetsch’s substance principle is applied to the accepted economic facts, with statutory text setting its boundary. Contemporaneous memoranda and the absence of any pretax economic return support the sham characterization together; no presumption against all tax-motivated transactions is created. This confines the ruling to a transaction lacking both relevant economic substance and a nontax business purpose, without deciding a universal conjunctive or disjunctive test for different arrangements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The necessary test for arrangements having substantial nontax purpose or meaningful pretax returns is outside this record.

## 31. Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-02-20, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Wisconsin Court of Appeals’ rejection of counting a merely anticipated transfer as the community spouse’s income in this eligibility calculation.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the matter for a resources-first calculation under §1396r-5(e)(2)(C), using supported income and resource figures. No immediate dollar award or categorical exemption of all marital resources follows.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Income attribution and the resource allowance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The final state judgment presents a reviewable statutory issue. No constitutional ruling is necessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the hearing decision may deny additional protected resources on the premise of a transfer that becomes available only after the eligibility decision and that the statute does not compel.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the retained text-first limits on administrative interpretation; adopt the resources-first construction for this spousal-allowance calculation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The income that defeats an increase under §1396r-5(e)(2)(C) must be attributed to the community spouse under the statute’s income rules. A merely anticipated, optional transfer from the institutionalized spouse cannot be deemed the community spouse’s income to prevent the resource adjustment needed for eligibility.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This resolves the disputed preeligibility calculation. Actual income belonging to the community spouse remains countable, and lawful posteligibility transfers and adjustments remain available.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress separately described ownership-based attribution, resource protection, and a transfer from the institutionalized spouse. Counting the possible transfer as present attributed income collapses those instructions and can prevent the eligibility on which the projection depends. Administrative flexibility cannot supply the omitted compulsory transfer. Protecting a statutory allowance does not establish a constitutional right to shelter assets. Statutory allocation rules carry decisive weight. Stone considers the Secretary’s practical and technical reasons under Skidmore and Mead, but an amicus position or guidance without delegated legal force cannot bind the court merely through expertise. The income-first approach is administratively attractive and can conserve public funds, yet those consequences do not establish that income legally belonging to the institutionalized spouse has already become the community spouse’s income. Stone therefore rejects that reading on the source’s own terms, not by announcing a new constitutional asset-protection right or abolishing Chevron for lawful delegated choices.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Later factual changes and any distinct request for extraordinary-expense relief require their own administrative record.
