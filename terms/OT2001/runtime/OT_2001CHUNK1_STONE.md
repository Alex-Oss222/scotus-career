## 1. United States Postal Service v. Gregory

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2001-11-13, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Federal Circuit’s categorical pending-grievance ruling and remand for the preserved consequences of the overturned warning; do not decide the unpreserved validity of Bolling’s particular review standard.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** A pending grievance alone does not exclude the earlier action. The reviewing court must address the effect and procedural availability of the arbitral expungement and, where necessary, return the penalty to the Board for reconsideration without the invalid predicate. No automatic reinstatement of removal or judicial selection of a new penalty follows.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Pendency is not invalidity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The categorical legal premise is reviewable; contested factual and preservation questions remain with the appropriate tribunal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an unfinished grievance necessarily prevents consideration of earlier discipline.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt a new, limited construction of the personnel-review framework. No controlling decision already answers the question.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An agency and the Board may consider earlier discipline while a grievance is pending, provided the employee receives a meaningful opportunity to challenge the predicate and its material use. Pendency alone does not invalidate the action. A completed reversal must receive the effect required by the governing agreement and review framework; the resulting penalty cannot rest on a premise that has lawfully disappeared.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This resolves the categorical pendency rule. It does not establish that a particular deferential review formula always provides meaningful review.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The civil-service statute permits parallel grievance and adverse-action channels; it does not make the speed of the former a categorical condition of the latter. Statutory structure carries decisive weight. The agency retains its statutory burden to justify removal and its penalty, while judicial review under §7703(c) respects lawful administrative factfinding and judgment. These are different burdens, so deference cannot establish that every collateral-review formula is valid. Gregory did not preserve a challenge to that formula. The completed expungement presents a different matter from mere pendency: a penalty may require reconsideration when one of its legal predicates disappears, through the available reopening and review mechanisms. Vacatur therefore answers the categorical question without deciding an unpreserved procedural challenge or pretending the warning remains valid.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The adequacy of Bolling’s particular collateral-review standard was not raised in the Federal Circuit or sought here and is outside the preserved question. The consequences of Gregory’s failure to notify the Board and the penalty’s dependence on the expunged warning require the remaining procedural and record application.

## A. STATUS

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s general-discovery ruling and remand without finally resolving the distinct injury-accrual contention.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit adjudication of properly preserved arguments about when liability arose and whether the express exception applies. Do not revive or extinguish claims merely by equating discovery, disclosure, and actionable injury.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The enacted exception controls delayed discovery

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statutory issue is properly before the Court; the record does not justify appellate factfinding about every claimed injury.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether delayed discovery alone extends this limitation period.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt a new construction of this statute, consistent with Rotella’s refusal to substitute a later discovery event for the statutory accrual inquiry.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The governing version of the Fair Credit Reporting Act does not contain a general discovery extension beyond its express exception. Liability arises when the elements necessary for the particular statutory claim exist. The absence of general discovery does not dispense with that element-specific inquiry or eliminate an independently applicable preserved exception.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule addresses the statute and version before the Court. It does not decide later legislation, fraudulent concealment on facts not established, or all federal accrual rules.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress’s decision to describe a particular discovery-based extension has textual significance. A general extension would substantially collapse the distinction it drew. But TRW’s textual argument is strongest against discovery as such, not against the requirement that liability actually exist. The proper disposition therefore corrects the general rule and returns the distinct injury question to the ordinary adjudicative process. Identity theft explains why discovery can be delayed; it does not authorize replacing the enacted line. Text and statutory structure are decisive; the practical difficulty of discovering identity theft informs application but cannot supply a general exception. Stone reaffirms Rotella within its distinct accrual setting and does not extend it into a universal discovery rule. The rule is necessarily general for this version of §1681p because the petition challenges the governing limitations construction; the remand concerns when the elements of these claims arose, not permission to select a different rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A claim-specific injury date and any independently preserved concealment theory require the lower court’s determination.

Dismiss the claims and counterclaims specified in the stipulation with prejudice; preserve the instrument’s jurisdiction, committee, accounting, and costs arrangements. ## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A lawful settlement may resolve the original action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The matter is a merits request for a consent decree; no absent party’s rights or unsubmitted technical findings can be adjudicated by implication.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the submitted agreement may replace the specified existing decree terms.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the governing enforcement and modification framework while recognizing consent as a legitimate means of resolving this controversy. No new substantive apportionment test is proposed.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Court may enter the parties’ comprehensive modified decree when their consent is established, the obligations remain within the interstate controversy, and the instrument preserves lawful judicial responsibility. The decree controls through its actual definitions, accounting provisions, and reserved authority. Approval settles the pleaded claims but is not a finding that every disputed allegation was true or that administrative representatives may revise allocations beyond their express power.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers this submitted stipulation and decree. It neither constitutionalizes the technical measures nor transfers judicial power to the committee.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Court’s duty is to make the parties’ lawful resolution enforceable with precision. The earlier distinction between enforcement and modification guards against treating every later complaint as a new equitable allocation. Consent can resolve those competing claims, but the resulting text must state what changes and how compliance is measured. The proposed committee’s value lies in administering that text. Retaining the judicial role where the decree provides for it prevents routine administration from becoming an unbounded power to alter the bargain. The earlier Nebraska decisions distinguish enforcement of an entitlement from contested alteration of the allocation; Stone reaffirms that distinction. This consensual resolution does not require a new balancing of interstate equities or a finding that a contested-modification burden was met on every surrendered allegation. The decree and the Court’s retained judicial responsibility supply the decisive legal boundaries. Because the parties request a specific instrument, a new general allocation rule would do no work necessary to judgment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Contested rights of absent persons and changes beyond the submitted stipulation are outside this original proceeding’s settlement request.

## 4. Brown v. City of Oneonta +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2001-11-13, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the rejection of the equal-protection claim insofar as it rests on a categorical victim-description exemption, and remand for application of the governing racial-classification standard to the actual conduct.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the affected claim without awarding damages or finding every encounter unlawful. Require differentiated consideration of selection criteria, investigative scope, coercion where relevant, personal responsibility, and immunity. Preserve independently resolved claims unless the corrected rule changes their legal premise.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A description can justify a classification without erasing it

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The express selection rule is reviewable; individualized encounter and damages questions require further proceedings.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether deliberate selection by race based on a victim’s description is a racial classification requiring justification without further proof of hostility, and whether strict scrutiny supplies the governing standard.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend Adarand’s common racial-justification rule to purposeful investigative selection. Reaffirm its important-objective/substantial-relationship standard rather than revive the strict-scrutiny formulation displaced by that decision.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When officials intentionally select people for investigation by race, a racial classification exists even if the information came from a victim and no hostility is shown. The government must establish an important lawful objective and a substantial, evidence-supported fit between the selection, its scope and burden, and that objective. A concrete offender description may justify appropriately limited measures; it does not confer categorical authority to investigate an entire racial community without regard to the description’s other features or practicable alternatives.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule permits evidence-based use of identifying information and requires review of the actual measures. It neither prohibits all racial descriptors nor makes innocent participation in questioning a compensable injury by itself.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Solving the attack is a lawful and important objective. The disputed question is how the information connects to the chosen people and measures. Treating the victim’s source as dispositive skips that connection. The movement from a described young male with a hand injury to broader encounters therefore requires record-based examination. The lower court’s categorical rule prevented that examination; correction should restore it while keeping personal liability and seizure questions separate. Adarand supplies the decisive scrutiny rule; ordinary factual comparison applies it to policing. A victim’s description can support an inference about identity, but cannot itself prove that every Black resident belongs in the investigative group. Race linked to age, sex, injury, location, and timing differs materially from race used to select an entire community. Testimony, the description, investigative records, and reasonable inferences can establish fit without commissioned studies or a universal least-restrictive-means requirement. The rule extends only the classification principle needed to correct the dismissal; unappealed dismissals and the earlier judgment protecting university officials for releasing the list remain undisturbed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual encounter findings, damages, and immunity remain for the appropriate tribunal.

## 5. Correctional Services Corp. v. Malesko

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2001-11-27, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the availability of a narrowly confined claim against the private custodial operator for its own unconstitutional conduct, and remand for application to the actual pleading.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require identification of the corporate policy, practice, or authorized decision allegedly causing deliberate indifference and injury. Permit the ordinary pleading process within applicable rules. Award no damages now, impose no automatic vicarious liability, and do not revive time-barred individual claims.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Responsibility follows the operator’s own exercise of custody

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the cause-of-action question, while treating deliberate indifference, corporate attribution, and causation as unproved allegations.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Bivens should extend to a private federal custodial corporation’s own unconstitutional policy or authorized decision, notwithstanding Meyer’s refusal to create direct federal-agency liability.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Propose a limited new remedial extension. The existing holdings permit the distinction but do not already establish this corporate claim.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A private corporation entrusted with federal custody may answer in damages for an Eighth Amendment violation caused by its own policy, established practice, or authorized corporate decision in performing that custodial function. Federal-agency immunity concerns do not automatically govern the private operator. Liability requires deliberate indifference, attribution, and causation; employee negligence or respondeat superior alone is insufficient.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns direct corporate responsibility in federal custody. It does not create a general constitutional tort action against every federal contractor.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Custody places the operator in control of basic conditions and access to necessary care. Where the company itself makes the unconstitutional decision, concentrating the remedy exclusively on subordinate employees can miss the responsible actor. Meyer’s federal-agency concerns do not mechanically extend to a private entity. The strongest restraint is therefore to demand the operator’s own constitutional conduct and to examine concrete alternative remedies, not to treat corporate status as a substantive immunity. The elevator allegation still needs that analysis; its seriousness does not establish a corporate policy or the required state of mind. Stone distinguishes Meyer’s federal-agency defendant and new public fiscal obligation, while accepting its instruction that attribution of a constitutional duty is not itself a cause of action. The proposed extension rests on Carlson’s recognized custodial damages function and the private operator’s own decision, not on immunity waiver or a general duty of every contractor. State negligence remedies and prospective relief matter, but neither makes an action directed at the corporation’s own deliberate constitutional misconduct redundant as a matter of law. This functional remedial analogy carries the decisive weight after the defendant and fiscal distinctions are established. It is an acknowledged extension, with direct responsibility and culpability limiting its reach, not a claim that Meyer already authorized it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The sufficiency of corporate attribution, medical causation, and deliberate indifference remains for remand.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgments rejecting the claimed chapter 35 state-equivalent exemption and remand for application to each tribe’s covered gaming and tax claims.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the lower courts to apply the construction to the actual assessments and requested relief. Do not declare unrelated tribal or member income exempt or determine refund amounts without the tax record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Give the named chapter operative work

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the federal statutory question without borrowing a state-tax immunity rule.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether chapter 35’s express inclusion carries the state wagering-tax exemption.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt a new interpretation of the federal cross-reference; the prior tribal tax holdings do not compel the outcome.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** For tribal gaming covered by the disputed provision, Congress’s express inclusion of chapter 35 and direction of state-equivalent treatment carry the chapter’s pertinent state wagering-tax exemption. The narrower reporting description creates genuine tension but does not justify deleting the named chapter. If ordinary textual tools leave that tension unresolved, the applicable Indian-law canon favors the tribes’ reading.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This is a construction of the particular IGRA cross-reference and covered gaming. It supplies no general exemption from federal taxation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The government’s contextual argument is substantial: the clause begins with reporting and withholding. But the tribes identify enacted language that does not naturally fit that limitation. The choice is between giving a specific statutory reference work and treating it as a mistake. Neither can be avoided by invoking tax policy at a high level. Giving the reference effect preserves Congress’s explicit enumeration and its state-equivalence instruction, and the tribal canon supplies a principled resolution of the remaining ambiguity. The resulting relief must follow the actual assessments rather than an assumed universal immunity. This combines contextual textual interpretation with the established Indian-law ambiguity canon: text establishes the competing plausible readings, and the canon carries decisive weight only after that conflict remains. The named chapter is not an unmistakable clerical mistake whose sole lawful correction is deletion. Oklahoma Tax Commission’s state-tax rules are distinguished, not enlarged into federal immunity. The holding must govern the chapter 35 cross-reference generally for covered tribal gaming, while transaction coverage and arithmetic remain application questions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Tax amounts, coverage of particular transactions, and unrelated tax obligations remain outside this interpretation.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss review as improvidently granted because the program pressed in the merits presentation does not match the properly presented appellate merits judgment.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the lower-court judgment in place without adopting its merits reasoning. Any challenge to direct federal procurement must properly present the standing ruling and the particular program; this disposition changes no equal-protection standard.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A constitutional ruling needs the actual controversy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the review-vehicle defect before reaching program constitutionality.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this proceeding permits deciding the direct federal procurement regime urged at the merits stage.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply jurisdictional and preservation limits. No new racial-classification holding is proposed.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Court will not decide the constitutionality of a materially different direct procurement program when the petition did not challenge the adverse standing determination governing that program and the court below did not decide its merits. Dismissal of the writ neither approves the program nor alters Adarand’s governing racial-justification rule.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The disposition is confined to this mismatched vehicle. It leaves the lower judgment undisturbed without adopting its merits rationale.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Adarand has a substantial interest in the rules governing competitive opportunity. That interest does not allow the Court to interchange government programs with different operations and records. The earlier Slater decision removed an inadequate mootness premise; it did not adjudicate every later standing issue or broaden the object of review. A merits answer here would therefore risk deciding a regulatory arrangement without the adversarial record needed to assess justification and fit. Preserving those limits is more precise than announcing a generalized procurement rule and leaving its factual basis unstated. The decisive ground is preservation of the particular judgment under review. It is not a merits balance between contracting policy and equality, and it supplies no reason to overrule Adarand. Slater’s rejection of an inadequate mootness premise is reaffirmed at its actual scope; it did not eliminate this separate unpetitioned standing ruling. Deciding no more is required because the procedural defect prevents the constitutional question from being presented in this vehicle.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The constitutionality of the direct federal program and any unpreserved claim are not reached.

## 8. Church of Scientology International v. Behar +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2001-11-27, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate dismissal of the Vancouver statements insofar as it rests on an independent subsidiary-meaning immunity, and remand for statement-specific adjudication.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve independently supported dismissals of other statements. Permit resolution of material falsity, materially distinct defamatory meaning, identification, and actual malice for the affected statements. Do not presume that the Church has enough evidence to survive a properly framed summary-judgment motion.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Materiality cannot become a license for distinct knowing falsehoods

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The question permits review of the legal dismissal ground; it does not permit a finding that the challenged statements were false or knowingly published as false.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether protected accusations elsewhere eliminate the need to examine the challenged statement.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Propose a narrow clarification of the constitutional boundary between materiality and actual malice. The constitutional fault requirement remains unchanged.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A challenged statement may be dismissed when it creates no materially false defamatory meaning in the publication’s context. It may not be immunized merely because it is subsidiary to other serious statements for which actual malice was not proved. If it conveys a materially distinct defamatory accusation, the required fault inquiry concerns that accusation; other protected criticism does not supply a substitute finding.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding protects contextual assessment and excludes trivial differences. It does not presume additional actionable injury from every sentence or weaken the public-figure burden.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defendants correctly insist that words must be read in context and that a legally immaterial difference cannot support recovery. Their broader proposed immunity does not follow. Protection of one accusation rests on the constitutional limits applicable to that accusation, including the plaintiff’s evidentiary failure. It does not establish the truth or innocent publication of another. The remand should determine whether the Vancouver statements actually add a distinct defamatory assertion, and, if so, whether the Church can meet the stringent fault requirement. That preserves vigorous criticism without transforming unrelated protected content into a defense to a proven knowing falsehood. Stone reaffirms New York Times v. Sullivan’s actual-malice burden and Masson’s distinction between material falsity and a constitutional incremental-harm immunity. He rejects Herbert’s subsidiary-meaning rule only insofar as it insulates a materially distinct accusation from the required fault inquiry because other accusations are protected. Constitutional fault and statement-specific materiality carry decisive weight; a general balance of reputational harm does not replace them. The correction is broad enough to govern this recurring dismissal ground, but leaves ordinary contextual truth, identification, and summary-judgment rules intact.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The truth, actual malice, and identification issues not decided below remain open; the amendment request retains its independent procedural limits.

## 9. United States v. Knights

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical purpose-based suppression ruling and remand under a condition-and-reasonable-suspicion analysis.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Admit the evidence if no independently preserved defect remains after application of that rule. Preserve any distinct scope or factual issue actually open on the record; do not declare the condition a blanket waiver.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: An objectively justified search does not fail merely because it investigates crime

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach this search’s reasonableness, with reasonable suspicion and the express condition as necessary premises.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether ordinary investigative purpose defeats an otherwise justified probation search.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the governing reasonableness and independent-review principles in a new probation-search combination.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A search of a probationer’s residence within a known, lawful search condition is reasonable when supported by particularized reasonable suspicion of criminal activity under the circumstances presented. Its ordinary criminal-investigative purpose does not independently invalidate it. Probation and notice diminish relevant expectations without extinguishing Fourth Amendment protection or establishing blanket voluntary consent.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding requires the combination present here. It does not authorize suspicionless residential searches or all conditions a sentencing court might impose.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The lower court’s purpose restriction treats a justified investigation as disqualifying even though the condition expressly contemplates law-enforcement officers and objective grounds connect the residence to crime. The constitutional inquiry should examine the actual intrusion and justification. The supervision relationship is relevant to the privacy balance, while individualized suspicion prevents the condition from operating as an unrestricted license. Scott remains meaningful because an unconstitutional search does not become immune from exclusion merely through the officer’s supervisory status. This is a reasonableness balance under the Fourth Amendment, not an inference of blanket contractual consent. The condition, notice, probation relationship, residential intrusion, and individualized suspicion do distinct work; the objectively supported suspicion and limited search authority are decisive in combination. Stone distinguishes Griffin’s special-needs justification without treating it as the exclusive means of sustaining a supervision search, and reaffirms Scott’s exclusion rule for an unconstitutional search. The present combination establishes a defined sufficient ground, not a ruling on suspicionless searches or unbounded conditions.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Suspicionless searches, blanket consent, and unpresented conditions are reserved.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the categorical ineligibility defense: plants meeting the utility-patent statute’s requirements may receive utility patents.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the matter for any remaining patent-specific or transaction-specific issues. Do not treat eligibility as proof of validity, infringement, or enforceability of every license condition.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Different statutory rights may overlap

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The presented defense concerns eligibility and exclusivity, not the resolution of every remaining infringement issue.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the plant statutes impliedly exclude plants from utility-patent protection.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt a new construction preserving the statutes’ concurrent operation; apply Asgrow only within its established certificate setting.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The plant-specific statutes do not categorically remove qualifying plants from the utility-patent statute. An applicant seeking a utility patent must satisfy that statute’s independent requirements and receives its statutory rights, while a plant-variety certificate remains governed by its own version-specific requirements and exceptions. Overlap alone does not establish repeal or exclusivity.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This includes the plant category before the Court but does not validate each asserted claim or contractual restriction.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The specialized regimes’ different protections and exceptions make the defendants’ argument consequential, but they do not make the statutes impossible to apply together. Congress can create a more accessible or differently structured form of protection without withdrawing an existing one. Importing certificate exceptions into utility-patent law would likewise create a hybrid Congress did not enact. The proper legal line is to require the claimant to establish the protection actually invoked. Asgrow’s saved-seed rule remains operative where its statutory version applies; it is not a universal exemption from every patent right. Section 101’s broad eligibility language and the absence of irreconcilable statutory conflict are decisive. Chakrabarty’s treatment of broadly expressed subject matter supports application; Asgrow’s distinct saved-seed construction is reaffirmed without transferring its certificate exception to a utility patent. History of the overlapping regimes confirms this reading but is not a license to revise either statute. A category-wide eligibility answer is required by the defense presented; validity and infringement still turn on the particular patent and conduct.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other validity, exhaustion, infringement, competition, and contract questions are not decided.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining the categorical exclusion of truthful affiliation information and remand for application of the burden-and-justification rule and appropriately bounded prospective relief.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The recurring election dispute remains reviewable despite completion of the election. Require consideration of a truthful, verifiable affiliation designation that does not falsely imply recognized-party nomination, and tailor prospective relief accordingly. Do not change the completed ballot or confer full party recognition.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The state must justify the information it withholds

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The recurring statutory restriction affects the party and its candidates in elections too short for complete review; the record supports the capable-of-repetition exception applied below. Reach the ballot-label question without ordering a new mootness inquiry merely because the election ended.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether full party recognition can be the categorical prerequisite for any truthful affiliation label.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the governing burden-and-fit framework in a new label-specific setting. Do not treat Timmons as deciding a materially different ballot burden.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Where a candidate independently qualifies for the ballot and establishes a genuine party affiliation, a state must justify withholding all accurate affiliation information by the actual burden and its demonstrated ballot interests. It may distinguish formal recognized-party nomination from affiliation through a neutral designation. Full party recognition is not, by itself, a sufficient explanation for prohibiting every truthful label.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not compel misleading or unverifiable labels, eliminate party-recognition thresholds, or confer other recognized-party privileges.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ohio has a legitimate interest in making the ballot’s official representations accurate. That interest can support reserving a formal nomination label, but the record must explain why no truthful affiliation format would serve it. The candidate has already met the access requirement, so a separate party-wide threshold needs its own connection to the information withheld. Timmons’s recognition of alternate channels of association is relevant to burden but not dispositive. The remand should resolve actual affiliation, wording, and administrative fit rather than assume the only choices are complete party recognition or silence. Anderson–Burdick supplies the burden-sensitive standard; no racial tier or newly created right to the state’s endorsement is necessary. Timmons is distinguished because a ban on fusion nomination does not decide whether a qualified candidate can convey truthful affiliation in a format that distinguishes nomination from association. The rule’s practical operation and the State’s actual accuracy interests carry decisive weight within that received standard. A neutral verification requirement may protect accuracy; an unexplained demand for full party status does not answer the distinct labeling burden.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The exact permissible label and any genuinely disputed verification facts remain for remedial application. Full party recognition and retrospective alteration of the completed election are outside the requested relief.

## 12. Dusenbery v. United States

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-08, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm on the established institutional delivery procedure, rejecting an absolute actual-receipt requirement.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the challenged forfeiture notice determination in place because the relevant procedure is reasonably calculated to reach the confined owner. State the procedure-based ground rather than a rule validating every mailing to a prison address.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Reasonable delivery requires a route, not a guarantee

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the adequacy of the established process; do not determine that Dusenbery actually received the notice.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the absence of proof of actual receipt defeats otherwise reasonable custodial notice.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the governing notice principles to a new delivery setting.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Due process does not invariably require proof of actual receipt. When the government knows the owner is confined, it must use a reasonably reliable means of directing notice through the institution to that person. Certified delivery coupled with an established, reasonably functioning internal distribution procedure can satisfy that obligation. A signature at the institutional entrance, without adequate evidence of the onward route, would not alone establish sufficiency in every case.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This holding concerns the procedure proved for the relevant period and the absence of an established known failure. It does not approve all prison mailing practices.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Actual-receipt proof would make validity turn on events the sender cannot always confirm, while a bare dispatch rule would ignore the government’s own custodial control. The appropriate middle inquiry is concrete: how notice enters the institution, how it is logged and assigned, and how it ordinarily reaches inmates. The reported evidence supports that route here without establishing receipt in this instance. West Covina’s concern with usable notice is therefore preserved, while the Constitution remains a reasonableness standard rather than insurance against every delivery failure. Mullane’s notice-specific reasonableness standard carries decisive weight, informed by the actual custodial delivery procedure. Mathews’s general calibration does not require replacing that established notice rule with a demand for guaranteed receipt. Stone reaffirms West Covina’s distinction between usable notice and an individualized legal manual. The government-controlled route must be evaluated as a route to the inmate, but testimony about its ordinary operation can suffice; no new statistical study or signed personal receipt is a constitutional element. The proved procedure establishes sufficiency here without validating a known failed delivery.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A case involving an unreliable process or known nondelivery would require its own record.

## 13. Toyota Motor Manufacturing, Kentucky, Inc. v. Williams

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-08, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Sixth Circuit’s partial summary judgment and remand for an individualized manual-activity determination under the governing disability rules.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Assess Williams’s underlying limitations without discounting them through beneficial mitigation. Do not impose a categorical requirement of permanent or nearly total inability, and do not grant Toyota automatic judgment. Keep accommodation, qualification, and other preserved coverage routes separate.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Measure the activity without importing a different activity’s shortcut

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the manual-task standard and summary-judgment error; leave genuinely disputed function and remaining liability elements for remand.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What evidence establishes substantial limitation of manual tasks.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Sutton and Murphy’s mitigation rule and propose an activity-specific clarification. Reject an unsupported categorical severe-and-permanent threshold.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A manual-task disability requires an individualized substantial limitation of important manual functioning, considered in breadth, frequency, duration, and practical effect. Inability to perform a class of occupational tasks is relevant but not conclusive merely because working has its own class-of-jobs inquiry. Important work-related functions cannot be excluded simply because they occur at work. Beneficial mitigation is disregarded under the governing baseline; ability to perform some household tasks does not automatically defeat coverage.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding does not make every task difficulty a disability or treat a diagnosis as sufficient. It permits consideration of expected duration without converting permanence into an indispensable element.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The appellate court moved too quickly from a job-task category to a different statutory activity. Toyota is entitled to correction of that reasoning, but not to a new rule that only the most pervasive daily-life limitations count. Sustained gripping, repetition, and reaching may be important capacities across life even where the person retains other functions. The record must identify the actual limitation and its practical magnitude. That inquiry also prevents accommodation analysis from silently determining coverage and preserves Sutton’s deliberate rejection of counting successful mitigation against the claimant. The statute’s individualized substantial-limitation requirement is decisive; the functional evidence applies that requirement rather than creating a new checklist. Breadth, frequency, duration, and practical effect are relevant considerations, not separately mandatory elements. Stone reaffirms Sutton and Murphy on mitigation and distinguishes the class-of-jobs standard for working from the manual-task activity. He rejects permanence as an absolute prerequisite because expected duration informs substantiality without rewriting substantial limitation as total incapacity. The new articulation is necessary to correct the legal standard; actual disability still requires proof.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Remaining factual disputes, other preserved disability routes, qualification, and accommodation are not finally adjudicated.

Harris Trust is reconciled through identifiable property, a proper recipient, and applicable equitable defenses. The named defendants’ lack of the asserted fund matters to the requested remedy, not to sympathy for either party. ## A. STATUS

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit’s categorical Coast Guard-jurisdiction exclusion and recognize OSHA coverage of the working conditions at issue, with remand for any remaining citation questions.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the enforcement proceeding to the extent wrongly displaced. Require attention to actual Coast Guard regulations and the statutory workplace boundary; do not enter an unreviewed penalty or factual violation finding solely from the jurisdictional correction.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Potential power does not itself displace exercised authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach statutory coverage and displacement; preserve remaining factual and penalty issues.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What exercise by another federal agency triggers the exclusion.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt a new application of the statute’s actual-exercise language.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The other-agency exclusion requires that another federal agency actually exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards affecting the working conditions at issue. Possession of potential authority over an uninspected vessel, or investigation after a casualty, does not by itself exclude OSHA. A barge within the Act’s geographic and employment coverage may be a workplace; any displacement extends only as the actual governing regulation supports.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not require duplicative enforcement where Coast Guard standards genuinely cover the conditions, and does not equate uninspected status with absence of all Coast Guard regulation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory choice of exercise matters. Replacing it with potential jurisdiction would create regulatory gaps whenever an agency could regulate but has not done so. Mallard Bay’s coordination concern is legitimate where real standards overlap, but cannot supply regulations absent from the record. The relevant contrast is therefore between the working conditions actually covered and those merely within theoretical power. The explosion’s gravity does not expand OSHA’s authority; the statute and actual regulatory coverage do. The actual-exercise language is decisive and creates a statutory boundary, not a free-floating balance of agency expertise. Stone distinguishes comprehensive Coast Guard regulation of inspected vessels from the limited regulation of this uninspected barge, and resolves the alternative workplace objection from §4(a)’s geographic coverage. Mead’s delegation threshold cannot confer displacement merely because an agency possesses expertise. The rule governs overlapping authority at the relevant working-condition level; individual citation and penalty issues need not be decided to restore the lawful enforcement channel.
