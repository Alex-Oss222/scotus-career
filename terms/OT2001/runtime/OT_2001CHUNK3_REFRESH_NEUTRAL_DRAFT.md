* **Question Presented:** Whether the Federal Power Act authorizes FERC to require open access for unbundled retail transmission in interstate commerce, and whether FERC was required in the same proceeding to extend that requirement to the transmission component of bundled retail sales. These are formulations of the two consolidated questions.
* **Question Granted:** The question above is a verified issue-level formulation of the historical scope of review, not a quotation of the certiorari petition. The cited opinion and any contemporaneous brief below identify its limits; no additional issue is inferred.

* **Relief Requested:** New York seeks removal of unbundled retail transmission from the federal rule. Enron seeks an additional requirement addressing bundled retail transmission. FERC seeks affirmance of both components.
* **Material Record:** Order No. 888 required open access and functional separation for wholesale transmission and covered separately sold retail transmission. * **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Each petition challenges a different component of one agency proceeding. ### Issue 1: Authority over unbundled retail transmission

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board recognizes federal implementation authority where Congress granted it despite an important state regulatory role. MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co. requires fidelity to the powers actually delegated rather than administrative enlargement of a limited term.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The operative standards examine enacted text, structure, and the particular delegation before deferring within its boundaries. Federal participation does not erase preserved state functions, and a federalism label does not override an affirmative congressional assignment.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 201(b) addresses interstate transmission separately from wholesale sales and preserves specified state-regulated activities, including local distribution. Sections 205 and 206 address unreasonable or discriminatory practices within federal jurisdiction.
* **Material Other Authority:** Federal Power Commission v. Southern California Edison Co. is relevant to the statute’s division of authority. Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc. remains subject to its actual delegated domain; Mead supplies the prior-term delegation threshold.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering rules govern how to identify the federal assignment. They do not already classify every modern retail transmission arrangement or decide this order’s exact reach.
* **Open Point:** Whether a separately sold interstate transmission service loses federal character solely because its electricity ultimately serves a retail customer.

### Issue 2: Scope of the bundled-service remedy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co. restricts an agency to its statutory authority; Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala permits reasoned choices within a lawful administrative framework. Neither converts permission to act into an invariably mandatory remedial step.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The agency must explain a choice responsive to the statutory problem and record. The Court may not substitute a preferred regulatory architecture for a lawful agency choice.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 206’s remedial duties operate upon the requisite findings of unlawful rates or practices; the challenged order addresses discriminatory transmission access.
* **Material Other Authority:** Motor Vehicle Manufacturers Assn. v. State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. supplies reasoned review of relevant factors and explanations, without authorizing judicial fact-finding about an unexamined market.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Reasoned-review requirements apply to leaving part of a market outside a particular rule. They do not resolve whether a finding made about one transaction necessarily compels the identical remedy for another.
* **Open Point:** Whether the findings and statutory duty here require immediate compulsory unbundling of bundled retail service or permit a narrower proceeding and remedy.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

New York argues that regulation of retail transactions belongs to the States and that the agency’s category crosses that boundary. FERC answers that Congress distinguished sales from transmission and that only the sales grant is limited to wholesale activity. Enron argues that leaving bundled transmission outside the order preserves the identified discriminatory advantage. FERC answers that the chosen open-access remedy addresses the established problem without resolving the different bundled-service questions. The circuit accepted the agency’s statutory distinction and its limited remedial choice. A claim of mandatory broader relief must identify the statutory trigger and findings, not merely show that broader regulation might be permissible.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirming the transmission component would recognize federal authority over interstate service independently sold to a retail customer while preserving local distribution and retail-sale authority. Reversal would exclude that category from Order No. 888. On Enron’s challenge, affirmance could rest on the reasonableness of the limited remedy without deciding the full extent of bundled-service jurisdiction; reversal could require further agency consideration or action under §206, not judicial construction of a replacement tariff. A jurisdictional or explanation defect in one component need not undo the other. The record does not require a universal allocation of every future grid service.

## 42. Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc.

* **Question Presented:** Whether the Labor Department may require an additional twelve weeks of protected leave whenever an employer failed to designate previously provided qualifying leave as FMLA leave, without a showing that the omission impaired the employee’s rights. ### Issue 1: Regulatory designation and statutory relief

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co. enforces limits on delegated authority. Christensen and Mead distinguish delegated legal force from advisory interpretation, and neither allows deference to override governing text. McKennon illustrates the distinct inquiries into violation and relief under an employment statute.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The operative standards require a statutory basis for an agency rule and attention to the conditions Congress attached to the remedy. A useful administrative purpose cannot alone authorize a materially different entitlement.
* **Other Governing Law:** The FMLA grants qualifying employees twelve workweeks of specified leave, prohibits interference, and provides damages and appropriate equitable relief tied to actionable violations. Section 2654 delegates necessary implementation regulations.
* **Material Other Authority:** Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc. remains subject to enacted limits. Mourning v. Family Publications Service, Inc. concerns a prophylactic implementation rule and supplies an argument for administrative latitude, not unlimited power to amend remedies.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The entering rules govern delegation and remedies but do not already decide whether this particular designation consequence is implementation or an unauthorized expansion.
* **Open Point:** Whether making all previously granted leave legally irrelevant, regardless of injury from deficient designation, is compatible with the statutory total and remedial conditions.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The courts below viewed the automatic consequence as an enlargement of the statutory leave guarantee. Ragsdale and the Government emphasize meaningful notice, predictable administration, and the Secretary’s implementation authority. Wolverine argues that an irrebuttable additional entitlement dispenses with impairment and causation even where proper notice would change nothing. A lawful notice rule can be important without every sanction chosen for its breach being authorized; conversely, voluntary generosity does not excuse an independently harmful interference with FMLA rights.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could invalidate the automatic consequence while leaving notice duties and proof of actual interference intact. Reversal could sustain designation as a permissible condition on counting leave and revive Ragsdale’s claim without proving reliance. An injury-sensitive construction would require a remand only if consistent with the regulation and a preserved record-supported claim. The case supports no general rejection of prophylactic agency rules or all employee remedies for defective notice. Any judgment must specify whether it reaches the penalty alone or the underlying notice obligation.

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The prior decisions do not contain a decision construing this public-housing clause. MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. ## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

An individualized objection to an actual eviction differs from the categorical statutory theory supporting the injunction.

Affirmance could adopt a knowledge-based construction or sustain a properly presented constitutional limitation. A statutory ruling would not itself establish the alleged drug activity, terminate the leases, or order removal. The supported holding concerns listed relationships and qualifying activity, not misconduct by anyone with a remote connection to the tenant or an unrestricted power to evict arbitrarily.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983 and pleading-stage review. Constitutional deprivation, personal causation, culpability, available defenses, and compensable injury are separate elements. A State’s family-reunification responsibilities do not themselves decide the federal question.

### Issue 1: Affirmative custodial transfer and danger

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence recognizes a bounded claim where an officer actually restrains a person and knowingly directs an involuntary transfer into appreciated serious danger, with authority and opportunity for a reasonable response. It expressly leaves general noncustodial danger liability open. Pinder v. Johnson distinguishes an affirmative deprivation from an unfulfilled protection promise; Uhlrig v. Harder resolves conduct-date immunity without adopting a general danger test.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The registers require the defendant’s own restraint or affirmative causal conduct and preserve the difference between providing no public protection and causing a constitutional deprivation. Custody of an assailant or protective assurances alone are insufficient.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment constrains state deprivations of liberty. DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services rejects a general protective duty and discusses return from temporary custody to an existing private danger.
* **Material Other Authority:** Youngberg v. Romeo concerns protection arising from actual civil custody. Its institutional professional-judgment context is relevant but does not mechanically decide the culpability of every later placement decision.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The direct-restraint rule already protects against the specified coercive transfer. The supplied law has not adopted a general rule for returning a child from protective custody to a parent who permits a specific private danger.
* **Open Point:** Whether the relevant comparison for this affirmative release is the protected custodial position immediately before it or an imagined situation in which the State never intervened, and how DeShaney’s return language limits that inquiry.

### Issue 2: Culpability after an opportunity to deliberate

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** County of Sacramento v. Lewis requires an unjustified purpose to harm in a genuinely rapid pursuit, while preserving the significance of actual opportunity to deliberate. City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence permits its bounded knowing-restraint claim without importing that rapid-pursuit requirement. Farmer v. Brennan informs actual knowledge and reasonable response, not an automatic transplant of prison doctrine.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Negligence does not become a constitutional deprivation because the eventual private harm is severe. Actual appreciation, genuine opportunity, responsibility, and unreasonable disregard must be tied to each official.
* **Other Governing Law:** Substantive due process prohibits the qualifying abuse of state power; §1983 does not federalize ordinary malpractice or every erroneous placement judgment.
* **Material Other Authority:** Daniels v. Williams excludes negligence. The contextual reasoning of Lewis requires examination of the official act, available choices, and competing obligations.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The supplied rules reject a universal malice requirement and a universal deliberate-indifference test. The actual circumstances determine whether deliberate disregard can constitute the relevant abuse.
* **Open Point:** Whether the pleaded specific knowledge and considered return, rather than an emergency judgment or negligent oversight, satisfy the constitutional culpability threshold.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The en banc court reasoned that return to the original home did not worsen the child’s position compared with no state intervention, and alternatively that the allegations did not reach the required culpability. S.S. argues that knowingly withdrawing actual custodial protection and choosing the dangerous placement is an affirmative act, and that ample deliberation makes the alleged disregard actionable. Officials invoke DeShaney, family reunification, and the need to distinguish constitutional abuse from professional error. S.S. must establish more than predictable private harm; officials cannot equate a legitimate general goal with a finding that the particular alleged choice was reasonable.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could preserve the original-situation comparison or hold that the pleaded culpability is insufficient. Reversal could recognize this custodial-transfer claim and permit proof of knowledge, causation, and unjustified disregard. A narrower vacatur could require the lower court to apply the supplied direct-restraint precedent without deciding every noncustodial danger theory. Any successful pleading path leaves conduct-date immunity and supported defenses for adjudication; neither the offender’s guilt nor the gravity of harm establishes personal damages liability. A broad duty to guarantee safe family placement would exceed the asserted affirmative-deprivation theory.
