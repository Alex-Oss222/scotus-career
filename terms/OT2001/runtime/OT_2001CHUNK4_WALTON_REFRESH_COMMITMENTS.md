## Corrected rulemaking premise and its legal significance

The final rule was published July 11, 2000, at 65 Fed. Reg. 42772–42792, and became effective August 10, 2000. The March 17 signature is neither publication nor effectiveness. The disputed trial-work exclusion was in the March 6, 1995 proposal; a sixty-day comment period followed, comments from ten individuals and organizations were considered, and the final preamble answered the duration, statutory-entitlement and timing objections. It is incorrect to characterize that provision as merely an unissued proposal or as adopted without notice and comment.

The agency described the interpretation as consistent with SSR 82-52 and unchanged in practice. That assertion does not eliminate the actual adoption of operative regulatory text. The statute's section 405(a) delegation authorizes necessary or appropriate rules and proof procedures consistent with the benefit provisions; section 1383(d)(1) imports that authority into the SSI part. The agency exercised that rulemaking authority in adopting the operative section 404.1592 provision, rather than simply distributing advice to staff or announcing counsel's litigation view. Together, the actual grant, implementing subject, binding regulatory text and completed process establish the force-of-law character of this final provision under Mead's governing threshold. Notice and comment alone, a document title, or expertise would not do so. Scalia reaches the same conclusion about this formally delegated rule without adopting Mead's full test for other instruments.

The refresh therefore removes the earlier uncertainty concerning whether the disputed final provision went through notice and comment. It also supplies the agency's actual reasoned response to the principal objections, which may be evaluated rather than invented. This is not a declaration that every sentence in the document is valid, that every possible procedural challenge was raised and rejected, or that all Social Security Rulings possess the same force. The earlier SSR's internal binding effect, the proposal's status and the final regulation's legal form remain distinct.

The APA good-cause finding applies to the specified statutory conforming changes concerning work without regard to legality, legal or illegal services, and blind-earnings indexing. It does not excuse notice and comment for the disputed duration/trial-work exclusion in section 404.1592(d)(2)(iii), and it does not eliminate the August 10 effective date. No general procedural exemption or immediate effectiveness follows from that separate finding.

The lower opinion's no-retroactivity statement remains respected. The final regulation is not backdated as a new operative condition for Walton's 1995 work. Its interpretation and actual reasons are relevant to the statutory issue without converting the effective date. Smiley permits qualified antecedent interpretive use; it does not authorize universal retrospective legislative change. The history here identifies the same agency duration approach in SSR 82-52 and the 1995 proposal, not an identified contrary official promise to Walton. The preamble's circuit-specific acquiescence rulings do not establish a contrary nationwide rule or a promised benefit in this case. No claimant-specific reliance finding is invented.

The common sufficient disposition continues to rest on independent statutory construction. The new process makes a delegated-interpretation route materially better supported; it does not alter the statutory language or the actual sequence on which the independently sufficient construction rests. The Associates do not withdraw their conclusion that the integrated duration and initial-entitlement reading is persuasive on its own terms merely because a further source of interpretive authority is now authenticated. The common disposition accordingly does not require an additional holding about the controlling antecedent effect of the later regulation or the precise force of the earlier SSR. Recognition of this final rule's actual legal form must not be rendered as denial of that form, nor as an alternative judgment ground silently counted without its temporal analysis.

## Exact timing qualifications and opposing paths

The final within-twelve-month exclusion requires both work demonstrating substantial gainful activity within twelve months after onset of the disabling impairment and that work's occurrence before any notice of a determination or decision finding disability. A disability finding, notice of that finding, an award notice, payment and statutory entitlement are not interchangeable. The waiting-period-work exclusion and the exclusion of months before application remain separate.

Two qualifications expressly prevent an overbroad pre-award rule:

- A claimant returning to work more than twelve months after onset but before adjudication may qualify for trial-work protection if the duration requirement and other entitlement conditions are satisfied. Passage of twelve months alone is insufficient if the disabling inability actually ended earlier for another reason. Prior receipt of benefits is not mandatory.
- A claimant returning within twelve months after a notice finding disability may qualify even if that notice precedes the notice awarding benefits. Prior payment is not mandatory, and an award notice must not replace the relevant disability-finding notice.

The pre-application exclusion concerns months before application, not a lifetime disqualification because a claimant once worked after an impairment began. An impairment may become disabling after its medical onset, and episodic conditions do not make every historical work episode permanently disqualifying. The separate unsuccessful-work-attempt rules require their actual predicates; the refresh creates no finding that Walton's affirmed October 1995 substantial gainful activity was unsuccessful work.

The original W1 reading remains: on the twelve-month branch the medically determinable impairment must cause disabling inability that lasts or is expected to last continuously for at least twelve months; expected death is a distinct alternative. A supported prospective finding may precede the anniversary. Actual substantial work within that period, known before a favorable disability determination or notice here, may defeat an earlier prediction in the initial decision. Section 422(c)'s cessation protection presupposes qualifying Title II entitlement; it does not itself supply a missing initial duration condition. Walton's later initial closed-period award was superseded on administrative review, and no favorable pre-return disability finding or notice is supplied. Neither medical recovery nor mere receipt of a payment is the operative predicate.

The strongest W2 objection remains that the relative clause modifies impairment and that entitlement does not require a prior award. The two newly documented qualifications reinforce the latter point and are accepted. They do not establish entitlement where the functional duration condition itself failed. W1 reads impairment as the condition producing the described inability, rather than an enduring diagnosis detached from the statute's work-disability criterion. The death alternative is not read as saying that work or inability itself causes death.

The strongest W3 objection remains that a reasonable earlier expectation may have accrued entitlement and that adjudication timing can produce unequal outcomes. The agency expressly acknowledged timing differences instead of denying them. Its response is the statutory accommodation of prompt prospective awards and exclusion of demonstrated short-term inability; the statutory present and completed-duration language does not make an earlier prediction permanently decisive before an initial favorable determination. Each Associate accepts that response at Walton's posture while preserving the two qualifications and declining to decide reopening, reliance or every awarded claimant's rights. Administrative delay is not declared harmless in all circumstances.
