## 44. Zahrey v. Coffey +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm reversal of the qualified-immunity dismissal and remand for the personal claim to proceed under ordinary civil standards.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit proof of deliberate fabrication, actual custody, material causation and injury. Determine the reserved functional classification and remaining defenses on the developed record. Award compensation only for proved injury attributable to actionable conduct; acquittal alone establishes no damages.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Manufactured evidence cannot be insulated by its intended later use

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Investigative capacity is assumed for this appeal. The pleaded detention supplies an actual liberty deprivation, and the challenged defense can be resolved without deciding disputed facts.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does intentional investigative fabrication foreseeably producing detention violate due process, and was that boundary sufficiently established in 1996?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the established prohibition of government-imposed liberty deprivation through known falsehood and Buckley’s investigative distinction to the causal sequence presented; reject an automatic interruption of causation by later advocacy.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An investigator who knowingly fabricates material evidence and thereby foreseeably causes an actual deprivation of liberty violates due process. The later presentation of that evidence does not, merely because presentation is an advocacy function, erase liability for the earlier investigative act. On these allegations, qualified immunity does not protect deliberate manufacture of the basis for detention.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule requires deliberate falsification, materiality and actual causation of custody or another independently protected deprivation. Mistakes, disputed inferences, an acquittal or unused false evidence do not suffice.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Mooney’s anti-falsification principle and Buckley’s functional line carry decisive precedential weight. Their combination protects the actual liberty interest without converting all police dishonesty into a constitutional tort. A prosecutor could otherwise create a false basis for imprisonment and immunize the causal act by personally carrying it into court. A genuinely independent, untainted reason for custody may defeat causation; the complaint alleges the opposite, so that factual possibility cannot support dismissal. The constitutional boundary was sufficiently concrete before the events: officials could not deliberately manufacture evidence to procure the target’s imprisonment. The later Kalina decision confirms the distinction but is not used retrospectively to supply notice. Stone decides only the personal detention claim litigated, preserving the separate requirements for a damages remedy.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual investigative-versus-advocacy classification is reserved because it was conceded only for this appeal. No general immunity rule for all testimony or new institutional damages action is decided.

## 45. Milstein v. Cooley +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm reinstatement of the investigative, complaining-witness and nonadvocacy claims; vacate the remaining dismissal to the extent it categorically immunizes knowing material factual fabrication or its deliberate false presentation causing an actionable deprivation.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for act-specific adjudication under the defined falsification exception and ordinary pleading and proof rules. Preserve immunity for otherwise protected acts outside it, including opposition to reappointment of counsel and ordinary charging or courtroom advocacy unless the particular act includes knowing material factual falsification. A malicious motive, reversal of the conviction or failure of the prosecution alone does not suffice. Constitutional elements, personal participation, materiality, causation and damages remain for independent adjudication. The lower court expressly reserved qualified immunity. Apply the law actually in force on remand, including any intervening controlling decision, without treating a proposed opinion in another pending case as law. This judgment neither grants nor rejects that unadjudicated defense. No damages judgment, mandatory discovery program or trial is ordered.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Ordinary functional limits remain controlling

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The complaint’s factual allegations are accepted for reviewing the dismissal, not adjudicated as true. The alleged acts remain separately classifiable by their actual functions.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does a prosecutor’s office or later presentation immunize preliminary fabrication, personal accusation or press activity?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Buckley and Kalina’s functional distinctions.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** No. Ordinary investigative fabrication, acting personally as an evidentiary accuser and press statements do not acquire absolute prosecutorial immunity because their author later advocates the prosecution. Genuine legal preparation and presentation retain the ordinary rule, subject to the separate exception stated below.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Classify acts by what the prosecutor did, not merely whether the act preceded indictment or helped a prosecution.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Function is decisive because immunity protects the judicial role rather than an occupational class. The pleaded manufacture of an accusation and the personal crime report require separate treatment from the legal presentation of charges. Public statements do not perform courtroom advocacy. Ordinary application of existing precedent resolves those functions without expanding either immunity or §1983’s substantive elements.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether each act occurred and caused an actionable deprivation remains a factual matter.

### Issue 2: A defined exception for knowing material falsification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The complaint squarely alleges fabrication and deliberate factual falsity; the protected-advocacy portion of the dismissal makes the immunity boundary necessary to decide.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** May absolute immunity defeat a supported claim that an official knowingly fabricated or deliberately presented materially false facts causing an independently actionable deprivation?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Partially overrule Imbler and Fletcher v. Kalina, and Burns to the same extent, insofar as they absolutely protect knowing material factual falsification within advocacy. Preserve their remaining functional protection. Reserve qualified immunity because this review presents absolute immunity and the lower court did not decide the separate personal defense.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Absolute functional immunity does not bar an official’s liability when the claimant establishes knowing fabrication of a materially false fact, deliberate material falsification of an evidentiary record or knowing presentation of such false facts, together with an independently actionable constitutional deprivation, the official’s own responsibility and the governing causal connection. Materiality concerns the fact’s significance in authorizing or sustaining the deprivation. Error, a disputed inference, adverse credibility, mistaken testimony, erroneous legal judgment and an unfavorable outcome do not satisfy the exception. The claimant retains the ordinary burden of proof; immunity’s loss establishes none of these elements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The exception is act-specific and supplies neither a general dishonesty tort nor liability for every malicious prosecution. Ordinary procedural screening remains, but a sufficiently pleaded claim within the exception can require litigation of knowledge and falsity. The rule does not preserve an absolute entitlement to avoid that litigation. No separate witness’s immunity, new interlocutory appeal or municipal liability rule is decided.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The decisive legal ground is the conflict between a categorical bar on adjudicating an official’s knowing material manufacture of a liberty deprivation and §1983’s remedial purpose. Functional distinctions define the remaining protection; they do not make this exception existing law. Imbler’s common-law reconstruction and the especially strong continuity owed a statutory precedent weigh against revision. Stone nevertheless would change the boundary because it systematically defeats redress for the deliberate creation of the factual predicate that constitutional process is meant to test. He does not claim that historical immunity already contained his exception or that congressional silence enacted it. The strongest opposing interest belongs to honest prosecutors: accusations of deliberate falsity may be easy to make, expensive to disprove and capable of chilling independent decisions. Criminal sanctions and discipline also provide genuine alternative deterrence. Stone accepts that his exception increases some discovery and litigation costs and weakens immunity from suit; the absence of a legitimate entitlement to falsify does not answer those costs. He gives greater weight, at this defined boundary, to adjudicating a deliberately manufactured, materially consequential constitutional injury than to categorical protection against that adjudication. Ordinary disagreement over evidence, legal decisions or motives remains insufficient, and the ordinary functional immunity for advocacy survives. Crawford-El’s ordinary-proof and case-management holdings remain controlling: courts may clarify allegations, resolve genuinely dispositive legal questions, sequence relevant discovery and apply ordinary summary judgment, but may not impose a new heightened evidentiary quantum or require the claimant to prove knowledge before obtaining discovery necessary to prove it. Those safeguards reduce costs without pretending to eliminate them. The alleged procurement and use of a fabricated accusation warrant act-specific reconsideration; the separate opposition to counsel’s reappointment does not become actionable merely because prosecutors allegedly wanted Milstein convicted. The rule changes the absolute-advocacy bar itself. The distinct qualified-immunity question remains for adjudication under the law actually governing that issue; the proposed exception does not silently answer it. The change applies to this open case and future open cases; it does not reopen final judgments. Institutional planning around immunity is a real reliance interest, but at this boundary it does not outweigh the claimant’s interest in a forum for the alleged deliberate deprivation. No property title, completed transaction or final judgment must be undone. Deciding only the investigative acts would leave the challenged advocacy dismissal resting on the very categorical bar Stone rejects, so the bounded rule is necessary to his proposed disposition.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No rule governing an independently applicable constitutional privilege, unrelated testimony by nonparties, or municipal liability is required.

## 46. Department of Housing and Urban Development v. Rucker / Oakland Housing Authority v. Rucker

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-03-26, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the injunction judgment insofar as it rests on a categorical tenant-knowledge requirement and remand both consolidated dockets.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require reconsideration of the preliminary injunction under the correct statutory construction. Leave actual eviction to the authorized forum upon proof of covered activity and satisfaction of applicable procedural and other preserved legal requirements. Do not direct eviction of any named tenant.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Lease authority and knowledge

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The statute supplies the challenged lease authority, so its meaning must be decided. Address the asserted facial due-process objection only to the extent necessary to reject the proposed compulsory knowledge qualification.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress authorized local termination discretion for qualifying household or guest drug activity without the tenant’s actual or constructive knowledge.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing statutory and constitutional distinctions to an unresolved clause; do not create a new criminal-liability rule or displace procedural protection.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1437d(l)(6) permits a lease to make qualifying household-member or guest drug activity a ground for termination without an additional tenant-knowledge requirement. It confers discretion to seek termination rather than commands eviction. That permissive civil condition is not facially invalid merely because personal criminal fault is absent; the authority must still be exercised through lawful procedures and within the actual lease and statute.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the statutorily identified relationships and activity. It does not reach a stranger merely by association, establish that a particular guest relationship existed, or remove the tenant’s ability to contest the charge and other applicable defenses.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory categories and consequence must be taken seriously. Permission to use the dwelling through household membership or guest status explains the tenancy nexus without pretending that the tenant committed the underlying crime. At the same time, cause for termination is not an inexorable sanction. Preserving that distinction respects the local decision Congress authorized and prevents the statutory ruling from becoming an unsupported eviction order. The statute’s categories, the absence of a tenant-knowledge condition, and its permissive termination consequence carry decisive weight. Stone uses text to identify authority and ordinary due-process review to reject only the facial claim that civil tenancy responsibility necessarily requires criminal fault. Neither a landlord label nor a civil label exempts government from notice, contest, and lawful exercise of discretion. Nor does the gravity of housing loss authorize rewriting the enacted relationship categories. The rule resolves the statutory challenge for both dockets while leaving actual eviction facts and genuinely independent defenses to the proceeding that adjudicates them.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The validity of a particular exercise of discretion and disputed facts in the state proceedings are reserved because those individualized issues are not resolved by this preliminary categorical challenge.

## 47. S.S. ex rel. Jervis v. McMullen +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the dismissal and remand for adjudication under the bounded custodial-transfer rule proposed below.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate the pleaded federal claim sufficiently to determine individual conduct, knowledge, authority, causation, defenses, and injury. Preserve conduct-date qualified immunity; award no damages and make no final liability findings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Affirmative custodial transfer and danger

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The allegations identify a completed state placement of a child already in state custody, rather than a request that officials prevent private abuse generally. They therefore present the affirmative-deprivation question necessary to the dismissal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether officials may avoid review of a knowing, dangerous custodial transfer by comparing it only with a hypothetical absence of all prior state assistance.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Extend the bounded direct-restraint principle to this protective-custody placement. To the extent DeShaney’s return language forecloses scrutiny solely because the child once faced the danger before custody, propose limiting that proposition. This is not represented as an already adopted general danger doctrine.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** When officials controlling a child’s custodial placement knowingly transfer the child into a specifically appreciated serious danger, their own affirmative act is subject to substantive-due-process review. The reference point is the actual protection and control they exercise when making the transfer; earlier exposure to a similar danger does not categorically erase their later causal act. The claimant must prove each official’s authority, knowing participation, unreasonable disregard, and resulting injury.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs an actual custodial placement into a known specific danger. It creates no duty to remove every child from private custody, preserve state custody indefinitely, investigate every warning, or guarantee that an otherwise reasonable reunification succeeds.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The decisive principle is responsibility for an affirmative exercise of custody, not a free-standing entitlement to competent child protection. City of Florence supplies the closest analogy: officials cannot use control over a person to deliver her knowingly into a specific grave danger. Stone would extend that principle to a deliberate protective-custody transfer, while retaining DeShaney's rule for failures to protect people already in private custody. To the extent DeShaney's statement about return to an earlier danger would categorically foreclose this affirmative-placement claim, he would narrow that statement: it cannot erase a later, separately culpable exercise of control. This limited correction protects legitimate reunification discretion and does not require retaining children whenever officials cannot guarantee safety. The pleaded knowledge, placement authority and causal exposure justify adjudication; they establish neither damages nor defeat of conduct-time immunity.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: Culpability after an opportunity to deliberate

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The court below offered culpability as an independent dismissal ground, so correcting only its danger comparison would not dispose of the case.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether knowingly choosing this placement after a real opportunity to evaluate the specific risk can constitute conscience-shocking disregard.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the contextual distinction in Lewis and extend the custodial-transfer standard stated above to the alleged facts. Do not establish liability from negligence or the offender’s conduct alone.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A considered custodial transfer made with actual awareness of a specific grave risk, and with deliberate and unreasonable disregard of a meaningful opportunity to avoid it, can satisfy substantive due process. The pleaded knowledge and deliberation suffice to survive the categorical culpability dismissal; intent to cause the eventual abuse is not indispensable in this deliberative setting.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule requires actual appreciation and an unreasonable choice by the defendant with authority to act. Competing lawful duties and reasonable professional judgments remain relevant; a bad outcome or an incorrect prediction alone is insufficient.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Lewis makes the opportunity for deliberation legally material; its purpose-to-harm requirement for a rapidly developing pursuit is not a categorical rule for considered custodial decisions. Stone uses that contextual culpability standard, with actual knowledge and causal responsibility carrying the decisive weight. A reasonable professional choice among lawful protective alternatives does not shock the conscience merely because abuse follows. But the complaint alleges a considered return despite a specifically appreciated risk of sexual abuse and an opportunity to avoid that exposure. Taking those allegations as true, a reunification label cannot settle the contested reasonableness of the choice. This is a pleading holding under a demanding abuse-of-power standard, not strict liability for the private assailant's acts.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Individual immunity cannot be finally resolved without the specific conduct dates, knowledge, and relevant contemporaneous law; that record-dependent defense is preserved.

## 48. Hoffman Plastic Compounds, Inc. v. NLRB

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm enforcement of the limited backpay award.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Enforce backpay through the actual discovery cutoff under the Board’s lawful calculation, with no reinstatement or later accrual premised on unauthorized employment. Immigration enforcement and sanctions remain separately available; this award confers no work authorization.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Reconciling labor remedies and employment authorization

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The labor violation is established and the disputed component is monetary relief. Resolve the statutory interaction without revisiting liability or ordering employment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether IRCA withdraws the Board’s authority to impose a limited pre-discovery monetary remedy for an established retaliatory discharge.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinction between liability and relief and propose a specific reconciliation of the statutes. McKennon is an analogy, not a controlling interpretation of the NLRA or IRCA.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** IRCA does not categorically eliminate the Board’s §10(c) authority to award backpay for an established antiunion discharge during the period before the employer discovers the worker’s disqualification. Such an award may enforce the labor duty without requiring unlawful hiring, continued work, or reinstatement. Discovery of the legal bar ends the ordinary basis for further lost-work relief, and the separate immigration violations remain enforceable.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns the limited remedial award actually ordered for an employee protected by the labor statute. It does not guarantee employment, erase document fraud, or decide an award for periods after the employer knows continued employment is forbidden.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 10(c)'s remedial authorization, read together with IRCA's actual prohibitions, supplies the decisive ground. Stone combines statutory construction with examination of the remedy's operation: backpay for an employer's completed unlawful retaliation neither orders future unauthorized work nor excuses false employment documents. ABF Freight rejects an automatic remedial forfeiture from misconduct; McKennon supports separating an established violation from a subsequently discovered employment bar. Neither controls IRCA, and Sure-Tan's treatment of employees who had departed the country does not compel an identical remedy for a worker who remained here. The award's discovery cutoff gives disqualification practical effect. The contrary argument—that compensation for work which could not lawfully be performed necessarily conflicts with IRCA—is substantial, but it supplies no statutory withdrawal of this bounded monetary sanction. The holding extends no right to reinstatement and rests on the two enactments, not deterrence alone.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Distinct disputes over the backpay computation or unlawful mitigation activity require a preserved record and are not newly decided here.
