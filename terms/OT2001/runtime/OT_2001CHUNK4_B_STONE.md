## 55. Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment invalidating the two challenged definitions on substantial-overbreadth grounds.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve relief against enforcement of §§2256(8)(B) and (D) through the challenged statutory scheme. Leave independent actual-child and obscenity prohibitions, and the unchallenged identifiable-minor provision, intact. Award no criminal immunity outside the invalidated definitions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Depictions made without an actual child

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The facial criminal restriction burdens the challengers’ expression and requires review of its substantial protected applications. The overbreadth ground suffices without a separate Fifth Amendment vagueness judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether apparent youth alone permits prohibition of otherwise protected depictions made without exploiting an actual child.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Reno and Playboy’s content-based speech rules to the new statutory category. Reject expansion of the actual-child exception without its harm predicate.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A criminal ban substantially reaching nonobscene depictions produced without an actual child cannot be sustained merely because the depicted person appears to be a minor. The actual exploitation that supports Ferber cannot be supplied by visual resemblance alone. The asserted preventive and enforcement interests do not justify the challenged provision’s substantial protected reach under the governing tailoring requirement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule protects expression that lacks the predicates of an established unprotected category. It does not protect actual abuse production, material obscene under Miller, or independently criminal conduct involving an image.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Ferber's categorical exclusion rests on injury to actual children in production and circulation; Miller supplies a separate obscenity rule. Stone distinguishes both rather than enlarging either to visual resemblance. Reno and Playboy require the Government to justify content-selected suppression of protected adult expression. Under that governing scrutiny, the prospect that a lawful image might stimulate misconduct and the difficulty of proving actual-child production do not justify suppressing substantial protected depictions. The absence of an actual exploited child is decisive in rejecting the Ferber analogy; the breadth of the criminal prohibition then defeats narrow tailoring. The law's concrete reach into nonobscene works warrants facial overbreadth relief, rather than forcing each lawful speaker to risk prosecution. This preserves the actual-child and obscenity categories without creating a general immunity for conduct involving an image.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: Presentation as depicting a minor

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The second definition independently reaches expression and must be resolved rather than assumed to rise or fall with the first.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether promotional characterization can make an otherwise protected depiction contraband under the challenged definition.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing overbreadth and severance limits. Do not deny legislative power over a separately defined unlawful offer or solicitation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The presentation-based definition is substantially overbroad insofar as it turns protected depictions into prohibited material through how they are described, without the predicates of actual exploitation, obscenity, or a separately defined unlawful transaction. It cannot be saved by judicially rewriting it into a narrower solicitation offense.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding reaches the challenged definition and connected liability, not truthful or false representations that independently satisfy a valid fraud or criminal-solicitation law.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Promotional characterization can help prove an actual obscenity, fraud or unlawful-offer offense; it cannot automatically change a protected work into contraband for later possessors unconnected with that characterization. Stone applies Reno's limit on judicial rewriting and distinguishes Ginzburg's use of pandering in identifying obscenity from a free-standing power to forbid nonobscene material. Text and actual statutory reach carry decisive weight: the definition reaches beyond a confined solicitation rule, and its defense does not cure all protected applications. Substantial overbreadth therefore defeats this definition independently of the appearance provision. Reserving a differently enacted offer offense is warranted because its elements and parties are absent here; it is not a reservation of the provision actually challenged.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The constitutionality of the unchallenged identifiable-actual-minor provision and a differently enacted offer offense lies outside the questions presented.

## 56. United States v. Craft

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the no-attachment ruling and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Recognize the lien against the husband’s qualifying rights and determine their value and lawful consequence for the escrow, preserving the wife’s independent rights and any applicable priority or enforcement protections. Do not prescribe an automatic fifty-percent recovery.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: State-created rights and federal lien classification

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The property issue was preserved through the appellate sequence and is necessary to the escrow dispute. The Court need not resolve the alternative transfer theory.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the husband’s actual Michigan entireties rights qualify under §6321 despite limits on unilateral transfer and separate creditor access.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Drye’s controlling allocation of state definition and federal classification to a different property arrangement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A taxpayer’s present rights to use, exclude, enjoy income, and participate in disposition of entireties property constitute property or rights to property under §6321 even though state law prevents unilateral conveyance of the entire estate and shields it from ordinary separate creditors. The lien reaches those actual taxpayer rights; it creates no ownership of the other spouse’s separate rights.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding applies to the established Michigan incidents of this estate. It does not hold that a bare future expectancy alone suffices or that all States create identical marital property rights.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Drye's two-step allocation is controlling: Michigan defines the rights, and §6321 determines their federal consequence. Stone extends that rule to an entireties estate because the taxpayer's use, exclusion, income and survivorship interests are existing legal advantages, even though he cannot convey the whole estate alone. The statutory reference to rights to property makes those actual incidents decisive over a state creditor-exemption label. This combines enacted language with functional examination of the ownership bundle; the federal statute, not a general preference for collection, determines the result. It does not turn the wife's property into the husband's or establish a fifty-percent valuation. Attachment is the granted question, while valuation and lawful administration of the escrow require their distinct determinations on remand.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Valuation, independent priority, and any additional enforcement or transfer issue require the remand record and are reserved on those grounds.

## 57. Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the preserved categorical taking claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny compensation on the temporal-segmentation theory. Do not reopen the unappealed Penn Central ruling or decide compensation for separate later periods. Preserve contextual review for properly presented temporary restrictions in other claims.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Temporary restriction and the relevant property

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The only viable reviewed compensation theory is categorical. Decide it without reopening the expressly abandoned contextual theory or extending the period through disputed later causes.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a moratorium becomes a Lucas taking solely by defining the relevant property as the time during which development was prohibited.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Resolve Lucas’s reserved denominator application through the continuing property interest and preserved contextual framework. Do not alter the right to compensation for a temporary taking once independently established.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A temporary development moratorium does not establish a categorical total-deprivation taking merely because development is barred throughout its duration. The continuing property interest is not divided into the exact temporal segment chosen by the regulation for that purpose. Its actual economic impact, expectations, duration, and governmental character remain subject to Penn Central where that claim is preserved. A temporary label supplies no blanket defense.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns regulatory development restrictions without physical appropriation. It preserves compensation for an established temporary taking and review of abusive, repeated, or exceptionally burdensome restrictions on an adequate record.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone distinguishes the physical-occupation rule: no government or third party occupies the land. Lucas addresses complete regulatory deprivation of the legally relevant property, while First English supplies compensation after a taking has been established; it does not make every temporary loss a taking. Palazzolo reaffirms that meaningful remaining use defeats the complete-deprivation category while preserving Penn Central's combined inquiry. The decisive point here is that isolating the restricted months from the continuing ownership would manufacture totality rather than measure it. Economic impact, expectations and governmental character ordinarily call for Penn Central's contextual standard; duration remains material within it and is no immunity. Because petitioners confined review to a categorical facial theory and abandoned the contextual claim, rejecting that theory ends this case. No safe harbor for all moratoria, fixed permissible duration or future finding of no taking follows.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The merits of the abandoned Penn Central theory, later-plan causation, and a differently preserved repeated-moratorium claim are outside the reviewed scope.

## 58. Michigan v. Environmental Protection Agency +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment rejecting the three presented categorical challenges, preserving the lower court’s separate state-specific vacaturs and notice remands.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the challenged budget framework operative to the extent already sustained, while preserving the circuit’s separate state-specific vacaturs and notice remands. Issue no judicial emissions budget, no compulsory state statute, and no approval of unreviewed technical choices.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Cost within the interstate-contribution provision

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The challenge concerns a factor EPA may use in implementing the interstate obligation, so statutory interpretation precedes the constitutional questions.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether demonstrated interstate contribution may be evaluated with the availability of highly cost-effective controls in determining the significant emissions to be eliminated.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing delegation limits and adopt a provision-specific reading. Do not establish a general presumption that cost is either required or forbidden throughout the Act.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The interstate-contribution provision permits EPA to consider the cost-effectiveness of controls as part of determining the significant contribution to be eliminated, after establishing the relevant connection between upwind emissions and downwind nonattainment or maintenance. Cost cannot substitute for that connection or authorize unrelated reductions. The categorical objection to its consideration therefore fails.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs implementation of the identified interstate duty. It does not decide cost considerations in setting national health standards, excuse unsupported modeling, or foreclose a properly preserved challenge to a particular cutoff or overcontrol.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Stone distinguishes Whitman's exclusion of implementation costs from national health-standard setting: §110(a)(2)(D)(i)(I) instead assigns elimination of significant interstate contribution during implementation. The text's relational term significant permits consideration of practicable, highly cost-effective correction once a real source-to-receptor contribution is established. Statutory scope is decisive; technical judgment then receives the respect authorized by the actual delegation and supported record. Cost alone could not justify regulating a State that contributes no relevant pollution or overriding an applicable downwind obligation. This provision-specific construction reconciles rather than narrows Whitman and rejects the categorical claim that cost is invariably forbidden. The particular modeling, selected threshold and surviving state-specific objections are governed by their own preserved review issues.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: State selection of control measures

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The State-discretion challenge remains after the cost construction and concerns the actual structure of the rule.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether statewide budgets themselves unlawfully dictate the States’ chosen pollution-control measures.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinction between a valid federal result and compulsory state administration, together with the Act’s independent preservation of state means.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A federally authorized emissions budget does not unlawfully displace state selection when States retain genuine options among control measures and the statutory scheme provides lawful federal implementation rather than an unavoidable command to enact a prescribed program. The budgets challenged categorically here set the result and do not require adoption of EPA’s illustrative control mix.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule permits federal adequacy criteria and real state implementation choice. It does not validate nominal alternatives that are impracticable or a separate order requiring a State to legislate one specified means.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** New York v. United States distinguishes federal regulation from conscription of state lawmaking. Stone applies that structural boundary together with the Act's express allocation of means to the States; the latter statutory protection can exceed the constitutional minimum. A budget fixes the federally authorized result while leaving States real choices among sources and controls, with lawful federal implementation if a State declines to administer a compliant plan. The availability of those alternatives, not the cooperative label, is decisive. EPA's illustrative control assumptions do not compel the same state mix. The categorical challenge therefore fails without validating an actual command to legislate or a later plan that eliminates the options the statute preserves.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 3: Congressional guidance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Because the statute permits the challenged cost consideration, reach the preserved delegation objection under Whitman’s controlling guidance and no-agency-cure rules.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress supplied the legislative policy and boundaries for this interstate implementation judgment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Whitman’s intelligible-principle holding and its independent prohibition on agency cure of a standardless delegation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The provision supplies adequate guidance through the regulated emissions, interstate connection, downwind national-standard problem, and significant-contribution or maintenance limitation. Those enacted boundaries permit technical implementation, including the bounded cost consideration described above. The delegation does not become valid merely because EPA presently exercises it in a limited area; its validity rests on Congress’s criteria.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule sustains this particular guided assignment, not every broad environmental delegation or a power to replace statutory objectives with self-selected ones.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Whitman now controls the guidance question: Congress must supply the policy and enforceable limits, and an agency cannot cure their absence by voluntarily constraining itself. Stone applies that rule here. The specified pollutants, interstate causal link, downwind attainment or maintenance problem and significant-contribution obligation bound the assigned judgment. Combining structural responsibility with the enacted criteria, he gives decisive weight to those congressional limits, not EPA's current restraint or the economic scale of regulation. Congress need not select every numerical budget. A wrong calculation or unexplained cutoff can fail ordinary review without making the delegation itself unconstitutional. This reasoning resolves the presented delegation objection and requires no new general nondelegation test.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The independent adequacy of the exact cost cutoff and unreviewed geographic and notice disputes remain outside the presented questions or their preserved scope.

## 59. Universal City Studios, Inc. v. Corley +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the injunction at its stated posting and knowing-purposeful-linking scope.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit enforcement only within the injunction’s defined technology and linking conditions. No damages are awarded. A distinct actual application to research, interoperability, public-domain material or a demonstrated indispensable lawful use requires its own preserved record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Protected code and a regulable operative function

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The injunction directly restricts the defendants’ conduct and publication; constitutional review is necessary after the statutory scope is fixed.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do the speech character of code and §1201(c)(1) invalidate this trafficking injunction?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend the existing content-neutral functional analysis to this technology and reaffirm independent scrutiny; distinguish Reno’s suppression of communicative content.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Code is protected expression, but §1201(a)(2)’s restriction on the supplied access-circumvention function is content-neutral in this application. Section 1201(c)(1) preserves defenses to copyright infringement; it does not create a general defense to trafficking in circumvention technology. Protecting lawful access controls is a substantial interest, and the specified posting restraint adequately fits that interest on this record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This governs dissemination of the identified functioning tool meeting the statutory conditions. It does not classify every discussion, algorithm, publication or hyperlink as unprotected conduct.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Statutory text and structure decide the asserted fair-use defense: the distinct infringement and anti-trafficking provisions, with specific exceptions, cannot be collapsed into one. Constitutional review then combines functional classification with intermediate scrutiny; the actual fit carries decisive weight on validity. The tool’s expressive value remains relevant, but its immediate circumvention capacity explains the restriction without reference to a disfavored idea. The record supports the practical connection to unauthorized access. Stone does not defer merely because Congress used a technical label, or announce that copyright concerns always prevail over speech.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** An actual application that blocks necessary lawful research or a substantiated fair use is not established here.

### Issue 2: Linking, fair-use objections and the rule’s limits

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The record contains findings supporting the specific linking restraint, so its validity can be decided without prescribing every future hyperlink rule.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Is the linking restriction overbroad, or does the presented record establish impermissible extension of copyright or destruction of fair use?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Sustain a defined adequately tailored application without treating its safeguards as a universal minimum test for all linking.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The injunction is valid where clear and convincing proof establishes knowledge that the linked material is present, knowledge that it is unlawfully offered circumvention technology and a purpose to disseminate that technology. Those restrictions adequately protect innocent or merely referential linking here. The absence of a demonstrated blocked fair use or application beyond a protected work defeats the presented broader constitutional challenges.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This identifies a sufficient set of safeguards for the actual injunction; it neither approves strict liability for linking nor decides all conduct outside this protected decisional boundary.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A hyperlink can communicate reference and also supply immediate operational access. Its speed does not remove speech protection, but the proved knowledge and dissemination purpose materially narrow the connection to the prohibited conduct. Stone approves that defined application rather than balancing away ordinary reporting. Nor does affirmance establish perpetual copyright: an actual restriction on public-domain access or a necessary lawful use must be evaluated when shown. The defendants’ broader claims fail on this record, not on a conclusion that fair use is constitutionally irrelevant. Deciding this sufficient application respects both the complete question before the Court and the absent facts needed for broader holdings.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The constitutional minimum for every linking injunction and an actual public-domain or indispensable-fair-use application are not presented.

## 60. A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc. +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the requirement to narrow the preliminary injunction and the sufficiency of the supported preliminary infringement and secondary-liability predicates; remand for a fitted order.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Keep the overbroad order stayed until replaced. Require owners to identify their works and infringing files with usable notice and require Napster to act within its actual service capabilities, including reasonable enforcement against identified repeat infringers. Address the remaining equitable predicates and applicable §512 protections expressly; order no final damages or guarantee of eliminating every unknown file.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Fair use is contextual and does not protect unrestricted redistribution

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The asserted defenses directly bear on likely success; no final judgment about every user or file is necessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do sampling, space-shifting, Sony or the Audio Home Recording Act defeat the preliminary infringement showing?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Campbell’s contextual factors and distinguish Sony’s private time-shifting from the challenged open redistribution.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The supported wholesale exchange of protected recordings to unrelated users is unlikely to be fair use on this record. Whole-work copying, lack of transformation and supported market substitution outweigh the asserted sampling or relocation purposes. Noninfringing files remain lawful. Section 1008 does not furnish immunity for this general-purpose computer service outside its defined recording-device and medium coverage.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This decides the asserted defenses to the identified preliminary showing, not every private copy, every sampling practice or a rule that all noncommercial copying infringes.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory factors carry decisive weight, considered together rather than as a per se commercial-use rule. A user’s claimed plan to buy some recordings later does not erase redistribution or market substitution. Moving one’s own music within a private setting is materially different from making it available through an open exchange. Sony remains important to technology liability, but its protected application cannot be expanded into immunity for every resulting service. Congress’s defined recording exemption likewise cannot be detached from its coverage limits.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Final liability for particular users and genuinely different lawful copying remain outside this preliminary record.

### Issue 2: Knowledge, control and equitable limits must each do their own work

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The ongoing operation and actual notices make secondary liability an issue beyond the bare sale of a technology.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** When may this service be preliminarily restrained without imposing liability from capability alone?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply existing secondary-liability elements to ongoing service conduct; preserve Sony and the statutory safe-harbor inquiry.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Knowing material assistance to identified infringement can support contributory liability despite substantial lawful uses of the technology. Vicarious liability separately requires the pertinent financial benefit and right and ability to supervise. The preliminary record supports those predicates, but an injunction must require intelligible owner notice and feasible service responses rather than shift all identification and prevention to Napster. Section 512 is not categorically unavailable merely because infringement occurred.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the identified ongoing service functions and preserves lawful files, defenses and limits on actual control. It creates no universal monitoring duty for every technology supplier.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Precedent supplies the elements; functional examination of what this service can actually do decides their application. Those methods are combined with the legal elements carrying decisive weight. Specific knowledge is not the same as general awareness that a product can be misused. Likewise a theoretical power to shut down the entire system is not automatically a duty to inspect inaccessible user files. The remedial limit follows the proved relationship between notice, contribution and practical control. Stone articulates that future-facing rule because otherwise the injunction would silently turn secondary liability into a guarantee against all infringement; remand is for a conforming order and necessary factual application, not to choose the governing rule.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Final damages, factual questions needed for particular §512 protection and control over technologies not in this record remain unresolved.

### Issue 3: The remaining defenses do not defeat fitted preliminary relief

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** These objections were presented in the preliminary-injunction appeal. Their rejection does not determine final damages or bar a materially different later showing.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Do the asserted owner-conduct defenses, speech interests, procedural objections, proposed royalties or bond require withholding or changing the preliminary remedy?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing defense elements and ordinary preliminary-review standards; reserve lawful exchange through the injunction’s limits rather than create blanket service immunity.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The present record establishes neither intentional surrender, permission for this service’s copying, nor use of copyright to control something outside the protected reproduction and distribution rights. Speech interests require preserving lawful exchange, not immunizing the identified infringement. No supported prejudice requires further discovery or an evidentiary hearing at this preliminary stage. The $5 million security falls within discretion on the competing evidence. Technological novelty and this record supply no basis for compulsory royalties instead of a fitted injunction.
* **Reach Within the Question:** These are record-specific preliminary determinations. The tailored order must preserve lawful files, artist-authorized distribution and independent user expression.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defense elements and the applicable standard of review carry decisive weight. Exploration of digital business models or toleration of some copying does not by itself communicate permission for Napster’s challenged activity or an intentional surrender of enforcement rights. Enforcing reproduction and distribution rights against the identified copying is not an extension of copyright to unrelated property. Speech protection therefore bears on the scope of restraint, while the infringement showing supports effective relief within that scope. The submitted preliminary record permits the necessary determinations without a demonstrated prejudicial denial of further process. Security must address reasonably supported wrongful-injunction loss; competing estimates do not make the amount an abuse of discretion. A court should not replace the statutory exclusive rights with a new compulsory license simply because the service is technologically novel. These limited conclusions dispose of the actual objections without announcing universal rules about every digital service.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A later supported defense, final damages, lawful distribution agreements and materially changed security evidence remain outside this preliminary decision.
