## 81. SEC v. Zandford

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded unanimously, 9 to 0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens wrote for the Court.
* **Historical Holding:** The SEC sufficiently alleged a fraudulent scheme in which the securities sales and misappropriation coincided. The Court rejected a requirement that the fraud concern the value of a security and left further proceedings on the civil record.
* **Historical Authority:** United States v. O’Hagan and the statutory connection requirement informed the analysis.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/813/).

## 82. United States v. Mackby +

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** No Supreme Court merits comparator is supplied. The historical anchor is the Ninth Circuit’s affirmance of liability and remand for an Excessive Fines determination.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** The reported panel comprised Thompson, O’Scannlain, and Tashima; no separate dissent is recorded in the amended disposition.
* **Historical Holding:** The combined False Claims Act penalties and treble damages were at least partly punitive and subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. The panel did not itself settle whether the award was excessive.
* **Historical Authority:** Austin v. United States and United States v. Bajakajian supplied the constitutional framework.
* **Historical Sources:** [Amended Ninth Circuit opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-9th-circuit/1214536.html).

## 83. Holmes Group, Inc. v. Vornado Air Circulation Systems, Inc.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded unanimously as to the judgment, with differing rationales.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Scalia wrote the principal opinion, fully joined by Rehnquist, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Breyer. Stevens joined Parts I and II-A and wrote separately. Ginsburg, joined by O’Connor, concurred in the judgment and emphasized the unadjudicated patent counterclaim.
* **Historical Holding:** A patent counterclaim did not supply the required arising-under basis for Federal Circuit jurisdiction. The separate opinions discussed the significance of later adjudicated claims and statutory policy.
* **Historical Authority:** Christianson and the well-pleaded-complaint rule informed interpretation of the patent jurisdiction statutes. The historical intervening trade-dress decision was TrafFix Devices v. Marketing Displays.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/826/).

## 84. Devlin v. Scardelletti

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 6 to 3.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor wrote for Rehnquist, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, and herself. Scalia dissented, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.
* **Historical Holding:** A nonnamed class member who timely objected could appeal settlement approval without first intervening. The Court distinguished constitutional standing from the procedural ability to appeal.
* **Historical Authority:** The class rules, Marino, and appellate party-status principles supplied the historical analysis. The simulation’s reasoned Felzen holding must not be replaced by its different historical procedural history.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion and dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/1/).

## 85. McKune v. Lile

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 5 to 4 in the judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy’s plurality was joined by Rehnquist, Scalia, and Thomas. O’Connor concurred in the judgment but rejected the plurality’s use of the atypical-and-significant-hardship measure as the compulsion standard. Stevens dissented, joined by Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
* **Historical Holding:** Five Justices concluded that the challenged consequences were insufficient to establish compulsion, but no majority adopted the plurality’s complete rationale. O’Connor relied on the limited severity of the consequences in this case.
* **Historical Authority:** The opinions addressed the penalty cases and the historical Sandin approach. The latter differs from the supplied simulated Sandin holding.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/24/).

## 86. Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Echazabal

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9 to 0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter delivered the unanimous opinion.
* **Historical Holding:** The ADA permitted the EEOC regulation allowing a defense that the job would endanger the worker’s own health. The regulation’s individualized risk requirements constrained that defense; its validity did not itself resolve every question concerning Chevron’s application.
* **Historical Authority:** The opinion examined the qualification provisions, the express direct-threat example, and Chevron deference. Historical disability decisions do not supersede the simulation’s different underlying-impairment rule.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/73/).

## 87. JPMorgan Chase Bank v. Traffic Stream (BVI) Infrastructure Ltd.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9 to 0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter delivered the unanimous opinion.
* **Historical Holding:** A British Virgin Islands corporation is a citizen or subject of the United Kingdom for alienage jurisdiction. The United Kingdom’s sovereignty, rather than the territory’s lack of separate sovereign recognition or individual nationality categories, supplied the relevant relationship.
* **Historical Authority:** The opinion examined the alienage statute’s text, history, and treatment of dependent territories.

## 88. National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded. The discrete-act rule was unanimous; the hostile-environment treatment commanded five votes.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Thomas’s full opinion was joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Rehnquist, O’Connor, Scalia, and Kennedy joined Part II-A. O’Connor concurred in part and dissented in part; Rehnquist joined that opinion, Scalia and Kennedy joined all but its Part I, and Breyer joined Part I.
* **Historical Holding:** Discrete acts require timely charges even when related to later acts. One hostile environment may include older components if a contributing act occurs within the filing period, subject to the stated limits and equitable doctrines.
* **Historical Authority:** The Court analyzed the statutory occurrence language, the discrete-decision precedents, and the collective character of a hostile environment.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion and separate opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/101/).

## 90. Franconia Associates v. United States

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9 to 0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Ginsburg delivered the unanimous opinion.
* **Historical Holding:** The 1988 restrictions were a repudiation of the asserted prepayment undertaking, rather than necessarily an immediate breach. Unless the borrowers elected to treat repudiation as breach, the contract claim accrued when prepayment was tendered and refused.
* **Historical Authority:** The opinion applied ordinary anticipatory-repudiation principles while leaving remaining contract issues for further proceedings.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/129/).

## 93. Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of New York, Inc. v. Village of Stratton

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 8 to 1 in the judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens wrote the Court’s opinion, joined by O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Breyer concurred, joined by Souter and Ginsburg. Scalia concurred in the judgment, joined by Thomas. Rehnquist dissented.
* **Historical Holding:** The ordinance’s broad permit prerequisite impermissibly burdened noncommercial religious and political advocacy, including anonymous and spontaneous speech. The decision did not establish a right to disregard private residents’ wishes or invalidate every commercial-solicitation rule.
* **Historical Authority:** The opinions considered anonymous pamphleteering, door-to-door advocacy, and the fit of the village’s crime, fraud, and privacy interests.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion and separate opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/150/).
