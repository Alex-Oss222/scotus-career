### Issue 1: Express exception and ordinary accrual

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Rotella separates discovery of an injury from later discovery of a complete pattern in its own statute. It does not establish a universal discovery rule for every federal action.

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Harris v. Garner applies the PLRA physical-injury restriction to mental or emotional injury claims by persons confined when they file; it does not automatically bar every form of relief for a constitutional violation. Carey v. Piphus distinguishes nominal relief from unproved compensatory loss, and Smith v. Wade requires punitive culpability beyond the violation alone.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate constitutional liability from compensatory injury, nominal recognition, and punishment. Each remedy requires its own legal basis, burden, and factual predicate. A statutory limitation on one injury category should not silently become immunity from the underlying right.
* **Other Governing Law:** The then-operative prisoner-litigation provision, the constitutional religious right actually adjudicated, and the rules governing damages instructions apply. Later statutory revisions are not imported.
* **Material Other Authority:** Carey v. Piphus supplies historical context for nominal relief when a violation is established without compensable injury; Smith v. Wade supplies the historical punitive-fault formulation, with its separate culpability requirement.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The statute bars the specified compensatory recovery absent physical injury. It does not by its text equate a constitutional violation with mental distress or expressly classify all punitive awards as compensation.
* **Open Point:** Whether nominal and punitive relief remain available, whether the compensatory award belongs to the barred category, and how the flawed punitive instruction affects the judgment.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The appellate court treated the compensatory award as barred mental or emotional injury damages, recognized nominal relief, and held punitive relief legally available but required reconsideration under proper instructions. **Official:** The statute was designed to restrict prison damages litigation where no physical injury exists, and constitutional labels should not permit recovery of the same barred distress damages. The existing punitive award was influenced by an invalid compensatory premise. **Searles:** A religious violation can exist without bodily harm. Nominal damages acknowledge the wrong, and punitive damages address sufficiently culpable conduct rather than compensate distress. His strongest rule preserves those remedies while applying their separate requirements. **Limits:** The absence of physical injury does not automatically establish punitive entitlement. Likewise, recognition of nominal damages does not authorize a compensatory award under another label. Any actual economic or other non-emotional injury would need its own preserved proof; none is invented to save this award.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Remedy-separation path:** Vacate barred compensation, require nominal relief for established liability, and remand punitive damages under a legally correct instruction. **Plausible Holding Scope:** The physical-injury rule reaches mental or emotional compensation, not every remedy for a constitutional violation. **Broad-bar path:** Treat the provision as preventing all damages absent physical injury. **Plausible Holding Scope:** This would add a categorical remedial immunity extending beyond the named injury category. **Instruction-only path:** If a punitive award is demonstrably independent of the invalid compensatory premise, preserve it under the applicable harmless-error standard. **Plausible Holding Scope:** Independence must be established from the actual instructions and verdict, not assumed. The present instruction linkage supports a new determination of punitive amount, preserving the separate culpability finding.

### Issue 1: Actual overlapping regulation and statutory displacement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling holding decides this precise interagency exclusion. The statutory framework requires the admitted statutory boundary rather than a general presumption that one agency’s potential jurisdiction excludes another.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish authority to regulate from exercise of that authority over the relevant working conditions. Assess the actual regulatory overlap and statutory terms before invoking deference or broad institutional policy.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Occupational Safety and Health Act’s coverage and other-agency provision, together with the Coast Guard regulations applicable to this uninspected vessel, govern.
* **Material Other Authority:** The working conditions addressed by an actual federal regulation may be displaced from OSHA even without a duplicate OSHA rule; mere residual authority or a post-accident investigation need not establish that displacement.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The statute supplies the exclusion’s actual-exercise requirement. It does not necessarily require identical wording in overlapping rules, nor establish that the entire maritime sector is beyond OSHA.
* **Open Point:** Whether the Coast Guard actually regulated the conditions OSHA cited and whether this barge falls within the workplace coverage before that exclusion is applied.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Fifth Circuit treated Coast Guard jurisdiction over the vessel as sufficient to displace OSHA, without requiring the condition-specific exercise urged by the Secretary. **Mallard Bay:** Maritime operations benefit from a coherent regulatory authority, and the Coast Guard’s vessel jurisdiction and investigation demonstrate an occupied field. Its strongest narrower contention would identify actual Coast Guard rules covering the hazards cited. **Secretary:** The statutory exclusion depends on exercise of authority, not its potential existence. Uninspected vessels are not subject to the same comprehensive rules as inspected vessels, leaving the disputed working conditions within OSHA’s coverage. **Alternative grounds:** The employer also contests workplace coverage. That threshold must be answered from the Act’s geographic and employment terms, not from an assumed categorical maritime exemption. Even recognition of OSHA authority does not prove the underlying violation, penalty, or every factual allegation in the citation.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Actual-exercise path:** Reverse the broad exclusion and remand for the remaining citation issues after determining that the relevant working conditions were not displaced. **Plausible Holding Scope:** Potential jurisdiction alone is insufficient; actual regulation of the pertinent conditions remains controlling. **Regulation-specific path:** Identify an existing Coast Guard rule that actually covers the cited conditions and displace OSHA to that extent. **Plausible Holding Scope:** The exclusion follows demonstrated regulatory coverage, not all activities aboard any vessel. **Workplace path:** Resolve the independent statutory coverage issue from the barge’s actual location and employment setting. **Plausible Holding Scope:** A vessel can be a covered workplace without making every maritime location subject to the same geographic rule.
