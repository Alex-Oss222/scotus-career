## 23. National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-16, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment insofar as it denies FCC authority over qualifying mixed cable-Internet and wireless telecommunications attachments.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for remaining objections to classification or particular rate rules. Preserve the distinction between jurisdiction and the validity of the rate ultimately imposed; do not fix an attachment price on this record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The statutory definition controls coverage

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide the presented authority questions while preserving unresolved method-specific objections.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the rate formulas narrow the general attachment jurisdiction and exclude wireless equipment.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the statutory allocation principles; adopt the specific coverage construction as a new holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The FCC’s authority over qualifying pole attachments is not lost because an attachment carries cable television together with Internet service or serves wireless telecommunications. The general definition and regulatory direction establish coverage; provisions prescribing rates for particular categories do not, without exclusionary language, exhaust that coverage. A covered attachment remains subject to the statutory limits governing its lawful rate, terms, and conditions.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding does not decide every service classification, validate every rate formula, or extend jurisdiction to property or entities outside the statutory definition.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The utilities’ method objection deserves a method-specific answer. Treating an incompletely specified formula as an exclusion would instead remove an otherwise qualifying attachment from the enacted general rule. The same distinction matters for wireless service: the transmission technology does not erase the provider’s physical equipment attached to the utility’s structure. A technology-specific exclusion requires textual support. This interpretation also preserves meaningful review of agency choices because broad coverage carries no presumption that a particular calculation is reasonable or statutorily authorized. The general definition and §224(b)’s just-and-reasonable direction are decisive for both questions. Stone reconciles them with the specialized formulas rather than allowing a formula’s incomplete coverage to repeal the general grant. Iowa Utilities Board supports enforcing a real congressional assignment; Mead requires identifying the actual delegation and does not itself supply missing authority. The mixed-service attachment remains one by a cable system, and wireless equipment remains an attachment by a telecommunications provider. This is a complete coverage rule, leaving only genuinely distinct rate or classification questions beyond the grant.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unresolved service classification and particular rate validity are reserved.

## 25. Kansas v. Crane

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-22, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Kansas Supreme Court’s complete-inability rule and remand for application of a serious functional impairment requirement; do not reinstate Crane’s commitment automatically.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require a finding of serious difficulty controlling the dangerous conduct, substantially connected to the qualifying mental impairment, under Kansas’s beyond-a-reasonable-doubt commitment burden. Determine whether the existing verdict and record lawfully establish that requirement; if not, the present order cannot stand, and any new proceeding requires independent lawful authority. Any properly preserved claim of retrospective punitive operation must be assessed under Hendricks and Seling on Crane’s actual program and circumstances.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A control test cannot cure independently unlawful punishment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Kansas judgment rests on the asserted need for complete inability to control behavior. That legal requirement is properly before the Court. Hendricks does not furnish a categorical alternative ground invalidating this later commitment without the required factual predicates.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether due process requires complete inability to control conduct and, if not, what functional impairment must distinguish this commitment from detention for ordinary criminal propensity.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Distinguish Hendricks’s as-applied punishment holding; propose a substantive due-process rule requiring serious impairment connected to the dangerous conduct, without requiring total incapacity. Apply Seling’s punishment framework only to an actually presented operational claim.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Due process does not require proof that a person lacks all control. It requires a serious present functional impairment producing serious difficulty controlling the dangerous conduct and substantially connected to the demonstrated danger that authorizes this form of civil custody. A diagnosis or a prediction of recidivism alone is insufficient. Kansas’s statutory beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden remains controlling; no lesser constitutional minimum reduces it. A verdict framed solely in terms of statutory diagnosis and likely repetition cannot be treated as necessarily resolving the required functional impairment.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule reaches the lack-of-control question in otherwise lawful civil commitment and is not confined to one diagnostic label. It neither validates punitive retrospective custody nor imports a complete treatment, review, or alternative-placement code into this grant.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Complete incapacity is an unrealistic boundary: serious impairment can make dangerous conduct exceptionally difficult to control while leaving some ability to choose. Eliminating impairment altogether would authorize confinement for predicted criminal propensity after punishment, which does not establish an independent civil basis. A functional standard is therefore decisive, grounded in liberty’s distinction between civil incapacity and ordinary culpable choice; clinical evidence informs the legal finding without making diagnosis conclusive. Hendricks is reaffirmed for its actual punishment holding and distinguished because its initial program facts are not findings about Crane. Seling requires the same separation of lawful civil purpose, actual operation, and retrospective effect. The State must establish the newly articulated control requirement under its own greater statutory burden. This answers the constitutional question the Kansas court used without either blessing the program generally or inventing a broader commitment regime.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The precise effect of Crane’s verdict and evidence requires application below. A facial redesign of civil commitment, new periodic-review procedures, and treatment or alternative-placement requirements not presented by the grant are not adjudicated; a preserved punishment challenge requires Crane-specific operational facts.

## 24. Lee v. Kemna

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-22, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment resting on the procedural-default ground and remand for the remaining habeas inquiry.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the inadequate state-default barrier. The federal court must adjudicate the continuance claim under the due-process rule against arbitrary denial of a meaningful opportunity to present a defense, considering the actual trial circumstances and any lawful remaining habeas restriction. The state appellate court did not adjudicate that federal merits claim, so §2254(d) does not attach merely to its procedural ruling. No release is ordered before violation and entitlement to relief are determined.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Adequacy requires a defensible application of the rule

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the adequacy issue before reaching the defense claim or an excuse for a valid default.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these written-motion and affidavit omissions adequately bar review on this record.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing default framework and adopt a narrow as-applied adequacy holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A state’s legitimate written-motion and affidavit requirements do not supply an adequate ground barring federal review when their application to an unexpected midtrial witness disappearance is exorbitant in the particular circumstances, the material reasons and anticipated testimony are substantially before the court, and the actual denial rests on other considerations. This is an exceptional application ruling, not a general dispensation from procedural compliance.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not treat every oral request as sufficient or every alternative trial reason as waiver of a formal requirement. The record must establish the exceptional mismatch.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Procedural rules serve decisions, including decisions refusing delay. Their general legitimacy does not explain why an additional piece of paper was essential after an unforeseen absence where the court already possessed the substance needed to consider the request. The State’s strongest point is that diligence and likely availability matter; those questions should be assessed against the actual record. The appropriate disposition permits federal examination without predetermining whether denying the requested delay violated the Constitution. The adequate-and-independent-ground doctrine carries decisive weight, including its requirement of a legitimate application, not only a facially legitimate rule. This is an as-applied exception to enforcement of an exorbitant formal demand, not replacement of Coleman or a free-floating hardship balance. Lee’s sworn explanation, subpoenas, prior disclosures, and short requested delay substantially supplied the information the rules seek; requiring a contemporaneous sworn account of an unexplained disappearance would demand what the emergency made unavailable. Because the state ground is inadequate, cause and prejudice are not a substitute threshold he must additionally satisfy. The remaining merits and relief inquiry is separate and must be resolved under its own legal burdens.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The due-process claim requires adjudication of the trial circumstances and disputed significance of the missing witnesses on a record the federal courts did not reach. The adequacy holding neither finds the alibi true nor resolves any distinct factual allegation about who caused the absence; those matters cannot be assumed in ordering ultimate habeas relief.

## 26. United States v. Microsoft Corp. +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-22, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the appellate disposition on the questions presented: require rule-of-reason treatment of the challenged tie, preserve reassignment and vacatur of the remedy, and decline a categorical rule requiring all liability findings to be vacated.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** On remand, require the new judge to revisit any retained finding whose material factual or credibility basis cannot be separated from the impartiality violation. Preserve genuinely independent determinations and conduct new lawful remedial proceedings. No additional liability ruling on unpresented antitrust components is proposed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Evaluate the actual competitive consequences of integration

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the preserved tying question without treating every monopolization finding as reopened.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this software combination warrants per se condemnation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the limits on categorical antitrust analysis; propose a narrow software-combination application.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The challenged integration of browser and operating-system functions must be evaluated under the rule of reason where the record presents material competitive and product-integration questions not reliably resolved by the existing per se tying category. The inquiry must examine product demand, coercion, power, foreclosure, and supported efficiencies. Integration is neither automatic justification nor immunity.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns this category and record; it does not invalidate all per se tying rules or resolve every exclusionary practice in the case.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The decisive concern is the reliability of the categorical inference. A product combination may change functionality while distribution restrictions may independently suppress rivals. An inquiry capable of distinguishing those effects provides a better legal basis for liability than either immediate condemnation or deference to the producer’s label. The government retains a full opportunity to prove anticompetitive harm, and Microsoft must substantiate the explanations material to its defense. Stone distinguishes Jefferson Parish’s per se inference for the product integration actually presented, rather than overruling tying law. California Dental’s insistence on an economically reliable basis for abbreviated condemnation is reaffirmed. The statutory competitive inquiry carries decisive weight: plaintiffs must establish anticompetitive effect, supported efficiencies must be considered, and any renewed condemnation must explain the resulting comparison. This defined exception to per se treatment is justified by the unreliable inference for the integrated platform, not by a general judicial preference for balancing.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Unpresented monopoly-maintenance and attempted-monopolization issues are not separately decided.

### Issue 2: Correct the appearance violation without inventing a universal vacatur rule

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Treat the secret interviews under the ordinary objective standard, then examine the dependence of each challenged component.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the violation necessarily requires vacating all liability as well as the remedy.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply §455(a), Liteky’s distinction between ordinary adjudication and extrajudicial conduct, and Liljeberg’s established vacatur factors.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Undisclosed press contacts during pending litigation that reasonably call impartiality into question require disqualification without proof of actual bias. The consequences for prior civil judgments require a remedial inquiry into the violation and the affected determinations. A new judge must reconsider findings materially dependent on compromised factfinding or discretionary judgment; independently sustainable determinations need not be erased solely because a violation occurred.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Independent legal review cannot be used to declare a genuinely tainted credibility judgment harmless by assumption. The decision supplies neither automatic total vacatur nor a blanket preservation rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Public confidence depends on impartial adjudication, including the opportunity to expose disqualifying conduct while the case is pending. An embargo on publication does not remove that concern. Still, the remedy should identify what must be redone to restore lawful adjudication. Reassignment and a new remedy are necessary here; broader liability reconsideration follows where the record cannot support meaningful separation. This approach protects both the litigants’ right to an impartial tribunal and the validity of determinations shown to have an independent foundation. Section 455(a) supplies disqualification; Liljeberg supplies the remedial factors of injustice to these parties, injustice in other cases, and public confidence. Stone applies that existing multifactor inquiry rather than inventing a solely factual-taint test. Liteky’s distinction between ordinary judicial rulings and disqualifying extrajudicial conduct reinforces the violation. Remedial factors are decisive for scope: secret interviews threaten confidence, but need not erase independently reviewable determinations. Reassignment and a fresh remedy are required; retained liability must be reconsidered wherever meaningful independence cannot be established.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The effect of a particular disputed liability finding remains subject to the directed remand inquiry.
