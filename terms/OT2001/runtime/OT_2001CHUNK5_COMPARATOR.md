## 65. Verizon Communications Inc. v. FCC

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed in part, reversed in part, and remanded. The cost-methodology portion commanded seven votes; the additional combination-rule portion commanded six. It is misleading to assign one undifferentiated vote to every proposition.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter wrote. Rehnquist, Stevens, Kennedy, and Ginsburg joined throughout; Scalia and Thomas joined Part III, and Thomas also joined Part IV. Breyer concurred in part and dissented in part; Scalia joined Part VI of his opinion. O’Connor did not participate.
* **Historical Holding:** The FCC could use the challenged forward-looking methodology and impose the bounded compensated combination requirements. The facial takings objection did not establish invalidity.
* **Historical Authority:** The Court applied the Telecommunications Act, Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, and the historical AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion and separate opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/467/).

## 67. Lapides v. Board of Regents of University System of Georgia

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded unanimously, 9 to 0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Breyer wrote for the Court.
* **Historical Holding:** Voluntary removal waived the State’s federal-forum immunity for the state-law claims on which it had waived immunity in state court. The Court expressly confined its decision to that setting and overruled Ford Motor Co. v. Department of Treasury to the extent inconsistent.
* **Historical Authority:** The Court distinguished constructive waiver and recognized the State-directed federal civil-rights claim’s statutory defect. Its historical sovereign-immunity background is not the simulation’s different Article I law.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/613/).

## 69. United States v. Cotton

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded unanimously, 9 to 0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote for the Court.
* **Historical Holding:** Indictment defects did not deprive the district court of jurisdiction; Ex parte Bain was overruled to the extent it held otherwise. Assuming substantial-rights prejudice, overwhelming and uncontroverted quantity evidence defeated correction under the fourth plain-error requirement.
* **Historical Authority:** United States v. Olano, Johnson v. United States, and Apprendi v. New Jersey supplied the principal framework.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/625/).

## 71. Alabama v. Shelton

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 5 to 4.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Ginsburg wrote for Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia dissented, joined by Rehnquist, Kennedy, and Thomas.
* **Historical Holding:** A suspended sentence that may result in deprivation of liberty may not be imposed without counsel or valid waiver at the trial of the underlying offense. Ordinary revocation proceedings do not repair that absence. The Court did not resolve freestanding probation.
* **Historical Authority:** Argersinger v. Hamlin, Scott v. Illinois, Nichols v. United States, and Gagnon v. Scarpelli were central.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion and dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/654/).

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 8 to 1.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Rehnquist wrote for the Court. Stevens dissented.

## 75. Festo Corp. v. Shoketsu Kinzoku Kogyo Kabushiki Co.

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Vacated and remanded unanimously, 9 to 0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy wrote for the Court.
* **Historical Holding:** Narrowing amendments made to satisfy any Patent Act requirement can trigger estoppel. ## 77. Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Ginsburg wrote for the Court. Scalia dissented.
