# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-02-26, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for reinstatement of the employment-discrimination claims under ordinary Rule 8 pleading.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Vacate the complaint’s dismissal. Permit appropriate Rule 11 enforcement, proportionate discovery, and later Rule 56 review; award no damages, reinstatement, or finding of discrimination.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Pleading and evidentiary frameworks

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The complaint and the dismissal ground permit resolution of the legal pleading requirement without factual findings about motive.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an evidentiary prima facie case is a necessary component of this discrimination complaint.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing Federal Rules reasoning and extend its source-based principle to the specific employment pleading requirement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A Title VII or ADEA complaint need not plead every element of the McDonnell Douglas evidentiary framework. A short and plain account identifying the employment decisions, their alleged prohibited basis, and the grounds of the claim satisfies Rule 8 when it gives fair notice; this complaint does so.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the asserted Title VII and ADEA claims, without reducing the eventual requirement of unlawful discrimination or governing a separately specified Rule 9 claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The prima facie framework organizes proof where direct evidence is absent. Making it indispensable at filing can demand facts that discovery is meant to test and require more than a claimant ultimately needs when direct evidence exists. The Rules already allocate pleading, discovery, and proof. Litigation expense is addressed through those powers rather than an added judicial pleading code. Rule 8 and Leatherman’s refusal to judicially invent heightened pleading carry decisive weight. McDonnell Douglas is reaffirmed as a conditional burden-shifting proof framework, not transposed into universal complaint elements; direct proof may make its inferential sequence unnecessary. Applying that distinction to the actual complaint gives a complete pleading answer. No general new evidentiary presumption or later plausibility doctrine is introduced, and proof of discrimination remains for the ordinary proceedings.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical exemption for individualized excessive-force claims and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply §1997e(a) to this prison-life claim. On the established failure to use an available grievance process, require dismissal without prejudice; an actually preserved and supported availability objection must be evaluated on its facts. No beating liability or damages is decided.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The scope of prison-conditions exhaustion

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The action’s subject and admitted absence of a grievance present the statutory question; the Court need not decide the alleged assault.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an individual excessive-force incident is an action with respect to prison conditions within §1997e(a).
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Reaffirm Booth’s actual-availability and no-damages-futility rule; resolve its reserved excessive-force coverage question from §1997e(a)’s text.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1997e(a) encompasses federal claims about prison life, including a single episode of excessive force. A covered prisoner must exhaust remedies actually available before filing, regardless of whether the wrong affects all prisoners or only one.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers individual and systemic prison-life claims. It does not convert a challenge to the fact or duration of custody into this statutory category, or deem an obstructed process available.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The broad phrase addresses the relationship between the suit and confinement, while the statute separately specifies covered plaintiffs and sources of federal claims. An episode-based exception has no stable textual boundary. Administrative review may investigate, preserve evidence, or change conduct even when it cannot award the exact judicial relief sought, as Booth now establishes for an actually available responsive process. The comprehensive language concerning prison life carries decisive weight, reinforced by Booth’s statutory-primacy reasoning. Distinctions between force and ordinary conditions for Eighth Amendment culpability do not transfer into this differently worded exhaustion provision. This is a category-wide statutory answer because the claimed episode exception would otherwise govern every such suit. Availability remains an enacted condition, so a process actually obstructed or incapable of responsive action is not deemed available by label; no free-standing equitable futility exception is added.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific obstruction, grievance timeliness, and a newly asserted futility theory require preserved facts not established here.

## 35. Raygor v. Regents of University of Minnesota

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-02-27, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical exclusion from §1367(d) and remand the state action.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Require the Minnesota court to apply the enacted tolling protection to related state-law claims timely asserted with a valid federal ADEA controversy, verify the actual periods, and adjudicate remaining defenses. The separate federal judgment is not reopened.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Supplemental tolling and a State’s immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state limitations judgment presents an independent federal question. The Court may resolve tolling while preserving the finality of the earlier federal case.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress’s reference to a claim asserted under subsection (a) includes a related claim dismissed because the State did not consent to its adjudication in federal court.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Kimel to reject the historical jurisdictional premise; propose a construction of §1367(d) that does not equate tolling with abrogation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1367(d) preserves a related state-law claim timely asserted in a federal action supported by original jurisdiction even when sovereign immunity requires dismissal of that supplemental claim. The provision does not itself authorize a federal merits judgment against the State; it preserves the time for a state action that the State otherwise permits.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the tolling of properly related claims and the specified postdismissal interval. It creates no new state cause of action, waiver of a substantive defense, or route around the State’s independent immunity in an otherwise unauthorized forum.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The enacted term concerns assertion of a claim and the protection against losing it while the federal tribunal determines its authority. Dismissal on immunity grounds does not mean that the related claim was never asserted. Congress’s Article III implementing authority, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, permits a reasonable procedural protection against forfeiture caused by presenting related claims in an authorized federal action. The State’s distinct power to insist on its own authorized forum survives. The constitutional and statutory clarity rules for a treasury judgment do not answer this different procedural measure. Text and federal procedural structure operate together, with the wording asserted under subsection (a) decisive. Kimel is reaffirmed for the lawful federal ADEA channel; the abandoned federal judgment is not reopened. Tolling preserves time in a state forum the State otherwise permits, while abrogation would authorize a merits judgment against a nonconsenting sovereign. Because these are different legal effects, the clear statement required for treasury liability cannot be imported to defeat the specific tolling instruction. The replacement construction governs that procedural effect and leaves independent state immunity and valid claim defenses intact.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The exact state filing arithmetic and any independent finality defense depend on the record below; no new federal reopening theory is presented.

## 36. Guest v. Leis +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-02-27, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment insofar as it rejects the preserved Fourth Amendment subscriber-record claims through categorical loss of privacy or grants the individual defendants qualified immunity on that component without the authoritative-reliance showing required below. Remand those claims for application of the stated privacy, warrant, and personal-defense rules. Leave the remaining judgment undisturbed. This changes the rule governing personal damages; it does not adjudge a search unlawful or find any defendant liable.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** First determine, under ordinary summary-judgment rules, whether supported evidence establishes an intrusion into a claimant’s own confidential subscriber records and whether that intrusion was authorized by a constitutionally sufficient, offense-limited warrant. The upheld hardware seizure and warrant particularity do not themselves answer the subscriber-access question the lower court declined to reach. If the claimant cannot establish an actionable violation, enter judgment for the relevant defendant without a reliance inquiry. If a violation is established, give the individual defendant an ordinary opportunity to identify and prove the authoritative permission, genuine reliance, and reasonable applicability specified below; legal uncertainty alone cannot sustain judgment. Resolve legal questions and undisputed facts judicially, and submit only genuinely material factual disputes to the lawful factfinder. Any surviving official-capacity or institutional claim separately requires a suable defendant, an applicable source of liability, the required policy or institutional connection, and causation. No new public defendant, cause of action, automatic trial, damages award, or prospective relief is ordered. The independent statutory dispositions and their defenses remain undisturbed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Entrusted identifying information

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The categorical third-party rule disposes of the subscriber-record claim and prevents examination of its remaining elements. The supplied question therefore requires deciding privacy, while each claimant must still prove a personal intrusion rather than rely on another person’s computer ownership or conjecture that messages were read.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether users lose all reasonable expectation of privacy in identifying subscriber information merely because they entrust it to a bulletin-board operator for limited account administration.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Narrow the categorical extension of Miller and Smith to confidential subscriber credentials and identifying records entrusted for limited account administration. This is a new constitutional application, not a claim that the precise privacy rule was already settled in 1995.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Limited entrustment of nonpublic subscriber information does not by itself extinguish a reasonable expectation of privacy. A user retains protection in confidential credentials and identifying records maintained for a restricted account when the user actually expects that confidentiality and the account’s terms and practices make that expectation reasonable. Publicly exposed information receives no protection from this holding. Miller and Smith remain effective in their decided settings, but their general third-party rationale does not automatically dispose of these confidential account records.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the claimant’s own qualifying subscriber information. It creates no possessory interest in an operator’s hardware and no immunity from a lawful evidence search. A warrant need not rest on suspicion that the subscriber personally committed a crime; it must constitutionally authorize the evidence search actually conducted. A notice allowing the operator and assigns to read messages is not, without more, proof that account credentials and identifying records were exposed to the public. The separate message claims remain outside this disposition.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A password enables restricted access; giving it to the operator for that purpose does not invite unlimited governmental examination. Katz’s protection of reasonable confidentiality supplies the decisive principle, and the function of account administration explains the material distinction from public exposure. This combination preserves the constitutional privacy inquiry while rejecting an automatic rule based solely on a third party’s custody. The warrants here limited their reach to the identified offenses. Their valid authorization to seize a system for an off-site search does not license unrestricted examination of every confidential record on it. Conversely, recognizing privacy does not invalidate an authorized offense-related search: Zurcher forecloses a requirement that every affected record holder be a criminal suspect. Because the lower court used its no-privacy conclusion to avoid the subscriber-specific warrant inquiry, that application requires remand. The legal rule applies to these pending claims; it is not merely a prospective observation. Its newness is addressed through the reliance defense below, not by treating confidentiality as unprotected until a factually identical case appears.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The record must establish what subscriber information each claimant kept confidential, what the officers actually examined, and whether the warrant lawfully authorized that examination. Those are the remaining applications, not open legal standards. Broader data aggregation, the separate statutory claims, and private-message reading unsupported by the existing proof are outside the supplied review.

### Issue 2: Personal protection for reliance on authoritative permission

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Personal qualified immunity is a preserved defense to the subscriber-record damages claim and an asserted alternative basis for affirmance. It must be resolved under an identified rule rather than assumed to follow from the privacy question’s novelty. The individual claims against the sheriff and deputies are distinct from the official-capacity and institutional claims.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an official who committed an actionable unconstitutional search receives personal protection merely because the particular application was unsettled, or must establish reasonable, actual reliance on authoritative legal permission for the challenged conduct.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Propose replacing Harlow and Anderson’s clearly-established-law defense, for the constitutional personal-damages defense at issue here, with the bounded authoritative-reliance defense stated below. Expressly overrule their contrary proposition that insufficiently clear law alone excuses an otherwise actionable unconstitutional search. Disapprove Wilson’s contrary immunity rationale to the same extent, while retaining its separation of the violation from personal relief. Elder’s independent consideration of relevant law remains, now directed to the asserted permission and its limits. Saucier’s entering-law remand retained the existing defense and did not adopt this replacement; this position proposes that change openly rather than redescribing the prior judgment.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** First apply the complete substantive constitutional rule, including the reasonable factual mistakes and circumstances that rule recognizes. At summary judgment, use the claimant’s supported account of genuinely disputed facts; no merits trial is required before a separable legal defense can be resolved. If that inquiry establishes a violation, an individual official has a distinct defense only by carrying the burden of identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct, proving genuine reliance on it, and establishing a reasonable basis for treating it as valid and applicable to the material facts. An instruction accurately conveying the identified authority may establish reliance. Legal silence, an unsettled question, general permission to investigate, departmental practice, or a supervisor’s or prosecutor’s assurance supplies no independent defense. The defense protects reliance on authoritative permission whose invalidity or inapplicability the official could not reasonably recognize, including an unexpected change in controlling law. It does not protect disregard of the authority’s limits, apparent unlawfulness under controlling constitutional principles, or a factual mistake already rejected under the merits rule. Knowing material falsification, concealment of disqualifying facts, or manufactured authority defeats reasonable reliance on the resulting permission.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This is a rule for personal constitutional damages, applied to the preserved §1983 search claims and intended to govern the same defense in future cases. It does not change the plaintiff’s burden to establish an actionable deprivation, personal responsibility, causation, and recoverable injury, create an institutional remedy, displace an independently applicable statutory defense, or abolish a separate absolute immunity. It does not reopen final judgments. The absence of a prior case with identical facts is not a defense, and a precedent granting immunity without authorizing the underlying conduct is not itself permission to conduct the search.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 1983 supplies a remedy for a proven deprivation under color of state law; its text does not condition that remedy on whether the particular constitutional application had previously been settled. Harlow’s concern for official decisiveness deserves protection where an official actually follows authoritative law, but its broader uncertainty defense can excuse a proven unreasonable search even when no law authorized it. That is the defect requiring revision, not an assertion that existing law demands an identical prior case. The merits inquiry already respects reasonable factual perceptions; repeating those allowances after finding a violation supplies an additional escape unrelated to distinct legal reliance. The enacted remedial commitment carries decisive weight, while workability and concrete reliance shape the limited defense. Requiring actual reliance adds a factual inquiry, but one directed to identified legal authority, instructions, and conduct rather than a general investigation of official motives; ordinary pleading and summary-judgment rules remain available. Here Miller, Smith, or applicable enacted authority may be invoked, but the officers must show what directly authorized the particular access and how they genuinely and reasonably relied on it. Their later litigation citations alone cannot prove that fact. The later subscriber-information decisions cannot supply permission they relied on in June or August 1995. A valid warrant may defeat the constitutional claim on its merits; its mere issuance does not prove this distinct defense, and prosecutor consultation alone does not establish accurate transmission of controlling authority. The disputed assertion that advice was disregarded establishes neither reliance nor its absence. The present record therefore supports neither automatic immunity nor automatic liability under the replacement rule. Remand permits the prescribed showing without treating a newly announced burden as a fact already lost below.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Which official relied on which qualifying authority, whether any instruction accurately conveyed it, and whether the proved conduct stayed within its reasonably understood limits require the relevant record. Those factual questions do not reserve the governing defense or its burden. The separate statutory claims are outside this review; their adjudicated defenses are not converted into a finding that every constitutional search or individual defense was lawful.

## 37. New York v. FERC

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-03-04, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm in both consolidated dockets, on the distinct grounds stated below.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the reviewed open-access requirements in force and deny an order compelling additional unbundling in this proceeding. No tariff, damages award, or state retail rate is judicially prescribed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Authority over unbundled retail transmission

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The State’s statutory challenge directly concerns an operative portion of the order. Resolve the statutory allocation without an independent constitutional ruling.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether interstate transmission separately sold for retail use falls within the federal transmission grant.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing delegation principles to a statutory category not previously resolved by the prior decisions.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The Federal Power Act’s grant over interstate transmission includes unbundled transmission serving retail purchasers. The wholesale limitation on the separate sales grant does not narrow the transmission grant. FERC may apply its lawful open-access remedy to that transmission; local distribution and retail sales remain governed by their own statutory provisions.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers interstate transmission actually within the federal grant, not every facility used somewhere in delivering electricity or all retail pricing.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory distinction does the work. Reading the wholesale modifier across the separate transmission category would insert a restriction Congress did not enact. The evolution of a connected grid supplies the relevant facts about interstate transmission; it supplies no new power independent of the text. Text and allocation of statutory categories carry decisive weight; the interstate grid’s operation identifies the covered facts without enlarging the grant. Iowa Utilities Board is applied for respecting the actual federal assignment while preserving state subjects expressly left outside it. Mead’s delegation inquiry is compatible with this independent conclusion and does not authorize an agency to create jurisdiction. The holding answers unbundled interstate transmission without absorbing local distribution or retail price regulation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: Scope of the bundled-service remedy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Enron’s challenge is reviewable, but the Court need decide only whether the statute and findings require the additional remedy sought.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this §206 proceeding compelled immediate unbundling of all bundled retail transmission.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply reasoned statutory administration rather than prescribe a regulatory program.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** FERC could address the established transmission-access discrimination through the requirements adopted while leaving compulsory unbundling of bundled retail service for a separately justified proceeding. Neither the identified statutory duty nor this record makes Enron’s additional remedy mandatory.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion concerns the reasoned scope of this order. It does not establish a permanent exemption or determine every jurisdictional question that a later bundled-service rule could present.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Authority, a statutory trigger for correction, and the necessary extent of correction are separate inquiries. Enron must show why the findings require this additional intervention. A court may require the agency to answer that question, but should not convert a defensible concern about remaining discrimination into its own regulatory command. Reasoned administration within the statutory remedial authority is decisive. Stone accepts the agency’s choice to remedy the proved wholesale-access discrimination without an immediate compulsory restructuring of every bundled retail transaction; neither Enron’s preferred remedy nor an agency’s abstract possession of power makes that remedy mandatory. The order’s findings and explanation are evaluated under the actual review standard. This is a bounded remedial holding, not a declaration of permanent immunity for bundled service.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A universal allocation of bundled-service jurisdiction beyond the authority needed for this order falls outside the particular remedial demand. The claim that this proceeding compelled additional unbundling is fully resolved.

## 38. Young v. United States

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment that this tax debt was not discharged.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Exclude the prior bankruptcy stay from the three-year calculation and permit collection of the nondischarged liability through otherwise lawful procedures. This judgment neither recalculates the tax nor imposes a sanction for the filings.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Tolling during a prior bankruptcy stay

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The petitioners seek a discharge ruling in a reopened case. The stipulated chronology and legal restraint allow resolution without a finding of bad faith.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the tax lookback runs while a prior bankruptcy stay disables the creditor from collecting the covered liability.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing text-sensitive approach to tolling; propose its specific application to this statutory period. Do not revise Brockamp or bankruptcy priority rules.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The three-year tax-priority lookback excludes the period during which a prior bankruptcy petition imposed an automatic stay preventing collection of the same liability. The suspension preserves the statutory collection interval rather than changing the tax claim’s priority by judicial preference. It applies without proof that the prior petition was abusive.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs a prior stay legally barring collection of the liability at issue. It does not authorize extensions for every creditor delay, every successive petition, or every deadline in the Code.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Congress legislated both the collection protection and the temporary prohibition. Reading the stay to consume the protected interval without opportunity to act would turn one statutory protection into the means of defeating the other. The nearby express provisions address different timing mechanisms and do not establish that this traditional suspension is forbidden. Brockamp’s detailed refund structure supplies a contrast requiring analysis, not a categorical rule that every tax-related period is untollable. The background suspension principle is applied through the bankruptcy scheme, with the stay’s legal prevention of collection decisive. Stone distinguishes Brockamp’s detailed refund limitation, rather than announcing a general exception for government creditors, and reaffirms Noland’s rejection of free-standing judicial rearrangement of statutory priority. No finding of bad faith is necessary because the legal disability existed regardless of motive. The rule is stated for this lookback-and-stay interaction, leaving other deadlines to their own text and structure.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 39. United States v. Vonn

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-03-04, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the appellate judgment and remand for application of Rule 52(b) to the relevant full record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The court of appeals must assess each required plain-error element, including whether the omission affected substantial rights and whether correction is justified. Do not conclusively reinstate the convictions or order new pleas without that application.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Unpreserved plea error and the relevant record

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The Rule 11 objection was first presented on appeal; the alternative withdrawal motion did not preserve this distinct omission. Reach the applicable review rule and evidentiary scope.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an omitted trial-counsel warning receives ordinary forfeiture review and whether prior judicial advice is relevant to the defendant’s substantial-rights showing.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Olano to Rule 11; reject the proposed special exception. Clarify the permitted record without changing the judge’s plea-taking obligation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A defendant who forfeits a Rule 11 objection must satisfy Rule 52(b). The reviewing court may consider relevant information throughout the existing record when determining the omission’s effect, including prior rights advisements. Rule 11(h) does not silently displace Rule 52(b). The omission remains an error; evidence of actual understanding bears on its consequences.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs direct review of forfeited Rule 11 errors. It does not convert intentional waiver into reviewable forfeiture, decide a collateral claim, or establish that every type of constitutional plea defect has the same consequences.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The general rule and the plea rule can operate together. A special harmlessness instruction protects against automatic reversal but says nothing sufficient to exempt silent litigants from the otherwise applicable allocation of burdens. Full-record review addresses actual effect more accurately than a transcript boundary that ignores known relevant advice. Relevance is not conclusiveness: timing, content, and demonstrated comprehension still matter. The procedural rules’ text and Olano’s allocation of burdens are decisive; record-wide consideration is a functional consequence of testing actual effect under that rule. Stone reaffirms the difference between harmless error preserved below and plain error forfeited below, rather than converting his position on ordinary constitutional harmlessness into an exception for Rule 11 omissions. The court must determine error, plainness, effect on substantial rights, and the separate basis for discretionary correction. Prior advice is relevant evidence of understanding, not a conclusive presumption, and the judge’s plea colloquy duty remains mandatory.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The record-specific substantial-rights and discretionary findings belong on remand because the appellate court applied a different framework; the trial-court and appellate functions remain distinct.

## 40. Delaney v. DeTella +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-03-04, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of qualified immunity on the assumed favorable facts, with factual sufficiency disputes outside the interlocutory review.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Return the case for proof of each defendant’s knowledge, response, causation, and compensable injury. Do not award damages or establish that every defendant participated merely because the common policy existed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Conditions of confinement

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The merits question can be resolved as a legal sufficiency issue on the favorable facts; those facts must not be converted into findings.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether knowing and unjustified maintenance of this prolonged near-total exercise deprivation is actionable punishment.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Farmer to the particular conditions, without creating strict liability or a fixed nationwide exercise entitlement.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A prolonged denial of meaningful exercise to a prisoner confined in a space inadequate for it violates the Eighth Amendment when it creates a substantial health risk that the responsible official actually appreciates and unreasonably disregards. The assumed six-month deprivation and complaints suffice to require adjudication under that rule; a lockdown label does not itself supply an adequate response.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule reaches responsible officials with actual knowledge and an unreasonable response. Short restrictions, genuine unavoidable security necessities, and reasonable alternative exercise or protective measures require their own facts.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Duration and confinement make this more than inconvenience. Farmer permits the inference that repeated complaints and the obvious conditions disclosed a serious risk, while allowing defendants to contest knowledge and responsibility at trial. The constitutional inquiry evaluates the actual response to the known risk, not compliance with a state regulation alone. Farmer’s two requirements are decisive: a serious health risk and knowing unreasonable disregard, proved through direct or circumstantial evidence. Lockdown necessity is relevant to the response; it is not a categorical exception authorizing six months of complete deprivation. Stone applies the existing culpability standard rather than imposing strict liability, weekly-hour minima, or a universally least-restrictive prison regimen. The assumed facts permit a legal sufficiency determination; disputed knowledge and each official’s responsibility remain for the factfinder.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

### Issue 2: Clearly established law and personal immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The officials ask for final protection from damages on the assumed facts, so conduct-date clarity must be resolved separately.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether reasonable officials in 1996 had fair notice of the prohibition in the circumstances assumed.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing objective immunity framework retained in Saucier, using Farmer and pertinent law predating the spring 1996 conduct. No later decision supplies retrospective notice.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** On facts establishing knowledge of substantial health danger from a six-month denial of meaningful exercise without a reasonable security response, the existing law gave sufficient notice. Qualified immunity therefore does not require summary judgment for these officials on that assumed record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion is limited by the facts necessary to establish notice and responsibility. It does not deny immunity for materially different conditions or for an official lacking the knowledge or authority required for liability.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The right must be described concretely, but officials need not receive a prior judgment involving the identical cell dimensions and number of days. The preconduct cases and Farmer identify both the protected need and the culpable disregard. Facts disproving those predicates may defeat liability independently of immunity. French and Davenport gave particular notice before the challenged lockdown, including Davenport’s treatment of this segregation unit; an identical cell-and-duration case is unnecessary. Stone reaffirms the existing notice framework for this disposition because it resolves the claim without reopening the broader immunity doctrine. His proposed authoritative-reliance replacement has not become law through Saucier and is unnecessary to deny immunity on these assumed facts. The holding guides analogous complete deprivations while preserving genuinely different security needs and available exercise alternatives.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual disputed knowledge and role of each officer require fact-finding and are reserved on that ground.

## 41. Ruiz v. United States (Ruiz v. Johnson) +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the appellate judgment requiring application of the PLRA and remand for adequate findings; reject automatic wholesale termination.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The district court must identify each live federal violation and connect each retained obligation to need, narrowness and intrusiveness, resolving the preserved cross-appeal issues. Retain or tailor relief satisfying §3626(b)(3), and terminate obligations that do not.
