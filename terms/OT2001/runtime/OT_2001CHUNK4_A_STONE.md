## 49. Mickens v. Taylor

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm denial of habeas relief on the reviewed automatic-relief theory.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the conviction and sentence undisturbed on this claim. No new trial or additional conflict hearing is ordered solely because the appointing judge failed to inquire; the existing no-adverse-effect determination is not independently set aside within this question.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Judicial inquiry and conflict-related prejudice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The circuit’s cause ruling permits consideration of the question it decided, while prejudice remains tied to constitutional entitlement. Reach the asserted exemption from an adverse-effect showing, not every underlying factual dispute.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a judge’s failure to investigate a known potential conflict itself entitles the defendant to reversal without showing that divided loyalties affected the lawyer’s work.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the distinction between potential conflict, actual impairment, and the established exceptions. Reject a new automatic rule based solely on the neglected inquiry.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Outside the established circumstances requiring automatic relief, a failure to inquire into a potential conflict does not by itself establish a Sixth Amendment violation. A defendant invoking the conflict-based relaxation of ordinary outcome prejudice must at least show that competing obligations actually affected counsel’s representation. The undisclosed prior representation of the victim is serious and requires inquiry, but its existence plus judicial omission does not dispense with that showing.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs the proposed automatic consequence of the inquiry failure. It preserves Holloway’s rule for compelled joint representation over an adequate objection and does not treat all prior-client conflicts as harmless or all ethical breaches as constitutional violations.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Holloway's automatic rule protects against compelled joint representation over an adequate objection; Cuyler's adverse-effect rule addresses an established conflict affecting representation. Stone reaffirms that distinction. The judge's serious failure to investigate is not proof that the lawyer withheld an available defense, so the Sixth Amendment's protection of loyal representation carries decisive weight over an automatic sanction for a neglected judicial procedure. Confidentiality survives the victim's death under Swidler, making the potential conflict real, but that does not identify a resulting impairment. The granted question concerns dispensing with adverse effect; the separate factual challenge to the adverse-effect finding was not granted. On that limited question, the existing finding defeats relief even assuming Sullivan governs successive representation. No new categorical rule for all successive conflicts is needed, and no harsher prejudice requirement is added.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether every category of successive representation receives Sullivan’s relaxed prejudice rule lies beyond the reviewed question about the effect of a judge’s failure to inquire. This claim fails the minimum adverse-effect requirement even assuming that rule applies. Separate challenges to the factual finding are also outside the reviewed question.

## 50. Barnhart v. Walton

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit’s statutory ruling and restore the denial on the reviewed eligibility grounds.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply the lawful duration and predecision trial-work rules to Walton’s established return to substantial gainful activity. No benefits award or new medical fact-finding is ordered on the legal theory rejected here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Duration and trial work

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Both grounds supporting the circuit’s reversal concern initial eligibility and must be resolved. The established duration of substantial inability is the premise for legal review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the agency may require twelve months of disabling inability and treat actual earlier return to work before determination as defeating the projected-duration and trial-work theories.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply existing statutory-delegation principles and adopt the linked reading of the disability definition. Do not propose a general replacement for Chevron or Auer.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The agency may construe the definition to require that inability to engage in substantial gainful activity, caused by the impairment, satisfy the twelve-month duration requirement. When substantial work actually resumes within that period before a favorable disability determination, the agency may use that fact rather than the superseded prediction. A trial-work provision for persons entitled to benefits does not independently create initial entitlement despite failure of that requirement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the actual predecision return and eligibility sequence presented. It does not withdraw an already established trial-work entitlement or classify a medically distinct unsuccessful work attempt.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The integrated disability definition is decisive: the lasting condition must disable the claimant from substantial gainful activity, rather than consist merely of a continuing medical diagnosis after disabling inability has ended. The expectation language permits timely decisions before duration is known; it does not require ignoring an actual return to substantial work before entitlement is determined. The trial-work provision protects an otherwise entitled beneficiary and does not independently establish that entitlement. Stone combines text and scheme with the agency's longstanding, expert administration, assigning the agency's reasons substantial persuasive weight because they fit those enacted distinctions. Under Mead, delegated force-of-law authority must be identified rather than inferred from expertise alone; the SSA's statutory rulemaking assignment and operative regulations provide it here. The interpretation also survives independent reading, so a wholesale reconsideration of Chevron or regulatory deference is unnecessary. The return-to-work and entitlement facts already resolve Walton's two legal theories.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different rules for unsuccessful attempts, existing awards, or clearly contrary prior guidance require facts and claims outside this question.

## 51. Steele v. Hamilton County Community Mental Health Board +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before March 27, 2002.
* **Conditions:** The disposition below is definite on the present record. It does not depend on a later assumption of recovery, continued confinement, renewed refusal or treatment authorization.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss the writ of certiorari as improvidently granted because the unresolved continuing-controversy facts make this an unsuitable vehicle for the federal medication question. Do not affirm or reverse the federal merits rule, and do not declare the case conclusively moot.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the Ohio Supreme Court’s judgment undisturbed without endorsing its federal reasoning. Order no federal remand, new treatment findings, medication, release, damages or vacatur of a state judgment. Any later request for compelled treatment must proceed under its own lawful authority on the then-existing facts; this dismissal supplies no authorization and changes no substantive treatment rule.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Dismiss discretionary review without deciding disputed jurisdictional facts or treatment merits

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach only whether to retain this writ. The last established treatment status is voluntary medication, the state judgment withheld immediate treatment authorization, and the record does not establish Steele’s circumstances at the federal event. This nonmerits dismissal does not assume the Article III premise needed for a constitutional treatment holding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this writ should proceed to a federal merits judgment when the state court’s public-interest rationale cannot establish Article III and the patient-specific facts supporting a present controversy or a federal recurrence exception remain unestablished.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the separation between Article III merits authority and discretionary termination of review. Distinguish Harper’s concrete recurrence record and Erie’s demonstrated continuing legal disability. Apply Bancorp’s rejection of automatic vacatur and ASARCO’s separation of state and federal justiciability. Izumi supplies the procedural consequence of an improvidently granted writ, not a claim that jurisdiction must have been included in the granted question. No precedent is overruled and no treatment exception is announced.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** This writ should be dismissed as improvidently granted. Ohio’s independent public-interest rationale does not support a federal merits judgment, and its October 2000 account does not establish the patient’s present exposure, a continuing coercive authorization, or sufficiently supported recurrence and evasion of review at the federal event. That unresolved vehicle problem warrants ending discretionary review without deciding the substantive medication question or making a definitive finding of mootness. The state judgment remains in place.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The disposition concerns the suitability of this writ on this record. It establishes no categorical mootness rule for voluntary medication, no restriction on Ohio’s own public-interest jurisdiction, and no federal approval of forced medication for Steele or other patients.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A bodily-integrity question cannot be answered by assuming the personal controversy necessary to decide it. Ohio reported voluntary treatment and declined a remand for the remaining medication findings. Its observation that the record contained no evidence of recovery or release cannot be converted into a finding of continued custody or likely renewed refusal seventeen months later. Harper had an actual history of repeated transfers and continuing incarceration; the comparable patient-specific facts and the duration premise are not established here. Nor does a precedent potentially governing a future treatment application itself prove a present coercive order or collateral injury. Laidlaw’s defendant-cessation burden does not automatically attach to Steele’s decision to take medication. A limited inquiry can clarify an identified live dispute, but the current record identifies no operative authorization to preserve and supplies no factual basis for selecting a jurisdictional-fact remand over ending discretionary review. The Court need not order Ohio to develop facts for federal review of a judgment Ohio could enter under its own justiciability rules. The decisive ground is this unresolved threshold and unsuitable vehicle, not a balance of medical benefit against refusal. Dismissal therefore decides no more than the record permits. Neither a federal jurisdictional error in the state proceedings nor an equitable basis for vacatur has been established, so the state judgment is left undisturbed rather than automatically erased.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The nonemergency substantive medication question is not reached because facts necessary to establish a continuing federal controversy are missing. Emergency powers and the unpreserved procedural due-process challenge also remain outside this disposition. The dismissal does not reject the possibility of justified substitute treatment for a patient actually unable to decide, but announces no merits rule or conditional medication judgment.

## 52. Adams v. Florida Power Corp.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical no-disparate-impact ruling and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the employees to pursue an adequately identified and supported impact claim under the ADEA, subject to the reasonable-factor exception and ordinary procedural requirements. Do not restore a class, find liability, or award backpay merely from recognition of the theory.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: ADEA disparate impact

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The certified legal bar prevents litigation of the theory and is properly reviewable. Resolve availability while respecting the district court’s express absence of factual findings about sufficiency.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether §623(a)(2), read with §623(f)(1), permits a claim based on a neutral employment practice’s adverse age effects without proof of age animus.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Decide the statutory question expressly reserved in Hazen Paper. This is a proposed new holding, not an application of an already settled impact rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** The ADEA permits a disparate-impact claim against a specifically identified neutral employment practice that causes an adverse effect on protected employees because of age. The claimant must identify the practice and establish the causal age effect; an aggregate workforce disparity alone is insufficient. The statutory permission for reasonable factors other than age remains an independent limit, so a proven reasonable nonage basis may justify the differentiation without a showing that the practice was the only possible business choice.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the effects-oriented employment provision and preserves intentional-discrimination liability separately. It neither treats every correlation with age as actionable nor imports Title VII’s amended business-necessity scheme wholesale.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The effects-oriented words of §623(a)(2), coupled with the reasonable-factors-other-than-age provision, are decisive. Hazen Paper expressly reserved disparate impact, so recognizing the claim extends the statute into an open application rather than overruling its intentional-discrimination holding. Congress protected legitimate nonage criteria through §623(f)(1); deleting effects liability altogether would deprive that textual arrangement of much of its function. Stone requires an identified practice and proof that it causes the challenged age effect, preserving reasonable nonage differentiation without importing Title VII's later amendments. The interlocutory certification presents availability of the theory, not the sufficiency of these employees' statistics, class allegations or identified practice. Those matters remain for the district court under the stated rule. The historical dismissal supplies no legal reason to evade a properly preserved statutory question in a live appeal.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The certified question is availability of disparate-impact liability. Whether the employees have identified a specific practice and established causation, whether the employer proves a reasonable factor other than age, and the class and remedial consequences require lower-court adjudication on the actual record; those unresolved applications do not alter the answer to availability.

## 53. Sao Paulo State of the Federative Republic of Brazil v. American Tobacco Co.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved; renewed 2026-11-10 by express user approval of the corrected-record version.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the recusal judgment and remand for ordinary further proceedings.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remove the requirement of disqualification on this asserted ground. Resolve no tobacco liability, damages, or separate procedural issue, and do not immunize any genuinely different recusal basis.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: An informed observer and mistakenly attributed advocacy

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The reviewed recusal ruling rests on an attributed organizational role whose correction is part of the record. The statutory question is therefore ready for application without reaching constitutional judicial-bias doctrine.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a fully informed observer would reasonably doubt impartiality because of this mistaken appearance on the filing, considered together with her continuing executive-committee membership in the filing association, her lack of any part in preparing or approving the submission, and her only vague awareness of it.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Liteky directly. No doctrinal change or heightened requirement of actual bias is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 455(a) does not require recusal on the basis of a mistaken, unauthorized attribution of advocacy together with the judge's continuing executive-committee membership in the filing association, when the informed observer also knows that she neither prepared nor approved the submission and had only vague awareness of it. Her genuine committee role receives weight, but on these combined facts it does not support a reasonable doubt about her impartiality. Those facts must be included in the objective appearance inquiry; they cannot be excluded to preserve the initial misleading impression.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs the specified mistaken association. Actual advocacy, responsibility for or participation in the organization's position, financial interest, or other supported relationships remain subject to the same appearance standard.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 455(a), applied through Liteky's informed-observer standard, supplies the complete answer. Liljeberg likewise requires consideration of all relevant circumstances; neither permits an observer to disregard established facts correcting the supposed association. Here the judge did not hold the attributed office, prepare the submission or approve it; her continuing executive-committee membership is a genuine affiliation that the informed observer weighs, but with no participation and only vague awareness it does not reasonably identify her with this filing. Including those facts is objective application, not acceptance of a subjective assurance of impartiality. Stone reaffirms the appearance standard without requiring proof of actual bias. Its contextual character is necessary because an erroneous name on a form and actual participation in adverse advocacy create different appearances. The statute decides the matter, leaving no reason to announce a constitutional judicial-bias rule or a general exemption for organizational affiliations.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** None.

## 54. Venetian Casino Resort, L.L.C. v. Local Joint Executive Board of Las Vegas +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the public-forum judgment for the replacement boulevard sidewalk.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Deny the casino’s requested blanket exclusion of the peaceful demonstration on this public route. Preserve lawful content-neutral measures addressing actual obstruction, safety and access; extend no order to the casino’s interior or unrelated private property.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Government cannot privatize the label while retaining the public street

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The public-passage obligation and replacement of the former municipal route establish the necessary attribution before any scrutiny of exclusion.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does this particular replacement sidewalk retain traditional public-forum protection?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the public-thoroughfare principle of Marsh and Grace; distinguish Lloyd and Hudgens’s privately provided commercial space.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A sidewalk deliberately substituted for an existing public street route, held open under a continuing public-passage obligation and integrated into that route remains a traditional public forum for peaceful expression. Private fee ownership does not authorize a general speech exclusion from that passage.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the defined replacement thoroughfare, not every privately owned walkway or every place open to customers. Neutral passage and safety regulations remain available.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The legal arrangement’s public function, history and enforceable passage obligation work together; the decisive fact is dedication to continuing the public route, not the casino’s size or the pavement’s appearance. Government and the owner cannot preserve the public’s compulsory route while transferring an unrestricted speech veto to the owner. The private-title cases are distinguished because this route replaces and continues a public sidewalk rather than furnishing optional interior shopping access. Historical public-forum treatment explains the consequence once the public character is established. Stone states a categorical forum consequence for that defined category, while leaving the validity of particular neutral regulations to their own standards.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No restriction within the casino, unrelated private walkway or specific obstruction sanction is presented.
