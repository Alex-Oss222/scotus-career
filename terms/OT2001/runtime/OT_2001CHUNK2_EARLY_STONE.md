## 17. Kelly v. South Carolina

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-09, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the state judgment insofar as it sustains the death sentence without the required parole-ineligibility instruction; remand for a new penalty proceeding if the State continues to seek death.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the guilt judgment. Require accurate explanation of life without parole when the governing conditions are met. On this presentation, do not treat the omitted information as harmless merely because substantial aggravating evidence existed.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The jury’s actual choice determines the information it needs

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the penalty instruction under Simmons and direct-review harmlessness; do not reopen guilt.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the State can avoid Simmons through a character label or an earlier statutory stage.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Simmons and apply Shafer’s staged-sentencing holding; determine from the full presentation that future danger was placed in issue and that the omission was harmful under Chapman.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** When the State’s evidence or argument materially places future dangerousness in issue, the defendant is entitled to an accurate and effective explanation that the only nondeath sentence the jury may select at the punishment stage is life without parole. Character terminology does not defeat the right when the presentation conveys a prospective threat. A different sentencing possibility before the required aggravating finding does not alter the jury’s later binary choice.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the actual death-selection stage and does not require a particular script or an instruction in every capital proceeding.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Evidence of escape and further violence invites jurors to consider what the defendant may do after conviction. The legal question cannot turn on whether the prosecutor uses a designated phrase. Nor can the State answer the jury’s punishment-stage concern by identifying an option that no longer exists once the necessary finding has been made. Accurate information about the nondeath alternative addresses the premise on which the State seeks death. The record does not permit dismissal of that omission as harmless solely from the strength of aggravation. Simmons and Shafer carry decisive precedential weight. Characterizing evidence as character evidence cannot erase its ordinary prospective implication: escape planning and violence while confined support the very inference the legal parole information answers. On this record, exclusion of that answer leaves reasonable doubt whether the misleading sentencing premise contributed to death; the State therefore fails Chapman. That is a record-specific harmlessness judgment, not structural error or a categorical command for every capital case. Guilt remains undisturbed because the defect concerns the selection of punishment.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Other conviction issues and sentencing arrangements without the Simmons conditions remain outside the ruling.

## 18. Riley v. St. Luke’s Episcopal Hospital +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-09, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the rejection of the Take Care and Appointments Clause challenges and remand for ordinary adjudication of the false-claims action.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the action to proceed subject to the government’s actual statutory powers and the relator’s statutory limitations. Resolve no fraud liability, damages, or future government dismissal motion now.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A limited assigned claim does not create a separate executive office

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply the governing standing holding, then independently decide the two Article II questions.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether nonintervened prosecution under this statutory structure violates executive control or appointment requirements.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Propose a new resolution of constitutional questions expressly left open in Vermont Agency; do not attribute the answer to that earlier case.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A private relator’s prosecution of the False Claims Act’s partially assigned pecuniary claim, within the statute’s retained government intervention, dismissal, settlement, and protective powers, does not violate the Take Care Clause merely because the government initially declines intervention. The relator’s case-specific private litigating role is not a continuing federal office requiring appointment under the Appointments Clause. The conclusion depends on the actual bounded role and statutory controls, not a general power to transfer executive functions to private persons.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding permits this civil enforcement mechanism. It does not authorize private criminal prosecution, rulemaking, or unrestricted exercise of federal coercive powers.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defendants correctly identify consequences for federal interests and litigation policy. The structure must therefore be examined in detail rather than justified solely by historical labels. Here the relator pursues an assigned financial claim through a court, while the government retains identified means of protecting its interests. That differs from giving a private actor a standing governmental office or general executive authority. Nonintervention allows the private litigant to bear the case initially; it does not necessarily terminate the executive’s statutory powers. Neither constitutional clause makes the assignment reasoning irrelevant, but neither is conclusively answered by standing alone. Constitutional structure carries decisive weight, informed by the early qui tam practice rather than displaced by it. Vermont Agency is reaffirmed for assignment-based standing and expressly not extended into a holding it reserved under Article II. The relator holds no continuing statutory office and has no general prosecutorial, rulemaking, or executive power. The retained intervention, dismissal, settlement, and protective controls keep this financial litigation from disabling the Executive’s constitutional responsibility. This resolves both appointment and Take Care objections without importing Morrison’s independent criminal office as the same institution.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Specific disputes over a later dismissal, intervention, settlement, or exercise of an ungranted sovereign power require their own record.

## 20. EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-15, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the restriction on victim-specific relief and remand for adjudication of the EEOC’s statutory action.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the EEOC to seek authorized damages, reinstatement, and other appropriate relief upon proof of the applicable elements. Prevent duplicate satisfaction if an actual overlapping recovery is established, without presuming one or treating the agency as a contracting party.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A private contract does not transfer the agency’s enforcement choice

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the agency’s authority and remedy availability, not the underlying discrimination merits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Baker’s contract restricts remedies in the EEOC’s independent action.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Circuit City’s employment-contract exclusion and independently enforce the EEOC’s own statutory authority. The result requires no revision of arbitration law.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The FAA does not govern this employment contract under Circuit City and therefore supplies no federal policy ground for restricting the EEOC’s relief. Independently, an employee’s private agreement does not bind the nonparty EEOC or eliminate Congress’s victim-specific remedies in its own enforcement action. Liability and relief still require their statutory showings, with actual overlapping satisfaction accounted for when proved.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This resolves the asserted FAA bar and the agency’s independent remedial authority. It does not determine the agreement’s possible state-law enforceability between the employee and employer or the effect of a completed settlement or award absent from the record.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Circuit City controls the antecedent FAA question: Baker’s employment agreement falls outside that Act, so its general arbitration policy cannot override the agency’s enacted remedies. The same result follows from the independent enforcement structure. Title VII’s provisions, incorporated by the ADA, authorize the EEOC to seek injunctive and victim-specific relief in its own action. First Options reinforces that a contract does not bind a nonassenting agency, but the statutory authority is decisive. Compensation serves public enforcement as well as the injured employee; the label victim-specific does not transfer control of the action to Baker. Courts can prevent actual duplicate recovery without stripping authorized remedies in advance. These two sufficient statutory grounds answer the presented question, while the employment agreement’s validity under another source remains outside it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Effects of an actual settlement, release, or completed arbitration outside this record remain reserved.

## 22. In re Cybernetic Services, Inc. +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-15, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm that state-law perfection of the nonownership patent security interest is sufficient against the trustee’s hypothetical lien-creditor claim.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the secured creditors’ priority to the extent established under the applicable state and bankruptcy rules. Do not require an additional federal filing or adjudicate priority against an unpresented actual purchaser.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal patent ownership rules do not occupy every collateral transaction

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the recording issue on the established nonownership transaction and trustee status.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether federal patent recording is an additional perfection prerequisite here.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the statutory coverage and ordinary displacement analysis; adopt a new patent-collateral holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A security interest that does not transfer patent ownership need not be recorded with the Patent and Trademark Office merely to obtain priority over a bankruptcy trustee exercising hypothetical lien-creditor powers, where the interest is otherwise perfected under applicable state commercial law. The Patent Act’s recording provision does not create a comprehensive substitute for that state perfection system. Actual ownership transfers and competing transferee rights require their own statutory analysis.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding does not validate a defective state filing, classify every mortgage or assignment, or decide priority against an actual subsequent purchaser.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The trustee’s national-notice argument addresses a plausible policy, but the enacted scheme must implement it before it displaces state commercial law. The instrument and adversary here fall outside the ownership-transfer contest on which the federal rule focuses. Recognizing state perfection does not alter the patent’s substantive exclusionary rights; it determines a creditor’s interest in the debtor’s asset. Keeping those categories distinct avoids inventing a federal filing obligation from the general federal character of the property. Section 261’s ownership-transfer language and the trustee’s lien-creditor status provide independent textual grounds. Waterman’s legal distinction between a transfer of title and a lesser interest is applied, not overruled; its title-transferring mortgage is materially different from this security interest. The trustee is also not a subsequent purchaser or mortgagee within the recording protection. Historical legal meaning assists the text and carries no independent power to create federal perfection requirements. State commercial law therefore operates alongside patent law without changing the patent monopoly.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ownership-transferring instruments, actual purchasers, and other state-law perfection defects are reserved.

## 21. Thomas v. Chicago Park District

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-15, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the rejection of the facial challenge to the ordinance’s neutral coordination structure.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the ordinance operative while preserving properly presented claims of discriminatory application or use of criteria outside the authorized objective grounds. Do not impose every content-censorship procedure on ordinary park scheduling.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Safeguards should address the system’s actual discretionary risk

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the facial structure; do not decide unsupported allegations about particular officials’ motives.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the neutral permit scheme requires the full censorship safeguards.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Forsyth’s demand for constrained discretion and distinguish the separate censorship setting.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A genuinely content-neutral public-park permit system may use objective logistical criteria, a definite decision period, written reasons, and available administrative and judicial review without adopting every procedure required for content censorship. Its validity depends on real limits on discretion and timely access to review. Facial validity does not authorize viewpoint discrimination, unexplained exceptions, or audience-hostility burdens.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers this coordination structure and does not immunize its administration or all permit ordinances.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The challengers identify a real risk: administrative control can suppress expression as effectively as an explicit censor. The answer is to examine what officials may decide and how quickly their decision can be challenged. The ordinance’s enumerated grounds and timetable confine ordinary park management rather than authorize approval of messages. Requiring a government-filed judicial proceeding for each scheduling denial would address a different problem. Forsyth remains the controlling warning against an ordinance that permits officials to price or condition expression through unbounded or content-linked discretion. Stone distinguishes Freedman’s content-censorship system and reaffirms Forsyth’s constraint on discretionary burdens. The ordinance’s objective grounds, 28-day decision period, reasons, and review are the relevant safeguards; content neutrality alone would not save unbounded discretion. Its structure, tested against the practical risk of suppression, carries decisive weight. No general safe harbor protects discriminatory administration, and no ruling on whether Freedman requires a prompt final judicial determination is needed after its applicability is rejected.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A preserved claim of discriminatory administration requires its own factual adjudication.

## 19. United States v. Arvizu

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001; proposed disposition for 2002-01-15, subject to any intervening controlling judgment.
* **Conditions:** Apply any controlling intervening judgment at its actual scope. The approved disposition expresses Stone’s position and does not determine the votes or judgment of the Court.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s suppression ruling because the supported pre-stop circumstances, considered together, establish reasonable suspicion.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Return for further proceedings consistent with the lawful stop determination. Preserve any separate issue concerning consent or search scope actually left open; use no post-stop discovery to justify the earlier seizure.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The relationship among facts matters

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the ultimate reasonable-suspicion question on the supported historical findings.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the combined observations justify the stop despite possible innocent explanations.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Ornelas and Wardlow’s contextual principles without weakening independent judicial review.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Reasonable suspicion depends on the objective totality of circumstances known before the stop. A court may not discard each supported observation merely because it admits an innocent explanation. It must assess whether the observations and their rational relationship produce a particularized basis for suspecting wrongdoing. Training and experience receive weight when they explain that relationship; they do not replace judicial evaluation or validate irrelevant facts.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule permits cumulative inference without treating mere numerical accumulation or an officer’s unexplained intuition as sufficient.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The route, timing, and sensor context make the vehicle observations more informative than they would be on an ordinary road without that background. The record supplies a connected inference rather than a requirement to find each action independently criminal. Arvizu’s concern about profiles remains valid: the court must ask how the observations bear on this vehicle and this suspected activity. Independent review can credit the combination here while rejecting the notion that any series of innocent acts necessarily warrants a stop. Cortez and Terry’s totality standard, reaffirmed with Ornelas’s independent legal review, is decisive. The observations gain meaning from their relationship: a remote bypass route, timing around the checkpoint shift, sensor information, and unusual vehicle behavior support an inference particular to this trip. Ordinary innocent acts do not become unlawful, and their sheer number is not the test. Stone decides reasonable suspicion on the accepted pre-stop findings because the record permits the legal application; another remand merely to redo that legal comparison is unnecessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Separate consent or search-scope disputes, if preserved, remain distinct.
