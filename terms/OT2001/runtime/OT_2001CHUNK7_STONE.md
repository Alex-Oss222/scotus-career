## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the dismissal of the Fourth Amendment claim within the presented question.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** No damages or search injunction follows from the inspections adjudicated here. Leave the ungranted employment-benefit rulings undisturbed and do not decide immunity after concluding that these searches were reasonable.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A bounded administrative search of a private workplace computer

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The question arises on a developed summary-judgment record. The actual conduct is a public employer’s misconduct investigation, rather than ordinary police evidence collection.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the initial file inventory and three limited follow-up examinations were sufficiently connected to suspected work misuse and proportionate in scope.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply O’Connor v. Ortega’s workplace reasonableness framework and retain the employee’s reasonable expectation of privacy. Ownership and administrative access do not establish a waiver; no new computer exception is created.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An employee’s retained privacy does not preclude a limited, work-connected inspection reasonably initiated to investigate neglect of duty. Follow-up examination is permissible only to the extent justified by the information lawfully obtained and the same employment purpose. These identified inspections satisfy that standard.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the actual inventory and selected program inspections, not blanket copying, unrelated communications, personal tax returns, or later police use of an administrative program.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The inquiry concerns concrete work performance, and the first inspection disclosed program names rather than opening all personal documents. Those discoveries provided a particular reason to confirm suspected private-business software. Approved outside work did not establish permission to use the department’s computer for it. Conversely, an anonymous complaint did not authorize every later search: the limited program-specific scope supplies the operative restraint. Nothing in the accepted record establishes an examination of client returns, so an adverse inference of that examination would invent the decisive fact. The controlling method is the workplace balance as applied to inception and scope, with the concrete employment need and the restrained inspection carrying decisive weight. The result is limited to the searches actually proved because an unperformed examination of return contents would present a materially greater intrusion.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Searching tax-return contents, general employee monitoring, and the independent demotion and salary claims are not within the presented question and established search record.

## 96. United States v. Drayton

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the Eleventh Circuit’s suppression judgment on the ground that this coordinated encounter effected an unsupported seizure, while rejecting a categorical warning prerequisite.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve exclusion of the cocaine obtained during the continuous unlawful restraint. Return for the consequences appropriate to Brown’s conditional plea and Drayton’s trial conviction; the suppression decision does not itself order an acquittal. No independent consent holding or new Terry justification is required.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Actual freedom to decline an officer’s request

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The search evidence is challenged through preserved suppression proceedings. The district court’s finding of cooperative behavior must be respected as a factual finding where supported, but does not settle the legal significance of the full coordinated encounter.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a reasonable passenger in this actual coordinated encounter would have felt free to decline the officers’ demands or terminate the exchange.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Bostick’s totality-of-the-circumstances test and Robinette’s rejection of an indispensable refusal warning. Distinguish ordinary questioning by an officer from this coordinated assumption of the passenger compartment.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The officers’ positions, coordinated progression, close approach and announcement of an interdiction operation objectively communicated that the passengers were expected to submit before their journey resumed. Brown and Drayton were seized without an adequate independent justification. The absence of a refusal warning contributes to that conclusion but is neither necessary nor sufficient by itself.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The result rests on the police-created display of control, not on the bus’s ordinary confinement or a rule that all onboard questioning is a seizure. Both passengers were subject to that operation before Brown’s arrest; his arrest intensified the same pressure on Drayton.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Bostick requires an objective assessment of communicated freedom, not a search for a physically blocked exit. An officer facing the passengers from the front, with two colleagues moving forward from the rear, occupied the positions ordinarily controlling movement and departure while asking for cooperation in an announced interdiction. The driver’s absence and retention of the tickets supplied context; those travel arrangements alone were not the seizure. Quiet requests, concealed weapons and a passable aisle weigh for the Government, but they do not overcome the combined display of official control in this confined setting. The decisive method is the totality assessment required by precedent, and the decisive fact is the coordinated operation rather than the wording of a single request. No independent break, release or intervening event separated the submission and searches from that restraint. The immediate searches therefore remain its product. This record permits a decision now; another open-ended assessment of the same settled encounter facts is unnecessary.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent consent untainted by detention and an unpresented Terry justification are outside the accepted question. The holding creates no warning code, damages ruling or categorical prohibition on police questioning aboard buses.

Preserve all other habeas gates; award no writ on the underlying conviction.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The statutory review process remains pending through timely continuation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The case requires interpreting the limitation statute, not deciding a new equitable exception or the habeas merits.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a reasonable interval between successive California filings remains pending and whether the merits language conclusively proves reasonableness.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Artuz v. Bennett’s filing-versus-relief distinction and Duncan v. Walker’s state-proceeding limitation; resolve the previously open meaning of pendency during timely continuation of state review.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A properly initiated state collateral review course remains pending during an interval needed for timely pursuit at the next available level. California’s original-petition procedure qualifies when it functions that way. An alternative merits ruling does not conclusively establish timely filing or override an actual state untimeliness determination.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The construction protects timely ordinary continuation, not abandoned proceedings, unreasonable gaps, or every later collateral attack. State timeliness and federal proper-filing questions retain their independent legal content.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reading pending only as a document physically awaiting one judge’s order would make the federal deadline run during the very steps ordinarily necessary to complete state review. Functional continuity avoids that textual and structural mismatch without excusing indefinite delay. The state order’s diligence language prevents treating its merits phrase as dispositive. The remand must identify the actual ground, the applicable California standard, and any supported reason for the interval; it may not presume that four and one-half months is always reasonable or always late. Text and the structure of the State’s review process carry decisive weight; functional treatment of California’s original petitions identifies what constitutes that process. Duncan forecloses treating time in federal court as equivalent, and Artuz does not convert a genuine filing deadline into a mere merits bar. The remand is needed for the ambiguous state order and the actual timeliness of this four-and-one-half-month gap, not to excuse delay because habeas relief is important.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The underlying constitutional claim and an unpresented equitable-tolling claim are outside this decision.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The complete private-possession disability requires the full core-burden inquiry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Love v. Pepersack settles the individual holder. Review of pretrial dismissal must separately determine whether this civil-order application has the justification required by United States v. Wright; no guilt verdict supplies missing facts.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a complete temporary firearm-possession disability can be justified without an express credible-threat formula and what inquiry this record requires.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Retain strict scrutiny for this complete private-possession disability under Wright’s core-deprivation rationale. Napier now controls the classification of its qualifying civil-order disability under intermediate scrutiny; Stone would overrule that classification to the extent it permits a complete private-possession prohibition to avoid strict scrutiny, consistently with his separate position there. Reaffirm Napier’s individual coverage and conclusion that an adequately adjudicated civil danger predicate can suffice without a criminal conviction. Fraternal Order of Police’s official-use holding does not resolve the private-possession burden.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The absence of an express credible-threat formula does not alone invalidate §922(g)(8)’s alternative force-prohibition predicate. But a complete private-possession disability burdens the core and requires a compelling protective interest, narrow tailoring, and no comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative, established from the actual adjudicative basis and operation. The lower court’s process-and-temporariness rationale does not complete that inquiry; this application must return for it.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The categorical home boundary bars general abolition of ordinary defensive keeping. It does not immunize a genuinely justified individual dangerousness restriction, but an ordinary civil status or unexamined order cannot itself remove a person from the protection. Temporary duration affects tailoring, not whether a total prohibition is merely incidental.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Preventing lethal domestic violence is compelling. The hearing, opportunity to contest a specific threat, express restraint against injury, and Texas requirement of a protective basis are material. They answer why a verbal formula is not constitutionally indispensable, but do not conclusively prove the entire fit of a federal prohibition on all possession. The court must examine whether the civil proceeding actually established the necessary individualized risk and whether its duration and review mechanisms constrain the disability. It must also address supported less restrictive alternatives, such as a narrower conduct or access restriction, rather than assume that any injunction makes complete disarmament necessary. Such alternatives may prove inadequate because continued weapon access preserves the identified danger; that is a justification to assess on this record, not a finding to invent. A federal constitutional inquiry is distinct from an unrestricted collateral retrial of the state order. The proposed remand resolves the governing rule and permits ordinary application without validating every domestic-relations form.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Final constitutional application requires the developed adjudicative and alternatives record identified above. No ex parte order, permanent disability, newly recognized collateral-attack route, or guilt determination is decided.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Third Circuit’s determination that nonretroactivity review was unnecessary and remand.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require resolution of Pennsylvania’s preserved Teague v. Lane defense before any renewed grant of the writ. Leave the new-rule, exception, instructional-merits, and remaining habeas questions for the properly sequenced analysis. Do not order a new penalty trial or uphold the existing sentences on an unexamined alternative.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: An additional statutory restriction does not erase a preserved defense

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state preserved the issue in both federal courts. The case does not present sua sponte invocation of a forfeited defense.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether satisfaction of §2254(d) permits a grant without deciding the preserved nonretroactivity objection.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Caspari v. Bohlen and the distinct function of Williams v. Taylor. No new universal ordering rule is necessary.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A federal court cannot grant habeas relief on a rule potentially barred by a properly preserved nonretroactivity objection without resolving that objection. Section 2254(d) adds a condition; it does not repeal the separate inquiry merely because the state court decided another ground.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding governs the justification for a grant, not a mandatory full checklist before every denial. The nonjurisdictional character, preservation rules, and event-date exceptions remain intact.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The two analyses ask different questions about different legal dates. A state court’s unreasonable application of a later established rule does not establish that a federal collateral court may apply that rule to an earlier final conviction. Conversely, a later decision may simply articulate an already dictated result, which would defeat a new-rule objection. That is a legal determination the lower court must actually make. The absence of a state retroactivity ruling removes no federal obligation to decide a defense fairly presented in the federal proceeding. The decisive reasoning is the different legal work performed by nonretroactivity and §2254(d), not a categorical preference for procedural defeat. Tyler v. Cain reinforces that retroactivity and permission to grant relief remain distinct questions, but its successive-application rule is not imported into this first-petition case. Since the grant challenges the refusal to conduct Teague review, deciding whether Mills itself was new or exceptional would exceed the correction necessary here.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not decide whether the mitigating-evidence rule was new or whether either then-existing exception applies.

The conflicting experts here require a reliable determination rather than appellate factfinding. A categorical rule is warranted because individualized mitigation leaves the same class-wide deficits capable of being treated as aggravation. No later diagnostic cutoff or later judicial rule is imported.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A safety label must be tested against the actual accommodation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The evidence has been tried, and the review question is sufficiency and legal instruction rather than a new choice among competing planning preferences.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the supported accommodation verdict can stand despite the Village’s safety defense and the instruction’s placement.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the statutory accommodation duty and the objective-risk method of Bragdon v. Abbott; adapt the defense allocation to the public-entity and housing provisions.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A municipality must reasonably modify its driveway rule when necessary to afford a qualified resident equal housing access unless it establishes an applicable defense. An asserted direct threat requires objective individualized support and reasonable consideration of mitigation. The record supports this verdict, and the safety finding reflected in the verdict renders the identified placement error harmless.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The duty reaches the necessary exception established for this residence. It neither guarantees the most valuable design nor requires a municipality to overlook proved, unmitigable danger.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The relevant comparison is actual usability for this resident, not formal availability of some driveway. Evidence concerning the longer rear route, the physical limitations, and practical design alternatives could support necessity and reasonableness. The Village may protect children and pedestrians, but its conclusion must connect this driveway and driver to a supported risk rather than treating impaired mobility as proof of unsafe driving. Requiring the entity to prove that affirmative justification leaves plaintiffs responsible for their own statutory elements. Because the jury rejected the actual safety defense under the submitted theory, repeating the same dispute solely to relabel it as accommodation would not remedy a consequential error. The constitutional injury is what the State made that characteristic do to a person's place in the political community. It is never how heavily the characteristic weighed in the State's decision. A complaint that alleges only motive, predominance, district shape or composition states no claim. Standing is decided first and is not a merits element. The plaintiff must then prove a specific operation on that operation's own elements. For race the operations are Exclude, Mark, Burden and Dilute; a fifth, Segregate, is drafted and reserved. For party they are Foreclose and Entrench. Predominance and shape are evidence and nothing more. Relief removes the proved operation and goes no further, with the State acting first. Voting Rights Act claims proceed on their own elements and are never gated by this test.
* **What Stone articulates in this case if he writes:** The principle and the rule that a design-only complaint states no claim, applied with motive assumed against the State. The contrast between the composition of an electorate and an operation such as Exclude or Mark. The rule that the same protection extends to every race. The separate holding under *Richards* that shared political interests do not bind nonparties. The limit that incumbent protection is not declared incapable of ever being a racial allocation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The governing capacity rule and the information needed for judgment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The filed direct appeal presents the congressional judgment, performance evidence, comparative geography and an objection to the adequacy of the findings. The lower opinion does not identify how it resolved that evidence apart from its majority-linked reasoning. Stone resolves the governing legal distinction and remands for the findings the correct inquiry requires, without finding a violation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the identified numerical-rule discrepancy requires vacatur of the affected part of the judgment, or whether independently sufficient grounds sustain it.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply and reaffirm Valdespino, Growe, Voinovich, and De Grandy. Distinguish a single group's crossover capacity from aggregation of distinct protected groups. No change to entering law is proposed.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A preserved § 2 district-formation claim by one protected group cannot be rejected solely for lack of a numerical majority when the admissible record supports dependable ability to elect in a lawful compact district. The claimant must still prove cohesion, relevant ordinary majority-bloc defeat, causal connection, and unequal opportunity under the totality. A reviewing court must preserve any independent sufficient lawful ground; an erroneous numerical screen warrants relief only when material to the disposition. Here the lower opinion links its rejection of the appealed arrangement to a majority requirement and does not sufficiently identify an independent ground, so the judgment is vacated in that part and remanded for findings under the correct rule.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs preserved single-group claims against this court-drawn congressional plan and comparable claims within Valdespino's existing scope. It neither requires a fixed number of Latino districts nor authorizes statutory liability for mere influence. A court's duty to remedy malapportionment does not exempt its replacement map from that rule.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 2 protects effective electoral opportunity rather than a favored racial percentage. “Effective opportunity” means a practical ability to elect the voters' demonstrated preferred candidates under the actual electoral structure; it is neither assured victory nor merely persuading an otherwise controlling electorate. “Dependable” requires support adequate to the actual causal prediction, not a mandatory number of studies, fixed winning percentage, or immutable coalition. “Compactness” requires a lawful and geographically workable alternative; broad statewide population totals cannot establish it. “Independent ground” means a finding sufficient under the correct law whose reasoning does not depend on the rejected majority-only premise. A totality conclusion that simply repeats that premise is not independent; a supported finding of genuinely unequal or equal opportunity may be.

  Plaintiffs bear the ordinary preponderance burden on the statutory elements: each required proposition must be more likely true than not. Rigorous proof of functional capacity demands reliable support for dependability, not a heightened proof standard or compulsory study. These distinctions make the requested clarification consequential. The court below invoked numerical exclusivity but also made compactness and totality determinations. Stone cannot assume either that those determinations survive unchanged or that they collapse. Nor can a finding against common cohesion among different minority groups be silently converted into a rejection of one group's dependable outside support. “Preserved error” means a material legal objection properly available for appellate resolution on the claims and record carried forward, not a theory newly constructed from demographic facts. Ordinary procedural rules govern that inquiry and any lawful exception; a mistaken citation alone supplies no missing claim.

  This combines statutory text, controlling precedent, and functional application. Precedent carries decisive legal weight because it already recognizes both capacity routes; district-specific voting and geographic facts determine their application. The remedial distinction is structural: judicial responsibility requires compliance with enacted voting rights while withholding an unsupported choice of racial or partisan outcomes. Stone does not balance away a proved local deprivation through aggregate gains. He also does not treat statutory protection as a command to maximize seats. The remand follows from findings that do not show how the correct inquiry was resolved, not a preference for leaving the legal rule obscure.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The legal entitlement of a combined protected-group coalition, an influence-only claim, and a universal population or statistical formula lie outside the identified single-group question. Separate House and Senate judgments lie outside this appeal. No constitutional deprivation test is imposed as an additional § 2 element. The limited vacatur and remand in Part B is the approved position.
