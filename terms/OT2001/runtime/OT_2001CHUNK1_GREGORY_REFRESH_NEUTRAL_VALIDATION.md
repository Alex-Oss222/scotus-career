## Validated additions

### Board reopening

The 2001 text of §1201.118 permits the Board to reopen an appeal and reconsider the judge's decision on its own motion at any time, notwithstanding other provisions of the part. The verified text supplies authority, not an obligation to reopen whenever a prior disciplinary action changes and not an entitlement to a party-initiated rehearing. Section 1201.117 supplies several possible actions after review or reopening, including disposition, remand and further evidence; it does not make a new evidentiary hearing inevitable.

The source-fact extraction reports that Gregory neither informed the Board of the warning's arbitral reversal while its review was pending nor subsequently moved to reopen. These reported omissions do not establish waiver of every legal consequence, exhaustion of a new mandatory route, an actual reopening, or a finding that removal remains lawful without the warning. A party may seek exercise of the Board's authority without converting its own-motion power into mandatory relief.

### Elected review channel

For a matter within §7121(e)(1), the statute permits the covered employee to choose the applicable Board appellate route or the negotiated grievance procedure, but not both for the same matter. The election occurs upon the first timely appeal or written grievance under the applicable procedure. The provision also addresses comparable matters under other covered personnel systems. Section 7121(e)(2) separately preserves the applicable §7701(c)(1) standard for an arbitrator in the specified matters.

The supplied record identifies Gregory's election of Board review of removal. That election is not an election concerning every earlier disciplinary action. Her preexisting grievances concerning the warning and suspensions remain distinct matters. Neither the election provision nor the source facts establish that she could personally compel union-controlled arbitration, accelerate it, obtain a favorable award, or undo the removal election. No additional Postal Service coverage or collective-agreement ruling is required to decide the supplied pendency question, and none is inferred from the general framework.

### Complete reported Bolling framework

The sanitized complete source description resolves the earlier packet's shorthand. Restricted collateral review applies only when all three procedural predicates exist: written notice of the earlier action, the action's status as a matter of record, and an opportunity to dispute the charges above the level of the authority imposing discipline. If any predicate is absent, the Board undertakes full fresh review of the earlier disciplinary action as part of reviewing the later action. If all are present, the employee may demonstrate clear error from the existing earlier record; the Board does not treat a clearly erroneous action as a valid aggravating factor.

The reported opportunity prerequisite does not itself mean that arbitration has concluded or that the employee personally controls arbitration. Full fresh review and restricted existing-record review are different branches. The Board's collateral-review formulation is distinct from the agency's burden on the appealed adverse action and from the reviewing court's §7703(c) standard. The supplied materials report that the administrative judge examined the prior actions under Bolling; they do not supply detailed independent findings for every prerequisite or authorize this context to recreate them.
