## Carey v. Saffold

### Components, controlling baseline and competing paths

Section 2244(d)(2) excludes the pendency of a properly filed state collateral application concerning the pertinent judgment or claim. Artuz (Ginsburg for all nine, November 7, 2000) distinguishes application-filing conditions from claim-relief bars; Duncan (O'Connor with Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas among the seven, June 18, 2001) excludes federal proceedings and preserves independent timing questions. Neither settles California intervals. Young's bankruptcy tolling and Lee's state-ground adequacy do not decide this statutory question.

**Functional-course path:** a properly pursued state collateral-review course remains pending through timely intervals between levels, and California's original-petition nomenclature does not defeat the exclusion when it performs that review function. The strongest objection is that no application is before any court during an interval and the statutory word application cannot simply become an indefinitely open process. The limiting answer is timely continuation under state law: a completed proceeding, untimely continuation, or unrelated new application does not obtain interval exclusion merely by being state collateral litigation.

**Discrete-application path:** the first original application ends when the state court disposes of it; a later independent original petition has not yet been filed during the gap. The strongest objection is that California's route requires repeated original petitions to obtain the same higher-court examination ordinarily sought by appeal, so treating the label as dispositive discourages exhaustion for a difference unrelated to Congress's design. This is a statutory disagreement, not a finding that the applicant deliberately delayed.

All eight reject a rule that a merits reference necessarily establishes timely filing. An independent state lack-of-diligence determination can remain effective alongside merits treatment. The present order's meaning and actual dates require examination under California law; no nationwide reasonable-time ceiling is prescribed. Equitable tolling is distinct and is neither granted nor categorically abolished here.

## Horn v. Banks

### Sufficient rule and competing paths

A court may not grant a writ while leaving unresolved a properly preserved, applicable nonretroactivity defense capable of defeating relief. Section 2254(d) restricts grants based on an unreasonable state merits adjudication; Teague asks whether the rule was dictated when the conviction became final and, if new, whether a then-available exception applies. Caspari's preserved-defense holding, the Williams statutory formulation, Tyler's separate successive-application requirement and Lambrix's nonjurisdictional sequencing rule retain their distinct work. Bell's application of §2254(d) does not repeal Teague.

Banks's strongest competing argument is that Mills follows dictated capital-mitigation law, so the defense ultimately fails. That is a newness argument to decide, not a reason to omit the inquiry. Conversely, finality before Mills alone does not prove that Mills announced a new rule. Both contemporary exceptions remain: substantive limits on punishable conduct or classes of persons or punishments, and watershed procedures essential to accuracy and fundamental fairness. The present decision need not adjudicate them or the instruction's meaning. The narrowly supported disposition vacates the grant of death-sentence relief and remands for the preserved analysis, leaving conviction and other claim treatment outside the reviewed error.

## United States v. Drayton — June 17, 2002

### Components and competing supported paths

The reviewed question is whether the officers seized Brown and Drayton before the searches. Bostick supplies an objective, contextual inquiry into freedom to decline the request or terminate the encounter when the passenger's decision to travel already limits movement. The Court must not treat inability to leave a bus as police restraint. Robinette rejects an indispensable warning rule while retaining a real totality inquiry. Neither decision makes every bus encounter consensual. The separate voluntariness of consent and any independent search issue are outside this reviewed seizure question. Arvizu's current separation of stop validity from preserved consent issues reinforces that boundary; it does not supply suspicion for this encounter.

**No seizure:** the officers displayed badges but kept weapons concealed, used quiet requests and did not command passengers to remain, seize their tickets or block the aisle. The driver checked tickets and left to do paperwork; the police did not collect or retain the tickets. ## United States v. Fior D'Italia, Inc. — June 17, 2002

### Components and competing supported paths

The employer's FICA liability is distinct from the employees' income-tax or FICA collection obligations. Sections 3111 and 3121(q) govern the employer's taxable tip wages, including the applicable notice-and-demand timing for unreported tips. Section 6201 supplies assessment authority; §6205's adjustment mechanism remains distinct. Every authorized assessment remains bounded by actual taxable wages: noncash tips, cash tips below the employee's monthly threshold and the applicable annual bases cannot be taxed merely by calling an amount an estimate. The 1991–1992 wage bases govern; later changes to Medicare's base and the later-enacted §45B credit cannot retroactively alter those years.

The taxpayer expressly confined this litigation to the legal authority for an aggregate employer assessment and conceded the factual assumptions and estimates for that purpose. That stipulation does not concede statutory power. Conversely, it prevents turning hypothetical inaccuracies into findings that this assessment in fact double-counted tips, missed an actual wage-base adjustment or overstated cash tips. The refund claim and the government's counterclaim turn on the same challenged statutory premise. Neither side's success on that premise supplies an unpresented recalculation.

**Authorization:** general assessment power permits a reasonable estimate of an independently imposed employer tax. Congress did not require separate employee assessments, collection from employees or allocation of Social Security benefit credits before assessing the employer. The applicable exclusions and wage bases constrain the lawful amount and remain available in a proper factual challenge. The principal objection is that an aggregate credit-card extrapolation may obscure the employee-specific facts needed to determine those statutory limits. The response on this submitted record is that the employer reserved no challenge to the estimates or factual assumptions, and the Court need not approve an inaccurate assessment to reject a categorical lack of authority.

**No authority for this aggregate method:** Congress defined the employer's liability through the individual recipient's taxable wages, with exclusions and annual bases that cannot be replaced by an estimate of restaurant-wide receipts alone. Section 6201 authorizes assessment of the tax Congress imposed; it does not redefine that base. This path does not require first assessing or collecting tax from an employee, and does not prohibit supported estimates directed to an employee's taxable wages. Its strongest counterargument is that the statute does not prescribe such an individualized computational prerequisite and that the taxpayer's stipulation removes the factual defects invoked to justify one. The narrower response is structural: agreement that an aggregate factual estimate is accurate does not decide whether that estimate supplies the statutorily required wage determination. The Court must state that legal premise expressly rather than pretend the record proves computational error.

## United States v. Hooper — June 17, 2002

### Components and competing supported paths

The reviewed property is the proved proceeds of the husbands' offenses. The wives assert interests arising under community-property law when those proceeds were acquired, not independently acquired interests that existed before the acts generating them. Their asserted state-law interests can support participation in an ancillary proceeding without establishing the superior federal entitlement needed for relief. Section 853(n)(6)(A) requires the claimant to establish by a preponderance that the qualifying vested or superior interest existed at the time of the criminal acts. Subsection (n)(6)(B) separately protects a bona fide purchaser for value who was reasonably without cause to believe the property subject to forfeiture; that purchaser theory is not asserted here. A successful statutory showing requires amendment of the forfeiture order, rather than discretionary grace. This record establishes neither qualifying path.

**Affirmance:** §853(a) expressly reaches criminal proceeds; subsection (c)'s relation-back rule determines the government's title upon forfeiture, subject to the actual third-party exceptions. A community interest acquired through the same criminal generation of proceeds cannot have vested before those acts or become superior to that federal interest merely because state law characterizes it as an immediate marital share. State law identifies the claimant's interest; federal law determines its priority in this statutory proceeding. Innocence alone is not the missing statutory temporal or purchaser predicate.

**The strongest contrary path:** state law treats the spouse's ownership as vested immediately and the wife neither committed nor knew of the offense. Simulated Bennis protects an innocent co-owner's established lawful interest where the required nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and reasonable-prevention conditions are met. The objection exposes a real limit: neither the husband's conviction nor federal supremacy authorizes confiscating all property a spouse owns. It does not prevail here because the property at issue was created by the criminal proceeds, rather than an independently existing lawful asset later misused. The supplied proceeding has separated legitimate property; the Court does not undo that separation or make innocence irrelevant to a different statutory or constitutional claim.

Buena Vista's civil-forfeiture treatment of donee eligibility and contested title does not eliminate §853's criminal ancillary requirements. Current Bollin requires actual statutory authority before federal supremacy can defeat a state exemption; §853(a) supplies that authority for the proceeds here, unlike the untainted potential substitute asset in Bollin. Current Farmer requires meaningful review of a supported claim of legitimate assets; it grants no right to retain proved proceeds and does not presume a process defect in this completed ancillary merits dispute. Craft's federal-consequence distinction also leaves the wife's actual state interest and the distinct federal priority question separate.

## Shared grounds and material distinctions

**Personal coverage.** Love's individual-holder holding and Hale's separation of coverage from regulation govern, and Napier applies them to an ordinary pistol. The right is not extinguished by describing the holder as subject to a domestic-relations order. The competing collective-only disposition is no longer lawful under entering doctrine. The individual-right question therefore requires application of settled law, not a new original-meaning decision or adoption of the Fifth Circuit's lengthy historical analysis.

**Statutory reading.** Subparagraphs (A) and (B) remain required; (C)(i) and (C)(ii) are alternatives. An express credible-threat finding is necessary for the first alternative, not a textual element of the second. An order satisfying those textual conditions can still impose an unconstitutional federal burden in a particular case. This distinction is already necessary to Napier's treatment of the alternatives, and no Justice makes the statute's disjunction disappear to avoid the constitutional inquiry.

**Civil process and prospective prevention.** Napier forecloses an indispensable-conviction rule and rejects the idea that government must wait for another assault. Its decided facts included completed violence and recurrence findings. It did not decide the precise threat-only boundary now presented. The present six-Justice commitment extends its principle to an adjudicated protective necessity established through the legally operative hearing-and-order process described below. The two contrary commitments do not demand a criminal conviction or require the statutory phrase; they demand further constitutional application to the actual full possession burden.

**Fair warning and statutory fault.** The public Staples holding requires knowledge of relevant weapon characteristics while rejecting a general requirement to know nonregistration or illegality. Bryan separately requires knowing unlawful dealing under a willfulness provision; its rule does not rewrite §924(a)(2)'s knowledge language. Lambert protects against the distinct combination of wholly passive conduct, no knowledge and no circumstances prompting inquiry. Emerson signed a form expressly identifying the restraining-order disability, later knew the order and its contents, and allegedly possessed the identified pistol more than sixty days afterward. The warning is not an irrebuttable finding that he remembered the law, and the present disposition does not find every offense element proved. It does establish that the blanket ignorance-of-law theory relied on below cannot support pretrial dismissal on this record. No additional warning from the divorce judge is constitutionally indispensable on these facts.

**Collateral review.** Ordinary relitigation of an operative state injunction and constitutional review of the additional federal disability are distinct. The six affirming Justices accept that the injunction retains its operative effect until lawfully altered and do not authorize routine federal retrial of the divorce evidence. They nevertheless evaluate whether the process and legal predicate can constitutionally support the federal restriction. Scalia and Thomas likewise reject ordinary collateral retrial but require a more complete restriction-specific constitutional determination. None joins an unlimited rule insulating every formally qualifying civil order from constitutional scrutiny.

## Affirming ground and its limits

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer commit to the following sufficient ground, at this level of generality:

A force-prohibiting domestic-relations injunction entered after actual notice and a meaningful opportunity to contest specific evidence of violent threats may supply Napier's constitutionally adequate civil danger predicate when the applicable law requires a real, nonspeculative protective necessity for the restraint, the order actually addresses that threatened injury, and the resulting disability remains dependent on that operative order. A completed assault and the precise (C)(i) words are not indispensable. On this record, the resulting possession restriction is a substantial regulation under Wright and satisfies intermediate scrutiny.

The application rests on the combined hearing, specific testimony, protective terms, substantive state-law requirement and continuing judicial control. The wife's testimony concerned a threat to kill her friend communicated to her; it was not testimony that Emerson threatened to kill her. But the state court could protect an intimate partner against violent intimidation communicated through a threat to another person, and its order restrained both that mode of threat and physical injury. A real threat may justify preventive protection before completed injury. The legally entered protective order after that hearing provides the adjudicative predicate; the Court does not manufacture an express finding or declare the underlying threat separately proved as a federal crime.

This is a judgment about the legal significance of an actual adjudication under the stated state-law standards. It is not a factual claim that the issuing judge uttered an unrecorded conclusion, and it is not a presumption that every civil-order form contains a danger finding. The best contrary argument is that the form could have issued routinely and that the judge never specifically evaluated a firearm disability. The answer of the six is that neither the federal Constitution nor Napier requires the predicate tribunal to anticipate every collateral federal consequence or use a particular formula; a real protective adjudication directed to threatened physical injury can justify the subsequently imposed order-dependent restraint. On this record the hearing and legal basis exist independently of the order's boilerplate wording. Requiring another adjudication solely because the order omitted the formula would collapse the statutory alternatives and mistake the record's missing words for missing process.

Preventing armed domestic injury is an important objective. Removing access to firearms while a qualifying protective order directed to violent intimidation remains effective substantially addresses the risk of armed escalation. A command not to threaten or injure does not itself remove access to the instrument of escalation; a restriction limited to carrying in public would not address access in private domestic encounters. Intermediate scrutiny does not demand proof that violence is inevitable or that a past completed assault involved a gun. The scope of the possession prohibition remains serious, but the demonstrated protective adjudication, actual opportunity to contest it and dependence on the continuing order supply the conduct-and-danger limits that distinguish it from withdrawal of the right by civil label alone.

The state proceedings permitted modification and mandamus review; direct interlocutory appeal was unavailable. Those are actual legal avenues, not a finding that Emerson obtained review or could secure immediate termination. The order ended at the divorce decree or further order, subject to the distinct treatment of any provision in a final decree. Neither a fixed short duration nor guaranteed restoration is used. Continued ordinary firearm access for other people supplies no answer to Emerson's individual burden. Wright's categorical prohibition on general abolition of ordinary defensive home possession remains intact; this holding addresses a defined individual protective disqualification and does not approve all temporary total prohibitions.

The six reject a finding that the later alleged pistol incident proves the September predicate. That conduct is not adjudicated in this posture and does no justificatory work. They likewise reject treatment of the wife's non-cross-examined account as a conclusive factual admission. An order issued without a meaningful opportunity to contest the protective basis, under rules requiring no protective justification, or directed to an unrelated interest is outside this adopted rule. Those are limits of this rule, not invented additional events or a catalogue of separate unresolved cases.

### Frozen proposition scope and remedy

All eight reject the stated virtual-representation basis. Four Associates affirm Counts I and II on existing constitutional law; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would vacate them under their proposed reconsideration of the territorial-injury rule. No final Court tally is assembled. The latter four support the same limited return and do not find racial predominance or a constitutional violation established. Their objection concerns the legal treatment of the actual evidence under their proposed cause, not a supposed missing pleading or required new filing.

The four supporting affirmance assume favorable reasonable inferences concerning racial motivation and rest on a legal insufficiency: the common territorial electorate and favorable incumbent reelection prospects produced by those same district lines do not supply the independent injury required by the governing doctrine. This is neither a requirement of a second governmental act or monetary loss nor a rule exempting every actual race-based benefit from review. Anderson v. Martin's official racial electoral labeling remains distinct; Adarand's justification rule does not supply a missing allocation or injury. The broader Fifteenth Amendment coverage question is unnecessary where no independently cognizable denial or abridgment is established on the actual territorial theory.

If the existing-law merits ground commands a majority, affirmance leaves the denial of relief intact while rejecting the preclusion reasoning; there is no need for an exploratory privity hearing or a remand to construct a new binding relationship. If the proposed constitutional path commands a majority, the return is limited to the actual Counts I and II under ordinary pleading, evidence and summary-judgment rules, with any relief following an established wrong and proper remedial scope. Neither path reaches Count III, a replacement plan, the validity of every map feature or a new election timetable. No inconsistent rationales may be combined into an invented controlling test.
