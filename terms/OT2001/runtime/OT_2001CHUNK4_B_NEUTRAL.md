## 55. Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-795; 535 U.S. 234.
* **October Term:** 2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment identified below under the supplied merits question.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-04-16. Historical final opinion or disposition date, used as the simulated merits event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Child Pornography Prevention Act’s provisions covering depictions that appear to show minors and depictions presented as showing minors substantially restrict protected speech. This is a formulation of the challenge to §§2256(8)(B) and (D).
* **Question Granted:** The supplied merits scope is the question stated above, with the preserved limitations identified below. No separate simulated grant order or grant date is supplied.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, reversing judgment for the Government and holding the two challenged definitions unconstitutional.
* **Relief Requested:** The Government seeks reinstatement of the challenged prohibitions or a sustaining construction. The coalition and other speakers seek protection against their enforcement.
* **Material Record:** The challengers are speakers and producers contesting definitions that reach some images made without using children, including depictions created with adults or digitally. The action is a facial speech challenge rather than review of a conviction involving an identified abused child. The separate provision concerning altered images of an identifiable actual minor is not challenged.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Substantial overbreadth requires examination of the provisions’ actual legitimate and protected reach. Scienter, affirmative defenses, and proposed limiting constructions must be assessed as enacted, not assumed to eliminate every protected application. The existence of actual child exploitation elsewhere does not establish it in every depiction the provisions cover.
* **Relevant Dates:** The challenged provisions were enacted in 1996. The merits event is April 16, 2002. Later amendments, later pandering cases, and later technology-specific legislation are not entering law.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Ashcroft v. ACLU concerns COPA restrictions on commercial web material harmful to minors, including the role of community standards. This case concerns definitions criminalizing virtual or promoted-as-child depictions without requiring an actual child; the statutory coverage and asserted harms differ.
* **Participation Matter:** No in-world recusal, vacancy, or nonparticipation is authenticated in the supplied materials.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. A proposed brief does not establish an effective same-term decision.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No completed OT2001 ruling affecting these questions is supplied. Ashcroft v. ACLU is later on the calendar and provides no entering rule.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** First Amendment facial review; 18 U.S.C. §§2256(8)(B), 2256(8)(D), and the connected offenses and defenses in their then-effective versions. Existing prohibitions involving actual children or legally obscene works remain distinct.

### Issue 1: Depictions made without an actual child

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union invalidates substantially overbroad content-based criminal restrictions that suppress protected adult expression and fail narrow tailoring; scienter does not cure an uncertain content boundary. United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc. requires the Government to justify content-selected suppression against concrete less restrictive alternatives. United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc. preserves knowledge requirements and the particular actual-child statutory category.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The registers preserve adult access, require substantial rather than imagined overbreadth, and keep unprotected categories tied to their actual predicates. Child protection is compelling but does not automatically expand every exception to protected expression.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; the two statutory definitions and available defenses. Miller v. California governs obscenity; New York v. Ferber and Osborne v. Ohio address the distinct harm associated with images produced through use of actual children.
* **Material Other Authority:** Broadrick v. Oklahoma supplies the substantial-overbreadth requirement. The possibility that expression is misused does not by itself establish incitement, solicitation, or another independently unprotected category.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing rules govern content-based criminal burdens and actual exploitation. They do not already classify every digital depiction; lawful adult production cannot simply be assumed to involve the injury that supports Ferber.
* **Open Point:** Whether the appearance-based category can be sustained without an actual child, obscenity, or another constitutionally sufficient predicate, and whether a faithful construction cures its protected reach.

### Issue 2: Presentation as depicting a minor

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union preserves lawful fraud, solicitation, and actual child-exploitation laws while rejecting judicial creation of a new narrower offense absent from the text. United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc. requires attention to the precise speech burden, not the Government’s label alone.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The operative rules distinguish an unlawful transaction or fraudulent offer from criminalization of an otherwise protected expressive work. A speaker’s or seller’s presentation may be evidence of an existing offense without automatically changing the work’s constitutional status for everyone.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 2256(8)(D) and the possession and distribution offenses that use the definition; ordinary criminal scienter and the First Amendment remain separate inquiries.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ginzburg v. United States concerns pandering as evidence relevant to obscenity, not an automatic rule that any work becomes obscene because someone describes it offensively.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The baseline permits tailored prohibitions with the necessary harmful-conduct predicates. It supplies no general power to make possession of protected material criminal through another person’s characterization.
* **Open Point:** Whether the presentation-based definition can be confined to a constitutionally permissible offer or promotion offense without rewriting the enactment.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Government invokes prevention of child abuse, use of depictions to facilitate abuse, and the difficulty of distinguishing fabricated from actual images in enforcement. The challengers argue that the definitions reach substantial lawful expression without the victimization or obscenity predicates that justify suppression. The Ninth Circuit accepted the overbreadth objection. A limiting construction must be textually available; an affirmative defense limited to some production methods does not necessarily protect all covered speech. The Government may regulate actual exploitation and unlawful solicitation, but must explain why those legitimate ends require these particular broader definitions.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could invalidate the challenged definitions for substantial overbreadth while preserving separate actual-child, obscenity, and identifiable-minor provisions. Partial reversal could sustain a textually supportable subset, or a remand could address a genuine unresolved construction, but judicial insertion of a missing offense element is not ordinary interpretation. Upholding either provision would need a defined constitutional basis for its nonobscene, no-actual-child reach. The case does not establish immunity for producing abuse, using actual victims’ altered images, or soliciting criminal acts; those categories retain their own predicates and remedies.

### Issue 1: State-created rights and federal lien classification

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Drye v. United States holds that state law defines legally effective control, while federal law determines whether that control is property or a right to property under §6321. State relation back cannot erase control already subject to the federal lien. United States v. Estate of Romani preserves distinct statutory priority protections.

* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. National Bank of Commerce and United States v. Bess support the two-stage inquiry. United States v. Rodgers concerns sale powers and protections that must not be treated as automatically decided by attachment.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Drye already determines which sovereign supplies which part of the analysis. Its inheritance-control application does not itself decide the aggregate of present rights in an entireties estate or their value.
* **Open Point:** Whether the taxpayer’s existing rights remain federally attachable despite the inability to convey the whole estate unilaterally and the State’s protection against separate creditors.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The circuit treated the absence of a separate state-law interest reachable by an individual creditor as dispositive. The wife argues that the unified marital estate leaves nothing distinct for the lien to attach to. The Government points to the husband’s actual powers and economic benefits and the federal statute’s breadth. The Government cannot collect the wife’s independent share as if it belonged to the husband; the wife cannot answer the federal classification question solely with Michigan’s different choice about ordinary creditors.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Reversal could recognize attachment to the taxpayer’s rights and remand valuation and remaining statutory questions. Affirmance could hold the indivisible state estate supplies no property of the individual taxpayer within §6321. A narrower ruling could identify which present rights are sufficient without deciding survivorship alone. Neither result requires a new federal definition of Michigan ownership. Recognition of a lien does not automatically distribute half the proceeds, impose personal liability on the wife, or resolve every enforcement mechanism.

* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Palazzolo is completed OT2000 law and is incorporated below. No completed OT2001 decision supplies a temporal-denominator holding.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments; the interstate compact and relevant TRPA enactments; the preserved appellate scope. The constitutional trigger for a taking and the compensation owed after one occurs must be analyzed separately.

### Issue 1: Temporary restriction and the relevant property

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Lucas preserves the complete-deprivation category and Penn Central's contextual inquiry. Palazzolo holds that meaningful remaining whole-parcel use defeats Lucas without defeating Penn Central, and that regulatory notice alone does not extinguish a claim. Palazzolo does not resolve a temporal denominator; First English addresses compensation once a taking exists.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable standards distinguish physical appropriation, complete regulatory deprivation of the relevant property, and contextual review of partial burdens. Valid public purpose does not eliminate a taking; duration affects both the existence and measure of the alleged loss.
* **Other Governing Law:** Takings Clause; Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City examines economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations, and character of government action. First English Evangelical Lutheran Church of Glendale v. County of Los Angeles addresses compensation once a temporary taking is established.
* **Material Other Authority:** Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis and Andrus v. Allard caution against isolating a selected strand of a larger property interest to manufacture total deprivation. Their applications do not alone answer every temporal restriction.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Lucas supplies the total-deprivation category but does not adopt segmentation of ownership into the exact period of regulation. First English does not establish that every temporary inability to develop is already a taking.
* **Open Point:** Whether a planning moratorium’s duration may be isolated from the continuing property interest to invoke the categorical rule, and whether a temporary label instead creates any blanket immunity from contextual review.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The circuit treated the continuing property interest as the relevant whole. The landowners argue that complete loss of use during an appreciable period is itself compensable and that a temporal distinction would let government evade Lucas by repeatedly calling restrictions temporary. TRPA argues that planning delays burden continuing ownership and require contextual analysis. A necessary legal limit is that unreasonable or prolonged restrictions can be compensable on an adequate record; this is not attributed to an oral-argument concession. The owners must confront their preservation limits and distinguish compensation for an established temporary taking from a rule making every moratorium one.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could reject temporal segmentation for the categorical claim while preserving Penn Central scrutiny in properly presented cases. Reversal could recognize a sufficiently defined categorical temporary deprivation and remand for background-law defenses and compensation for the actual taking period. A duration-sensitive categorical rule would require a legally justified threshold rather than an invented numerical cutoff. A remand for the abandoned Penn Central theory would not follow merely because that framework ordinarily applies. The result should not decide later restrictions outside the preserved question or create immunity for every planning moratorium.

## 58. Michigan v. Environmental Protection Agency +

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Michigan v. Environmental Protection Agency +
* **Citation or Docket:** 213 F.3d 663, District of Columbia Circuit. No simulated Supreme Court docket or grant date is supplied.
* **October Term:** 2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment identified below under the supplied merits question.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-04-23. Assigned in the supplied CASE LIST. The historical lower-court judgment remains the judgment under review.
* **Question Presented:** (1) Whether the Clean Air Act permits EPA to consider the availability of highly cost-effective pollution controls when determining the emissions reductions necessary to address an upwind state’s significant contribution to another state’s air-quality violations? (2) Whether EPA’s imposition of statewide nitrogen-oxide emissions budgets unlawfully displaces the states’ statutory authority to select their own pollution-control measures? (3) Whether the interstate-pollution provision, interpreted to authorize EPA’s cost-based approach, supplies an intelligible principle sufficient to satisfy constitutional limits on delegating legislative power?

States retained choices among means of compliance. The reviewed judgment separately addressed Wisconsin, Missouri, Georgia, and notice defects. The case concerns this transport rule, not the permissible cost considerations for setting national ambient standards.

The exact $2,000 threshold’s independent arbitrary-and-capricious validity must not be confused with the preserved legal permissibility of considering cost. * **Participation Matter:** No in-world recusal, vacancy, or nonparticipation is authenticated in the supplied materials.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. A proposed brief does not establish an effective same-term decision.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Whitman and Mead are completed prior-term law and are incorporated below. No completed OT2001 decision is assumed.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Clean Air Act §§110(a)(2)(D)(i)(I), 110(k)(5), and 307; the Administrative Procedure Act’s review limits; Article I and the constitutional rules governing federal and state regulatory responsibility.

### Issue 1: Cost within the interstate-contribution provision

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** MCI requires an actual statutory delegation. Arkansas v. Oklahoma respects authorized interstate-pollution administration and supported findings. Whitman excludes implementation cost from §109 national-standard setting but preserves provision-specific implementation authority; Mead requires actual delegated interpretive authority before controlling deference.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable standards require statutory fit, a reasoned agency explanation, and the proper record-review standard. Courts may not replace supported technical judgments merely because another approach is possible, or excuse use of a forbidden factor because the policy is beneficial.
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 110 requires adequate provisions against emissions contributing significantly to another State’s nonattainment or interfering with maintenance. Section 110(k)(5) authorizes correction of inadequate plans. The meaning of significant contribution must be tied to that interstate problem.
* **Material Other Authority:** Union Electric Co. v. EPA concerns economic infeasibility in its own statutory approval setting. Its reach must be examined rather than converted into a cost prohibition across every Clean Air Act provision.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law supplies bounded interpretation and record review. It does not already decide whether control costs are a permissible dimension of significance in this transport provision.
* **Open Point:** Whether EPA may consider cost-effective controls after establishing the relevant interstate pollution contribution, without converting cost alone into an unrelated national emissions power.

### Issue 2: State selection of control measures

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** New York v. United States distinguishes lawful federal regulation and available state choices from an unavoidable command that a State regulate private conduct or assume responsibility. AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board recognizes concurrent implementation roles where Congress actually assigned them.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The registers preserve a real lawful option to decline state administration and distinguish a federal outcome standard from compulsory state selection of a federally dictated method. Statutory state discretion may be more protective than the constitutional minimum.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Clean Air Act allocates plan formation to States subject to federal adequacy criteria and provides federal implementation mechanisms when statutory conditions for them are met.
* **Material Other Authority:** Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn. and FERC v. Mississippi distinguish cooperative regulatory arrangements from a command with no lawful alternative. Virginia v. EPA supplies the circuit-level dispute over preserving meaningful choice of measures.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing rules prohibit commandeering but do not immunize interstate pollution from valid federal regulation. They require examination of the actual regulatory options and statutory allocation.
* **Open Point:** Whether an emissions budget sets a permissible federal result while leaving meaningful choice of controls, or effectively dictates the States’ regulatory decisions.

### Issue 3: Congressional guidance

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Whitman holds that enacted pollutant, health-protection and safety-margin criteria satisfy the intelligible-principle requirement, without Congress selecting a unique number. It separately holds that agency self-limitation cannot cure a genuinely unguided delegation. The transport provision must be assessed under those controlling principles on its own text.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable rule separates authorization, guidance, and a particular exercise’s validity. Useful expertise, political accountability, or an agency’s present modesty cannot alone repair an unguided delegation.
* **Other Governing Law:** Article I; the statute’s identified pollutants, national standards, interstate movement, significant-contribution requirement, maintenance protection, and assigned implementing agency.
* **Material Other Authority:** J. W. Hampton, Jr., & Co. v. United States and Mistretta v. United States supply the received intelligible-principle framework within the authenticated baseline. Whitman applies that framework and rejects agency cure of a standardless delegation.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing doctrine permits guided implementation without Congress choosing every technical number. It does not already determine whether this combination of statutory limits is adequate.
* **Open Point:** Whether the transport provision itself sufficiently defines the object and boundaries of the delegated judgment, independently of the agency’s current geographic or economic reach.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The circuit accepted cost as part of significance, meaningful state choice of controls, and sufficiently confined authority. Petitioners argue that significant contribution describes physical pollution, that cost-based budgets override state selection, and that cost discretion lacks a congressional stopping point. EPA answers that modeling supplies the interstate nexus, implementation permits cost-sensitive judgments, and States may choose their own measures. The Solicitor General defended statutory guidance independently of the circuit’s emphasis on the agency’s limited exercise. Neither side may merge the national-standard provision with this distinct implementation clause.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance on the supplied questions could permit cost-informed transport budgets, preserve actual state choices, and sustain Congress’s statutory guidance while leaving independent geographic and notice corrections intact. Reversal on statutory grounds could require EPA to reconsider budgets without the forbidden factor, making constitutional decision unnecessary. A state-choice defect could require revision of the means structure without invalidating the interstate duty. A genuine lack of congressional guidance would require a constitutional remedy directed to the invalid authority, not court-created numerical limits. The record supports no wholesale approval of all future air rules or reopening of every technical ruling.

Facial invalidation would require more than speculation about materially different uses; §1201(c)(1) must be interpreted rather than converted into an unrestricted circumvention exception.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The owners rely on widespread copying, identified notices, Napster’s search facilities and its ability to exclude users. Napster invokes lawful uses, sampling, personal relocation of music and statutory protection. The owners’ enforcement interest does not permit an impossible guarantee against every unidentified file; Napster’s lawful uses do not authorize deliberate continued assistance to identified infringement. Napster also asserts waiver, implied license, copyright misuse, speech interests, inadequate discovery or hearing, a compulsory-royalty alternative and insufficient security. Each objection must be resolved on its own record rather than subsumed into secondary liability.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Blanket shutdown or automatic technology immunity would exceed the respective showings. Statutory safe-harbor issues and final liability retain their independent elements.
