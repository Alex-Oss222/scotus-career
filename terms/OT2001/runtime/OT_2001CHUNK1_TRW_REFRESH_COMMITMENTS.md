## Corrections to the original assumptions

The affirmative statement that the injury-completion alternative was preserved below is withdrawn for all eight Associates. It was first presented in this Court and was not raised or briefed below. No Justice now supports a remand instruction affirmatively entitling Andrews to litigate that theory or obtain an injury-date hearing.

The express statutory misrepresentation exception has no pleaded predicate: the complaint does not allege a material and willful misrepresentation by TRW of information the FCRA required it to disclose to Andrews. All eight therefore reject application of that exception to these claims. Its existence does not support an additional exception hearing or an inferred concealment allegation.

The first-two-disclosure claims allege willful §1681e(a) violations and request punitive as well as actual damages under the pre-1996 §1681n. The later statutory-damages alternative does not govern. Actual compensatory loss cannot simply be assumed to exhaust the statute's liability or relief. No finding about actual loss in May 1995 is made by accepting that this was Andrews' new argument.

## Rules and sufficient explanations

### I. The express exception does not authorize general discovery

The then-operative §1681p requires suit within two years after liability arises. Its specified discovery extension requires a defendant's material and willful misrepresentation of information the FCRA required the defendant to disclose to the individual, where the misrepresented information is material to establishing that defendant's liability to that individual; in that circumstance suit may be brought within two years after the individual discovers the misrepresentation. A general extension until discovery of any violation would bypass those restrictive statutory conditions.

All eight join this rule at that level of generality. The practical difficulty of discovering identity misuse cannot substitute for the enacted exception. The complaint's allegations do not satisfy that exception, so the Court's rule applies to the disputed disclosures without an additional exception inquiry. The rule does not depend on an inference that a document's absence proves litigant misconduct; the addendum affirmatively supplies the complaint's pleaded scope.

Rotella, February 23, 2000, rejects pattern-discovery delay only when its civil-RICO claim is complete and the injury known, while preserving the general accrual choice in that statute. Klehr's June 19, 1997 separate new-injury, unprovable-damages and diligent-concealment rules likewise remain confined to their stated predicates. Neither establishes a universal discovery rule or makes actual compensatory loss the indispensable first condition of every other statutory action.

### II. Seven Associates reach the actual-damages alternative within the existing claim

The new argument seeks to sustain the same favorable appellate result under §1681p's liability-arises language; it does not add a different disclosure, defendant or claim for relief. First presentation permits the Court to decline consideration, but does not itself withdraw the Court's discretion to resolve a legal ground within the preserved claim. Lebron's February 21, 1995 rule permits consideration where the argument falls within the presented question and the established record supplies the necessary predicates without a new factual theory. The favorable-judgment defense rule recognized in X-Citement Video permits such alternatives in its proper setting but does not independently compel consideration.

Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer exercise that discretion here. The necessary accepted predicates are the willful statutory claim, the request for punitive relief, the operative remedial text, the disclosure dates and the filing date. They need not determine the truth of Andrews' proposed May 1995 actual-loss date: even assuming that date for the argument, the legal premise that no liability could arise before actual loss is unsound for the willful claim. Reaching that confined premise settles the effect of the statutory rule on the disputed first two claims without a factfinding inquiry or an affirmative remand entitlement based on an unpreserved theory.

O'Connor declines that discretionary course. Her prior Lebron position supplies a concrete prudential basis for declining a first-presented alternative despite its relationship to the same claim. She would correct the preserved general-discovery rule and state that the new actual-damages theory was not preserved below. She joins no conclusion that the argument is beyond the Court's jurisdiction, and no conclusion that it is automatically available on remand. Existing mandate, preservation and waiver rules govern any lower-court request; silence supplies no permission to reopen a foreclosed argument.

### III. Seven Associates reject an actual-loss prerequisite to accrual of these willful claims

Under the contemporaneous §1681n, a consumer reporting agency or user that willfully fails to comply with an applicable requirement concerning the consumer is liable through separately specified remedial components: actual damages sustained as a result of the failure, punitive damages the court may allow, and the prescribed successful-action costs and fees. The actual-damages component measures compensatory recovery. It does not say that the consumer must first sustain compensable actual loss before any willful claim or punitive remedy becomes actionable.

The seven therefore hold that, for the pleaded willful violations in the first two disclosures, liability was actionable when the willful noncompliance occurred, without waiting for the later actual losses Andrews asserts. The separate punitive provision would otherwise be made contingent on a condition its text does not impose. The fact that the ultimate punitive amount is discretionary does not prevent bringing the action when the violation is complete. A requested punitive award is not itself proof of willfulness or entitlement; it identifies a statutory remedy available to the alleged complete claim, which must still be proved if timely.

The strongest contrary reading treats the list's reference to a sum including actual damages as making an actual compensatory loss an initial prerequisite. But adding independently specified remedies does not require every component to have a positive amount before the statutory liability exists. The original actual-damages paragraph governs the actual losses attributable to the violation; the punitive paragraph performs its separate work. The post-1996 statutory-damages provision is not needed for this conclusion and does not apply to the events.

This rule resolves the particular late-actual-damages alternative for the pleaded willful claims. It does not rest on a new universal occurrence rule for all federal causes. No new wrongful act or distinct later disclosure is alleged as the basis for reviving the two old claims. Their dates preceded the two-year filing window, and the actual-damages alternative does not change that result. The remand restores the limitations disposition for those claims and proceeds with the other claims at their existing scope.
