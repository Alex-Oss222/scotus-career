## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The courts below treated the borrowing arrangement as a substantive sham notwithstanding real policies and actual payments. Winn-Dixie argues that Congress deliberately drew an objective four-of-seven line and that applying an additional judicial restriction nullifies the permitted exception. It also disputes a rule that would condemn ordinary tax-motivated investment. The Commissioner answers that §264(c)(1) merely removes one disallowance and does not confer deductibility on borrowing engineered to produce a tax loss without independent economic purpose. The dispute does not require condemning every transaction with a tax motive or denying Congress’s power to confer intentional incentives.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could hold that satisfying §264(c)(1) leaves the ordinary substantive-sham inquiry intact and that this undisputed arrangement fails it. A narrower remand could correct an erroneous legal formulation if the Tax Court had equated any tax motive with sham, but the accepted findings are materially stronger than that. The rule’s scope should distinguish this negative-pretax-return borrowing from economically substantive insurance and congressionally intended tax incentives.
