## 123. Stewart v. Smith

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-28, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Ninth Circuit’s conclusion that the state procedural ruling necessarily depended on federal merits, and remand for proceedings consistent with its independent character.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve the distinction among independence, the adequacy determination already accepted below, and any remaining properly presented excuse for default. Do not grant habeas relief, decide the ineffective-assistance merits, or order execution. Do not reopen settled questions without a procedurally proper basis.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A procedural classification does not itself adjudicate the classified claim

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve the independence ground actually decided below, respecting the authoritative state-law answer and the state order’s full context.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether identifying the right asserted makes this waiver determination dependent on federal merits.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing independent-ground framework to a categorical waiver rule without expanding procedural default into a jurisdictional bar.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Arizona’s rule, as clarified for the relevant time, categorizes the right asserted to determine the necessary form of waiver; it does not require adjudication of the alleged federal violation. The actual state order used omission and rejection of a stated excuse rather than a merits determination, so the Ninth Circuit’s nonindependence rationale fails.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns this rule and order. A different state decision that actually rests primarily on federal merits would require a different independence assessment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A State may distinguish rights that require a personal waiver from claims subject to ordinary procedural omission without deciding whether each underlying claim would prevail. Treating any mention of a constitutional category as a federal merits decision would collapse that distinction. The order’s colorability reference addressed why an office-affiliation conflict supposedly prevented earlier presentation; it did not adjudicate counsel’s performance at sentencing. The federal court must nevertheless keep a valid cause argument separate from the independence conclusion and apply the governing exception rules to the actual presentation. The independent-ground doctrine and the State’s authoritative description of its own rule are decisive. This is a classification inquiry, not balancing the importance of capital review against default. The limited disposition is required because independence was the basis for reversal, adequacy was accepted below, and cause or other preserved exceptions must retain their distinct predicates.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No new personal-waiver category, automatic office-conflict rule, adequacy exception, or ineffective-assistance merits disposition is adopted.


## 124. United States v. Bass

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-28, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the present discovery order, set aside that order and the dependent exclusion of the death-penalty notice, and remand for the first-stage threshold inquiry under United States v. Armstrong.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The District Court conducts the first stage on the existing proffer: whether specific, reliable evidence raises a substantial question of discriminatory effect and purpose at an identified selection stage, whether the missing comparisons are reasonably unavailable to Bass, and which existing records would supply them. Do not reinstate the original order or order production. A later focused order under Rule 16(a)(1)(C) must reach only existing, material and nonprotected records, explain materiality, the inadequacy of less intrusive evidence and confidentiality protections, and compel no new statistics; any sanction for refusing a valid order must be fitted to the violation. Direct no new indictment, and make no guilt, discrimination, or capital-sentencing finding.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A preliminary comparison can be inferential but must be grounded

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply the discovery standard rather than a completed-merits burden; identify the challenged selection stage and the source of disclosure authority.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this statistical presentation supports the order entered, and what United States v. Armstrong requires when it does not.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the governing rule, including Rule 16(a)(1)(C) as United States v. Armstrong construes it, its three stages, its express allowance for grounded statistical and alternative evidence, and its prohibition on treating access difficulty as proof of the missing connection.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The presented aggregate charging counts and plea-outcome figures do not establish the required reliable inference that relevantly comparable defendants of another race received more favorable treatment at the challenged decision, so they cannot support the order entered. Those limits do not defeat a confined first-stage inquiry; whether the missing comparisons are reasonably unavailable, and which existing records would supply them, is for the District Court to determine at that stage. National, circumstantial, and non-name-specific proof can satisfy the threshold in an appropriate case.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding is record-specific. It creates no local-only rule, named-comparator prerequisite, or presumption that racial disparities are harmless. The central Attorney General’s role may make properly framed national evidence relevant.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The charging totals describe who entered the federal system, not the racial distribution and legally material characteristics of people eligible but not charged. Plea outcomes also combine whether an offer was made with whether a defendant accepted it; Bass’s declined offer matters to that inference. The information asymmetry warrants allowing grounded alternative forms of proof, as United States v. Armstrong already does. It does not turn every unequal total into evidence of differential treatment of similarly situated people. The lower court crossed that line when it sustained discovery on the present showing. Armstrong’s staged framework then governs what follows. The survey is the Justice Department’s own, its national scope matches the Attorney General’s centralized authorization decision, and the comparisons it lacks at that stage concern cases submitted within the Government’s protocol. A court-run first stage can determine whether those comparisons are reasonably unavailable to Bass and identify the existing records that would supply them, without imposing the final merits burden or opening the Government’s files. Reliable data, concrete indications of comparably eligible defendants and a supported reason that missing information lies with the prosecutor can justify a narrow later order; offense, eligibility, place, time and purpose bear on reliability without becoming mandatory checklist items.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No independent purpose deficiency, ultimate discrimination finding, or categorical rule about direct discriminatory admissions is decided.

### Issue 2: Correct the dependent sanction without foreclosing a lawful inquiry

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The present death-notice exclusion rests on refusal of the challenged order.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What follows when the discovery demand lacks the required foundation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply United States v. Armstrong’s controlling rules that an unsupported order and its dependent sanction fall together, and that failure of an order entered without the framework’s findings does not end the inquiry the framework prescribes.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An unsupported discovery demand cannot sustain the dependent death-notice exclusion. Setting both aside leads to the first-stage inquiry on remand and does not entitle the Government to avoid all constitutional scrutiny.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The mandate sets aside the present orders and directs the first stage without directing production or determining any capital punishment.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A sanction enforces an existing obligation; it cannot create one after the defendant has failed to establish the required basis. Setting it aside therefore follows from the discovery error. Equally, treating this failure as proof that no discrimination occurred would decide a different question on an inadequate record. The first-stage remand preserves both limits, and any sanction for refusing a later valid order must be fitted to the violation rather than imposed automatically.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The remedy for proved discrimination and the particular sanction appropriate for refusing a valid discovery order remain open.
