## 13. Toyota Motor Manufacturing, Kentucky, Inc. v. Williams - reconciled

### Operative clean handoff and review scope

The affected neutral validation and frozen scope commitments supersede only the earlier Toyota handoff. They establish that review concerns Williams's favorable partial summary judgment under the manual-tasks theory. Toyota's request to reinstate its own summary judgment is outside the accepted question. That limit is preserved independently of the merits analysis. No functional fact has changed, no excluded coverage branch is reopened, and no final employer entitlement may be ordered in this review.

The complete historical report, 534 U.S. 184 to 203, supplies strong comparator evidence of every Associate's judgment and manual-task construction. Its unanimity is not an assignment instruction or an independent source of current law. Reconciliation separately tests the statutory substantiality threshold, the nature of the manual-task activity, duration, the treatment of workplace evidence, mitigation and the precise remand.

### Final judgment and common propositions

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer all reverse Williams's partial summary judgment and remand. All eight directly join the following propositions, including their qualifications:

1. Under the original ADA, substantial limitation in performing manual tasks requires an impairment that prevents or severely restricts the individual from performing tasks central to most people's daily lives. The impairment's impact must be permanent or long term. The assessment remains individual: diagnosis alone is insufficient, and the nature, severity and duration of this person's underlying limitation matter. The rule demands a considerable functional limitation, not complete inability or loss of virtually every manual ability; it sets no numerical duration threshold.
2. A working-specific class-of-jobs formula cannot establish substantial limitation in the distinct major life activity of performing manual tasks. Workplace evidence remains relevant insofar as it establishes limitation of the general manual functions the activity encompasses. Inability to perform repetitive overhead work or specialized assembly tasks for prolonged periods is not, simply because those tasks form an occupational class, sufficient to establish this disability. Ordinary household and self-care evidence is also relevant and cannot be disregarded. Ability to perform some such tasks is not a categorical defense; their actual extent and the other relevant manual limitations must be considered under the stated demanding standard.
3. Sutton and Murphy supply the controlling mitigation rule: assess the individual's underlying substantial limitation without treating beneficial correction or medication as eliminating it. Apply the manual-task threshold to that legally defined condition. Individual proof remains necessary. Coverage is distinct from qualification, accommodation and safety. The Court does not revive historical Sutton's actual-treated-state construction or historical Kirkingburg's compensation-for-impairment method.
4. Williams's appellate partial judgment cannot stand because the occupational-task shortcut does not establish the required substantial limitation as a matter of law, and the appellate treatment disregarded relevant nonwork function. The lower courts must reassess that judgment under the corrected manual-task rule, the controlling mitigation baseline and ordinary summary-judgment standards. The scope of review independently prevents reinstatement of Toyota's separate summary judgment. Neither party receives a final Supreme Court disability determination.

The daily-life formulation is specific to the manual-task activity being asserted. It does not amend Bragdon by restricting every possible major life activity to something performed each day or by excluding reproduction. Likewise, permanent or long-term impact describes the functional impact required here, not a demand that a medical diagnosis itself be incurable. A claimant need not stop treatment to establish the underlying limitation. The original statute and contemporaneous rules govern; later amendments do not enter.

### Why the provisional broader formulation changes

The original and scope-refreshed commitments correctly rejected an automatic job-class finding, diagnosis-only coverage, complete incapacity and a categorical ban on workplace evidence. Those points survive. They did not, however, supply a concrete Justice-specific reason to replace the historical severe-restriction, central-daily-task and permanent-or-long-term criteria with a general inquiry into any important manual function and duration as only an unconstrained consideration.

The changed mitigation baseline matters to what functional condition is assessed. It removes reliance on the historical Sutton proposition that successful correction defeats actual-disability coverage. It also prevents reliance on that decision's population estimate as a reason to reconstruct the rejected mitigation rule. But the meaning of substantial limitation in a particular major life activity is a separate statutory question. Nothing in simulated Sutton or Murphy holds that a minor or short-lived limitation is substantial, that any important work task defines the manual-task activity, or that an enduring-impact requirement is forbidden. Bragdon's individual substantiality holding rejects complete inability while preserving a meaningful threshold. Simulated Kirkingburg supplies no disability-method holding that can be cited for this purpose; the manual-task rule must rest on the original statute and the relevant contemporaneous regulatory guidance, not a nonexistent simulated proposition.

The historical opinion supplies independent grounds that remain available: substantial denotes a considerable limitation; major distinguishes general manual abilities central to ordinary life from isolated specialized tasks; the Act's common disability definition is not limited to employment; and the contemporaneous duration guidance addresses expected duration and permanent or long-term impact. The historical comparator shows all eight Associates accepting those grounds, including the two historical Sutton dissenters. None of the inherited public positions supplies a contrary manual-task or duration holding. Accordingly, a different mitigation precedent alone does not justify the unanimous broader departure in the provisional handoff. Reconciliation corrects that unsupported extension while preserving the genuinely changed governing mitigation rule.

The formal judgment also changes from provisional vacatur to reversal of the employee's partial judgment, followed by remand. The judgment is erroneous under the rule applied by all eight. No changed record, remedial entitlement or review scope requires departure from the historical reversal form. This is not reinstatement of the employer's judgment and not a determination that Williams can never satisfy the Act. Both the substance and the exact scope, rather than terminology alone, have been checked.

### Alternatives, precedent treatment and remand

Williams's strongest argument is that sustained gripping and movement can be important general manual capacities even when some self-care remains possible. The final rule accepts that such evidence can matter; it rejects the inference that a class of demanding occupational tasks itself establishes disability as a matter of law. Toyota's strongest argument is the appellate category error and disregard of daily functions. That warrants reversal of Williams's partial judgment, not the excluded reinstatement of Toyota's judgment. A near-total-incapacity formulation would overstate substantiality; an outside-work-only rule would discard relevant proof; a general important-task rule without the central-activity and enduring-impact requirements lacks the necessary support for departure.

Sutton and Murphy are applied for the underlying-impairment baseline and the separate occupational-breadth rules; their working rules are not extended to all major activities. Bragdon is applied for individual proof and the distinction between substantial restriction and complete inability; its reproduction and direct-threat holdings remain intact. Kirkingburg retains its distinct safety-qualification holding and supplies no additional manual-task method. The original ADA's common disability definition and the contemporaneous manual-task/duration guidance supply the present construction. No general binding-agency-authority ruling is necessary or adopted merely by consulting that guidance.

On remand the lower courts reconsider the reviewed manual-task partial judgment under the stated rule and ordinary procedural standards. They may conduct further proceedings as otherwise warranted. The remand creates no new coverage claim, reopens no independently unreviewed judgment or forfeited branch, and directs no mandatory trial, hearing, new evidence, employment award or final disability finding. Qualification, accommodation, safety and other matters retain their existing procedural status and independent elements. No special mandate timing is supplied.

**Historical departure:** All eight Associates apply simulated Sutton and Murphy's binding underlying-impairment rule, so beneficial correction or medication cannot eliminate the substantial limitation being assessed. That changed precedent alters the mitigation premise of the historical analysis; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer preserve their own controlling coverage joins, while O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas follow the governing rule despite their published dissents. It does not alter their independently supported manual-task threshold or the reversal and limited remand.
