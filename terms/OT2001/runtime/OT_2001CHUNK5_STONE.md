## 65. Verizon Communications Inc. v. FCC

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the recognition of forward-looking pricing authority, reverse the invalidation of the challenged efficient-cost methodology and additional bounded combination rules, and remand for proceedings consistent with their statutory limits.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the challenged regulations against these facial objections. Preserve challenges to particular rate results and disputes over feasibility, compensation, and whether an element must be made available. Award no carrier damages or guaranteed historical-cost recovery.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Efficient-cost pricing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach statutory methodology review; reject a facial confiscation conclusion unsupported by a complete rate record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the delegated cost-setting authority permits an efficient forward-looking estimate tied to existing wire centers.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board’s allocation of FCC authority and propose a new holding on the previously open methodology.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The statutory cost language permits the FCC’s forward-looking efficient-technology benchmark, including reasonable common costs and profit, because cost need not mean reimbursement of the incumbent’s historical investment. The method remains reviewable for statutory fidelity and actual unconstitutional rate effects.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding validates this method’s legal availability, not every input, state commission calculation, or resulting charge.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board establishes the FCC's implementing authority but leaves the particular cost method for this case. Mead requires an actual delegated lawmaking function; the Act's cost-setting assignment and formal regulations provide that function here. Reading cost in the enacted competitive-access scheme does not compel reimbursement of historical monopoly investment. An efficient technology model anchored to existing wire centers, with common costs and a reasonable profit, is a permitted policy choice. Stone combines statutory interpretation with evaluation of the technical design: text and delegated scope are decisive on authority, and reasoned expert judgment receives substantial weight within that scope. A competing accounting policy does not prove illegality. The facial statutory challenge can be resolved without calculating individual rates or adjudicating a confiscation claim lacking a concrete financial record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual confiscation requires a particular rate and financial record absent here.

### Issue 2: Assembly needed for usable access

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the additional combination rules as a separable regulatory question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the statutory duty can include compensated technical work necessary to deliver usable authorized elements.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing access framework and propose an additional implementation holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The FCC may require technically feasible, compensated combination work under the challenged conditions when needed to make authorized access usable without impairing the incumbent’s operation or disadvantaging other competitors.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Existing-element eligibility and necessity-and-impairment limits remain independently operative.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 251(c)(3)'s duty to provide access in a manner allowing combination would be ineffective if an incumbent could furnish unusable fragments while withholding work only it can practicably perform. Stone extends the authorized-access principle beyond the existing-combination rule, subject to the challenged regulations' feasibility, requested-access, compensation and operational conditions. The statutory connection to usable access is decisive; a general desire to assist competitors is not a sufficient source. AT&T's independent necessity-and-impairment limits on which elements must be supplied remain fully operative. The ruling validates this implementation tool, while actual engineering and compensation disputes need transaction-specific findings and do not justify denying the legal power categorically.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Particular engineering disputes and element-eligibility determinations lack the necessary transaction-specific facts here.

## 66. Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union

* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it treats the community-standards feature as sufficient by itself, and remand without dissolving the preliminary injunction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve interim protection while the lower courts assess the complete offense, its adult-speech burden, and the unresolved grounds under the proper standard. Do not authorize prosecution merely because one facial argument fails.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The whole statutory restriction must be assessed

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the reviewed community-standards question, but not the unadjudicated ultimate validity of the entire enactment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether geographic variation alone proves that this statute unconstitutionally suppresses protected expression.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing protection of adult Internet expression and propose a specific holding rejecting a categorical medium-based inference.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Incorporating community standards into this multi-element commercial-Web offense does not, standing alone, establish facial invalidity. Constitutional review must assess the offense’s actual reach, objective limiting elements, defenses, and demonstrated burdens together. That conclusion supplies no presumption that the complete enactment survives.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns the claimed independent defect in COPA. It does not approve criminal punishment for protected adult speech or imply that an Internet speaker can practicably segregate every community.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reno protects adult Internet speech against a broad criminal restriction, but it does not establish that every reference to community standards independently invalidates a different multi-element law. Stone distinguishes that proposed categorical inference. Miller's objective serious-value limitation and COPA's actual statutory elements and defenses must be considered with the geographic burden; their existence is not a finding that COPA ultimately survives strict scrutiny. The legal function of each element is decisive on the limited question before the Court. A combined assessment is required because one phrase cannot be assessed as though it were the whole offense. The remaining tailoring and alternative-means grounds were not resolved by the appellate rationale, so the preliminary injunction remains while those grounds are decided. No new speech exception or presumption for commercial Web restrictions is announced.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Full statutory validity, the factual adequacy of age verification, and the effectiveness of alternative protections were not finally adjudicated in the reviewed appellate rationale.

## 67. Lapides v. Board of Regents of University System of Georgia

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit’s immunity ruling for the state-law claims Georgia permits in its own courts, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Board may not defeat those claims by asserting immunity from the federal forum it selected. The district court must independently resolve federal cause-of-action defects, supplemental jurisdiction, and the merits. No damages, reinstatement, or correction of personnel records is ordered by the waiver holding.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Actual submission to the selected forum

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach waiver for the state-law claims, keeping statutory defendant status and the individual defendants' defenses distinct.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the State’s voluntary selection of a federal forum is compatible with an immunity defense based solely on that forum.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinction between actual litigation submission and constructive commercial consent; propose the open removal-waiver holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A State that voluntarily removes claims it allows to be litigated against it in its own courts relinquishes its immunity objection to their adjudication in the federal forum it chose. Removal does not create substantive liability, repair an invalid cause of action, or surrender unrelated defenses.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the state tort claims and the State’s own authorized removal. It does not infer consent from receiving regulatory benefits or conducting business.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Clark and Gunter recognize waiver through actual submission to adjudication; College Savings Bank rejects a different fiction of consent from ordinary regulated activity. Stone extends the former rule to authorized removal and distinguishes the latter. Georgia deliberately selected the federal tribunal for claims it permits against itself in state court, making its procedural choice decisive. Letting it then invoke forum immunity would use removal to extinguish adjudication rather than relocate it. This is a categorical litigation consequence within a defined class, justified by coherent administration and actual consent rather than a balance of governmental dignity against the plaintiff's injury. Will's separate statutory defendant rule remains, and supplemental jurisdiction must still be exercised lawfully. No broader rule for claims immune in the State's own courts is necessary to resolve these tort claims.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Removal of claims for which the State retains substantive immunity in its own courts is outside the necessary question. Individual liability and the tort merits require separate adjudication.

## 68. Voting Integrity Project, Inc. v. Keisling +

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment rejecting the election-day preemption claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave Oregon’s receipt-by-election-day mail-voting system operative against this challenge. Order no restructuring of state election machinery and grant no relief concerning ballot receipt after the deadline, a question not presented.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Final selection rather than each preliminary act

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the federal statutory question; no constitutional voting restriction is necessary to the judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress’s uniform date prohibits ballots cast in advance for a selection completed on election day.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Foster v. Love’s operative distinction and propose its further application to Oregon’s system and the relevant federal offices.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Federal election-day statutes do not preempt advance casting of ballots where the State’s election remains open and the final legal selection is consummated on the prescribed day. Oregon’s system, requiring receipt by that day and not fixing an earlier winner, satisfies that requirement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the challenged universal mail procedure as well as the materially equivalent advance-ballot feature. It does not permit early final selection or decide a different post-deadline receipt system.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Foster distinguishes the final selection of officials from preliminary electoral acts. Stone applies that enacted distinction to Oregon's advance ballots, with the statutory timing rule decisive. Voting remains open until the federally prescribed day; counting and final selection are not moved to an earlier binding election. Earlier ballot preparation or casting therefore does not establish the conflict that existed in Foster. The interpretation preserves both Congress's uniform date and state authority over the means of receiving votes. Practical access reinforces the reading but does not create an exception to federal law. This is a safe harbor for advance casting within an election completed on the designated day, not a decision about ballots first received afterward or about a genuinely earlier final selection.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Post-election ballot receipt, specific ballot disputes, and independent constitutional claims lie outside the question presented.

## 69. United States v. Cotton

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit judgment: the omission is nonjurisdictional, and this forfeited error does not satisfy the fourth requirement for plain-error correction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for further proceedings consistent with rejection of the stated vacatur grounds. Leave the enhanced sentences undisturbed on this claim; do not treat the decision as a new jury finding, conviction, or validation of a preserved-error procedure.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Nonjurisdictional error and bounded appellate consequence

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach jurisdiction first, then the forfeited constitutional sentencing claim. Treat the omission as error and assume the substantial-rights requirement without turning that assumption into a finding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the charging defect destroys judicial power and, if not, whether this record requires discretionary correction.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Apprendi, Olano, and Johnson. Propose clarification that contrary jurisdictional language in Ex parte Bain does not survive for an element omission in a federal prosecution otherwise within statutory jurisdiction.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Omission of the aggravating quantity does not eliminate subject-matter jurisdiction. Although the enhanced sentences lack the necessary jury authorization, an unpreserved error supported by overwhelming and uncontroverted threshold-quantity evidence need not be corrected under Rule 52(b)'s fourth requirement on this record. The error remains constitutional error.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns a forfeited element omission and this evidentiary setting. It does not authorize sentencing judges to disregard Apprendi or reduce every constitutional defect to a prediction of guilt.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Subject-matter jurisdiction comes from the statutory criminal grant, not flawless pleading; Stone would overrule Ex parte Bain only to the extent it makes this element omission jurisdictional. That correction preserves the grand jury's constitutional role while putting remedial review under Rule 52(b), where settled doctrine locates forfeited errors. Johnson's 1997 judgment is a close analogy, not a universal overwhelming-evidence exception. Here the record of the conspiracy, the threshold quantities, notice of the enhanced exposure and absence of a material quantity contest support the conclusion that this unpreserved error does not seriously impair the fairness, integrity or public reputation of the proceeding. Those actual circumstances are decisive under the fourth condition; no substitute jury finding is made and substantial-rights impact remains assumed. Preserved errors retain Neder's distinct actual-verdict inquiry. Neither a changed legal classification nor this remedy legitimizes future omission of a required element.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preserved error, contested quantity, intentional waiver, and an entirely missing indictment are outside this record.

## 70. Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission of Maryland / United States v. Public Service Commission of Maryland

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand because ordinary federal-question jurisdiction and the prospective officer-relief route permit the challenge to proceed.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate adjudication of the federal-law claim against the responsible commissioners. Determine the merits and shape any injunction only after adjudication. Do not order payment between carriers, damages from Maryland, or a blanket waiver by the commission.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A forum for the alleged ongoing federal violation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide jurisdiction and the available officer-relief route. Do not adjudicate the underlying compensation question or direct commission waiver where unnecessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this substantial federal claim can be heard and prospectively remedied despite the State’s immunity objection.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinction among jurisdiction, statutory authority, consent, and remedy; propose the specific telecommunications review holding left open by prior holdings.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Telecommunications Act does not exclude general federal-question jurisdiction over this claim. A suit to prevent commissioners' continuing enforcement of an order alleged to violate federal law may proceed against those officers for genuinely prospective relief. The intercarrier financial consequences do not make it a damages action against the State.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule applies to a substantial federal challenge and responsible officers enforcing an ongoing order. It does not federalize every contract dispute or supply a cause against every state agency.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 1331 supplies jurisdiction over the substantial federal claim unless Congress displaces it; §252's review provision does not do so. Ex parte Young permits relief against officers enforcing an allegedly unlawful ongoing order, and Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene's distinct sovereign-title setting does not convert intercarrier payments into state-treasury damages. Statutory jurisdiction and the prospective character of relief are decisive, with each source doing its separate work. Stone thus answers that the complaint may proceed without relying on constructive consent from participation or adjudicating the compensation merits. Because those sufficient grounds dispose of the barrier below, a broader construction of §252(e)(6) and direct commission waiver would add no necessary relief. The lower court must decide federal compliance and tailor any injunction, not presume Verizon wins the underlying dispute.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Direct commission waiver is unnecessary to the disposition. The correct treatment of Internet-bound traffic and the exact injunction depend on merits issues not resolved at this threshold stage.

## 71. Alabama v. Shelton

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment invalidating the suspended imprisonment while leaving the conviction and otherwise lawful noncustodial consequences intact.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The uncounseled conviction may not be used to activate this jail term. Remand for any necessary state-law adjustment consistent with that limit. Do not order acquittal, damages, or invalidation of a distinct lawful probation disposition not adjudicated here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Counsel at the adjudication that authorizes jail

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the suspended-imprisonment question on the established absence of valid waiver.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether trial counsel is required when the original conviction authorizes a jail term whose execution is deferred.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing counsel-and-imprisonment principle and propose its specific suspended-sentence application; do not extend Nichols beyond its valid fine-only setting.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not impose a suspended jail term for an offense tried without constitutionally adequate counsel or a valid waiver, because activation executes punishment for that original offense. Counsel at an ordinary revocation hearing cannot cure the absence of counsel when guilt and the imprisonment authorization were adjudicated.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers a sentence directly authorizing imprisonment, including deferred execution. It does not require appointment for every fine-only prosecution or decide a new charge tried with counsel.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Argersinger and Scott tie appointed counsel to imprisonment for the offense; Nichols preserves a valid fine-only conviction without answering whether the original court may impose jail subject to deferred execution. Stone extends the imprisonment rule to that direct authorization. The decisive point is the source of the eventual incarceration: activation executes the assault sentence, not a new sentence for independently tried misconduct. Counsel at revocation cannot reconstruct lost investigation, advice and trial choices concerning guilt. This categorical boundary follows the Sixth Amendment's allocation of assistance at the critical adjudication, rather than balancing each defendant's apparent guilt against administrative expense. The State remains free to select a lawful noncustodial sentence or provide counsel. Stand-alone probation and a genuinely new counseled prosecution are different arrangements not before the Court.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent probation without a suspended term is not the sentencing arrangement presented. A genuine new counseled trial before imprisonment would require a different procedural record.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the facial Establishment Clause challenge under Agostini, Chaudhuri and the independent coercion rules.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the neutral minute of silence in force. Do not authorize teacher-led prayer or substantial religious pressure. No remedial monitoring program is ordered absent a proved implementation violation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Private activity within a nondevotional interval

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the facial claim on the statute and implementation record. Do not decide a hypothetical classroom violation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether requiring silence while leaving the individual's activity private constitutes an official religious exercise.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Chaudhuri and the government-attribution and coercion distinctions in Agostini, Lee and Santa Fe.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A daily public-school minute requiring silence but no religious act, expression, or disclosure, with genuine freedom to pray, reflect, or do neither, does not establish religion. This statute's permission of private prayer and some legislators' religious motives do not convert its neutral required conduct into official worship.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the facial requirement and neutral implementation described here. It does not protect teacher-directed devotion, religious evaluation of students, or substantial pressure to pray.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Agostini's government-function inquiry, Chaudhuri's treatment of silence and Good News Club's distinction between private expression and governmental adoption control the analysis. Stone applies them without adding an independent Lemon-purpose veto that current doctrine no longer supplies. The statute requires silence and genuine private choice, not prayer, religious disclosure or participation in an official exercise. Religious statements by some sponsors remain evidence of actual purpose and operation, but on this facial record they do not turn the required conduct into worship. Function and attribution carry decisive weight, with Lee and Santa Fe independently preserving the prohibition on official devotion and substantial school-created religious pressure. This limited holding protects silent nonreligious activity as fully as silent prayer; it authorizes no teacher direction of either belief or disbelief.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A particular coercive classroom practice requires facts not established in this facial challenge.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss the writ of certiorari as improvidently granted because petitioners seek review only of nonbinding reasoning in a judgment favorable to them.

### Issue 1: No advisory correction of nonbinding reasoning

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve appellate aggrievement first. Do not reach the statutory or immunity merits because the petitioners prevailed and the disputed findings have no established binding consequence.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a favorable judgment can be reviewed solely because its unnecessary reasoning is unwelcome.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the favorable-judgment rule of New York Telephone Co. v. Maltbie; do not reach the telecommunications questions or announce that all prevailing-party appeals are constitutionally barred.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A party that received a favorable judgment and identifies only unnecessary, nonbinding adverse findings is not entitled to appellate review for the purpose of revising those findings. The importance of the underlying issue does not supply an adverse judgment.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers this request to edit reasoning. It does not foreclose a prevailing party's challenge to an actual adverse judgment component or independently binding collateral effect.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** New York Telephone's favorable-judgment rule resolves the petition. These commissioners obtained the operative judgment they sought and identify only unnecessary findings lacking binding effect in later litigation. Stone applies that threshold rule and dismisses the writ as improvidently granted, rather than pronouncing an unnecessarily broad Article III prohibition on every appeal by a prevailing party. The judgment's legal consequences are decisive; disagreeable reasoning alone supplies no relief for this Court to grant. Deciding the three telecommunications questions here would add an advisory resolution to a case that does not require one. A genuinely adverse component or binding collateral consequence would present a materially different appellate question. The lower judgment remains undisturbed, and its unnecessary reasoning receives no endorsement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** All three substantive questions are unnecessary once this threshold defect is resolved. No opinion on their merits is proposed here.

## 74. Bell v. Cone

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit’s grant of habeas relief because these particular omissions do not trigger presumed prejudice and the state adjudication does not meet the statutory standard for relief on this record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand consistently with denial of relief on this ineffective-assistance ground. Preserve separate claims only if independently pending and legally available; do not invent such claims or disturb the convictions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Particular omissions within active representation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the Cronic categorization and the state adjudication under the proper collateral standard.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these sentencing choices constitute total adversarial abandonment and, if not, whether they justify federal collateral relief under Strickland and AEDPA.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinction between complete denial and particular errors, together with Williams v. Taylor's unreasonable-application rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A represented capital sentencing proceeding does not become a complete absence of adversarial testing merely because counsel presents no additional witnesses and waives closing, where counsel relies on evidence already introduced, contests aggravation, and makes a supported tactical choice about rebuttal. Those decisions remain subject to Strickland, and this state adjudication is not objectively unreasonable on the established record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding addresses this active representation and documented strategic account. It does not excuse abandonment, uninformed investigation, or a categorical refusal to present available mitigation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Cronic supplies a narrow presumption for a complete loss of meaningful adversarial assistance; Strickland governs particular failures within representation. Cone's lawyer relied on mitigation already introduced, contested the aggravation evidence and waived closing to avoid a stronger prosecutorial rebuttal. Those concrete acts defeat a characterization of the entire sentencing phase as abandonment, while leaving the quality of each choice open to Strickland review. Under Terry Williams, objective unreasonableness remains distinct from mere disagreement and the full supported mitigation record must be examined. The state adjudication used a permissible standard and made a record-supported assessment of these decisions; the proposed Cronic shortcut does not overcome §2254(d). Statutory collateral limits are decisive after correct classification of the Sixth Amendment claim. Stone therefore denies relief on this ground without treating all tactical explanations as conclusive or importing later categorical restrictions on habeas fact development.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A claim based on additional uninvestigated evidence would require evidence not established on this record. No new mitigation facts are assumed.

## 75. Festo Corp. v. Shoketsu Kinzoku Kogyo Kabushiki Co.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the Federal Circuit’s categorical complete-bar judgment and remand for limitation-specific application of a rebuttable surrender rule.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Apply the estoppel trigger to narrowing required for any patentability requirement, including disclosure. Require Festo to show why the asserted equivalent falls outside the surrender. Only then determine equivalence and any remaining infringement or damages issues. No damages amount or finding of infringement is ordered.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A legally consequential but bounded amendment

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach both the trigger and the scope of estoppel because the categorical rule controls the judgment under review.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What a required narrowing amendment concedes, and whether the concession can exclude less than every equivalent of the amended limitation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Warner-Jenkinson’s existing amendment burden and limitation-specific approach, and propose a more explicit rebuttable-surrender rule for the point left open.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Narrowing required to secure compliance with the Patent Act presumptively surrenders the territory excluded by the amendment, including when the requirement concerns disclosure. The patentee may establish that a particular asserted equivalent was not reasonably foreseeable, bears only a tangential relation to the amendment’s rationale, or could not reasonably have been described within the claim at that time. An amendment does not create an irrebuttable bar to every equivalent.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs required narrowing and the affected claim limitation. Voluntary explanatory changes require their own prosecution analysis. A patentee still cannot erase a limitation or recover expressly relinquished subject matter.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Warner-Jenkinson preserves equivalents and treats amendment explanations as legally consequential, but it did not turn every narrowing amendment into surrender of every conceivable equivalent. Stone reaffirms those principles and extends their limitation-specific application through a rebuttable presumption. The applicant bears the burden because the applicant chose and explained the narrower claim; the presumption ties public notice to that actual concession. Unforeseeability, tangential relation and inability reasonably to describe the equivalent are legally relevant rebuttal grounds, not a free-ranging fairness balance. The decisive inquiry is what the amendment reasonably surrendered at the time. A complete bar would destroy settled patent reliance beyond that surrender, while an unstructured exception would defeat competitors' notice. The two asserted ring and sleeve equivalents require application to their particular amendments; this supplies a future rule without deciding infringement facts not yet resolved under it.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The actual status of each asserted equivalent requires amendment-specific evidence and findings. That record-dependent application, and any infringement remedy, belongs on remand.

## 76. In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation +

* **Approval Status:** Approved.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical evidence restriction and remand to confine any injunction to actual prosecution or relitigation of matters disposed of by the federal judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the Florida action concerning expressly excluded policies to proceed subject to ordinary state evidentiary rules and the enforceable release of other claims. The district court may identify and prohibit an actual attempt to recover on released claims within the statutory exception. No award on the retained claims, admission of a particular document, or reopening of the settlement follows.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Finality without extinguishing the preserved action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the Anti-Injunction Act and the scope of the operative order. Assume only the settlement’s established validity as to the released policies, not universal federal control of related proof.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the judgment-protection exception authorizes the categorical exclusion of released-policy evidence from claims expressly preserved by the settlement.

* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A settlement-enforcement court may prevent prosecution of released claims within the Anti-Injunction Act’s exceptions, but may not bar evidence in a separately retained claim merely because the evidence concerns a released transaction. It must identify the claim or conclusively resolved issue whose relitigation the injunction prevents and tailor the order to that adjudicative conflict.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers policy-specific exclusions and evidence offered for those preserved claims. It does not permit another recovery on a released policy or invalidate a demonstrated contractual restriction through a holding unrelated to its terms.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Anti-Injunction Act's exceptions are the controlling statutory boundary; settlement-management convenience cannot enlarge them. Baker distinguishes respect for a judgment from control of another tribunal's evidence, and Matsushita protects an actually binding release without erasing claims expressly retained. Stone applies those principles to the policy-specific exclusions. Evidence of a released transaction may help prove a different retained claim without seeking a second recovery or reopening a decided issue. The federal court must therefore identify the particular claim or issue being relitigated, including any genuine threat to jurisdiction, rather than prohibit proof merely because its subject touches the settlement. Finality and federalism support that textual limit but do not replace it. A narrower injunction may prevent an actual end run around the release; admissibility for the preserved Florida claims remains with that court under its ordinary rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Admissibility of particular evidence and the merits of the excluded-policy claims require proffers and state-law determinations outside this record. No reopening of the settled claims is authorized.

* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Powell’s surviving §1983 holding and reconcile it with Sandoval’s express partial overruling.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** No. Section 601’s intent protection and §602’s agency-directed implementation provisions do not create the broader individual effects right asserted under §1983. * **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion concerns the asserted regulation-created effects right; it does not foreclose enforcement of an independently conferred individual statutory right or a preserved intentional-discrimination claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Text, statutory structure and the precise treatment of precedent are decisive. The source of an action and the source of a right under another remedial statute are distinct legal questions. Treating any valid regulation as a new §1983 right would remove Congress’s role in defining the entitlement; treating Powell’s original direct-action bar as unchanged would disregard Sandoval’s actual holding. * **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No new damages remedy for disparate impact is presented.

### Issue 2: The prospective claim must receive its own adjudication

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The action began with the direct Title VI theory; the intervening legal premise used to abandon that route is incompatible with the governing law. Its pending procedural footing must be applied without inventing a new unrelated claim.

* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A statutory beneficiary may seek prospective compliance by a covered recipient with a valid and sufficiently definite EPA §602 requirement. * **Reach Within the Question:** This permits adjudication of the identified permitting claim; it does not impose an unlegislated environmental equality code, presume intentional discrimination or compel a particular permit outcome.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The decisive rule is the authorized statutory channel. The agency’s administration of a funded program must be measured against the actual regulation, including legally relevant cumulative effects if that duty reaches them. The private action protects those the statute benefits without giving the court general permitting authority. Remand is necessary for unresolved coverage, regulatory and equitable determinations; it does not reserve whether this category of prospective action exists.
