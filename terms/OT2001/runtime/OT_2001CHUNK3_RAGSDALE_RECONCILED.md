## Full historical comparator and material premises

The syllabus establishes the argument date but supplies no substitute for either writing’s reasoning.

The historical Court opinion separates the unadjudicated validity of individualized notice from the categorical consequence of missing designation. It finds the latter inconsistent with the total entitlement and the remedial inquiry into a violation affecting the employee, loss caused by it, and appropriate equitable relief. It acknowledges that missed notice can actually impair an employee’s use of intermittent leave. Its consideration of administrative convenience, the reasonableness of a categorical approximation, the twelve-week compromise, the separate posted-notice penalty, and encouragement of more generous policies supports the invalidity conclusion without establishing a rule against all protective regulations.

O’Connor’s dissent answers the validity of individualized notice affirmatively, then directly confronts the argument that the only permissible response must be an individually proved section 2615 injury. It views the implementation delegation as permitting effective enforcement of the notice requirement and finds the designation consequence reasonable because notice permits informed planning and use of the Act’s options. The dissent recognizes that ordinary recovery under section 2617 requires proof, but rejects treating that remedial arrangement as necessarily foreclosing a categorical regulatory enforcement consequence. It discusses the difficulty of reconstructing lost planning opportunities, the manageable designation obligation and provisional designation, and treats the greater-policy encouragement as an interpretive principle rather than an absolute prohibition. Its full reasoning, rather than a generalized pro-agency preference, matters especially for Breyer.

## Effective reconciled commitments

## 42. Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc. — March 19, 2002

### Posture, facts and separable questions

On certiorari in No. 00-6029, 535 U.S. 81, the Court reviews the Eighth Circuit's affirmance of summary judgment against Ragsdale's FMLA claim. The legal question is whether the Secretary may condition concurrent counting of qualifying company leave against the statutory twelve weeks on timely designation, and whether the challenged automatic consequence fits the statute's individual-remedy conditions. The underlying individualized-notice duty and the automatic no-count consequence are distinct; rejecting the latter does not invalidate every notice rule.

Ragsdale started employment March 17, 1995 and began cancer leave February 21, 1996. The district court treated her as eligible as of her first extension on March 18, 1996, not when leave began. She received seven months of company leave with extensions, was discharged September 20, and sought further FMLA leave or reduced hours on September 26. Her physician released her in December. The amicus description of the parties' apparent assumption that she could return after seven months plus twelve weeks is not a finding of earlier work capacity, reliance, a medically necessary reduced schedule or actual loss caused by missing notice. Leave was apparently unpaid; that characterization is not converted into a finding. No retroactive-designation exception is found satisfied.

The ordinary nine-member roster applies; no recusal is established. The eight Associates below are assessed independently. The official report verifies argument on January 7, 2002. The source cutoff is March 19, 2002. ### Enacted rules and exact qualifications

Section 2612(a) grants an eligible employee a total of twelve workweeks in a twelve-month period for the listed family or medical reasons, subject to certification under section 2613. A serious health condition must make the employee unable to perform the position's functions. Unpaid leave is permitted. Birth/placement leave expires at twelve months; intermittent or reduced-schedule birth/placement leave requires agreement, while serious-health-condition leave may be intermittent or reduced when medically necessary, subject to the specified certification and scheduling provisions. A temporary alternative position for foreseeable planned-treatment leave must be available, one for which the employee is qualified, carry equivalent pay and benefits, and better accommodate recurring leave.

Section 2612(d)(2) permits substitution of the enumerated accrued paid leave, at employee election or employer requirement, within the statutory period. The serious-health-condition provision does not require paid medical or sick leave where the employer would not normally provide it. This text does not establish that Ragsdale's leave was paid. The employee's thirty-day foreseeable-notice requirement has a practicability exception when the birth, placement or treatment requires an earlier start; planned treatment also requires reasonable scheduling efforts, subject to the health-care provider's approval. These employee obligations are distinct from the disputed employer designation rule.

Section 2615 prohibits interference with, restraint of or denial of the exercise or attempted exercise of statutory rights, and separately protects opposition and participation. Section 2617(a) makes an employer violating section 2615 liable to an eligible employee affected: compensation denied or lost by reason of the violation, or, absent such compensation loss, actual monetary loss directly resulting from it, subject to the twelve-week wage/salary ceiling for that latter category. Interest and equal liquidated damages follow the specified amount; a court may reduce liquidated liability when the employer proves both good faith and reasonable grounds. Appropriate equitable relief, including employment, reinstatement and promotion, remains distinct. Monetary loss is not imposed as an invariable prerequisite for every equitable remedy, but an actionable violation affecting the employee and appropriate relief remain necessary. The fee provision follows a plaintiff's judgment. The Secretary-action termination provisions and their voluntary-without-prejudice-dismissal exception are not triggered by any supplied fact.

Section 2619 requires posted notices and permits a civil penalty for a willful violation; it is not the individualized designation rule. Sections 2652–2653 preserve greater company rights, prohibit diminution of statutory rights and encourage greater policies. They do not specify that all company leave must run sequentially with statutory leave. Section 2654 delegates regulations necessary to carry out the relevant subchapters.

Former section 825.208 puts designation and notice responsibility on the employer, based on information received from the employee or spokesperson. The employee need not invoke the Act by name but must supply a qualifying reason. Insufficient information may require inquiry; intermittent or reduced-schedule leave ordinarily needs one notice unless circumstances change. Once the employer has the required knowledge, the ordinary notification period is two business days absent extenuating circumstances. Oral notice needs written confirmation by the following payday, or the subsequent payday if the first is less than one week away. An employer knowing the qualifying reason but failing timely designation generally may designate only prospectively. Later-acquired information may permit retrospective counting of the qualifying period. After return, prompt designation within two business days is permitted when the employer first learns the qualifying reason then; an unaware employer and employee seeking retrospective protection also implicate the employee's two-day notification obligation. Provisional designation pending sufficient information, medical certification or second/third opinion becomes final when qualifying information is supplied and must be withdrawn with written notice if it is not confirmed. These exceptions are not a universal cure or a finding that this employer used one.

Former section 825.700(a)'s challenged final sentence says undesignated paid or unpaid leave does not count against the FMLA entitlement. The same provision preserves greater plan rights, prevents diminution and does not automatically extend FMLA health-benefit obligations to additional company leave. The automatic no-count consequence is evaluated with section 825.208's actual timing rules and exceptions, not as an invariably uncorrectable first-day omission.

### Entering-law framework

The operative register rules are **Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing**, effective June 17, 1994 (MCI); **Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review**, effective April 21, 1999 for Haggar and June 18, 2001 for Mead; **Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation**, effective May 1, 2000 with Mead's June 18, 2001 application; and **After-acquired misconduct and age-discrimination remedies**, effective January 23, 1995 (McKennon). MCI requires preservation of enacted obligations within the actual delegation, not a universal ban on consequential implementation. Haggar and Mead distinguish authorized legislative rulemaking from a nonbinding advisory letter. Christensen's advisory-letter and regulatory-text holdings do not already invalidate a duly issued prospective designation rule. McKennon keeps a statutory violation and the lawful limits on its remedy separate; it supplies no direct FMLA formula.

Waffle House, January 15, 2002, preserves enacted remedy categories while requiring their individual liability, causation and relief predicates. Gulf Power, January 16, distinguishes coverage from conditions on exercising authority. Sigmon Coal, February 19, rejects creation of an omitted liability category merely to advance statutory policy. Blumer, February 20, permits a supported forecast without treating optional payments as accomplished or eliminating an actual-payment condition. These rules require attention to the provision actually being implemented; they do not mechanically select the answer here.

Raygor, February 27, excludes immune State claims through section 1367(d)'s express linkage to subsection (a). It neither invalidates procedural implementation in general nor supplies a sovereign clear-statement requirement for this private-employer leave regulation. Its dissent, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer, distinguishes a procedural preservation rule from newly authorized liability; that is material continuity for those Justices but is not controlling statutory law. Its pending-period/thirty-day tolling rule and FMLA designation periods have different texts, triggers and consequences.

New York v. FERC, March 4, separately sustains an express transmission grant and the refusal of an additional compulsory remedy on the agency's actual independent finding that it was unnecessary. Its six-Justice remedial holding preserves section 206's mandatory trigger; it supplies no free-standing discretion to replace statutory predicates. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined that holding. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas required an agency remand because they found the remedial explanation insufficiently independent of the jurisdictional error. Both positions insist on actual authority, actual reasons and the duty's proper trigger. Neither answers whether designation itself lawfully determines FMLA counting: D1 regards that as delegated implementation of the entitlement, while D2 regards its automatic consequence as replacing the enacted entitlement/relief inquiry. FERC supports the separation of these questions without determining the present choice. Its actual-agency-ground requirement does not create a demand for additional market or medical evidence here.

Delaney, March 4, concerns an assumed-fact personal-immunity appeal and prison conditions. It does not transfer its deliberate-indifference elements, constitutional injury threshold or immunity defense to the FMLA. Its separation of assumed legal predicates from unresolved evidence supplies no new medical finding in this summary-judgment record.

Vonn, March 4, retains the mandatory personal plea-warning duty while applying Rule 52(b) to a forfeited Rule 11 error and permitting relevant whole-record consideration of its effect. The eight-Justice review-standard holding does not import criminal forfeiture, plainness, a defendant's burden or discretionary appellate correction into the FMLA. O'Connor's opinion, joined by seven other Justices, and Stevens's separate position both distinguish the duty from the legally required inquiry into relief. Their disagreement about Rule 11(h)'s burden does not answer what counts as FMLA leave. In particular, D1's valid designation condition is not a claim that every notice breach itself warrants a monetary award.

Young, March 4, excludes the duration of a prior legally disabling bankruptcy stay from the particular three-year tax lookback, without a bad-faith requirement. Breyer's unanimous opinion preserves an interval Congress protected where the Code itself prevented collection; it does not authorize extension of every statutory period or alteration of every benefit's limit. All eight Associates' joins preclude describing their present positions as a universal opposition to implied procedural protection. D1 finds a comparable preservation function in designation; D2 distinguishes a statutory legal disability from the rule's contested premise that already received qualifying leave must automatically be ignored. Neither Young's bankruptcy rule nor Raygor's sovereign-claim linkage determines the FMLA choice.

### Adversarial alternatives

**D1 — valid designation condition; reverse and remand.** Section 2654 permits making timely identification a condition of counting voluntarily supplied leave concurrently with FMLA leave. Meaningful notice allows an employee to understand protected time, plan leave and exercise reinstatement and benefit rights. Employer compliance normally requires only timely identification, and the regulation contains information/certification exceptions. On this account the regulation determines when the statutory twelve weeks are used; it does not command every employer to offer more than twelve FMLA weeks.

The strongest D1 objection is the regulation's automatic operation when notice would change nothing: an employee who already received more than twelve qualifying weeks may demand another protected period without showing impairment attributable to omission. Section 2617's affected-employee, causal-loss and appropriate-equity terms resist that result. D1 answers that valid prospective counting rules define the entitlement before remedies are assessed; deprivation of the remaining protected leave may itself supply a statutory violation and ensuing loss, while no compensation or reinstatement is automatically awarded. The rule's text and actual conditions, rather than generic solicitude for agencies, must carry this construction.

**D2 — reject the automatic consequence; affirm the reviewed entitlement-based judgment.** Section 2654 does not permit a missing designation alone to make qualifying leave legally irrelevant and conclusively create a fresh twelve-week entitlement without prejudice to protected leave rights. Sections 2612 and 2617 link the total entitlement, violation affecting the employee, actual monetary loss and appropriate equitable relief. A notice failure may support an interference claim when it actually impairs the exercise of protected rights; the employer's generosity is no defense to such a violation. The automatic no-count rule nonetheless replaces that inquiry with a rule that operates even where notice could not have altered protected conduct.

The strongest D2 objection is circularity: treating all qualifying company leave as statutory leave regardless of designation defeats the very notice right Congress allowed the Secretary to implement. D2 answers that the information duty and relief for harmful interference survive. Congress did not authorize the particular automatic additional entitlement as the consequence of every unexcused designation failure. This is not a holding that all prophylactic rules require individualized reliance, all leave must be concurrent, or actual monetary loss is required for all equitable relief.

**D3 — injury-sensitive regulatory construction or remand.** A construction preserving ordinary notice obligations and individual interference claims is available. Rewriting the final sentence of section 825.700(a) to contain an unwritten rebuttable prejudice exception, however, would alter its categorical counting consequence rather than interpret an ambiguous remedial qualification. No Justice uses D3 to invent a medical capacity, an alternative course Ragsdale would have taken, or an independent preserved claim absent from the supplied account. The validity issue can be decided without those facts. A record-dependent award would require them.

### Frozen compatibility boundary

Four Associates commit to D1 (O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer) and four to D2 (Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas). Each group can share the stated sufficient ground without importing its members' distinct views on advisory deference, federalism or other statutes. All eight retain section 2617's actual conditions and reject an automatic monetary or reinstatement award. The disagreement is whether designation validly controls which leave consumes the entitlement, not whether the court may dispense with all remedy conditions. No Chief-Justice vote or opinion assignment is supplied.
