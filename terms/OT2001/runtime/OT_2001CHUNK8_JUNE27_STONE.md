## 117. Zelman v. Simmons-Harris / Hanna Perkins School v. Simmons-Harris / Taylor v. Simmons-Harris

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-27, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the blanket program disposition and remand under the controlling financed-function rule, with a binding rejection of the claim that parental routing alone permits public purchase of integrated devotional instruction.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require prompt, effective relief against any demonstrated public purchase of the integrated religious instructional function, using the existing record where sufficient. Preserve lawful secular and tutorial applications and determine state-law severability. Do not order an all-provider cutoff, school closure, pupil transfer, repayment, or a newly designed voucher scheme.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The function purchased controls the expenditure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The relevant government act is a restricted tuition payment redeemed by a provider, not a parent’s unrestricted private expenditure. The actual use must be proved at the scope necessary for relief.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether neutral eligibility and parental selection cure public purchase of integrated devotional instruction.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Jackson v. Benson, Strout v. Albanese, Agostini v. Felton, and Mitchell v. Helms. No new categorical funding theory or overruling is required.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Ohio may not finance the demonstrated integrated devotional instructional function through provider-redeemed tuition merely because parents choose the school. The program’s neutral criteria and other educational options do not alter what the challenged payment purchases. Reliably separated secular work remains eligible for its own analysis.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns actual financed instruction, not religious affiliation, doctrinal evaluation, or the number of families selecting a school. It preserves genuine private benefits and independently controlled secular services recognized by the precedents.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The program’s educational purpose is legitimate, and parents retain authority to choose religious education. The constitutional issue is whether the State finances that religious function. Restricting a payment to tuition and routing endorsement through a parent does not turn it into unrestricted private resources. Record descriptions of integrated teaching are relevant to the service purchased, while affiliation and enrollment statistics alone are inadequate. The strongest defense invokes a broad choice among public and private options. That defense may rebut coercion, but it does not answer the separate prohibition on financing the devotional function. The financed function is decisive under Jackson and Strout. The form of parental endorsement is relevant to identifying the transaction but cannot change the restricted service the State purchases; this is a functional application of the established expenditure rule. Mitchell preserves reliably separable secular assistance and forecloses treating a school’s affiliation alone as proof of prohibited use.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not decide every school’s actual curriculum from its name, nor resolve an absent unrestricted cash-benefit or genuinely separate secular-service program.

### Issue 2: Correct the established expenditure without eliminating lawful uses

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The lower decree rests on a broader historical theory and must be matched to the controlling rule and the actual evidence.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What relief prevents the proved conflict while preserving lawful applications.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the controlling remedial holdings of Jackson v. Benson and Mitchell v. Helms, while preserving lawful severable applications.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Relief must prevent the supported devotional purchase at the appropriate provider/payment scope and preserve lawful severable applications. Uncertainty about the full program does not justify continuing a proved violation or invalidating every religious provider.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The lower court may use the existing evidence and must identify any material additional issue; no automatic trial or indefinite transition follows.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A blanket judgment can be both too broad for lawful secular applications and insufficiently precise about the actual unconstitutional purchase. The mandate should require operative relief where the record establishes that purchase and ordinary state-law severability analysis for its consequences. That approach respects the students’ continuing lawful services without making administrative convenience a license to continue the prohibited expenditure. The record’s actual descriptions of integrated religious teaching must be used where they establish the funded function; the mandate does not require the parties to prove those same facts again. The remand is confined to identifying affected payments and the lawful severable applications because the existing blanket disposition did not perform that task.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Past recoupment, damages, compulsory transfer, and a judicially invented replacement program are not before the Court.


## 118. Hope v. Pelzer

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-27, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the materially-similar-case basis for qualified immunity and hold that the accepted allegation of prolonged, knowingly gratuitous punitive restraint was clearly prohibited in 1995. Remand for remaining individual-responsibility and factual issues.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The officers may not obtain summary judgment merely because no earlier case used this precise device and duration. The legal fair-warning issue is resolved against immunity on the accepted account; each defendant’s participation, knowledge and causation still must be supported. The remand neither imputes the June conduct to May-only defendants nor awards damages or revives withdrawn claims.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Gratuitous punishment is distinct from immediate restraint

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The underlying Eighth Amendment violation is an accepted premise of this immunity review. Use the nonmovant-favorable supported account and do not resolve disputed personal responsibility.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Which accepted conduct supplies the constitutional premise for the fair-warning inquiry, without deciding ungranted ultimate responsibility.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing conditions and force principles without creating strict liability for every restraint device.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The accepted account describes knowingly inflicted painful restraint after the security need ended, for punishment rather than restoration of order. That gratuitous treatment violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule. An officer’s liability nevertheless requires his own supported participation, culpability and causal contribution.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding reaches the alleged punitive operation, not every temporary restraint following disruption. The more severe June conduct cannot be automatically imputed to officers involved only in May.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The governing distinction is between restraints needed to regain control and needless suffering imposed after control has been regained. In May, water and bathroom offers mitigate the conditions but do not supply a justification for painful two-hour punishment of a prisoner who had not refused work. The June allegations are substantially more severe. That difference affects facts and responsibility; it does not erase the accepted premise that each episode involved gratuitous punitive restraint. The analysis takes that premise as given for the granted issue and leaves the disputed attribution of acts to the proper proceedings. Farmer’s knowledge requirement and Hudson’s treatment of punitive force remain in operation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The final factual account, personal culpability, and damages remain for proper proceedings.

### Issue 2: Fair warning does not require a previously litigated replica

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The notice inquiry concerns 1995 law. Later decisions articulate the method but cannot supply missing earlier notice.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the circuit’s factual-similarity demand defeats an otherwise obvious application of established constitutional limits.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the fair-warning principle recognized in United States v. Lanier and the context-sensitive civil inquiry retained in Wilson v. Layne.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** By May and June 1995, the existing prohibition of needless punitive suffering, read with Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White, gave fair warning against knowingly prolonging painful restraint after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued. A materially identical prior case is unnecessary. On the accepted account, novelty of the hitching-post details cannot sustain qualified immunity.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule preserves personal conduct, temporal eligibility, and the need for legal clarity. It creates no general subjective good-faith or policy-compliance substitute for the existing immunity standard.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Gates condemned prolonged painful restraint as punishment, and Ort distinguished measures needed to address immediate resistance from punishment after the emergency ended. Those principles reach the accepted conduct directly. State policy could permit justified temporary restraint without authorizing its gratuitous continuation, while DOJ warnings and policy safeguards reinforce the distinction without independently creating a constitutional right or proving every officer received a report. The decisive method is conduct-specific fair notice under the existing objective standard; Lanier explains that method without retroactively creating the underlying 1995 rule. Later decisions and later district-court approval of some uses cannot erase the earlier prohibition. The material-similarity rule must be rejected generally because it would protect an obvious violation whenever an official changed its apparatus. Saucier supplies no reason to substitute a new immunity defense here.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Ultimate responsibility of McClaran, Pelzer and Gates, including who affixed Hope and what each knew or prolonged, remains outside the accepted question. No collective liability or replacement immunity framework is adopted.


## 119. Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-27, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the judgment sustaining the challenged party-contribution limits and remand for application of FEC v. Colorado Republican’s demonstrated-risk and close-fit standard to Missouri’s actual ceilings.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Allow the State to rely on relevant federal evidence and reasonable preventive inference, but require findings or record-based conclusions connecting that evidence to Missouri’s mechanisms and actual ceilings. Determine relief for the challenged limits under the resulting record; do not order a new dollar amount, invalidate every contribution restriction, or decide retrospective liability.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Borrowed evidence can support a justification but cannot replace it

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Review the presented party-contribution restrictions and preserve genuinely independent expression outside an unsupported contribution classification.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether federal circumvention evidence and an existing earmarking ban establish the constitutionality of these state limits.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm FEC v. Colorado Republican’s permission to regulate supported circumvention risks and distinguish its federal facial disposition from automatic approval of materially lower state limits. Reject both a local-scandal prerequisite and a challenger-impossibility burden.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The federal evidence establishes a materially relevant risk from tacit donor-party-candidate arrangements and defeats the claim that Missouri must await a completed local scandal or rely exclusively on formal earmarking. It does not alone establish the close fit of these actual Missouri ceilings. The judgment cannot stand on the propositions that Colorado II validates them as a matter of law or that challengers must show political expression practically inaudible.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule addresses direct candidate-resource transfers and the evidentiary basis for the challenged ceilings. It does not restrict independent party expression merely because a party and candidate share objectives.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Colorado II controls the governing contribution standard and the relevance of informal circumvention; its actual evidence describes the same intermediary mechanism Missouri regulates. Formal earmarking does not reach every tacit fundraising credit or understanding, so the State has established a sufficient preventive interest without a Missouri-specific scandal. Close fit remains a separate burden. Missouri’s much lower ceilings were sustained because the lower court found no showing that association had become ineffective and pointed to unlimited independent spending. That reverses the government’s burden and fails to evaluate meaningful candidate-connected party support; independent expression is constitutionally distinct and is not a complete substitute. The decisive method is the established closely drawn contribution review, applied separately to interest and fit. The remand concerns the actual limits’ fit and resulting relief, not the already answered relevance of national evidence.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The adequacy of particular Missouri ceilings, any severability issue, and the separate reliance contention remain for their proper scope and record.


## 120. Republican Party of Minnesota v. White

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-27, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the announce clause and remand for declaratory and appropriate prospective injunctive relief against that clause.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Protect the plaintiffs’ discussion of disputed legal and political issues. Limit the decree to the challenged clause and preserve separately justified rules concerning disqualifying commitments, litigant bias, adjudicative conduct, and recusal without deciding their validity here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A judicial candidate may discuss legal positions without promising a result

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the continuing announce-clause challenge on the established prospective speech controversy; do not require an actual disciplinary sanction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the State may broadly suppress a candidate’s views to protect impartial adjudication and its appearance.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend Suster v. Marshall’s rejection of an institutional speech exemption to this direct content-based prohibition, while giving the distinct adjudicative interest its own strict-scrutiny analysis.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The announce clause violates the First Amendment because a broad prohibition of legal and political views is not narrowly tailored to impartial adjudication or its appearance. A legal view is not, without more, bias toward a litigant or a commitment to disregard law and evidence.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns announcing views. It does not immunize an enforceable promise to decide a particular case regardless of law or prohibit constitutionally permissible disqualification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Judges necessarily approach legal questions with experience and views. Electoral accountability makes information about those views especially consequential, while the judicial role requires the eventual decision to remain responsive to governing law and the case. Minnesota’s clause regulates the information broadly rather than the specific commitment that threatens that duty. Its campaign-focused coverage leaves analogous statements outside a campaign, undermining the claimed fit. Protecting confidence in courts cannot mean preserving a misleading appearance that candidates have no legal views. The State retains authority to address concrete prejudgment and party bias through measures directed to those harms. Strict scrutiny carries decisive weight because this is a direct content-based restriction on political and legal expression. Institutional concerns explain the asserted interest but do not lower the tier. Suster’s refusal to create an exemption for judicial elections is extended to this different rule; the narrower distinctions among issue views, actual litigant bias and promises are needed to invalidate the announce clause without adjudicating separate canons.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The exact validity of separate pledges, promises, recusal, and campaign-finance rules remains undecided.


## 121. Board of Education of Independent School District No. 92 of Pottawatomie County v. Earls

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-27, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment rejecting the challenged extension of suspicionless testing to nonathletic competitive extracurricular participants, under the contextual standard rather than a mandatory epidemic or safety-sensitive-duty prerequisite.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Return for declaratory and appropriate prospective relief against the challenged nonathletic expansion. Preserve suspicion-based testing and the distinct athlete program. Confidentiality evidence informs this search judgment; no independent disclosure cause of action or damages remedy is created.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: School responsibility requires a demonstrated fit for this bodily search

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the search question on Earls’s established injury; do not predicate the judgment on a resolution of James’s unnecessary standing dispute.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the asserted protective need justifies suspicionless urinalysis of this expanded competitive population.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply and distinguish Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton without replacing its controlling contextual balance with Stone’s earlier noncontrolling preference.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** On this record, general drug deterrence and competitive extracurricular participation do not sufficiently justify the suspicionless bodily search of the nonathletic groups covered by the policy. The conclusion rests on the combined need, population, intrusion, and safeguards, not on a universal requirement of a demonstrated epidemic or exhaustion of alternatives.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The ruling concerns the challenged expansion and preserves the separate athlete program previously sustained. It does not establish that nonathletes can never be tested under a differently supported program.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Band, choir, and academic participation do not themselves recreate the safety circumstances, athletic privacy expectations, and documented athlete-centered concern in Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton. The district’s collection safeguards and limited sanctions carry genuine weight, as does its protective responsibility. But the State must still explain why selecting these activities supplies an adequate basis for requiring bodily specimens without individualized suspicion. A general interest shared by every student cannot perform that task automatically. The record supports a drug concern, but not the necessary relation for this broader intrusion. The program’s educational purpose and exclusion of police use distinguish Edmond and Ferguson and permit the special-needs inquiry; they do not establish reasonableness automatically. Vernonia’s contextual balance is decisive, particularly the attenuated relationship between the selected nonathletic activities and the need for compelled specimens. Neither a compulsory epidemic threshold nor a least-intrusive-alternative rule is imposed.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Different supported populations, materially different safeguards, and reasonable-suspicion testing remain open.

### Issue 2: A written safeguard is not a finding of actual compliance

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Consider confidentiality as part of the intrusion and safeguards in the granted policy challenge, not as a separately granted disclosure claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What weight the written confidentiality promise and the contrary implementation evidence receive in assessing this testing program.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply ordinary summary-judgment rules within the Fourth Amendment reasonableness inquiry. Do not infer perfect implementation from policy language or a separate constitutional claim from every administrative lapse.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The written restriction on access and police use reduces the policy’s asserted intrusion. Supported contrary evidence about handling prescription information prevents treating confidentiality as conclusively perfect, but the insufficiency of the nonathletic program’s justification independently supports prospective relief even on the district’s stronger account of its safeguards.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This holding does not declare every alleged disclosure proved or automatically establish an independent damages claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Urinalysis can disclose medical information beyond evidence of illicit drugs, so limits on access and use matter to the search’s burden. The court must give the written safeguards real weight while respecting supported factual disputes about handling and disclosure. Those disputes need not delay the granted judgment because, even crediting the written protection, the fit between the selected nonathletic population and the asserted need is inadequate. This bounded evidentiary treatment avoids deciding an unpresented damages or informational-privacy action.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Any independently pleaded disclosure claim requires its own preserved source, elements and remedy. None is decided or ordered to trial by this holding.


## 122. People Who Care v. Rockford Board of Education, School District No. 205 +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the categorical termination order and remand for application of the complete Freeman and Jenkins release showing.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require supported findings on full satisfactory compliance, whole-decree good faith, practicable elimination of relevant vestiges and the district’s causal showing. Partial withdrawal is discretionary: determine separately whether continued control over a completed domain is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere. Retain only control justified by those findings and end supervision when its constitutional work is complete. Preserve settled findings absent clear error, impose no automatic additional supervision period, and award no new funding or damages.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The district must establish completion without proving educational perfection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The decree and the request to terminate present a continuing federal controversy. The unappealed liability judgment and previously resolved remedy provisions are not reopened.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the appellate command to terminate properly apply the district’s release and causal burdens?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Freeman and the controlling Jenkins holding; reject a burden shift requiring students to prove the adjudicated discrimination anew.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The district seeking release must show full satisfactory compliance, good faith toward the decree and practicable elimination of the relevant vestiges, including that remaining conditions it disclaims are not traceable to the adjudicated violation. School-assignment integration, money spent and program installation are relevant but not conclusive across every remedial domain. Achievement parity is not a release requirement. The appellate reasoning does not justify categorical termination under that complete standard. Partial release remains discretionary after separately determining whether continued control in the completed domain is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs release from the existing decree and permits domain-specific termination. It neither creates a general federal adequacy right nor prescribes race-based university admissions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Controlling precedent and its burden allocation carry decisive weight. The functional inquiry asks what remedial work remains, but it operates within that rule rather than shifting proof because supervision has lasted a long time. The district’s strong evidence of integration can support release in the domains it establishes; it does not automatically answer whether a retained educational measure repairs a remaining consequence. The students cannot demand national test-score parity or a fixed fifteen-year waiting period, and elected officials may criticize the decree without constitutional penalty. At the same time, the appellate demand for plaintiffs to produce a new comparative study cannot substitute for the district’s existing causal showing. Testimony, records, program operation and sound inferences may establish that showing; a study is neither invariably necessary nor sufficient. Remand resolves the missing application while making clear that genuinely completed obligations must not be maintained for broader social improvement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No reopening of the 1994 liability finding, previously adjudicated quota provisions, higher-education admissions or general school-finance adequacy claim is authorized.
