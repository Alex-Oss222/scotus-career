## 55. Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed. Six Justices supported full affirmance, with O’Connor agreeing in part.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy wrote for Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Thomas concurred in the judgment. O’Connor concurred in part and dissented in part, with Rehnquist and Scalia joining her Part II. Rehnquist dissented with Scalia except for a legislative-history paragraph.
* **Historical Holding:** Both challenged definitions were substantially overbroad; protected no-actual-child depictions could not be suppressed through those provisions.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only. The separate opinions’ narrower approaches are not aggregated into a different Court rule.
* **Historical Sources:** [Historical judicial opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/234/).

## 56. United States v. Craft

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 6 to 3.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor wrote for Rehnquist, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Thomas dissented with Stevens and Scalia. Scalia also dissented with Thomas.
* **Historical Holding:** The husband’s Michigan entireties rights were property or rights to property under the federal tax-lien statute; valuation remained open.
* **Historical Authority:** Comparator only; the operative prior simulated authority is Drye, within its stated limits.
* **Historical Sources:** [Historical opinion and dissents](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/274/).

## 57. Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Affirmed, 6 to 3.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Stevens wrote for O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Rehnquist dissented with Scalia and Thomas; Thomas separately dissented with Scalia.
* **Historical Holding:** The thirty-two-month planning moratoria did not establish a categorical taking by temporal segmentation; the unpreserved Penn Central theory was not reopened.
* **Historical Authority:** Historical comparator only, without treating all language about temporary restrictions as an absolute exemption.
* **Historical Sources:** [Historical judicial opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/535/302/).
