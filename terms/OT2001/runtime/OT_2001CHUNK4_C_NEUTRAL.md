## 61. Thompson v. Western States Medical Center

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Thompson v. Western States Medical Center
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 01-344; 535 U.S. 357.
* **October Term:** 2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment identified below under the supplied merits question.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-04-29. Historical final opinion or disposition date, used as the simulated merits event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether conditioning the federal compounded-drug exemptions on restrictions against soliciting prescriptions and advertising particular compounded drugs violates the First Amendment. This is a formulation of the reviewed constitutional question.
* **Question Granted:** The supplied merits scope is the question stated above, with the preserved limitations identified below. No separate simulated grant order or grant date is supplied.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, holding the advertising and solicitation restrictions unconstitutional and the relevant exemption provision nonseverable.
* **Relief Requested:** The federal officials seek reversal of the constitutional ruling. The pharmacies seek preservation of the judgment against the speech restrictions.
* **Material Record:** The statutory exemption for qualifying drug compounding required unsolicited prescriptions and prohibited promotion of particular compounded drugs, while allowing general promotion of compounding services. Licensed pharmacies challenged those conditions. The Government appealed the constitutional ruling; neither side properly brought the circuit’s separate nonseverability ruling into review.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The question concerns truthful, nonmisleading commercial information about otherwise lawful compounding, not authority to prohibit unsafe drugs or false medical claims. The speech condition cannot defeat scrutiny simply by defining the exemption around silence. Severability is outside the reviewed scope; the Court must not replace the enacted scheme with a newly designed exemption.
* **Relevant Dates:** The provision was enacted in 1997. The merits event is April 29, 2002. Later statutory treatment of compounding is not date-eligible.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Trans Union concerns nonconsensual commercial disclosure of information drawn from credit files. Thompson concerns advertising restrictions attached to a drug-compounding exemption; the asserted harm, statutory alternatives and relationship between speech and regulated conduct differ.
* **Participation Matter:** No in-world recusal, vacancy, or nonparticipation is authenticated in the supplied materials.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. A proposed brief does not establish an effective same-term decision.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Lorillard is completed prior-term law and is incorporated below. No completed OT2001 decision is assumed.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** First Amendment; 21 U.S.C. §353a as enacted through the Food and Drug Administration Modernization Act. Judicial review of speech conditions is distinct from substantive drug approval and the unreviewed severability consequence.

### Issue 1: Commercial speech and the compounding exemption

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Central Hudson and Fox require material advancement and reasonable fit for truthful lawful commercial speech. 44 Liquormart retains that framework. Lorillard applies it separately to advancement and the actual breadth of tobacco-advertising restrictions, rejecting insufficiently justified adult-speech burdens without adopting a universal least-restrictive-means requirement. Edenfield, Rubin and Greater New Orleans remain applicable to actual harms, scheme coherence and fit.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable rule protects truthful information about lawful activity and places justification on the Government. Evidence and reasonable prediction may suffice without a mandatory type of study, but speculation and unexplained mismatch do not. Concrete alternatives bear on fit without mechanically converting the inquiry into strict scrutiny.
* **Other Governing Law:** Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission of New York; Board of Trustees of State University of New York v. Fox; the enacted prescription, promotion, and exemption provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc. addresses a different content-based noncommercial setting and does not automatically replace the operative commercial-speech framework.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law already governs the advertising condition’s constitutional analysis. The unresolved application is whether this restriction reasonably fits the asserted need to preserve individualized compounding without allowing mass-market avoidance of drug approval.
* **Open Point:** Whether restricting drug-specific truthful promotion materially serves that distinction with an adequate fit, considering direct controls on compounding scale and practice and the useful speech swept in.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The circuit found inadequate advancement and tailoring. The Government seeks to preserve drug approval, allow necessary individualized compounding, and prevent the exception from becoming a route to industrial manufacture; it treats advertising as a proxy for the scale of production. The pharmacies argue that truthful information helps clinicians and patients locate appropriate preparations and that direct production and distribution limits address the actual concern. The Government need not prove an absolutely least restrictive rule, but must explain the speech proxy and its mismatch. The pharmacies gain no entitlement to misleading claims or exemption from otherwise valid safety rules.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could hold these particular conditions insufficiently fitted under existing commercial-speech doctrine while leaving substantive safety authority intact. Reversal could sustain the information restriction as a justified proxy within the statutory balance. A narrower ruling could address only supported lawful speech applications if the posture permits it, but cannot rewrite the omitted production safeguards itself. Because nonseverability is unreviewed, affirmance does not judicially create the exemption stripped of its speech conditions. The effect of the operative judgment must be distinguished from a policy preference for compounding access.

## 62. US Airways, Inc. v. Barnett

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** US Airways, Inc. v. Barnett
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-1250; 535 U.S. 391.
* **October Term:** 2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment identified below under the supplied merits question.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-04-29. Historical final opinion or disposition date, used as the simulated merits event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the ADA requires consideration of reassignment or retention as a reasonable accommodation when it conflicts with an employer’s seniority system, and how reasonableness and undue hardship allocate the inquiry. This is a formulation of the reviewed question.
* **Question Granted:** The supplied merits scope is the question stated above, with the preserved limitations identified below. No separate simulated grant order or grant date is supplied.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** En banc Ninth Circuit, reversing summary judgment for US Airways and requiring an individualized accommodation inquiry.
* **Relief Requested:** US Airways seeks judgment based on the seniority conflict. Barnett seeks further proceedings on his request to remain in the mailroom position.
* **Material Record:** After a back injury, Barnett moved from cargo work to a mailroom job. When senior employees planned to bid for it, he requested retention as an accommodation. The employer denied the request. The seniority policy was employer-created; the handbook disclaimed contractual status and reserved amendment. The district court emphasized longstanding reliance on the system.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The question does not establish every disability, qualification, vacancy, or damages predicate. An employer-created seniority policy differs from a collective-bargaining obligation or an incumbent’s actual job. The request’s effectiveness, ordinary reasonableness, and particular undue hardship must not be merged.
* **Relevant Dates:** The injury occurred in 1990 and the competing bids arose in 1992. The merits event is April 29, 2002. The applicable ADA is the original statutory regime, with the simulation’s existing interpretations; later amendments do not apply.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Toyota concerns the disability definition for manual tasks. Echazabal concerns a medically supported threat-to-self defense. Barnett instead concerns a qualified worker’s requested reassignment and the operation of an employer-created seniority policy; no medical-danger finding resolves that conflict.
* **Participation Matter:** No in-world recusal, vacancy, or nonparticipation is authenticated in the supplied materials.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. A proposed brief does not establish an effective same-term decision.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Toyota Motor Manufacturing, Kentucky, Inc. v. Williams and EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. are earlier on the schedule, but no effective simulated decisions are supplied. Their historical holdings cannot supply a disability threshold or remedial rule. Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Echazabal is later and unavailable.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** ADA §§12111(9), 12111(10), and 12112(b)(5)(A); summary judgment. The employee’s initial accommodation showing and the employer’s statutory undue-hardship showing have different functions.

### Issue 1: Accommodation and an employer-created seniority system

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Garrett preserves the ADA's separate coverage, qualification, accommodation and defense requirements. Reassignment to a vacant position is an accommodation example; §12112(b)(5)(A) assigns the employer the significant-difficulty-or-expense defense under its actual statutory factors. Sutton and Murphy distinguish coverage from qualification and accommodation. No completed holding creates a seniority-system presumption for the arrangement presented here.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable doctrine requires an individual statutory inquiry, identifies the essential functions, and preserves the distinct accommodation question. Olmstead v. L.C. applies reasonable accommodation within its public-service context and does not decide this employment seniority issue.
* **Other Governing Law:** The ADA identifies reassignment to a vacant position as a possible accommodation and permits an employer to demonstrate undue hardship. A preference may be necessary to provide the statutory accommodation; equal application of a rule does not alone answer the claim.
* **Material Other Authority:** Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Hardison addresses religious accommodation and a protected collectively bargained seniority system under a different statute. Its outcome cannot be transplanted without examining those differences.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The supplied law resolves disability-definition and distinct safety questions but does not establish either a categorical seniority defense or a presumption against every conflicting accommodation.
* **Open Point:** Whether the employer’s policy itself makes the request unreasonable, whether a rebuttable presumption is warranted, or whether demonstrated reliance and operational effects must be assessed through ordinary reasonableness and hardship.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court found the seniority disruption unduly burdensome; the circuit rejected automatic treatment and required case-specific analysis. US Airways emphasizes predictable advancement and reliance of many workers, arguing that accommodation cannot override a neutral system. Barnett emphasizes statutory reassignment, his existing performance in the position, and the employer’s retained power to depart from its own policy. He must show a reasonable accommodation rather than merely an effective preference. The employer must distinguish real disruption and others’ rights from the bare assertion that any exception is incompatible with seniority.

**Verified argument limitation:** Barnett’s counsel expressly disclaimed entitlement to summary judgment and accepted that the employer could present undue-hardship and other statutory defenses. That concession limits the requested relief to further adjudication; it does not establish that every seniority exception is reasonable. [Argument transcript, December 4, 2001, p. 30](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/argument_transcripts/2001/00-1250.pdf#page=30).

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could require an individualized inquiry without a special policy-based presumption. Reversal could make conflict categorically unreasonable, while an intermediate rule could presume unreasonableness subject to demonstrated exceptions or weak reliance. A vacatur could clarify burdens and allow renewed summary judgment. None of those paths automatically proves Barnett entitled to the job or permits displacement of an existing employee. The record supports a rule about this employer-created system; enforceable labor-contract conflicts require attention to their different legal predicates.

## 63. City of Los Angeles v. Alameda Books, Inc.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** City of Los Angeles v. Alameda Books, Inc.
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-799; 535 U.S. 425.
* **October Term:** 2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment identified below under the supplied merits question.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-13. Historical final opinion or disposition date, used as the simulated merits event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the evidence relied on by Los Angeles can support its asserted substantial interest in prohibiting multiple adult businesses in one building, consistently with the First Amendment. This states the substance of the reviewed evidentiary question; the preserved classification issue remains material.
* **Question Granted:** The supplied merits scope is the question stated above, with the preserved limitations identified below. No separate simulated grant order or grant date is supplied.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, sustaining summary judgment for the businesses because it found the supporting evidence insufficient even assuming content neutrality.
* **Relief Requested:** The City seeks reversal of summary judgment and permission to defend its ordinance further. The businesses seek preservation of the declaration and injunction.
* **Material Record:** A 1977 study linked concentrations of adult establishments with neighborhood crime. The City later prohibited multiple adult businesses in one building. Two combined bookstore and video-arcade operators challenged that rule. The study did not directly compare all single-use and combined operations. The district court found expressive targeting; the circuit assumed neutrality without deciding it.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The evidentiary question and the proper level of scrutiny are distinct. Summary judgment cannot demand a perfect study or resolve genuinely disputed predictive facts, but neither can a label of secondary effects remove the Government’s burden. The businesses may defend their judgment through preserved alternative grounds without obtaining a broader decree.
* **Relevant Dates:** The study was completed in 1977; the combined-use amendment was enacted in 1983. The merits event is May 13, 2002. Actual support for the City’s inference is assessed on the proper record, not presumed from later developments.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** No additional listed matter is combined with this case beyond the caption. Material chronological dependencies appear below.
* **Participation Matter:** No in-world recusal, vacancy, or nonparticipation is authenticated in the supplied materials.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. A proposed brief does not establish an effective same-term decision.

Western States Medical Center ruling must be checked for an actually adopted relevant proposition. Their proposed positions do not control. Same-day Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union is not deemed prior by index placement alone.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** First Amendment, 42 U.S.C. §1983, Los Angeles Municipal Code §12.70(C), and summary-judgment review. The City’s evidence, statutory operation, actual purpose, and availability of expression must each remain open to the appropriate inquiry.

### Issue 1: Classification and evidence for the zoning restriction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** City of Erie v. Pap’s A.M. controls the precise proposition that an ordinance cannot receive incidental-burden treatment when its justification and operation principally target the communicative adult-entertainment setting. Five Justices directly adopted that proposition across writings; neither the lead opinion’s full strict-scrutiny reasoning nor its separate intermediate-scrutiny alternative is governing. United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc. preserves the Government’s burden for content-selected suppression.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable standards examine actual operation and justification. Genuinely supported regulation of nonexpressive harms remains available, but expressive targeting cannot be converted into incidental regulation through a formal description alone. Evidentiary sufficiency must be tied to the scrutiny actually applicable.
* **Other Governing Law:** First Amendment; Renton v. Playtime Theatres, Inc. remains relevant to genuine land-use regulation addressing secondary effects and alternative avenues of communication. No supplied simulated decision overrules it or makes its rationale conclusive for every adult-business restriction.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. O’Brien requires a justification unrelated to suppressing expression within its field. Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC permits supported predictive judgment but not an irrebuttable legislative assertion. Commercial-speech reasonable-fit cases do not automatically establish the test for these expressive businesses.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law settles the anti-targeting boundary and permits supported regulation of nonexpressive harms. It does not determine whether this combined-use rule satisfies that classification or what inference this study supports under the correct test.
* **Open Point:** Whether the City’s correlation evidence and concentration rationale can support further adjudication without a study of the exact configuration, and whether the ordinance operates through dispersal of harmful conditions or suppression of protected expression.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The circuit found an inadequate link between multiple establishments in a neighborhood and multiple operations in one establishment. The City argues that concentrating operations can concentrate patrons and associated crime regardless of an internal wall, so the evidence need not isolate that architecture. The businesses argue that separating complementary uses may suppress or close the arcade business rather than reduce harmful concentration, and that the study does not demonstrate the required causal fit. The district court’s expressive-targeting ground remains a distinct issue. The City need not disprove every conceivable competing theory, but must support its own and confront material contrary evidence.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Vacatur could reject an excessively categorical demand for configuration-specific studies and return the preserved classification and merits questions for proper scrutiny. Affirmance could hold the actual evidence insufficient under the applicable test or sustain the preserved expressive-targeting ground. Reversal cannot by itself establish final validity of the ordinance. A narrow evidentiary holding permits relevant inferences without a presumption that all adult businesses create the same harms. A broader classification holding would require the actual operation and justification rather than the historical label of content neutrality. Interim relief on remand requires its own lawful assessment.

## 64. Henderson v. Norris +

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Henderson v. Norris +
* **Citation or Docket:** 258 F.3d 706 (8th Cir. 2001). No simulated Supreme Court docket or grant date is supplied.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-13. Supplied simulation placement of the lower-court addition.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a life sentence without parole absent executive commutation is grossly disproportionate under the Eighth Amendment for a first conviction of delivering 0.238 grams of cocaine base?
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eighth Circuit reversed the denial of federal habeas relief and directed a conditional writ unless Arkansas resentenced Henderson within ninety days after issuance of the mandate. The State seeks reversal of that judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** The State seeks restoration of the habeas denial; Henderson seeks preservation of sentence-only conditional relief.
* **Material Record:** Henderson received life imprisonment for delivering 0.238 grams of crack cocaine. This was his first conviction. Arkansas law made ordinary parole unavailable for a life sentence unless executive commutation first reduced it to a term of years. The Arkansas Supreme Court affirmed, with disagreement over proportionality. Federal habeas relief was denied before the appellate reversal. The offense was a completed sale, not merely possession; the State may rely on the harms of distribution. The supplied judicial record does not establish a violent transaction, a substantial distribution enterprise, or a prior conviction that would justify recidivist treatment. The record includes evidence of two additional rocks of cocaine and of interference with a prosecution witness; the Arkansas Supreme Court relied on the additional drug activity in rejecting the proportionality claim. The Eighth Circuit reports that the jury was not instructed that either additional drug conduct or witness interference could be considered in fixing the sentence, and it declined to presume that the jury used the latter evidence as a sentencing basis. Executive clemency is discretionary grace and does not create the regular parole eligibility assumed in a comparison to parole-eligible sentences.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The habeas petition was filed before April 24, 1996, so the amended §2254(d) restriction on relief for unreasonable state adjudications does not govern. Federal legal questions receive independent review under the earlier regime, with proper respect for supported state factual findings. The State invoked Teague to argue for deference to reasonable state interpretations of federal law; the Eighth Circuit rejected that proposed standard of review. Teague remains a distinct limit on granting relief under a rule not dictated when the conviction became final. The published opinion does not identify a separately developed claim that a particular new proportionality rule is necessary. The question concerns the sentence, not guilt. A legislatively authorized punishment is not exempt from constitutional review merely because the sentencing jury selected it.
* **Relevant Dates:** The offense occurred April 23, 1993; trial began August 29, 1994; the Arkansas Supreme Court affirmed on November 13, 1995. The reviewed opinions do not establish the exact subsequent certiorari-expiration or denial date. Rummel (1980), Solem (1983), and Harmelin (1991) all preceded the offense and necessarily preceded finality; no later proportionality rule supplies the claim. The published federal appellate opinion issued July 9, 2001; rehearing was denied August 20, 2001. The supplied May 13, 2002 placement controls this simulated merits event.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Bell involves an application governed by AEDPA; Henderson’s federal application predates that statute and presents sentence proportionality under the earlier review regime. Horn addresses a preserved retroactivity defense, not a new minimum proportionality threshold.
* **Participation Matter:** None independently established for the simulation.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Atkins v. Virginia is scheduled later and concerns capital punishment; its proposed treatment cannot govern this earlier noncapital case.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Proportionality of noncapital imprisonment

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No supplied later simulated holding establishes a different noncapital imprisonment framework for this first-offender sentence. United States v. Bajakajian concerns monetary punishment and does not itself decide the imprisonment question.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate punishment severity, offense gravity, individualized culpability, and the proper comparator. A finding that an exaction is punishment does not establish its constitutional excessiveness, and no monetary proportionality formula mechanically determines a prison term.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Eighth Amendment as applied to the States; the pre-AEDPA federal habeas framework; Arkansas sentencing and parole provisions as described in the appellate record. Lindh governs the temporal reach of amended §2254(d). Teague separately asks whether the requested result was dictated by precedent when the conviction became final. Caspari and Lambrix require that actual comparison: neither describing a claim as an application nor observing judicial disagreement settles newness. Wright does not establish general deference to reasonable state legal interpretations in place of independent review.
* **Material Other Authority:** Solem v. Helm supplies a noncapital proportionality comparison. Harmelin v. Michigan supplies the competing account of a narrow gross-disproportionality principle. Rummel v. Estelle concerns recidivism and meaningful parole eligibility. Their factual and opinion-structure limits matter.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Ordinary legislative choices about sentence severity receive substantial latitude. That latitude does not convert a life sentence into a term of years or allow a nonexistent criminal history to supply its justification.
* **Open Point:** Whether this first-offense, very small drug sale crosses the constitutional threshold, and what comparison is required after the initial inference of gross disproportionality.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Eighth Circuit distinguished a serious distribution offense from the vastly larger drug quantity in Harmelin and distinguished genuine parole eligibility from possible clemency. It found an inference of gross disproportionality supported by comparisons with other Arkansas offenses and punishment elsewhere.

**State’s position.** Drug dealing is a serious public wrong even in small units; legislatures need not calibrate punishment in direct proportion to weight. Judicial comparison can become unauthorized resentencing, and the Constitution does not guarantee a first offender probation or a short term. The State requests a highly deferential threshold that leaves this sentence intact. It also argues that Teague’s restriction on new law requires federal acceptance of reasonable state interpretations of federal law even in this pre-AEDPA proceeding. That review-standard argument should not be recast as a separately specified new-rule defense that the published record does not describe.

**Henderson’s position.** Permanent imprisonment for this first conviction is qualitatively different from a severe finite sentence. The State cannot justify it by attributing all harms of the drug trade to this offender. His rule permits exceptional relief without judicially fixing an ordinary sentencing schedule.

**Limits and alternatives.** The challenge does not deny the State power to imprison drug sellers. A sentence-specific constitutional conclusion need not invalidate the underlying penalty range for all defendants or reopen the conviction.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A threshold rejection based on amended §2254(d) deference would apply the wrong temporal regime. Rejecting the State’s Teague-based demand for general legal deference does not eliminate the separate nonretroactivity inquiry: relief still must rest on a dictated application of finality-era law or an applicable exception. On the merits, reversal would restore the denial of habeas relief and the life sentence. Affirmance would preserve a conditional sentencing remedy, giving Arkansas an opportunity to impose a lawful sentence before federal relief becomes operative.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** One path treats this life sentence as an exceptional grossly disproportionate punishment because of the combination of first-offender status, the small completed sale, and no ordinary parole. It would govern materially similar sentences without creating a numerical drug-weight ceiling. The competing path concludes that legislative judgments about distribution keep even this sentence within constitutional bounds. A remand for missing comparative or criminal-history findings would be appropriate only if the actual record is inadequate, not merely because proportionality is contestable.

**Uncertainty:** The packet does not supply a numerical maximum constitutional term. The record must not be augmented with assumed violence, recidivism, or future clemency.
