# Scoped approved assembly supplement

## 122. People Who Care v. Rockford Board of Education, School District No. 205 +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the categorical termination order and remand for application of the complete Freeman and Jenkins release showing.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require supported findings on full satisfactory compliance, whole-decree good faith, practicable elimination of relevant vestiges and the district’s causal showing. Partial withdrawal is discretionary: determine separately whether continued control over a completed domain is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere. Retain only control justified by those findings and end supervision when its constitutional work is complete. Preserve settled findings absent clear error, impose no automatic additional supervision period, and award no new funding or damages.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The district must establish completion without proving educational perfection

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The decree and the request to terminate present a continuing federal controversy. The unappealed liability judgment and previously resolved remedy provisions are not reopened.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does the appellate command to terminate properly apply the district’s release and causal burdens?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Freeman and the controlling Jenkins holding; reject a burden shift requiring students to prove the adjudicated discrimination anew.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The district seeking release must show full satisfactory compliance, good faith toward the decree and practicable elimination of the relevant vestiges, including that remaining conditions it disclaims are not traceable to the adjudicated violation. School-assignment integration, money spent and program installation are relevant but not conclusive across every remedial domain. Achievement parity is not a release requirement. The appellate reasoning does not justify categorical termination under that complete standard. Partial release remains discretionary after separately determining whether continued control in the completed domain is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs release from the existing decree and permits domain-specific termination. It neither creates a general federal adequacy right nor prescribes race-based university admissions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Controlling precedent and its burden allocation carry decisive weight. The functional inquiry asks what remedial work remains, but it operates within that rule rather than shifting proof because supervision has lasted a long time. The district’s strong evidence of integration can support release in the domains it establishes; it does not automatically answer whether a retained educational measure repairs a remaining consequence. The students cannot demand national test-score parity or a fixed fifteen-year waiting period, and elected officials may criticize the decree without constitutional penalty. At the same time, the appellate demand for plaintiffs to produce a new comparative study cannot substitute for the district’s existing causal showing. Testimony, records, program operation and sound inferences may establish that showing; a study is neither invariably necessary nor sufficient. Remand resolves the missing application while making clear that genuinely completed obligations must not be maintained for broader social improvement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No reopening of the 1994 liability finding, previously adjudicated quota provisions, higher-education admissions or general school-finance adequacy claim is authorized.

## 120. Republican Party of Minnesota v. White

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-27, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the judgment sustaining the announce clause and remand for declaratory and appropriate prospective injunctive relief against that clause.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Protect the plaintiffs’ discussion of disputed legal and political issues. Limit the decree to the challenged clause and preserve separately justified rules concerning disqualifying commitments, litigant bias, adjudicative conduct, and recusal without deciding their validity here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A judicial candidate may discuss legal positions without promising a result

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the continuing announce-clause challenge on the established prospective speech controversy; do not require an actual disciplinary sanction.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the State may broadly suppress a candidate’s views to protect impartial adjudication and its appearance.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Extend Suster v. Marshall’s rejection of an institutional speech exemption to this direct content-based prohibition, while giving the distinct adjudicative interest its own strict-scrutiny analysis.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The announce clause violates the First Amendment because a broad prohibition of legal and political views is not narrowly tailored to impartial adjudication or its appearance. A legal view is not, without more, bias toward a litigant or a commitment to disregard law and evidence.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding concerns announcing views. It does not immunize an enforceable promise to decide a particular case regardless of law or prohibit constitutionally permissible disqualification.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Judges necessarily approach legal questions with experience and views. Electoral accountability makes information about those views especially consequential, while the judicial role requires the eventual decision to remain responsive to governing law and the case. Minnesota’s clause regulates the information broadly rather than the specific commitment that threatens that duty. Its campaign-focused coverage leaves analogous statements outside a campaign, undermining the claimed fit. Protecting confidence in courts cannot mean preserving a misleading appearance that candidates have no legal views. The State retains authority to address concrete prejudgment and party bias through measures directed to those harms. Strict scrutiny carries decisive weight because this is a direct content-based restriction on political and legal expression. Institutional concerns explain the asserted interest but do not lower the tier. Suster’s refusal to create an exemption for judicial elections is extended to this different rule; the narrower distinctions among issue views, actual litigant bias and promises are needed to invalidate the announce clause without adjudicating separate canons.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The exact validity of separate pledges, promises, recusal, and campaign-finance rules remains undecided.

## 117. Zelman v. Simmons-Harris / Hanna Perkins School v. Simmons-Harris / Taylor v. Simmons-Harris

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law through June 28, 2001; position for 2002-06-27, subject to any actually effective intervening decision.
* **Conditions:** Any intervening controlling decision must be applied or expressly addressed before the vote.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the blanket program disposition and remand under the controlling financed-function rule, with a binding rejection of the claim that parental routing alone permits public purchase of integrated devotional instruction.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Require prompt, effective relief against any demonstrated public purchase of the integrated religious instructional function, using the existing record where sufficient. Preserve lawful secular and tutorial applications and determine state-law severability. Do not order an all-provider cutoff, school closure, pupil transfer, repayment, or a newly designed voucher scheme.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The function purchased controls the expenditure

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The relevant government act is a restricted tuition payment redeemed by a provider, not a parent’s unrestricted private expenditure. The actual use must be proved at the scope necessary for relief.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether neutral eligibility and parental selection cure public purchase of integrated devotional instruction.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Jackson v. Benson, Strout v. Albanese, Agostini v. Felton, and Mitchell v. Helms. No new categorical funding theory or overruling is required.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Ohio may not finance the demonstrated integrated devotional instructional function through provider-redeemed tuition merely because parents choose the school. The program’s neutral criteria and other educational options do not alter what the challenged payment purchases. Reliably separated secular work remains eligible for its own analysis.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns actual financed instruction, not religious affiliation, doctrinal evaluation, or the number of families selecting a school. It preserves genuine private benefits and independently controlled secular services recognized by the precedents.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The program’s educational purpose is legitimate, and parents retain authority to choose religious education. The constitutional issue is whether the State finances that religious function. Restricting a payment to tuition and routing endorsement through a parent does not turn it into unrestricted private resources. Record descriptions of integrated teaching are relevant to the service purchased, while affiliation and enrollment statistics alone are inadequate. The strongest defense invokes a broad choice among public and private options. That defense may rebut coercion, but it does not answer the separate prohibition on financing the devotional function. The financed function is decisive under Jackson and Strout. The form of parental endorsement is relevant to identifying the transaction but cannot change the restricted service the State purchases; this is a functional application of the established expenditure rule. Mitchell preserves reliably separable secular assistance and forecloses treating a school’s affiliation alone as proof of prohibited use.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The Court does not decide every school’s actual curriculum from its name, nor resolve an absent unrestricted cash-benefit or genuinely separate secular-service program.

### Issue 2: Correct the established expenditure without eliminating lawful uses

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The lower decree rests on a broader historical theory and must be matched to the controlling rule and the actual evidence.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What relief prevents the proved conflict while preserving lawful applications.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the controlling remedial holdings of Jackson v. Benson and Mitchell v. Helms, while preserving lawful severable applications.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Relief must prevent the supported devotional purchase at the appropriate provider/payment scope and preserve lawful severable applications. Uncertainty about the full program does not justify continuing a proved violation or invalidating every religious provider.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The lower court may use the existing evidence and must identify any material additional issue; no automatic trial or indefinite transition follows.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** A blanket judgment can be both too broad for lawful secular applications and insufficiently precise about the actual unconstitutional purchase. The mandate should require operative relief where the record establishes that purchase and ordinary state-law severability analysis for its consequences. That approach respects the students’ continuing lawful services without making administrative convenience a license to continue the prohibited expenditure. The record’s actual descriptions of integrated religious teaching must be used where they establish the funded function; the mandate does not require the parties to prove those same facts again. The remand is confined to identifying affected payments and the lawful severable applications because the existing blanket disposition did not perform that task.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Past recoupment, damages, compulsory transfer, and a judicially invented replacement program are not before the Court.
