## 86. Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Echazabal

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9 to 0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Souter delivered the unanimous opinion.
* **Historical Holding:** The ADA permitted the EEOC regulation allowing a defense that the job would endanger the worker’s own health. The regulation’s individualized risk requirements constrained that defense; its validity did not itself resolve every question concerning Chevron’s application.
* **Historical Authority:** The opinion examined the qualification provisions, the express direct-threat example, and Chevron deference. Historical disability decisions do not supersede the simulation’s different underlying-impairment rule.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/73/).

## 84. Devlin v. Scardelletti

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 6 to 3.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** O’Connor wrote for Rehnquist, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, and herself. Scalia dissented, joined by Kennedy and Thomas.
* **Historical Holding:** A nonnamed class member who timely objected could appeal settlement approval without first intervening. The Court distinguished constitutional standing from the procedural ability to appeal.
* **Historical Authority:** The class rules, Marino, and appellate party-status principles supplied the historical analysis. The simulation’s reasoned Felzen holding must not be replaced by its different historical procedural history.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion and dissent](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/1/).

## 90. Franconia Associates v. United States

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 9 to 0.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Ginsburg delivered the unanimous opinion.
* **Historical Holding:** The 1988 restrictions were a repudiation of the asserted prepayment undertaking, rather than necessarily an immediate breach. Unless the borrowers elected to treat repudiation as breach, the contract claim accrued when prepayment was tendered and refused.
* **Historical Authority:** The opinion applied ordinary anticipatory-repudiation principles while leaving remaining contract issues for further proceedings.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinion](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/129/).

## 85. McKune v. Lile

# SECTION III: HISTORICAL COMPARATOR SUPPLEMENT

## A. MERITS COMPARATOR

* **Historical Outcome and Vote:** Reversed and remanded, 5 to 4 in the judgment.
* **Historical Opinion Structure:** Kennedy’s plurality was joined by Rehnquist, Scalia, and Thomas. O’Connor concurred in the judgment but rejected the plurality’s use of the atypical-and-significant-hardship measure as the compulsion standard. Stevens dissented, joined by Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
* **Historical Holding:** Five Justices concluded that the challenged consequences were insufficient to establish compulsion, but no majority adopted the plurality’s complete rationale. O’Connor relied on the limited severity of the consequences in this case.
* **Historical Authority:** The opinions addressed the penalty cases and the historical Sandin approach. The latter differs from the supplied simulated Sandin holding.
* **Historical Sources:** [United States Reports opinions](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/536/24/).
