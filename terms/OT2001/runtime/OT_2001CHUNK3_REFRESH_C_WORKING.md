## 46. HUD v. Rucker / Oakland Housing Authority v. Rucker

### Reach, components and proposed disposition

Nos. 00-1770 and 00-1781 present the statutory and associated categorical constitutional grounds for a preliminary injunction. All eight Associates would reverse the judgment sustaining the categorical tenant-knowledge requirement, vacate the injunction to the extent it rests on that requirement, and remand for disposition of any properly presented individual grounds under the actual leases, applicable law and preliminary-relief requirements. This is not an eviction judgment.

**R1: statutory cause.** Section 1437d(l)(6) makes covered criminal activity by the listed persons a permissible termination ground without an additional condition that the tenant knew or should have known. Mandatory inclusion of the clause is distinct from mandatory termination. The tenant, household member, guest and other controlled-person categories must actually be satisfied; the provision does not reach a stranger merely connected remotely to a tenant. The control language does not require proof that a tenant could supervise every covered person's conduct as a prerequisite to statutory cause. The criminal act must meet its own elements. The threatening-criminal-activity branch and the drug-activity branch have different text and must not be combined.

**R2: categorical constitutional objection.** Authorization of termination without tenant knowledge is not, for that reason alone, unconstitutional. A housing authority may protect the residential community through lawful conditions on continued household occupancy; an innocent tenant does not have an unconditional substantive right to retain a tenancy after every covered person's violation. Bennis cannot support the injunction's mere-ignorance rule: it requires a separately held automobile interest, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention. Neither its automobile scope nor its prevention requirement disappears by analogy. Conversely, neither the civil label nor local mitigation discretion proves the constitutionality of every particular deprivation. The Court should preserve consideration of a properly supported individual constitutional claim rather than announce universal constitutional approval of all no-knowledge evictions.

**R3: remaining application.** The lower courts must identify each actual lease, its applicable geographical terms, the alleged conduct and relationship, lawful notice, document access, required hearing route and any preserved individual constitutional theory. The 1997 regulation's on-or-near-premises language is not retrospectively replaced by the Code's on-or-off-premises language. Optional mitigation under section 966.4(l)(5), including permission for continued occupancy without the responsible family member and the stated treatment option, remains optional. A grievance hearing is required only under the applicable conditions; a lawful judicial eviction route remains available. If relevant requested documents are not made available as section 966.4(m) requires, the agency may not proceed with eviction. No tenant's factual entitlement is established here.

### Competing lawful paths

The strongest argument for affirmance reads the control language, the general ban on unreasonable lease conditions, the good-cause provision and Bennis together to protect tenants who could not prevent another person's misconduct. Its weakness is that it turns an enumerated other-person ground into a categorical personal-knowledge requirement and treats ignorance as satisfying a constitutional rule that requires substantially more. Constitutional avoidance supplies no license to adopt that unsupported universal condition.

The strongest argument for unqualified reversal treats every covered lease violation as a sufficient answer to all substantive due-process objections because eviction is civil and mitigation is available. Its weakness is that Bennis rejects both categorical civil-forfeiture immunity and discretion as a substitute for an established constitutional protection. The supported result removes the injunction's categorical premise while retaining the constitutionally relevant distinctions and the remaining actual-case inquiry. It does not extend Bennis automatically to every tenancy, nor decide an individualized defense on missing predicate facts.

## 47. S.S. ex rel. Jervis v. McMullen

### Reach, component rules and actual allegations

The supplied merits review of 225 F.3d 960 concerns dismissal of the personal substantive-due-process action. No Supreme Court docket, grant date or argument date is invented. The vacated panel opinion is not the judgment under review. All eight Associates would vacate the categorical dismissal and remand for ordinary further proceedings under the bounded custody rule described below. This is a pleading result, with independent defenses preserved.

**S1: affirmative custodial placement.** Officials exercising actual state custody cannot deliberately and unreasonably use their placement authority to expose a child to a serious danger they actually appreciate when they have authority and a real opportunity to respond. Personal conduct and attributable injury are necessary. The relevant inquiry concerns the State's actual exercise of custody and choice of placement, not an imagined history in which it never intervened. Returning to a biological parent or a previously dangerous home is not categorical immunity for that affirmative custody conduct. Mere failure to furnish protective services after a lawful release remains governed by DeShaney.

**S2: culpability.** The alleged considered use of custody authority despite actual appreciation of a particular serious abuse danger can satisfy the required substantive-due-process threshold. Negligence, professional disagreement and a dangerous outcome are insufficient. Genuine reunification obligations and the official's lawful options matter to whether the response was unreasonable; the general legitimacy of reunification does not conclusively answer the alleged deliberate choice. Lewis's emergency-pursuit harmful-purpose requirement cannot be transplanted to unhurried custody decisions. Farmer informs actual awareness, reasonable response and permissible inference, without converting the claim into an Eighth Amendment prison case.

**S3: separate acts and persons.** March 8, 1996 began full-time residence on an extended visit while the Division retained legal custody. The August 22 judicial legal-custody return is separate and followed McMullen's request. Later warnings cannot establish an earlier official's knowledge retrospectively, but may bear on later continued placement decisions within retained authority. The reported allegations support an individual inference at the pleading stage, not pooled knowledge: McMullen received the September 1995 warning, reports of continuing contact, the May 1996 evaluation, July report and August 13 call; Jacoby documented her own warnings, symptoms and reported contact; Barnett allegedly received and approved reports and participated in the placement decisions, including the August responsibility decision described in the dictation. Supervisory title alone is insufficient. McMullen's July investigation is part of the alleged response and must be considered; its existence does not conclusively establish reasonableness.

The later assaults, November 15, 1996 and January 15, 1997, occurred after the judicial return. The claimant therefore must establish attributable injury from each actionable earlier decision. The court order cannot be ignored or treated as the officials' decree, but does not justify dismissal by assuming that every recommendation or earlier placement choice was legally compelled. The remand must identify actual authority, personal contribution, the order's constraints, causation and any function-specific defense. A truthful recommendation, constrained implementation, discretionary placement and knowing misleading presentation are not interchangeable; no deception is found or presumed.

**S4: immunity and procedure.** Current merits law and conduct-time notice are distinct. For March 1996 acts Baby Richard was not yet decided; its June 21 decision may be relevant only to later acts and only within its actual transition-interest scope. Lewis and Chipman were decided in 1998 and supply no retrospective notice. Harlow, Anderson and all relevant contemporaneous law govern any qualified-immunity defense on each defendant's actual act and information date. Guest confirms that later rights recognition cannot defeat that defense and that no actual-authority-reliance replacement has been adopted. The record supplied does not justify a final all-defendant immunity finding. Ordinary pleading procedure remains; Swierkiewicz supports the source-based rejection of invented proof prerequisites but does not decide every individual-officer pleading question or establish this substantive claim. The reported detailed acts and knowledge allegations suffice for the bounded further inquiry without presuming their truth at later stages.

### Alternative paths tested

Affirmance on the original-home comparison has its strongest support in DeShaney's refusal to make a rescue agency a permanent guarantor and in the parent's distinct lawful status. It fails as a categorical resolution of the March extended placement and the ensuing period of retained state custody: the claimant challenges what officials did with actual placement authority, not only their failure to remove a child from private custody. Chipman's unanimous direct-restraint rule and Youngberg supply the relevant security principle. The full legal order and actual authority may defeat a particular later theory; they cannot be assumed to defeat all pleaded earlier custodial acts.

Affirmance on culpability has its strongest support in Daniels, the need to distinguish social-work error from constitutional wrongdoing, and the legitimate objective of reunification. It fails at this stage because the particular warnings, alleged personal decisions and opportunity to deliberate permit an inference of actual appreciation and unreasonable disregard. The offender's shocking acts do not establish official culpability; the pleaded official choices must themselves satisfy the rule. A broad state-created-danger doctrine, automatic deliberate-indifference liability whenever time exists, or a right to the safest/preferred home would go beyond this supported disposition.

## 48. Hoffman Plastic Compounds, Inc. v. NLRB

### Reach, material facts and law

No. 00-1595 concerns the Board's limited backpay for unperformed work after an established antiunion discharge. The underlying unfair-labor-practice determination and other lawful relief are distinct. Reinstatement was denied; the backpay period ends at actual discovery on June 14, 1993. The finding of reasonable interim-work efforts is the Board's, not a Court finding that every interim job was lawfully obtained. Initial calculation answers, the later administrative contest and the preserved statutory challenge must not be described as universal waiver.

The present-tense testimony supplies evidence supporting a continued-use inference through the hearing. It is not a named interim employer, dated new tender or finding that every interim job involved documents. Counsel's suggested verification failures/casual work and contrary suggestions of Board-induced fraud remain arguments, not findings. No position below depends on falsely saying that no evidence supports continued use or on treating every interim job as proved document fraud.

Section 10(c) confers reviewable remedial discretion; its actual-discharge-for-cause bar does not transform a later-discovered reason into the employer's actual 1989 motive. ABF rejects automatic forfeiture for hearing misconduct but preserves review where misconduct defeats a remedial predicate. McKennon supplies a pre-discovery starting measure under the ADEA after the required actual-would-discharge showing, not a command to pay for work in conflict with another statute. MCI, Mead and Christensen preserve statutory boundaries and the distinction between lawful delegation and judicial statutory interpretation. IRCA's original prohibitions, verification duties and section 1546(b) must retain their exact scope; section 1324c's later enactment is not a prohibition in force for the earlier hiring.

### Competing paths and independent components

**H1: categorical statutory conflict.** One path disallows backpay for unperformed work throughout the period of established lack of authorization where the job was obtained through fraudulent verification documents. A make-whole award assumes a lawful compensable employment opportunity that the federal verification scheme excludes. Southern Steamship requires the Board to respect the separate federal prohibition; Sure-Tan's lawful-availability limitation is material. The strongest objection is that money neither employs Castro nor legalizes the fraud, and a pre-discovery cutoff preserves the statutory command against knowing continued employment. This path answers that the conflict concerns the remedial premise and the verification scheme's operation, not literal criminalization of payment or complete loss of NLRA protection.

**H2: permissible statutory accommodation.** The competing path sustains the limited award because IRCA specifies verification, knowing-employment and fraud consequences without repealing the Board's monetary response to an independent labor violation. Denying reinstatement and postdiscovery accrual respects actual future-employment restrictions; ABF and McKennon support separating wrongdoing, violation and remedy. The strongest objection is that the award reconstructs earnings from unauthorized work and could blunt the verification system or make mitigation depend on further fraud. This path answers that neither backpay nor mitigation authorizes fraud, separate immigration sanctions remain, and the Board's actual limited award and interim-work findings do not direct any future unlawful hiring. It does not deem continued fraud immaterial to every discretionary or calculation challenge.

**H3: narrower remand.** An explanation/calculation remand would require a preserved defect material to the actual legal rule, not mere discomfort with the two statutes or a fiction that the Board overlooked Castro's known fraud. The present evidence supports a temporal inference but does not compel a job-specific finding. Neither side's preferred categorical construction needs such a new factual finding. The model does not use an invented waiver, hypothetical interim violation or compelled new hearing to break the statutory division.
