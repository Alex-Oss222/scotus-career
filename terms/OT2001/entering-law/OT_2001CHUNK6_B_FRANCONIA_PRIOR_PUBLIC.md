#### Suitum v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-243.
**Decided:** 1997-05-27.

##### The final land-use decision does not depend on selling transferable rights

**Holding:** When the responsible land-use agency has definitively prohibited development of the claimant's parcel and no further land-use procedure can change that decision, the final-decision requirement is satisfied. The owner need not first sell transferable development rights that cannot alter the parcel's permitted use; uncertainty about their value or ultimate constitutional significance does not make that completed regulatory determination provisional.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Transfer-right saleability, award and value are not alternative finality conditions. Their ultimate relevance to liability or compensation, the relevant parcel, takings framework, defenses and any independent state-compensation requirement remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the finality dismissal and remand without the attempted-sale prerequisite, 9–0; no taking, total deprivation, compensation or permit is found or ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Williamson County and MacDonald require a definitive agency account of permitted use; Lucas separates reviewability from complete deprivation and compensation. Penn Central's merits framework and treatment of transfer rights are not altered by the noncontrolling concurrence.

#### Palazzolo v. Rhode Island

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-2047.
**Decided:** June 28, 2001.

##### Whether another materially equivalent application is required after a definitive use restriction

**Holding:** When the responsible agency has definitively excluded the requested class of use and the established remaining use sufficiently defines the challenged restriction, an owner need not submit materially equivalent proposals to obtain a final decision. These denials and the governing private-filling restriction permit review of the existing compensation claim without another private-filling proposal; they do not fix every permissible upland use or the property's maximum value.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Parts I–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy; six direct joins.

**Material application:** The denials definitively exclude ordinary private wetlands filling for lack of the required public purpose. The exception requires all three conditions: a compelling purpose benefiting the public as a whole through public infrastructure, a water-dependent activity producing substantial economic gain for the State, or broad public access to the shore; all reasonable measures to reduce environmental impacts or conflicts of use; and no reasonable alternative means or location to serve that purpose. Satisfying all three permits, but does not compel, an exception. The public-purpose denial supplies no separate findings on the other conditions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** An application remains necessary if a different density, design or genuinely available exception could change the agency’s determination. Additional upland sites, access and maximum value remain unsettled; no universal two-denial rule applies.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Suitum separates final use determination from uncertain valuation. Lucas preserves reviewability of an established prohibition without deciding compensation.


##### Whether acquisition after regulation extinguishes the claimant’s own takings challenge

**Holding:** Acquisition after a regulation's enactment does not by itself bar the claimant's own takings challenge or turn that regulation into an antecedent limitation on property rights. Actual regulatory conditions and acquisition circumstances may bear on reasonable investment-backed expectations within the combined Penn Central inquiry, but notice alone does not extinguish the claim or decide that inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Parts I–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy; six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Notice creates neither a presumption against successors nor a price-paid compensation ceiling. The holding does not determine injury timing or transfer a predecessor’s accrued compensation claim with title.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lucas requires genuine background property or nuisance limits, not regulation transformed into title law by transfer. Penn Central and Dodd permit actual regulatory and acquisition circumstances to bear on expectations within the combined inquiry, without making acquisition alone dispositive.


##### Whether meaningful whole-parcel upland use defeats the complete-deprivation category

**Holding:** The whole parcel's meaningful residential upland use defeats the Lucas category requiring complete deprivation of economically beneficial use. That conclusion does not defeat a partial-taking claim under Penn Central, and it requires no finding that one house or approximately $200,000 is the maximum remaining use or value.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Parts I–III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy; six direct joins.

**Material application:** One supported upland home and approximately $200,000 retained value are meaningful use, not findings of a maximum. The claimed subdivision value and loss percentage do not establish complete deprivation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The wetlands-only denominator was not presented below or in the petition. No universal parcel definition or automatic partial-taking result follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The rejection of the whole-parcel complete-deprivation claim is affirmed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lucas requires total economic deprivation; Dodd preserves the distinction between profitable remaining use and a partial-taking claim.


##### What inquiry governs the existing contextual compensation claim

**Holding:** The existing compensation claim must be considered under Penn Central's combined inquiry into economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action, subject to actual preservation requirements and independently supported lawful grounds. Acquisition after enactment, retained value or a legitimate public purpose alone cannot substitute for that inquiry; no compensation result follows from the present remand.

**Proposition-level authority:** Thomas's Part IV, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy; five directly adopt the remand. Stevens joins Parts I–III but rejects this remand on his own-injury ground; the three finality dissenters’ conditional merits views add no joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual uses and value, expectations, background law, injury timing and claim ownership remain for lawful adjudication where material. No taking at enactment or in 1986, accrued-claim transfer, nuisance defense or preservation of every future theory is established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Categorical finality and post-regulation-acquisition barriers are reversed and the existing case is remanded under ordinary preservation rules. No compensation, permit, new trial or predetermined valuation is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Penn Central supplies the combined economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and governmental-character inquiry. Dodd rejects any single categorical shortcut while preserving proof of actual injury and duration.

#### Lujan v. G & G Fire Sprinklers, Inc., 532 U.S. 189

**Docket or dockets:** No. 00-152.
**Decided:** April 17, 2001.

##### Judicial contract proceedings as due process for disputed public-works payment

**Holding:** An actually available judicial contract action capable of deciding and enforcing entitlement to a disputed public-contract payment can provide due process without a second administrative hearing. Assuming G & G's protected interest, the supported action against its prime contractors defeats the categorical no-process challenge; an inaccessible action, destruction of the claim or conclusive unreviewable assessment would not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The ordinary action against prime contractors is distinct from the contractor-or-assignee statutory action against the awarding body, which has its own exclusive-remedy provision and ninety-day period after completion and acceptance. Lack of assignment does not itself eliminate the ordinary action, and neither assignment nor timely filing is presumed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The property interest is assumed, not denied categorically. Wage violations, penalties, entitlement amounts and ultimate recovery remain undecided; a properly preserved and supported barrier to all effective adjudication must be addressed. Ordinary delay alone does not destroy this debt claim, but the decision does not immunize all costs or delays or govern already possessed property and established benefit awards. It creates no section 1983 exhaustion requirement and relies on no procedure taking effect July 1, 2001.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand to set aside the constitutional declaration and dependent injunction, without a federal payment award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** American Manufacturers distinguishes an adjudicable entitlement from unconditional advance payment; its private-carrier ruling does not immunize state-directed withholding. Mathews supplies attention to the actual interest and forum. Good's possessory seizure is distinguished; Logan continues to prohibit destruction or effective foreclosure of a protected claim. J & K Painting supports the separate ordinary contract route, subject to California courts' authority over state-law construction.

# Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency, No. 00-1167 — 2002-04-23

[Authoritative Record](../records/tahoe_sierra_preservation_council_v_tahoe_regional_planning_agency_merits_2002-04-23.md)

## Event

Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency, No. 00-1167; 535 U.S. 302. Argued January 7, 2002; decided April 23, 2002. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 216 F.3d 764, which rejected the categorical taking determination for two development moratoria. The question is whether the restrictions are compensable solely because they prevented all economic use during their thirty-two-month operation. The district court rejected the owners' Penn Central claim, and they did not appeal that ruling and expressly disavowed that theory. No physical occupation occurred. Later plans and judicial restraints have separate causal and procedural histories outside this question.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment rejecting the preserved categorical claim is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would reverse and remand for the categorical inquiry and actual-period remedy.

Compensation is denied on the temporal-segmentation theory. The abandoned Penn Central ruling remains undisturbed; no remand revives it or determines compensation for separate later periods.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirms rejection of the preserved categorical claim | Continuing property, no temporal segmentation and all qualifications |
| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia | Would reverse and remand | Complete present-use deprivation during the actual challenged period; background-law and compensation inquiry |

## Holdings

**A regulatory moratorium is not a categorical taking merely through temporal segmentation.** For a temporary development restriction without physical appropriation, an owner cannot establish Lucas's total-deprivation category merely by defining the relevant property as the exact time during which the restriction bars development. The continuing property interest must be assessed as such; economic impact, expectations, duration and governmental character remain subject to Penn Central when that claim is properly presented, and a temporary label supplies no immunity.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly support the rule, preservation limit and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Lucas addresses complete deprivation of the legally relevant property without resolving every temporal denominator. Penn Central, Keystone and Andrus caution against manufacturing totality from a selected burdened strand. First English supplies compensation after a temporary taking is independently established, not the conclusion that every temporary prohibition is a taking. Physical occupation and appropriation of a leasehold are different governmental acts. The claimants' strongest objection—that present use has real value and serial temporary labels could evade compensation—is answered by preserving contextual review and refusing to make the label conclusive. No fixed thirty-two-month safe harbor, environmental exception or numerical duration limit follows. Palazzolo preserves the difference between meaningful remaining use and the distinct contextual inquiry. Here the owners abandoned the rejected Penn Central claim; categorical rejection therefore ends this reviewed claim without its revival.

The relevant restraints are Ordinance 81-5 beginning August 24, 1981, with case-by-case approval available for some single-family homes on Nevada-side Class 1–3 lots, but unavailable on stream-environment-zone lands or California-side parcels, and Resolution 83-21 from August 27, 1983 through April 25, 1984, initially ninety days and then extended. The categorical claim concerns their application to the relevant owners; the ordinance did not impose an identical prohibition on every parcel. Later plan and judicial-restraint periods have separate causal/procedural predicates outside review. No compensation follows on the preserved temporal-segmentation theory.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council:** Its complete-deprivation category and genuine background-law exception remain. The Court rejects using the exact moratorium period alone as the relevant property for this regulatory claim; Lucas did not settle every temporal denominator.
- **Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104:** Its combined economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and governmental-character inquiry remains available for properly preserved claims, but this abandoned theory is not revived.
- **First English Evangelical Lutheran Church of Glendale v. County of Los Angeles, 482 U.S. 304:** Compensation is required for a temporary taking once independently established; it does not make every temporary development bar a taking.
- **Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470, and Andrus v. Allard, 444 U.S. 51:** Applied to the prohibition on manufacturing complete deprivation from a selected burdened strand of a continuing interest.
- **Palazzolo v. Rhode Island:** Its distinction between meaningful remaining ownership and the separate contextual inquiry remains. Neither regulatory notice nor a valid environmental purpose independently defeats compensation, and its unresolved general parcel question is not converted into a contrary temporal rule.

## Law After Decision

A temporary regulatory development restraint does not become a Lucas taking solely by treating its precise duration as a separate property interest. Contextual review remains for properly presented claims, including examination of duration, actual impact, expectations and governmental character. Temporary status is no immunity, while compensation for an independently established temporary taking remains required. This preserved categorical claim is concluded.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** A complete suspension of economically beneficial use for a substantial defined period can take that present use even though ownership and future use remain afterward. Lucas protects the complete-deprivation category, and First English and Lucas's actual-period remedy distinguish whether a taking occurred from how long compensation runs. Later restoration limits compensation; it does not eliminate the earlier complete loss. The majority's whole-interest position recognizes future value, but in the dissent's view that value does not supply usable property during the defined prohibition.

The two would reverse and remand for a sustained complete-deprivation determination, genuine background property or nuisance limitations and compensation for an actual taking within the challenged thirty-two months. They impose neither a numerical minimum duration nor a rule covering every ordinary permit-processing interval. The case-by-case Nevada Class 1–3 single-family approval possibility, unavailable to stream-environment-zone lands and California parcels, requires attention to the owners actually covered. The State must substantiate an antecedent background rule independently prohibiting the use under ordinary proof requirements; a new legislative harm declaration is insufficient. The abandoned Penn Central theory is not restored, and later plans or judicial restraints cannot be attributed to the two enactments through an unproved six-year causal extension. Their remedy concerns the actual established taking period, not presumed permanent value.

## Procedure After Action

The categorical rejection stands. The owners' unappealed contextual ruling is not reopened, and the judgment does not adjudicate separately caused later restraints. This merits review ends subject to the ordinary mandate.

## Source Notes

[Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. TRPA, 216 F.3d 764](https://openjurist.org/216/f3d/764); Ordinance 81-5; Resolution 83-21. [535 U.S. 302](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-535/pdf/USREPORTS-535-302.pdf), for chronology and the preserved question. Ordinance 81-5 allowed case-by-case approval of some single-family homes on Nevada-side Class 1–3 lots; that possibility was unavailable to stream-environment-zone lands and California-side parcels. The claim concerns the relevant owners rather than an identical prohibition on every parcel.

### Mobil Oil Exploration & Producing Southeast, Inc. v. United States / Marathon Oil Co. v. United States, Nos. 99-244 and 99-253

Plenary merits decision, June 26, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 22, 2000; decided June 26, 2000. On writs of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, reviewing its revised May 13, 1999 judgment, 177 F.3d 1331, which reversed the Court of Federal Claims' liability and subsequent bonus-restitution judgments.

Each petitioner paid more than $78 million for approximately one-third interests in five offshore North Carolina leases. Four leases were included in the Manteo Unit final exploration plan submitted August 20, 1990; no plan was submitted for the fifth. The leases conveyed conditional exclusive rights for a ten-year primary term with specified continuations and an incorporated regulatory process, not a guarantee of production. Congress enacted the Outer Banks Protection Act on August 18, 1990. North Carolina separately objected to coastal-consistency certifications, and Commerce refused an override in September 1994. The contract appeal concerns whether the federal process undertaking was materially repudiated and whether restitution follows; it does not review the administrative refusal to override the State or a takings claim.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mobil, No. 99-244 | Reversed and remanded, 8-1 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Stevens | Established restitution entitlement; genuinely remaining, properly preserved amount and restoration questions return to the Federal Circuit. |
| Marathon, No. 99-253 | Reversed and remanded, 8-1 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer | Stevens | Established restitution entitlement; genuinely remaining, properly preserved amount and restoration questions return to the Federal Circuit. |

The petitioners are entitled to restitution of proved up-front bonus consideration for the repudiated bargain, subject to return of corresponding rights and lawful credits or offsets. The Federal Circuit must address its previously unreached, properly preserved monetary objections. The Court does not set a rounded dollar award, reopen every benefits finding, order a new hearing regardless of need, award the denied rentals or speculative profits, or authorize interest without an independent legal basis. No plan, drilling permit, state concurrence, Commerce override or production entitlement is ordered.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Part I | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer | Common contract and actual-breach ground | Clear continuing undertaking, incorporated conditions and failure of submitted-plan approval obligation, Holding 1 |
| Same opinion, Parts II-V | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer | Restitution and remand | Material and anticipatory repudiation, restoration, election and bounded monetary remedy, Holdings 2-5 |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stevens | Filed alone | Joins I; rejects restitution | Actual breach does not amount to total repudiation; no fifth-lease anticipatory ground; only properly presented and proved delay damages remain possible |

All nine join the contract construction and actual submitted-plan breach; eight join material repudiation and restitution. The common rationale requires no resolution of the distinct noncontrolling Winstar canon theories and no narrowest-grounds synthesis.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. The leases contain a clear continuing process undertaking that the Government breached

**Holding and operative rule:** These leases incorporate a continuing obligation to provide the specified Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act (OCSLA) exploration-plan approval process, including approval, after necessary modifications, of a compliant plan within thirty days of submission unless serious harm to life (including aquatic life), property, minerals, national security or defense, or the marine, coastal or human environment cannot be avoided by modification and requires disapproval. The Government's refusal to provide that approval because of OBPA's new conditions breached the undertaking; independent permits and state coastal-consistency requirements do not make the federal promise disappear.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's opinion controls Holding 1 unanimously, with all nine Justices joining as shown above.

**Controlling explanation:** Winstar requires an authorized actual undertaking and an examination of the risk the Government assumed; a valuable regulatory opportunity alone is insufficient. Section 1's incorporation of OCSLA, sections 302-303 of the Department of Energy Organization Act, existing regulations and limited future regulations, read with the term and conditional operating rights, establish a future process promise. The catchall does not transfer every later statutory change to the lessees and nullify those distinctions. Future regulations for waste prevention, resource conservation and correlative rights remain incorporated. Sections 9-10 require approved plans and compliance; section 13 preserves OCSLA suspension and cancellation, and separately suspension on the Defense Secretary's recommendation during war or a national emergency declared by Congress or the President, with just compensation. Section 19 reserves ungranted rights: other geological and geophysical exploration and other-mineral operations may not unreasonably interfere with or endanger lease operations; easements may serve other lands' work or product shipment. A defense designation requires the Defense Secretary and presidential approval; surface operations then require Defense concurrence. A resulting suspension also suspends rentals and royalties, extends the term and carries constitutionally required compensation. None supplies a blanket no-liability reservation. OCSLA permits suspensions at the lessee's request in the national interest, for proper development or transportation facilities and separate suspensions for threatened serious, irreparable or immediate harm to life, property, minerals or the marine, coastal or human environment; neither predicate is newly found merely because further environmental study is useful. A qualifying suspension extends the affected lease or permit for an equivalent period, except when the suspension results from gross negligence or willful violation of the lease, permit or applicable regulations. Approval remains conditional on compliance, necessary modifications and the specified serious-harm exception. Yet Interior's completed evaluation did not supply the legal approval withheld under the new Act. North Carolina made actual objections here; operations still require a lawful state-consistency resolution and separate discharge and drilling permissions. No concurrence or permit entitlement is found.

##### 2. The statutory replacement of the promised process was material repudiation

**Holding and operative rule:** OBPA's prohibition and new prerequisites materially withdrew an essential part of the bargained-for federal process and constituted repudiation, rather than an ordinary permissible delay under the incorporated terms. That repudiation applies to the four submitted-plan leases and anticipatorily to the same promised future process on the fifth lease, without inventing a submitted plan or missed deadline on that tract.

**Authority:** Parts II-V of Ginsburg's opinion control Holdings 2-5 through the eight-Justice coalition shown above.

**Controlling explanation:** Roehm v. Horst supplies the contractual consequence of an anticipatory refusal to render essential promised performance. The Act barred the relevant approvals and drilling until the later of October 1, 1991 or forty-five days of continuous congressional session after the required report and certification. Continuity is broken only by adjournment of Congress without a further date, and recesses when either House adjourns for more than three days to a day certain are excluded from the count. The Secretary must consider the scientific panel and explain disagreements, but the panel has no absolute veto. The Court does not treat the gate as forty-five calendar days or an indefinite ban, or attribute every later delay to it. Even at its actual initial scope, the new prohibition and conditions substantially replaced the paid-for process. The statutory and regulatory powers already incorporated had different predicates; their existence did not permit this categorical substitution without contract consequences. Further administrative effort and preserved lease time had value, but did not deliver the withheld timely approval process. Congress retained power to change the law; under Winstar, that sovereign authority does not erase monetary responsibility for the particular risk actually assumed. The fifth lease was subject to the same categorical refusal, making its claim anticipatory rather than an invented completed application breach. The holding does not turn every delay or legislative adverse effect into total repudiation.

##### 3. Restitution does not require proof that the project would have earned profits

**Holding and operative rule:** After this material repudiation, the injured lessees may elect restoration of consideration paid for the promised performance without proving that all independent approvals would have been obtained or profitable production would have occurred. Independent regulatory obstacles bear on the actual bargain and any cognizable benefit accounting, but do not automatically defeat restitution by making expectation profits unprovable.

**Controlling explanation:** United States v. Behan distinguishes restoration after the promisor prevents the agreed undertaking from compensation measured by expected profits. The companies bought a conditional opportunity through an identified process, not certain oil or immunity from North Carolina's objections. The Government's strongest response is that those objections prevented drilling anyway. That may defeat a claim that the federal delay caused lost operating profits; it does not establish that the Government performed the separate process for which it accepted consideration. The materiality inquiry considers the bargain as a whole, including existing evaluation and preserved time, but those benefits do not cure the repudiated essential undertaking found here. Winstar's contractual responsibility therefore operates with ordinary remedy limits, not a guarantee of success. No cancellation-compensation award under OCSLA is substituted for the contract remedy: the Court makes no statutory cancellation finding or compensation award.

##### 4. Continued performance does not bar restoration on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** Urging the repudiating party to perform, submitting the requested plan, pursuing independent approvals or seeking extensions does not by itself surrender the injured party's restitution election. Acceptance of significant substituted performance may matter, but the documented acts here do not establish acceptance of the withheld federal process as fulfilled or a waiver of restoration.

**Controlling explanation:** Roehm and Pence v. Langdon preserve the distinction between seeking performance or retraction and conduct that makes restoration unavailable through an effective contrary election or acceptance of material substitute performance. The companies' submission, unsuccessful override efforts and extensions related to the memorandum of understanding kept the original opportunity alive; they did not obtain the approval process the Government had repudiated. The 1992 restitution suit asserted restoration rather than relinquishing it. Administrative work and additional lease time are not denied; they remain relevant at their lawful scope without being treated as performance of the withheld undertaking. The record also distinguishes earlier rent-free suspensions from the June 1995 letter's rent-bearing, five-year-capped suspension. No later renewal, continuous rent exemption or payment history through decision is assumed. The defense fails on the documented acts even with their real value acknowledged. The Court finds no unproved retraction, settlement, abandonment, forfeiture of a preservation objection or attribution of every act by one petitioner to the other. It does not reopen entitlement merely to decide an unsupported election theory.

##### 5. The monetary remand is confined to genuine restoration and amount questions

**Holding and operative rule:** The petitioners' restitution entitlement requires return of proved bonus consideration with legally required restoration of corresponding rights and any established lawful credit or offset. The Federal Circuit must resolve its unreached, properly preserved calculation objections on the actual record; recovery of rentals, profits or interest does not follow automatically, and interest requires independent authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Restitution restores the exchanged value rather than giving both the price and the retained contract rights without accounting. The Court of Federal Claims separately denied rental recovery and, after a benefits hearing, found no cognizable offset established. The Federal Circuit never reached the Government's monetary challenges because it rejected liability. Reversal therefore permits review of genuinely remaining objections; it does not erase the existing benefits determination or command a new evidentiary hearing regardless of what remains disputed. Approximate descriptions of payments cannot substitute for the actual judgment and supported calculations. No decree compels a regulatory approval, invalidates North Carolina's objection, awards speculative production profits or converts OBPA's later repeal into performance at the promised time.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Winstar Corp. supplies actual-undertaking and assumed-risk responsibility while preserving sovereign regulatory power and separate remedy analysis. Its noncontrolling broad sovereign-acts and unmistakability theories are not made controlling here; the particular continuing promise is clear even under the requirement to identify one unmistakably.
- Roehm v. Horst supplies anticipatory-repudiation principles and the distinction between urging performance and surrendering a restoration election. It is applied to the essential future process, not every prospective administrative delay.
- United States v. Behan supplies the distinction between recovery of value after prevented performance and proof of anticipated profits; independent obstacles to profitable production do not erase the restitution claim established here.
- Pence v. Langdon supplies the distinction between preservation of a restoration right and conduct inconsistent with that election; the Court rejects waiver from the documented acts without assuming an unproved acceptance of significant substitute performance.

#### Law After Decision

A clearly promised federal regulatory process can be an essential contractual performance even though the contract guarantees no approval of other permits or profitable operations. Congress may change regulatory law while the Government remains financially responsible for the specific risk it undertook. Material withdrawal of that process supports restitution distinct from expectation damages; ordinary election, restoration and amount rules remain applicable. The decision does not create a general right to compensation for unfavorable regulation or adopt a comprehensive new sovereign-acts or unmistakability doctrine.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins the clear contractual undertaking and recognizes actual breach of the submitted-plan approval requirement. He rejects the conclusion that this breach destroyed the essential bargain or constituted total repudiation. The whole transaction included conditional exploration rights, actual government evaluation, continuing work and extensions preserving lease time, while the companies had assumed independent state-approval risks. Those circumstances matter to materiality even though they do not excuse the missed federal approval. Winstar establishes responsibility for an actual promise but does not make every actionable impairment a right to recover the entire investment.

He therefore would sustain the Federal Circuit's rejection of restitution on this different ground. No submitted plan established a missed deadline for the fifth lease, and he rejects the majority's anticipatory-repudiation ground there. At most, a properly presented claim for proved delay damages could remain under ordinary causation and preservation rules; he orders no new claim, automatic damages hearing or sum. His position does not depend on an assumed continuously rent-free suspension or a renewal after the documented five-year cap, and he makes no waiver or contrary-election finding. His disagreement concerns total-breach materiality and restoration, not merely unresolved arithmetic.

#### Source Notes

The revised Federal Circuit ruling is 177 F.3d 1331; the liability ruling below is 35 Fed. Cl. 309, followed by the July 24, 1997 restitution judgment. The lease clauses, memorandum of understanding, submissions and lower judgments appear in the [Mobil record](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0329) and [Marathon record](https://archive.org/details/micro_IA40385014_0330). OBPA is section 6003 of [Public Law 101-380, 104 Stat. 484, 555-558](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-104/pdf/STATUTE-104-Pg484.pdf). OCSLA sections 5 and 11, 43 U.S.C. sections 1334 and 1340, supply the incorporated conditions; coastal consistency remains governed separately by CZMA section 307(c)(3).

The [United States' merits submission](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/mobil-oil-exploration-and-producing-southeast-inc-v-united-states-merits) appends the June 2, 1995 letter requiring rentals and capping the new suspension at five years. Its terms qualify a broader description of continuously rent-free treatment. No later renewal or payment fact is assumed. Rounded payment descriptions do not determine the judgment amount; the existing findings and properly preserved monetary objections govern. The one-day variation in reported July 1990 objection dates resolves no issue here.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Both dockets return to the Federal Circuit for the remaining, properly preserved amount and restoration questions consistent with the established entitlement. Existing findings remain effective except to the extent a lawful unresolved objection requires review. No particular new hearing, drilling permission, state override, interest award or exact payment date is ordered. The separate administrative challenge and unpresented takings theory are not adjudicated.

### United States v. Winstar Corp., No. 95-865

Merits decision, July 1, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 24, 1996; decided July 1, 1996. On certiorari under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) to the Federal Circuit, 64 F.3d 1531, which on August 30, 1995 affirmed partial summary judgments on contractual liability after certified interlocutory review under §1292. Damages remain undetermined.

The consolidated disputes concern the Glendale–Broward transaction in 1981, Winstar/United–Windom in 1984 and Statesman in 1988. The institutions assumed failing institutions' liabilities in transactions containing particular accounting and capital arrangements. The questions are whether the integrated writings establish actual authorized promises, whether later withdrawal of the promised treatment breaches them, and whether unmistakability, reserved sovereign powers, sovereign acts or impossibility defeat contractual liability.

FIRREA imposed new capital requirements and limited supervisory goodwill. OTS separately treated capital credits as qualifying supervisory goodwill through 12 C.F.R. §567.1(w); that exact treatment was not itself statutory wording referring to capital credits. Congress's authority to regulate, the institutions' duty to obey valid law and the Government's financial responsibility under its promises are distinct questions. The case does not adjudicate FIRREA's validity or a damages amount.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 8–1.** The same vote applies to Glendale's undertaking, Winstar's undertaking, Statesman's goodwill undertaking and Statesman's separate capital credit.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Ginsburg, who would vacate the present liability affirmance and remand for separate construction of the undertakings under unmistakability, without directing judgment for the Government or a trial.

The affirmed liability is partial contractual liability, not a fixed monetary award. Causal loss, recoverable categories, mitigation, applicable periods and amounts remain for ordinary proceedings. No goodwill balance or capital credit equals damages automatically; no finding establishes that the regulatory change caused an institution's failure. No injunction, invalidation of FIRREA, specific performance against valid law, solvency guarantee or takings judgment is entered. The unadjudicated takings claims and Winstar's implied-in-fact alternative are not silently decided.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### 1. Actual contracts and actual authority are required; these writings establish the identified promises

**Holding and authority:** The Tucker Act, 28 U.S.C. §1491(a)(1), provides contract jurisdiction but does not create a substantive promise. A claimant must establish an actual authorized express or implied-in-fact undertaking, not an obligation imposed only for fairness; all nine join that requirement. Eight join the holding that these integrated transaction writings establish express promises of the particular accounting and capital treatment, with the financial risk of its withdrawal, subject to their actual qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** Hercules requires identification of the duty actually undertaken and preserves genuine authorized warranties despite uncertain eventual damages. Romein prevents converting regulation alone into an immutable contract; Cisneros requires enforcing the bargain with its real limits. These institutions rely on more than a generally available accounting rule. The assistance agreements preserve contemporaneous resolutions and letters that identify the treatment offered in exchange for acquiring liabilities. The transaction-specific forward terms give content to that exchange. Winstar's en banc judgment rests on an express agreement, not solely on the trial court's implied-in-fact theory. Statesman's express capital credit is a separate promise from its goodwill allowance. The Government's present-permission construction must account for those incorporated benefits and their particular durations, rather than treating approval as the entire bargain. The Court finds the specified undertaking, not insurance against every future regulation. General assistance terms, changing-regulation language and lawful-action clauses remain relevant; the precise benefit is read with them, not erased by them or enlarged into a perpetual exemption. The separate doctrinal opinions differ over unmistakability's threshold operation, but all eight find the actual financial undertaking sufficiently established in these writings.

##### Transaction-specific operative terms

The operative terms are distinct:

- **Glendale:** The November 19, 1981 Supervisory Action Agreement preserves contemporaneous Board/FSLIC resolutions and letters rather than superseding them. Resolution 81-710 requires a satisfactory independent accountant's opinion describing goodwill, justifying purchase accounting and substantiating amounts and amortization, with compliance with Memorandum R-31b. The approved accountant treatment allocates $18 million over twelve years and $716.666 million over forty years, straight line. The lower judgment found the accounting condition satisfied; the Court does not turn that finding into a concession or treat every dollar as forty-year goodwill.
- **Winstar:** The integrated agreement, Resolution 84-363 and July 13, 1984 letter permit straight-line amortization of purchase-accounting intangibles over a period not exceeding thirty-five years for regulatory reporting. The maximum is permission, not a required period or a promised damages term. Those accounting forbearances are not acceptable in financial statements required to follow GAAP. Ongoing reserve/net-worth records and regulatory reporting remain required. The Net Worth Maintenance obligation concerns the required capital level, while the agreement's accounting terms define what counts toward it; a change in level is not identical to withdrawal of a promised component.
- **Statesman goodwill:** The March 11, 1988 agreement and Resolution 88-169 permit straight-line amortization not exceeding twenty-five years, subject to a satisfactory accountant's analysis within ninety days describing intangibles and substantiating amounts and methods. The lower judgment found the condition satisfied. Approximately $25.8 million of acquisition goodwill is distinct from the capital credit.
- **Statesman capital credit:** The express $26 million credit consists of $21 million of the FSLIC contribution and $5 million debenture principal. It is credited to the regulatory capital account and constitutes regulatory capital under the identified Insurance Regulation §561.13 definition. Its amount and scope do not borrow the goodwill undertaking's twenty-five-year maximum.

##### 2. Accounting priorities, reservations and duration qualify the promises

**Holding and authority:** Eight join the construction that the incorporated specific benefits retain contractual significance alongside ordinary regulatory evolution and the assistance agreements' general terms. That construction does not delete a limitation, make a maximum period mandatory or establish that a termination event occurred. The following qualifications govern the liability holding and any later relief.

**Controlling explanation:** The Government correctly insists that the entire writing be read. A priority clause among agency instruments cannot prevent Congress from legislating; a general reference to amended regulations also cannot be read in isolation from the particular preserved consideration. The specific accounting undertaking explains what was purchased, while compliance and lawful-action provisions preserve obedience to valid law. Ordinary assistance periods do not automatically reduce every separately specified forward accounting benefit to that shorter period. Conversely, the long accounting period does not eliminate a proved termination or dictate the duration of compensable loss. The Court affirms the promise and breach at the scope established by the integrated documents. It does not invoke a general surviving-indemnity clause, infer that a qualifying sale or insurance event occurred, or guarantee payment through every stated maximum. A contractual good-faith-contest proviso is preserved as written but supplies no finding that a contest was made or that public law permits noncompliance. Those distinctions answer both an unlimited regulatory-guarantee construction and an automatic cancellation of the particular negotiated financial undertaking.

##### Operative accounting, compliance and termination qualifications

**Accounting hierarchy and reserved powers.** Winstar §10 begins with GAAP for going-concern savings-and-loan computations except where the agreement, applicable regulations or contemporaneous Board approval requires otherwise. Ambiguity is resolved consistently with the regulations and approval; when those conflict, the Board's transaction resolution or action controls. The section also includes subsequent Board/FASB clarification, interpretation and amendment, with Board requirements controlling conflicts with FASB. Both priority and change language remain operative.

The July 13 letter reserves statutory powers except enforcement of its enumerated waived requirements and their authorizing statutory provisions insofar as waived. The referenced benefits concern direct net-worth credit for the FSLIC cash contribution; the amortization just described; loan-discount recognition by a level-yield method assuming contractual payments for fifteen years and repayment of the balance at that point, or another level-yield method described in R memoranda or other accounting documents acceptable under GAAP; and dividend limits. The dividend waiver permits sixty, seventy and sixty percent of net income for fiscal years beginning July 1, 1985, 1986 and 1987 respectively, while forbidding dividends that reduce net worth below the level §563.13 requires for institutions insured at least twenty years. These ancillary terms identify the reservation's scope and add no separate damages claim. Powers outside those waivers remain reserved; the exception is neither an unconditional cancellation right nor surrender of Congress's authority.

Statesman §17 similarly preserves agreement/approval exceptions to GAAP, resolves ambiguity by the regulations and transaction approval, and gives that approval priority over conflicting regulations. Its changing-accounting language refers to Board changes, amendments, clarifications and interpretations, including the proposal at 52 Fed. Reg. 39112, its staff, FASB and the stated AICPA-successor reference; Board interpretation controls a conflict with FASB or AICPA. Real-estate valuation separately follows the proposed regulations at 52 Fed. Reg. 39070, those regulations as finally adopted by the Board, or any successor regulations then in effect. It is not simply Winstar's clause under another name.

**Compliance, duration and termination.** Glendale's assistance agreement ordinarily terminates after ten years, when FSLIC payment obligations cease. Appointment of a receiver or other official to liquidate Glendale, or termination of Glendale's or its successor's FSLIC-insured status, terminates the agreement and all FSLIC obligations at that event. Contemporaneous resolutions and letters remain preserved; modifications require written agreement by the parties or successors. The agreement requires no unlawful action or inaction. The particular forward accounting benefit is construed with, not equated to, the ordinary assistance/payment term.

Winstar §13(a) ordinarily ends the agreement after two years except as otherwise specifically provided, or at a different date agreed in writing. Appointment of a receiver to liquidate the resulting association, or cessation of its insured-account status, separately terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations at that event. Section 15 requires no unlawful action or inaction. Section 13(b) concerns limited continuing indemnity for the liabilities/claims and related or prosecution litigation expenses it identifies, conditioned on litigation at termination and continued observance of the agreement's conditions, covenants, cooperation and best-efforts duties, with recoveries payable over under its referenced limits. It is not a general continuation of every contractual claim; this judgment does not decide an interaction with a receiver event or find a qualifying indemnity claim.

Statesman §20 requires the acquiring association and its subsidiaries, affiliates and service corporations materially to comply with applicable public laws, regulations, orders and restrictions governing property and business, but does not itself require compliance while applicability is contested in good faith. The proviso establishes neither an actual contest nor an exemption from valid public law. Section 19 ordinarily supplies a five-year term or a different written agreed date, with its specific-exception language and separate §16 sale provision. Appointment of a receiver to liquidate the acquiring association or cessation of its insured-account status terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations. Its lawful-action term remains. FIRREA's enactment within the five-year period defeats only an assertion that ordinary expiration had already eliminated every undertaking; it decides neither other triggers nor recoverable duration.

Statesman §19(b) directs distribution/payment of the Special Reserve Account's net credit or debit at termination under the agreement. Section 19(c)'s identified liability/claim and related litigation indemnities require litigation at termination and continuing covenants, cooperation, reporting and best efforts; recoveries must be paid over under the applicable limitations, and continuation remains subject to the liquidation/insurance terminating proviso. Section 19(d) requires action on reports, applications, requests and other submissions actually received by termination that require FSLIC approval, consent or action, with cooperation and final-accounting treatment. None is a general survival clause or a finding of an eligible claim or pending submission in this case.

**Statesman's separate sale conditions.** During seven years, a defined Sale by the acquiring association of any of its own assets, or of capital stock of a subsidiary or affiliate formed to conduct its operations or hold its assets, requires prior written approval and prompt payment of an agreed percentage of net proceeds, capped at fifty percent. In calculating net proceeds, an appropriate return of the acquirer's investment is allocated in proportion to the assets sold relative to total assets. Sale aggregates sales consummated or agreed during ninety days with an aggregate sales price of $20 million. An unapproved defined Sale during five years terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations immediately after consummation notwithstanding §19; approval lies in FSLIC's sole discretion. The exclusion concerns transfers to an acquirer affiliate of stock or assets and liabilities of Federated Financial, Combined Mortgage Corporation, Homestead Mortgage Corporation or Perpetual Financial Corporation, not every affiliate transaction. Seven-year approval/proceeds duties and the five-year termination consequence remain distinct. No such Sale, approval, excluded transfer or termination is found.

##### 3. Actual authority supports these financial undertakings without disabling legislation

**Holding and authority:** Eight hold that the transaction-effective contracting and assistance powers, exercised through the actual resolutions and approvals, authorized the identified financial undertakings. No separate delegation to prevent Congress from changing the law is required for this ordinary monetary responsibility, because the contracts do not confer that veto. General agency power alone would not cure an undertaking outside actual authority.

**Controlling explanation:** Hercules and Sutton require actual authority for express and implied-in-fact promises alike. Former §1725(c)(3) authorized FSLIC to contract, and the transaction-effective §1729(f) powers authorized the assistance arrangements with their statutory conditions. The authority supporting Glendale included assistance to prevent default or restore normal operation and assistance facilitating merger or assumption of a defaulting institution's liabilities. Contributions and guarantees were limited to the amount found reasonably necessary to save liquidation costs unless continued operation was found essential to adequate community savings or home-financing services. The actual transaction resolutions supplied the required determinations and approvals; no failure of such a condition is established. Winstar and Statesman likewise rest on their effective assistance powers and approvals, not merely a later general description of agency mission. The Government's argument that an agency cannot surrender Congress's regulatory power addresses a different undertaking. Congress remains free to change capital law, and the institutions remain subject to it. Lynch preserves the Government's contractual obligations; the authority and retained-powers cases do not erase an authorized financial promise because the promisor is sovereign. No hypothetical appropriation or delegation defect supplies a defense, and the Court finds no authority for a regulatory injunction or a promise inferred solely from assistance policy.

##### 4. Withdrawal breaches the allocated undertaking; assumed risk defeats discharge

**Holding and authority:** Eight hold that withdrawal of the promised treatment breached these actual financial undertakings. Even assuming the relevant sovereign action is public and general, the sovereign-acts defense and ordinary impossibility do not discharge the change-of-law risk the contracts allocated to the Government. This sufficient holding does not depend on whether every monetary claim escapes unmistakability or whether the challenged measure fails public/general classification.

**Controlling explanation:** Horowitz prevents ordinary contracting liability from disabling public and general sovereign action; it does not place the Government in a better contractual position than a private promisor on a risk that promisor assumed. Ordinary impossibility therefore still requires examination of the bargain's risk allocation. Here the specified regulatory treatment was the subject of the exchange, and its withdrawal is the risk attached to the particular promises. Maintaining legislative power does not make that financial undertaking disappear. Bowen's retained power to amend its statutory arrangement does not prove that every negotiated allocation of monetary consequences is void. The institutions must obey the new law; liability does not compel prohibited reporting or specific performance. FIRREA's supervisory-goodwill limits and OTS's separate capital-credit treatment defeat the promised treatment, but their validity is not the issue. The Government cannot use the occurrence of the allocated risk alone to excuse the contractual consequence. Nor does every later regulation breach a contract: the result depends on the particular duty and qualifications established here. Actual loss, its connection to that breach and the amount recoverable remain different questions.

##### 5. Liability affirmance leaves causation and damages for ordinary adjudication

**Holding and authority:** Eight affirm partial liability and remand for contract-specific causation, compensable loss, mitigation, applicable contractual periods and damages. They do not treat nominal goodwill or capital-credit figures as awards, promise a particular recovery or direct a hearing or trial regardless of what remains disputed.

**Controlling explanation:** A breach of an authorized promise is different from proof of every consequence alleged to follow. Hercules preserves genuine contract remedies while requiring the actual undertaking; it supplies no automatic causal chain or fixed measure here. Each institution must establish the loss recoverable under its own bargain and ordinary contract principles, with applicable defenses and qualifications. The distinct goodwill periods, capital-credit terms, regulatory obligations and any properly proved termination matter to that inquiry; one component cannot borrow another's amount or duration. Nothing in the liability judgment finds that the regulatory change caused insolvency or guarantees that the entire remaining goodwill balance would have been usable. Mitigation and other genuinely remaining issues belong below. The en banc express-contract judgment makes it unnecessary to decide Winstar's implied-in-fact alternative independently, and unadjudicated takings claims receive no merits disposition. Congress's authority and the validity of FIRREA remain undisturbed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Hercules:** Its actual-authorized-duty requirement controls. The Court distinguishes the absent reimbursement undertaking there from the specific integrated promises here, while preserving its limits on fairness-based liability and automatic damages.
- **Sutton and Merrill:** Actual governmental authority remains necessary; neither the Tucker Act nor equitable appeal supplies missing assent or authority. No particular approval defect is found here.
- **Romein:** Regulation governing a relationship is not itself an immutable contractual obligation. These holdings rest on the additional transaction writings and incorporated promises.
- **Cisneros:** The obligation is enforced with its real qualifications, not enlarged into an unconditional payment or regulatory guarantee.
- **United States v. Texas:** An existing obligation is distinct from a mechanism governing its enforcement. It supplies no damages amount or formula in this case.
- **Lynch and Perry:** Genuine government contractual obligations remain legally significant despite sovereign status. This decision preserves legislation and applies the financial undertakings actually made; it does not adopt a general power to enjoin legislative change.
- **Horowitz:** Public/general sovereign treatment does not itself answer ordinary assumed-risk impossibility. The eight-Justice holding rests on allocated risk, not the additional three-Justice substantial-effect theory.
- **Bowen:** Its retained-power and promise-construction principles remain. The Court does not convert the statutory arrangement there into a rule cancelling every express financial-risk undertaking here.
- **Merrion, United States Trust and Home Telephone:** Retained governmental powers and the particular commitments at issue must be distinguished. No majority adopts a universal rule making the sovereign-construction doctrine irrelevant to all money claims.
- **OPM v. Richmond:** Authority and appropriations limits are not erased by monetary relief; no unsupported defect or general equitable right to payment is found.

No earlier decision is overruled. The additional unmistakability and substantial-effect explanations have only the joins stated in the topology; they do not independently alter controlling doctrine.

#### Current Law

An actual authorized promise of identified treatment can allocate the financial risk that valid later law prevents performance. These incorporated agreements did so, with their contract-specific conditions, and the Government is liable for the breach. Even assuming public/general sovereign action, ordinary impossibility does not excuse that allocated risk. Congress retains its legislative authority, and the institutions must comply with valid law.

The decision establishes neither a promise from regulation alone nor compensation whenever regulation changes. Calling a claim damages does not prove formation, authority, assumed risk or a recovery amount. The four-Justice limited-inapplicability account of unmistakability is not a complete Court framework; neither is the three-Justice account applying the canon and finding it met. The Chief Justice joins the narrower common financial-risk holding. The objective substantial-contractual-effect alternative has three votes, not five, and is not controlling.

The actual contract terms govern future proceedings: distinct sums and maximum amortization periods, accounting priorities and changing rules, compliance qualifications, statutory authority, assistance duration, sale restrictions and applicable termination or surviving-claim conditions. No trigger event is presumed. Liability does not establish causation of institutional failure, a goodwill-balance award, a guaranteed recovery period or a takings entitlement. The ordinary damages process remains.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Souter's Part V, joined by Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer:** The unmistakability doctrine protects against an effective surrender of sovereign authority, but these particular financial undertakings leave Congress free to regulate. Lynch's recognition of government obligations and the distinctions in Bowen and Merrion require identifying what the promise and remedy would actually do. A demand for money does not automatically avoid the doctrine: repayment that effectively supplies a tax or comparable sovereign exemption can implicate it. Here, however, the authorized transaction allocated financial risk rather than immunizing institutions from regulation. The Government cannot obtain the acquired liabilities' assumption on the particular promised terms and then erase the ordinary contractual consequence merely by invoking its power to change law. These four distinguish the protected exercise of sovereign power from the limited obligation actually undertaken; they do not declare all damages suits outside unmistakability. Stone does not join this complete threshold account, and Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas instead apply the doctrine.

**Souter's Part VI, joined by Stevens and Breyer:** Independently of assumed risk, these three would reject the sovereign-acts protection where release from the Government's own contractual obligations is a substantial effect of the challenged measure rather than an incidental consequence of public regulation. The inquiry is objective. They do not require subjective bad faith, a predominant contractual-avoidance motive or predominant contractual effect. Inclusion in a broad financial reform statute does not itself make direct release from the promised accounting/capital performance incidental. The particular disallowance of that treatment has the required substantial effect for them. They add no quantitative industry-impact finding, condemn no legislative motive and do not classify every FIRREA provision as non-general. O'Connor withholds this ground; the other affirmers rely on the independent assumed-risk conclusion. The additional theory has three votes.

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurring:** Unmistakability applies and is satisfied. Sovereign contracting displaces the ordinary presumption that a promisor undertakes not to disable performance by its own acts; courts should not lightly infer a surrender or financial undertaking against legislative change. But the unmistakable subject of these bargains was the identified future accounting and capital treatment. The Government need not utter a second promise that it will keep the first before that actual promise has effect. Incorporation, priority, duration, reservation and compliance terms must all be read, not replaced by a general rescue-policy assumption. They find the particular risk assignment clear even under the sovereign-contract canon, while recognizing immediate regulatory permission could form a different bargain in another case.

Actual statutory assistance authority supports the financial commitments. No agency veto over Congress is needed, and no implied-in-law indemnity or universal governmental insurance is recognized. Clear assumption of this risk defeats the asserted sovereign-acts and impossibility discharge. They join the Court's contract, authority, common discharge and remedy parts, not the claim that unmistakability is inapplicable or the separate objective substantial-effect route. Damages still require proof under the individual contracts.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring:** Sovereignty determines whether Congress may change the law; the established contract determines who bears the financial consequences of withdrawing the promised treatment. These express authorized undertakings supply the relevant risk allocation. The doctrines protecting legislative authority from implied surrender cannot erase that actual monetary responsibility, but regulation alone would supply no contract. He joins the Court's common parts and its damages remand without choosing a complete threshold rule between the other affirmers' unmistakability accounts. He does not join the substantial-effect alternative or find bad faith. The result neither requires compensation for every legal change nor surrenders sovereign power or authorizes specific performance contrary to a valid statute. Causation, the losses covered and their amounts remain contract-specific.

**Ginsburg, dissenting from present liability affirmance:** The existence of written assistance arrangements and valuable regulatory permissions does not itself resolve the alleged promise to bear future legislative risk. Unmistakability is a construction rule that must inform identification of the undertaking before a court declares that risk was assigned. Immediate permission to use favorable accounting can be valuable consideration even if no longer-term protection against legislative change was promised. Describing the bargain as risk shifting and the remedy as damages does not answer that prior question.

She requires separate attention to Glendale's incorporation and assistance term, Winstar's reporting permission, regulatory-change and reservation language, and Statesman's distinct goodwill and express capital credit with their own conditions. She does not deny the actual $26 million credit wording or hold that no contract or authorized warranty can exist. Hercules requires the real duty; it does not supply the further continuing promise merely because damages would be lawful if such a promise were proved. General agency authority and partial liability findings reached under an insufficient construction criterion do not settle that dispute.

She would vacate the present interlocutory affirmance and remand for bounded reconsideration under unmistakability for each writing set and each Statesman benefit. She would not order ultimate judgment for the Government, declare a specific delegation absent, adopt a broad sovereign-acts discharge, require a new trial or decide damages and takings. Her disagreement is with present affirmance of the asserted continuing risk undertaking, not a categorical denial of government contract liability or a refusal to recognize express terms.

### Palazzolo v. Rhode Island, No. 99-2047

Merits decision, June 28, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 26, 2001. Certiorari to the Supreme Court of Rhode Island, which affirmed rejection of an inverse-condemnation action, 746 A.2d 707. The questions concern finality of the coastal agency's determinations, acquisition after the regulations, and the distinction between complete and partial deprivation on the whole-parcel premise presented.

Shore Gardens acquired the property in 1959; Palazzolo obtained personal title in 1978 after the corporation's charter was revoked. The later filling applications included a private beach club. The Council's coastal program protected the salt marsh and denied the application for failure to establish the required compelling public purpose. Section 130A permits an exception only when the applicant demonstrates all three conditions: a compelling public purpose benefiting the public as a whole through public infrastructure, a water-dependent activity generating substantial economic gain to the State, or broad public shore access; all reasonable steps to minimize environmental impacts or use conflict; and no reasonable alternative means or location for serving that purpose. Satisfaction permits an exception; it does not compel one. The stated denial does not establish separate findings about the other two conditions.

At least one upland home and substantial retained value, approximately $200,000, are supported. They are not maximum-use or maximum-value findings. Additional sites, necessary access and their feasibility remain disputed. The State did not waive all additional upland possibilities or concede that every possible access road was forbidden. The proposed 74-lot subdivision supplies the owner's $3,150,000 valuation position, not a submitted application, an adjudicated before-value or an award. The wetlands-only denominator was not presented below or in the petition. The asserted 1986 taking date is the owner's contention, not a finding of injury then or at enactment.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The Court affirms rejection of the whole-parcel complete-deprivation theory, reverses the finality and categorical post-regulation-acquisition grounds, and remands the existing case for proceedings under the contextual takings standard and ordinary preservation rules.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Authority common to Parts I–III:** The six-Justice coalition identified in the opinion table directly adopts these parts. **Part IV:** The five-Justice coalition identified there directly adopts the contextual remand. The three threshold dissenters' conditional views are not needed to establish any holding.

##### I. A definitive restriction does not require an equivalent application

**Holding and operative rule.** When the responsible agency has definitively excluded the requested class of use and the established remaining use sufficiently defines the challenged restriction, an owner need not submit materially equivalent proposals to obtain a final decision. These denials and the governing private-filling restriction permit review of the existing compensation claim without another private-filling proposal; they do not fix every permissible upland use or the property's maximum value.

**Controlling explanation.** Suitum separates a completed land-use determination from valuation and other merits consequences: an additional act that cannot change the agency's answer is not a condition of review. Lucas likewise separates reviewability of an established prohibition from proof of a taking. The Council rejected ordinary private wetlands filling under a standard requiring a compelling benefit to the public as a whole. Shrinking the private beach-club proposal would not supply that missing public purpose. The recognized residential upland use also confirms that this is a compensation claim involving retained use, rather than an unresolved request for permission to pursue the same prohibited filling.

The State correctly identifies uncertainty about additional upland sites and access. That uncertainty remains material to the actual burden and the contextual claim; it does not make the settled ordinary-filling restriction provisional. The Court finds no agreed one-home ceiling, universal road prohibition or waiver of all further development. Nor does the unsubmitted subdivision defeat review merely because it supplies a damages valuation rather than a separate permit request. An application remains necessary when its density, design or a genuinely available exception could materially change the relevant agency determination. The rule does not make two denials universally sufficient or permit a litigant to isolate an unpresented parcel.

##### II. Acquisition after regulation is not a categorical defense

**Holding and operative rule.** Acquisition after a regulation's enactment does not by itself bar the claimant's own takings challenge or turn that regulation into an antecedent limitation on property rights. Actual regulatory conditions and acquisition circumstances may bear on reasonable investment-backed expectations within the combined Penn Central inquiry, but notice alone does not extinguish the claim or decide that inquiry.

**Controlling explanation.** Lucas requires an actual background property or nuisance limitation before the State may treat a forbidden use as outside the owner's title; a regulation does not acquire that status merely because title later changes hands. Penn Central evaluates the real economic and legal setting, and Dodd specifically rejects acquisition under an existing restriction as a dispositive substitute for that evaluation. A contrary categorical rule would allow an otherwise compensable public burden to become immune from challenge upon transfer and would burden an original owner's ability to sell.

The Court also rejects the opposite shortcut that regulatory circumstances never matter. The actual conditions, assurances or absence of assurances may affect the reasonableness of expectations when considered with economic impact and governmental character. Mere notice does not receive exclusive weight, create a presumption against later purchasers, cap compensation at the price paid or establish a background-title defense. This rule concerns the claimant's own injury. It does not itself decide when a taking occurred, establish that an earlier owner suffered one, or transfer an accrued compensation claim with title. The separate disposition of that objection belongs to Part IV and Stevens's contrary position.

##### III. Meaningful retained use defeats the total-deprivation theory

**Holding and operative rule.** The whole parcel's meaningful residential upland use defeats the Lucas category requiring complete deprivation of economically beneficial use. That conclusion does not defeat a partial-taking claim under Penn Central, and it requires no finding that one house or approximately $200,000 is the maximum remaining use or value.

**Controlling explanation.** Lucas supplies a categorical compensation rule only when complete economic deprivation has been established; Dodd applies that distinction where profitable use remains. Here the supported upland residential opportunity is substantial, not a nominal interest. Comparing the asserted subdivision value with the retained value does not transform a severe percentage loss into complete deprivation. Neither the owner's proposed before-value nor any particular loss percentage determines compensation.

The case was presented on the whole-parcel premise. A wetlands-only denominator was not raised below or included in the petition and cannot be introduced to supply the missing total-loss premise. The Court adopts no universal parcel-definition rule. The separate contextual inquiry remains necessary where the existing claim calls for it; rejecting the total-loss category establishes neither that environmental regulation is invalid nor that its burden can never require compensation.

##### IV. The existing partial-taking claim receives the combined inquiry

**Holding and operative rule.** The existing compensation claim must be considered under Penn Central's combined inquiry into economic impact, reasonable investment-backed expectations and the character of governmental action, subject to actual preservation requirements and independently supported lawful grounds. Acquisition after enactment, retained value or a legitimate public purpose alone cannot substitute for that inquiry; no compensation result follows from the present remand.

**Controlling explanation.** Penn Central supplies the governing partial-taking inquiry, and Dodd requires its considerations to operate together rather than as competing categorical shortcuts. The lower decisions addressed that framework. The owner's emphasis on Lucas does not erase the federal contextual question they passed upon or preserve an acquisition-only answer to it. The Court returns that existing case, not a new or unrestricted cause of action.

The facts necessary for liability and relief have not been determined here. Remaining development opportunities, actual value, reasonable expectations, background property law and the timing and ownership of any compensable injury must receive their proper work where material. The Court does not find a taking at enactment or in 1986 and does not assume an assignment of a prior owner's claim. Nor does it resolve the State's disputed nuisance theory merely from its public-interest characterization. Genuine background-law defenses and ordinary limits on claim presentation remain available on their actual predicates. Those are matters of application or preserved independent adjudication under the stated rules; the contextual standard itself is settled.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Suitum v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency: applies the requirement of a definitive use determination and its distinction from uncertain valuation; no completed determination is reopened by an act incapable of changing it.
- Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council: applies complete deprivation and genuine background-title limits, preserves ordinary finality and distinct partial-taking analysis, and rejects a percentage-loss substitute for total deprivation.
- Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City: applies the combined economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and governmental-character inquiry; no single consideration is made conclusive.
- Dodd v. Hood River County: applies its distinction between retained use and partial deprivation, its rejection of acquisition alone as dispositive, and its requirement of actual injury and duration; no new claim-ownership finding is attributed to Dodd.

#### Current Law

No precedent is overruled. The holding adds a direct rejection of the categorical successor-acquisition defense and applies existing finality and takings rules to this record. It creates no new parcel formula, price-paid ceiling, automatic compensation, permit entitlement or categorical transfer of an accrued claim.

The contextual takings standard is settled. Its application requires actual findings where material; title history alone supplies neither immunity from compensation nor a transfer of a prior owner's accrued claim. No legal uncertainty about the governing standard authorizes bypassing the combined inquiry.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, concurrence in part and dissent in part.** Stevens agrees that the existing fixed-restriction challenge is final and joins the bounded acquisition and complete-loss holdings. He would nevertheless affirm because he reads this compensation claim as seeking redress for a restriction imposed before the present owner took title. Danforth and Dow distinguish the owner who suffered the taking from a later title holder. Ripeness for review and the time of injury are different questions; repeating a denial under an unchanged restriction does not itself transfer the same property interest again. The owner's asserted 1986 date does not resolve that objection. Stevens makes no finding that a taking occurred at enactment and finds no assignment. He accepts Dodd's requirement of a combined inquiry and therefore does not say that acquisition notice alone reduces all acquired property to a discretionary opportunity for a hearing. A genuinely new post-acquisition deprivation would involve a different supported injury theory. His disagreement is the present owner's compensable injury, not finality or a universal notice bar.

**Ginsburg, joined by Souter and Breyer, dissenting.** They would affirm because the additional upland sites and their access remain materially unresolved aspects of actual permitted use on the same parcel. Suitum concerned an already definitive agency answer; it did not treat uncertainty about on-parcel development as merely a question about saleable rights elsewhere. The State's statement that it would permit a valuable home establishes a floor, not a ceiling, and the trial testimony supplies possible additional sites without establishing issued permission. Their approach requires neither endless resubmission of the same rejected design nor an exact maximum valuation. It requires a sufficiently settled regulatory burden for the whole-parcel challenge actually litigated. If the merits were properly reached, they agree that acquisition alone does not bar the owner's own claim, meaningful retained use defeats total deprivation and a preserved partial-taking claim receives Penn Central's combined inquiry. Those conditional views supply no vote for this remand, no new findings about additional uses and no automatic resolution of title, claim ownership or preservation.
