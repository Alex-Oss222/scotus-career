# Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb: exact public-law reading copy

Mechanically copied bounded Public Projection. The Record controls. Effective June 27, 2002.

## Event

Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb; reviewed Eighth Circuit Nos. 00-1773 and 00-2686, 270 F.3d 567. Merits decision, June 27, 2002, on the full merits record. The Court reviews the November 2, 2001 judgment sustaining Missouri's challenged party-to-candidate contribution restrictions after reconsideration in light of FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee. The presented question concerns reliance on federal circumvention evidence despite the absence of comparable completed Missouri practices and the existence of earmarking controls, together with the justification for the actual party ceilings. The party and candidate plaintiffs seek relief from the restrictions; state officials seek to sustain them.

The restrictions concern actual money and in-kind candidate resources under Mo. Rev. Stat. §130.032.4 and .7, with contribution ceilings and enforcement consequences. Missouri also attributes earmarked contributions to their original source. The federal evidence describes tacit donor-party-candidate understandings, fundraising credits and allocation arrangements that can convey candidate benefit without an express earmark. The Eighth Circuit treated Colorado II as establishing validity as a matter of law and sustained the materially lower Missouri ceilings without a showing that association had become ineffective, also pointing to independent spending opportunities. Those are the challenged legal premises. The separate reliance contention and unpresented discrimination claim are outside this review.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at submission and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment sustaining the challenged party-contribution limits is vacated, and the case is remanded, 9–0, for application of the governing close-fit standard to Missouri’s actual ceilings.

## Holdings

### I. Actual candidate resources remain distinct from independent and joint expression

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may regulate actual party-to-candidate resource transfers and genuine contribution conduits under the contribution standard: government must establish an objectively grounded, sufficiently important corruption or circumvention interest and a restriction closely drawn to it while preserving meaningful association and effective advocacy. Party identity, shared objectives, endorsement or ordinary access alone does not establish an actual candidate-resource connection or corruption; genuinely independent expression remains protected.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by all eight Associate Justices. All nine directly adopt each holding at its stated actual-resource scope. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas separately preserve the distinct joint-expression question.

**Controlling explanation:** Buckley distinguishes financial support delivered to candidates from independent advocacy. Colorado I rejects presumed coordination based solely on party status and protects genuinely independent party expression. Colorado II permits justified regulation of an actually demonstrated circumvention channel, while leaving distinct expressive applications to their own characterization.

This grant concerns real money and in-kind resources conveyed to candidates. It does not require treating every jointly developed party message as candidate-controlled funds. The party has no categorical exemption for actual contributions merely because association with candidates is central to its purpose. Conversely, the State cannot relabel independent advocacy as a contribution from shared ideology alone. The common resource rule therefore resolves the presented category without erasing the expression distinction that the concurrence preserves.

**Precedent treatment:** Buckley's functional distinction and Colorado I's independent-expression rule are applied. Colorado II supplies no universal classification of every coordinated message or automatic validity of every party ceiling.

### II. The federal record establishes a relevant preventive interest beyond formal earmarking

**Holding and operative rule:** Relevant experience outside the State may establish an objective preventive justification without a completed local scandal, mandatory new local study or proof that legislators subjectively relied on that evidence when acting. Here the federal record establishes a materially relevant risk that tacit donor-party-candidate understandings and fundraising credits can circumvent valid candidate limits despite formal earmarking attribution; that resolved interest does not by itself establish close fit of Missouri's actual ceilings.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine direct joins at the genuine-resource and conduit scope. It resolves the relevance and preventive-interest issue rather than postponing it.

**Controlling explanation:** Colorado II's record describes the intermediary mechanism Missouri regulates. Donors can support a party on an understanding that favored candidates will benefit, and candidate fundraising can earn later party support from a general treasury. An express source attribution rule need not detect those tacit arrangements. The State may prevent the demonstrated channel before a completed local evasion or bribe occurs.

The party's demand for exclusively Missouri experience would discard relevant evidence of the same mechanism. Objective justification does not turn on whether the legislature had that later-developed evidence in mind. The lower court's borrowed-evidence analogy is sound to that limited extent. It does not import a different First Amendment category's entire test or permit an unsupported corruption label.

Ordinary political agreement, access and a tally alone do not prove an unlawful transaction. Nor does this evidence show that every contribution is corrupt. It establishes the practical preventive interest; the chosen restriction must still satisfy the separate burden in Part III. Accordingly, the remand is not an invitation to require a new scandal or to repeat the already answered question whether federal experience can matter.

**Precedent treatment:** Colorado II's demonstrated informal-circumvention reasoning is applied. Renton's borrowed-evidence analogy does not displace the contribution-specific burden. Formal earmarking retains its own attribution function without becoming an exclusive remedy.

### III. Missouri must justify its actual ceilings while preserving meaningful association

**Holding and operative rule:** Missouri bears the separate burden to connect the supported circumvention risk to the actual operation and amounts of its challenged party ceilings, showing close fit that preserves meaningful chosen-party association and effective candidate-connected support. A federal ceiling's validity, unlimited independent spending or challengers' failure to prove that expression becomes practically inaudible does not discharge that burden; no fixed dollar floor or universal numerical safe harbor governs.

**Authority:** Part III has all nine direct joins. The three concurring Justices accept this rule for actual transfers without accepting a lower-protection rule for every joint message.

**Controlling explanation:** Russell places the justification burden on government and evaluates actual recipient functions and campaign capacity. Shrink sustains its particular statewide individual ceiling on a supported record, not a presumption that every amount is valid until political speech becomes impossible. Day protects the chosen association and rejects reliance on other available speakers as a sufficient answer to its burden.

Colorado II similarly weighed association costs and the substantial candidate-connected activity still allowed by the federal Senate ceiling. Its federal facial disposition cannot establish the fit of materially lower state ceilings as a matter of law. The inquiry must relate Missouri's actual operation and scale to the mechanism, practical fundraising burdens and capacity for meaningful support.

Independent spending preserves an important freedom but may entail duplication, expense and lost communication; it does not replace the party's chosen candidate-connected channel. The strongest affirmance argument—the repeatability of circumvention—establishes a real interest, not the adequacy of every amount chosen to address it. Conversely, a lower number alone does not establish invalidity. Findings or record-based conclusions must perform the actual fit inquiry, using sufficient existing evidence where available.

**Precedent treatment:** Russell, Shrink and Day are applied to their government-burden and meaningful-association rules. Colorado II is applied at its limited federal record and amount; it supplies no statewide safe harbor or challenger-impossibility threshold.

### IV. Vacatur requires a fit decision and corresponding relief within the presented scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The reviewed judgment cannot stand on automatic validation by Colorado II or a burden on challengers to prove practically ineffective expression. It is vacated and the case remanded for findings or record-based conclusions connecting the already supported mechanism to the actual Missouri ceilings, followed by appropriate relief for any limits the State cannot justify; adequate existing evidence may suffice without a new study or compulsory trial.

**Authority:** Part IV and the formal vacatur/remand have nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court answered a genuine evidentiary objection but treated that answer as conclusive of constitutionality. Because interest and fit do separate work, the Court cannot preserve the judgment on that rationale or declare the ceilings invalid solely from their relative amounts. The proper proceedings apply Parts I–III to the actual limits and record, then determine the relief those conclusions support.

Dollar adequacy remains an application task under settled law. Any severability question requires its proper scope and record. The separate prior-restraint or reliance contention lies outside the grant, and the unpresented discrimination claim is not adjudicated. The Court therefore directs neither blanket invalidation, a replacement amount nor retrospective liability, and it does not require additional fact development where the existing record permits the necessary conclusions.

**Precedent treatment:** Colorado II's limited remedy is distinguished from automatic approval of a different State's ceilings. Russell and Shrink retain record-specific application and remedial boundaries.

## Precedent Treatment

- Buckley v. Valeo: applies the functional distinction between contributions and independent expenditures; legitimate corruption prevention does not dispense with fit.
- Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC, June 26, 1996: preserves genuinely independent party expression and the need for an actual candidate connection; party identity alone is insufficient.
- FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee, June 25, 2001: applies demonstrated informal circumvention and close-fit review; its federal Senate facial judgment establishes neither validity of Missouri's actual ceilings nor a universal classification of joint expression.
- Russell v. Burris: applies government's objective justification burden, actual operation and meaningful-association standard, without converting its office-specific outcomes into a dollar floor.
- Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC: retains its record-specific statewide individual ceiling; it supplies no party-ceiling safe harbor or requirement that challengers prove political expression impossible.
- Day v. Holahan: protects meaningful chosen-intermediary association while preserving realistic conduit regulation; other channels alone do not cure the burden.
- Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics & Election Practices and Mariani v. United States: retain the affirmative contribution burden and the distinction between direct candidate resources and materially different financing arrangements; their other holdings do not decide this ceiling.
- City of Renton v. Playtime Theatres: its borrowed-evidence analogy supports considering relevant outside experience, without replacing the contribution-specific constitutional inquiry.

## Law After Decision

A State may rely on relevant federal experience to prevent demonstrated tacit circumvention of lawful candidate limits without awaiting a local scandal. For these actual party-resource transfers, that interest is established; Missouri must separately justify the operation and amounts of its ceilings under close-fit review preserving meaningful association. Existing evidence may suffice. The decision removes automatic validity and challenger-impossibility premises while preserving independent expression and the distinct joint-message question.

## Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They join the complete opinion and vacatur/remand because the presented restrictions concern actual money, candidate-controlled financial substitutes and genuine conduits. Their Colorado II dissent accepted regulation of those transfers and the relevance of donor-understanding and fundraising-credit evidence. The absence of a completed bribe or express earmark is not conclusive against prevention.

They retain their separate protection for the party's own electoral message, including a jointly developed communication. Consultation can encompass expression beyond paying a candidate's bills or transferring financial control. Party-candidate collaboration serves the party's political purpose, and compelled separation can impose duplication, inefficiency and lost coherence. Unlimited independent speech therefore cannot itself justify a restriction on that chosen joint channel.

Allocation can also reflect electoral prospects and party priorities; a tally is not necessarily an illegal earmark. Source rules, disclosure and tailored conduit enforcement remain relevant alternatives when evaluating a burden on expression. Their prior objection concerned treating the entire coordination category as candidate-controlled support without an adequate operational answer. Today's bounded transfer rule makes no such classification and does not require them to abandon it. They neither demand strict scrutiny for every contribution nor immunize direct resources, undifferentiated transfers or sham conduits. Missouri must justify the actual ceilings under the Court's rule; the remand does not compel automatic invalidity or a new local study.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for a decision on the actual challenged ceilings’ close fit and the resulting appropriate relief under the government’s burden. The relevance of the federal mechanism and sufficient preventive interest are settled. The court may use an adequate existing record and must state findings or record-based conclusions linking risk, operation and amounts. Any necessary severability issue requires its proper record; the excluded reliance and unpresented discrimination claims do not become additional merits questions through this remand.
