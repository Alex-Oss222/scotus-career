## Standards and Tests selection: Prosecutorial investigation

### Prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements

**Governing question:** What instructions govern prosecutorial investigation, advocacy and press statements?

**Current rule:** Ordinary investigative work to identify a perpetrator before probable cause has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for alleged fabrication; later courtroom use does not retrospectively transform it into advocacy. Genuine evaluation and preparation in an advocate's role remain protected; neither all pre-indictment nor all post-indictment acts share one classification. Statements to the press announcing an indictment also lack absolute prosecutorial immunity merely because they concern prosecution or cause injury through subsequent proceedings. Qualified immunity, an actionable constitutional deprivation and other liability predicates remain separate.

Preparing and filing charging papers or a warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions and presenting the State's legal case receive absolute immunity when they are advocacy functions. Bad purpose or alleged false evidence does not remove that protection; the Court adopts no general dishonesty exception. A prosecutor's distinct personal oath supplying facts to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant is instead a complaining-witness evidentiary function without absolute prosecutorial immunity. That classification does not require proof of falsity and leaves ordinary courtroom testimony immunity intact. Denial of this immunity does not establish liability: constitutional deprivation, culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury and available personal defenses remain separate. A judge's warrant does not categorically sever causation where material deception procured it, while a truthful corrected account may still establish probable cause.

**Authority by component:**

- Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity: Buckley v. Fitzsimmons — Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity, 1993-06-24. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- A press conference does not carry an advocate’s absolute immunity: Buckley v. Fitzsimmons — A press conference does not carry an advocate’s absolute immunity, 1993-06-24. Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- Protected advocacy and personal warrant certification: Fletcher v. Kalina, Fletcher v. Kalina — I. Protected advocacy and a distinct evidentiary act (eight Associates for the complete advocacy rule), Fletcher v. Kalina — II. Personal factual certification supporting an arrest warrant (all nine), and Fletcher v. Kalina — III. The immunity ruling permits further proceedings, not a liability judgment (eight Associates for the source-specific causation and ordinary qualified-immunity qualifications), 1997-06-16.

**Present operation effective:** Fletcher v. Kalina, 1997-06-16; Buckley's investigation and press components retain their original operation.

**Holdings navigation:** Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259 (1993). Fletcher v. Kalina (1997-06-16) — Protected prosecutorial advocacy remains distinct from personal evidentiary acts; Fletcher v. Kalina (1997-06-16) — Civil Rights / Prosecutorial investigation and press statements.

## Standards and Tests selection: Culpable material deception

### Culpable material deception in warrants and additional detention

**Governing question:** When official deception defeats an asserted categorical warrant-based or communication-system-based immunity defense.

**Current rule:** Judicial issuance does not insulate an officer personally responsible for knowing or reckless factual falsehood material to probable cause; the corrected truthful account and actual culpability remain necessary. An officer’s deliberate material false communication causing otherwise unjustified additional unreasonable physical detention likewise receives no categorical immunity merely because it used an electronic system or a different custodian and no earlier case involved that same system. Falsehood, culpability, materiality, personal causation and an actual unreasonable seizure remain necessary.

**Authority by component:**

- Material warrant deception; deliberate detention-message deception: Clanton v. Cooper, 1997-12-29; Ginsburg Part II-A’s complete knowing-or-reckless rule has all eight Associates; Part II-B’s deliberate additional-detention rule is unanimous.

**Present operation effective:** 1997-12-29.

**Exceptions and limits:** Negligence, recantation, bad motive, state-law breach or delay alone is insufficient. The same independently lawful detention or absence of material causal responsibility may defeat the claim. No general fabrication right, universal Fourth Amendment duration through prosecution, derivative Fifth Amendment privilege or automatic liability follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Clanton v. Cooper — Collateral appeals from immunity denials; Clanton v. Cooper — Material official deception and qualified immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Circumstantial proof

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

**Authority by component:**

- Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims: Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; nine votes support the rule and its operative limits.

- Crawford-El v. Britton — Proof of unconstitutional motive, 1997-06-16; Stone-Zsela, all nine
- Crawford-El v. Britton — Discovery and stage-specific screening, 1997-06-16; all nine
- Crawford-El v. Britton — Actual causation and independent defenses, 1997-06-16; all nine

**Present operation effective:** Crawford-El, 1997-06-16, rejecting a special proof quantum and derivative categorical discovery barrier while preserving the carried direct/circumstantial-evidence rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims. Crawford-El v. Britton (1997-06-16) — Civil Rights / Qualified immunity and motive evidence.

## Standards and Tests selection: Instant decisions

### Instant decisions during a high-speed police pursuit

**Governing question:** The substantive-due-process fault threshold for a rapidly evolving police pursuit requiring instant competing enforcement and safety judgments.

**Current rule:** In a rapidly evolving high-speed pursuit requiring instant choices among competing enforcement and safety obligations, reckless or deliberately indifferent risk-taking alone does not establish a substantive-due-process violation. A purpose to cause harm unrelated to a legitimate law-enforcement objective is required.

**Authority by component:**

- Kennedy’s six-Justice pursuit rule: County of Sacramento v. Lewis, No. 96-1337. — controlling Holdings, 1998-05-26.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-05-26.

**Exceptions and limits:** The threshold is tied to that urgent decision setting; it does not establish a universal rule for all executive action, all vehicle conduct or circumstances allowing deliberation.

**Holdings navigation:** County of Sacramento v. Lewis — Substantive due process in rapid police pursuits.

## Standards and Tests selection: Police restraint

### Police restraint and involuntary transfer into known danger

**Governing question:** When an officer’s own restraint and forced transfer create a due-process duty to respond to danger.

**Current rule:** An officer who actually restrains a person’s ability to protect herself and personally directs or carries out her involuntary transfer into a serious danger the officer actually appreciates may not deliberately and unreasonably disregard that danger when the officer has authority and a real opportunity to respond. Injury must be attributable to that defendant’s conduct.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 1: direct-restraint due-process rule: City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-01.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-01.

**Exceptions and limits:** Negligence, constructive or pooled knowledge, genuinely voluntary departure, and a reasonable protective response that fails are insufficient. This is no general duty to protect everyone from private danger.

**Holdings navigation:** City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence — Knowing dangerous restraint and arbitrary selective enforcement.

## Standards and Tests selection: Hearing misconduct

### Hearing misconduct and reviewable NLRB remedial discretion

**Governing question:** Whether deliberate employee falsehood at a remedial hearing forfeits reinstatement or backpay under NLRA §10(c).

**Current rule:** Deliberate hearing falsehood does not automatically forfeit reinstatement or backpay for an established unfair labor practice. The Board may withhold relief for supported misconduct, but need not impose categorical forfeiture. The separate prohibition for an employee actually suspended or discharged for cause concerns the actual employment ground; later testimony does not establish that earlier ground. The Board must recognize material misconduct and provide reasons connecting its remedy to the Act. Review remains available for arbitrariness, inadequate explanation or departure from statutory purpose, without requiring a new sanctions formula or separate express forfeiture discussion in every case.

**Authority by component:**

- No automatic forfeiture and distinct discharge-for-cause bar: ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB — Hearing falsehood and the statutory remedy, 1994-01-24. Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices. Scalia and O'Connor agree with the lawfulness of the result in a separate judgment concurrence.
- Explanation and review of delegated remedy: ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB — Review of this remedial choice, 1994-01-24. Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices. Scalia and O'Connor find the action lawful on their narrower statutory-discretion ground, without endorsing the Court's public-remedy assessment.

**Present operation effective:** ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB, 1994-01-24.

**Open question:** A falsehood undermining the violation finding or a remedial predicate may present a different case; no automatic relief entitlement is created.

**Holdings navigation:** Hearing falsehood and the statutory remedy; Review of this remedial choice.

## Standards and Tests selection: After-acquired misconduct

### After-acquired misconduct and age-discrimination remedies

**Governing question:** The effect of misconduct discovered after an allegedly age-discriminatory discharge.

**Current rule:** Misconduct unknown to the employer at discharge does not erase an ADEA violation or categorically eliminate all relief. The employee retains the liability burden. To limit relief, the employer must prove misconduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone, not merely that discharge would have been permissible. Once proved, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily inappropriate; backpay’s starting measure runs from unlawful discharge to actual discovery, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests.

**Authority by component:**

- Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation: Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation, 1995-01-23. O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this proposition and explanation; it is a direct majority holding.
- Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief: Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief, 1995-01-23. O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the proof requirement and bounded remedial rule.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-23; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Actual age motivation, ultimate persuasion and separate willfulness requirements remain unchanged. A litigation assumption of discrimination does not establish liability.

**Holdings navigation:** Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation; Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Protection against serious

### Protection against serious avoidable harm in compulsory custody transitions

**Governing question:** The manner of state-compelled severance of an established caregiving relationship to implement otherwise lawful custody entitlement.

**Current rule:** The child has an independent liberty interest against serious, avoidable injury attributable to the manner of compulsory implementation. Before implementation, the child's representative must receive a meaningful, prompt opportunity to present concrete evidence of that transition harm and practicable means of avoiding it; the entitled parent must be able to respond, and the court must consider the issue under Mathews and give a reasoned implementation decision. Mathews requires attention to the identified private interest, error risk and value of further procedures, and governmental interests and burdens. Procedures and protective measures must fit the actual showing, proceed promptly and preserve lawful parental custody entitlement.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded child interest: I. The child's own interest limits the manner of compulsory implementation; fitted process and relief: II. The required opportunity and relief must fit the actual transition claim, 1996-06-21; Stone-Zsela Parts I–II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Baby Richard v. Kirchner, 1996-06-21.

**Exceptions and limits:** Caregiving duration does not itself prove serious injury. No right to preserve an invalid adoption, defeat a fit parent through a best-interests comparison, or remain indefinitely with nonparents follows. Unsupported requests may be rejected and adequate consideration may support immediate implementation. There is no automatic evidentiary hearing, fixed delay, appointed-clinician requirement, prescribed proof burden or renewed custody contest. Independently lawful emergency protection remains available.

**Holdings navigation:** Baby Richard, by his guardian ad litem, v. Kirchner (In re Petition of Kirchner).

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

- Required persuasive consideration of tariff-letter reasons: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the Customs-letter application and specified persuasive inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).

## Standards and Tests selection: No direct federal-agency

### No direct federal-agency Bivens damages action

**Governing question:** Whether sovereign consent itself permits constitutional damages against a federal agency.

**Current rule:** A waiver removes sovereign immunity but does not create constitutional damages liability. Bivens’s individual-officer action does not extend directly to a federal agency: that extension would create a new defendant and public obligation. Independently, even assuming compensation and deterrence benefits, the expansion of direct federal fiscal liability and redesign of the remedial structure counsel against judicial creation of that agency action. Existing individual Bivens, Davis and Carlson actions and genuinely authorized statutory remedies retain their own scope; no adequate substitute remedy is presumed.

**Authority by component:**

- Separation of waiver from agency cause: FDIC v. Meyer — Waiver does not create a direct agency Bivens action, 1994-02-23. The cause-of-action portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this independently sufficient ground for rejecting the agency award.
- Independent fiscal-and-remedial reason against extension: FDIC v. Meyer — Independent reason to leave this fiscal and remedial extension to Congress, 1994-02-23. The independent fiscal-and-remedial portion of Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Nine Justices adopt this independently sufficient alternative ground.

#### Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-7849.
**Decided:** June 24, 1993.

##### Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity

**Holding:** A prosecutor who performs ordinary investigative work to obtain evidence identifying a perpetrator before probable cause has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for alleged fabrication in that function; later courtroom use does not transform the earlier work into advocacy. Genuine evaluation and preparation in an advocate’s role remain protected, and neither all pre-indictment acts nor all post-indictment acts receive a categorical classification.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Imbler v. Pachtman, 424 U.S. 409: protected advocacy and genuine preparation preserved; ordinary investigation distinguished.
- Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478: function-specific limits applied to investigation.
- Forrester v. White, 484 U.S. 219, and Antoine, June 7, 1993: function and claimant’s burden control; importance to litigation is insufficient.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Fletcher v. Kalina, June 16, 1997, Ninth Circuit No. 95-36129: the functional distinction extends to a prosecutor’s personal arrest-warrant certification while preserving protected advocacy.

##### A press conference does not carry an advocate’s absolute immunity

**Holding:** A prosecutor’s statements to the press announcing an indictment have no absolute prosecutorial immunity merely because they concern a pending prosecution or cause injury through later judicial proceedings. The ordinary qualified-immunity inquiry and the requirement of an actionable constitutional deprivation remain separate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. Genuine advocacy preparation and unreviewed in-court presentation immunity remain; paid or coerced witness allegations require initial treatment below. Qualified immunity, constitutional injury, causation and damages are unresolved; defamation alone is not held actionable.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand: no absolute immunity for the pleaded preliminary fabrication (6–3) or press statements (9–0). No liability or witness-immunity change.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Imbler, 424 U.S. 409, and Burns, 500 U.S. 478: press activity lacks the protected advocacy function.
- Tower v. Glover, 467 U.S. 914: policy alone cannot create a new §1983 absolute immunity.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800, and Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635: separate qualified-immunity law left unchanged.

#### Fletcher v. Kalina

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit No. 95-36129; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 16, 1997.

##### Protected prosecutorial advocacy remains distinct from personal evidentiary acts

**Holding:** A prosecutor has absolute immunity under §1983 for preparing and filing an information or warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions, and presenting the State's legal case when performing an advocate's function. An allegation of bad purpose or false evidence does not remove that protection from the advocacy function, but the protection does not extend to a distinct personal evidentiary certification merely because the same prosecutor performs both acts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins protection of legitimate advocacy and separation of the personal evidentiary act, but not refusal of an additional dishonesty exception.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The function, rather than a categorical pre-charge or post-charge boundary, determines immunity. No general dishonesty exception to otherwise protected advocacy is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Imbler preserves charging and courtroom advocacy; Burns separates legal presentation from other acts. Buckley preserves genuine preparation while excluding ordinary investigation; Antoine requires historical and functional justification rather than importance to adjudication.

##### A prosecutor personally certifying arrest-warrant facts acts as a complaining witness

**Holding:** A prosecutor who personally swears to factual assertions offered to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant performs the evidentiary function of a complaining witness and has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for that certification. This classification depends on the personal evidentiary undertaking, not on proof of falsity, and does not withdraw immunity from ordinary courtroom witness testimony.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, unanimous.

**Material application:** Kalina personally guaranteed the facts offered to obtain Fletcher's arrest warrant; that evidentiary undertaking is separate from preparing and presenting the charging papers.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither actual falsity nor the availability of another truthful affiant determines the functional classification. Ordinary courtroom testimony immunity remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the denial of absolute immunity for the personal certification and remand, while preserving immunity for protected legal preparation, filing and presentation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Malley supplies the warrant-affiant comparison; official prosecutorial title does not immunize the same evidentiary function. Buckley and Antoine extend their functional distinction to the personal certification, without changing their other boundaries.

##### The function-specific immunity denial leaves constitutional liability and personal defenses unresolved

**Holding:** Denial of absolute immunity for the personal oath leaves Fletcher to establish an actionable constitutional deprivation, the required culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury; Kalina retains qualified immunity and other available personal defenses. The existing qualified-immunity framework governs the remand, using the law and information relevant to the conduct at issue, without a new defense-burden rule or a finding that any element or defense has been established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Stevens's opinion, adopted in full by all eight Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the unresolved-liability and bounded-remand propositions and accepts the existing qualified-immunity framework, without joining the source-specific causation and notice explanations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A judge's warrant does not categorically break causation when material deception procured it, but a corrected truthful account may still establish probable cause. Neither question is resolved here. Disputed historical facts follow ordinary procedural rules; legal immunity is for the judge. No damages judgment or mandatory trial follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hafer preserves personal-capacity liability only on proof of the officer’s own violation and preserves personal defenses. Harlow and Anderson retain objective, particularized clearly-established-law protection. Elder requires consideration of all relevant precedent; Lanier rejects categorical source and factual-twin restrictions, without supplying retrospective notice.

#### Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-7247.

**Decided:** 1994-06-06.

##### Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger

**Holding:** A prison official violates the Eighth Amendment by disregarding a substantial risk of serious harm only if the official knows facts indicating that risk, actually appreciates the risk, and fails to respond reasonably. A danger that an official merely should have discovered does not establish deliberate indifference; an official who responds reasonably is not liable under this rule even if harm occurs.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this rule; Thomas joins only the judgment.

**Material application:** The official need not foresee the precise attacker, timing or mechanism when the known danger encompasses the prisoner's situation. No defendant is found to have the required knowledge, responsibility or causal role; reasonable but unsuccessful protection remains outside deliberate indifference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: applies its distinction between deliberate indifference and negligence without reopening the accepted conditions framework.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its separate objective and subjective requirements and specifies actual-awareness recklessness for this failure-to-protect claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517: applies its recognition of custodial protection duties; it supplies no automatic liability from an assault.
- Hudson v. McMillian, February 25, 1992: preserves the nontrivial malicious-beating holding and leaves its unresolved broader force standards untouched; no universal malicious-and-sadistic requirement enters this conditions claim.

##### Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment

**Holding:** Actual awareness may be proved by ordinary circumstantial evidence, including an obvious risk, and does not require an express warning by the prisoner. Obviousness permits but does not compel an inference: a genuine failure to appreciate the risk defeats the knowledge element, whereas deliberately refusing to confirm strongly suspected facts or their obvious inference may establish awareness; summary judgment here must be reconsidered without treating Farmer's silence as dispositive.

**Proposition-level authority:** The proof-and-remand portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight adopt its reasoning; all nine support vacatur and remand.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The transfer officials may contest their knowledge, responsibility, causation and reasonable response. The Court does not combine different defendants' innocent information into collective knowledge, find the alleged assault proved, or decide qualified immunity. The event-date rule is Rule 56(f).

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its actual culpability requirement without making a prisoner warning an additional element.
- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: preserves the negligence boundary while allowing ordinary evidence of deliberate indifference.

##### Current danger governs preventive relief

**Holding:** Prospective protection requires an established current substantial danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse that persists through the litigation and is likely to continue. The court may consider later developments bearing on present danger and knowledge; litigation notice cannot create past damages liability, and officials may defeat prospective relief by showing that the danger or culpable response has ceased and will not recur.

**Proposition-level authority:** The prospective-relief portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and the bounded remand.

**Current-force modification:** The constitutional liability rule is unchanged. PLRA §802 now separately conditions prospective relief in a federal-law civil prison-conditions action, excluding habeas challenges to the fact or duration of confinement: the court must find the relief narrowly drawn, no broader than necessary to correct the particular plaintiff's federal violation and the least intrusive necessary means, giving substantial weight to adverse public-safety or criminal-justice effects. Relief requiring or permitting officials to exceed state or local authority, or otherwise violating state or local law, additionally requires that federal law permit that contrary relief, that it be necessary to correct the federal violation and that no other remedy correct the violation; the statute authorizes neither prison construction nor tax increases. Section 802(b)(1) applies amended §3626 to prospective relief granted before, on or after enactment. No existing order is terminated by admission, and the original constitutional holding does not alone establish these additional statutory findings.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Terre Haute warden and Bureau Director were sued only in official capacities; the Oxford warden, case manager and two regional officials faced both-capacity claims. Equitable discretion and appropriate internal grievance procedures are not converted into a new mandatory exhaustion holding. No later statutory exhaustion regime is applied.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Helling v. McKinney, June 18, 1993: applies preventive protection against serious future harm and its direction to examine current conditions.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: retains the separate culpability requirement for the threatened conditions violation.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: preserves the presented individual-officer damages channel without creating a Treasury claim.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves the individual-officer Eighth Amendment damages source; damages entitlement remains unproved.
- FDIC v. Meyer, February 23, 1994: applies the distinction between an individual claim and an unavailable direct agency constitutional damages action.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: preserves review of immunity using all relevant law existing when the conduct occurred; this decision supplies no retroactive notice in 1989.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit's judgment is vacated and the case remanded for reconsideration of the Rule 56(f) request and summary judgment without an express-warning prerequisite. Discovery may be allowed or denied on a proper basis, and renewed summary judgment remains available. Knowledge, responsibility, causation, reasonable response, capacities and immunity remain individualized. No trial, damages or placement injunction is ordered; prospective relief must fit any established continuing danger.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Prison Litigation Reform Act of 1995, Pub. L. 104-134, Title VIII, enacted April 26, 1996: §802, additional §3626(a)(1) findings and express prospective-relief transition. This is a noncase-law change, not an overruling or new judicial holding.

#### Crawford-El v. Britton

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 94-7203; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### Ordinary proof governs unconstitutional motive in this retaliation claim

**Holding:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides the quantum Kimberlin reserved; it neither abolishes every specificity practice nor resolves Leatherman’s individual-officer pleading reservation beyond the barriers decided here. ##### Necessary discovery cannot be barred by the rejected heightened proof requirement

**Holding:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court may clarify allegations, resolve actual dispositive legal defenses and limit discovery to material issues. No fixed discovery plan, particular deposition, unrestricted inquiry, mandatory trial or new interlocutory appeal follows.

**Holding:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the heightened motive burden and dependent directions; preserve the existing remand otherwise. Resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. #### Pinder v. Johnson

**Docket or dockets:** Fourth Circuit No. 93-2125; no Supreme Court docket stated in the Record
**Decided:** May 21, 1996

##### Do protective assurances or custody of a private assailant establish a custodial duty to protect the threatened family?

**Holding:** Official knowledge of danger, promises of protection, and custody of the private assailant do not establish a custodial special relationship with a mother or her children. An alleged affirmative deprivation of otherwise available private protection remains analytically distinct from an omission of public protection; absence of custody of the threatened persons does not itself resolve that different claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling rationale. Part I's separate appellate holding appears under Federal Courts, Qualified-immunity appeals and presentation.

**Controlling explanation:** DeShaney rejects a protection duty founded merely on official knowledge and assurances, but its discussion of increased vulnerability does not fully resolve this alleged affirmative course of conduct. The substantive-due-process inquiry concerns the official's own deprivation and the culpability attached to that deprivation, not simply the making of a statement. Daniels excludes mere negligence; the Court does not decide its reserved questions about recklessness or gross negligence. Pinder alleged that Johnson's assurance induced her to return to work rather than provide or arrange private protection. That favorable account must remain distinct from a general right to police protection.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court establishes no general constitutional duty to detain an assailant, warn a victim, or fulfill every protective promise. It does not hold that custody is indispensable to every affirmative-danger claim, or decide the constitutional merits of the assumed affirmative-deprivation theory.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- DeShaney rejects knowledge-and-promise protection duties without deciding this entire affirmative-increased-vulnerability theory.
- Daniels excludes merely negligent deprivation while leaving its reserved culpability questions unresolved here.

##### Was Johnson's alleged conduct sufficiently established as unconstitutional by March 10, 1989 to defeat qualified immunity?

**Holding:** Qualified immunity turns on whether all relevant law existing when the officer acted made the particular conduct's unlawfulness apparent to a reasonable officer, without demanding a factual twin. On the favorable allegations here, law existing March 10, 1989 did not sufficiently establish that this noncoercive undertaking, release of the assailant, and uncorrected exposure constituted Johnson's own substantive-due-process deprivation; he therefore receives individual immunity on the reviewed claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Part II's notice standard joined by all nine Justices; Part III's application and affirmance joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, a five-Justice majority. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissent from the immunity disposition; their substantive theory is not controlling.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson requires particularized apparent unlawfulness, not identical prior facts, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant law. DeShaney had rejected an omission-based duty while leaving the distinct affirmative theory incomplete. Bowers discussed active official wrongdoing but rejected release-based liability; Archie discussed obstruction of private aid in a materially different culpability setting and reached an adverse outcome; Jensen's discussion of a distinct danger did not preserve a knowledge-and-promise special relationship after DeShaney. Those sources did not sufficiently identify the assumed noncoercive interference here as the officer's own deprivation. A judge's disagreement, the number of judges on each side, or an officer's subjective belief in legality does not independently establish immunity. Later cases cannot retroactively supply the missing conduct-time notice.

**Material application:** The Court assumes that the assurance caused the mother to return to work and forgo alternative protection and that the remaining favorable allegations are true. It does not award immunity because actual reliance was absent, choose disputed facts against Pinder, or reject constitutional liability on the merits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding is notice-specific and claim-specific. Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets do not supply retroactive notice; Love establishes no mandatory merits-first sequence. Neither broader affirmative-danger liability nor the ultimate culpability and causation merits is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fourth Circuit's reviewed individual-immunity judgment for Johnson is affirmed, 5-4. No new constitutional merits judgment or damages award is entered. Official-capacity and municipal claims, equal protection, section 1985, state wrongful-death and survival claims, and other excluded matters remain outside review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**
- Anderson supplies the objective, particularized notice inquiry without an exact-facts requirement.
- Elder requires review of all relevant contemporaneous authority rather than an isolated citation or subjective belief.
- DeShaney supplies the rejection of a general protective duty but does not itself resolve the assumed affirmative-deprivation merits.
- Bowers, Archie and Jensen are considered as conduct-time circuit-law notice sources; their distinct facts, outcomes and culpability limits are preserved, and they are not converted into Supreme Court holdings.
- Collins, Hodge, Doe, Love and Sheets cannot establish notice for conduct preceding those decisions; Love does not impose mandatory sequencing.

#### Baby Richard, by his guardian ad litem, v. Kirchner (In re Petition of Kirchner)

**Docket or dockets:** Illinois Supreme Court No. 78101; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** June 21, 1996.

##### Whether a child has an independent liberty interest in the manner of compulsory custody transfer

**Holding:** When the State abruptly severs an established caregiving relationship to implement an otherwise lawful custody transfer, the child has an independent liberty interest against serious, avoidable injury from the manner of that compulsory implementation. That interest permits a claim directed to concrete transition harm and practicable means of avoiding it; it confers no right to retain an invalid adoption, remain indefinitely with nonparents or defeat a fit father's custody entitlement through a best-interests comparison.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Part I, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five Justices.

**Material application:** The interest concerns personal security during affirmative state enforcement. Years of caregiving identify the relationship but neither prove serious injury nor establish permanent custody rights. Neither state action nor lawful custody entitlement alone resolves an actual implementation injury.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary grief, a preferred-home comparison and unsupported delay do not establish the protected injury. The adoption’s invalidation and the fit father’s lawful custody entitlement remain fixed. This §1257 review is not barred by Ankenbrandt’s limited diversity exception.

##### What opportunity and relief the supported implementation claim requires

**Holding:** Before compulsory implementation in these circumstances, the child's representative must have a meaningful, prompt opportunity to present concrete evidence of serious transition harm and practicable measures to avoid it, and the father must have an opportunity to respond; the state court must consider that issue under Mathews and give a reasoned implementation decision. Procedures and any protective measures must fit the actual showing and preserve the father's entitlement: unsupported requests may be rejected, immediate implementation may follow adequate consideration, and no automatic evidentiary hearing, fixed delay or renewed custody contest is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela’s Part II, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Material application:** Mathews applies after identification of the bounded liberty. Competent evidence must concern actual circumstances; the father may contest it. Expedition protects both his entitlement and the child’s need for a settled home. A supported brief transition may be permissible when necessary to avoid the serious harm shown; it is not mandatory.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic full trial, clinician, recurring professional approval, visit count, special proof burden, fixed delay or permanent nonparent contact is required. Independently lawful emergency protection remains possible; no emergency or actual injury is found.

- **Stanley v. Illinois, 405 U.S. 645, and Lehr v. Robertson, 463 U.S. 248:** Preserve parental relationship and responsibility protections; the implementation inquiry cannot reopen Kirchner's lawful entitlement.
- **Santosky v. Kramer, 455 U.S. 745:** Preserves its termination and error-allocation principles without importing its proof burden or establishing a right to retain this adoption.

**Holding:** Misconduct unknown to the employer when it discharged an employee cannot defeat liability for an age-discriminatory discharge or categorically bar every remedy. **Holding:** To limit relief through after-acquired evidence, the employer must prove misconduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone had it known, rather than merely showing that discharge was permissible. ### ABF Freight System, Inc. v. National Labor Relations Board, No. 92-1550

Merits decision, January 24, 1994

#### Chronology and Posture

510 U.S. 317. Argued December 1, 1993. Certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 982 F.2d 441, which enforced the Board order. The granted question is whether deliberate false testimony before the administrative law judge requires forfeiture of reinstatement and backpay.

All nine participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

#### Judgment

The judgment enforcing reinstatement and backpay is affirmed, 9–0.

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Automatic forfeiture for hearing falsehood | Reject, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Enforcement affirmed |
| Case-specific arbitrariness or inadequate explanation | No ground to disturb this order, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | No explanatory remand, new sanction or recalculation |

The established unlawful-discharge finding and remedy despite the original false excuse are outside the granted question. Both reinstatement and backpay remain enforceable.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | Affirm | Both holding blocks, seven votes |
| Concurrence | Kennedy | No other Justice | Affirm; joins Court in full | Honesty in workplace and adjudicative proceedings |
| Concurrence in judgment | Scalia | O'Connor | Affirm | Lawful discretion without endorsing its exercise |

There is an Opinion of the Court; no narrowest-ground inference is needed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Hearing falsehood and the statutory remedy

**Controlling proposition:** Under NLRA section 10(c), an employee's deliberate false testimony at the remedial hearing does not automatically forfeit reinstatement or backpay for an established unfair labor practice; the Board may withhold relief for supported misconduct but is not required to apply a categorical forfeiture rule. The separate statutory prohibition applies when the employee was actually suspended or discharged for cause; later hearing testimony does not itself establish that earlier discharge ground.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices. Scalia and O'Connor agree with the lawfulness of the result in a separate judgment concurrence.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress assigned this remedial choice to the Board, within the Act's limits. Section 10(c) authorizes affirmative action that effectuates labor policy and separately withholds reinstatement and backpay when the actual suspension or discharge was for cause. Phelps Dodge places the relationship between remedy and labor policy primarily within the Board's competence. Virginia Electric preserves judicial review when relief pursues ends outside that policy. False testimony is serious, but a court cannot convert that concern into a forfeiture command Congress did not enact. The remedy addresses an established employer violation and the public interest in lawful labor relations, as well as the employee's loss. Precision Instrument's equitable refusal of relief does not displace the statutory allocation of authority here. The Board may address misconduct through its lawful remedial discretion, and independently authorized sanctions remain available. The Court neither awards automatic employee entitlement nor orders a criminal sanction; the accepted unlawful-discharge premise remains outside this limited review.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Phelps Dodge Corp. v. NLRB: applied to the Board's primary responsibility for connecting relief with the Act's policies.
- Virginia Electric & Power Co. v. NLRB: applied to the judicially enforceable boundary against remedies pursuing unrelated ends.
- Precision Instrument Manufacturing Co. v. Automotive Maintenance Machinery Co.: distinguished; equitable misconduct principles do not impose an extra categorical restriction on section 10(c).
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB: preserved; its binding statutory construction limits Board authority but does not decide remedies for this established unlawful discharge.

##### Review of this remedial choice

**Controlling proposition:** A Board remedy remains reviewable for arbitrariness, an inadequate explanation or departure from section 10(c)'s statutory purpose; the Board must recognize material misconduct and give reasons sufficient to connect its selected remedy to the Act, but need not adopt a new sanctions formula or expressly discuss a separate forfeiture sanction in every case. On this record, the Board's recognition of the false account, its accepted finding of antiunion discharge and its stated restorative labor rationale support enforcement without a further explanatory remand.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven Justices. Scalia and O'Connor find the action lawful on their narrower statutory-discretion ground, without endorsing the Court's public-remedy assessment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court evaluates the Board's actual reasons, not a sanctions analysis the record does not establish. The Board accepted that Manso's excuse was false, yet found that ABF used tardiness as a pretext for retaliation. Those findings are premises of the limited question before the Court. Reinstatement and backpay repair that labor violation, and the Board's explanation provides a sufficient connection between the relief and the statutory policy. Phelps Dodge protects that delegated judgment from substitution of a court's preferred remedy; Virginia Electric permits intervention when the connection to the Act fails. Here the repeated falsehood does not reopen the established violation or identify a defeated remedial predicate. A lie about ability to perform the restored job, for example, could require a different remedial assessment. The Court does not infer that the Board separately balanced a perjury sanction. Nor does it equate every disbelieved employer witness with proven perjury. The absence of a mandatory forfeiture rule leaves genuine review of inadequate reasons and arbitrary relief intact.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Phelps Dodge Corp. v. NLRB: applied to review the remedy's statutory connection without replacing agency discretion with judicial policy.
- Virginia Electric & Power Co. v. NLRB: applied to preserve meaningful review of the actual remedial explanation and statutory purpose.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB: distinguished on the issue decided; enforcing a statutory limit and reviewing an expressly delegated remedy are different inquiries.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

No earlier holding is overruled. The Court preserves ordinary sanctions for false testimony without ordering one or importing an additional forfeiture rule into section 10(c).

Section 10(c) supplies delegated, reviewable discretion rather than either automatic forfeiture or automatic entitlement. Actual discharge for cause remains a distinct statutory bar. Recognition of material misconduct and a sufficient statutory explanation remain necessary; a separate express sanctions balance is not an invariable requirement. This known false account and accepted retaliatory-discharge finding do not require disturbing the remedial order. The Court does not decide how a falsehood undermining a violation finding or a remedial predicate would affect a different order.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Kennedy concurs, joined by no other Justice, and joins the Court's opinion in full. He stresses that truthful dispute resolution serves employees and employers as well as the Board and the courts. Dishonesty can damage workplace trust even when it does not defeat the established unfair-labor-practice finding. That legitimate concern falls within the Board's remedial discretion and should not be trivialized. His emphasis permits consideration of a supported misconduct sanction; it does not require a new formula, reopen the original discharge or impose a sanction here.

Scalia concurs in the judgment, joined by O'Connor. They agree that the Board acted within section 10(c), so judicial disagreement with its exercise of lawful discretion does not authorize reversal. They nevertheless criticize its failure to confront the injury that deliberate hearing falsehood inflicts on an administrative proceeding culminating in a federal judgment. Precision Instrument illustrates a lawful tradition of refusing discretionary relief for misconduct; it does not compel the Board to do so here. They distinguish Manso's demonstrable falsehood from testimony about employer motives that a factfinder simply disbelieved. They do not join an affirmative assessment that the Board treated hearing dishonesty appropriately, and they do not infer an unrecorded balancing of sanctions. Their concern preserves the distinction between legally permissible relief and the remedy they would favor; neither concurrence supplies a controlling mandatory-sanction rule.

### Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, No. 91-7849

#### Chronology and Posture

**Question:** Whether prosecutors have absolute immunity from § 1983 damages claims for allegedly fabricating evidence during preliminary investigation and making false statements at a press conference announcing an indictment.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Investigative fabrication is not protected by absolute prosecutorial immunity

**Controlling proposition:** A prosecutor who performs ordinary investigative work to obtain evidence identifying a perpetrator before probable cause has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for alleged fabrication in that function; later courtroom use does not transform the earlier work into advocacy. Genuine evaluation and preparation in an advocate’s role remain protected, and neither all pre-indictment acts nor all post-indictment acts receive a categorical classification.

**Authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the prosecutor must justify immunity for the function actually performed. Imbler protects initiating and presenting a prosecution and genuine preparation for those acts. Burns, Forrester and the Antoine decision reject immunity based merely on official title or an activity’s importance to litigation. The complaint describes a joint effort with detectives to secure a boot identification during the preliminary investigation of an unsolved crime. On those allegations the prosecutors were searching for evidence needed to build a case, not preparing an established advocate’s presentation. Their later use of the evidence before a grand jury or at trial does not change the earlier function. The competing view correctly observes that advocacy can include out-of-court preparation, but that principle does not establish the protected function alleged here. The Court does not decide qualified immunity, ultimate constitutional injury or damages. Unreviewed immunity for the actual in-court presentations remains undisturbed, and unresolved allegations about paid or coerced witness statements require initial treatment below.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Imbler v. Pachtman, 424 U.S.409: protected advocacy and genuine preparation preserved; ordinary investigation distinguished.
- Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S.478: function-specific limits applied to investigation.
- Forrester v. White, 484 U.S.219, and Antoine, June 7, 1993: function and claimant’s burden control; importance to litigation is insufficient.

##### A press conference does not carry an advocate’s absolute immunity

**Controlling proposition:** A prosecutor’s statements to the press announcing an indictment have no absolute prosecutorial immunity merely because they concern a pending prosecution or cause injury through later judicial proceedings. The ordinary qualified-immunity inquiry and the requirement of an actionable constitutional deprivation remain separate.

**Authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that informing the public is not the judicial advocacy function protected by Imbler. The challenged press conference neither initiates a prosecution in court nor presents the State’s case to an adjudicator. Burns requires historical and functional support for absolute immunity, and none supports this out-of-court press function. The Seventh Circuit’s focus on whether injury depends on a later judge’s decision confuses the merits of a damages claim with immunity for the challenged act. A prosecutor’s title cannot convert public statements into judicial advocacy when other executive officials making comparable statements receive no such absolute protection. Tower also forecloses creating a new immunity simply because the Court considers it desirable policy. Rejecting absolute immunity does not establish that defamation alone violates the Constitution, that these statements caused compensable harm, or that qualified immunity is unavailable. Those questions remain for the appropriate proceedings after reversal of the categorical immunity ruling.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Imbler, 424 U.S.409, and Burns, 500 U.S.478: press activity lacks the protected advocacy function.
- Tower v. Glover, 467 U.S.914: policy alone cannot create a new §1983 absolute immunity.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S.800, and Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S.635: separate qualified-immunity law left unchanged.

Effective June 24, the pleaded investigative fabrication and press statements lack absolute prosecutorial immunity. Genuine advocacy preparation and unreviewed in-court presentation immunity survive. No constitutional liability, damages award, witness-immunity change or qualified-immunity revision results.

Kennedy, joined by White and Souter, agrees that press statements lack absolute immunity but views the expert consultation as an advocate’s preparation for presenting evidence. Imbler protects preparatory evaluation as well as the eventual use of testimony; denying that protection, in their view, allows an immunity challenge to be relabeled as a challenge to preparation. They reject probable cause as a universal line between investigation and advocacy and would affirm the fabrication ruling. Scalia joins the Court but emphasizes the claimant’s obligation to establish historical immunity. He also distinguishes immunity from an actionable deprivation: mere manufacture of evidence, without constitutionally harmful use, may not establish a §1983 claim. His observation does not decide that remaining question.

The affirmance does not authorize liability for protected preparation, filing or legal presentation. The constitutional violation, required culpability, materiality, causation, damages, qualified immunity and other personal defenses remain unresolved. No damages judgment is entered or reversed, and no mandatory trial is ordered.

#### Holdings

##### I. Protected advocacy and a distinct evidentiary act

**Holding and operative rule:** A prosecutor has absolute immunity under §1983 for preparing and filing an information or warrant motion, evaluating evidence for those submissions, and presenting the State's legal case when performing an advocate's function. An allegation of bad purpose or false evidence does not remove that protection from the advocacy function, but the protection does not extend to a distinct personal evidentiary certification merely because the same prosecutor performs both acts.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, eight Justices. Stone-Zsela joins the protection of legitimate charging and advocacy and the need to distinguish the personal evidentiary act, but not Part I's refusal to recognize an additional exception for knowing fabrication or deliberately false material assertions causing a constitutional deprivation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the act performed determines the immunity. Imbler protects initiating a prosecution and presenting the State's case; Burns preserves the advocate's presentation to a judge while requiring a separate inquiry into other functions. Buckley preserves genuine evaluation and preparation for advocacy while withholding absolute protection from ordinary investigative evidence gathering. Antoine requires historical and functional justification rather than reliance on an act's importance to judicial proceedings. Those decisions require protection for Kalina's legal preparation and presentation without treating every act in the charging transaction as advocacy. A prosecutor may present another person's evidence without personally guaranteeing its truth. Separating those functions therefore does not expose the protected legal work merely by relabeling a claim. The line also does not turn on whether advocacy occurred before or after probable cause, or on whether an allegation of dishonesty ultimately succeeds. The Court does not adopt a general dishonesty exception to absolute immunity for otherwise protected advocacy.

##### II. Personal factual certification supporting an arrest warrant

**Holding and operative rule:** A prosecutor who personally swears to factual assertions offered to establish probable cause for an arrest warrant performs the evidentiary function of a complaining witness and has no absolute prosecutorial immunity for that certification. This classification depends on the personal evidentiary undertaking, not on proof of falsity, and does not withdraw immunity from ordinary courtroom witness testimony.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Kalina's personal oath supplies evidence rather than the advocate's legal presentation of another person's evidence. Malley denies absolute immunity to the officer who supplies a warrant application and provides the relevant comparison; prosecutorial title does not change the function of furnishing the factual assurance needed to procure process. Imbler and Burns protect advocacy, but their protection does not absorb a separately assumed witness function. Buckley and Antoine require that distinction even when the acts contribute to one prosecution. Kalina's claimed protection therefore fails as to her personally executed certification. The question is not whether someone else actually would have supplied a truthful certification, nor whether Kalina knowingly lied. Those are not premises necessary to classify the act. Nor does this holding make every sworn statement actionable: it concerns the factual certification used to obtain this arrest warrant, preserves ordinary testimony immunity, and leaves constitutional injury and the remaining defenses for further proceedings.

##### III. The immunity ruling permits further proceedings, not a liability judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** Denial of absolute immunity for the personal oath leaves Fletcher to establish an actionable constitutional deprivation, the required culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury; Kalina retains qualified immunity and other available personal defenses. The existing qualified-immunity framework governs the remand, using the law and information relevant to the conduct at issue, without a new defense-burden rule or a finding that any element or defense has been established.

**Authority:** Part III of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stone-Zsela joins the unresolved-liability and bounded-remand propositions and accepts the existing qualified-immunity framework as governing because his proposed reforms are not adopted; he adds no separate ground on the source-specific causation and notice explanations below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a function-specific denial of immunity is not a decision on damages liability. Hafer permits a personal-capacity §1983 claim while preserving personal immunities and the plaintiff's obligation to establish the officer's own constitutional violation. A judge's issuance of the warrant does not categorically break causation when material deception procured it; a truthful, corrected account may nevertheless establish probable cause. Neither culpability nor the content and effect of that corrected account is decided here. Harlow and Anderson preserve the existing objective inquiry into clearly established law in the particular context. Elder requires independent consideration of relevant precedent on the preserved immunity issue, and Lanier rejects a categorical demand for a factually identical prior case. Neither later decision supplies retrospective notice of unlawfulness. Disputed historical facts remain subject to the ordinary procedural rules, and the legal immunity determination belongs to the judge. The broader fourth question presented cannot turn this interlocutory immunity appeal into a determination of constitutional liability. The lower court must separate any claims resting only on protected advocacy from those resting on the personal oath; no damages award, mandatory trial or automatic finding against either party follows.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Imbler v. Pachtman, 424 U.S. 409:** Preserved for prosecutorial advocacy; distinguished from personally supplying the warrant-supporting factual oath. The Court adopts no general dishonesty exception to otherwise protected advocacy.
- **Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478:** Applied to protect legal presentation to a judge while requiring historical and functional justification for protection of a different act.
- **Malley v. Briggs, 475 U.S. 335:** Applied to the personal warrant-affiant function; a prosecutor does not gain absolute protection for the same evidentiary undertaking through official title. The warrant's issuance does not by itself resolve responsibility for material deception.
- **Buckley v. Fitzsimmons, 509 U.S. 259:** Applied and extended to distinguish personal factual certification from genuine advocacy preparation; its investigation, press-activity and qualified-immunity boundaries remain intact. No categorical pre-charge/post-charge line is adopted.
- **Antoine v. Byers & Anderson, Inc., 508 U.S. 429:** Applied its historically justified functional inquiry; importance to adjudication does not itself establish absolute immunity.
- **Hafer v. Melo, 502 U.S. 21:** Applied the separation between personal-capacity liability, an actionable constitutional violation and personal defenses; official authority alone does not bar the suit.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald, 457 U.S. 800:** Preserved the existing objective clearly-established-law protection; no replacement qualified-immunity test is adopted.
- **Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635:** Preserved the particularized inquiry into apparent unlawfulness without requiring a prior case with identical facts.
- **Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510:** Applied independent consideration of all relevant precedent on the preserved immunity issue, with notice fixed to the conduct date and claim/evidence preservation limits intact.
- **United States v. Lanier, decided March 31, 1997:** Applied its rejection of categorical source and factual-twin restrictions on fair warning; it supplies no retrospective notice and does not decide Kalina's immunity.

#### Law After Decision

From June 16, 1997, absolute prosecutorial immunity does not protect a prosecutor's personal factual oath used to procure an arrest warrant. Protected preparation and legal presentation remain distinct and retain absolute immunity; the charging transaction cannot be classified as a single undifferentiated act. The holding adds that function-specific certification rule without changing ordinary testimony immunity, the elements of §1983 liability or the existing qualified-immunity framework. Stone-Zsela's proposed dishonesty exception, replacement test and defense-burden allocation are noncontrolling. No actual constitutional violation, falsity, materiality, causation, damages or ultimate personal defense is determined.

#### Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and in the judgment; no other Justice joins.** He joins the distinction between legitimate charging or advocacy and personally swearing to factual assertions as a complaining witness. He also joins affirmance and the bounded remand: the constitutional violation, materiality, causation, damages and remaining personal defenses must be determined below, with no present finding of liability. He withholds the Court's refusal to recognize a narrow exception to absolute immunity for knowingly fabricated evidence or deliberately false material assertions causing a constitutional deprivation. Honest mistakes and disputed factual judgments would not satisfy that proposed exception.

He would also replace the existing qualified-immunity formulation with a Contextual Fair-Notice and Reasonable-Conduct Test. The proposed test protects genuinely reasonable mistakes assessed in light of the official's responsibilities, available information and governing law, excludes knowing constitutional violations and objectively indefensible conduct, and places the burden of establishing the defense on the official. These proposals receive no additional joins and are not holdings of the Court. Because they are not adopted, he accepts the existing qualified-immunity framework as governing the remand. His opinion neither finds deliberate falsehood nor imposes liability.

**Justice Scalia, concurring, joined by Justice Thomas.** Both join the Court's opinion in full. Scalia explains that the modern functional categories do not reproduce every feature of the common law: the privilege for testimony concerned defamation, while a complaining witness could face malicious-prosecution liability for procuring proceedings even without personally testifying. Following Imbler under stare decisis and Malley's treatment of the warrant affiant supports the present result without creating a general exception for all sworn statements. Their agreement concerns procuring arrest through the personal factual certification and does not make ordinary defamation a constitutional tort or dispense with constitutional injury, causation or the remaining defenses.
