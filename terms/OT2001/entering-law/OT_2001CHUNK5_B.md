## Standards and Tests selection: Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

### Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

**Governing question:** What instructions govern forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)?

**Current rule:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of forfeited error only if there is error, it is plain, and it affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, unlike failure timely to assert it. Plain means clear or obvious under current law. The defendant ordinarily must show outcome-affecting prejudice. Even then correction is discretionary and should occur only if the error seriously affects fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings; actual innocence is unnecessary, but substantial-rights impact alone is insufficient. Presence of alternates instructed not to participate supplies neither prejudice nor a presumption without a showing of participation or chilling effect. Initially unsettled trial law, special outcome-independent categories, justified prejudice presumptions and hearing authority remain unresolved. Johnson assumes substantial-rights injury without deciding whether withholding materiality is structural or harmless; its fourth-condition application supplies no general guilt-based exception or innocence prerequisite. When settled law permitted the trial action but intervening controlling law makes it erroneous during direct review, plainness is measured at appellate consideration. A forfeited omission of a genuine jury element proceeds under the distinct Rule 52(b) conditions rather than automatic reversal.

**Authority by component:**

- Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority: United States v. Olano — Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority, 1993-04-26. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Correction remains discretionary: United States v. Olano — Correction remains discretionary, 1993-04-26. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Instructed alternates do not establish prejudice by presence alone: United States v. Olano — Instructed alternates do not establish prejudice by presence alone, 1993-04-26. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

- Johnson v. United States — I. The jury-element violation is reviewed as forfeited error, 1997-05-12; Stone-Zsela, all nine
- Johnson v. United States — II. Plainness is measured at appellate consideration in this intervening-law setting, 1997-05-12; Stone-Zsela with Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight

**Present operation effective:** Johnson, 1997-05-12, resolving plainness where trial law was settled to the contrary.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993). Johnson v. United States (1997-05-12) — The jury-element violation is reviewed as forfeited error; Johnson v. United States (1997-05-12) — Plainness is measured at appellate consideration in this intervening-law setting.

## Standards and Tests selection: Present-offense facts authorizing a higher sentencing maximum

### Present-offense facts authorizing a higher sentencing maximum

**Governing question:** Which additional present-offense facts must receive constitutional element protections.

**Current rule:** Under the Sixth Amendment jury guarantee as applied to the States and the Fourteenth Amendment’s Due Process Clause, a fact about commission or consequences of the present offense is an element when it is legally necessary to authorize punishment above the maximum supported by the verdict or plea. The fact must be validly admitted as an element or proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt. A sentencing label, strong evidence, notice of possible exposure or unused aggregate punishment on other counts does not supply the missing verdict or admission. Judicial findings may continue to select punishment within existing conviction-based authority.

**Authority by component:**

- Apprendi v. New Jersey; No. 99-478. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, Present-offense facts necessary for a higher maximum require element protections, 2000-06-26; Stone-Zsela with Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six).

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-26; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Prior-conviction facts are undecided, and no constitutional recidivism exception is created. State charging form and Fifth Amendment grand-jury incorporation, unchanged-maximum minima, general consecutive sentencing, capital eligibility or selection, and materially different systems including general Guidelines classifications remain open. McMillan and Walton are not overruled. No universal structural-error or aggravator-omission harmless-error rule follows; waiver retains its independent conditions.

**Related current rules:** Aggravated unlawful-reentry elements and authorized maximum; Original armed carjacking and cumulative firearm punishment; Firearm-type elements under former section 924(c)(1). Those statute-specific federal charging rules remain independent of this new constitutional rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Reliable Guidelines use of a valid fine-only misdemeanor

### Reliable Guidelines use of a valid fine-only misdemeanor

**Governing question:** When a valid uncounseled misdemeanor can affect a later counseled Guidelines sentence.

**Current rule:** A valid uncounseled misdemeanor punished only by a fine under Scott may be used in the later counseled Guidelines sentencing setting when the defendant has a genuine opportunity to contest its reliability and seek the authorized §4A1.3 departure for overrepresentation of criminal history or recidivism risk. The narrow controlling agreement rests on that safeguarded use, not on a general rule for every enhancement. Ordinary scoring is not optional merely because departure is permitted. Section 4A1.3 requires reliable information, not a prior arrest alone, and a downward criminal-history departure cannot go below category I.

**Authority by component:**

- Four plurality votes plus Souter’s narrower safeguarded-use agreement: Nichols v. United States — This Guidelines use of a valid fine-only misdemeanor is permissible, 1994-06-06. O'Connor's plurality, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, permits this application under a broader valid-conviction rule; Souter's concurrence in the judgment expressly permits this Guidelines application on the narrower reliability-and-opportunity ground. Five Justices support the stated permission at this level; no complete rationale has five joins, and Souter supplies no fifth vote to overrule Baldasar generally.

**Present operation effective:** Nichols v. United States, 1994-06-06.

**Open question:** Mandatory enhancements without that opportunity, invalid uncounseled imprisonment convictions and a general overruling of Baldasar are not established. The decision imposes no universal warning requirement for misdemeanor pleas.

**Holdings navigation:** This Guidelines use of a valid fine-only misdemeanor is permissible.

## Standards and Tests selection: Capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver

### Capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver

**Governing question:** What instructions govern capacity to plead or waive counsel and independent valid waiver?

**Current rule:** Dusky's standard governs capacity to stand trial, plead guilty and choose to waive counsel: sufficient present ability to consult with counsel with rational understanding, and rational as well as factual understanding of proceedings. A competent person's actual waiver must separately be knowing, intelligent and voluntary. Further competence determination is necessary when circumstances give reason to doubt present competence, not automatically for every plea or waiver. Existing competence findings do not settle informed, uncoerced relinquishment; then-effective treatment of state findings remains independent.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prospective officer relief for ongoing federal property violations

### Prospective officer relief for ongoing federal property violations

**Governing question:** Whether a federal tribal-property claim may proceed against responsible state officers despite disputed title or sovereign interests.

**Current rule:** An alleged ongoing violation of federal tribal property rights may support prospective declaratory or injunctive relief against officers actually connected to it. A disputed federal title question or important sovereign interest does not alone bar the claim. Relief must govern proved unlawful future official conduct; it may not bind the immune State's title, award damages or retrospective compensation, execute against state property or order the State itself to convey title. A general obligation to obey law does not supply the required officer connection.

**Authority by component:**
- Officer route and real remedial boundary: Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe, Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe of Idaho — An ongoing federal property-right claim may proceed against responsible officers within real remedial limits, Stone-Zsela's five-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Present operation effective:** Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene Tribe, 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** State-law commands remain barred by Pennhurst. The rule decides neither title nor every requested remedy, and supplies no sovereign-lands exception or general balancing of state interests. The distinct IGRA officer route does not supply procedures or a general State-amenability ruling for this claim.

**Holdings navigation:** Idaho v. Coeur d’Alene Tribe of Idaho (1997-06-23) — Federal Courts / Prospective officer suits and disputed tribal property rights.

## Standards and Tests selection: Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim

### Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim

**Governing question:** Whether Eleventh Amendment immunity affecting some claims defeats removal of an otherwise federal-question action.

**Current rule:** An action containing substantial §1331 federal claims is removable under §1441(a) even when Eleventh Amendment immunity bars particular State or official-capacity claims. Proper assertion of immunity prevents adjudication of the affected claim; it does not eliminate jurisdiction over the other federal claims or require remand of the entire action under §1447(c).

**Authority by component:**

- Immunity affecting one claim does not eliminate removal jurisdiction over remaining federal claims: Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-22.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-22.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule decides neither whether removal itself waives immunity nor every unasserted-immunity issue, and does not supply jurisdiction over a claim lacking its own necessary foundation.

**Holdings navigation:** Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht, 524 U.S. 381 — Removal jurisdiction and claim-specific state immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Intervention before a shareholder

### Intervention before a shareholder’s derivative-settlement appeal

**Governing question:** Whether an otherwise nonparty shareholder may appeal a derivative settlement solely after appearing and objecting under Rule 23.1.

**Current rule:** Notice, shareholder interest and a heard objection under Rule 23.1 do not themselves create appellate party status. An otherwise nonparty shareholder must properly become a party through intervention to appeal the derivative settlement judgment. The opportunity to assist the settlement court by objection differs from authority to continue the corporate litigation on appeal. Review by an actual party and an appropriate appeal from an actual intervention denial remain available under their own conditions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit

### Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit

**Governing question:** Whether explicit prospective regulation and continued ordinary commercial activity establish State consent to private federal enforcement.

**Current rule:** A State’s continued operation of an otherwise lawful commercial undertaking is not unequivocal submission to federal jurisdiction merely because Congress clearly and prospectively subjects that activity to private federal enforcement, even if the undertaking is realistically optional. Unmistakable congressional intent remains distinct from the State’s voluntary act of consent; an opportunity to leave ordinary commerce does not itself constitute acceptance of a specifically offered federal benefit or voluntary invocation of judicial authority. A State’s continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes federal patent suit likewise is not consent. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available.

**Authority by component:**

- College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit”, 1999-06-23; Part III with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part IV, all nine Justices. Applies College Savings Bank Part III to continued operation of the prepaid-tuition program after the express patent remedy took effect; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept that holding as controlling.

**Present operation effective:** College Savings Bank and Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Actual voluntary waiver, properly conditioned federal benefits and interstate compacts remain distinct. Those decisions do not resolve particular funding or coercion questions, litigation-waiver acts or governmental-function classifications. Bradley separately supplies IDEA-specific consent from actual voluntary acceptance under its express funding condition; other benefits and coercion questions remain distinct. Hilton’s retained FELA coverage and specialized state-court enforcement remain intact.

**Former-rule note:** The residual Parden inference of federal-forum consent from ordinary commercial participation fails even under the new explicit prospective enactment. This does not wholesale overrule retained substantive FELA coverage or Hilton’s state-court rule.

**Open question:** Whether a State’s litigation conduct—appearance, a §1404(a) transfer request or requests for declarations of invalidity, noninfringement and unenforceability—waives immunity remains undecided. Florida Prepaid need not decide it because its Section 5 and Article I holdings sustain the judgment, and it makes no waiver finding.

**Holdings navigation:** [College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board — Continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit](HOLDINGS.md#continued-ordinary-commerce-is-not-unequivocal-consent-to-federal-suit); [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit](HOLDINGS.md#continued-commercial-participation-does-not-supply-consent-to-this-suit).

**Related current rules:** IDEA funding acceptance and program-specific federal-suit consent supplies Bradley’s bounded accepted-benefit holding of June 12, 2000, without changing the commercial-participation rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article I enforcement against States in federal court

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part III with Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Independently sufficient alongside the Section 5 holding.

**Present operation effective:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires a valid enumerated-power obligation, an enforcement measure within that power and unmistakable congressional authorization; enacted clarity alone does not supply power. It does not rest on commercial-participation consent, which College Savings Bank rejects, and decides no actual litigation waiver. Validity, infringement, culpability and each remedy’s statutory and properly presented constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication; no compensation, injunction, fee or enhanced award is preapproved. The action is not converted into an officer suit; Young, Coeur d’Alene, Edelman and Pennhurst retain their boundaries.

**Former-rule note:** Union Gas’s retained five-vote constitutional conclusion supplies the foundation; the rule extends that conclusion without treating every proposition in its plurality rationale as binding. Seminole Tribe separated unmistakable statutory authorization from constitutional amenability and set aside the State-dismissal direction without a controlling constitutional rationale; it established no categorical Article I prohibition, overruling of Union Gas or abrogation-before-officer sequence. Florida Prepaid resolves the constitutional question Seminole left open.

**Related current rules:** “Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement” supplies the independent Section 5 ground for the same patent remedy. “Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers” separately governs the state-court forum. “Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit” remains intact.

**Holdings navigation:** [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action](HOLDINGS.md#article-i-independently-sustains-the-expressly-authorized-action).

## Standards and Tests selection: Telecommunications network elements and bounded access

### Telecommunications network elements and bounded access

**Governing question:** Which telecommunications elements may require access and which incumbent combinations and approved-agreement arrangements remain available.

**Current rule:** Section 153(29)’s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment as well as physical facilities. Eligibility does not itself require unbundling. Under §251(d)(2), proprietary necessity and general impairment must independently constrain element selection, including consideration of realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent network. Any additional cost or quality decline, with outside alternatives categorically ignored, is insufficient. Technical feasibility may matter but cannot replace those statutory constraints. No irreplaceable-monopoly or numerical threshold is imposed.

Section 251(c)(3) supplies no freestanding requirement that a requesting carrier own local facilities. Particular access remains subject to lawful criteria and conditions. Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prevents dismantling already-combined requested elements unless the requester seeks separation; separately available or valued elements need not be physically destroyed. Under §252(i), Rule 51.809 permits access to an individual arrangement in an approved agreement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions, subject to proof of greater provision cost or technical infeasibility and availability only for a reasonable period after public filing. A favorable price cannot be detached from its relevant obligations.

## Standards and Tests selection: Lemon test

### Lemon test

**Governing question:** How the Lemon test governs Establishment Clause claims.

**Current rule:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases. Clear cases apply that test; its former independent prongs do not remain a separate or fallback method. Neutral eligibility, parental selection and restrictive endorsement do not cure public payment for demonstrated devotional instruction integrated into the financed educational work without reliable separation. The inquiry concerns the instructional function purchased; religious identity alone does not establish the defect, and genuinely independent private benefits, private forum access and reliably separated secular services remain distinct.

For publicly administered equipment and library-book assistance, the actual funded instructional function governs. Actual financing of religious instruction or demonstrated religious use under recipient control as the funded instructional function requires correction; neutral allocation, public title or a secular label cannot cure it. Religious affiliation or location, control, fungibility, possible diversion and incidental savings, singly or accumulated, do not themselves prove forbidden financing. Reliably lawful secular use or an independently protected benefit cannot be condemned categorically because the object is equipment or a library book. Meek and Wolman lose force only to the extent their equipment-category reasoning conclusively condemns that relationship by recipient character or presumed diversion; unrelated holdings and applicable safeguards remain. Effective correction must reach proved prohibited use while preserving independently lawful applications; a categorical declaration cannot substitute for that scope inquiry.

**Authority by component:**

- General inquiry and operative qualifications: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function, 1997-06-23. Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.
- Object of the inquiry, evidence role and Holding I limits: Agostini v. Felton — I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function (controlling explanation and precedent treatment), 1997-06-23; the same five Justices.
- Safeguarded secular instruction: Agostini v. Felton — II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location, 1997-06-23. Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

- Jackson v. Benson — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Parental routing does not justify the demonstrated purchase of integrated devotional instruction”, 1999-03-16; O’Connor Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

- Mitchell v. Helms; No. 98-1648. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, 1. Neutral allocation does not cure the actual publicly financed religious function; 2. Actual use is established here; affiliation and possible diversion cannot replace proof; 3. Equipment and library-book form do not justify a categorical prohibition; 4. The categorical declaration must be replaced by effective relief at the proved scope, 2000-06-28; Breyer Parts I–III with all eight other Justices; Part IV with all seven other Associates (eight).

**Present operation effective:** Mitchell, June 28, 2000, for equipment/library-book category displacement and proof-and-remedy limits; Agostini’s June 23, 1997 unified inquiry and Jackson’s March 16, 1999 financed-function components retain their original force.

**Object of the inquiry:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Safeguarded secular instruction on religious-school premises:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Exceptions and limits:** Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. An institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone does not excuse devotional instruction or diversion; independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain.

**Former-rule note:** Lemon's independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs ceased to govern as separate or fallback tests on June 23, 1997 (Agostini v. Felton, Holding I); those considerations now serve as evidence within the updated Lemon test. Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises (Agostini v. Felton, Holding II, June 23, 1997); no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed.

**Open question:** Jackson resolves only the demonstrated provider-cost arrangement purchasing integrated devotional instruction; it adopts no universal voucher prohibition or exclusion of religious providers and no constitutional entitlement to subsidy. Other voucher arrangements, monuments, legislative prayer, general accommodation and other aid retain their actual governing rules. Witters and Mueller remain effective for their actual private-benefit relationships.

## Standards and Tests selection: Official school worship and independent religious pressure

### Official school worship and independent religious pressure

**Governing question:** Officially organized school worship, delegated representative religious choices and independently coercive school prayer.

**Current rule:** Officials violate the Establishment Clause by selecting clergy, requesting invocations and benedictions, guiding their religious content and incorporating the worship into the official ceremony. Nonsectarian wording and optional attendance do not cure that sponsorship; no separate punishment or quantified-pressure showing is required for this ground. Independently, school-directed clergy prayer is forbidden when the school's control and the occasion's importance place objecting adolescents under substantial pressure to participate, appear to participate or forgo the ceremony. Optional attendance and standing or remaining silent do not necessarily remove that pressure; mere offense is insufficient. The official-sponsorship prohibition also applies when a school organizes, adopts, directs or religiously supervises worship as part of its own event but uses student initiation or delivery or a statutory disclaimer. Those features alone do not make the worship private, and the sponsorship ground remains independent of quantified pressure. The official-worship prohibition also reaches a public university’s selection of a religious leader and incorporation of actual invocation and benediction prayers into commencement. Mature attendance, meaningful freedom to abstain, brevity, nonsectarian language and a sincere secular ceremonial purpose do not cure that official worship; independent clergy composition does not make it private forum speech. Separately, actual pressure to participate in school-directed worship in compulsory or comparably pressured settings violates the Establishment Clause; offense at a genuinely private viewpoint alone does not suffice.

A school may not make an invocation an official representative component of its own ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that component includes religious exercise. Such a mechanism is not transformed into private access by student authorship, optional attendance, possible silence or secular speech, or nonsectarian/nonproselytizing limits. The defect does not require a prayer already delivered or official editing of its words. Genuinely voluntary private prayer, independently chosen student expression, lawful secular messages and neutral private access remain protected; the holding adds no separate religious-pressure test.

#### Lee v. Weisman, 505 U.S. 577 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-1014.

##### Whether officials may organize clergy-led worship at public secondary-school graduation

**Holding:** Public secondary-school officials violate the Establishment Clause when they select clergy, request invocations and benedictions, guide the prayers' religious content and incorporate that worship into the official graduation program. **Limits and questions not reached:** The holding does not prohibit genuinely private prayer or private forum speech, replace the general Establishment Clause framework, or resolve adult legislative prayer, funding, denominational aid or universal accommodation questions. **Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the First Circuit and preserve the prospective injunction against officials' authorizing or encouraging the challenged clergy-led graduation prayer practice. ## Holdings source: first-amendment / Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

#### Agostini v. Felton; Chancellor, Board of Education of the City of New York v. Felton

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.
**Decided:** June 23, 1997.

**Material judgment:** Both judgments vacated and remanded, 5–4, for tailored prospective Rule 60(b)(5) relief; Lemon modified as the governing test; Aguilar's categorical rule overruled and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale displaced at the stated scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** All four holdings are delivered by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela in the Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Each has five direct joins at its stated level of generality, including the modification of Lemon in Holding I. No aggregation of separate opinions supplies controlling authority.

##### I. Lemon governs through one inquiry into governmental religious authority and function

**Holding:** Lemon remains the governing Establishment Clause test, modified so that purpose, effects and entanglement are evidence within one inquiry: a specific prohibition or distinct doctrine controls when it resolves the conduct; an established permissible relationship defeats the claim on that ground; otherwise a legally sufficient unresolved allegation is decided through attribution and function under ordinary procedure. These are three routes within the updated Lemon test, not freestanding prongs, exemptions from review or an alternative test for uncertain cases.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of the Opinion of the Court; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas join this modification in full, supplying five direct joins at this level of generality.

**Operative qualifications:** The court identifies the governmental act—an official message, organized event, exercise of supervisory power or expenditure—and the function it performs. For financing, the question is what government purchases or performs; all religious conduct of the recipient need not become government speech. Private-access authorization, religious identity and physical location are relevant in context but are not individually decisive.

Government may not organize worship as its own activity, prescribe religious profession or observance, adopt doctrine as governmental truth, solicit religious adherence through public authority, govern theology or confer civil authority on a religious basis. Denominational classifications retain their distinct strict-scrutiny rule. Independently identifiable secular work in a religious setting may be purchased with enforceable controls against devotional use and diversion. Restoration of private exercise restricted by custody or comparable governmental control remains subject to the applicable accommodation inquiry, not an automatic financing prohibition. Those distinct doctrines retain their own sources and burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** Lemon addresses governmental sponsorship of religion, financing of religious functions and the improper union of civil and religious authority. Its method must identify those acts rather than condemn an independently secular service because officials supervise it to remain secular. Aguilar exposes that defect: location and repeated contact become substitutes for examining what the public program actually does. The Court therefore modifies Lemon's operation while retaining its constitutional concerns.

Purpose and effects help identify the governmental function; institutional relationships show who directs it. Once prohibited conduct is established, actual conversion, religious success or a completed penalty is unnecessary. A secular objective cannot justify prohibited religious means. Conversely, a provider's religious motivation does not make its secular work devotional. Ordinary monitoring of secular performance is not itself establishment, even when repeated; civil control of doctrine and religious control of public authority remain forbidden.

This method requires real operational separation and reliable accountability for funded work, but not invariably separate institutional governance or wholly distinct personnel. Shared structures cannot excuse devotional financing, and they do not automatically defeat a genuinely severable public service. Here public selection, staffing, materials and supervision identify the remedial service to be examined. The school's entire religious mission is not the governmental act.

The dissent's institutional-support objection warrants scrutiny of the service, not a presumption that its secular and religious functions are inseparable. Existing specific holdings continue to govern their established relationships: Kiryas Joel restricts denominational allocation of civil power; Rosenberger permits its controlled publication benefit; Pinette and Chabad distinguish private expression and governmental attribution. The Court does not decide absent voucher, monument, legislative-prayer or accommodation programs, or declare every future application compatible with every preserved precedent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides no tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme, monument, legislative-prayer program, general accommodation rule or all forms of direct aid. Parental selection does not itself answer what a particular expenditure purchases; no conclusion about an absent program displaces Witters or Mueller. Religious editorial viewpoint in Rosenberger is not automatically purchased devotional instruction. Private speech, free exercise, institutional religious autonomy, denominational scrutiny and accommodation retain their distinct sources and burdens. No controlling rationale is drawn from separate opinions under Marks.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lemon v. Kurtzman remains governing, with its independent purpose, primary-effect and excessive-entanglement prongs modified into the three routes of this single inquiry. The actual holdings of Kiryas Joel, Rosenberger, Pinette, Chabad, Witters and Mueller remain in force; Zobrest supplies no Establishment Clause merits holding. Kiryas Joel's denominational-allocation and accommodation holdings remain intact; its preservation of Aguilar and Ball established the rule entering this decision, not a bar to the Court's express overruling. Zobrest's antecedent-law remand remains; it supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding and did not already undermine Aguilar by such a holding. Rosenberger's actual controlled vendor-funded printing benefit remains permissible on its program terms; it concerns financing, not merely facility access, and neither decided this public-teaching case nor authorized every devotional expenditure. Pinette's bounded private-expression and access holding remains; it supplies no universal forum immunity or automatic attribution from location. Chabad's contextual-attribution and access holding remains; location alone does not establish governmental sponsorship. Witters's actual benefit holding remains in force; no absent tuition-voucher or provider-redemption scheme is decided. Mueller's actual tax-benefit holding remains in force; the Court does not decide an absent funding program or silently enlarge or displace that holding.

##### II. Separately administered secular instruction is not forbidden because of its religious-school location

**Holding:** The Establishment Clause permits the described supplementary Title I service for children independently selected under neutral public eligibility criteria, delivered by public employees under public supervision with public secular materials, without religious instruction, team teaching or other cooperative instructional activity with religious-school teachers, or participation in worship, and with effective controls against diversion. Religious-school location, presumed inculcation arising from that location, incidental benefit to the school, and ordinary monitoring of that separately administered public service do not by themselves establish unconstitutional sponsorship, devotional financing or institutional control; Aguilar's categorical prohibition and Ball's corresponding Shared Time rationale are displaced at that scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Court's opinion; the five-Justice coalition stated above directly joins the rule, its secular-control qualifications and its precise treatment of precedent.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional inquiry must distinguish the public remedial service from the religious school's own instruction. Lemon, as updated in Part I, identifies the governmental act and its religious or secular function, using purpose, effects and institutional relationships as evidence within that inquiry. Here the remedial purpose, independent selection of eligible children, public staffing and supervision, secular materials and restrictions on religious participation identify a supplementary public service. Those safeguards establish this program's severable secular operation; operational separation and reliable accountability do not invariably require wholly distinct personnel or separate institutional governance. Ordinary consultation about a child's secular educational needs is distinct from joint teaching. Neutral eligibility or public employment alone would not excuse devotional instruction or diversion.

Aguilar treated the supervision needed to preserve secular instruction as a reason to prohibit the instruction on religious premises. That categorical inference is unsound: supervising public teachers' secular work is not, without more, governing the school's religious affairs. The absence of a finding of indoctrination does not prove every possible defect absent, but location cannot substitute for the connection between the challenged public service and an actual constitutional objection. Nor does an incidental institutional benefit alone turn this separately controlled service into religious teaching.

These safeguards existed when Aguilar was decided. The legal defect is the categorical inference that confuses secular supervision with religious sponsorship and control, not a newly discovered factual transformation. Continuing expense and impaired access make the consequence substantial; they are not the constitutional rule. Property, contractual, completed-transaction and educational-service interests receive their actual weight. No identified concrete property or completed-transaction entitlement requires retaining the categorical ban; established obligations and service continuity remain protected in implementation. Ball's Shared Time program implicated the corresponding inference about supplementary public teaching. Its distinct Community Education program predominantly used religious-school teachers and principals, and today's holding neither approves that arrangement nor reopens its judgment. The onsite rule applies Lemon as modified in Part I; the Court does not approve every program considered in Ball or reopen its completed judgment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent prohibitions on governmental worship, devotional financing and improper institutional control remain; public employment and neutral eligibility do not immunize supported devotional conduct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Aguilar v. Felton is overruled as authority for categorically excluding this safeguarded public Title I service from religious-school premises; no reopening of completed claims or authorization of religious instruction follows. School District of Grand Rapids v. Ball loses its contrary Shared Time rationale at the same scope, insofar as religious location, presumed inculcation, incidental school benefit or ordinary secular monitoring categorically condemns the separately administered public service; its Community Education judgment and unaddressed grounds remain undisturbed. Lemon v. Kurtzman governs through the updated inquiry stated in Holding I.

For the significant-change inquiry and injunction remedy in Holdings III–IV, see Federal Courts, “Modification of continuing injunctions,” Agostini, June 23, 1997, Nos. 96-552 and 96-553.


## Holdings source: first-amendment / Chaudhuri v. Tennessee

#### Chaudhuri v. Tennessee

**Docket or dockets:** Sixth Circuit No. 96-5538; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied.

**Decided:** 1998-04-27.

##### Genuine silence leaves religious thought to the participant

**Holding:** A public university may designate a genuine moment of silence at an official function, including as a replacement for prayer, when it neither prescribes nor directs prayer, religious profession or other devotional exercise. A period called silence loses that justification if its actual administration directs worship; genuinely private religious expression does not become governmental merely because it occurs during the silence.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Breyer’s opinion is joined by all eight other Justices and controls unanimously.

##### A university may not present devotional prayer as its own program

**Holding:** A public university violates the Establishment Clause when officials select or invite a speaker to deliver an invocation, benediction or other spoken prayer as part of its official function. Nonsectarian wording, an adult audience, voluntary attendance, freedom to abstain and a secular ceremonial purpose do not cure that official worship; no separate finding of employment pressure is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author, and controls by six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule reaches functions with established official invitation and program placement, without a separate employment-pressure finding. Private audience recitations outside review, spontaneous speech, private forums and nondevotional acknowledgments remain distinct; no finding resolves the disputed pressure allegations.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The genuine-silence judgment is affirmed unanimously. The official-prayer judgment is reversed and remanded 6–3, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer supporting. No present damages, personal-immunity result or prospective injunction follows; prospective protection requires a live controversy and must preserve private expression and genuine silence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied. Purpose, effects and entanglement inform one inquiry; no former independent Lemon prong returns.
- **Tanford v. Brand:** Applied to officially arranged devotional prayer at the supported university functions beyond commencement; its private-expression and remedial limits remain.
- **Lee v. Weisman:** Applied for the independent prohibition on official worship. Its practical-pressure ground remains intact and is not decided here.
- **Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District:** Applied for official attribution and relief preserving private expression; student or private delivery alone cannot privatize official worship.
- **Sherman v. Community Consolidated School District 21:** Applied to distinguish actual civic function from worship, without extending permission to devotional prayer.
- **Marsh v. Chambers:** Distinguished as legislative prayer; no general exemption for adult ceremonies follows.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Preserved. A damages request against the State or an official-capacity defendant who is not a §1983 damages person cannot alone sustain jurisdiction; this live controversy does not decide any defendant’s ultimate liability.
- **Will v. Michigan Department of State Police; Hafer v. Melo:** Their distinct state/official-capacity and personal-capacity rules remain; no capacity allegation is adjudicated here.
- **Harlow v. Fitzgerald; Anderson v. Creighton:** Personal immunity, if applicable, remains tied to the challenged conduct’s date, not the announcement of this decision.


## Holdings source: first-amendment / Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe, 530 U.S. 290 (2000)

#### Santa Fe Independent School District v. Doe, 530 U.S. 290 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-62.
**Decided:** June 19, 2000.

##### The school may not delegate its official religious choice to a student majority

**Holding:** A public secondary school may not organize an invocation as a representative component of its own football pregame ceremony and delegate to a student-majority election whether that official component will include religious exercise. The challenged board-created election mechanism violates the Establishment Clause under both its unrestricted and nonsectarian/nonproselytizing versions; relief reaches that mechanism while preserving genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access.

**Proposition-level authority:** Justice Breyer's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes for the rule, explanation and limited affirmance. The particular government-created representative religious choice is essential to all seven joins; the Court adopts no independent religious-pressure holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The District stresses secular purposes, student authorship, no required prayer, optional attendance and the possibility of silence or a secular message. Those features do not remove the existing governmental mechanism. The Court assumes no official textual preapproval and no delivered October prayer; it does not rest on a finding that every secular purpose is a sham. The ability to decline use of the delegated religious authority does not transform it into neutral access. Pinette and Bronx Household protect actually private expression, where government has not adopted the devotion as its own event component. Tanford likewise distinguishes official organization from private composition, without making its adult setting identical to this school policy.

The backup's religious-content limits retain the same official mechanism and do not cure it. The Court does not direct officials to edit private religion into acceptable language. Nor does it prohibit every election, invited student speaker or religious message. The existing mechanism suffices to affirm; Lee and Ingebretsen's separate protection against substantial practical religious pressure remains intact without requiring legal compulsion or compulsory attendance. No additional pressure finding, universal facial-challenge rule, new forum entitlement, graduation ruling or damages award is necessary.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment restrains the challenged official representative invocation mechanism. It preserves genuinely voluntary private prayer, independent student expression, lawful secular messages and genuine neutral access, and requires neither a new forum nor censorship of otherwise lawful private religious viewpoints. It does not decide graduation, damages or later incidents outside this record.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Agostini v. Felton:** Applied as the governing governmental-act/function inquiry; purpose, effects and entanglement remain evidence within that inquiry, not independent surviving prongs.
- **Lemon v. Kurtzman:** Continues in the form Agostini adopted; the Court revives neither an independent purpose test nor an independent endorsement test.
- **Lee v. Weisman:** Its official-organization rule applies to this school-created invocation mechanism. Its independently sufficient substantial-pressure rule remains, without a compulsory-attendance prerequisite; no new pressure holding is made here.
- **Ingebretsen v. Jackson Public School District:** Its rejection of automatic insulation by student delivery and its protection of lawful private expression apply. Its provision-sensitive interim ruling is not recast as a universal rule invalidating every future religious message.
- **Tanford v. Brand:** Preserved for its established official devotional program and its distinction between independent wording and private access; the Court does not transplant every feature of university commencement to football.
- **Capitol Square Review and Advisory Board v. Pinette:** Its actual generally available private forum is distinguished from this selective representative event component; religious content and government location remain insufficient by themselves to establish attribution.
- **Bronx Household of Faith v. Community School District No. 10:** Its independently organized private use remains protected; the school's own representative ceremony presents a different governmental function.
- **Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School District:** Its protection of religious viewpoints within genuine comparable private access remains intact; no schoolwide private forum is inferred here.
- **Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia:** Its independently directed private-expression relationship remains intact; it does not authorize government-organized worship.
- **Chabad of Southern Ohio v. City of Cincinnati:** Its contextual attribution inquiry remains; neither a forum label nor location alone supplies the answer.
- **Strout v. Albanese:** Its distinction between genuine private expression and governmental performance or purchase of a religious function is preserved; no football-specific rule is imported from its tuition setting.


## Holdings source: first-amendment / Good News Club v. Milford Central School, 533 U.S. 98

#### Good News Club v. Milford Central School, 533 U.S. 98

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-2036.
**Decided:** June 11, 2001.

##### Devotional expression does not remove this moral instruction from the admitted subject

**Holding:** When a public school opens its community forum to comparable private moral and character instruction, it may not exclude an otherwise eligible program because that instruction uses prayer, Bible lessons, worship or invitation to religious adherence. Genuine reasonable and viewpoint-neutral subject, activity and operational limits remain available, but religious form cannot itself reclassify comparable admitted instruction as an excluded subject.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly adopt Part I of the Opinion of the Court at this level of generality and in its application. Breyer delivers that opinion, and the other seven members of this coalition join. Eight direct votes control; Stevens dissents from the application.

**Material application:** Milford admitted secular moral instruction but excluded the Club's integrated prayer, Bible teaching, songs, games and invitation to Christian adherence. Superintendent testimony about Aesop instruction established the policy comparison, not an actual Aesop meeting. The Club need not remove its devotional components to qualify as comparable instruction.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not classify every worship service as instruction or decide uses divorced from the actual admitted subjects. Genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral boundaries survive, including scheduling, supervision, safety, capacity and activity limits. Viewpoint neutrality creates no general resource or financing entitlement.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lamb’s Chapel and Ladue are applied to the admitted instructional subject. Bronx Household’s instruction and bounded devotional-access holdings remain controlling. Perry Education and Cornelius retain genuine reasonable, viewpoint-neutral forum limits. Rosenberger reinforces viewpoint neutrality within the actual private-expression program without creating a financing entitlement.

##### These private-use arrangements defeat the asserted establishment defense and establish equal-access entitlement

**Holding:** Independent adult leadership, parental authorization and after-school operation in this community forum constitute private access where the asserted school arrangements amount to administration of that access rather than governmental religious instruction, adoption, solicitation or substantial school-created religious pressure. On these circumstances the asserted establishment defense fails, and the Club is entitled to equal access under the same genuine neutral rules as comparable groups, with appropriate declaratory and access relief determined below.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court, delivered by Breyer, is joined by the other members of the six-Justice coalition comprising Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer. Those six directly control the establishment conclusion and remedy. Souter and Ginsburg join neither; Stevens dissents.

**Material application:** Private adult leadership, parental authorization and after-school operation, considered with the four-minute dismissal interval, elementary ages, room assignment, permission practices, school activities and audiovisual use, establish administration of private access rather than a governmental devotional function or substantial school-created pressure. The defense's legal insufficiency is determined separately from rejection of Milford's own summary-judgment motion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Agostini's modified Lemon rule asks whether government exercises religious authority or function; purpose, effects and entanglement inform that inquiry rather than operating as independent fallback prongs. Lee and Santa Fe separately prohibit official worship and substantial governmental religious pressure. Parental permission and neutral availability are relevant circumstances, not automatic immunity. Official recruitment, teacher-led devotion, actual compelled participation or materially different governmental adoption require their own inquiry. A possible child's misunderstanding and religious intensity alone do not supply governmental action. Separate equality and RFRA theories need not be reached because the speech holding supplies relief.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Second Circuit is reversed for implementation of equal access and appropriate declaratory and access relief under genuine neutral rules. The rejected establishment defense is not reopened. Damages and fees require independent determinations; no fixed permit, injunction, resource entitlement, disclaimer or permanent forum-opening obligation is imposed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Agostini’s modified Lemon inquiry is applied unchanged. Bronx Household supplies the private-access attribution rule. Lee and Santa Fe retain their independent official-worship and pressure protections. Marshfield is distinguished because it addresses an actual continuing governmental presentation; its functional inquiry remains operative.
