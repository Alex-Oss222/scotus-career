## Standards and Tests selection: Municipal civil-rights pleading under the Federal Rules

### Municipal civil-rights pleading under the Federal Rules

**Governing question:** What instructions govern municipal civil-rights pleading under the Federal Rules?

**Current rule:** Courts may not impose a judge-created heightened factual-particularity requirement on municipal §1983 complaints beyond the then-operative Federal Rules. Rule 8 governs; Rule 9(b)'s fraud-or-mistake categories do not add municipal liability. Monell and Canton retain their substantive attribution and causation requirements. Rule 11, discovery control and Rule 56 remain distinct. No individual-officer pleading standard is decided.

**Authority by component:**

- The Federal Rules supply the pleading standard for municipal § 1983 claims: Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit — The Federal Rules supply the pleading standard for municipal § 1983 claims, 1993-03-03. Stone's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; unanimous controlling authority.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-03-03; Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

**Authority by component:**

- Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims: Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims, 1995-06-12. Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer; nine votes support the rule and its operative limits.

- Crawford-El v. Britton — Proof of unconstitutional motive, 1997-06-16; Stone-Zsela, all nine
- Crawford-El v. Britton — Discovery and stage-specific screening, 1997-06-16; all nine
- Crawford-El v. Britton — Actual causation and independent defenses, 1997-06-16; all nine

**Present operation effective:** Crawford-El, 1997-06-16, rejecting a special proof quantum and derivative categorical discovery barrier while preserving the carried direct/circumstantial-evidence rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Circumstantial proof remains available in motive-dependent claims. Crawford-El v. Britton (1997-06-16) — Civil Rights / Qualified immunity and motive evidence.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory exhaustion and screening of prisoner conditions actions

### Statutory exhaustion and screening of prisoner conditions actions

**Governing question:** The original §1997e prefiling condition and distinct screening commands.

**Current rule:** A confined prisoner may not bring a prison-conditions action under §1983 or any other federal law until available administrative remedies are exhausted. On its own motion or a party's motion, the court shall dismiss such an action if satisfied that it is frivolous, malicious, fails to state a claim or seeks money from an immune defendant. A claim that on its face has one of those four defects may be dismissed without first requiring exhaustion. State failure to adopt or adhere to an administrative grievance procedure alone supplies no action under CRIPA §3 or §5. “Prisoner” in §1997e means a person incarcerated or detained in any facility who is accused of, convicted of, sentenced for, or adjudicated delinquent for violations of criminal law or terms or conditions of parole, probation, pretrial release or a diversionary program.

An actually accessible grievance process authorized to take responsive action concerning the complaint remains available even if it cannot award the money damages demanded. A prisoner cannot avoid the enacted exhaustion condition by requesting damages alone; lack of compensatory authority supplies no general futility exception. The permission to dismiss a facially defective claim before exhaustion remains distinct from an entitlement to nonexhausted merits adjudication.

**Authority by component:**

- Available-remedies command, screening and its prior-exhaustion exception: PLRA §§802–804 — Source, authority and operative text, §803(d), original 42 U.S.C. §1997e(a)–(c), (h), April 26, 1996.

- Availability despite inability to award requested money and distinct permissive screening: Canonical Decision Record `Booth_v_Churner_merits_2001-05-29.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / A responsive administrative process remains available although it cannot award money, 2001-05-29; O’Connor’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Booth, May 29, 2001, for availability of a responsive process despite lack of compensatory authority; original statutory screening and definition components remain effective April 26, 1996.

**Effective transition:** The statute was enacted April 26, 1996. Section 802's express all-prospective-relief transition is not generalized to §1997e or §1915; disputed application to pending matters remains unresolved unless an applicable authority separately resolves it.

**Unresolved interaction:** McCarthy’s former damages-only BOP conclusion survives in its no-congressional-command setting and supplies no exception to the PLRA command. Booth decides no comprehensive unavailability or procedural-default rule, no general classification of excessive force as prison conditions and no limitations rule. A nonexistent, wholly powerless or actually inaccessible process was outside its established record. Section 1915’s fee-screening mechanisms remain distinct.

**Holdings navigation:** [Booth v. Churner, 532 U.S. 731 — Prisoner litigation and available administrative processes](HOLDINGS.md#booth-v-churner-532-us-731).

## Standards and Tests selection: Express federal ADEA actions against state employers

### Express federal ADEA actions against state employers

**Governing question:** Statutory authorization and constitutional amenability for a private federal ADEA action against a State employer.

**Current rule:** Sections 630(b), 626(b)-(c) and the incorporated FLSA provisions unmistakably authorize the private federal action against a covered State employer. Section 626(b) incorporates the specified FLSA powers, remedies and procedures except section 216(a), and treats amounts due from an ADEA violation as unpaid wages for enforcement. Under the Article I enforcement rule, the expressly authorized action may enforce the valid federal age-employment duty when both duty and enforcement measure fall within Congress's Article I power. Authorization and power do not establish consent, discrimination or entitlement to an award.

**Authority by component:**

- Statutory clarity and incorporated remedies: Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents / United States v. Florida Board of Regents, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The enacted provisions unmistakably authorize the private federal action, January 11, 2000; Ginsburg Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer (seven).
- Article I application: same Record, II. Article I independently sustains this authorized enforcement measure; Ginsburg Parts II-III with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Kimel, January 11, 2000; Article I enforcement against States in federal court retains its June 23, 1999 general operation.

**Exceptions and limits:** Section 626(b) allows liquidated damages only for willful violations. Under section 626(c)(1), the individual's right to bring an action ends when the EEOC commences an action enforcing that employee's right. Separately, incorporated section 216(b) terminates its action/party right upon the Secretary's section 217 complaint seeking to restrain delay of that employee's unpaid minimum wages or overtime, or to obtain legal or equitable relief for alleged section 215(a)(3) retaliation. These are distinct conditions. State exhaustion, constructive consent and an individual constitutional tort are not prerequisites added by this rule.

**Open question:** ADEA validity under Fourteenth Amendment section 5 remains unresolved. Statutory coverage, defenses, proof and award-specific statutory and constitutional limits remain separate.

**Related current rules:** Article I enforcement against States in federal court supplies the general power rule; Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit retains its rejection of constructive consent.

## Standards and Tests selection: Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim

### Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim

**Governing question:** Whether Eleventh Amendment immunity affecting some claims defeats removal of an otherwise federal-question action.

**Current rule:** An action containing substantial §1331 federal claims is removable under §1441(a) even when Eleventh Amendment immunity bars particular State or official-capacity claims. Proper assertion of immunity prevents adjudication of the affected claim; it does not eliminate jurisdiction over the other federal claims or require remand of the entire action under §1447(c).

**Authority by component:**

- Immunity affecting one claim does not eliminate removal jurisdiction over remaining federal claims: Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-22.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-22.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule decides neither whether removal itself waives immunity nor every unasserted-immunity issue, and does not supply jurisdiction over a claim lacking its own necessary foundation.

**Holdings navigation:** Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht, 524 U.S. 381 — Removal jurisdiction and claim-specific state immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

### Relevant precedent in appellate qualified-immunity review

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. This does not admit a new claim or factual record, make each considered precedent controlling or permit later decisions to supply notice retrospectively. The notice inquiry stays fixed to the conduct date. Ordinary claim and evidence preservation, particularized objective immunity and proper summary-judgment treatment remain distinct; no mandatory merits-first sequence follows.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent legal review, contemporaneous notice and preservation limits: Elder v. Holloway — Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue, 1994-02-23. Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. All nine adopt the legal-authority rule and the limited reversal and remand.

**Present operation effective:** Elder v. Holloway, 1994-02-23.

**Holdings navigation:** Appellate review must consider all relevant law on the preserved immunity issue.

## Standards and Tests selection: Nonassisting media observers during a home search

### Nonassisting media observers during a home search

**Governing question:** Government-arranged media entry and recording during warrant execution.

**Current rule:** Officers may not procure private media observers' entry and recording inside a home during a warranted search when that presence serves no legitimate execution or investigative purpose. The evidence warrant supplies no authority for that additional exposure of home life.

**Authority by component:**

- Ayeni v. Mottola — I. An evidence warrant does not authorize an unrelated audience in the home, 1997-05-27; Stone-Zsela, all nine

**Present operation effective:** Ayeni, 1997-05-27.

**Exceptions and limits:** Genuine assistance, valid occupant consent and justified investigative documentation are different circumstances. Individual immunity still requires the separate conduct-date clearly-established-law inquiry; the current violation alone does not resolve it, and actual responsibility, causation and compensable injury require proof.

**Holdings navigation:** Ayeni v. Mottola (1997-05-27) — Criminal Procedure / Warrant execution and private observers.

## Standards and Tests selection: Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

### Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing

**Governing question:** Whether the Communications Act’s power to modify filing requirements permits eliminating them for an entire class.

**Current rule:** Section 203(b)(2) permits the Commission, in its discretion and for good cause shown, to modify requirements made by or under §203 in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions; no required notice period may exceed 120 days. That authority permits adjustments to filing form, contents, timing or place, including consequential and class-wide technical changes, but not replacement of mandatory filing throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system. The inquiry examines the obligation and whether its essential operation is adjusted or removed, not a numerical market-share threshold. Good cause alone cannot authorize the replacement. The Act’s exception for connecting carriers preserves their separate inspection duties; §203(c)’s allowance for otherwise lawful authority does not confer unlimited dispensing power.

**Authority by component:**

- Bounded modification and distinct notice authority: MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co — The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing, 1994-06-17. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Present operation effective:** MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co, 1994-06-17.

**Exceptions and limits:** Limited deferral or waiver is not categorically foreclosed, but no particular waiver is approved. Failure of the special-circumstances-or-conditions predicate is a four-Justice alternative, not a separate controlling holding. No universal major-questions doctrine is created; the statute’s permissible flexibility remains available within the actual delegation.

**Holdings navigation:** The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change

### Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change

**Governing question:** What instructions govern medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change?

**Current rule:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii) commands suitable retroactive corrective adjustments when aggregate reimbursement for a provider and period proves inadequate or excessive. The Secretary may construe that command as reconciling interim payments to sums payable under lawful reasonable-cost methods and ceilings, without an individualized exception whenever actual costs exceed the ceiling. Authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods remain. Interpretive change can reduce weight without automatically invalidating a considered permissible construction; text, delegation and reasons must be examined. Unsupported litigation positions neither replace required agency explanation nor gain automatic controlling weight. A changed interpretation is not disqualified merely by statutory age or a litigation setting, but unexplained reversal or disregard of legitimate reliance may be arbitrary and capricious. For antecedent transactions lacking clear prior agency guidance, a court may use the agency’s current authoritative statutory interpretation without backdating the regulation’s effective date.

## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation](HOLDINGS.md#trade-court-factfinding-does-not-eliminate-lawful-delegated-interpretation-1). [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).


**Related current rules:** Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation governs independent judicial consideration when controlling force is unavailable.

## Standards and Tests selection: Statutory priorities and claim-specific equitable subordination

### Statutory priorities and claim-specific equitable subordination

**Governing question:** Whether §510(c) permits a court to reduce rank because an allowed penalty competes with compensatory creditors.

**Current rule:** Statutory priority does not immunize a claim from §510(c), and lack of priority does not itself justify subordination. After notice and a hearing, §510(c) permits distributional subordination of all or part of an allowed claim to another allowed claim, or an allowed interest to another allowed interest, under principles of equitable subordination, and transfer of a lien securing a subordinated claim. A legally sufficient, supported claim-specific ground is required; noncompensatory character, estate insufficiency and reduced recovery for innocent or value-giving creditors cannot alone replace Congress’s distribution choices, even when described as individual balancing. This limit applies to both postpetition administrative penalties and prepetition nonpriority penalties.

**Authority by component:**

- Noland — Parts I–II, real statutory equity and forbidden class replacement, 1996-05-13; Thomas for all nine.
- CFI — Part II, the same boundary within the general-unsecured class, 1996-06-20; Souter for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** CFI, June 20, 1996, for application to nonpriority penalties; Noland’s administrative-priority rule effective May 13, 1996.

**Controlling construction:** Allowed §503(b)(1)(C) postpetition tax penalties retain case-era §507(a)(1) priority after conversion through §348(d)’s §503(b) exception. Section 726(a)(4)’s fourth-rank category instead requires a fine, penalty, forfeiture or multiple/exemplary/punitive damages arising before the earlier of the relief order or trustee appointment, and only to the extent noncompensatory. Section 726 distribution remains expressly subject to §510.

**Open question:** Misconduct’s universal necessity remains unresolved; no entitlement without misconduct or frozen catalogue of equitable grounds follows. Rejection of a class-based reason neither orders a new misconduct hearing nor establishes a different ground, final payment or tax priority.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. Noland, 517 U.S. 535; United States v. Reorganized CF&I Fabricators of Utah, Inc., 518 U.S. 213 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Underlying tax burdens of persuasion in bankruptcy

### Underlying tax burdens of persuasion in bankruptcy

**Governing question:** Whether bankruptcy changes the ultimate persuasion burden attached to a tax claim.

**Current rule:** Unless the Bankruptcy Code expressly changes it, the substantive law creating a tax claim supplies its ultimate burden of persuasion in bankruptcy. Rule 3001(f)’s prima-facie evidentiary effect does not reallocate that ultimate burden after rebuttal. Claims-allowance and tax-adjudication authority under sections 502 and 505 likewise supplies no such change by itself.

**Authority by component:**

- Raleigh v. Illinois Department of Revenue; No. 99-387. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, Bankruptcy does not itself change the tax claim's burden of persuasion, 2000-05-30; Breyer with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-05-30; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule establishes neither a universal taxpayer burden nor state control of all bankruptcy procedure. Liability elements, production requirements, express federal departures and federal dischargeability remain separate.

## Standards and Tests selection: Tax-refund filing and payment limits do not permit incapacity tolling

### Tax-refund filing and payment limits do not permit incapacity tolling

**Governing question:** Whether mental incapacity or extraordinary inability to file permits a nonstatutory extension of the applicable §6511 tax-refund limits.

**Current rule:** The integrated filing, allowance and recovery-amount scheme in §6511 rebuts the requested equitable extension for mental incapacity or extraordinary inability to file, even assuming Irwin's rebuttable presumption applies. The separate §6511(b)(2) payment lookback is not enlarged by that extension even if the claim is otherwise timely under §6511(a). Actual filing, the return due date and the separately recoverable amount remain distinct; express statutory adjustments retain their own conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Brockamp — The enacted filing scheme rebuts nonstatutory equitable suspension, 1997-02-18; Breyer Part I for all nine supplies the filing-scheme exclusion

**Present operation effective:** Brockamp, 1997-02-18.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule concerns tax payments; whether a remittance instead is a deposit requires its own determination. It establishes no universal jurisdictional classification and resolves no separate fraud, inducement, recoupment or constitutional theory.

#### Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit, 507 U.S. 163 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1657.

**Decided:** March 3, 1993.

##### The Federal Rules supply the pleading standard for municipal § 1983 claims

**Holding:** A federal court may not impose a judge-created heightened factual-particularity requirement on a § 1983 complaint against a municipality beyond the then-operative Federal Rules. Rule 8 governs the complaint, Rule 9(b)'s fraud-or-mistake categories do not create a municipal-liability category, and Monell and Canton retain their separate substantive requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by the other eight Justices; unanimous controlling authority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S. 41 (1957): applied for fair-notice pleading; no subsequent plausibility rule is imported.
- Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658 (1978): preserved; municipal policy/custom and causation remain necessary, while freedom from employer-based liability supplies no immunity from suit.
- Owen v. City of Independence, 445 U.S. 622 (1980): applied to reject municipal qualified immunity and the pleading argument built on it.
- City of Canton v. Harris, 489 U.S. 378 (1989): preserved as substantive failure-to-train attribution law, not authority for greater pleading particularity.
- Elliott v. Perez, 751 F.2d 1472 (5th Cir.1985), and Palmer v. City of San Antonio, 810 F.2d 514 (5th Cir.1987): rejected insofar as their rule demands heightened particularity for municipal § 1983 complaints; individual-immunity pleading remains unresolved.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary Rule 8 and Rule 11 duties, discovery control and Rule 56 remain. Monell/Canton attribution and the underlying constitutional wrong are not relaxed. Individual-officer immunity pleading, collateral estoppel and unreviewed alternative summary-judgment grounds remain undecided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the Fifth Circuit and remand under the ordinary pleading rules; no municipal liability finding or new trial is ordered.

#### Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., 530 U.S. 133 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-536.
**Decided:** June 12, 2000.

##### II. Rule 50 preserves reasonable jury inferences while considering the whole record

**Holding:** Rule 50 requires consideration of the whole record, drawing reasonable inferences for the verdict winner and making no credibility choices or weighing of evidence. #### Booth v. Churner, 532 U.S. 731

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1964.
**Decided:** May 29, 2001.

##### Whether the absence of administrative power to award damages excuses statutory exhaustion

**Holding:** Amended section 1997e(a) requires exhaustion of an actually accessible grievance process authorized to take responsive action on a covered prison-conditions complaint even when the process cannot award the money demanded. Limiting the prayer to damages does not remove that condition or create a compensation-based futility exception.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's unanimous opinion of the Court.

**Material application:** The process could investigate and respond, and Booth failed to complete its accessible review steps. The enacted removal of adequacy and effectiveness qualifications, rather than administrative usefulness alone, controls.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A nonexistent, wholly powerless or actually inaccessible process is outside this record; no comprehensive unavailability, procedural-default or limitations rule is adopted. Whether excessive force generally constitutes prison conditions is outside the accepted question. Section 1997e(c)(2) separately permits dismissal before exhaustion of a facially frivolous or malicious claim, a claim failing to state a claim, or one seeking money from an immune defendant; that permission creates no entitlement to unexhausted merits review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm dismissal without prejudice. Completion of the actually available process and any subsequent timing questions remain subject to their lawful requirements; neither wrongdoing nor damages is established.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** McCarthy v. Madigan's statutory-primacy rule is applied; its damages-only result survives where no statute imposes exhaustion, while the PLRA supplies the previously absent command for covered actions. McNeil v. United States enforces an express administrative sequence without importing the FTCA's separate exceptions or timing rules.

## Verbatim authority selection: state/holdings/civil-rights.md

#### Crawford-El v. Britton

**Docket or dockets:** D.C. Circuit No. 94-7203; no separate Supreme Court docket assigned.

**Decided:** 1997-06-16.

##### Ordinary proof governs unconstitutional motive in this retaliation claim

**Holding:** The ordinary preponderance standard governs proof of unconstitutional motive in this §1983 retaliation claim; neither qualified immunity nor litigation-burden concerns authorize a special clear-and-convincing requirement. Legally competent direct and circumstantial evidence remain available, and all actual constitutional elements and independent defenses must still be established or resolved under their applicable burdens.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court decides the quantum Kimberlin reserved; it neither abolishes every specificity practice nor resolves Leatherman’s individual-officer pleading reservation beyond the barriers decided here. A motive allegation alone defeats no constitutional element, ordinary procedure or conduct-date immunity defense.

##### Necessary discovery cannot be barred by the rejected heightened proof requirement

**Holding:** A court may resolve a genuinely dispositive legal defense first and require specific, nonconclusory support, but it may not categorically demand proof satisfying the rejected heightened quantum before allowing discovery necessary to obtain that proof. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, relevant and proportionate discovery limits, sequencing, protective orders and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available; no unrestricted discovery or particular deposition is required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The court may clarify allegations, resolve actual dispositive legal defenses and limit discovery to material issues. An adequately supported summary-judgment motion requires responsive evidence; a general credibility attack is insufficient. No fixed discovery plan, particular deposition, unrestricted inquiry, mandatory trial or new interlocutory appeal follows.

##### An actual same-decision showing remains distinct from a conceivable lawful explanation

**Holding:** A merely possible lawful explanation for an official's conduct does not itself establish a same-decision defense or make competent proof of unconstitutional motive inadmissible. Under Mt. Healthy, the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without that conduct. The separate clearly-established-law defense remains available on the law governing the conduct when it occurred.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, unanimous on all three propositions and their limits. His separate proposed immunity revision is not adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Protected communication, personal responsibility, actual motive, causation, injury and conduct-date immunity remain unresolved. Later Kimberlin and Settle doctrine supplies no notice in 1989. Prison-access limits remain and no unrestricted press-conference entitlement is recognized.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate only the heightened motive burden and dependent directions; preserve the existing remand otherwise. Resolved access-to-courts and due-process claims are not revived. The municipal issue retains its separate panel posture; the reinstated pendent conversion claim remains subject to ordinary jurisdiction and merits. No liability, immunity, damages or trial entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Kimberlin v. Quinlan: applies the distinction between motive as a constitutional element and objective immunity, competent circumstantial proof and lawful case management; decides here the proof quantum Kimberlin left open.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald: retains the objective clearly-established-law defense and early legal screening; it supplies no independent heightened evidentiary code.

#### Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-531.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States

**Holding:** Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly subject States and their instrumentalities to private patent actions in federal court for covered violations on or after October 28, 1992. Section 296(b) makes the otherwise available patent remedies available on their own statutory terms; that authorization does not itself establish constitutional power, State consent, infringement, or entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part I, joined by all nine Justices, directly adopts this statutory proposition and its limits.

##### Appropriate respondent alternatives are legally available within their limits

**Holding:** A respondent may defend an unchanged favorable judgment on an appropriately supported alternative that neither enlarges its rights nor reduces the adversary's rights under that judgment. CSB's posture permits consideration of its preserved defenses of the immunity denial, but preservation and briefing alone do not expand the §5 question or establish an alternative's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' joins at this precise level. Their actual decisions on the alternative grounds remain distinct.

##### Section 5 independently sustains the authorized patent action

**Holding:** Section 5 permits Congress to prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional enforcement legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional. Sections 271(h) and 296 validly authorize this patent action on that basis; the plaintiff need not establish a separate constitutional violation to proceed under the Act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas disagree.

##### Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports this federal patent remedy as an independent ground for sustaining the immunity denial.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas oppose the extension.

##### Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit

**Holding:** A State's continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes suit does not constitute consent: Florida Prepaid did not consent to federal patent suit by continuing its prepaid-tuition program after §§271(h) and 296 took effect. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available. Whether Florida Prepaid's litigation conduct waived immunity need not be decided, and no waiver finding is made.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part IV, joined by all nine Justices, applies College Savings Part III and states these limits. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, who did not join College Savings Part III, accept it as controlling. Neither constitutional holding depends on consent.

The Court makes no finding of patent validity, infringement, willfulness, Florida-remedy adequacy, constitutional injury, or entitlement to relief. Section 296(b) retains its own conditions and any properly presented award-specific constitutional limits. Neither constitutional holding nor the judgment preapproves every compensation, injunction, fee, or enhanced award. Kolstad's Title VII punishment rules do not determine patent enhancement.

The appearance, transfer request under §1404(a), and request for affirmative declarations of invalidity, noninfringement, and unenforceability remain distinct litigation-consent contentions. The Court finds no waiver from any of them. Schacht does not settle their effect. No actual-violation exception, willfulness exception, State-remedy exhaustion, new constitutional damages cause, or Just Compensation Clause justification is adopted.

The action is not converted into an officer suit. Young and Coeur d'Alene require a connected officer, ongoing federal violation, proper cause, and genuinely prospective relief; Edelman and Pennhurst preserve retrospective-compensation and state-law boundaries. #### Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents / United States v. Florida Board of Regents, 528 U.S. 62 (2000)

##### Whether the ADEA unmistakably authorizes these private federal actions against state employers

**Holding:** Sections 630(b), 626(b)–(c), and the incorporated FLSA provisions, read together, authorize private ADEA actions against state employers in federal court. That authorization establishes neither constitutional power, State consent, discrimination nor entitlement to relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Seminole Tribe and Florida Prepaid separate unmistakable authorization from constitutional power; combined enactments satisfy the former question here.

Coverage, defenses, proof and award-specific statutory and constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication. No officer action, state exhaustion requirement or universal authorization of every federal remedy is created.

College Savings Bank, Part III, is applied to the ADEA's authorized employment-enforcement measure; its Article I rule remains controlling beyond its patent application. Pennsylvania v. ##### Corrective adjustments operate within the authorized cost-method system

**Holding:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii) requires suitable retroactive corrective adjustments when, for a provider and fiscal period, aggregate reimbursement produced by the methods proves inadequate or excessive; the Secretary permissibly construes that command as reconciliation of interim reimbursement to the amount payable under lawful reasonable-cost methods and limits. #### MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co., 512 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 93-356 and 93-521.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** United States et al. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. et al., No. 93-521, is consolidated with No. 93-356; the same rule invalidation applies to both.

##### The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing

**Holding:** Under 47 U.S.C. §203(b)(2), the Commission may, in its discretion and for good cause shown, modify tariff requirements in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but may not replace §203(a)'s mandatory filing obligation throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system or lengthen the statutory notice period beyond 120 days. The challenged permissive-filing rule exceeds that delegation and is invalid; Chevron does not require deference to a construction outside this statutory boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The November 1992 rule makes filing optional, not forbidden, for nondominant long-distance carriers; AT&T alone remains classified as dominant in this record. A modification can have consequential effects. Courts must examine the obligation changed, the scope of the modification clause and whether the agency adjusts the obligation's operation or removes its essential operation for the regulated class. This inquiry does not turn on a numerical market-share threshold or automatically invalidate a class-wide adjustment. Changes to filing form, contents, timing or place remain possible under the statutory conditions; timing changes cannot extend the 120-day notice ceiling. The Court does not categorically foreclose deferral or waiver in limited circumstances, and decides no particular proposed waiver. Good cause alone cannot authorize replacement of mandatory filing throughout this sector.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the challenged order independently fails the special-circumstances-or-conditions requirement; no separate alternative ground commands a majority. It prescribes no tariff rates or replacement program, decides no damages or retrospective nonfiling consequences, resolves no general rule for individual nonenforcement decisions, and adopts no independently sufficient ratification or prohibition theory from §§226 or 332.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the inquiry into statutory meaning before deference; no new exception based on economic importance and no replacement of Chevron is adopted.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: preserves contextual interpretation within an actual delegation and the requirement of a discernible agency ground; the agency's competitive-policy rationale is examined, not replaced by a judicial rationale.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves permissible interpretive change within a statutory system; the defect here is the rule's statutory reach, not the fact that the Commission changed its policy.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves the principle that agency expertise operates within binding statutory limits; its distinct nonemployee-access rule is not imported into tariff regulation.
- NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, May 23, 1994: distinguishes a permissible functional construction preserving the statute's operative distinctions from removal of the filing obligation here; its supervisory-status holding remains intact.
- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, May 31, 1994: distinguishes broad conditioning authority grounded in that Act's operative compliance language; no general rule against consequential agency action follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside the permissive-detariffing rule is affirmed in Nos. 93-356 and 93-521. No tariff rate, replacement program, damages or retrospective monetary consequence of earlier nonfiling is decided, and no new direction is given in the distinct complaint proceeding.

#### AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board

**Decided:** 1999-01-25.

##### I. National implementing rules coexist with state determinations

**Holding:** Section 201(b) authorizes FCC rules implementing the local-competition obligations added to the Communications Act by §§251–252. **Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Parts I–II are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices control the general-authority and associated remand propositions; O'Connor, Thomas and Breyer dissent on their distinct allocation grounds.

**Holding:** The categorical absence-of-federal-authority grounds for vacating Rules 51.405 and 51.303 and displacing otherwise lawful federal rules from state implementation cannot stand. **Proposition-level authority:** The same six-Justice coalition controls Part II. The remand does not adopt a new rule resolving the returned substantive objections.

##### III. A network element need not be a detachable physical object

**Holding:** Section 153(29)'s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment, as well as the physical facilities or equipment themselves. Eligibility under that definition does not establish that §251(d)(2) requires unbundled access to every eligible function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Those eight supply a controlling majority; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on the independent definition component.

##### IV. Necessity and impairment must perform independent limiting work

**Holding:** In selecting required network elements under §251(d)(2), the FCC must give independent limiting effect to necessity for proprietary elements and impairment for network elements generally, considering realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent's network. Rule 51.319 is set aside because the adopted comparison and impairment standard did not perform that task.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IV is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, giving the complete rule seven votes. ##### V. The Act imposes no freestanding own-facilities prerequisite

**Holding:** A requesting telecommunications carrier need not own some local facilities merely to qualify to seek unbundled access under §251(c)(3). Its entitlement to particular elements remains governed by §251(d)(2) and the lawful conditions of access and combination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part V is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the independent own-facilities prerequisite, without a ground.

##### VI. Existing combinations need not be dismantled

**Holding:** Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prohibits an incumbent from separating requested network elements already combined in its network unless the requesting carrier asks for separation. Unbundled availability and valuation do not require physical destruction of an existing combination.

## Event

TRW Inc. v. Andrews, No. 00-1045. Argued October 9, 2001; decided November 13, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which reversed partial summary judgment treating the first two disputed credit disclosures as untimely. The question is whether the then-operative Fair Credit Reporting Act supplies a general discovery extension beyond its express misrepresentation exception.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's general-discovery ruling and remands for further proceedings consistent with the statutory construction.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The first two disclosure claims cannot proceed on the rejected general discovery theory. The mandate supplies no entitlement to reopen the newly presented actual-damages theory, amend to allege misrepresentation, or obtain an accrual or exception hearing. ## Holdings

**No general discovery extension under the governing FCRA.** The version of 15 U.S.C. §1681p governing these claims requires suit within two years from when liability arises. Its express extension permits suit within two years after discovery only when the defendant materially and willfully misrepresented information the Act required it to disclose to the individual and that information was material to establishing that defendant's liability to the individual; ignorance of a violation alone satisfies neither those conditions nor an additional general exception.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, controls this statutory construction, the exception's application and the limited nonreach below. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on their separately stated ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the specified discovery exception must retain its restrictive operation. A general discovery extension would allow a claimant to bypass its conditions and make it largely redundant. Andrus supports giving an enumerated exception its limiting effect; Menasche requires the enacted parts to perform their assigned work. The words identifying when liability arises do not transform liability into discovered liability. The practical difficulty of detecting identity misuse therefore cannot supply an exception Congress did not enact. Holmberg's fraud and concealment rule and Urie's latent-disease setting do not establish a universal discovery presumption overriding this text. Rotella and Klehr retain their distinct RICO questions and qualifications; they do not rewrite the FCRA. Andrews's complaint does not allege the material and willful misrepresentation of required information specified by §1681p, so that express extension supplies no basis for these claims.

**Precedent treatment:** Holmberg and Urie are distinguished at their stated scopes; Andrus and Menasche are applied to the statutory structure; the distinct Rotella and Klehr rules are preserved.

**Question left undecided.** The Court does not decide Andrews's argument that liability first arose upon actual loss rather than disclosure, because that distinct argument was first presented here and was not raised or briefed below. This nonreach does not resolve the substantive requirements of every willful claim, establish the asserted loss date, or authorize reopening the theory on remand. The confined statutory ruling corrects the general-discovery judgment without deciding a universal federal accrual rule.

## Precedent Treatment

- Andrus v. Glover Construction Co., 446 U.S. 608 (1980): applied for the significance of an expressly enumerated exception.
- United States v. Menasche, 348 U.S. 528 (1955): applied to preserve operative work for the exception's enacted conditions.
- Holmberg v. Armbrecht, 327 U.S. 392 (1946): its fraud/concealment rule is preserved; it does not establish the universal discovery rule urged here.
- Urie v. Thompson, 337 U.S. 163 (1949): its latent-disease setting does not govern this express FCRA structure.
- Rotella v. Wood (February 23, 2000) and Klehr v. A. O. Smith Corp. (June 19, 1997): their distinct RICO accrual, new-injury, unprovable-damage and concealment components remain at their actual scope.

## Law After Decision

The governing version of §1681p has no general discovery extension. Its precise statutory misrepresentation exception remains available when its conditions are met. The Court's ruling does not decide the first-presented actual-loss theory or create a universal accrual rule for federal statutes.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** Stone-Zsela joins the Court's statutory ruling and judgment. He adds that liability arises when the elements necessary for the particular statutory claim exist: rejection of general discovery does not erase the need for an element-specific inquiry or an independently applicable preserved exception. Rotella rejects substitution of a later discovery event for the governing accrual inquiry, but does not make disclosure, discovery and actionable injury interchangeable. Properly preserved questions remain subject to ordinary adjudication. The newly presented theory receives no affirmative permission to reopen merely because the Court leaves it undecided.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** They begin with the traditional rule that a limitations period ordinarily runs when the cause of action is complete and enforceable, rather than when the claimant learns of it. A general discovery presumption does not follow from Congress's failure expressly to reject discovery. Congress can prescribe a different start and did enact a confined exception here. Traditional fraud/concealment protection and the latent-injury setting remain separate rules. They object that leaving the general background uncertain invites uncertainty under other statutes. Their agreement with reversal and remand does not establish that every disclosure completes every FCRA claim or decide the newly raised actual-loss theory.

The case returns for proceedings consistent with rejection of the general discovery theory, subject to the stated mandate and ordinary procedural limits. The independent dispositions concerning other claims remain intact.

## Event

United States v. Knights, No. 00-1260. Merits decision, December 10, 2001. Argued November 6, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed suppression because the residence search pursued ordinary criminal investigation. The question is whether a known probation search condition and individualized reasonable suspicion justify the search notwithstanding that purpose. ## Holdings

### The known search condition and individualized suspicion justify this probation search

**Holding and operative rule:** A warrantless search of a probationer's residence within a known, lawful search condition is reasonable when supported by particularized reasonable suspicion of criminal activity in the circumstances presented. Ordinary criminal-investigative purpose does not independently invalidate that combination; neither the condition alone nor reasonable suspicion alone supplies this holding.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly adopt this rule, its justification, and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the probation relationship and notice of the express condition reduce the privacy interest implicated by this residential search, while individualized suspicion supplies an objective justification for this intrusion. Griffin establishes that lawful probation supervision changes the constitutional setting; it does not make its special-needs rationale the exclusive ground for every probation search. Knights's proposed purpose restriction would invalidate a search supported by this combined justification merely because the officer investigates crime. The Fourth Amendment does not require that result here.

Whren supports examining the objective justification after authority for the intrusion has been established. Its ordinary probable-cause traffic rule does not itself confer residential search power or decide motive doctrine for every search based on reasonable suspicion. Edmond and Ferguson retain their purpose inquiries in their suspicionless and special-needs settings. Scott governs the consequence of an unconstitutional supervisory search, not the antecedent validity of this condition-and-suspicion search; its exclusion rule remains intact. Any ultimate suspicion question properly remaining is independently reviewed under Ornelas, with its protection for historical findings and due weight to grounded contextual inferences.

The holding rests on reasonableness, not complete contractual consent or forfeiture of Fourth Amendment protection. Suspicionless residential searches and blanket consent are outside the question necessarily resolved because individualized suspicion supplies the sufficient ground here.

**Precedent treatment:** Griffin's supervisory-relationship principle is applied without making its rationale exclusive; Whren's objective method is applied within its source-specific limits; Edmond and Ferguson are distinguished; Scott's exclusion rule and Ornelas's review allocation remain operative.

## Precedent Treatment

- Griffin v. Wisconsin, 483 U.S. 868: the lawful supervisory relationship informs diminished privacy; special needs is not the exclusive possible justification.
- Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806: objective assessment follows a demonstrated source of search authority; its traffic holding is not generally extended to every reasonable-suspicion search.
- City of Indianapolis v. Edmond, 531 U.S. 32, and Ferguson v. City of Charleston, 532 U.S. 67: purpose remains material in their distinct suspicionless and special-needs settings.
- Pennsylvania Board of Probation & Parole v. Scott, 524 U.S. 357: exclusion for the institution's unconstitutional supervisory search remains unchanged; this decision finds the bounded search reasonable.
- Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690: the division between factual and ultimate legal review is applied unchanged.

## Law After Decision

The Fourth Amendment permits the defined probation-condition and individualized-suspicion combination despite ordinary investigative purpose. Existing scope, execution and exclusion rules retain their separate operation; the decision creates no general suspicionless-search or consent-waiver rule.

## Event

Searles v. Van Bebber. Merits decision, January 8, 2002. Review of the Tenth Circuit judgment reported at 251 F.3d 869. The questions concern original 42 U.S.C. §1997e(e)'s restriction on mental or emotional compensation absent physical injury, the availability of nominal and punitive damages for the proved religious-diet violation, the consequence of a punitive instruction tied to invalid compensation; and the preserved court-access and due-process objection to the restriction at this remedial scope. ## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument or submission and decision.

## Public Action

Affirm the reviewed remedial judgment, 9–0.

## Judgment & Remedy

## Holdings

### The named injury restriction bars the compensatory award, not every remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** Original 42 U.S.C. §1997e(e) bars an action by a prisoner confined at filing for mental or emotional injury suffered in custody without the required prior showing of physical injury. That restriction bars the actual distress compensation awarded here even though the underlying wrong is constitutional; it does not erase the violation or compensation-independent remedies.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this statutory holding in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress restricted recovery for a named injury category. Harris v. Garner establishes the confinement-at-filing trigger and the mental-or-emotional-injury limit; it does not convert that limit into immunity from every remedy. The jury found no physical injury, and the $3,650 award compensated mental or emotional harm. The religious source of the right does not exempt that recovery. Carey and Stachura also prevent compensation based merely on an abstract valuation of the constitutional right. The all-damages reading goes too far because it ignores the distinct functions of nominal recognition and punishment, while the contrary compensation argument cannot save a jury award for the expressly barred injury.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris is applied at its actual injury-and-filing scope; Carey and Stachura retain the distinction between a violation and compensable loss.

### Nominal damages recognize the established violation

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1997e(e) does not bar nominal damages recognizing an established constitutional violation without compensable injury. On the preserved violation and lack of compensable injury here, the plaintiff is entitled to one dollar.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Nominal relief does not compensate the mental or emotional injury named in the statute. Carey permits recognition of an established constitutional wrong without inventing injury or valuing the right itself as compensatory loss. The preserved liability verdict therefore requires the one-dollar award even though the distress compensation cannot stand.

**Precedent treatment:** Carey v. Piphus supplies nominal recognition; Stachura's prohibition on abstract-right compensation remains.

### Punitive eligibility survives, but the amount requires a lawful redetermination

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1997e(e) does not categorically bar punitive damages for conduct satisfying Smith v. Wade's evil-motive-or-intent or reckless-or-callous-indifference standard. Punishment remains discretionary even when that culpability is established; an amount expressly tied to invalid compensation must be redetermined independently, preserving separate liability and culpability findings.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding and remedy in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Punitive damages punish culpable conduct and deter its repetition; they do not compensate distress. Smith supplies a distinct remedial predicate rather than a rule that punitive fault must invariably exceed every substantive violation standard. The jury separately made the required culpability finding, which survives the compensation defect. But the instruction expressly relied on the invalid compensatory award in setting the punitive amount. That linkage prevents preserving $42,500 as an independently sound calculation. The corrected proceeding concerns amount, without retrying established liability or punitive culpability. A positive punitive award is not compelled, punishment may not reproduce barred compensation, and no automatic punitive-to-nominal ratio applies. Fees must then be reconsidered in light of the lawful recovery.

**Precedent treatment:** Smith v. Wade's culpability requirement and discretionary punishment are applied; the distinct remedial functions recognized in Carey and Stachura prevent relabeling barred compensation.

### The preserved constitutional objection fails at this remedial scope

**Holding and operative rule:** Applying the physical-injury restriction to this distress award does not deny access to court or due process where the established right remains enforceable through nominal relief and punitive relief on its distinct predicates. This ruling resolves the preserved challenge in these circumstances, without validating every possible application of the statute.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join this portion of the Opinion of the Court. Stone-Zsela agrees in its disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute restricts one injury-based recovery rather than withdrawing adjudication of the underlying right. # National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co. — 2002-01-16

[Authoritative Record](../records/national-cable-telecommunications-assn-inc-v-gulf-power-co_merits_2002-01-16.md)

## Event

National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co.; Nos. 00-832 and 00-843. Merits decision, January 16, 2002; argued October 2, 2001. Review of the Eleventh Circuit's judgment setting aside the FCC's authority over mixed cable-Internet and wireless telecommunications pole attachments. The questions are whether section 224's category-specific rate formulas restrict its general coverage, and whether qualifying wireless equipment falls outside the statute.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the challenged categorical exclusions from the FCC's pole-attachment authority and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Component | Disposition | Supporting | Opposing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mixed cable-Internet attachments | Reverse the categorical jurisdictional exclusion and remand, 7-2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | Souter, Thomas; they would vacate for agency classification and explanation |
| Wireless telecommunications attachments | Reverse the categorical exclusion and remand, 9-0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | No opposing Justice |

Further proceedings must recognize coverage while preserving every applicable statutory formula and any properly remaining classification or particular-rate objection. The judgment does not approve a particular attachment charge.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Ginsburg, Breyer throughout; Souter and Thomas as to Part II only | Supports both reversals | Seven join Part I, mixed-service coverage; all nine join Part II, wireless coverage |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Thomas | Souter | Concurs in wireless reversal; would vacate the mixed-service component for agency explanation | Mandatory formula classification and reasoned statutory basis |

## Holdings

### I. Adding Internet service does not remove a qualifying cable-system attachment from the statute

**Holding and operative rule.** An attachment by a cable television system that satisfies 47 U.S.C. §224(a)(4) remains within the FCC's authority under §224(b) when it carries Internet service together with cable television. Sections 224(d) and (e) prescribe mandatory rates within their respective terms; they do not exhaust the broader attachment definition or permit the agency to evade an applicable formula.

**Authority.** Part I of Kennedy's opinion has the full support of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplying seven votes.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress defined the regulated attachment before prescribing methods for setting its rate. An attachment by a cable system does not cease to meet that definition when the system adds a service. The limitation to attachments used solely for cable service controls the cable formula's application; it does not rewrite the general definition. Section 224(b)'s direction to ensure just and reasonable rates, terms and conditions and its rulemaking grant therefore continue to govern covered attachments. Iowa Utilities Board supplies the distinction between an actual congressional assignment and the limits governing its exercise. The utilities' method objection deserves examination when a particular rate method is challenged, but does not establish a categorical absence of authority. Nor must the Commission resolve Internet classification to answer this coverage question: either possible classification leaves an attachment by a cable system within the general definition. Mandatory formulas remain mandatory when their conditions are met. Mead requires an actual delegation before an agency interpretation receives controlling force; the enacted text itself resolves this question, so no new deference rule is needed. The Eleventh Circuit's categorical exclusion cannot stand. Classification of Internet service and validity of a particular rate are outside the questions decided.

### II. Qualifying wireless equipment is a covered attachment

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 224 covers an attachment by a provider offering telecommunications to the public for a fee, without excluding the provider's equipment because it serves wireless transmission. Both the attachment and its owner must satisfy the statutory definitions; the utility's wire-use condition does not impose a separate wire-only restriction on the attached provider's service.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join Part II of Kennedy's opinion.

**Controlling explanation.** The telecommunications-service definition addresses service offered for a fee regardless of the facilities used. The reference to wire communications in the utility definition identifies the covered owner and structure; it does not alter the separately defined service or exclude physical wireless equipment attached to that structure. Reading the definitions together covers both wireline attachments used by wireless providers and qualifying wireless equipment. The contrary construction relocates an owner-related condition into a service limitation Congress did not enact. Iowa Utilities Board's distinction between statutory eligibility and the duties governing eligible facilities supports preserving those separate functions. The statute's exclusions for railroads, cooperatively organized persons and federal or state ownership remain, as do the incumbent-local-exchange-carrier limitation in §224(a)(5) and §224(c)'s allocation to qualifying state regulation. The holding reaches qualifying poles, ducts, conduits and rights-of-way; it does not extend authority to every independent wireless tower or property outside those definitions. The wireless categorical exclusion is reversed, with lawful rates, terms and conditions governed by the applicable statutory provisions.

## Precedent Treatment

- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board (January 25, 1999): applies its separation of congressionally conferred FCC authority from the independent limits on particular implementing rules; its local-competition holdings remain intact.
- United States v. Mead Corp. (June 18, 2001): preserves its actual-delegation requirement; the Court resolves section 224's coverage from its text without adding a deference holding.
- Permian Basin Area Rate Cases, 390 U.S. 747: the separate writing invokes reasoned ratemaking to require an agency explanation; that objection does not become a controlling ground for additional remand.

## Law After Decision

Section 224's general coverage encompasses qualifying mixed cable-Internet and wireless telecommunications attachments. A gap or dispute about an applicable rate formula does not itself create a jurisdictional exclusion. The statutory formulas, ownership and provider limits, and qualifying state-regulation allocation retain their force. These are new coverage holdings; no Internet-service classification or particular rate is approved.

## Separate Writings

Thomas, joined by Souter, concurs in the wireless holding but would vacate the mixed-service portion for a reasoned agency determination. If cable-modem access is cable service, subsection (d)'s formula controls; if it is telecommunications service, subsection (e)'s formula controls. Only after resolving those possibilities can residual authority under subsection (b) support the particular regulatory choice. The service supplied by an Internet service provider and the transmission carrying customers to that provider are distinct classification questions. The Commission's treatment of one does not necessarily resolve the other. Permian Basin's demand for a reasoned rate method supports returning that choice to the agency. Probable ultimate jurisdiction and policy favoring broadband deployment cannot supply the missing statutory explanation. This position rejects categorical noncoverage but withholds approval of the existing unexplained agency determination.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Eleventh Circuit for proceedings consistent with both coverage holdings, preserving properly remaining classification and rate-method objections under the applicable statutory commands.

# Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer — 2002-02-20

[Authoritative Record](../records/wisconsin-department-of-health-and-family-services-v-blumer_merits_2002-02-20.md)

## Event

Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer, No. 00-952. Argued December 3, 2001; decided February 20, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Wisconsin Court of Appeals, which required a resources-first Medicaid calculation after rejecting the administrative determination and circuit-court judgment; the Wisconsin Supreme Court declined review. The question is whether section 1396r-5 permits considering anticipated support from the institutionalized spouse before increasing the community spouse resource allowance.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the state judgment requiring resources first and remands for proceedings consistent with the permissible income-first construction.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4; the statute permits the income-first method within its stated limits.**

**Supporting:** Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Scalia.

The Wisconsin Court of Appeals' federal-law restriction on the income-first method is reversed. The matter returns for proceedings and any remaining calculation under the permissible method, using supported income and resource figures and the statute's distinct actual-payment requirements.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Supports the judgment | Entire opinion; every controlling proposition and its stated limits |
| Dissent | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia | Opposes the judgment | Entire statutory disagreement and contrary remedy |

## Holdings

### A State may consider prospective support before increasing protected resources

**Holding and operative rule:** At the preeligibility hearing under 42 U.S.C. §1396r-5(e)(2)(C), a State may consider the community spouse's expected posteligibility income, including support that may be transferred under subsection (d)(1)(B), before increasing the community spouse resource allowance. The statute permits income first; it does not require nationwide adoption or independently adjudicate every resources-first program.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, supplies five direct votes for this construction, its statutory qualifications and the reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this hearing anticipates the circumstances in which eligibility and the maintenance allowance operate. Both methods require forecasts. Congress protected a maintenance-income floor while preserving resources for care; it did not guarantee the largest possible resource reserve. Subsection (a)(1) supersedes inconsistent Medicaid provisions, and (a)(3) preserves ordinary methodology except where the section otherwise provides. Neither clause eliminates the need to read the specific provisions together.

Subsection (b)(1) prevents deeming the community spouse's income available to the institutionalized spouse. It does not state the reciprocal prohibition asserted against considering prospective support. Subsection (b)(2) governs posteligibility attribution according to its ownership rules, not the antecedent assessment's exclusive list of potential support. Subsection (c) separately governs resource assessment and later separation. Under (d)(1)(B), a deduction for the community spouse allowance is available only to the extent income is actually made available to or for the benefit of that spouse; transfer remains optional. A forecast neither establishes an actual payment nor commands one.

The contrary reading treats (e)(2)(C)'s resource-increase command as requiring present ownership of every income source considered. But that hearing assesses future maintenance under the statutory arrangement. It may include the contemplated support allowance without deeming ownership or payment already accomplished. Once inadequacy is established under the lawful calculation, the statute requires the resource adjustment. The Court imposes no additional consent, escrow or payment-guarantee prerequisite and makes no finding that a later transfer occurred. Good Samaritan supports considered administration within the statute, not displacement of these specific protections.

**Operative qualifications:** For the separate posteligibility attribution under (b)(2), a governing nontrust instrument may specify the allocation; otherwise income solely payable to one spouse is available to that spouse, jointly payable income is divided equally, and income shared with others follows the spouse's interest or half of the spouses' unspecified joint interest. Trust income follows the governing Medicaid trust provisions and the trust's allocation, or the parallel payment rules when no specific allocation appears. Nontrust income without an ownership instrument is divided equally. The institutionalized spouse may rebut the nontrust payment and no-instrument presumptions by a preponderance of evidence of different ownership. These rules do not turn a forecast into payment. Under (e)(2)(B), if either spouse establishes that exceptional circumstances causing significant financial duress require community-spouse income above the ordinary minimum monthly maintenance needs allowance, an amount sufficient to supply the necessary additional income must replace that allowance. Under (d)(5), when a court orders the institutionalized spouse to provide monthly income for the community spouse's support, the community spouse monthly income allowance must be at least the ordered amount. Those income-allowance adjustments remain distinct from (e)(2)(C)'s resource-increase command; none substitutes another provision's trigger or establishes that its conditions are met here.

**Precedent treatment:** Good Samaritan's permission for considered administration remains bounded by the specific enactment; its holding supplies no universal asset-preservation or administrative-discretion rule.

### Persuasive agency reasoning supports independent statutory construction

**Holding and operative rule:** The agency materials here receive the weight their reasoning earns rather than automatic controlling force. The Court independently concludes that income first is permissible; no new exception to, or abolition of, lawful delegated interpretive authority is adopted.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice Court opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The November 1993 instruction described income first as required. The March 3, 1994 clarification instead permitted income first or another reasonable interpretation pending a final rule. The September 7, 2001 publication remained a proposed rule at this decision. Those different legal forms and dates cannot be collapsed into a binding final mandate. Mead and Christensen distinguish delegated legal force from persuasive reasons, and Skidmore supplies the latter inquiry. The agency's experience and explanation support the coordinated reading the Court independently adopts. Good Samaritan establishes that change alone does not disqualify a considered interpretation; it does not command acceptance of every revised view. The statutory conclusion therefore does not depend on automatically deferring to an amicus submission, guidance or a proposal.

**Precedent treatment:** Mead, Christensen and Skidmore are applied at their actual scope; Good Samaritan's change-of-position rule is preserved, and Chevron remains operative for lawful delegated choices.

## Precedent Treatment

- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: considered administration must fit the enactment, and a change of interpretation alone does not establish invalidity.
- United States v. Mead Corp., June 18, 2001: congressional delegation and exercise of interpretive authority remain prerequisites for controlling statutory force; these materials are considered for persuasion.
- Christensen v. Harris County, May 1, 2000: an advisory interpretation's authority and persuasive quality remain distinct; optional conduct is not converted into a compulsory transfer.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co., 323 U.S. 134: the actual reasoning and experience receive the persuasive weight they earn.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: its role for lawful delegated choices remains; the independent construction here creates no broader revision.

## Law After Decision

States may use the income-first method in the section 1396r-5(e)(2)(C) resource-allowance hearing while respecting ownership attribution, the optional character of transfer, and the actual-availability condition for a posteligibility deduction. A lawful forecast is not an accomplished transfer. Resource inadequacy, exceptional financial duress and court-ordered support retain their separate statutory treatments.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor and Scalia, dissenting.** The dissent would affirm the resources-first requirement and return the matter for a calculation using supported income and resource figures. Section (e)(2)(C) directs an increased resource allowance when the community spouse's protected income is inadequate. Predicting income from resources legally attributable to that spouse differs from predicting another person's optional payment. Section (d)(1)(B)'s posteligibility transfer does not add a prerequisite that the spouse depend on that payment before receiving the resource protection; denying eligibility can prevent the projected arrangement from beginning.

The dissent recognizes that (b)(1) is not a reciprocal nonattribution rule and that (b)(2) expressly addresses posteligibility attribution. Its point is the scheme's separation of ownership, resource protection and voluntary support, not an invented symmetric prohibition. The Court's answer that every method forecasts future circumstances fails to explain why Congress's resource protection may depend first on another person's voluntary transfer. A future payment might not occur or might diminish; those are objections to the legal method, not findings about Irene's later conduct. Administrative flexibility, conservation of funds and expertise cannot supply the missing compulsory transfer or displace the specific statutory command. The agency's changed view is not invalid merely because it changed, but its reasoning must answer this objection. The dissent asserts no constitutional right to preserve assets and would award no unsupported dollar amount.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Wisconsin courts for proceedings consistent with the permitted income-first method. Remaining factual calculations must respect the distinct statutory attribution and actual-payment rules; the decision fixes no eligibility date or benefit amount.

#### Denius v. Dunlap

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 99-1402; no separate Supreme Court docket supplied; judgment below, 209 F.3d 944
**Decided:** January 17, 2001.

##### The protection against unjustified employment demands for intimate medical information

**Holding:** The Fourteenth Amendment protects against a public employer's demand for unrestricted access to objectively intimate medical information without an adequate connection to a legitimate employment function and protections reasonably fitted to that use. ##### When financial content falls within the intimate-information protection

**Holding:** Financial information falls within the protected personal interest only to the extent its contents reveal objectively intimate personal or family matters. A court cannot establish constitutional coverage from the financial label, the breadth of an authorization, or the owner's preference for secrecy alone; once protected content is identified, the employer's demand is governed by the same actual-need, scope, use and safeguards rule as other intimate information.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** An ordinary financial or credit record does not qualify merely by category or confidentiality. No particular protected financial item has yet been identified. Coverage follows objectively intimate personal or family content, if established.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the categorical financial-privacy determination while preserving the equitable claim under the content rule. Unpreserved release-of-liability theories and undeveloped other information categories remain outside review.

##### Conduct-date notice and attorney and financial damages immunity

**Holding:** An official remains protected from personal damages when all relevant law at the time of the challenged conduct did not give sufficiently particularized objective notice that the conduct violated the asserted right; an identical prior case is unnecessary, but later recognition of a right supplies no retrospective notice. The 1996 law did not sufficiently establish these legal-consultation and intimate-financial acquisition extensions on this authorization, so the judgments granting personal damages immunity on those two claims are affirmed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part III, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; all eight Associate Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The defense is objective; officials need not prove actual reliance on a binding authority specifically permitting the demand. Sheets did not itself decide acquisition, and later decisions cannot give 1996 notice. Personal immunity does not defeat a separately live equitable claim or confer immunity on entities outside its scope.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm attorney and financial personal-damages immunity, 8–1. For the underlying consultation right, see First Amendment — Independent legal consultation as a public-employment condition, Denius v. Dunlap, January 17, 2001, Seventh Circuit No. 99-1402.

##### The distinct notice basis for the conditional medical damages remand

**Holding:** If the authorization reaches objectively intimate medical information, the existing summary-judgment record does not support personal damages immunity merely because no previous decision addressed this exact form. The cumulative law before the 1996 demand gave notice that an unlimited civilian-medical access condition, unsupported by an articulated countervailing employment purpose, could not be justified solely by public-employer status; medical coverage and genuinely supported factual or independent defenses remain for adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy’s Part IV, joined by Stevens, O’Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela adds a seventh vote for the conditional medical remand on a different ground.

**Material application:** The notice conclusion rests on the combined preconduct civilian-medical authorities, including their upheld justified disclosures and prisoner-specific limits; it does not make the circuit cases controlling Supreme Court holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm reversal of medical damages summary judgment, conditioned on coverage. Determine actual coverage, intimate content, legitimate purpose and safeguards, personal participation, causation, injury and supported defenses under ordinary procedure. Prospective relief requires a proper defendant and continuing injury. No damages, reinstatement, automatic trial or prescribed discovery follows; no Rule 60(b) motion was needed before appeal from the legal dismissal of equitable claims.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sheets v. Salt Lake County: preserves its intimate-publication holding and objectively intimate-content boundary; develops protection against an unjustified employment demand for access without converting Sheets itself into an earlier acquisition holding.
- Whalen v. Roe: preserves legitimate safeguarded collection; its reservation of possible abuses does not foreclose the bounded acquisition right recognized here.
- Doe v. Southeastern Pennsylvania Transportation Authority: preserves its assumed-right disposition of confined benefits administration; distinguishes that supported function from the unrestricted additional access demanded here.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton: retain objective, particularized conduct-date immunity; the Court declines to replace it with an actual-authoritative-reliance defense.
- Elder v. Holloway: applies independent consideration of all relevant conduct-date authority, without requiring an identical case or importing later notice.
- Crawford-El v. Britton: preserves ordinary proof, actual causation and lawful procedural screening alongside immunity; its later date supplies no 1996 notice.
- Wasson v. Sonoma County Junior College: preserves distinct present constitutional merits and contemporaneous notice; its unresolved attributed-speech question does not decide the different medical-law notice record here.
- Pesce v. J. Sterling Morton High School District 201, Schaill v. Tippecanoe County School Corp., and Anderson v. Romero: considered as preconduct circuit authority for the cumulative civilian-medical notice inference, with their justified-disclosure and prisoner-specific limitations retained; none becomes a controlling Supreme Court precedent by that use.

#### Sterling v. Borough of Minersville

**Docket or dockets:** Third Circuit No. 99-1768; judgment below, 232 F.3d 190; no Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** April 24, 2001.

##### Whether an official ultimatum can presently invade an adult's intimate confidentiality

**Holding:** An officer infringes Fourteenth Amendment intimate confidentiality by credibly using official authority to require an adult to reveal suspected sexual orientation to family or face disclosure by the officer, where neither course serves a legitimate governmental purpose. Operative compelled surrender is the injury; actual officer publication is unnecessary, but an idle insult or ineffective threat is insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual demand, coercion, responsible actor, causation, injury and lawful damages require proof. The decision concerns an adult and a no-purpose ultimatum, not routine guardian notice, an absent emergency, concealment of criminal evidence or all threats. It adopts no universal informational-privacy test or automatic liability for every defendant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sheets's protection against unjustified dissemination of objectively intimate information extends to this coerced family disclosure; Denius supports a present burden before publication. Doe's justified benefits administration and Whalen and Nixon's legitimate safeguarded uses remain distinct.

##### Whether the supported April 1997 ultimatum defeats Wilinsky's legal qualified-immunity defense

**Holding:** The supported no-purpose use of arrest authority to compel this adult's intimate family disclosure violated a sufficiently particularized constitutional boundary established by April 17, 1997. Wilinsky therefore lacks qualified immunity on the asserted legal ground; operative coercion and absence of official purpose are material, while subjective malice alone is insufficient and a factually identical precedent is unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins. O'Connor and Kennedy join the present right but not this notice determination.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm denial of Wilinsky's legal immunity defense, 5-4, permitting ordinary proceedings without deciding participation, causation of death, compensable injury, municipal liability or any damages amount; no automatic trial is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Harlow and Anderson's objective, particularized inquiry remains; Elder requires all relevant contemporaneous law. Sheets's 1996 holding and the preconduct circuit confidentiality authorities supply cumulative notice at their confined scopes. Walls's employment inquiry is distinguished. Denius, the Supreme Court's May 1997 Doe decision, Shahar and Wilson v. Layne supply no retrospective April 1997 notice.

For Sterling's exclusion of Hoban's factual-sufficiency contention from interlocutory review, see Federal Courts, this case, April 24, 2001, Third Circuit No. 99-1768.

#### Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., 530 U.S. 133 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-536.
**Decided:** June 12, 2000.

##### I. Prima facie proof and material falsity can support discrimination

**Holding:** A prima facie case together with evidence permitting rejection of the employer's material explanation may support a finding of intentional discrimination without an additional category of direct or independent proof. It neither compels liability nor invariably defeats judgment as a matter of law: the strength of the initial inference, the probative force of falsity and the rest of the evidence the court must credit determine whether discrimination is a reasonable inference.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Hazen Paper requires age actually to motivate the decision. Falsehood, unfairness, personal hostility or another unlawful motive is not automatically age discrimination. A conclusively established different lawful reason can defeat the proposed inference. So can weak falsity evidence together with otherwise conclusive independent evidence against discrimination. These are applications of ordinary sufficiency, not a checklist demanding more evidence in every case. Merely imagining another possible motive does not defeat an inference reasonably supported by the actual proof. Substantial prima facie and falsity evidence can therefore sustain liability without a separate evidentiary category. The Court does not define every insufficient future case, alter an unpresented mixed-motive allocation or decide disparate impact.

##### III. This record supports the verdict and cannot be rejected for lack of extra proof

**Holding:** Reeves's prima facie showing, material evidence rebutting the recordkeeping explanation, and evidence connecting age-directed comments to Chesnut's role and influence permit a reasonable finding of intentional age discrimination. The employer is not entitled to judgment as a matter of law on this record, and the remand cannot restore the same sufficiency judgment through a renewed demand for additional proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Opinion of the Court, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 623(a)(1) concerns covered employment action because of age; §631(a) begins the Act's age protection at forty. The Court does not decide every ADEA proof framework or impose an under-forty comparator requirement. Employer production, plaintiff persuasion, evidentiary sufficiency and remaining remedial questions remain distinct. No liability follows merely from pension status, a mistaken business judgment, personal animosity or any false explanation.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Fifth Circuit's Rule 50 judgment for the employer is reversed. The trial evidence is legally sufficient to support the age-discrimination verdict; the verdict is to stand unless an ordinary, properly preserved and verdict-compatible independent ground warrants further relief. The remand permits consideration of remaining appellate issues, including an unresolved alternative new-trial ground if otherwise reviewable. It does not reopen the sufficiency question decided here, direct a particular new trial or make a new monetary, liquidated-damages or front-pay award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **St. Mary's Honor Center v. Hicks:** Applies its existing rejection of both compulsory liability from pretext alone and a mandatory further category of proof. No replacement persuasion allocation or revived presumption is adopted.
- **Texas Department of Community Affairs v. Burdine; McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green:** Preserve employer production and ultimate plaintiff persuasion under the assumed indirect framework; disbelief has evidentiary force without shifting that burden.
- **Postal Service Board of Governors v. Aikens:** Applies the focus on actual discrimination and the availability of circumstantial proof.
- **Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins:** Applies actual age motivation as the liability question; falsehood or another improper motive is not by itself age discrimination. Its willfulness and reserved disparate-impact issues are not redecided.
- **O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp.:** Preserves the evidentiary significance of relative age without an under-forty replacement condition or a categorical result from workforce ages.

For the whole-record Rule 50 standard, see Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc., No. 99-536, June 12, 2000, under Federal Courts, judgment as a matter of law.
