#### United States v. O'Hagan

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-842.

**Decided:** 1997-06-25.

##### I. Deceptive use of entrusted information in securities trading can be primary §10(b) fraud

**Holding:** A person who, with scienter, owes an information source a duty of loyalty and confidentiality, feigns fidelity, and secretly uses material nonpublic information entrusted in that relationship for securities trading commits deception in connection with the transaction when the securities use consummates the concealed disloyalty. The source need not be the trading counterparty; a qualifying duty, deception and transaction connection are indispensable, and full disclosure of the trading plan to every person owed the relevant duty removes this theory's deception.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Stevens's Court opinion is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. Stone-Zsela supplies a seventh judgment vote on his narrower source-participation ground, which is not a limitation adopted by the Court.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A source's participation in an actual or contemplated securities transaction is not an additional majority requirement. Status, innocent receipt, informational inequality or another person's fraud does not establish primary liability. Disclosure to one principal may leave duties to another; eliminating this securities theory does not authorize conduct prohibited elsewhere.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical rejection of counts 21–37 is reversed, 7–2 in judgment, without deciding preserved instruction, sufficiency or error-effect objections.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Chiarella supplies the duty requirement for deceptive silence; Carpenter supplies source-property and fraud principles without settling every securities connection. Central Bank's confined private affirmative-assistance action retains its distinct predicates and supplies neither this primary crime nor a criminal mental-state shortcut.

##### II. Section 14(e) authorizes the bounded preventive rule in this tender-offer setting

**Holding:** Section 14(e)'s express authority to prescribe means reasonably designed to prevent tender-offer fraud permits Rule 14e-3(a), in the source-misappropriation setting presented, to prohibit the covered trading without proof of a fiduciary breach as an additional element. The rule's actual commencement, information, source, knowledge, instrument and public-disclosure conditions remain necessary; the delegation does not authorize treating every informational disparity as fraud or regulate outside the tender-offer and antifraud domain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of the same Court opinion is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, Stone-Zsela and Scalia: eight direct votes. Thomas dissents from this application while accepting that the statute permits some preventive rules.

**Material application:** A person must have taken a substantial step or steps to commence, or commenced, a tender offer. The trader must be someone other than the offering person and must possess material information relating to the offer, knowing or having reason to know both that it is nonpublic and that it was acquired, directly or indirectly, from the offering person, the issuer, or an officer, director, partner, employee or other person acting on behalf of either. The prohibition covers purchasing, selling or causing purchase or sale of the securities sought or to be sought in the offer, securities convertible into or exchangeable for them, and options or rights to obtain or dispose of those securities. Its disclosure alternative requires the information **and its source** to be publicly disclosed a reasonable time before trading. Private disclosure to the source, which can eliminate §10(b) deception, does not itself satisfy that separate public-disclosure alternative.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Principal-authorized warehousing and every other application are not decided. Preserved pre-offer scope, due-process and trial objections remain; unpreserved contentions are not decided initially by the Court.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The categorical invalidity ruling on counts 38–54 is reversed, 8–1, subject to the rule's actual conditions and unresolved challenges.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** MCI requires action within enacted authority and is distinguished by §14(e)'s express prevention delegation. Chevron is neither generally replaced nor subjected to a new categorical exception.

##### III. The criminal-punishment qualification and unresolved trial questions remain operative

**Holding:** Section 78ff(a) requires a willful violation for criminal punishment and bars imprisonment for that rule violation when the defendant proves lack of knowledge of the rule. That lack-of-knowledge protection does not erase the underlying rule, create general immunity from fines or require the government to prove knowledge of the rule as an element of every violation.

**Proposition-level authority:** This qualification is part of Part II and has the same eight-Justice coalition. It states the limit under which the criminal Rule 14e-3(a) ruling is sustained, not a factual adjudication of O'Hagan's knowledge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule's knowledge or reason-to-know conditions about information and source remain distinct from willfulness and the defendant's lack-of-rule-knowledge protection. O'Hagan's actual knowledge and punishment eligibility are not determined.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Central Bank's private accessory severe-recklessness standard and Princeton's copyright willfulness rule are not criminal securities standards.

##### IV. The mail-fraud reversal cannot rest on the rejected securities premise

**Holding:** The Eighth Circuit's reversal of mail-fraud counts 1–20, insofar as it rested on the securities theories' legal invalidity, must be reversed now that the Court rejects that premise. The case is remanded for consideration of preserved alternate grounds not reached below; money-laundering counts 55–57 were not presented for review and remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of the Court opinion is joined by O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer and Stone-Zsela: seven direct votes. Scalia and Thomas join the mail-fraud reversal and remand, making that judgment unanimous, but not this entire rationale; Thomas's independent Carpenter ground is separate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Mail-fraud reversal and remand are unanimous in judgment, with seven adopting the Court's rationale. Reinstatement of reviewed convictions is appropriate only to the extent they survive preserved instruction, sufficiency, harmless-error and other lawful challenges. No conviction is unconditionally restored; the unreviewed laundering reversal stands.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Thomas's separate Carpenter ground is not a controlling independent alternative holding, and Scalia's agreement in result is not a join of either complete rationale.

#### Central Bank of Denver, N.A. v. First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A., 511 U.S. 164

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-854.

**Decided:** 1994-04-19.

##### The received private action includes culpable substantial assistance

**Holding:** The established private action under §10(b) and Rule 10b-5 permits recovery from a person who knowingly or with severe recklessness substantially assists an underlying securities fraud through affirmative conduct, subject to proof of that fraud, the defendant’s qualifying participation, legally sufficient reliance, causation and recoverable loss. This is accessory responsibility within the received investor action, not a finding that the assistant made a primary misrepresentation or a rule imposing liability through professional status or control alone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the existence of the confined action and its received-remedy rationale. No fractured-decision synthesis is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Superintendent of Insurance v. Bankers Life & Casualty Co., 404 U.S. 6 — applied as recognition of the received private action; it did not itself decide accessory liability.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau, 508 U.S. 286 — applied for bounded development of the received action and distinguished as to its actual contribution holding; shared liability must still be established before allocation.
- Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. Curran, 456 U.S. 353 — used by analogy for the significance of an established judicial remedy; its different statutory setting does not establish congressional enactment of this action.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — applied: the underlying wrong must involve the required manipulation or deception, not unfairness or fiduciary misconduct alone.
- Pinter v. Dahl, 486 U.S. 622 — distinguished: its construction of express seller liability under §12 does not settle this received §10(b) action; its own statutory holding remains intact.
- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — its reservation of aiding and abetting is resolved here for the confined private affirmative-assistance action; Hochfelder itself did not decide that question.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — its reservation of aiding and abetting is resolved here for the confined private affirmative-assistance action; Huddleston itself did not decide that question.

##### Knowledge or severe recklessness in affirmative substantial assistance

**Holding:** For affirmative substantial assistance, the defendant must know or severely recklessly disregard both the fraudulent character of the activity and the assisting role of its conduct; severe recklessness means an extreme departure from ordinary care presenting a specific danger of deception known to the defendant or so obvious that the defendant must have appreciated it. No independent disclosure duty is required for that affirmative participation, but negligence, valuation concerns alone, professional status, ordinary services without qualifying culpability and assistance, or mere silence without a relevant duty do not suffice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; all five adopt this confined mental-state and affirmative-assistance rule, not a universal recklessness rule for every securities claim.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ernst & Ernst v. Hochfelder, 425 U.S. 185 — its exclusion of negligence remains controlling; its recklessness reservation is resolved only for this private affirmative-assistance setting.
- Santa Fe Industries, Inc. v. Green, 430 U.S. 462 — its deception-or-manipulation requirement prevents ordinary investment risk and professional error from becoming securities fraud.
- First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. v. Pring, 969 F.2d 891 — its confined affirmative-assistance and extreme-recklessness analysis is adopted for the bank claim; inconsistent knowledge-only or categorical duty requirements for such affirmative assistance cannot govern after this decision. Its separate rulings concerning Pring are outside this review.

##### Triable issues require further proceedings, not a liability award

**Holding:** Viewed favorably to the purchasers at summary judgment, the appraisal warnings, internal concerns and agreement postponing an already demanded independent review until after the offering permit a genuine dispute over severe recklessness and substantial affirmative assistance; the Tenth Circuit’s reinstatement of the pleaded bank claim is affirmed and the case remanded. The purchasers must still prove the underlying violation, the bank’s qualifying assistance and culpability, legally sufficient reliance on the underlying fraud, causation and recoverable loss under the ordinary civil burden; neither the default nor the bonds’ face value establishes fraud damages.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt the record application, preserved proof requirements and limited affirmance and remand.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- First Interstate Bank of Denver, N.A. v. Pring, 969 F.2d 891 — affirmed insofar as it reverses summary judgment on the purchasers’ affirmative-assistance claim against Central Bank; triable issues are not findings of fraud or damages.
- Herman & MacLean v. Huddleston, 459 U.S. 375 — the ordinary preponderance standard is preserved; neither institutional status nor information held by the defendant shifts that burden.
- Basic Inc. v. Levinson, 485 U.S. 224 — its reliance requirement is preserved. Today’s decision establishes no presumption for these municipal bonds and, as part of the retained accessory action, requires reliance on the underlying fraud rather than on the assistant’s concealed participation.
- Musick, Peeler & Garrett v. Employers Insurance of Wausau, 508 U.S. 286 — its shared-liability prerequisite and contribution limits remain effective; this remand establishes no entitlement or allocation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide the full rules for duty-based omissions, duty-free inaction, agency attribution, a universal securities recklessness rule, other enforcement or statutory settings, or Stone's additional concurrence formulations. The §20(a) defense for a controller acting in good faith without directly or indirectly inducing the violation remains distinct. Reliance on the underlying fraud is required; investors need not personally rely on concealed assistance, and no reliance presumption is established for these bonds.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Tenth Circuit insofar as it reinstated the pleaded bank claim and remand, 5–4. The bank's summary judgment cannot rest on the rejected categorical grounds or the disputed assistance and culpability record. No liability, damages, automatic indemnity, unpleaded primary claim or adjudication of another defendant's claim is supplied.

#### Austin v. United States, 509 U.S. 602 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-6073.
**Decided:** June 28, 1993.

##### These statutory forfeitures are covered fines

**Holding:** Forfeiture to the United States under 21 U.S.C.§§ 881(a)(4) and(a)(7) constitutes payment in kind to the sovereign at least partly as punishment and is subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Its civil designation, proceeding against property, and concurrent remedial aims do not remove coverage; coverage does not establish excessiveness or import all criminal-procedure safeguards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris v. Kelco Disposal, 492 U.S. 257: government prosecution/receipt distinguishes this fine from private punitive award.
- United States v. Halper, 490 U.S. 435: partly punitive function recognized by analogy; separate Double Jeopardy analysis not imported.
- One 1958 Plymouth Sedan v. Pennsylvania, 380 U.S. 693: lawful property is not contraband merely because used in crime.
- One Lot Emerald Cut Stones v. United States, 409 U.S. 232, and United States v. One Assortment of 89 Firearms, 465 U.S. 354: remedial customs/contraband characterizations distinguished, not abolished.
- Buena Vista, February 24, 1993: statutory owner defenses remain provision-specific; no § 881(a)(7) or constitutional innocent owner holding imported.

##### Historical in rem form does not negate punishment

**Holding:** The historical treatment of statutory in rem forfeiture includes punishment and does not establish categorical exemption from the Excessive Fines Clause for these forfeitures. The traditionally limited common-law innocent-owner defense does not prove that forfeiture was solely remedial; the constitutionality of forfeiting a truly innocent owner’s property remains unresolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Current-force modification:** Bennis v. Michigan, No. 94-8729, decided March 4, 1996, answers the reserved due-process question for a co-owner’s separately held interest in an ordinarily lawful automobile when she establishes nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention of another’s prohibited use. Ordinary co-ownership and permission for shared lawful use do not conclusively defeat that protection; knowingly tolerated misuse, culpable entrustment and omitted reasonably expected precautions remain outside it. Austin’s punitive-forfeiture holding and Excessive Fines remand remain unchanged. Bennis supplies no comprehensive innocent-owner or instrumentality rule and no state-incorporation holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Peisch v. Ware, 4 Cranch 347: punitive understanding and stranger-removal qualification applied.
- The Palmyra, 12 Wheat 1: no prior personal conviction required, not an absence of punishment holding.
- J.W. Goldsmith, Jr.-Grant Co. v. United States, 254 U.S. 505, and Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht, 416 U.S. 663: punitive/deterrent features recognized; truly innocent owner question reserved.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Bennis v. Michigan, No. 94-8729, March 4, 1996 — qualified due-process owner protection and conditional property relief.

##### Remand leaves excessiveness distinct from statutory connection

**Holding:** The lower court must evaluate whether Austin’s actual forfeiture is excessive; statutory facilitation and coverage as punishment do not themselves decide that question. The Court adopts no comprehensive excessiveness test and does not limit the remand to instrumentality alone: the property’s connection to the offense may matter, and other relevant considerations may also be examined.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Coverage does not decide excessiveness, incorporate all criminal safeguards or establish state incorporation. Truly innocent ownership, taxation and solely compensatory recovery remain distinct; neither a comprehensive formula nor instrumentality alone is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand for an excessiveness inquiry on a developed record. No automatic property return, value finding, ratio or fixed cap; the lower court’s concern was not a completed excessiveness determination.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Browning-Ferris, 492 U.S. 257: fine coverage principle applied without inventing a comprehensive proportionality formula.
- Calero-Toledo, 416 U.S. 663: statutory/historical connection not converted into a final excessiveness answer.

#### United States v. Bajakajian, 524 U.S. 321

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1487.

**Decided:** June 22, 1998.

##### A punitive criminal forfeiture must not be grossly disproportionate to its offense

**Holding:** Section 982(a)(1)’s forfeiture imposed on this convicted offender as punishment is a fine subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. A punitive forfeiture violates that Clause when grossly disproportionate to the gravity of the offense for which it is imposed; statutory property connection alone does not answer excessiveness, and instrumentality is not a categorical prerequisite barring every currency-reporting forfeiture.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial respect for legislative punishment judgments and the imprecision of gravity assessments make gross disproportionality, rather than strict equivalence, the governing boundary. Solem supplies that respect and proportionality analogy without deciding a new imprisonment question. The nature of the offense, culpability, harm, property’s relation to wrongdoing and authorized sanctions inform the comparison where relevant. They are not a compulsory exhaustive checklist, fixed ratio or government-cost formula. Congress may punish a reporting violation through property forfeiture, but its statutory authorization does not foreclose the constitutional inquiry. The Court rejects the circuit’s conclusion that such currency can never be forfeited for want of an instrumentality.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Austin v. United States:** Partly punitive coverage and an excessiveness inquiry beyond statutory connection are applied; its holdings are not converted into an instrumentality-only test or a universal historical forfeiture theory.
- **Alexander v. United States:** Its independent offense-related Excessive Fines inquiry for criminal forfeiture is applied and given the gross-disproportionality formulation here.
- **Browning-Ferris Industries v. Kelco Disposal:** Its distinction between governmental punishment and private awards supports fine coverage.
- **Solem v. Helm:** Its legislative-deference and proportionality principles inform this fines rule without a new imprisonment holding.
- **Department of Revenue v. Kurth Ranch, United States v. Ursery and Hudson v. United States:** Their distinct Double Jeopardy classification rules remain intact and supply no automatic Excessive Fines result or universal cost ceiling.
- **One Lot Emerald Cut Stones and One Assortment of 89 Firearms:** Their distinct customs and forfeiture classifications establish no exemption or proportionality answer for this direct criminal reporting fine.

##### The legal proportionality judgment receives independent appellate review

**Holding:** Appellate courts independently review the constitutional determination whether this punitive forfeiture is grossly disproportionate, while reviewing supported underlying factual findings for clear error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela does not join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The facts found and the constitutional evaluation perform different work. Deference to the trial court’s supported findings does not transfer the legal boundary of the Excessive Fines Clause to unrestricted sentencing discretion. Independent review supplies no authority to invent an additional offense or substitute adverse facts for the findings. The Court announces no universal review standard for every mixed question.

##### Full forfeiture is grossly disproportionate on these findings

**Holding:** Forfeiture of the full $357,144 is grossly disproportionate to the willful reporting offense established here, considering the lawful funds and purpose, absence of another-crime connection, limited reporting harm and particular sentencing assessment together. The refusal of full forfeiture is affirmed without reviewing the existing $15,000 amount or setting a constitutional dollar ceiling.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Material application:** The reporting duty is serious, and willful failure remains culpable even when transporting the funds would otherwise be lawful. But the proved offense withheld information; the findings establish neither criminal proceeds nor laundering, trafficking, fraud or a separate revenue loss against which the whole fund may be measured. Dismissal of the false-statement count does not establish that no false statement occurred.

The applicable six-month and $5,000 Guidelines maxima reflect the particular offense’s assessed severity. Congress’s broader five-year and $250,000 maxima and the forfeiture authorization deserve weight but do not conclusively make this full exaction proportionate. Treating them as conclusive would deprive independent excessiveness review of work. The lawful source, lawful debt and lack of another-crime connection are relevant together; lawful property is not automatically immune from a fine.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The reporting system’s preventive function and the large unreported transaction supply the strongest contrary argument. They do not overcome the extraordinary mismatch on these supported findings. The Court substitutes no new amount. Because Bajakajian did not cross-appeal, neither $15,000’s validity nor authority to select it is decided; no rule identifies $15,001 as excessive. Wealth, income, livelihood effects, other materially different crime-connected forfeitures and a comprehensive innocent-owner or in-rem rule remain unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment refusing the Government’s demand for full forfeiture remains effective. No new calculation or further forfeiture hearing is directed. The unappealed partial forfeiture and other sentence components remain undisturbed without affirmative adjudication of their independent validity.

#### Hudson v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-976.

**Decided:** 1997-12-10.

##### Civil intent remains rebuttable through examination of actual punitive operation

**Holding:** Congress's civil designation of these banking monetary sanctions and the regulatory character of the occupational restriction establish the intended civil framework. That designation may be overcome by the clearest proof that the sanctions' purpose or effect is criminal; their supported design and operation must be assessed together, without treating deterrence, severity, occupational burden or overlap with crime as individually conclusive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The original monetary authorization governs; later increased penalty tiers do not apply. Good-faith and gravity considerations affect amount without establishing a universal willfulness element.

##### These monetary assessments survive Halper's retained personal-penalty inquiry

**Independent alternative holding:** A personal civil penalty remains subject to Halper's requirement of a rational remedial relationship, including reasonable approximation; that rule is not overruled or replaced by a universal statutory-face inquiry. On the supported record, the actual $44,000 in total monetary assessments, compared with the District Court's finding of $72,000 in government costs pursuing these defendants, permits a remedial characterization and requires no further accounting.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Breyer's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; six Justices. This independent accounting ground does not receive Stone-Zsela's join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable approximation of legitimate pursuit costs suffices; the agency need not have calculated costs initially. The $200,000 initial demand is not the $44,000 obligation imposed. No automatic monetary ratio, immunity for every penalty below asserted costs or universal compensation ceiling is established.

##### The combined sanctions do not bar this prosecution

**Holding:** The banking restriction, which permits participation with written regulatory authorization, protects positions of trust in a regulated industry. Considered with the actual financial assessments, its supported operation does not provide the clearest proof of criminal punishment; the subsequent prosecution therefore is not barred on the presented multiple-punishment theory.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg; seven Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The banking restriction permits participation with written regulatory authorization. Different schemes or materially different actual operation may classify differently. No offense-identity, attachment, Excessive Fines or independent due-process question is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The unanimous judgment affirms reinstatement of the criminal prosecution without an additional accounting hearing or any determination of guilt or offense identity.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Halper remains binding in its exceptional personal-civil-penalty field and is applied to the supported monetary obligations and costs. Ursery's rebuttable civil classification and preservation of that field remain intact. Hendricks supplies cumulative actual-operation analysis without transferring its confinement outcome. Ward and Mendoza-Martinez retain their intent/effect and punitive-indicator roles. Kurth Ranch's extraordinary-tax holding is distinguished, not narrowed. Dixon, Grady, Blockburger and Felix retain their offense-identity rules; none is applied to determine the identity of these charges. Austin's distinct Excessive Fines protection remains available in an appropriate case. No precedent is overruled.

#### Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1971.

**Decided:** 1998-02-24.

##### Federal claim preclusion remains a defense

**Holding:** Under 28 U.S.C. §§1331 and 1441, a prior federal judgment's claim-preclusive effect is a defense and does not transform a complaint presenting only state-law claims into an action arising under federal law. Complete statutory preemption differs: Congress must displace the state claim with the exclusive federal cause that supplies its legal character; the completeness of an asserted judgment defense does not perform that work.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Louisiana mortgage claims may be defeated by bankruptcy judgments without acquiring a federal character. The Court decides neither the mortgage rights nor the bankruptcy orders’ validity or actual preclusive effect; an otherwise available enforcement proceeding needs its own authority.

##### Dependent merits judgments must be vacated

**Holding:** Because this action lacks the asserted original federal jurisdiction and no later jurisdictional cure is established, the dependent federal merits judgments must be vacated and the whole action returned to state court. A different merits ground for one respondent does not cure the same jurisdictional defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The entire action must return to state court. Vacatur includes the Bank and Fountainbleau Storage Associates preclusion judgments and the Browns’ differently reasoned failure-to-state-a-claim judgment. The state court may apply governing federal preclusion law.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Moitie's ordinary claim-preclusion rule is preserved; its footnote is not extended into a general removal exception for prior federal judgments.
- Peacock supplies the independent-authority requirement for a later action while preserving ordinary enforcement within its proper sphere.
- City of Chicago is distinguished because substantial federal claims appeared on the complaints' face there; supplemental jurisdiction has no comparable original anchor here.
- Caterpillar is distinguished because actual jurisdiction arose before trial there; finality alone cannot save this action's merits judgments.
- Matsushita preserves judgment recognition without equating that effect with adjudicatory power.
- Richards preserves constitutional limits on binding parties; it supplies no removal authority and no finding about these petitioners' binding status.
- Baker preserves the distinction among recognition, execution and new duties in another forum; that distinction creates no universal removal grant.

#### Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1263.

**Decided:** December 10, 1996.

##### The erroneous refusal to remand remains error

**Holding:** Complete diversity was absent when Caterpillar removed, and Lewis's timely motion required remand under the applicable removal and remand statutes. The later dismissal of the nondiverse party does not make the earlier ruling correct, waive Lewis's preserved objection, or require him to have sought extraordinary pretrial relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No collateral challenge to an entered covered remand is authorized. No late alternative removal argument or independent fee or sanction entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Things Remembered and Quackenbush retain the mandatory-remand and covered-remand-review rules at their proper stages. This case concerns an erroneous refusal to remand followed by trial, not review of an actually entered remand protected by section 1447(d).

##### Actual jurisdiction before trial preserves the completed judgment against this earlier defect

**Holding:** When complete diversity actually arises through the nondiverse party's lawful dismissal before trial and persists through judgment, an earlier erroneous refusal of a timely remand motion does not by itself require vacating the completed federal judgment. The rule depends on actual section 1332 jurisdiction during trial and judgment; consent, finality or efficiency cannot cure a judgment entered without that jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal cure for every removal defect follows. The decision authorizes no collusive manufacture of diversity, finds no manipulation, and does not extinguish a separately authorized fee or sanction request.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Finn's defect persisting through judgment is distinguished from actual diversity before trial; its bar on jurisdiction by consent survives. Grubbs's completed-judgment distinction is expressly extended beyond its unobjected-removal facts to this preserved objection and actual pretrial cure. Newman-Green retains lawful dismissal, dispensability and prejudice limits without authorizing manufactured trial jurisdiction. Willy preserves independently authorized collateral consequences but supplies no merits jurisdiction or cure for an uncured trial.

##### Restoration against jurisdictional vacatur leaves the ordinary appeal for decision

**Holding:** The Sixth Circuit's jurisdiction-based vacatur and state-court-remand direction are reversed; the district-court judgment is restored against that jurisdictional ground, subject to the unresolved ordinary appeal. The case returns to the Sixth Circuit to decide the five preserved issues it did not reach: prior-accident discovery restrictions, the discovery-sanction request, excluded exhibits, excluded witnesses and the omitted failure-to-warn instruction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with this remand disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not finally affirm the defense verdict against the five remaining challenges or decide any of their merits.

#### California Public Employees' Retirement System and Florida State Board of Administration v. Paul Felzen et al.

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1732.
**Decided:** 1999-01-20.

##### 1. Rule 23.1 objection does not itself confer the party status needed for this appeal

**Holding:** A shareholder who is otherwise not a party does not acquire appellate party status merely by appearing under Rule 23.1 notice and presenting objections that the district court hears and rejects when approving a derivative settlement. To appeal that settlement judgment, the shareholder must properly become a party through intervention; shareholder interest, notice and the recognized opportunity to object do not themselves supply the necessary status.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court is joined by the other four members of the coalition comprising Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. All five adopt both holdings and their stated qualifications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is a rule for the presented derivative-settlement appeal, not a categorical prohibition of every nonparty appeal under every procedural regime. Section 1291 supplies the final-decision framework, Appellate Rule 3(c) concerns identification of appellants, Rule 23.1 governs the recognized objection role, and Rule 24 supplies intervention; neither §1291 nor Rule 3(c) is treated as expressly deciding every party-status category. The decision rests on actual participation and the absence of intervention, not on a finding that the institutions violated a notice or service condition.

##### 2. Intervention review and nonparty due-process protection remain distinct

**Holding:** Requiring intervention for this settlement appeal does not foreclose review by an actual party or an appropriate appeal from an actual denial of intervention. Nor does it impose a general duty to intervene in another person's suit to preserve an otherwise independent claim: constitutional limits on binding genuinely unrepresented persons remain, without establishing an appellate entitlement or a collateral remedy in this case.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same five-Justice opinion of the Court controls this boundary and the confined affirmance.

**Limits and operative remedy:** The Court orders no reopening, tolling, late intervention, new hearing, discovery or review of a fictional denied motion. It does not independently decide the petition's requested pure-prospectivity question or adopt the Court of Appeals' broader assertion that every possible operation of Chevron Oil was eliminated. Harper's rule against selective prospectivity after application of a federal rule to the Court's litigants remains at its established scope; it creates no unsupported transition entitlement here. No settlement-fairness, fee-merits, collusion, representation-adequacy, personal-claim or collateral-preclusion judgment follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with consideration of the ruling letter's reasons according to their actual persuasive force under Skidmore. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither definitive classification commands a majority. The return rests on exclusion of permissible agency reasoning, not a missing product fact, a general right to first lower-court interpretation or a need for agency action. The distinct noncontrolling textual and deference grounds for sustaining the category do not combine into a Court construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit must independently classify the planners while considering the actual persuasive force of Customs' reasons. Any assessment or refund depends on that classification and entry calculations; no amount, new hearing, signature search or future rulemaking is required.

#### Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens, 529 U.S. 765 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1828.
**Decided:** May 22, 2000.

##### I. Transfer of the government's financial claim supplies standing

**Holding:** The FCA's partial assignment of the United States' concrete financial claim gives the relator standing to enforce that assigned claim, subject to the ordinary injury, traceability and redressability requirements at the litigation stage. A prospective reward alone would not create injury, and this assignment conclusion does not itself make the relator's action the United States' own suit for every other legal purpose.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices, giving nine direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Standing leaves defendant coverage, constitutional immunity and Article II control separate. The Court does not settle every assignment, penalty-only suit or private public-interest action. The pleaded federal financial loss remains subject to the proof required at each litigation stage; no fraud is found.

##### II. This private-relator action is not authorized against a sovereign State

**Holding:** Section 3729's generic person category, read with the FCA's private-relator structure and section 3733's investigation-specific definition, contains no affirmative indication authorizing this privately maintained action against a sovereign State. The action against the Vermont Agency must therefore be dismissed on statutory grounds without deciding constitutional State immunity.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer with Scalia, seven direct votes for this complete bounded ground. Stevens and Souter dissent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court leaves open suits brought by the United States, municipal liability, retaliation claims, differently drafted legislation and Article II questions. It decides neither ultimate fraud nor any remedy amount. Its rule is confined to this private-relator action against the State.

**Material application:** The United States' appellate participation did not take over the relator's action. Receipt of federal grants establishes no consent; the decision supplies no categorical Article I immunity bar.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The case returns through the Second Circuit for dismissal of the private-relator action against the Vermont Agency on the statutory ground. The dismissal does not adjudicate the alleged fraud, the amount of loss or constitutional immunity and authorizes no recovery.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Applied to require a real injury and litigation-stage support; an assigned government financial claim supplies a different basis from bare enforcement permission.
- **Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.:** Applied by deciding Article III before contested statutory coverage.
- **Friends of the Earth v. Laidlaw:** Distinguished from a claim that reward or deterrence alone creates injury; its independent-injury requirement remains.
- **Florida Prepaid v. College Savings Bank:** Its Article I enforcement rule and distinct unmistakable-authorization requirement remain controlling; congressional power alone supplies no FCA coverage.
- **Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents:** Applied to keep statutory clarity separate from constitutional power; its express authorization does not decide this differently drafted statute.
- **College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid:** Its rejection of consent inferred from ordinary continued activity remains; no consent is found from receipt of grants.

#### Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett, 531 U.S. 356

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1240.
**Decided:** February 21, 2001.

##### Whether Congress validly authorizes private Title I employment actions against state employers

**Holding:** Congress may subject a State to the expressly authorized private Title I federal action when the covered employment obligation and its enforcement measure fall within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. Those conditions are satisfied here, so the State defendants have no categorical immunity from these actions; authorization and amenability establish neither liability nor entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; the same five Justices adopt both independent grounds.

**Material application:** Section 12202 unmistakably authorizes the actions and section 12117 supplies the enforcement channels. Covered employment is an economic relationship in actual labor markets; the ordinary private action enforces that valid duty rather than compelling state administration of a federal program. Consent and proof of a separate constitutional tort are unnecessary to maintain these actions.

**Independent alternative holding:** Title I's protection of qualified workers against disability discrimination, including reasonable accommodation subject to its enacted qualifications and defenses, is congruent and proportional preventive and remedial enforcement of equal protection against state employers. Section 5 therefore independently sustains the expressly authorized actions without requiring each employee to prove a separate constitutional violation.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The same five Justices adopt Section 5 as an independently sufficient ground.

**Material application:** Congress may use bounded prevention against irrational disability prejudice without turning every statutory violation into a constitutional violation. Differentiated evidence of public-employment exclusion supports that inference; no quota of adjudicated violations, formal-findings requirement, geographic restriction or sunset is imposed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Covered-employer status requires an industry affecting commerce and ordinarily at least 15 employees for each working day in at least 20 calendar weeks of the current or preceding year; the original two-year transition used 25 employees. Agents are included; the United States, wholly owned federal corporations, Indian tribes and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations that are exempt from taxation under 26 U.S.C. section 501(c) are excluded from this employer definition. Actual coverage remains to be proved.

Disability retains impairment substantially limiting a major life activity, record-of-impairment and regarded-as branches. A qualified worker must perform essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation. Employer judgment receives consideration; a job description prepared before advertising or interviews is evidence, not a conclusive veto. Accommodation examples are permissive and include reassignment to a vacant position. Section 12112(b)(5)(A) concerns known limitations and allows the employer to demonstrate undue hardship: significant difficulty or expense assessed by cost, facility and entity resources, workforce, operational and organizational factors. Section 12112(b)(6) separately addresses screening criteria shown job-related for the position and consistent with business necessity; section 12113(a) separately requires that performance cannot be accomplished by reasonable accommodation. Direct threat concerns a significant risk to the health or safety of others that reasonable accommodation cannot eliminate. No general reasonableness exception or Title II fundamental-alteration defense replaces those provisions.

No medical-inquiry, foreign-workplace, religious-entity, food-handling or drug/alcohol defense is established or adjudicated here. The Court creates no exception or holding about those distinct provisions. Title II, Rehabilitation Act waiver, individual liability and award-specific limitations lie outside this Title I abrogation question; no Title II dismissal or abandonment is ordered. Qualification, discrimination, causation and relief are ordinary remaining merits issues, not unresolved aspects of the constitutional enforcement holdings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Eleventh Circuit immunity judgment is affirmed. Coverage, qualification, discrimination, causation, defenses and lawful relief remain for ordinary adjudication; no payment, accommodation or employment change is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999: applies both its express Article I enforcement rule and its independent congruent-and-proportional prevention principle; neither requires consent or an individual constitutional tort.
- Kimel, January 11, 2000: applies its Article I employment-enforcement holding; its reservation of the ADEA's Section 5 validity remains untouched.
- Lopez: applies the identifiable commerce connection before aggregation; no general social-cost theory or automatic jurisdictional-word test is adopted.
- Garcia and EEOC v. Wyoming: apply their support for valid federal regulation of state employment.
- Boerne: applies congruence and proportionality, preserving preventive authority and the prohibition on constitutional redefinition.
- Cleburne, Heller and Romer: apply actual rationality review without heightened disability scrutiny or a constitutional guarantee of accommodation.
- Olmstead: distinguishes its statutory integration holding and express nondecision of immunity and Section 5; its Title II defense is not imported.
- Union Gas and Seminole Tribe: preserve the constitutional conclusion extended in Florida Prepaid and the distinction between authorization and power; no categorical Article I prohibition displaces current law.
- College Savings Bank: preserves the rejection of consent inferred from ordinary State activity; these actions rest on legislative authority.
