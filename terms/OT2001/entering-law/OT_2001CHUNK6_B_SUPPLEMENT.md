## Standards and Tests selection: Initiative circulator qualifications and individual identification

### Initiative circulator qualifications and individual identification

**Governing question:** Speech burdens imposed on initiative circulators by registered-voter, personal-name and individual-payee requirements.

**Current rule:** Excluding otherwise qualified resident-adult initiative circulators solely because they are unregistered voters substantially burdens protected petition conversation; ease of registration does not remove the burden. Traceability justifications must address retained affidavits, signature review, process and targeted fraud enforcement rather than treating voter registration as a necessary speech qualification. A requirement to display the circulator’s personal name during face-to-face advocacy must be justified at that immediate expressive encounter; materially less burdensome post-circulation identification and enforcement matter to fit.

Compelled publication of each paid circulator’s identity, addresses and individual compensation in final or monthly reports requires a substantial relationship to important informational or enforcement interests. The messenger is not automatically a financing source or candidate contributor. Sponsor identity and aggregate spending disclosures retain a distinct financing function. Timing may matter independently, but disclosure during circulation does not cure an insufficient relationship between each messenger’s identity and the asserted interest.

**Authority by component:**

- Voter-registration and badge rules: Canonical Decision Record `buckley_v_american_constitutional_law_foundation_merits_1999-01-12.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Registered-voter condition; II. Personal name displayed during solicitation, 1999-01-12; six direct joins to I, eight to II.
- Individual-payee reporting inquiry and timing: Canonical Decision Record `buckley_v_american_constitutional_law_foundation_merits_1999-01-12.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III-A. Final individual-circulator reports; III-B. Monthly individual-circulator reports, 1999-01-12; six direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-01-12.

**Exceptions and limits:** These holdings concern Colorado’s reviewed restrictions and their supported burdens and alternatives. Residence, age and circulation-period requirements, standalone paid-status or employer attribution, and independent monthly-report fields are not adjudicated. No categorical initiative-finance disclosure exemption or anonymity from lawful investigation follows; materially narrower reporting requires its own justification.

## Standards and Tests selection: Residential signs and preservation of a distinctive speech medium

### Residential signs and preservation of a distinctive speech medium

**Governing question:** Whether a municipality may largely eliminate the homeowner’s own-sign channel of expression.

**Current rule:** A municipality cannot virtually eliminate residential signs when the channel has distinctive communicative value and the offered substitutes do not adequately replace it, even assuming the ordinance is content neutral. A sign at the speaker’s own home combines location, attribution, cost and accessibility in ways that handbills, newspapers or other channels may not reproduce. Legitimate aesthetic and safety interests permit appropriate physical regulation, but do not themselves justify the near-total loss of that medium.

**Authority by component:**

- Distinctive residential channel and inadequate alternatives: City of Ladue v. Gilleo — The replacement prohibition leaves no adequate residential channel, 1994-06-13. Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. All nine adopt the same residential-channel holding and limited remedy; no fractured-opinion inference is necessary.

**Present operation effective:** City of Ladue v. Gilleo, 1994-06-13.

**Open question:** No fixed size, number or duration limit, categorical exemption scheme or facial ruling on every content distinction is adopted.

**Holdings navigation:** The replacement prohibition leaves no adequate residential channel.

## Standards and Tests selection: Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX

### Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX

**Governing question:** Sex-based teacher harassment and compensatory responsibility of the funding recipient.

**Current rule:** Sexual subject matter or offense alone does not show discrimination because of sex. Same-sex harassment can constitute sex discrimination; the claim requires sex-based causation, unwelcome treatment, sufficient educational harm, recipient responsibility and causal injury. Participation or later continuation does not conclusively establish earlier welcome. The environment must be sufficiently severe or pervasive to be objectively abusive and actually experienced as such, assessed in the educational context without mandatory grade-loss, psychiatric-injury or factor requirements. One sufficient route to recipient damages is actual notice to an official empowered to correct the harassment or its substantial risk and deliberate, unreasonable disregard of that known danger causing injury or continuation. This actual-corrective-official-notice route is not exclusive. A separate sufficient attribution route exists when school-conferred authority actually and materially enables the teacher’s intentional discrimination and the recipient’s own actual knowledge or willfully blind, deliberately indifferent reporting failure causes that discrimination to continue. This route does not itself establish discrimination, causal loss, damages or defeat of an independent defense.

**Authority by component:**

- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — I. The sex of the harasser does not exclude a Title IX claim, 1997-05-27; Stone-Zsela Part I, all nine
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — II. Participation does not establish welcome, and educational harm is contextual, 1997-05-27; the same nine
- Kinman v. Omaha Public School District — III. Recipient compensation requires its own culpable response, 1997-05-27; Stone-Zsela Part IIA with O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins independent of the remand coalition
- Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. — The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex, 1997-02-24; Ginsburg for all nine supplies this independently sufficient statutory element

- Gebser I: five direct votes rejecting exclusivity; II: the narrower authority-plus-recipient-fault attribution component under Marks and Cipollone: Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, No. 96-1866. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-22.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-22, for the new component; earlier component authorities retain their stated dates.

**Exceptions and limits:** Sexual behavior alone does not prove the elements; an opposite-sex comparator is not invariably necessary. Employment, ordinary teacher access or a technical policy breach alone does not establish recipient responsibility. A reasonable response does not become unlawful merely because harm continues. Gebser’s four broader agency adherents do not formally join Stone-Zsela’s recipient-fault reasoning: their materially-aided-authority rationale necessarily covers every case satisfying his narrower conjunction, supplying the component-specific five-Justice ground. Neither Stone-Zsela’s complete proposed framework nor his disputed-evidence application has five joins.

**Holdings navigation:** Kinman v. Omaha Public School District (1997-05-27) — Civil Rights / Teacher harassment and institutional responsibility; Brown v. Hot, Sexy & Safer Productions, Inc. (1997-02-24) — The Title IX claim lacks discrimination because of sex. Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District, 524 U.S. 274 — Title IX recipient responsibility for teacher harassment.

**Material development:** Gebser, 1998-06-22, removes Kinman’s exclusivity for actual notice to a corrective official while retaining that route and the independent substantive discrimination requirements. Its judgment does not find knowledge, willful blindness, material aid, causation or liability.

## Standards and Tests selection: Recipient responsibility for peer harassment under Title IX

### Recipient responsibility for peer harassment under Title IX

**Governing question:** When a funding recipient’s response to student peer harassment supports compensatory liability.

**Current rule:** A sufficient route to recipient damages exists when an official empowered to correct harassment or its substantial danger has actual notice, the recipient substantially controls the harasser and the educational context, and deliberate and unreasonable disregard of that danger causally contributes to further harassment or vulnerability producing educational deprivation and compensable injury. Harassment must be because of sex and sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an objectively abusive educational environment actually experienced as abusive and impair educational access or benefit in context. Notice, culpability, control, discrimination and causal injury remain independent requirements; peer attendance does not impute the peer’s acts to the recipient.

**Authority by component:**

- Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A school’s own culpable response can support damages for controlled peer harassment”, 1999-05-24; Ginsburg Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Breyer. Part I supplies the sufficient actual-notice, control and recipient-own-wrong route.
- Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Educational deprivation follows the contextual severe-or-pervasive standard”, 1999-05-24; Ginsburg Part II with Stevens, O’Connor, Souter and Breyer. Five direct joins supply the disjunctive harm threshold; Stone-Zsela does not join that component.

**Present operation effective:** Davis, May 24, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Generic constructive notice, a harasser’s knowledge, ordinary negligence, policy breach and an unsuccessful reasonable response do not establish this route. No mandatory grade loss, psychiatric harm, physical exclusion, total loss of schooling or one-incident rule is adopted. Courts prescribe no preferred punishment; legal limits on discipline inform control and response.

**Related current rules:** Teacher harassment and recipient responsibility under Title IX retains its distinct school-delegated-authority route; that route is not imputed to peers. Receipt of federal assistance remains separately necessary.

## Standards and Tests selection: Meaningful review of punitive-damage amounts

### Meaningful review of punitive-damage amounts

**Governing question:** Due process protection against arbitrary punitive amounts, including meaningful judicial review, substantive excessiveness and territorial limits.

**Current rule:** A State may not remove meaningful judicial review of the amount of a punitive-damage award without an adequate substitute protecting against arbitrary deprivation. Review limited to whether any evidence supports an award, or safeguards directed only to entitlement rather than amount, do not necessarily fill that gap. The inquiry concerns meaningful protection against arbitrary magnitude; it does not establish a fixed constitutional ratio, mandatory remittitur, a prescribed written-opinion format or a required number of reviewing courts. Independently of adequate procedure, due process forbids punitive awards grossly excessive in relation to legitimate punishment and deterrence; fair notice includes possible severity as well as punishable conduct. Review considers reprehensibility, the relationship to actual or supported potential harm to the plaintiff, and authorized or imposed civil or criminal sanctions for comparable misconduct. Reprehensibility is especially important; the guideposts are neither an exhaustive mechanical checklist nor a ratio or dollar ceiling. Small compensation, egregious wrongdoing, difficult detection or difficult valuation can support a larger ratio; speculative potential loss and wealth cannot replace the required relationship to the wrong. A State may not punish conduct lawful where it occurred and without impact on the State or its residents, or use punitive deterrence to impose its policy on other States. Relevant foreign transactions may nevertheless illuminate pattern, intent and reprehensibility.

Appellate courts must independently apply BMW’s constitutional limit to ordinary civil punitive awards while respecting supported historical findings about conduct, culpability, causation and harm. The Seventh Amendment does not require abuse-of-discretion review of that legal determination. Independent legal review confers no appellate factfinding power and leaves Gasperini’s compensatory-valuation allocation intact.

**Authority by component:**

- Amount review and inadequate substitutes: Honda Motor Co. v. Oberg — Removing amount review without adequate replacement violates due process; Oregon's actual safeguards do not cure the amount-review gap, 1994-06-24. Justice Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt this bounded procedural rule, without a substantive numerical rule. Justice Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; eight Justices adopt the application and limited review remedy.
- BMW — Parts I and III-A, substantive excessiveness, notice and qualified guideposts, 1996-05-20; Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer (five).
- BMW — Part II, territorial punishment and evidence distinction, 1996-05-20; the same five plus Stone-Zsela (six).

- Independent appellate constitutional review and protection of supported historical findings: Canonical Decision Record `Cooper_Industries_v_Leatherman_Tool_Group_merits_2001-05-14.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Holding and operative rule, 2001-05-14; Thomas with Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, seven direct joins, applying BMW as the binding substantive premise.

**Present operation effective:** Cooper Industries, May 14, 2001, for independent appellate application of the constitutional punitive limit; BMW’s substantive and territorial rules and Oberg’s procedural protection retain their existing scope.

**Open question:** The decision does not itself determine the permissible amount of the award or prescribe the only constitutionally sufficient system of review.

**Holdings navigation:** Removing amount review without adequate replacement violates due process; Oregon's actual safeguards do not cure the amount-review gap; BMW of North America, Inc. v. **Open question concerning territorial punishment:** Whether a State may seek to change unlawful conduct elsewhere remains unresolved. The rule imposes no universal exclusion of foreign evidence or rule governing repeated punitive awards for the same conduct.

**Exceptions and limits:** Cooper Industries decides no universal mixed-question review rule and no rule for materially different legislatively fixed multiples or exact deterrence schemes. It does not reconsider BMW’s substantive validity or announce a permissible ratio or dollar amount.

## Standards and Tests selection: Civil RICO limitation, new injury and concealed discovery

### Civil RICO limitation, new injury and concealed discovery

**Governing question:** Whether a later predicate or concealment permits recovery of earlier civil RICO losses after the four-year period.

**Current rule:** A later predicate in the same pattern, including an act injuring someone else, does not renew the four-year limitation period for an already actionable injury. A later wrongful act directly causing a genuinely new injury may support recovery for that injury without reviving older damages. Damages genuinely too speculative or incapable of proof earlier may accrue when they occur; continuing calculable loss, insufficient investigation or a later calculation of old harm does not meet that qualification. Fraudulent-concealment tolling requires the plaintiff to show concealment prevented timely discovery despite reasonable diligence. Misleading conduct alone is insufficient where reasonable inquiry would reveal the claim; extraordinary investigation overcoming otherwise successful concealment is not required.

Once the civil RICO claim is complete and the plaintiff knows the injury, the four-year period cannot await actual or reasonable discovery of the racketeering pattern. Later recognition of that pattern does not revive the stale injury. The ten-year interval allowed between predicate acts defines a potential pattern, not a civil filing period.

**Authority by component:**
- No stale-injury revival and distinct new-injury/unprovable-damage routes: Klehr, Klehr v. A.O. Smith Corp. — I. A later predicate does not renew a stale injury and Klehr v. A.O. Smith Corp. — II. New injury and previously unprovable damages are different from continuing loss, Breyer Parts I–II, six direct joins, 1997-06-19.
- Diligence for concealment tolling: Klehr, Klehr v. A.O. Smith Corp. — III. Concealment does not eliminate reasonable diligence, Breyer Part III, eight Associates, 1997-06-19.

- Completed claim and known injury do not await pattern discovery: Rotella v. Wood, Public Projection / Holdings / A complete claim and known injury do not await discovery of the pattern, February 23, 2000; Souter for all nine. Distinct new injury, unprovable damages and diligent-concealment rules survive preserves the existing Klehr components.

**Present operation effective:** Rotella v. Wood, February 23, 2000, for rejection of pattern-discovery delay on a complete claim and known injury; Klehr's distinct routes remain effective from June 19, 1997.

**Open question:** The general choice between injury-occurrence and injury-discovery accrual remains unresolved, as does an injury preceding completion of the pattern. Common product, damage type, defendant or pattern does not conclusively make an injury old; the direct causal relation remains necessary. Diligent-concealment tolling is distinct from accrual and is not a universal equitable-estoppel rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Klehr v. A. O. Smith Corp. (1997-06-19) — Business and Commercial Law / Civil RICO limitations and concealment.
