## Event

United States v. Angle, including the consolidated appeals of Corey Angle and James Edward Phifer. Merits decision, June 20, 2002. On review of the Fourth Circuit's en banc judgment, 254 F.3d 514, in Nos. 96-4662, 96-4672 and 99-4187. The questions concern judge-found present-offense facts raising binding Guidelines ranges within an outer statutory maximum and hypothetical consecutive terms as an answer to an excessive count sentence. The matter is decided on the merits submission.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at submission and decision. No Justice is recused.

The Court preserves Angle's sentence vacatur, directs resentencing under the constitutional range rule, and vacates the appellate affirmance of Phifer's sentence for renewed plain-error review.

## Holdings

### 1. Present-offense facts changing a binding punishment range receive element protections

**Holding and operative rule:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to a jury, unless a lawful jury waiver permits the judge to determine the element under that same proof standard. A larger outer statutory maximum does not make an enforceable inner Guidelines range discretionary; ordinary judicial assessment remains permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions.

**Authority:** Part I-A of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The law's actual constraint determines whether the additional fact changes punishment authority. Apprendi supplies the principle that a present-offense fact authorizing greater punishment cannot escape element protections through a sentencing label. Its express reservation of general Guidelines classifications and unchanged-maximum minima left this application for decision. The mandatory Guidelines withhold sentencing choices unless an additional quantity fact is established. A still higher outer ceiling does not erase that legal consequence.

The traditional judicial role continues inside the range already authorized. Witte and Watts preserve that within-authority role; they do not hold every binding constraint constitutionally immaterial. Mistretta establishes the Commission's institutional validity, a different question. Limited departures likewise do not make the ordinary mandatory range advisory.

A mandatory floor changes the legal obligation as a ceiling changes permission: a formerly permissible lesser sentence becomes unavailable. McMillan's contrary maximum/minimum distinction cannot sustain that allocation and is overruled to this extent. The floor matters here because quantity compelled the bottom of Angle's 210-to-262-month range even though his 210-month term remained below the 240-month statutory ceiling.

The Guidelines remain binding subject to these constitutional conditions. Prior-conviction questions, a general advisory-system remedy and consecutive sentencing independent of the disputed range premise lie outside the questions resolved. The existing resentencing must identify the lawful range; no unsupported numerical replacement is supplied.

### 2. Federal charging protection accompanies the covered aggravated fact

**Holding and operative rule:** In a federal prosecution, a present-offense fact performing the aggravating legal work identified in Part I-A must receive the applicable grand-jury charging protection as an element. A quantity-free charge and verdict, ordinary sentencing findings, or notice of possible exposure do not themselves establish that element; a valid element admission or lawful waiver must satisfy its own governing requirements.

**Authority:** Part I-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins the disposition on this component without adopting the separate charging proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Jones and Castillo supply the charging consequences of federal aggravated elements. Cotton confirms that omission of aggravating quantity is a genuine constitutional charging defect without withdrawing subject-matter jurisdiction. Part I-A identifies the additional fact by its binding legal consequence. Its placement in a Guideline does not eliminate the protection attached to that function. Here the indictment and verdict specified no quantity, and ordinary judicial calculations are neither grand-jury allegations nor valid admissions of an aggravated element. Proceedings on remand remain governed by existing charging, admission, waiver and sentencing law; this remand itself authorizes no new charging or jury procedure.

### 3. Phifer's actual drug-count term is erroneous and plain

**Holding and operative rule:** Phifer's 292-month drug-count sentence exceeds the 240 months authorized by his quantity-free verdict and is constitutional error. Applying the established review-time rule in the settled-law-then-changed setting, the count excess is plain; substantial-rights effect and discretionary correction remain separate requirements.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct rationale votes. Scalia agrees that the count is erroneous and assumes plainness without joining the affirmative timing application.

**Controlling explanation:** Apprendi supplies the verdict-authorized count limit. Available maxima on other counts do not enlarge it. Johnson supplies review-time plainness where settled trial law changes during direct review, and Cotton applies that framework to an aggravating-quantity omission. This decision applies those authorities to the actual count excess. It does not declare the newly resolved Guidelines question settled at original sentencing or announce a rule for every initially unsettled trial-law question. Olano continues to require substantial-rights effect and the distinct discretionary condition before correction follows.

### 4. An unsupported mandatory aggregate cannot dispose of the remaining plain-error inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** A demonstrated lawful and inevitable identical aggregate may bear on substantial-rights effect under Rule 52(b), but unused cumulative statutory exposure or a supposed mandatory total derived from the constitutionally disputed quantity findings does not itself establish that result. Because the categorical no-prejudice ruling depends on that defective range premise, the appellate affirmance of Phifer's sentence must be vacated for the remaining substantial-rights and discretionary-correction inquiry on the actual record.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The entered judgment imposes a 292-month drug term and concurrent 240-month money-laundering terms. A hypothetical judgment with different terms and consecutive service is not that judgment. The dissent's strongest point concerns mandatory inevitability: if a lawful total compelled identical imprisonment, correcting the count arrangement might cause no prejudice. But the total must first be valid under Part I. Section 5G1.2(d) does not independently establish the total whose legality is contested.

Olano keeps error, plainness, substantial rights and discretionary correction distinct. The defendant ordinarily bears the burden of showing that the error affected the outcome. Even when that showing is made, correction remains discretionary: a reviewing court may correct only an error that seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Vonn permits the relevant existing record to inform that inquiry. Cotton permits refusal of correction under the independent fourth condition on a properly supported record even while substantial-rights effect is assumed. Its particular overwhelming and essentially uncontested threshold evidence is not a finding about Phifer. A general assessment of sufficiency cannot replace that inquiry. Neder concerns its qualified preserved-error setting and does not transfer the burden to the Government here.

The court of appeals must consider the lawful range, actual evidence and any properly supported alternative. Vacating its insufficient rationale neither guarantees correction nor establishes harmlessness. Range calculation and the two remaining conditions are remand work under governing rules, not questions without governing law.

## Precedent Treatment

- Apprendi v. New Jersey is extended to present-offense facts raising binding Guidelines ceilings or floors. Its earlier reservations are resolved to that extent; its within-authorized-range boundary remains.
- McMillan v. Pennsylvania is overruled insofar as ordinary judicial sentencing findings of present-offense facts may raise a mandatory floor merely because the maximum is unchanged.
- Jones v. United States and Castillo v. United States supply the federal aggravated-element charging consequences applied in I-B; their statutory holdings retain their distinct scope.
- Mistretta v. United States retains its institutional approval of the Commission; it does not settle allocation of aggravating factfinding.
- Witte v. United States retains its within-authority sentencing and later-prosecution holdings, without an exemption for facts first changing a binding legal limit.
- United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra retains ordinary consideration within constitutionally authorized punishment, subject to the new binding-range rule.
- United States v. Olano supplies the four distinct forfeiture conditions, ordinary defendant burden and discretionary correction. No condition is eliminated.
- Johnson v. United States supplies its settled-trial-law/intervening-law timing rule, applied to the count excess without a new general timing holding.
- United States v. Cotton preserves nonjurisdictional treatment of the charging omission and record-specific fourth-condition review. Its evidentiary result is not transposed to Phifer.
- United States v. Vonn permits relevant-existing-record review under Rule 52(b); its separate Rule 11 setting creates no burden shift here.
- Neder v. United States remains a qualified preserved materiality-error decision. It supplies neither a hypothetical quantity verdict nor the burden on this forfeited claim.

## Law After Decision

Present-offense facts legally raising either end of a mandatory punishment range receive element protections, including binding Guidelines constraints below an outer statutory maximum. McMillan's contrary minimum rule is displaced. Applicable federal charging requirements attach under I-B's five-Justice holding. Judicial choice remains within the independently established range; the Guidelines are not made advisory.

Count error and appellate correction remain distinct. Phifer's count error and plainness are established while ordinary prejudice and independent discretionary correction remain. A lawful inevitable aggregate may matter to effect; theoretical exposure or the invalid range premise is insufficient. The mandate requires actual application and preserves the convictions.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Scalia joins the binding-range and federal charging holdings and the remaining Rule 52(b) remand. His published Jones range principle supports protection of both floor and ceiling. He agrees the count exceeds its verdict-authorized maximum but assumes plainness instead of joining the affirmative timing application, preserving his Johnson and Cotton limitation. That difference supplies no objection to the range rule or reconsideration of the defective prejudice rationale. He treats neither evidence as a verdict nor sentencing error as automatically correctable.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Kennedy and Breyer.** They preserve Angle's ordinary findings-based resentencing and would affirm Phifer. McMillan and their Apprendi position distinguish facts defining the statutory offense from rules regulating judicial choice within its authorized range. Making such rules binding does not, in their view, transform each aggravating direction into an element. Witte and Watts support the continuing judicial role. They acknowledge the Guidelines' legal force but reject the majority's classification and overruling of McMillan.

They accept that Phifer's actual drug-count term exceeds its limit but would deny relief at the substantial-rights condition. Under their constitutional premise, the judicially calculated total is lawful and section 5G1.2(d) requires enough consecutive service to reach it. That conclusion rests on an obligatory lawful total, not merely unused cumulative maxima. They reject automatic correction while retaining Olano's separate conditions.

## Procedure After Action

Angle returns for the already required resentencing under a constitutionally established binding range and supported ordinary findings within it. The court of appeals reexamines Phifer's sentence under the remaining Rule 52(b) conditions, using the actual record and lawful range. Any resulting restructuring or further factfinding requires an independent basis in governing procedural and sentencing law. The convictions stand.

## Event

BE&K Construction Co. v. NLRB, No. 01-518. Merits decision, June 24, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 8. Argued April 16, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, 246 F.3d 619, which enforced an unfair-labor-practice order based on BE&K's unsuccessful litigation against unions. The question is whether the Board may impose liability for a losing retaliatory lawsuit even when it is reasonably based. BE&K seeks removal of that liability and its dependent remedies; the Board and unions seek enforcement. The underlying claims were lost or withdrawn. Their allegations of union misconduct are not findings.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Sixth Circuit's enforcement judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded, 9–0.

## Holdings

### I. The Board's unsuccessful-suit and broad retaliation standard is invalid

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 8(a)(1) of the National Labor Relations Act does not authorize liability for a reasonably based but unsuccessful employer lawsuit merely because it sought to stop protected union activity or was accompanied by hostility toward the union. An unsuccessful result and that broad conception of retaliatory motive do not distinguish genuine pursuit of judicial relief from abuse, so an order resting on that standard cannot stand.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this confined proposition. The four limited joiners do not join the broader explanation in Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that losing a lawsuit is different from lacking a reasonable basis to bring it. An employer may genuinely seek a ruling that union conduct is unlawful even though the conduct ultimately proves protected. A desire to stop that conduct, or hostility toward the opponent, does not by itself show that the employer used litigation simply as an instrument of abuse. Bill Johnson's Restaurants protects reasonably based pending suits and permits consideration of a completed suit's result; it does not make defeat virtually sufficient proof of retaliation. Professional Real Estate Investors likewise distinguishes the objective basis for judicial resort from an unfavorable outcome, within its single-suit antitrust setting.

The Board's concern that litigation costs can suppress organizing is substantial. It does not answer the defect in a standard encompassing reasonable attempts to obtain judicial relief. The present standard and dependent enforcement therefore fail. This conclusion does not establish the reasonableness of every underlying claim or find a different abuse theory proved.

### II. The statute receives a limiting construction to avoid serious petitioning concerns

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 8(a)(1)'s general prohibition on interference, restraint and coercion must be construed not to reach the entire class of reasonably based unsuccessful lawsuits brought with the broad retaliatory purpose used here, because that construction would burden substantial genuine petitioning and create serious First Amendment doubt. This statutory holding does not adopt the entire antitrust sham-litigation test as the NLRA's rule or invalidate section 8(a)(1).

**Authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct votes for this explanation and construction. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer withhold assent to this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that reasonable judicial resort retains petitioning value after a loss. It can air disputed facts, develop legal arguments and provide a peaceful means of resolving a genuine grievance. A declaration that the petition itself was unlawful burdens that activity through its legal and reputational consequences. The Board's screening of charges and remedial powers may make its burdens different from private antitrust litigation and treble damages; they do not eliminate the constitutional concern.

DeBartolo supplies the method: where the labor statute's general language admits a construction avoiding serious First Amendment difficulty, the Court adopts that construction. Nothing in section 8(a)(1)'s text requires the Board's whole class of liability. Bill Johnson's discussion of completed suits did not decide the precise reasonable-suit and retaliation question now presented; its protection of pending reasonable suits remains. Professional Real Estate Investors illustrates why objective and subjective genuineness matter, but its antitrust rule does not automatically govern every labor-law category.

The Board's actual standard is broader than a rule confined to an unsuccessful, reasonably based suit that would not have been brought but for a purpose to impose litigation costs regardless of outcome in retaliation for protected activity. Whether a properly confined rule may reach that different category remains open because this order was not based on it. Serial abuse, fraud and independently unlawful objectives likewise require their own rules and supported predicates; their precise standards are outside this decision. Court-imposed litigation sanctions and statutes merely authorizing fees against a losing litigant retain their force.

### III. The enforcement judgment and dependent obligations fall on the rejected basis

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's enforcement judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with Parts I and II; the litigation-based cease-and-desist, notice and reimbursement obligations must be set aside to the extent they depend on the invalid standard. Reconsideration may proceed only on a properly preserved, legally sufficient ground supported by the record, rather than merely relabeling defeat and union hostility as abuse.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this disposition and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that remedies dependent on an invalid liability rule cannot remain in force on that rule. The remand permits the proper court and Board to address a genuinely available alternative under its own legal and factual requirements. It supplies no finding that another violation occurred. The separate general question whether the Board possesses authority to award attorney's fees upon a valid unfair-labor-practice finding lies outside the question granted. The earlier lawsuit's adverse results remain undisturbed, and the decision does not displace independent valid sanctions imposed by courts.

## Precedent Treatment

- Bill Johnson's Restaurants, Inc. v. NLRB, 461 U.S. 731: its protection of reasonably based pending litigation remains; its completed-suit discussion cannot sustain the Board's present standard. The Court's five-Justice explanation treats that discussion as insufficient to resolve the precise reasonable-suit question, while the four limited concurring Justices preserve its broader completed-litigation force.
- Professional Real Estate Investors, Inc. v. Columbia Pictures Industries, Inc., 508 U.S. 49: its distinction between objective baselessness and unsuccessful outcome informs this decision; its single-suit antitrust test is not automatically extended to the NLRA, fraud, serial proceedings or independently unlawful conduct.
- Edward J. DeBartolo Corp. v. Florida Gulf Coast Building & Construction Trades Council, 485 U.S. 568: the five-Justice statutory explanation applies its limiting-construction method to avoid serious First Amendment doubt.

## Law After Decision

An unsuccessful reasonably based employer suit cannot be condemned under section 8(a)(1) merely because it seeks to stop protected union conduct or reflects hostility to a union. The five-Justice statutory construction prevents that whole class of liability without constitutional invalidation or wholesale adoption of the antitrust sham test. Genuinely different abuse theories require their own lawful rule and supported record. Existing court sanctions and fee-shifting statutes remain operative, and the Board's general fee-award authority is not resolved.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Breyer, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.** They join Parts I and III: the actual finding rests too heavily on the unsuccessful suit and antiunion hostility, which do not adequately establish abuse. They retain Bill Johnson's as meaningful authority permitting a properly supported inquiry into completed retaliatory litigation and reject the broader implications of Part II. A reasonable suit brought only to impose process costs regardless of outcome, or as part of a broader course of antiunion conduct, presents a different question; no such violation is found here. Labor law's central concern with employers' use of litigation against organizing, the Board's screening function and its remedial setting distinguish private antitrust actions and treble damages. Those differences preserve room for lawful Board action and prevent wholesale transfer of antitrust immunity. Their agreement does not depend on treating Bill Johnson's completed-suit discussion as mere dicta.

**Justice Scalia, concurring, joined by Thomas.** They join the entire Court opinion and favor applying the objective-baselessness-before-subjective-abuse requirement to an appropriate ordinary single-suit NLRA case, while preserving the existing category limits. Professional Real Estate Investors supplies the proposed gate: a suit must lack an objectively reasonable prospect of success before its subjective use of process as a weapon is examined. An executive agency's motive findings, reviewed for substantial evidence, can threaten access to an Article III court at least as seriously as a later antitrust suit adjudicated by a court. This institutional distinction answers the limited concurrence's reliance on differences between labor and antitrust enforcement. Their proposed extension is not the replacement rule adopted today. Sanctions imposed by courts under their own procedural rules remain distinct.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Sixth Circuit for proceedings consistent with the decision. The litigation-based cease-and-desist, notice and reimbursement obligations must fall to the extent based on the rejected standard. Any reconsideration must rest on an available, preserved and legally sufficient alternative supported by the record.

## Event

Harris v. United States, No. 00-10666. Argued March 25, 2002; decided June 24, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 243 F.3d 806, which sustained the seven-year firearm sentence. The granted question is whether brandishing that raises the mandatory minimum under 18 U.S.C. §924(c)(1)(A) must be charged and proved beyond a reasonable doubt; statutory construction is a necessary antecedent. Harris seeks sentence relief; the United States seeks affirmance.

The indictment charged carrying a firearm during and in relation to a drug transaction, without alleging brandishing. A lawful bench trial established the basic offense. Harris objected at sentencing that brandishing was an uncharged statutory element and added the constitutional argument on appeal. The sentencing judge treated brandishing as a close question, found it by a preponderance and imposed the seven-year minimum, which determined the guideline sentence under §2K2.4. Although the judge considered seven years appropriate, he declined to commit to that term if the enhancement were reversed. The basic conviction and separate evidentiary rulings are outside the granted challenge. Harris and Ring are separately decided matters; neither supplies a prior holding for the other. United States v. Angle was decided June 20, 2002.

## Holdings

### I. The amended statute uses a mandatory sentencing condition

**Holding and operative rule:** Amended §924(c)(1)(A) defines the basic firearm offense and assigns graduated mandatory minimums; brandishing is a sentencing condition under that structure, a classification that does not dispense with constitutional element protections. The opening exception applies to the extent a greater minimum is otherwise provided by §924(c) or another provision of law; brandishing requires displaying all or part of the firearm, or otherwise making its presence known to another person, for the purpose of intimidating that person, whether or not the firearm is directly visible.

**Authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, supplies nine direct votes for this construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's completed offense clause and ensuing directions for how the offender shall be sentenced identify a basic offense followed by penalty conditions. The five-, seven- and ten-year clauses establish the structure; the present claim concerns the seven-year brandishing condition. Jones requires attention to text, structure, tradition and consequence. Its statutory analysis does not make the placement of a provision conclusive. Castillo construed the materially different former firearm-type provision and therefore does not decide this amendment.

Defined intimidating conduct and two additional mandatory years make the aggravated-element reading plausible. The enacted arrangement nevertheless supplies the better statutory account. Avoidance does not license rewriting an ascertainable penalty provision merely to avoid deciding its constitutional operation. The statute itself does not prescribe preponderance factfinding or dispense with charging protection. Applying the independent constitutional requirements thus gives effect to its words without treating the sentencing label as dispositive. The greater-minimum exception remains intact; no dispute concerning its application is presented. The distinct discharge condition is not independently adjudicated on this record.

**Precedent treatment:** Jones supplies the interpretive method and retains its carjacking construction. Castillo is distinguished because its former firearm-type text differs materially; its statutory-element holding remains intact.

### II. Compulsory additional punishment requires element protections

**Holding and operative rule:** A present-offense fact legally necessary to raise a mandatory minimum must receive the charging protections applicable to the prosecution and must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the proper guilt factfinder. A lawful jury waiver permits the judge to determine the element under that same proof standard; it neither enlarges the indictment nor converts an ordinary sentencing finding into the required adjudication.

**Authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for the rule and its federal application.

**Controlling explanation:** Angle already holds that a present-offense fact changing either binding endpoint receives element protections and separately requires applicable federal grand-jury protection. The Court applies those holdings to brandishing. Before that additional finding, the law permits a sentence beginning at five years; afterward, it forbids a sentence below seven. The unchanged outer maximum does not erase that alteration of lawful punishment. Apprendi supplies the actual-authorization principle, and Gaudin and Winship supply the factfinder and proof protections once the aggravating fact is identified.

The Government and the dissent emphasize that seven years could have been selected within the outer range. That argument confuses discretionary selection with a new legal obligation. Judicial findings remain permissible for choosing punishment within a range already authorized; they cannot establish the fact that first changes the binding range. This is an allocation rule, not a policy judgment against mandatory sentences. Angle has already overruled McMillan's contrary unchanged-maximum distinction, and the Court adheres to that treatment. Legislative reliance does not sustain this pending sentence under a displaced rule; final judgments retain their own review doctrines.

Harris's indictment omitted brandishing, and his lawful jury waiver preserved the reasonable-doubt requirement. The contested preponderance finding supplies neither an element admission nor the missing aggravated adjudication. Prior-conviction facts, collateral retroactivity and a general redesign of sentencing are outside the present question; this decision creates no constitutional prior-conviction exception.

**Precedent treatment:** Angle is applied, including its prior overruling of McMillan to the stated extent. ### III. This record satisfies all four plain-error requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming without deciding that Harris forfeited the constitutional theory, Rule 52(b) requires error, clarity at review in this settled-law-then-changed setting, the defendant's ordinary showing of outcome-affecting prejudice, and a separate determination that the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings before discretionary correction is warranted. Harris satisfies each condition on this record; the Court reverses the sentence-sustaining judgment, vacates the firearm sentence and remands for lawful resentencing while preserving the conviction.

**Authority:** Part III of Scalia's opinion has the same six direct joins as Part II, including its affirmative plainness application, independent prejudice finding and discretionary correction.

**Controlling explanation:** Olano separates forfeiture from intentional waiver and preserves the defendant's ordinary prejudice burden. The statutory objection and later constitutional argument need not be treated as identical: even the stricter forfeiture standard yields relief. Applying Angle, compulsory use of the uncharged, lower-standard aggravator is error. Johnson makes it plain now because settled contrary trial law changed during direct review; the Court decides no timing rule for initially unsettled law.

Harris demonstrates outcome-affecting prejudice. He contested brandishing; the judge found it a close question under a preponderance standard; and the resulting minimum supplied the sentence through §2K2.4. The carrying verdict did not decide the distinct intimidation purpose beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge's view that seven years was appropriate is contrary evidence, but his express refusal to select that term independently leaves a reasonable probability of a different lawful sentencing outcome. This is an affirmative inference from the actual record, not presumed injury from lost discretion or a burden shifted to the Government. Possible future departure authority is not an entered alternative judgment.

Correction is independently warranted because genuinely contested conduct, never adjudicated with the required protections, compelled additional imprisonment. That circumstance seriously impairs sentencing fairness and integrity. Substantial-rights injury alone would not suffice, and actual innocence is unnecessary. Johnson's clear-materiality setting and Cotton's overwhelming, essentially uncontroverted threshold record are materially different. Neder's preserved-error inquiry supplies neither automatic reversal nor a hypothetical brandishing verdict; clear-error affirmance cannot supply the missing reasonable-doubt adjudication.

Resentencing begins from the five-year minimum and its applicable guideline consequence. Any different sentence requires an independently lawful basis and reasons. A further aggravated proceeding requires independent charging, waiver, procedural and double-jeopardy authority. Neither an automatic enhancement trial nor a particular replacement term follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Olano's four conditions and ordinary burden are applied; Johnson supplies limited review-time plainness but its refusal of correction is distinguished. Cotton preserves nonjurisdictional treatment and record-specific discretion. Neder retains its distinct preserved-error burden; Vonn permits relevant existing-record consideration without a burden shift.

## Event

Kirk v. Louisiana, No. 01-8419. Summary merits decision, June 24, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 8. Decided on the submitted record without oral argument. ## Holdings

### I. Probable cause alone does not authorize entry into a home

**Holding and operative rule:** Police may not make a nonconsensual entry into a home to arrest or search on probable cause alone: an appropriate warrant or a valid exception must independently authorize entry. When officers rely on exigency, both probable cause and circumstances sufficient to justify immediate entry without a warrant must exist; an arrest and a search incident to it cannot supply the missing authority to cross the threshold.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices. Stone-Zsela's disagreement about the formal disposition does not qualify his join in this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a basis for believing a crime occurred is different from permission to enter a home. Payton protects that threshold even when probable cause would authorize an arrest in public. The Louisiana court treated the drugs' discovery on Kirk's person as sufficient because the officers had probable cause to arrest and could search incident to arrest. That reasoning omits the antecedent question whether they could lawfully enter to make the arrest.

The officers' concern that the departing buyer's arrest would lead to destruction of evidence is an asserted justification requiring assessment on the actual record. It neither establishes exigency by itself nor becomes irrelevant because probable cause existed. McArthur permitted a limited temporary restraint under a combined preservation justification and diligent warrant pursuit; it does not authorize this entry on probable cause alone. Kyllo likewise separates the need for authority at the time of an intrusion from a warrant obtained later. The later warrant cannot retroactively authorize the initial entry.

### II. The omitted exigency and evidence inquiries require remand under the correct rule

**Holding and operative rule:** The Louisiana Court of Appeal's judgment is reversed insofar as it sustained the warrantless entry, arrest and search on probable cause alone, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with Payton. The receiving court must decide the actual exigency issue and address any properly remaining causal, independent-source and evidence consequences under their governing requirements; the conviction's ultimate disposition follows those determinations.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight votes for reversal and remand. Stone-Zsela agrees that the omitted inquiries require remand but would vacate the appellate judgment instead.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the appellate court's categorical legal error requires correction. Its omission of the exigency inquiry does not establish either that an emergency existed or that none existed. The competing record-based arguments must receive the adjudication the incorrect probable-cause rule displaced.

Entry authority and the admissibility of particular evidence are separate questions. Murray preserves use of a genuinely independent later source; a later warrant is not independent merely because it was issued after entry, and independence affecting admissibility does not validate the earlier intrusion. If that ground remains properly available, the court must determine whether the warrant and recovered evidence actually rested on a source independent of the unlawful entry, including whether the entry prompted the decision to seek the warrant and whether information from it affected issuance. Those are ordinary factual and legal applications on remand. The record supplies no final exigency or independence determination for this Court to substitute. The mandate therefore leaves the ultimate suppression and conviction consequences for the receiving court under the corrected rule.

- Kyllo v. United States, 533 U.S. 27: its separation of advance authority from a later warrant is preserved; its technological-search holding is not extended.
- Murray v. United States, 487 U.S. 533: genuine independence remains available under its actual requirements, without a later-warrant cure for the initial intrusion.

## Holdings

### I. The jury must determine the fact that first authorizes death

**Holding and operative rule:** A statutory present-offense aggravating fact necessary to make death legally available must be validly admitted as an element or proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt in a jury-tried prosecution. Walton is overruled insofar as it permits a sentencing judge, sitting without a jury, to supply that necessary aggravating fact; existing lawful jury-waiver requirements remain, and waiver does not lower the reasonable-doubt burden.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes. Breyer's judgment vote rests on a different Eighth Amendment ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that capital eligibility turns on lawful punishment authority. Without an additional statutory aggravator, Arizona law does not permit death on Ring's felony-murder verdict. Finding that aggravator therefore enlarges the punishment the law allows. Apprendi supplies this actual-authorization principle; Angle reinforces the distinction between a binding legal condition and ordinary selection within existing authority. Neither earlier case decided Walton's reserved capital boundary. The Court decides it now.

The State's description of murder as generally punishable by death overlooks the additional statutory prerequisite. A sentencing label cannot do the constitutional work of an element. Winship supplies proof beyond a reasonable doubt, and Gaudin protects the proper factfinder's determination of the legally necessary fact. Judicial expertise or consistency cannot transfer that constitutional responsibility. Capital narrowing identifies the permissible class; it does not require that a judge find the fact establishing membership.

Walton's contrary allocation cannot coexist with this principle. Procedural reliance deserves respect in lawful implementation, but does not justify retaining the inconsistent rule for the fact that first permits death in a pending case. Judicial final selection and review are distinct from eligibility; Harris v. Alabama's adjudicated advisory-weight and override questions do not determine this allocation issue. Nor can appellate reweighing itself supply the missing eligibility verdict.

Ring's granted claim concerns the necessary aggravating fact. Mitigation, ultimate selection and complete weighing procedure are outside that question, as are state grand-jury incorporation, prior-conviction facts and collateral retroactivity. The holding supplies no constitutional prior-conviction exception or new general capital-trial procedure. Neither a valid admission nor a lawful jury waiver supplies the missing adjudication here.

**Precedent treatment:** Apprendi's rule is extended to the expressly reserved capital-eligibility boundary; Angle is applied as supporting range doctrine. Walton's contrary necessary-aggravator allocation is overruled. Winship and Gaudin supply proof and factfinder protections; Harris v. Alabama retains its distinct final-selection holding.

### II. The omitted eligibility finding receives qualified actual-verdict review

**Holding and operative rule:** This omitted statutory eligibility finding is subject to the qualified Neder inquiry: the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the omission did not contribute to the actual verdict, examining the actual instructions, the evidence they directed to the jury, required findings and material conflicts. Necessary equivalent jury findings suffice but are not exclusive; supported whole-record inferences may establish noncontribution only when reliably connected to the actual adjudication, without new credibility findings or substitution of sufficient evidence for a hypothetical verdict.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Thomas, supplying five direct votes for the method and conditional remedy. Scalia and Ginsburg permit only genuine equivalent jury findings and do not join this broader method; neither Breyer nor O'Connor supplies an additional rationale vote.

**Controlling explanation:** Neder applies Chapman's burden to a preserved element omission through examination of the actual verdict. Its method is extended to this omitted statutory eligibility finding because the question remains whether the proper factfinder's actual adjudication makes the omission harmless, not whether an appellate court believes the aggravator probably true. Roy and Yates supply attention to the instructions, required findings and material evidentiary conflicts. Sullivan's invalid reasonable-doubt instruction remains distinct from omission of this particular aggravating element.

Arizona actually argues that the robbery verdict implicitly establishes pecuniary gain. The word robbery does not decide that contention. The reviewing court must compare the particular aggravator under Arizona law with the elements, actual charge and necessary jury findings, then evaluate any qualifying record inference. Strong or uncontested evidence alone is not an automatic cure; it must support noncontribution to the actual adjudication beyond a reasonable doubt. New credibility resolution is forbidden, and appellate reweighing of selection factors cannot substitute for the eligibility inquiry.

The narrower view would permit cure only where the jury necessarily made an equivalent finding. Such findings are sufficient, but Neder's qualified method also permits a reliable inference from the instructions and whole record without converting review into an initial factfinding trial. The burden remains the State's throughout. The argument's actual-record application was not decided below under the correct constitutional rule, so it returns for initial determination there. The Court determines neither harmlessness nor harmfulness here.

If the State fails its showing, the death sentence must be vacated; further sentencing requires independent state-law authority and compliance with constitutional and double-jeopardy limits. If the burden is met, a consistent sentence judgment may be reinstated. A remand for initial harmlessness review does not itself require unconditional sentence vacatur and creates no automatic enhancement trial.

**Precedent treatment:** Neder's qualified actual-verdict method is extended to the omitted eligibility finding; Chapman retains the State's burden. Roy and Yates supply the instruction-centered inquiry. Sullivan's distinct structural rule remains intact. Clemons retains lawful appellate reweighing but supplies no missing eligibility adjudication.

The felony-murder conviction stands; the appellate reversal is distinct from unconditional vacatur of the death sentence.

Respect for state expectations counsels caution about other extensions and lawful implementation; it does not reconcile this judge-only eligibility allocation with Apprendi. He supports the qualified actual-record remand rather than a general jury-selection command.

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment; Ginsburg joins Part II.** In Part I, Scalia supports the eligibility holding despite his continued objection to the capital-narrowing requirements associated with Furman. Once the State makes an aggravating fact legally necessary, its origin does not remove the jury guarantee. Distinguishing factors adopted voluntarily from those responding to constitutional narrowing would not provide a reliable basis for dispensing with that protection. Gaudin and the punishment-authority principle underlying Apprendi and Angle require the fact to receive element safeguards. He preserves the distinction between that finding and the ultimate life-or-death selection, disagreeing with Breyer's independent Eighth Amendment approach. The concern that legislative sentencing labels could diminish the jury's constitutional role reinforces his allocation position; it is not a new factual finding about Ring.

In Part II, joined by Ginsburg, Scalia rejects the Court's broader whole-record inference method. Their Neder position permits harmlessness only through genuine equivalent findings the jury actually made. A court's assessment that the jury probably would have found pecuniary gain cannot supply an unasked element. They join Court Parts I and III and support reversal and remand, leaving the actual charge and findings to be examined, but withhold Court Part II. Ginsburg joins only this error-review limitation, not Scalia's separate account of capital-narrowing doctrine. Neither finds that robbery automatically establishes pecuniary gain or adopts a new appellate credibility determination.

**Breyer, concurring in the judgment.** Breyer continues to reject Apprendi's Sixth Amendment reclassification and joins neither the Court's eligibility rationale nor its broader harmlessness method. He nevertheless concludes that the Eighth Amendment requires a jury to take responsibility for the ultimate decision to impose death. He expressly reconsiders his own join in Harris v. Alabama and adopts the jury-responsibility view expressed by Stevens in that case and Spaziano. Gregg and Furman require special capital safeguards; Witherspoon identifies the jury's role in expressing community judgment, and proportionality requires attention to whether this defendant should suffer death. In Breyer's view, retribution is the principal justification for capital punishment, and a jury better expresses the community's moral judgment about its use in the particular case than a single official, even an elected judge. He treats death differently from ordinary guided sentencing. His ground reaches final selection, not simply an aggravator that makes death available: harmlessness of the omitted eligibility finding alone would not answer his objection to judge-only final selection. He concurs in reversal and remand, but his separate rule commands no Court majority.

**O'Connor, dissenting.** O'Connor would affirm against the allocation claim by retaining Walton and reconsidering Apprendi. She acknowledges that the two rules are incompatible; capital guidance does not harmonize them. Her Apprendi and Angle dissents defend legislative authority to distinguish offense elements from sentencing considerations and the historical judicial role in selecting punishment. She regards the Court's maximum-fact rule as constitutionally unwarranted and its extension as disruptive of settled state procedures. Those objections support her choice of which precedent to reconsider, not a finding that an aggravator is legally unnecessary. She does not join Breyer's Eighth Amendment rule, supply a vote for the Court's broader harmlessness directions, or sustain the sentence by making a substitute pecuniary-gain finding. Ordinary error review and collateral limits remain distinct from her preferred no-error ground.

## Procedure After Action

The Arizona Supreme Court's death-sentence affirmance is reversed, and the matter returns for initial application of the State's burden under the Court's qualified actual-verdict standard. The felony-murder conviction remains intact. If the State establishes harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, a consistent sentence judgment may be reinstated. If it fails, the death sentence must be vacated; any further sentencing proceeding requires an independent basis in state law and compliance with constitutional and double-jeopardy limits. The mandate neither creates a new Arizona procedure nor itself unconditionally vacates the death sentence.

## Event

Trans Union Corp. v. Federal Trade Commission; review of D.C. Circuit No. 00-1141, 245 F.3d 809 and 267 F.3d 1138. Merits decision, June 24, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 8. On review of the denial of relief from the FTC order, including the reported rehearing clarification, on the developed merits record. The question is whether restricting sale of targeted marketing lists compiled from consumer credit records violates the agency's First Amendment rights. Trans Union seeks relief from the restriction; the FTC seeks its enforcement within the FCRA's permissible-use scheme. The lists selected consumers through credit-file attributes and conveyed names and addresses; membership could communicate the selected financial trait. Statutory classification and administrative-law claims outside this First Amendment review are not reopened.

### Statutory setting of the comparison

These are the statute's permissions and duties, not findings that any consumer, reporting agency, recipient or offer complied.

**Consumer direction and financial purposes.** Section 604(a)(2), 15 U.S.C. §1681b(a)(2), permits furnishing in accordance with the consumer's written instructions; silence or failure to opt out is not an instruction. Section 604(c)(1)(A) separately permits consumer authorization for the agency to provide that report to that recipient in a credit or insurance transaction not initiated by the consumer. That clause does not itself require a writing and does not authorize unrelated marketing generally. For the credit and insurance routes, the agency must have reason to believe the recipient intends use for credit extension, account review or collection involving the consumer, or insurance underwriting involving the consumer. Under section 604(a)(3)(F), the agency must have reason to believe that the recipient has a legitimate business need in connection with a consumer-initiated transaction or to review an account for continued compliance with its terms. Section 603(m) excludes a relationship holder's review of the consumer's existing account or insurance policy, or collection of that account, from the unsolicited-transaction definition; it does not create a general new-marketing permission.

**Offers without affirmative authorization.** Section 604(c)(1)(B) permits the unsolicited credit or insurance route only when the transaction consists of a firm offer, the agency has complied with subsection (e), and no effective subsection (e) election excludes the consumer's name and address. Under section 603(l), the offer must be honored if the consumer meets the specific consumer-report criteria used for selection. Further conditions may require application information establishing satisfaction of specific creditworthiness or insurability criteria fixed before selection for deciding whether to extend the offered credit or insurance; verification of continuing satisfaction of selection criteria through a report, application or other relevant creditworthiness or insurability information, or verification of application information to determine satisfaction of the applicable creditworthiness or insurability criteria; and collateral required before selection and disclosed in the offer. These are conditional offers, not guaranteed extensions, and the statute supplies no minimum economic value or favorable-rate requirement.

**Information and use.** Under section 604(c)(2), that route permits only names and addresses, a nonunique identifier used solely to verify identity, and other information that does not identify the consumer's relationship or experience with a particular creditor or other entity. The last category and list membership can disclose a financial trait; the permission is not confined to two address fields. Section 604(c)(3) separately bars furnishing unsolicited-transaction inquiry records except section 609(a)(5)'s disclosure to the consumer, on request with proper identification, of inquiries identifying that consumer during the preceding year. Section 604(f) separately requires an authorized furnishing purpose and a prospective user's general or specific purpose certification under section 607. A permitted transfer does not authorize unrestricted subsequent use.

**Consumer elections and agency duties.** Section 604(e) allows notice through the agency's notification system or its signed election form. Exclusion begins five business days after notice and applies to affiliates. System-only notice lasts two years beginning five business days after notice, unless cancelled sooner; a signed-form election continues until cancellation through the system. The agency must explain the two-year limit after system notice and provide the signed form within five business days if requested during that notice. Its system must include a toll-free telephone number and allow notice with appropriate identification. Publication at least annually in a general-circulation publication for the service area must describe possible file use and give the exclusion address and toll-free number; the original deadline was 365 days after September 30, 1996. A qualifying system and publication maintained for affiliates satisfies those obligations for them; nationwide reporting agencies must maintain the system jointly. These safeguards do not make an election immediately effective, a telephone election perpetual, or general publication proof of individual awareness.

**Separate duties of users.** Section 615(d), 15 U.S.C. §1681m(d), requires each written solicitation based on the firm-offer route to provide a clear and conspicuous statement disclosing use of report information, satisfaction of selection criteria, any applicable selection or further creditworthiness or insurability criteria or collateral conditions that may prevent extension, the right to prohibit unsolicited-transaction use, and how to exercise that right through the system, including its address and toll-free number. Selection, extension and collateral criteria must be retained for three years from the offer. These are separate user duties, not additional express elements of section 604(c)(1)(B); the written-solicitation trigger neither guarantees notice before first disclosure nor covers every oral communication. Federal and state unfair- or deceptive-practice authority remains. Section 615(c)'s distinct liability defense requires proof by a preponderance that reasonable compliance procedures were maintained at the time of the alleged violation; it does not erase those duties or authorize an otherwise impermissible section 604 purpose.

## Holdings

### I. The identified marketing use receives independent speech scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** Restricting sale of these credit-derived lists for commercial targeting requires independent review under the governing commercial-speech standard, even though the agency lawfully possesses the data and can market through other channels. The government must establish a substantial interest, direct material advancement and a reasonable proportional fit; corporate status, payment and lawful possession do not themselves answer those questions or classify every publication as commercial speech.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the sufficient application in Parts I–III; Thomas retains his broader theoretical qualification without withholding an application join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that names and addresses selected through a financial characteristic communicate that characteristic to a buyer. The product here supplies consumer attributes for commercial targeting. Its classification rests on its actual function, not merely the seller's incorporation or profit. U.S. West requires independent speech scrutiny of a marketing-use condition despite lawful custody and alternative channels. Central Hudson and Fox supply the substantial-interest, advancement and proportionality requirements. The Court applies those requirements to this external sale of credit-derived information. Statutory and administrative-law claims outside the granted review, public-concern publication and independently acquired information are not decided.

### II. Preventing unrelated credit-file repurposing materially protects confidentiality

**Holding and operative rule:** Protecting confidential financial information against unrelated commercial repurposing is substantial, and prohibiting the identified nonconsensual sale of credit-derived marketing classifications directly and materially advances that interest. The authorization, financial-purpose and firm-offer permissions, with the actual conditions stated above, do not defeat that conclusion even though permitted prescreening can disclose the same financial trait without affirmative authorization.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies nine direct votes for this confined advancement determination.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this restriction addresses the disclosure constituting the privacy harm itself. A buyer learns the selected characteristic even without receiving an entire credit report. Preventing the unrelated sale therefore protects the information against a further purpose and recipient; no prediction that consumers will refrain from buying products supplies the causal connection.

The firm-offer permission is a real qualification. It can transmit private selection information before individual notice, without affirmative authorization and without guaranteeing completed credit or insurance. The Court therefore does not describe the Act as preventing every unchosen disclosure. Its financial-purpose boundary, conditionally binding offer, information limits, consumer elections and restricted use instead distinguish a constrained financial transaction from unrestricted unrelated targeting. The residual information category is part of that assessment, not treated as harmless.

Discovery Network and Greater New Orleans require this comparison of actual permissions and prohibitions. Their materially inconsistent schemes do not make every partial confidentiality protection invalid. U.S. West recognizes protection against repurposing but does not transfer its different approval mechanism to this statute. The supported credit-source and external-sale setting supplies the direct relationship here; statutory commands supply no finding of actual compliance or universal consumer awareness.

### III. The actual statutory arrangement reasonably fits this application

**Holding and operative rule:** The FCRA restriction on these unrelated credit-derived marketing lists reasonably fits its confidentiality interest when assessed with the separate consumer-direction routes and the defined financial-purpose, conditional-offer, information, election and user-duty limits; the D.C. Circuit's denial of relief on this First Amendment challenge is affirmed. Fox does not require extending a comparable opt-out regime to unrelated marketing merely because it would burden the seller less, and this holding neither declares opt-out generally inadequate nor makes written authorization constitutionally necessary for every information use.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies nine direct votes for the fit application and confined affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the actual distinction is proportionate. Written consumer instructions authorize disclosure at their own scope; recipient-specific authorization separately operates within unsolicited credit or insurance. The alternative financial route combines an offer enforceable when its bounded criteria are met, restricted information and purpose, effective consumer elections, and separate written-solicitation and retained-criteria duties. Those duties have different actors and triggers and are not interchangeable consent exceptions.

Trans Union's proposed extension of opt-out is substantial: the statute itself shows that such a system can protect some privacy interests. Obtaining written instructions for unrelated use imposes a real burden, unlike U.S. West's flexible oral, written or electronic approval. But the proposed extension would expand the purposes and recipients allowed to obtain credit-derived classifications without consumer direction beyond the specifically constrained financial transaction. The credit-source relationship and the external repurposing at issue justify requiring instructions for that expansion. Fox requires reasonable proportionality, not the least burdensome imaginable arrangement.

The comparison includes the actual remaining privacy loss: the initial disclosure can precede individual solicitation notice, elections take five business days, system-only elections expire after two years, and residual information can reveal financial traits. No minimum offer value, actual awareness or universal compliance is assumed. These limits do not erase the financial transaction and information-use boundaries. Lorillard requires examination of the actual burden; Thompson's rejected product-promotion proxy differs from a restriction directly addressing the confidential disclosure itself. Alternative marketing channels mitigate the burden but do not independently validate it. The order remains confined to the challenged lists and does not reach consented uses, independently collected public information or other statutory permissions.

## Event

United States v. Ruiz, No. 01-595. Merits decision, June 24, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 8. Argued April 24, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 241 F.3d 1157, which vacated Ruiz's sentence and remanded for factual and remedial proceedings on the plea-disclosure condition. The questions are whether the Government may condition a fast-track sentencing recommendation on waiver of impeachment and affirmative-defense information, and whether the asserted constitutional injury permits sentence review under 18 U.S.C. §3742.

The proposed agreement retained a continuing promise to disclose information establishing factual innocence but required the identified disclosure waivers. Ruiz rejected the term, the offer was withdrawn and she later pleaded guilty without agreement. The judge understood departure discretion but denied the requested two-level reduction. The United States seeks reversal; Ruiz seeks a lawful sentencing remedy for the lost recommendation. This is a sentence appeal, not enforcement of an accepted bargain or a proved suppression claim seeking plea withdrawal.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Holdings

### I. The asserted legal error must be examined to determine sentence-review authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3742(a)(1) permits a court to determine whether an asserted constitutional condition connected to a sentence establishes that the sentence was imposed in violation of law; a merely discretionary refusal to depart, when the judge understood the discretion, supplies no such appeal. A mistaken belief that departure authority was absent could supply legal error, but that premise is not present here.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court has the direct support of Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela also joins this statutory inquiry. Thomas agrees in his concurrence that the claimed statutory basis can be determined, without joining the Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that disagreement with the sentencing judge's choice differs from a claim that the choice followed an unconstitutional bargaining condition. The judge understood departure authority and declined to exercise it. That excludes the mistaken-authority route, but Ruiz's refusal of the waiver, withdrawal of the offer and request for the associated sentencing treatment connect her legal allegation to the sentence sufficiently to require its examination.

Mine Workers supplies the power to determine jurisdiction. Steel Co.'s insistence on an actual jurisdictional basis is respected: the constitutional question must be resolved to determine the asserted statutory basis, rather than being decided as unrelated merits advice on assumed jurisdiction. This inquiry creates neither general review of discretionary leniency nor a guaranteed bargain. The constitutional answer and its jurisdictional consequence follow in Parts II–IV.

### II. The Constitution does not require advance impeachment disclosure for this plea agreement

**Holding and operative rule:** The Constitution does not require the Government to provide material impeachment information concerning informants or other witnesses before entering the plea agreement presented here. A competent defendant must actually understand the right relinquished, the charge and the constitutionally relevant consequences and act voluntarily; that requirement does not guarantee advance knowledge of every witness weakness or every fact useful to counsel's assessment.

**Authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court has seven direct joins: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Thomas supports the result on his separate trial-function ground; Stone-Zsela dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a constitutional trial-disclosure guarantee does not itself fix the timing of every item useful to bargaining. Brady, Bagley and Kyles protect fair adjudication through favorable evidence, including combined impeachment and preparation uses. Brady v. United States and the received plea cases nevertheless permit a knowing, voluntary decision made with uncertainty about the prosecution's proof. Knowledge of the charge and the constitutional choice differs from complete knowledge of evidentiary consequences.

The contrary position is substantial: known weakness in a decisive witness may materially affect investigation, competent advice and willingness to plead. A duty limited to useful known information would be narrower than open-file discovery. But practical importance does not establish the asserted constitutional timing duty. The usefulness of impeachment varies with the information and the defendant's existing knowledge; an actually informed choice can accept uncertainty about credibility without making that evidence irrelevant.

Legitimate early-resolution and witness-confidentiality interests also weigh against making trial-oriented preparation a constitutional prerequisite to this agreement. These are general considerations, not findings of danger to a witness here. The continuing factual-innocence disclosure promise and ordinary plea safeguards form part of this setting; neither is treated as proof of compliance or an automatic cure. Godinez and Bousley retain their requirements of capacity, actual choice and knowledge of the offense. Vonn preserves the judge's distinct duties and does not establish forfeiture here. The holding rejects this categorical timing claim, not a supported claim of actual deception, ineffective assistance or an uninformed plea.

### III. The separate affirmative-defense term is not categorically invalid in this agreement

**Holding and operative rule:** In the context of the presented agreement, including its continuing promise to disclose information establishing factual innocence, the Constitution does not require advance provision of the identified affirmative-defense information as a prerequisite to the plea bargain. Affirmative-defense information is not equated with impeachment or factual innocence, and the holding does not decide a proved concealed dispositive defense, false representation, factual-innocence suppression, defective advice or an actual uninformed plea.

**Authority:** Part III has the same seven direct Court-opinion joins. Thomas reaches the result under his separate fair-trial account; Stone-Zsela dissents from the constitutional conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the two disclosure categories require separate treatment even though much of the timing analysis is shared. A complete affirmative defense may matter despite admitted conduct and may fall outside the factual-innocence promise. That possibility makes the information important; it does not make a categorical advance-disclosure requirement a necessary condition of the offered bargain.

Informed choice can allocate uncertainty about proof while preserving the requirements of knowledge, voluntary decision and competent advice. The timing demand can also require substantial trial preparation before early resolution. The trial-fairness guarantee does not entail that demand in this agreement's setting. The continuing innocence promise remains a separate term, not a finding that all defense information was furnished or that all defenses establish innocence. The Court rejects the asserted categorical duty on its merits; absence of a particular withheld item is not an alternative rule that an unlawful bargaining condition never causes injury.

### IV. The condition supplies no ground for vacating this sentence

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the identified disclosure condition is not unconstitutional at the scope decided, section 3742(a)(1) supplies no authority on this theory to review the understood discretionary refusal to depart; the Ninth Circuit's sentence vacatur and condition-based remand are reversed. Ruiz's unaccepted offer creates no enforceable recommendation, and her later guilty plea without agreement did not itself assent to the rejected waiver; her conviction remains undisturbed.

**Authority:** Part IV has the seven Court-opinion joins; Thomas supplies the eighth judgment vote on his separate ground. Stone-Zsela would affirm vacatur and a limited sentencing remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the asserted unlawful-condition basis fails because the condition is valid at the decided scope. It does not fail merely because Ruiz lacked an entitlement to the bargaining benefit or had not accepted the proposed contract. An unlawful condition can cause injury without such an entitlement, but that necessary predicate is absent here.

Mezzanatto enforces a knowing, voluntary and limited statement-use agreement; it neither creates a universal power to demand every waiver nor converts this rejected offer into an accepted bargain. The sentence therefore remains operative against this presented challenge, and the Ninth Circuit's remand to remedy the rejected constitutional theory cannot continue. The decision adds no innocence prerequisite or second harmlessness hurdle to a completed constitutional violation and changes no independent sentencing rule.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: applied for authority to determine the statutory jurisdictional basis, not unrestricted appellate review of a discretionary sentence.
- Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment, 523 U.S. 83: its requirement of an actual jurisdictional basis remains; the constitutional issue here determines that claimed basis rather than supplying advisory merits review.
- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83, United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667, and Kyles v. Whitley, April 19, 1995: trial-disclosure protection, collective lawful uses and case-team responsibility retain their force; they do not establish the categorical preagreement duty asserted here.
- Strickler v. Greene, June 17, 1999: its actual suppression, reliance and retrospective materiality inquiries remain distinct from this proposed timing requirement.
- Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742, and Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238: voluntary, informed constitutional choice remains required without a guarantee of complete evidentiary knowledge.
- Godinez v. Moran, June 24, 1993, and Bousley v. United States, May 18, 1998: competence remains distinct from an actually informed choice, and knowledge of the real offense remains necessary.
- United States v. Mezzanatto, January 18, 1995: its limited knowing and voluntary impeachment-use waiver remains operative; it establishes neither assent to Ruiz's rejected term nor a universal advance-waiver rule.
- United States v. Vonn, March 4, 2002: its mandatory plea-warning duty and relevant-whole-record review retain their scope; no Rule 11 forfeiture is inferred in this different constitutional-condition appeal.

## Law After Decision

The Constitution does not impose the asserted advance impeachment-disclosure duty before this plea agreement, and the separate affirmative-defense condition is not categorically invalid in the agreement's context, including its continuing factual-innocence disclosure promise. Actual competence, knowledge of the offense, voluntary choice, counsel duties and distinct deception or suppression claims retain their own requirements. Section 3742 permits examination of the connected constitutional allegation to determine jurisdiction; its failure leaves no review of the understood discretionary refusal on this theory. No universal waiver rule or independent sentencing change results.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** He agrees that the asserted statutory basis may be determined and votes to reverse the sentence vacatur. Brady protects against an unfair trial; that guarantee does not itself create the claimed bargaining-stage disclosure duty, regardless of how useful the information might be to a defendant. He therefore reaches the result for both impeachment and affirmative-defense information without joining the Court's reliance on predicted degree of assistance and practical balancing. His objection is not that impeachment is unimportant, but that usefulness supplies neither the source nor the boundary of this fair-trial guarantee. He also rejects any enforceable promise from the unaccepted offer. His position does not count as a join in Parts II or III or an endorsement of every waiver.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting except as to Part I's statutory inquiry.** He would affirm the sentence vacatur and limited remand because the blanket waiver is invalid to the extent it permits withholding known favorable information materially useful for investigation, counsel's advice or the plea decision. Before obtaining a binding plea, the prosecution must provide such information timely enough for meaningful use, including significant impeachment and affirmative-defense evidence. The obligation reaches case-team information already known after reasonable inquiry, not new evidence the Government must create or complete trial discovery. Genuine privilege and safety concerns call for protective arrangements, sequencing and judicial review compatible with an informed choice.

Credibility weaknesses in a central witness or a supported affirmative defense may change competent advice without conclusively proving factual innocence. The continuing innocence promise therefore does not answer the entire problem. Kyles's preparation and team-responsibility principles support meaningful use; the trial setting does not make the same information irrelevant when the defendant must decide whether to surrender trial. Mezzanatto remains intact because the defendant there knowingly accepted a defined use of his own inconsistent account. A blanket prospective surrender of unknown information needed to assess the very plea choice is different. He rejects neither all limited informed waivers nor bargaining pressure categorically.

The withdrawal of the recommendation after Ruiz refused the condition connects the asserted legal injury to her sentence under section 3742. He would require the sentencing court to address the lost fast-track recommendation on the corrected legal basis and consider lawful relief without treating refusal of the invalid waiver as a permissible reason to deny consideration. The prospective duty and relief from a completed plea or sentence require distinct inquiries; retrospective relief still requires its own causal and materiality showing. He finds no particular suppressed item or involuntary plea and would leave the conviction undisturbed. His remand would not guarantee a two-level reduction, compel a plea agreement, permit plea withdrawal or award damages.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit must implement the reversal of its sentence vacatur and condition-based remedial direction. The presented constitutional theory supplies no further sentencing proceedings. Ruiz's conviction and the district sentence remain undisturbed by this challenge.
