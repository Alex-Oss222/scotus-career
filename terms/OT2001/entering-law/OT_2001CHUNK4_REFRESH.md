## Standards and Tests selection: Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change

### Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change

**Governing question:** What instructions govern medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change?

**Current rule:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii) commands suitable retroactive corrective adjustments when aggregate reimbursement for a provider and period proves inadequate or excessive. The Secretary may construe that command as reconciling interim payments to sums payable under lawful reasonable-cost methods and ceilings, without an individualized exception whenever actual costs exceed the ceiling. Authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods remain. Interpretive change can reduce weight without automatically invalidating a considered permissible construction; text, delegation and reasons must be examined. Unsupported litigation positions neither replace required agency explanation nor gain automatic controlling weight. A changed interpretation is not disqualified merely by statutory age or a litigation setting, but unexplained reversal or disregard of legitimate reliance may be arbitrary and capricious. For antecedent transactions lacking clear prior agency guidance, a court may use the agency’s current authoritative statutory interpretation without backdating the regulation’s effective date.

**Authority by component:**

- Corrective adjustments operate within the authorized cost-method system: Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala — Corrective adjustments operate within the authorized cost-method system, 1993-06-07. White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- A changed agency interpretation is not automatically disqualified: Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala — A changed agency interpretation is not automatically disqualified, 1993-06-07. White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Smiley — Part III-A, permissible explained change, 1996-06-03; all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join.
- Smiley — Part V, qualified antecedent interpretive use, 1996-06-03; all eight Associates; the absence of clear contrary guidance is independently established in Part III-B.

**Present operation effective:** Smiley, June 3, 1996, for qualified antecedent interpretive use; Good Samaritan’s Medicare rule remains unchanged.

**Holdings navigation:** Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, 508 U.S. 402 (1993); Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., 517 U.S. 735.

**Open question:** A later rule displacing clear earlier agency interpretation presents a materially different antecedent-conduct problem left unresolved. No universal authorization of retroactive legislative rulemaking, finding of actual reliance, or reopening of settled judgments follows.

## Standards and Tests selection: Controlling weight for a responsible agency's fair regulatory interpretation

### Controlling weight for a responsible agency's fair regulatory interpretation

**Governing question:** Judicial treatment of a responsible agency's interpretation of its own regulation, including a construction presented in an amicus brief.

**Current rule:** The responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Courts must assess the reading against the operative text, lawful allocation of interpretive responsibility and supported agency treatment. An amicus presentation neither disqualifies the construction nor establishes its correctness. Interpretive authority does not permit amendment of contrary text, unsupported litigation rationalization, automatic authority for an agency lawyer or deference to unproved employer-specific factual assertions.

**Authority by component:**

- Auer v. Robbins — The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds, 1997-02-19; Kennedy Part III, joined by all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela's replacement proposal is noncontrolling

**Present operation effective:** Auer, 1997-02-19, for the amicus and factual-assertion boundaries.

**Related current rules:** Authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary preserves its separate statutory and constitutional limits. Medicare educational costs shifted from a related institution remains the distinct substantive reimbursement rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Auer v. Robbins (1997-02-19) — The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds.

## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation](HOLDINGS.md#trade-court-factfinding-does-not-eliminate-lawful-delegated-interpretation-1). [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).


**Related current rules:** Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation governs independent judicial consideration when controlling force is unavailable.

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

- Required persuasive consideration of tariff-letter reasons: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the Customs-letter application and specified persuasive inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).

## Standards and Tests selection: An agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision

### An agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision

**Governing question:** Whether an agency's decision contains its own sufficient statutory ground when later counsel states that ground in more explicit words.

**Current rule:** A reviewing court may recognize an interpretation necessarily embodied in the agency's own decision even when the agency did not use the exact words later employed by counsel. The actual decision must disclose that operative premise. This permits recognition of the agency's ground, not judicial invention of a new ground, missing findings or a substitute policy; appellate rationalizations cannot sustain a rationale absent from the agency decision.

**Authority by component:**
- Discernible-agency-ground boundary: National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992, holding 2, “The agency's discernible ground”; Kennedy for Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter, applying Chenery while preserving State Farm.

**Present operation effective:** National Railroad Passenger Corp., March 25, 1992.

**Holdings navigation:** National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., 503 U.S. 407 (1992) — Passenger-rail acquisition and agency review.

#### Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, 508 U.S. 402 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2079.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

##### Corrective adjustments operate within the authorized cost-method system

**Holding:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii) requires suitable retroactive corrective adjustments when, for a provider and fiscal period, aggregate reimbursement produced by the methods proves inadequate or excessive; the Secretary permissibly construes that command as reconciliation of interim reimbursement to the amount payable under lawful reasonable-cost methods and limits. The clause does not require an individualized exception whenever actual costs exceed a ceiling, and authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods remain intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: traditional tools followed by permissible construction of remaining ambiguity applied.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204: unauthorized retroactive rulemaking distinguished; its holding does not decide this clause’s meaning.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, March 9, 1992: text/coexistence principle preserved; the dispute concerns what these provisions require, not implied repeal.

##### A changed agency interpretation is not automatically disqualified

**Holding:** An agency’s change of statutory interpretation may reduce the weight accorded its view, but change alone does not make a considered, otherwise permissible construction unlawful. The Court must examine the text, delegated setting and reasons; an unsupported litigation position cannot substitute for the legally required agency explanation or obtain automatic controlling weight.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The hospitals concede no applicable regulatory exemption or exception. Independent challenges to unlawful reimbursement methods remain available outside this review; no automatic actual-cost escape or replacement of Chevron is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm; no unconditional individualized reimbursement above otherwise valid ceilings or particular additional payment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FEC v. Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, 454 U.S. 27: reasonable agency interpretation within the statutory delegation applied.
- INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S. 421: judicial interpretation and the significance of inconsistent agency positions preserved.
- Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204: unsupported litigation rationalizations receive no automatic controlling weight; change itself is a different issue.


## Copied opening holding: state/holdings/administrative-law.md


#### Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., 517 U.S. 735

**Docket or dockets:** 95-860
**Decided:** June 3, 1996

##### Does interpretive change or a rule's development during litigation categorically defeat deference?

**Holding:** A lawful, adequately explained interpretation within delegated statutory ambiguity does not lose eligibility for deference merely because it changes or addresses an old statute during litigation. Unexplained reversal or disregard of legitimate reliance may be arbitrary and capricious; the holding independently rejects the categorical inconsistency objection without approving every change.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-A, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice controlling majority. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment and Parts I, II, IV and VI but does not join Parts III-A, III-B or V. Parts III-A and III-B are independently sufficient answers to the asserted prior-policy barrier; they are not combined into a narrower rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Good Samaritan permits reasoned change within lawful authority while requiring statutory fit, adequate explanation and attention to legitimate reliance. Neither statutory age nor litigation impetus supplies a categorical exclusion. The OCC formally proposed its interpretation on March 3, 1995, received 112 comment letters, and explained its response and grounds in the final rule published February 9, 1996. That process differs from a position invented by litigation counsel. The Court relies on the agency's stated clarification of uncertainty, not a judicially invented economic or reliance finding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Those procedural facts do not certify compliance with every rulemaking statute. Permission to change an interpretation does not itself authorize retrospective alteration of completed conduct's legal consequences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Good Samaritan permits explained interpretive change while retaining authority, statutory-fit and reliance limits. Bowen continues to reject automatic weight for unsupported litigation rationales.

##### Did the earlier letters establish clear official guidance contrary to this rule?

**Holding:** Independently, the identified 1964 and 1988 letters did not establish clear, settled official guidance contrary to the current interpretation; their existence does not itself make the formal rule an unexplained reversal of a binding prior position. This is a second independently sufficient answer to the claimed prior-policy obstacle.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-B, joined by the same seven other Associate Justices; eight-Justice majority. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The 1964 informal automobile-financing statement listed late charges as noninterest items, while the 1988 deputy-counsel response used a home-state-definition approach. Their authority and context differ, and they conflict on whether federal or state law defines the category. They therefore do not establish the clear prior interpretation asserted. The formal rule's explanation that it resolves uncertainty supplies its own reason for a federal definition. Good Samaritan requires attention to agency reasons without treating every informal formulation as binding; Bowen's rejection of unsupported litigation positions does not erase the separately published rule's own explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This conclusion concerns these documents' legal force, not a factual finding about any borrower's actual reliance or a general rule that informal agency materials never matter.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Good Samaritan's reason-giving requirement remains; Bowen's bar against unsupported litigation rationales is distinguished from the independently explained formal interpretation.

##### May a court use the current authoritative interpretation for these antecedent transactions?

**Holding:** In determining statutory meaning for antecedent transactions that lacked clear prior agency guidance, a court may use the current authoritative interpretation without backdating the rule's effective date. The materially different case of a later interpretation displacing clear earlier guidance remains reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part V, joined by the same seven other Associate Justices; eight-Justice majority. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The issue is what section 85 means for these transactions. Part III-B establishes that the cited letters supplied no clear contrary guidance; Part III-A's general allowance for reasoned change would not alone establish retrospective permissibility. Bowen bars unauthorized retroactive legislative rulemaking but does not require a court to disregard current authoritative statutory interpretation in these circumstances. An objection based on displacement of a clear prior interpretation could have substance and is not decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The April 1, 1996 effective date remains unchanged. The rule has no special transition or retroactivity clause; no settled judgment is reopened, no actual-reliance finding is made, and no waiver or preservation ruling resolves the objection. The Court gives no universal permission to alter completed conduct's legal consequences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bowen's prohibition of unauthorized retroactive legislative rulemaking remains; qualified judicial use of current statutory interpretation without clear prior guidance is distinguished.

##### Does merits deference resolve independent section 43 procedure objections?

**Holding:** Deference to the substantive statutory interpretation does not excuse an established procedural violation or adjudicate every independent rule-validity challenge. Section 43's coverage, advance procedure, final-publication duty and differently scoped exceptions remain operative, without a finding of applicability, compliance, violation, preservation or an exception here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part VI, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling qualification.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 43 covers agency opinion letters or interpretive rules, issued on request or on the agency's initiative, concluding that federal law preempts application to a national bank of state community-reinvestment, consumer-protection, fair-lending or intrastate-branch law, and the specified section 36(f)(1)(A)(ii) determinations. Subsection (a) requires advance Federal Register notice describing the issues and each state law, at least thirty days for written comment, and consideration of comments received. Subsection (b) separately requires publication of the specified final action.

Subsection (c)(1) exempts the entire section where essentially identical preemption issues were previously resolved by courts or addressed in an agency opinion or interpretive rule, or where a response concerns a request with no significant legal basis for a preemption determination. Subsection (c)(2) exempts the entire section for materials prepared for judicial proceedings, submission to Congress or a Member, or intragovernmental use. Subsection (c)(3) exempts only advance subsection (a) procedure: it requires a written necessity determination to avoid a serious and imminent threat to a national bank's safety and soundness, or concerns an opinion or rule connected to acquisition of banks in statutory default or danger of default, or an acquisition receiving FDIC assistance under section 1823(c). Final publication remains required under that exception.

Institutional default requires official custodial appointment. Danger of default requires the specified agency advice or determination of inability to meet demands or obligations, or depletion of substantially all capital, with no reasonable prospect of recovery without federal assistance. Borrower delinquency supplies none of these institutional conditions. Published proposals and received comments do not prove every statutory requirement; interpretive form, later use in litigation, section 93a authority and lack of clear prior guidance prove neither exemption nor compliance. Distinguishing substantive classification from its preemptive consequence does not categorically remove section 43.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independently dispositive procedural defect is established; missing documentary proof creates no presumption for either party or alternative disposition. The Court does not decide every paragraph of the rule or every potential procedural challenge.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California judgment is affirmed, 9-0, without agency or evidentiary remand, a special transition rule or newly ordered agency action. The confined section 85 classification and state-law displacement appear under Business and Commercial Law, National-bank interest classification and interstate permission.

#### Christensen v. Harris County, 529 U.S. 576 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1167.
**Decided:** May 1, 2000.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding:** The supported advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the established advisory status and reasoning of this letter, not every informal agency interpretation. No unprovided endorsement, internal delegation or changed policy is assumed.

##### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Auer requires a responsible agency's fair and considered reading that is not plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. This decision imposes no universal rulemaking-format prerequisite and does not decide the Secretary's authority to adopt a valid future rule restricting compulsory use.

**Operative remedy or transition:** These grounds sustain the defense judgment on the categorical FLSA claim without ordering an agreement hearing, agency amendment or new award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's supported advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

For the compensatory-time use holding in Christensen, May 1, 2000, No. 98-1167, see Labor and Employment — Public compensatory time and compulsory paid use.

#### United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1434.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** The revisable, transaction-specific letters issue from many offices and do not govern unrelated importers as regulations do. Their place in protest and judicial review shows administration rather than delegated interpretive lawmaking. The Solicitor General's representation, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, establishes Customs' official position but does not establish congressional delegation to this mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Formal rulemaking and adjudication are evidence of delegation, not exclusive prerequisites. A label or independent judicial factfinding does not alone defeat lawful deference. Later ruling-change procedures do not retroactively govern the 1993 action. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations remain outside the grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

##### Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** Customs' dictionary, commercial-usage and ring-binding reasons must be evaluated. Changed treatment may weaken consistency without eliminating all persuasive force; expertise and official endorsement likewise establish neither persuasion nor the answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Skidmore consideration is independent judgment, not a lesser mandatory deference or fixed weight. No choice between bound diaries and the residual duty-free category is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.

##### The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with consideration of the ruling letter's reasons according to their actual persuasive force under Skidmore. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither definitive classification commands a majority. The return rests on exclusion of permissible agency reasoning, not a missing product fact, a general right to first lower-court interpretation or a need for agency action. The distinct noncontrolling textual and deference grounds for sustaining the category do not combine into a Court construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit must independently classify the planners while considering the actual persuasive force of Customs' reasons. Any assessment or refund depends on that classification and entry calculations; no amount, new hearing, signature search or future rulemaking is required.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia joins the statutory construction and regulatory textual-fit ruling but withholds the Court's no-Chevron ground. Advisory form alone should not exclude authoritative consideration of a responsible agency's construction within a genuine implementing delegation. Even assuming the claimed authority here, however, the separate accrual-agreement and request provisions cannot reasonably bear the letter's additional restriction. His judgment does not depend on finding an unproved endorsement or internal delegation.

Justice Souter joins the Court in full and emphasizes that the decision does not foreclose the Secretary's authority to issue a valid future regulation restricting compelled use. The present statute-and-letter dispute does not decide that different exercise of power.

Justice Stevens, joined by Justices Ginsburg and Breyer, would remand for agreement-specific proceedings. The compensatory-time scheme is a conditioned, consensual substitute for ordinary cash overtime. Prior agreement, protected availability and an express cash-redemption power support requiring an agreement covering compulsory consumption. Cash payment and forced leisure are different transactions; authorization of one does not itself authorize the other. The agency's experienced reading reinforces that construction. The writing does not require negotiation of every optional term, identical class agreements or a new bargaining right. Actual earlier-hire practice and later-hire agreement routes govern the remand, and neither the categorical declaration nor its fee award would automatically revive. Breyer adopts these statutory reasons as persuasive support, without declaring this the only permissible judicial reading.

Justice Breyer, joined by Justice Ginsburg, additionally explains that Skidmore remains independently available. The agency's experience and the statutory reasons supporting consent make this construction persuasive; if authoritative Chevron treatment applies, the construction is reasonable within the actual implementing delegation. These grounds support agreement-specific remand without presuming the letter's full contents or any particular agreement terms.

# Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co. — 2002-02-19

[Authoritative Record](../records/barnhart-v-sigmon-coal-co_merits_2002-02-19.md)

## Event

Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co., No. 00-1307. Argued November 7, 2001; decided February 19, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit's judgment rejecting the challenged Coal Act assignments. The question is whether asset succession to an original signatory operator, without an independently qualifying statutory relationship, permits assignment under the Coal Act.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment against the challenged assignments on the asserted asset-successor theory.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6–3; the 86 assignments cannot stand on the asserted asset-successor theory.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer.

The 86 assignments remain set aside to the extent they rest solely on the rejected direct asset-successor theory. Any independently established statutory relationship or enforceable contractual obligation retains its own force; administration of unassigned beneficiaries remains governed by the Coal Act's lawful mechanisms.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Supports the judgment | Entire opinion; every controlling proposition and its stated limits |
| Dissent | Stevens | O'Connor, Breyer | Opposes the judgment | Entire statutory disagreement and contrary remedy |

## Holdings

### Asset succession alone does not create a new assignment category

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 9701(c)(2) and 9706 do not authorize assigning a signatory operator's beneficiaries to an asset purchaser solely because it directly succeeded to that operator's business. A purchaser independently within an enumerated related-person class remains subject to the statute; the holding does not determine an independent contractual obligation or every contested meaning of successor.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, controls the complete statutory rule, its limits and the affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that financial responsibility must arise from the relationships Congress enacted. Section 9701(c)(2)(A) identifies a member of the signatory's controlled group under section 52(a), including the signatory; a trade or business under common control under section 52(b); and a person with a partnership interest or joint venture with a signatory in a coal-industry business employing eligible beneficiaries, except an interest held solely as a limited partner. The successor sentence refers to those listed classes rather than adding all direct successors of an original signatory. Subsection (c)(2)(B) measures the relationship on July 20, 1992, or immediately before an earlier cessation of business. The assignment priorities in section 9706(a) do not enlarge these classes.

Section 9706(b)(2) separately permits a post-enactment assignment transfer to a successor under its conditions, retaining the transferor and related persons as guarantors and requiring notice to the trustees. That provision does not automatically reach this earlier asset purchase. Russello gives effect to Congress's differing language; Ron Pair requires enforcement of the resulting text. The strongest objection is that reaching a related person's successor while excluding the direct successor creates an anomalous financing gap. But an imperfect allocation of statutory liability is not an absurdity authorizing a new liable-person class. Jericol's continuation of the business and agreement assumption do not establish the claimed statutory relationship. The contextual reading required by Chickasaw and J.E.M. does not replace the specific classes with general successor policy.

**Precedent treatment:** Russello and Ron Pair are applied to the differentiated categories; Chickasaw and J.E.M. retain contextual construction without supplying an omitted category.

### Clear statutory limits resolve this assignment theory

**Holding and operative rule:** The definite assignment classes cannot be enlarged by an administrative preference for preserving program revenue. Because that statutory limit resolves these assignments, the Court does not reach the unnecessary constitutional objections or decide an unestablished independent contractual ground.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Mead distinguishes an agency's interpretive authority from the persuasive force of its reasoning; neither permits liability contrary to clear statutory categories. Eastern Enterprises invalidated particular tier-three assessments without a controlling affirmative constitutional rationale. It did not invalidate the Coal Act generally and does not supply a constitutional shortcut here. The Court's statutory judgment resolves the challenged theory while leaving any genuinely independent obligation to its own legal predicates.

**Precedent treatment:** Mead's statutory boundary is applied; Eastern Enterprises is preserved at the scope of its judgment and independently majority-supported property proposition.

## Precedent Treatment

- Russello v. United States, 464 U.S. 16: different language in related provisions supports the enacted distinction.
- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S. 235: the statutory text governs despite a claimed policy anomaly.
- Chickasaw Nation v. United States, November 27, 2001, and J. E. M. Ag Supply, Inc. v. Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc., December 10, 2001: contextual reading preserves each provision's actual function; neither adds this successor category.
- United States v. Mead Corp., June 18, 2001: interpretive authority and persuasion remain distinct, and neither authorizes departure from clear liability conditions.
- Eastern Enterprises v. Apfel, June 25, 1998: the limited assessment judgment and absence of a controlling affirmative constitutional rationale remain; the Coal Act is not invalidated as a whole.

## Law After Decision

Direct asset succession to an original signatory is insufficient by itself for the challenged statutory assignments. Independently qualifying related persons, the separate prospective assignment-transfer mechanism and enforceable contractual obligations remain governed by their own conditions. This is a statutory coverage rule, not a general immunity for asset purchasers or a constitutional invalidation of the program.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by O'Connor and Breyer, dissenting.** The dissent would reverse and remand for application of an inclusive succession rule. Congress allocated existing health-benefit obligations across continuing business relationships. Reading the successor sentence in that integrated structure avoids the anomaly of reaching a related person's successor while exempting the closer direct successor that continued the coal business and assumed its agreement. The contextual treatment of successors in 1 U.S.C. sections 1 and 5, together with the labor-law succession principles illustrated by Wiley, Golden State Bottling and Fall River Dyeing, supports that reading; those authorities do not establish liability for every solvent purchaser.

The dissent recognizes that a differentiated list and a separate prospective transfer provision could reflect compromise. Its answer is that the entire succession structure supplies the context for that list, and the Court's separation defeats the allocation Congress made. Legislative explanations corroborate that construction rather than replacing the text. The dissent does not treat a bargaining discount as a proved fact, impose unrestricted federal common-law liability, or decide the constitutional question. Preserved successor-status and other application issues remain for the proper tribunal on remand.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit's judgment against these assignments stands. The statutory basis asserted in this review cannot support reassignment; any independent obligation must be established on its own terms.

26 U.S.C. §§9701(c)(1)–(2), 9706(a), (b)(2); Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co., No. 00-1307; the asset purchase, agreement assumption and 86 challenged assignments described in 534 U.S. 438.

# Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer — 2002-02-20

[Authoritative Record](../records/wisconsin-department-of-health-and-family-services-v-blumer_merits_2002-02-20.md)

## Event

Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer, No. 00-952. Argued December 3, 2001; decided February 20, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Wisconsin Court of Appeals, which required a resources-first Medicaid calculation after rejecting the administrative determination and circuit-court judgment; the Wisconsin Supreme Court declined review. The question is whether section 1396r-5 permits considering anticipated support from the institutionalized spouse before increasing the community spouse resource allowance.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the state judgment requiring resources first and remands for proceedings consistent with the permissible income-first construction.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4; the statute permits the income-first method within its stated limits.**

**Supporting:** Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Scalia.

The Wisconsin Court of Appeals' federal-law restriction on the income-first method is reversed. The matter returns for proceedings and any remaining calculation under the permissible method, using supported income and resource figures and the statute's distinct actual-payment requirements.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Supports the judgment | Entire opinion; every controlling proposition and its stated limits |
| Dissent | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia | Opposes the judgment | Entire statutory disagreement and contrary remedy |

## Holdings

### A State may consider prospective support before increasing protected resources

**Holding and operative rule:** At the preeligibility hearing under 42 U.S.C. §1396r-5(e)(2)(C), a State may consider the community spouse's expected posteligibility income, including support that may be transferred under subsection (d)(1)(B), before increasing the community spouse resource allowance. The statute permits income first; it does not require nationwide adoption or independently adjudicate every resources-first program.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, supplies five direct votes for this construction, its statutory qualifications and the reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this hearing anticipates the circumstances in which eligibility and the maintenance allowance operate. Both methods require forecasts. Congress protected a maintenance-income floor while preserving resources for care; it did not guarantee the largest possible resource reserve. Subsection (a)(1) supersedes inconsistent Medicaid provisions, and (a)(3) preserves ordinary methodology except where the section otherwise provides. Neither clause eliminates the need to read the specific provisions together.

Subsection (b)(1) prevents deeming the community spouse's income available to the institutionalized spouse. It does not state the reciprocal prohibition asserted against considering prospective support. Subsection (b)(2) governs posteligibility attribution according to its ownership rules, not the antecedent assessment's exclusive list of potential support. Subsection (c) separately governs resource assessment and later separation. Under (d)(1)(B), a deduction for the community spouse allowance is available only to the extent income is actually made available to or for the benefit of that spouse; transfer remains optional. A forecast neither establishes an actual payment nor commands one.

The contrary reading treats (e)(2)(C)'s resource-increase command as requiring present ownership of every income source considered. But that hearing assesses future maintenance under the statutory arrangement. It may include the contemplated support allowance without deeming ownership or payment already accomplished. Once inadequacy is established under the lawful calculation, the statute requires the resource adjustment. The Court imposes no additional consent, escrow or payment-guarantee prerequisite and makes no finding that a later transfer occurred. Good Samaritan supports considered administration within the statute, not displacement of these specific protections.

**Operative qualifications:** For the separate posteligibility attribution under (b)(2), a governing nontrust instrument may specify the allocation; otherwise income solely payable to one spouse is available to that spouse, jointly payable income is divided equally, and income shared with others follows the spouse's interest or half of the spouses' unspecified joint interest. Trust income follows the governing Medicaid trust provisions and the trust's allocation, or the parallel payment rules when no specific allocation appears. Nontrust income without an ownership instrument is divided equally. The institutionalized spouse may rebut the nontrust payment and no-instrument presumptions by a preponderance of evidence of different ownership. These rules do not turn a forecast into payment. Under (e)(2)(B), if either spouse establishes that exceptional circumstances causing significant financial duress require community-spouse income above the ordinary minimum monthly maintenance needs allowance, an amount sufficient to supply the necessary additional income must replace that allowance. Under (d)(5), when a court orders the institutionalized spouse to provide monthly income for the community spouse's support, the community spouse monthly income allowance must be at least the ordered amount. Those income-allowance adjustments remain distinct from (e)(2)(C)'s resource-increase command; none substitutes another provision's trigger or establishes that its conditions are met here.

**Precedent treatment:** Good Samaritan's permission for considered administration remains bounded by the specific enactment; its holding supplies no universal asset-preservation or administrative-discretion rule.

### Persuasive agency reasoning supports independent statutory construction

**Holding and operative rule:** The agency materials here receive the weight their reasoning earns rather than automatic controlling force. The Court independently concludes that income first is permissible; no new exception to, or abolition of, lawful delegated interpretive authority is adopted.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice Court opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The November 1993 instruction described income first as required. The March 3, 1994 clarification instead permitted income first or another reasonable interpretation pending a final rule. The September 7, 2001 publication remained a proposed rule at this decision. Those different legal forms and dates cannot be collapsed into a binding final mandate. Mead and Christensen distinguish delegated legal force from persuasive reasons, and Skidmore supplies the latter inquiry. The agency's experience and explanation support the coordinated reading the Court independently adopts. Good Samaritan establishes that change alone does not disqualify a considered interpretation; it does not command acceptance of every revised view. The statutory conclusion therefore does not depend on automatically deferring to an amicus submission, guidance or a proposal.

**Precedent treatment:** Mead, Christensen and Skidmore are applied at their actual scope; Good Samaritan's change-of-position rule is preserved, and Chevron remains operative for lawful delegated choices.

## Precedent Treatment

- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: considered administration must fit the enactment, and a change of interpretation alone does not establish invalidity.
- United States v. Mead Corp., June 18, 2001: congressional delegation and exercise of interpretive authority remain prerequisites for controlling statutory force; these materials are considered for persuasion.
- Christensen v. Harris County, May 1, 2000: an advisory interpretation's authority and persuasive quality remain distinct; optional conduct is not converted into a compulsory transfer.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co., 323 U.S. 134: the actual reasoning and experience receive the persuasive weight they earn.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: its role for lawful delegated choices remains; the independent construction here creates no broader revision.

## Law After Decision

States may use the income-first method in the section 1396r-5(e)(2)(C) resource-allowance hearing while respecting ownership attribution, the optional character of transfer, and the actual-availability condition for a posteligibility deduction. A lawful forecast is not an accomplished transfer. Resource inadequacy, exceptional financial duress and court-ordered support retain their separate statutory treatments.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor and Scalia, dissenting.** The dissent would affirm the resources-first requirement and return the matter for a calculation using supported income and resource figures. Section (e)(2)(C) directs an increased resource allowance when the community spouse's protected income is inadequate. Predicting income from resources legally attributable to that spouse differs from predicting another person's optional payment. Section (d)(1)(B)'s posteligibility transfer does not add a prerequisite that the spouse depend on that payment before receiving the resource protection; denying eligibility can prevent the projected arrangement from beginning.

The dissent recognizes that (b)(1) is not a reciprocal nonattribution rule and that (b)(2) expressly addresses posteligibility attribution. Its point is the scheme's separation of ownership, resource protection and voluntary support, not an invented symmetric prohibition. The Court's answer that every method forecasts future circumstances fails to explain why Congress's resource protection may depend first on another person's voluntary transfer. A future payment might not occur or might diminish; those are objections to the legal method, not findings about Irene's later conduct. Administrative flexibility, conservation of funds and expertise cannot supply the missing compulsory transfer or displace the specific statutory command. The agency's changed view is not invalid merely because it changed, but its reasoning must answer this objection. The dissent asserts no constitutional right to preserve assets and would award no unsupported dollar amount.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Wisconsin courts for proceedings consistent with the permitted income-first method. Remaining factual calculations must respect the distinct statutory attribution and actual-payment rules; the decision fixes no eligibility date or benefit amount.

42 U.S.C. §1396r-5(a)–(f); Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer, No. 00-952, 534 U.S. 473; 66 Fed. Reg. 46763–46768 (September 7, 2001), proposed rule. The reviewed method forecasts support; a later actual transfer is not a finding in this judgment.

# New York v. FERC / Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC — 2002-03-04

[Authoritative Record](../records/new-york-v-ferc-enron-power-marketing-inc-v-ferc_merits_2002-03-04.md)

## Event

New York v. FERC, No. 00-568, and Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC, No. 00-809; 535 U.S. 1. Argued October 3, 2001; decided March 4, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 3. On certiorari to the D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining Order 888. New York challenges FERC's authority over unbundled interstate transmission serving retail purchasers. Enron seeks compulsory extension of the open-access requirements to transmission in bundled retail service. Order 888 found discriminatory wholesale transmission access and required functional unbundling: separate generation, transmission and ancillary-service rates, equal tariff terms for a utility's own wholesale transactions and equal use of electronic transmission information. It also regulated existing independently supplied interstate transmission serving retail purchases. For bundled retail service, FERC both characterized the delivered transaction as a state-regulated retail sale and separately determined that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary to remedy the discrimination it found.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The D.C. Circuit's judgment is affirmed in both dockets. The two affirmances rest on distinct grounds.

## Judgment & Remedy

|---|---|---|---|---|

| No. 00-809: compulsory extension to bundled retail service in this proceeding | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Enron's additional compulsory remedy is denied; the three dissenters would vacate this component and remand for a lawful agency explanation |

The judgment sustains the existing order at the reviewed scope without approving a particular tariff, facility classification or retail price. It adopts no permanent exemption for bundled transmission.


## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer throughout; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas in Part I | Affirms both dockets | Part I: unbundled interstate transmission; Part II: sufficient scope of this remedy |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Thomas | Scalia, Kennedy | Joins Part I and affirmance in No. 00-568; would vacate and remand No. 00-809 | Requires a reasoned bundled-transmission determination under the proper jurisdictional understanding; would not order a tariff |

## Holdings

### I. The transmission grant includes unbundled interstate service for retail purchasers

**Holding and operative rule:** The Federal Power Act's separate grant over transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce includes independently supplied interstate transmission serving retail purchases. The wholesale limitation governs the separate sales grant; it does not narrow the transmission grant, and FERC may regulate the covered transmission's rates, terms and conditions without ordering a utility to begin retail wheeling.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court. That direct unanimous coalition controls the category and the following limits.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 201(b)(1) retains state retail-sale authority and, except as the Act specifically provides otherwise, excludes facilities used for generation, local distribution, intrastate-only transmission or transmission of energy consumed wholly by the transmitter. Its preservation of lawful state authority over hydroelectric exports remains. Section 201(b)(2) applies sections 210, 211 and 212 and their enforcement to the entities those provisions cover; compliance with a section 210 or 211 order does not otherwise subject an entity to general FPA jurisdiction. Section 201(f)'s exclusion of federal, state and local governments, their agencies, authorities and instrumentalities, corporations wholly owned directly or indirectly by one or more of them, and their officials acting in duty remains unless a provision specifically refers to them.

Section 212(g) prohibits orders inconsistent with state retail-marketing-area law. Section 212(h) prohibits an order conditioned on, or requiring, direct transmission to an ultimate consumer. Its separate rule for transmission to or for a resale entity permits the covered exception only when both conditions hold: the entity is a federal power marketing agency, TVA, a State or political subdivision or its agency, a corporation or association that has ever received a Rural Electrification Administration loan to provide electric service under the Rural Electrification Act of 1936, a person with a state- or local-law public electric-service obligation arising independently of contract alone, or a corporation or association wholly owned directly or indirectly by one or more of those listed entities; and it either served that consumer on October 24, 1992 or would deliver all the energy through transmission or distribution facilities it owns or controls. State and local authority over direct-to-consumer transmission remains preserved. Section 212(e) makes use of sections 210, 211, 213 or 212 unnecessary as a substitute for other lawful authority and, except as those sections provide, prevents them from limiting other Commission authority; it does not eliminate the retail-wheeling restrictions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a service does not become a retail sale merely because it transmits energy that the customer purchases at retail. The statute grants authority over interstate transmission separately from wholesale interstate sales. Reading the wholesale limitation into both clauses would insert a restriction Congress did not enact. Section 201(a)'s general policy statement must be read with those operative grants and specific exclusions, not made a repeal of them.

New York's strongest objection is that federal regulation of retail-associated transmission shifts an activity traditionally administered by States. The answer lies in the actual service regulated. The order reaches independent interstate transmission already provided voluntarily or under state direction; it does not require new retail wheeling or transfer retail-rate and local-distribution authority wholesale. State siting, local reliability and retail-service functions remain, and no particular facility classification is decided. The connected grid identifies the interstate facts; it creates no power apart from the text.

Iowa Utilities Board supplies the distinction between an enacted federal assignment and independent limits governing its exercise. Gulf Power likewise separates coverage from a particular regulatory method. Mead requires a real delegation before agency interpretation acquires controlling force; the enacted categories resolve this question independently, without a new deference rule. The statutory boundaries thus answer the allocation objection while sustaining the order's limited transmission coverage.

### II. This proceeding did not compel Enron's additional bundled-service remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** FERC could remedy the wholesale transmission-access discrimination established in this proceeding through the adopted open-access requirements while declining compulsory unbundling of bundled retail service on its independently sufficient determination that this additional remedy was unnecessary. Section 206 does require a lawful prospective determination and order after a hearing and finding that a jurisdictional rate, charge, classification or affecting rule, regulation, practice or contract is unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or preferential; the burden to prove that unlawfulness lies on the Commission or complainant, and the agency may not leave established discrimination uncorrected.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has the direct support of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those six votes control. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would remand this component for corrected agency analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that authority, the trigger for a duty to act and the necessary extent of correction are different questions. Sections 205 and 206 preserve lawful rates, nondiscrimination, filed schedules and mandatory correction when the required finding is made. They do not make every proposed structural extension obligatory merely because the Commission may possess authority to consider it.

The agency did characterize bundled delivered energy as a state-regulated retail sale. That ground cannot be erased by calling the order purely discretionary. But the agency also separately determined that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary to eliminate the discrimination it found. The Court sustains that actual, record-linked remedial determination without adopting a categorical jurisdictional exclusion. The functional-unbundling requirements address the established wholesale-access discrimination; Enron's demand does not show why the findings require the additional restructuring in this proceeding. Counsel's claim of inadequate record support is not substituted for the agency's own explanation.

The opposing Justices contend that the jurisdictional characterization undermines the agency's understanding of the corrective duty. That concern would require remand if the refusal depended on legal powerlessness. Here the independent unnecessary-remedy determination adequately supports the refusal of the particular additional remedy sought. Good Samaritan permits considered administration within a mandatory statutory structure but rejects unsupported litigation rationalizations. MCI forbids dispensing with an enacted obligation, and Blumer distinguishes a mandatory trigger from a permissible means; none requires the Court to prescribe Enron's preferred remedy. Gulf Power's demand to preserve applicable classifications and formulas does not remove the actual independent ground present here.

The holding establishes no permanent bundled-service exemption. A universal allocation of bundled-service jurisdiction beyond the authority needed for this order falls outside the particular remedial demand and is not decided. The demand for compulsory additional unbundling in this proceeding is fully resolved.


## Precedent Treatment

- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board: applied to distinguish an affirmative federal statutory assignment from the independent constraints on its exercise and the functions Congress preserves for States.
- National Cable & Telecommunications Assn. v. Gulf Power Co.: applied to separate coverage from the validity of a particular regulatory method; its actual formula and classification limits remain.
- United States v. Mead Corp.: its actual-delegation requirement remains; independent statutory construction resolves the transmission category without a new general deference holding.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.: its prohibition on replacing an enacted mandatory obligation remains. This order's sufficient remedy does not dispense with section 206's triggered duty.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala: applied to considered administration within statutory commands and to the requirement of actual agency reasons; counsel's litigation assertions receive no substitute status.
- Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer: applied to distinguish permission to use a method from the independent circumstances in which the statute commands action; no general exemption from a corrective duty follows.


## Law After Decision

Independent interstate transmission serving retail purchasers falls within the FPA transmission grant, subject to the retained facilities, entity, state-function and retail-wheeling boundaries. Section 206 remains mandatory upon its stated hearing and unlawfulness finding. This order's separate determination that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary sustains its limited remedy; neither the possession of regulatory power nor Enron's preference makes that additional remedy compulsory here. The Court establishes no permanent bundled-transmission exemption or general allocation of bundled-service jurisdiction.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia and Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The three join the unbundled-transmission holding and affirmance in No. 00-568. They would vacate the Enron component and require FERC to reconsider under the proper statutory understanding. Interstate transmission does not cease to be transmission merely because a utility sells it with energy for one charge. Section 206's mandatory corrective duty must be assessed without a categorical jurisdictional exclusion. The agency's coexisting unnecessary-remedy statement is real, but insufficiently explained and independent of its contrary account of jurisdiction to justify the refusal. MCI requires compliance with actual statutory obligations; Gulf Power keeps coverage and an adequately reasoned regulatory choice separate; the reasoning requirements preserved in Mead cannot be satisfied by probable power or counsel's substitute explanation. Existing wholesale and unbundled-retail requirements would remain while the agency supplied the necessary explanation.

The D.C. Circuit's judgment is affirmed in Nos. 00-568 and 00-809. The reviewed open-access requirements continue in force, and the request for compulsory additional unbundling in this proceeding is denied. This merits review concludes without an agency remand. Any later proceeding must rest on its own lawful authority, findings and explanation.

# Edelman v. Lynchburg College — 2002-03-19

[Authoritative Record](../records/edelman-v-lynchburg-college_merits_2002-03-19.md)

## Event

Edelman v. Lynchburg College, No. 00-1072. Argued January 8, 2002; decided March 19, 2002. October Term 2001, chunk 3. On certiorari to the Fourth Circuit. After denial of tenure, Edelman submitted an unverified November 1997 letter within the applicable 300-day period and a verified form on day 313. The Fourth Circuit sustained dismissal because it considered the EEOC's relation-back regulation incompatible with Title VII. Edelman seeks reversal; the College seeks affirmance and preserves the distinct objection that the letter was not a charge. The reviewed question concerns verification timing, not the merits of the discrimination claim.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Fourth Circuit's rule invalidating later verification is reversed. The original letter's status as a charge and the other preserved defenses remain for decision under the lawful relation-back rule.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Reverses and remands | Independent statutory construction sustaining later verification of an otherwise sufficient timely charge; Thomas joins with his stated procedural-authority understanding |
| Concurring opinion | Thomas | No other Justice | Joins the Court and judgment | Express procedural delegation, reasonableness, consistency with Title VII and compliance with the Administrative Procedure Act |
| Opinion concurring in the judgment | O'Connor | Scalia | Reverses and remands | Reasonable delegated procedural choice within statutory ambiguity; does not join the Court's independent best-reading rationale |

## Holdings

### A timely charge may receive later verification

**Holding and operative rule:** An otherwise sufficient Title VII charge filed within the applicable filing period may receive its required verification afterward through 29 C.F.R. section 1601.12(b), with the verification relating back to the timely charge. Verification remains mandatory and must protect the employer before it is obliged to answer the substance; the cure does not extend the filing deadline or turn a document that otherwise is not a charge into one.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer, giving seven direct votes for this independent statutory construction. O'Connor and Scalia support the same operative cure and judgment on their distinct delegated-choice ground; that rationale is not added to the Court's reasoning through vote aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that filing and verification are distinct requirements whose performance need not be simultaneous. Section 706(b) requires a written charge under oath or affirmation. Section 706(e)(1) supplies the filing period. Neither provision defines a charge as necessarily verified at the instant of its first timely submission. Prompt filing addresses staleness; verification assures that the complainant adopts the accusation under oath before the employer must respond on the merits.

The College would treat the verification requirement as part of the filing deadline merely by combining the two provisions. That reading adds a simultaneity command that Congress did not state. The regulation instead preserves both obligations: a timely charge initiates the process, and an actual later oath completes the formal requirement. The process is commonly initiated by laypersons, as Love and Commercial Office Products recognize, but ease of filing does not excuse either enacted obligation.

Section 713(a) expressly authorizes procedural rules. This relation-back rule is procedural, reasonable, consistent with the statute and promulgated in conformity with the Administrative Procedure Act. Becker provides a useful analogy for completing a timely filing through an authorized cure; Title VII and this procedural grant, rather than Becker itself, authorize this cure. The independent reading is sufficient without resolving the full degree of administrative deference.

The November letter preceded the applicable 300-day deadline; the verified form arrived on day 313. Those dates make the verification issue concrete but do not decide whether the letter otherwise constituted a charge. That independently preserved question, the subsequent correspondence and the effect of the agency's failure to give notice within ten days require the lower court's analysis on remand. Agency delay is not itself an adjudicated bar or proof that the initial document failed.

## Precedent Treatment

Becker v. Montgomery is applied by analogy to distinguish completion of an existing timely filing from initiation of a late proceeding; its express cure rule is not transplanted into Title VII as independent authority.

Zipes v. Trans World Airlines, Inc. supplies the distinction between the timely-charge condition and subject-matter jurisdiction, without deciding every processing requirement's classification.

Love v. Pullman Co. and EEOC v. Commercial Office Products Co. inform the lay-initiated administrative process; they do not authorize disregard of the actual filing deadline or oath requirement.

United States v. Mead Corp. and Christensen v. Harris County retain their distinctions between delegated legal force and persuasive interpretation. The Court's independent construction makes a broader deference ruling unnecessary; O'Connor and Scalia rely on the actual procedural delegation for their separate ground.

Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit retains its source-based rejection of invented pleading requirements in its own field. Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A. governs the later federal complaint, not what constitutes a timely administrative charge.

EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. retains the distinction between statutory enforcement authority and the actual prerequisites for relief; it supplies no independent verification-timing rule.

## Law After Decision

An otherwise sufficient timely Title VII charge may be verified after the filing period under the lawful relation-back regulation. Seven Justices adopt the independent statutory explanation; two concur in the judgment on a reasonable delegated-choice rationale. Actual verification, timely initiation and charge sufficiency remain separate requirements. The ruling governs verification cure, leaving the underlying employment-practice accrual question to its own statutory analysis.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring.** He joins the Court on the understanding that section 713(a) expressly delegates procedural rulemaking, this regulation is reasonable, Title VII does not prohibit it and its promulgation complies with the Administrative Procedure Act. All four conditions matter. The rule completes verification of an existing timely charge; it neither waives a statutory deadline nor recognizes an unrestricted equitable power to excuse procedural requirements. His qualification accompanies his full join in the Court's explanation, rather than substituting a judgment-only ground.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, concurring in the judgment.** Reading the provisions together most naturally calls for a verified charge within the filing period. But the statute does not expressly define charge in a way that unambiguously compels that reading. Section 713(a)'s procedural delegation therefore permits the EEOC's reasonable alternative, which preserves both timely initiation and later verified accountability. The rule's binding operation and notice-and-comment repromulgation in 1977 distinguish it from the revisable, nonbinding tariff letters addressed in Mead. Commercial Office Products supports deference on this technical agency procedure. The concurrence also questions how recognition of a possible future reasonable agency change can be reconciled with wholly reserving the deference issue. Neither Justice joins the assertion that the current rule is the best independent statutory reading. Both preserve actual verification, the employer's protection before a required merits response and the separate inquiry into whether the initial letter was a charge.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Fourth Circuit for analysis of whether the original letter was a charge, the preserved significance of later correspondence and the agency's ten-day notice failure, and any remaining defenses under the correct verification rule. Ordinary mandate procedure applies.

The original document's independent charge status and the preserved processing issues were not resolved by the verification ruling.

# Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc. — 2002-03-19

[Authoritative Record](../records/ragsdale-v-wolverine-world-wide-inc_merits_2002-03-19.md)

## Event

Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc., No. 00-6029. Argued January 7, 2002; decided March 19, 2002. October Term 2001, chunk 3. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit's affirmance of summary judgment against Ragsdale's FMLA claim. She seeks an additional statutory allotment because Wolverine did not designate her company medical leave as FMLA leave; Wolverine seeks affirmance. She began employment March 17, 1995 and cancer leave February 21, 1996. The district court treated her as eligible at the March 18 extension. Wolverine provided approximately seven months of leave and discharged her September 20; her further leave or reduced-hours request followed September 26. Her physician released her in December. The reviewed question concerns the automatic consequence of absent designation, separately from the underlying notice duty.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Eighth Circuit's judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Affirmed, 5–4. The judgment rejecting Ragsdale's claimed automatic additional leave stands.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, who would sustain the designation condition and remand for application of the rule and the separate statutory requirements for relief.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas | Affirms | Invalidates the challenged automatic no-count consequence; preserves lawful notice regulation and supported harmful-interference remedies |
| Dissenting opinion | O'Connor | Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Would reverse and remand | Treats timely designation as a lawful condition on counting company leave, while retaining individual liability and remedy predicates |

## Holdings

### Missing designation cannot automatically create a new twelve-week allotment without impairment of protected leave rights

**Holding and operative rule:** The Secretary's implementation authority does not permit former 29 C.F.R. section 825.700(a)'s categorical consequence that undesignated qualifying leave never counts against the FMLA entitlement, thereby creating an additional twelve-week allotment without demonstrated impairment of statutory rights. An employee may obtain relief for a notice-related violation when the applicable duty, record and statutory remedy conditions establish harmful interference; invalidating this automatic consequence does not invalidate every notice duty or make all company leave invariably concurrent with statutory leave.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion is joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia and Thomas. Those five direct joins control the rule, its limits and affirmance.

**Operative entitlement and remedy limits:** The Act grants an eligible employee a total of twelve workweeks in a twelve-month period for qualifying family or medical leave. Greater contractual or company rights remain protected. Section 2617 requires a section 2615 violation affecting the employee. Recoverable lost compensation must result from that violation; absent compensation loss, actual monetary loss must directly result and is capped at twelve weeks' wages or salary. Interest is added to the compensation or monetary-loss amount, and liquidated damages equal that amount plus interest. The court may reduce liquidated liability when the employer proves both good faith and reasonable grounds. Appropriate equitable relief, including employment, reinstatement or promotion, is separately available; monetary loss is not an invariable prerequisite to every equitable remedy. These conditions cannot be replaced by the automatic additional-leave rule.

**Regulatory timing and exceptions:** Former section 825.208 ordinarily required designation within two business days after the employer obtained qualifying information, absent extenuating circumstances. The employee need not name the Act but must give a qualifying reason, and inadequate information can require inquiry. Oral notice requires written confirmation by the next payday, or the following payday if the first is less than a week away. An employer with qualifying information that misses timely designation generally may designate only prospectively. Later-acquired information can permit retrospective counting of the qualifying period. Designation after return is barred except for two circumstances. When the employer first learns the reason upon return, retrospective designation with appropriate notice is permitted within two business days of return. An employee seeking retrospective protection from an unaware employer must also give the reason within two business days of return; without timely notice, the employee may not later assert protection for that absence. When the reason is known but qualification or certification, including a second or third opinion, is pending, the employer should provisionally designate and notify at the start of leave or when the reason becomes known. Confirmation makes the designation final; medical certifications failing to confirm a qualifying reason require withdrawal with written notice. Intermittent or reduced-schedule leave ordinarily requires one notice unless circumstances change. No retrospective exception is established on this record.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that useful notice and a lawful sanction for its omission are different questions. Designation can enable an employee to make informed choices about protected leave. But section 2654's implementing grant does not authorize a consequence that treats qualifying leave already received as legally irrelevant regardless of whether the omission impaired those choices. The statutory total and section 2617's employee-specific conditions must still do their work.

The contrary view calls designation a condition on counting company leave rather than an extra entitlement. That characterization does not answer the rule's operation: after an unexcused omission, it confers a new full allotment even where correct notice could not have changed the employee's use of protected leave. Greater-benefit provisions preserve company promises; they do not compel sequential stacking of every company and statutory benefit. Employer generosity likewise does not excuse independently harmful interference.

The regulation's timing and information safeguards mean that not every first-day omission is incurable. They nevertheless do not cure the categorical consequence once it applies. The decision therefore addresses that consequence without treating its actual exceptions as unavailable in every case.

Mead recognizes genuine delegated rulemaking but preserves statutory limits. MCI likewise bars replacement of an enacted obligation. Mourning permits supported prophylactic implementation, not every sanction that might simplify administration. McKennon and Waffle House distinguish a violation from the remedy the particular record permits. These principles leave room for notice enforcement without this automatic additional entitlement.

Ragsdale received more than twelve weeks of leave after the district court's March 18 eligibility date. Her September 26 requests followed the September 20 discharge; her physician released her in December. The record supplies no demonstrated alternative use of protected leave lost because of absent designation. The Court therefore affirms the judgment on the categorical theory presented rather than creating an unsupported alternative claim. The general validity and precise content of other notice duties fall outside this reviewed consequence.

## Precedent Treatment

MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. is applied to require an implementing rule to preserve the enacted statutory scheme; no general hostility to prophylactic regulation is adopted.

United States v. Mead Corp. and Christensen v. Harris County retain the distinction between delegated binding rules, advisory interpretations and statutory validity. A formal rulemaking grant does not authorize contradiction of the statute.

Mourning v. Family Publications Service, Inc. is distinguished at the scope of its authorized prophylactic implementation; it supplies no unlimited power to alter the substantive entitlement and individual relief conditions.

McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co. and EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. are applied at the distinction between a violation and lawful individual relief. Neither supplies a direct FMLA damages formula or makes monetary loss necessary for every equitable remedy.

New York v. FERC retains its separation of statutory authority, a mandatory corrective trigger and a sufficient actual agency remedy. Its FPA holding neither requires nor validates this different automatic counting consequence.

Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer and National Cable & Telecommunications Assn. v. Gulf Power Co. retain their requirements to respect actual statutory predicates and operative classifications; neither supplies a general regulatory exception to the FMLA's conditions.

Raygor v. Regents of University of Minnesota and Young v. United States retain their statute-specific timing holdings. Neither establishes a universal rule against procedural protection or authorizes enlargement of every statutory benefit.

## Law After Decision

The challenged automatic no-count consequence cannot create an additional twelve-week FMLA entitlement without impairment of the employee's protected rights. Applicable notice obligations and claims for actual harmful interference retain their independent scope, as do greater company rights. The majority's rule invalidates this consequence rather than every designation rule, all prophylactic regulation or every equitable remedy without monetary loss.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** The FMLA does not give an employer an unconditional right to count all qualifying company leave retroactively against the protected allotment. Section 2654 permits the Secretary to require timely designation as a condition on that counting. The rule identifies which leave consumes the statutory right, rather than creating a monetary award for every omission. Greater-benefit provisions and the need for usable notice support keeping undesignated company leave distinct from the statutory allotment.

The rule's safeguards matter. Designation follows qualifying information; the ordinary two-business-day period permits extenuating circumstances. Later knowledge and provisional designation pending certification can permit the specified retrospective operation. The dissent does not treat every first-day failure as irreversible or require all company benefits to run sequentially. Nor does it assume that Ragsdale was eligible when she first stopped work in February; the supported eligibility date is the March extension.

The majority's lack-of-prejudice objection, in the dissent's view, confuses the entitlement's lawful definition with the requirements for an actual award. A predictable designation rule makes leave usable without forcing an employee to reconstruct every hypothetical choice. Mourning supports appropriate prophylactic implementation, and Christensen's distinction between an advisory position and a valid regulation leaves this delegated rule open for independent evaluation. MCI's explicit filing duty and Sigmon Coal's missing liable-person category differ from the absence of an unconditional concurrent-counting command here.

The four would reverse and remand to apply the undiminished entitlement and then determine the actual violation and appropriate relief under section 2617. They do not infer earlier work capacity, a medically available reduced schedule, actual monetary loss or automatic reinstatement from the omitted designation. Ragsdale's postdischarge request and December release retain their actual dates. Ordinary employer and employee notice obligations remain distinct, and no paid-leave finding is supplied.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment against the asserted categorical additional-leave theory stands, and this merits review concludes. Ordinary mandate procedure applies. The decision establishes the stated limit on the regulation without reopening an unsupported alternative claim.

**Decided:** 1994-06-17.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** United States et al. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. et al., No. 93-521, is consolidated with No. 93-356; the same rule invalidation applies to both.

##### The modification power does not permit this replacement of mandatory filing

**Holding:** Under 47 U.S.C. §203(b)(2), the Commission may, in its discretion and for good cause shown, modify tariff requirements in particular instances or by general order applicable to special circumstances or conditions, but may not replace §203(a)'s mandatory filing obligation throughout the nondominant long-distance sector with an optional system or lengthen the statutory notice period beyond 120 days. The challenged permissive-filing rule exceeds that delegation and is invalid; Chevron does not require deference to a construction outside this statutory boundary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg. Five of eight participating Justices directly adopt the structural modification ground, its application and its stated limits; no aggregation of separate rationales is necessary.

**Material application:** The November 1992 rule makes filing optional, not forbidden, for nondominant long-distance carriers; AT&T alone remains classified as dominant in this record. A modification can have consequential effects. Courts must examine the obligation changed, the scope of the modification clause and whether the agency adjusts the obligation's operation or removes its essential operation for the regulated class. This inquiry does not turn on a numerical market-share threshold or automatically invalidate a class-wide adjustment. Changes to filing form, contents, timing or place remain possible under the statutory conditions; timing changes cannot extend the 120-day notice ceiling. The Court does not categorically foreclose deferral or waiver in limited circumstances, and decides no particular proposed waiver. Good cause alone cannot authorize replacement of mandatory filing throughout this sector.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether the challenged order independently fails the special-circumstances-or-conditions requirement; no separate alternative ground commands a majority. It prescribes no tariff rates or replacement program, decides no damages or retrospective nonfiling consequences, resolves no general rule for individual nonenforcement decisions, and adopts no independently sufficient ratification or prohibition theory from §§226 or 332.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837 (1984): applies the inquiry into statutory meaning before deference; no new exception based on economic importance and no replacement of Chevron is adopted.
- National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992: preserves contextual interpretation within an actual delegation and the requirement of a discernible agency ground; the agency's competitive-policy rationale is examined, not replaced by a judicial rationale.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: preserves permissible interpretive change within a statutory system; the defect here is the rule's statutory reach, not the fact that the Commission changed its policy.
- Lechmere, Inc. v. NLRB, January 27, 1992: preserves the principle that agency expertise operates within binding statutory limits; its distinct nonemployee-access rule is not imported into tariff regulation.
- NLRB v. Health Care & Retirement Corp. of America, May 23, 1994: distinguishes a permissible functional construction preserving the statute's operative distinctions from removal of the filing obligation here; its supervisory-status holding remains intact.
- PUD No. 1 of Jefferson County v. Washington Department of Ecology, May 31, 1994: distinguishes broad conditioning authority grounded in that Act's operative compliance language; no general rule against consequential agency action follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The D.C. Circuit's judgment setting aside the permissive-detariffing rule is affirmed in Nos. 93-356 and 93-521. No tariff rate, replacement program, damages or retrospective monetary consequence of earlier nonfiling is decided, and no new direction is given in the distinct complaint proceeding.

**Holding:** Section 201(b) authorizes FCC rules implementing the local-competition obligations added to the Communications Act by §§251–252. **Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Parts I–II are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices control the general-authority and associated remand propositions; O'Connor, Thomas and Breyer dissent on their distinct allocation grounds.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six-Justice coalition controls Part II. The remand does not adopt a new rule resolving the returned substantive objections.

##### III. A network element need not be a detachable physical object

**Holding:** Section 153(29)'s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment, as well as the physical facilities or equipment themselves. Eligibility under that definition does not establish that §251(d)(2) requires unbundled access to every eligible function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Those eight supply a controlling majority; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on the independent definition component.

##### IV. Necessity and impairment must perform independent limiting work

**Holding:** In selecting required network elements under §251(d)(2), the FCC must give independent limiting effect to necessity for proprietary elements and impairment for network elements generally, considering realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent's network. Rule 51.319 is set aside because the adopted comparison and impairment standard did not perform that task.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IV is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, giving the complete rule seven votes. ##### V. The Act imposes no freestanding own-facilities prerequisite

**Holding:** A requesting telecommunications carrier need not own some local facilities merely to qualify to seek unbundled access under §251(c)(3). Its entitlement to particular elements remains governed by §251(d)(2) and the lawful conditions of access and combination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part V is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the independent own-facilities prerequisite, without a ground.

##### VI. Existing combinations need not be dismantled

**Holding:** Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prohibits an incumbent from separating requested network elements already combined in its network unless the requesting carrier asks for separation. Unbundled availability and valuation do not require physical destruction of an existing combination.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Scalia's Parts VI–VII; those parts control the existing-combination and individual-arrangement rules with their stated limits.

##### VII. An entrant may select an individual agreement arrangement with its pertinent conditions

**Holding:** Section 252(i) permits Rule 51.809's access to an individual interconnection, service or network-element arrangement in an approved agreement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions. The rule preserves proof of greater provision costs or technical infeasibility and limits availability to a reasonable period after public filing.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same nine-Justice coalition joins Part VII and controls this rule and its qualifications.

**Holding:** The categorical absence-of-FCC-authority ground for vacating intraLATA toll-dialing-parity rules is rejected, with remaining objections returned for review. **Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part VIII parity-authority ground is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela agrees in the parity disposition without an additional ground; the five-Justice lead rationale independently controls. **Holding:** The partial vacatur of Rule 51.317's technical-feasibility presumption and Order paragraphs 278 and 281 is affirmed because the presumption and its actual exceptions fail to preserve the required necessity and impairment limits. **Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IX is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven control the substantive presumption holding. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the partial invalidation; Souter would remand this component without a final validity ruling.

##### Whether the Waste Act's general responsibility clause independently commands state regulation

**Holding:** Read as a whole, the 1985 Low-Level Radioactive Waste Policy Amendments Act's general state-responsibility language describes the objective implemented through its monetary, access and take-title mechanisms and adds no independently enforceable command to regulate. That plausible construction avoids serious structural problems without contradicting Congress's arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The construction does not erase the express compulsory title and liability terms or authorize judicial rewriting; those terms receive their own constitutional review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to identify the structural problem with a freestanding state-legislative command.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452 (1991): applied; displacement of the usual federal balance requires statutory clarity.
- Edward J. DeBartolo Corp. v. Florida Gulf Coast Building & Construction Trades Council, 485 U.S. 568 (1988): applied; a plausible construction avoiding serious constitutional problems is preferred, without judicial rewriting.

##### Whether the Act's surcharge collection and milestone-conditioned distributions are lawful federal monetary inducements

**Holding:** Congress may authorize the Act's interstate waste surcharges, collect a share for a segregated federal spending program, and condition distributions on explicit, disposal-related milestones. On this record these conditions are authorized inducements; segregation of the funds and state choice whether to qualify do not remove the program from the spending power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** The program serves safe disposal, supplies clear notice of the disposal conduct and deadlines earning distributions, and relates the conditions to that expenditure. No independent constitutional prohibition breached by satisfying those conditions is identified. The federal tax on interstate commerce is not independently challenged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Dole's general-welfare, notice, relatedness, independent-constitutional-limit and noncompulsion requirements remain. No numerical coercion threshold or authority to condition unrelated programs is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the monetary incentives in all three dockets. Lawful surcharge collection and distributions remain operative; the monetary 1993 take-title-or-forfeit alternative is not the compulsory 1996 mechanism severed below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied; general welfare, clear notice, relatedness, independent constitutional limits, and the boundary against compulsion remain in force.
- Wyoming v. Oklahoma, January 22, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished on the decisive express-congressional-authorization ground; unilateral state discrimination remains invalid.
- Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin, 328 U.S. 408 (1946): applied; Congress may authorize otherwise impermissible interstate-commerce burdens.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981): applied as Dole's notice requirement; the milestones unambiguously state what earns a distribution.

##### Whether Congress may authorize higher access charges and exclusion of waste from jurisdictions missing statutory disposal deadlines

**Holding:** Congress may conditionally authorize sited States and regional compacts to increase access charges and exclude waste from jurisdictions missing the Act's deadlines. These provisions do not compel an unsited State to legislate, spend, or administer a federal program because the consequences of its refusal fall on waste generators seeking disposal, and the State remains free to choose its own regulatory response.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** Declining the milestones exposes private waste producers to increased costs and eventual loss of outside disposal access; it does not itself transfer obligations to the State or require an appropriation. Political pressure from affected residents is distinct from a legal command, and New York remains free to regulate in its own manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision supplies no federal disposal fallback, no duty on sited States to accept New York waste and no permission for discrimination beyond Congress's express authorization.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the access incentives in all three dockets. The authorized higher charges and exclusion remain operative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied by analogy to a lawful alternative borne outside the unwilling State government; the actual federal fallback in Hodel is not fabricated here.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): applied in its cooperative-regulation scope; incentives do not establish unlimited power to direct legislation.
- Northeast Bancorp, Inc. v. Board of Governors, 472 U.S. 159 (1985): applied; express congressional authorization removes the otherwise applicable interstate-commerce restraint.
- Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; unauthorized origin-based private-waste exclusion remains impermissible.
- Chemical Waste Management v. Hunt, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; its invalid unauthorized discriminatory surcharge is not approval or disapproval of the express federal choices here.

##### Whether Congress may require a State to regulate private waste or assume ownership, possession and liability, and whether state officials' consent cures the command

**Holding:** Congress may not use its ordinary commerce authority to make an unwilling State choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting their waste into state ownership and possession with associated liability. Combining two unauthorized commands does not create a lawful option, and state officials' advocacy for the statute does not enlarge Congress's powers or estop this noncompacting State from challenging the command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The compulsory 1996 mechanism leaves no lawful way for New York to decline federal program responsibility: the State must arrange disposal under federal instructions or take private generators' waste and assume liability for all damages directly or indirectly incurred through failure to take prompt possession. New York joined no compact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual compact duties, direct regulation of state market activity, and distinct constitutional bases for state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law remain reserved. Garcia's generally applicable regulation of state activities survives; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The compulsory title, possession and associated liability mechanism is declared invalid in each docket; the severability holding below determines the surviving Act.

**Material judgment:** The take-title component is reversed, 6–3: Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support invalidation; White, Blackmun and Stevens would affirm. O'Connor's five-Justice opinion supplies the controlling rationale independently of Stone's concurrence.

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): clarified and extended; its legislative-compulsion boundary applies to mandatory state ownership/liability offered as the alternative to state regulation.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): distinguished and retained; federal-law adjudication and limited consideration in a preemptible field do not authorize an unavoidable duty to run this program.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528 (1985): distinguished, not overruled; generally applicable regulation of state activity is not the issue decided.
- South Carolina v. Baker, 485 U.S. 505 (1988): distinguished and retained; compliance with federal rules governing state activity does not decide compulsory state regulation of private actors.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1 (1976): applied by structural analogy; governmental consent does not enlarge constitutionally assigned powers.
- INS v. Chadha, 462 U.S. 919 (1983): applied by structural analogy; assent to legislation does not cure its departure from the constitutional allocation of authority.
- Testa v. Katt, 330 U.S. 386 (1947): distinguished; the Supremacy Clause's duties for state judges remain intact.
- West Virginia ex rel. Dyer v. Sims, 341 U.S. 22 (1951): distinguished in the asserted compact/estoppel setting; this Court decides no duty under a compact New York actually joined.
- National League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S. 833 (1976): not revived; Garcia's rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains effective.

##### Whether the remaining monetary and access incentives deny New York a republican form of government

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that this Guarantee Clause claim may be heard, the surviving monetary and access incentives do not deny New York a republican form of government because they preserve its legislative choices and officials' accountability to its voters. The Court decides neither general Guarantee Clause justiciability nor a separate Guarantee objection to the already invalidated take-title command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, supplies five votes for the assumption-and-merits rationale. Stone-Zsela separately rejects the claim after finding this limited institutional claim justiciable. White, Blackmun, and Stevens vote to reject the challenge as part of their affirm-all disposition but do not join Part V.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes this claim is justiciable without deciding that issue. Stone's affirmative decision to hear the limited institutional claim has no other join and creates no controlling justiciability rule. No separate Guarantee ground is decided for the already invalidated take-title command.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remaining Guarantee Clause challenge is rejected in all three dockets. The surviving incentives leave New York's legislative choice and elected officials' accountability intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Luther v. Borden, 48 U.S. 1 (1849): left intact in its rival-government recognition setting; no blanket justiciability or nonjusticiability rule is announced here.
- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 (1962): retained; the Court does not resolve the general political-question boundary.
- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied to the continuing choice under conditional spending; no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule is inferred.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to the distinction between an authorized conditional arrangement and compulsory state regulation; it supplies no blanket Guarantee Clause justiciability rule.

##### Whether the invalid compulsory 1996 take-title mechanism is severable from the remainder of the Act

**Holding:** An invalid provision is severed when the remainder remains fully operative and it is not evident that Congress would have rejected that remainder independently. The compulsory 1996 title, possession, and associated liability mechanism is severable here; the remaining financing and access incentives stay in force, without a court-created replacement program or a duty on other States to accept New York's waste.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The remaining financing and access provisions function independently and advance disposal capacity. Absence of a severability clause creates no presumption against severance; the Act's objective is capacity, not the invalid sanction for its own sake.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained 1993 monetary take-title-or-forfeit alternative remains distinct from the invalid compulsory 1996 mechanism. The Court creates no federal substitute or duty on other States to accept waste.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever the compulsory 1996 title, possession and associated liability mechanism and remand all three dockets for corresponding declaratory relief. The remaining financing and access arrangements stay in force; no damages, disposal site or federal administrator is ordered.

**Material judgment:** Severance and remand are supported by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, 6–3; White, Blackmun and Stevens would uphold the entire Act.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock, 480 U.S. 678 (1987): applied without changing the severability standard or treating silence as nonseverability.
- Reagan v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co., 154 U.S. 362 (1894): applied; the failure of an enforcement device need not defeat the independently operative statutory purpose.
- United States v. Jackson, 390 U.S. 570 (1968): applied; an invalid enforcement measure may be severed while preserving an independently functioning scheme.

##### Did Congress authorize the challenged presidential limits on military capital murder punishment?

**Holding:** UCMJ Articles 18 and 56 authorize the President to restrict the availability of the death punishment Congress selected for Article 118(1) and (4); Article 36 supplies additional statutory confirmation and procedural authority. Together they authorize the challenged RCM 1004 restrictions, but not new capital offenses, death under Article 118(2) or (3), or punishment exceeding or contradicting Congress's choice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Holding I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight Justices). Thomas concurs in the judgment only and supplies no join to this or the following three holdings.

**Material application:** Withholding death from part of an already capital class is a punishment limit under Articles 18 and 56 even though it is not a numerical maximum. Congress defined the capital murders and death-or-life punishment; these factors neither add an offense nor enlarge its penalty. Article 36 supports implementing procedures: generally recognized federal criminal-trial principles apply so far as the President considers practicable; regulations may neither contradict nor be inconsistent with the Code, must be uniform insofar as practicable, and must be reported to Congress. Calling a factor procedural does not independently establish substantive authority.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether Article 36 alone would suffice remains undecided. The assignment permits neither creation of civilian crimes nor vague or otherwise unconstitutional capital factors. **Holding:** The enacted punishment-limiting authority suffices without an additional formula expressly naming capital aggravating factors. Article 106a's later express espionage provisions neither withdraw that authority nor make it inapplicable to these murder offenses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Holding II; the same eight Justices directly join.

**Material application:** Articles 18 and 56 expressly assign punishment limitations. **Holding:** Congress may assign the President identification of narrowing factors for military capital sentencing when Congress defines the capital offenses and punishment, supplies intelligible legal boundaries, and confines implementation to the President's assigned military-administration role. Congress's offense selection, death-or-life alternatives, express punishment-limiting assignment and consistency restraints adequately bound this delegation even assuming that criminal and capital delegations require greater specificity than the ordinary standard.

**Holding:** Alcohol-control authority supplies no additional presumption relaxing First Amendment protection for these off-premises truthful-price restrictions. **Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-A, unanimous; this agreement does not attribute balancing to Scalia or Thomas.

**Holding:** The Twenty-first Amendment does not qualify the First Amendment's protection of speech. *LaRue*'s reliance on the Twenty-first Amendment to enhance deference is disavowed; its holding remains supported by ordinary police powers over the regulated conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; six. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor and Breyer do not join this broader rule.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Covered cigarette advertising is expressly preempted

**Holding and operative rule.** Under 15 U.S.C. §1334(b), a State may not impose a smoking-and-health requirement or prohibition with respect to the advertising or promotion of cigarettes whose packages satisfy the federal labeling requirements. Massachusetts's cigarette applications of §21.04(5)(a)–(b) impose such prohibitions even though they regulate advertising location; general zoning unconnected to smoking and health and independent restrictions on sales to minors are not displaced by this holding.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies five direct votes.

**Controlling explanation.** Cipollone requires examination of the actual duty and its basis, rather than the name given a claim or rule. The amended federal clause includes both requirements and prohibitions and expressly identifies advertising and promotion as the regulated activity. These rules select cigarette advertising to reduce tobacco use and its health consequences. A location prohibition remains a prohibition with respect to that advertising; it does not become unrelated to smoking and health because children are its intended beneficiaries.

The contrary reading properly considers the federal warning scheme but gives inadequate effect to the broader enacted words. Congress did not confine this clause to additional warning language. Treating every placement ban as outside the clause would exclude a direct advertising prohibition while reaching a lesser demand to alter its message. Medtronic and Geier require attention to the particular statutory scheme; they establish neither general tobacco immunity nor an impossibility-only preemption rule. The federal package-compliance condition remains necessary. Because preemption gives complete relief on the covered cigarette-advertising component, the Court does not reach its First Amendment alternative. Neither smokeless tobacco nor cigars becomes directly covered by the cigarette statute.

##### II-A. The outdoor preventive showing establishes material advancement at this limited level

**Holding and operative rule.** On the existing record and the protected-speech assumption, the product-specific evidence and supported preventive inferences establish that limiting youth exposure to smokeless-tobacco and cigar promotion can materially advance prevention of underage use. That conclusion does not establish that the particular 1,000-foot burden reasonably fits the benefit or that every product, medium and application is equivalent.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part II-A, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies five direct votes. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join; Stone-Zsela's fit rationale adds no independent vote on this ground.

**Controlling explanation.** Central Hudson requires actual advancement rather than an asserted interest alone. Edenfield requires a responsive evidentiary basis; it does not prescribe one exclusive empirical form. The reports, marketing history and experience concerning these products support more than speculation about the connection between youth exposure and underage use. The record permits preventive inference without a precise causal coefficient or a universal controlled experiment.

The products nevertheless require separate attention. Declining reported smokeless use does not eliminate continuing use or historical targeting. The cigar materials must be assessed on their own terms despite the small outdoor expenditures and the absence of identified Massachusetts cigar billboards. Past-month use is not interchangeable with regular use. The conclusion neither relies on a later evidentiary Lodging nor gives an unauthorized federal rule controlling legal weight. The State's supported preventive showing answers this prong at the stated level; it does not discharge the separate obligation to justify the actual communication burden. Four members of this coalition accordingly seek further tailoring proceedings, while O'Connor concludes that the rule already fails reasonable fit.

##### II-B. The categorical outdoor restrictions lack reasonable fit

**Holding and operative rule.** The challenged 1,000-foot smokeless and cigar advertising prohibitions suppress substantially more protected adult communication than the State has adequately justified in relation to its child-protection objective. Their protected-advertising applications must be reversed for conforming relief, without a new trial to justify this categorical scheme or a judicially prescribed replacement radius.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part II-B is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Kennedy; Thomas expressly joins the failure under the lesser test conditionally while independently demanding stronger protection. Those five adopt this operative failure, not one shared endorsement of the ordinary tier.

**Controlling explanation.** Central Hudson and Fox require proportional fit, not the least conceivable restriction. Edenfield, Rubin and Greater New Orleans require justification responsive to the actual means. Even crediting the State's strongest permissible advancement showing, these rules cover different sizes and settings, brands and prices, oral proposals, enclosed stadiums and indoor information visible or directed outdoors. Their substantial urban retail burden is established without attributing every contested map percentage to this rule alone.

The restriction reaches 1,000 feet from a public playground, the playground area of a public park, or an elementary or secondary school; it does not concern every park or educational institution. It has no adult-only outdoor exception. Its breadth matters because other media do not necessarily replace an immediate on-site offer to adults. The struck cigarette and smokeless generic-sign provision supplies no remaining channel. The separate cigar permission supplies only a small generic sign, not brand or price information. A retailer's limited advertising budget does not diminish the constitutional importance of its principal practical outlet.

Reno confirms that protecting children does not eliminate adult communication interests. Existing enforcement and public education do not prove this additional burden proportionate, but the Constitution requires neither exhaustion of every alternative nor a particular substitute program. The defects are sufficient on this record even accepting advancement. Independent proceedings concerning actual deception or unlawful transactions retain their own predicates; the present protected-speech assumption is not a universal factual finding.

##### III-A. The height rule lacks demonstrated material advancement

**Holding and operative rule.** The five-foot advertising prohibition, which applies indoors only at establishments within 1,000 feet of a public playground, a playground area in a public park, or an elementary or secondary school, does not sufficiently demonstrate material alleviation of youth exposure under the ordinary commercial-speech test, even with that geographic limit and its adult-only exception. That independent deficiency requires rejection of the challenged smokeless and cigar applications; it does not establish zero conceivable effect or demand a height-specific experiment in every case.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part III-A is joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; Thomas conditionally adopts this lesser-test failure. These five expressly support the operative ground. Stone-Zsela does not join this independent explanation.

**Controlling explanation.** Central Hudson and Edenfield require a demonstrated relationship between the actual restraint and the asserted benefit. Evidence that minors encounter low signs does not establish the necessary material alleviation from a rule barring any portion of an advertisement below five feet. Older minors are not confined below that line, and younger children can look upward. Advertising above the line remains possible, while the outside-visibility prohibition separately restricts that channel's practical availability.

The geographical limitation and the exception for premises admitting no person under eighteen at any time, even with an adult, are real limits; they do not supply the missing relationship. The State's general evidence about tobacco advertising does not automatically validate this particular device. Unlike staff-mediated physical acquisition, the rule operates through the message's effect on its viewer. Calling it a sales adjunct cannot substitute for the justification required for that communicative restraint. The conclusion is one of insufficient demonstrated advancement, not a prohibition on supported preventive inference.

##### III-B. The height rule also independently fails reasonable fit

**Holding and operative rule.** The undifferentiated five-foot placement requirement also fails to justify its burden on protected adult advertising in reasonable proportion to the asserted reduction in children's exposure. This separate fit defect supports the same smokeless and cigar advertising reversal, without prescribing a different height or display arrangement.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part III-B is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter; Thomas conditionally adopts the ordinary-test failure. Six support this operative ground.

**Controlling explanation.** Fox requires a reasonable relationship between means and ends even when the State describes the burden as modest. The any-portion height line insufficiently distinguishes exposure of children from adults' practical access to advertising. Neither the importance of reducing youth tobacco use nor general evidence that advertising affects demand explains this particular placement burden. The retained above-height channel, its separate outside-visibility restriction and the actual adult-only exception must be considered together.

The Court does not require one exclusive statistical method. Maps, experience, testimony, the rule's design and practical adult communication can establish or defeat fit. Here the limited justification does not answer the chosen line. A direct message restraint is not sustained merely because it appears beside valid physical-sales regulation. The Court therefore rejects the rule on fit independently of Part III-A's advancement conclusion and leaves different, unpresented display arrangements outside this decision.

##### IV. Staff-mediated access survives the presented speech challenge

**Holding and operative rule.** Assuming that product display and inspection have a cognizable expressive component, the challenged staff-mediated access requirements validly regulate physical acquisition for reasons unrelated to suppressing expression and impose an appropriately limited incidental burden. They survive for Lorillard and Brown & Williamson, USSTC and the cigar petitioners, with the amended exceptions and practical assisted inspection preserved.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Six adopt this assumed-speech conduct ground; Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer concur in the access judgment on their distinct physical-conduct ground.

**Controlling explanation.** The incidental-conduct principles of United States v. O'Brien distinguish regulation of physical acquisition for an expression-independent purpose from suppression of a message. Uncontrolled product access can bypass age checks and facilitate unauthorized taking. Requiring the customer to obtain assistance directly advances the substantial age-verification interest without discounting speech because it is commercial.

The rules retain visible information, informative empty packages, actual products accessible through staff, assisted adult inspection and lawful purchases. For premium cigars, an empty box cannot convey aroma, feel or weight; assisted examination of the actual cigar answers that concern. No prohibition on all pre-purchase inspection is found. The amended adult-only self-service permission and the other applicable access exceptions remain operative. The rule need not eliminate every route by which a minor obtains tobacco to address this route. No finding that most youth tobacco is stolen, that adult-only admission is costless, or that handling goods can never communicate is necessary.

United Foods requires attention to the distinct burden and its independent function. Its compulsory-advertising-finance rule neither converts staff-mediated acquisition into direct message regulation nor supplies a new commercial-speech alternative. The Court decides only the parties' presented access claims; the cigarette challenge supplies no retail-preemption question.

##### V. Sampling and derivative inseverability remain distinct questions

**Holding and operative rule.** The Court declines a separate merits adjudication of the identified cigar-sampling restriction because the complete merits presentation does not develop its distinct constitutional challenge; the lower judgment remains undisturbed subject to the amended adult-only daily exception. USSTC's preserved argument that cigarette preemption requires derivative smokeless relief is also left undecided because neither lower court resolved that state-law consequence and the independent smokeless constitutional ruling supplies relief; any necessary unresolved severability work remains below under ordinary procedure.

**Authority.** O'Connor's Part V is joined by all eight other Justices. These are express limits on adjudication, not affirmative merits rulings.

**Controlling explanation.** The actual burdens must be considered separately. United Foods distinguishes the supported ground presented for decision from a different theory that need not be addressed. A developed challenge to staff-mediated access does not itself establish the merits of a free-distribution claim. Sampling was named; the Court does not invent a failure to preserve it or a default. Its amended exception permits adult-only sampling no more than once per person per day, with the product-specific quantity limits retained. No future waiver is declared.

USSTC's inseverability argument is likewise preserved. Section 21.06 and the separate-product operation inform that argument, but a severability clause does not automatically answer it. The earlier cross-product excision of the distinct small-sign provision decides no broader consequence. The Court can resolve the independently presented smokeless speech challenge without deciding this state-law issue in the first instance. Section 22.08 separately governs the cigar regulations; its existence does not collapse the two schemes. No unresolved question here authorizes reopening the unappealed small-sign or cigar-warning relief.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Cipollone v. Liggett Group: extends its predicate-duty method to the express advertising-location prohibition; preserves its claim-specific statutory conditions rather than creating industry-wide immunity.
- Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr: applies attention to enacted scope and actual regulatory context; does not control the meaning of this different cigarette clause.
- Geier v. American Honda Motor Co.: preserves scheme-specific analysis; no new implied-conflict ground is needed or adopted here.
- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission: applies the distinct advancement and fit inquiries to the protected-speech applications presented; no tobacco-specific tier is created.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox: applies reasonable proportional fit without requiring the least restrictive conceivable measure.
- Edenfield v. Fane: applies the government's burden to show real advancement through evidence responsive to the restriction; no exclusive experimental format is imposed.
- Rubin v. Coors Brewing Co.: applies scrutiny of the restriction's actual relationship to its stated objective.
- 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island: applies protection for lawful adult commercial communication and the requirement of adequate justification, without converting separate stronger theories into a common holding.
- Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association v. United States: applies scrutiny of supported advancement and actual fit; no general right to a second causal or tailoring trial follows.
- Reno v. ACLU: applies the continuing importance of lawful adult access when the State seeks to protect minors.
- United States v. O'Brien: applies expression-independent incidental-conduct scrutiny to physical access; does not supply a conduct exception for the direct advertising rules.
- United States v. United Foods, Inc.: distinguishes burdens and their independent regulatory functions; its compulsory-financing holding remains unchanged.

No precedent is overruled. FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee concerns the distinct contribution mechanism and supplies no substitute test for these direct advertising restraints.

#### Current Law

Covered health-based cigarette advertising-location prohibitions fall within the federal express clause when its federal-labeling condition is met. The challenged noncigarette outdoor rules fail reasonable fit; the height rules fail both demonstrated advancement and fit under the precise majority joins stated above. Supported narrower youth protections, deception restrictions and lawful age limits remain available. Staff-mediated product access survives for the parties and provisions actually challenged. The Court establishes no tobacco-wide immunity, new empirical-proof formula, general sampling rule or complete severability rule.

The advertising provisions cover oral as well as identified written and visual proposals under their actual definitions; utilitarian items not exceeding 200 cubic inches remain excluded from the definition. The separate surviving cigar-sign permission allows, but does not require, one sign of no more than 576 square inches, black text on white, stating only that tobacco products are sold there, without brand or price. It is not an adequate substitute for all protected retail communication and is not struck by this judgment.

The remainder of the sales scheme is not invalidated. The amended subsection (4) exceptions retain adult-only self-service, limited adult-only sampling, mail-order sales without coupon redemption or free samples and with at least a valid identification copy from the recipient, and qualified vending in premises licensed for on-site alcohol consumption. Such vending requires staff-controlled lockout without continuous operation, employee observation and exclusive control, and the required notices. Ordinary face-to-face and identification provisions retain the apparent-age exception at twenty-seven. The premium provision concerns the amended branded-premium restriction, not every gift; the amended cigar unpackaging provision concerns little cigars. Training and compliance rules retain their separate employee-number thresholds, six visits at different days and times and, where practical, to different employees, and the outlet-specific next-year presumption after passing the official check. No challenged-access disposition expands or removes those independent provisions.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Kennedy, joined by Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** They join preemption, the sufficient advertising failures, access and nonreach, but withhold Part II-A. The product-and-medium-specific causal showing remains doubtful and unnecessary to decide because the actual outdoor burden fails fit even on the State's strongest supported account. Edenfield and Rubin require justification of the precise restriction; neither accepting ordinary commercial-speech analysis nor joining a fit conclusion establishes an affirmative finding at every prong. They do not find that advertising never affects youth, deny the existence of evidence or adopt Thomas's entire stronger theory. Their sufficient fit ground supports reversal without another tailoring trial.

**Thomas, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Thomas expressly accepts the identified outdoor-fit and two height failures on the condition that the ordinary test is applied, while refusing to endorse reduced protection for lawful truthful commercial expression. His independent ground demands strict scrutiny for suppression operating through the message's effect on willing adult listeners. His positions in 44 Liquormart, Greater New Orleans and United Foods distinguish failure even under a weaker test from approval of that test. The serious child-protection interest does not permit broad suppression of lawful adult information without the required fit; he can assume the State's strongest supported preventive case and still reject these means. He withholds affirmative outdoor advancement and does not turn contested map attribution or disputed characterizations into new factual findings. He joins expression-independent access scrutiny because it does not discount speech as commercial. His stronger rationale does not become the Court's framework, a sampling holding or a decision about derivative inseverability.

**Stevens, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, and by Souter in the preemption and outdoor-remand portions, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The four read the federal clause in its warning-and-information setting. Congress's concern about diverse cigarette messages and traditional local placement authority support preserving these location rules, despite their health purpose and the broader 1969 wording. Cipollone does not exempt every positive command, but neither its predicate-duty method nor Medtronic and Geier establish an exclusive federal spatial policy. They would reach cigarette speech as well as the noncigarette claims. They accept the supported outdoor advancement proposition but would vacate for trial on fit: disputed incremental coverage, statewide variation, traffic and quality of permissible sites, actual alternative media and differences between retailers and manufacturers prevent final judgment about practical adult channels. USSTC's own-advertising claim belongs in that inquiry. Greater New Orleans involved structural defects stronger evidence could not cure; it does not forbid this application-sensitive trial. They predict no winner and do not reopen unappealed relief.

Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer alone would sustain the height rule as a limited indoor adjunct to valid staff-mediated physical sales and display. They acknowledge doubt if it is treated as a direct speech restraint, and adopt no affirmative ordinary-test advancement or fit finding. They also concur in access affirmance because the requirement regulates physical acquisition, without joining the Court's separate assumed-speech analysis. Assisted inspection preserves the real sensory information important to cigar purchasers. Souter instead joins the Court's independent height failures and assumed-speech access ground. All four accept the distinct sampling and inseverability nonreach. Their alternative cigarette speech positions do not add controlling constitutional grounds to the preemption disposition.

### 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island, No. 94-1140

Merits decision, May 13, 1996

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 1, 1995, in October Term 1995. The Court reviews under 28 U.S.C. §1254(1) the First Circuit's October 24, 1994 judgment, 39 F.3d 5, reversing a constitutional declaration and directing judgment for defendants. The earlier July opinion and judgment were withdrawn and vacated.

Rhode Island General Laws §3-8-7 prohibits manufacturers, wholesalers, out-of-state shippers and covered license holders from advertising prices of malt beverages, cordials, wine or distilled liquor offered for sale in Rhode Island. Its exception permits price signs or tags attached to or placed on merchandise for sale within licensed premises, under departmental rules. Regulation 32 prohibits alcoholic-price references on signs visible from outside Class A premises. The exception does not authorize unrestricted indoor or exterior-visible price advertising.

Section 3-8-8.1 separately prohibits Rhode Island-based newspapers, periodicals, broadcasters and advertising businesses from accepting, publishing or broadcasting in Rhode Island advertisements stating or referring to alcoholic-beverage prices. It excepts trade journals recognized and authorized by the administrator. Violations carry a $50 first-offense fine and fines of at most $100 for additional offenses; publication or broadcast may also be enjoined on a retail licensee's or association's complaint. Section 3-8-8 supplies general enforcement and license-suspension authority but is not independently invalidated. Nonprice promotion remains permitted. Section 3-1-5 declares temperance and reasonable control of alcohol traffic as purposes.

44 Liquormart's 1991 advertisement displayed liquor bottles, prices for nonalcoholic goods and prominent WOW bursts. It paid a $400 administrative fine without appealing that proceeding. Peoples, a Massachusetts retailer, could not place truthful advertisements of lawful Massachusetts liquor prices in Rhode Island media. Both plaintiffs have standing; Peoples' distinct First Amendment claim remains, while its Commerce Clause claim was abandoned on appeal.

After trial, the District Court declared the challenged provisions unconstitutional and found no significant effect of this price-advertising ban on Rhode Island consumption. It did not decide the effects of general alcohol-advertising bans. The First Circuit regarded the economic inference supporting the ban as constitutionally sufficient and invoked an added Twenty-first Amendment presumption. The parties' treatment of the proposed speech as lawful and presumably truthful does not establish the disputed consumption effect.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. This record does not establish material advancement of temperance by the price-information ban

**Controlling proposition:** Even under ordinary Central Hudson review and treating temperance as substantial, Rhode Island has not demonstrated that these truthful-price restrictions directly and materially reduce consumption. This conclusion addresses the actual record and restriction, not every possible effect of advertising, price or demand.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Stevens affirmatively adopts this conditional sufficient ground without accepting consumer ignorance as a legitimate objective or ordinary scrutiny as the complete account.

**Controlling explanation:** The First Amendment protects the lawful, nonmisleading information at issue. Central Hudson requires material advancement, not merely an important interest and a plausible causal possibility. Saffer's theory supplies a real argument: suppressing comparison raises search costs and price dispersion, higher average prices tend to discourage purchases, and opposing experts acknowledged price responsiveness and a theoretically reasonable prediction. Their concessions cannot be omitted.

They nevertheless do not establish the necessary effect of this restriction on this record. The District Court credited contrary testimony and found no significant consumption effect. Saffer's international research concerned general advertising bans, with acknowledged control problems, rather than this price-information rule. The record lacked estimates connecting the projected Rhode Island price change to consumption, and market-share or profit gains could occur without greater total consumption. Smart's acknowledgment of the mechanism accompanied his criticism of its missing data; Pittman's theoretical concession likewise did not erase his contrary conclusion.

The Court separately assesses constitutional sufficiency while respecting the evidentiary character of findings and concessions. It neither decides credibility by counting experts nor treats the First Circuit's economic inference as a new trial finding. Supported prediction remains permissible; no state-specific numerical study, experimental certainty or universal study design is required. Here the whole showing fails the material-advancement requirement. The plaintiffs' expected private profits and the difficulty of measuring advertising effects do not discharge the State's burden.

##### II. The complete price-information restraint independently lacks reasonable fit

**Controlling proposition:** Even assuming material advancement, suppressing the challenged category of truthful retail-price information is disproportionate to the asserted temperance objective on this record. Concrete direct price and tax alternatives expose the inadequately justified fit without becoming mandatory replacements or imposing a least-restrictive-means rule.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven. O'Connor and Breyer assume advancement without deciding it; Souter independently adopts both failures; Stevens's fit application is conditional and independently sufficient.

**Controlling explanation:** Fox requires a reasonable proportional relationship between the speech burden and the regulatory end. Rhode Island seeks to affect the amount purchased through the price consumers pay, yet withholds accurate comparison information continuously. Saffer's own acknowledgment that minimum prices or higher taxes could serve the consumption objective supplies a concrete comparison with measures addressing price directly.

The State need not select the least restrictive conceivable policy or exhaust every alternative. But it must justify this substantial informational burden in light of practical alternatives. Its interstate-shopping objection does not show why its own indirect price-raising mechanism avoids the same leakage or why the blanket speech burden is proportionate. Midcal and 324 Liquor identify defects in particular price arrangements, not the illegality of every state-imposed price measure. The Court neither designs a replacement nor adjudicates an antitrust dispute.

Permitting nonprice promotion and the specific merchandise and trade-journal exceptions does not by itself defeat or establish fit. Price information may have a distinct competitive effect, and incremental regulation is not automatically invalid. The decisive problem remains the unexplained breadth of suppressing accurate prices to reach an objective directly addressed by the identified controls. Calling the price category complete retains those exact exceptions; it does not describe a ban on all alcohol speech.

This ground suffices even taking the predicted consumption effect seriously. It creates no new heightened tier, universal alternatives checklist or all-sources-at-once requirement. Those who separately favor broader protection do not turn this conditional application into assent by the whole coalition to that broader theory.

##### III-A. The Twenty-first Amendment supplies no added presumption validating this restraint

**Controlling proposition:** Rhode Island's alcohol-control authority supplies no additional presumption relaxing First Amendment protection for these off-premises truthful-price prohibitions. Authority over alcohol sales does not itself establish the validity of suppressing accurate information about lawful sales.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III-A, joined by all eight other participating Justices: nine. This common proposition does not attribute Central Hudson balancing to Thomas or either sufficient balancing ground to Scalia.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2 of the Twenty-first Amendment addresses transportation and importation for delivery or use contrary to state law. It creates no express commercial-speech exception. LaRue and Bellanca involved regulation of conduct on licensed premises; those holdings do not decide this restraint on accurate off-premises information. The State's power to regulate the underlying product cannot alone carry its distinct First Amendment burden. Nor are constitutional speech rights surrendered simply by accepting a liquor license. The challenged bans fail without an added alcohol-based presumption; the Justices supporting the broader conclusion in III-B state that separate ground expressly.

##### III-B. The Twenty-first Amendment does not qualify freedom of speech; LaRue's contrary rationale is disavowed

**Controlling proposition:** The Twenty-first Amendment does not qualify the First Amendment's protection of speech. LaRue's reliance on the Twenty-first Amendment to enhance deference is disavowed, while its holding remains supported by ordinary police powers over the conduct at issue.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: six. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor and Breyer do not join this broader treatment.

**Controlling explanation:** Constitutional authority over the traffic in alcohol is not authority to diminish a separate constitutional freedom. Giving a liquor-related restriction an extra speech presumption would permit the subject regulated to displace the protection owed to the means of expression. LaRue's result does not require that step: its licensed-premises conduct regulation can rest on the State's police powers. Removing the Twenty-first Amendment rationale therefore neither reverses that conduct holding nor invalidates Bellanca. The rule concerns the interaction with freedom of speech, not the validity of every direct alcohol restriction.

##### V-B. The completed record supports an implementing remand preserving the constitutional declaration

**Controlling proposition:** Reversal of the operative First Circuit judgment requires implementation of the district court's constitutional declaration against the challenged price-information prohibitions. No new merits trial is required on any adopted sufficient ground. The Court does not issue an additional injunction directive.

**Authority:** Kennedy's Part V-B, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight. Stone-Zsela separately would direct prospective injunctive relief.

**Controlling explanation:** The case was tried and the challenged provisions declared unconstitutional. The appellate reversal rested on legal treatment that cannot sustain these restrictions under the grounds adopted here. An implementing remand preserves the declaration rather than reopening resolved record development. It does not convert the paid fine or unappealed administrative order into a refund dispute, reach the waived Commerce Clause claim or require a substitute alcohol-control program. Stone-Zsela agrees with reversal but regards an express prospective injunction as necessary relief; that additional direction does not command the remedial majority.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Central Hudson and Fox remain operative. The Court's advancement and fit grounds are independently supported at their exact levels, and no majority creates a higher tier or Thomas's categorical rule. Discovery Network, Edenfield, Ibanez, Lanphere and Rubin retain their restriction-specific burdens and limits. Florida Bar invalidates its finite waiting period on its record. Edge retains its narrower, record-specific holding sustaining the border-broadcast restriction, not an automatic vice or federalism exception.

LaRue's Twenty-first Amendment rationale is disavowed by six Justices, with its conduct holding and Bellanca preserved. The broader attack on Posadas's informational paternalism, greater-power syllogism and lawful-vice exception has four votes in IV-C; it is not a wholesale Court overruling. The controlling fit application denies automatic Posadas authorization here under subsequent developed scrutiny.

Queensgate's summary dismissal is a merits disposition. Mandel confines its force to the issues necessarily decided; it does not adopt every explanation below or require that comparable liquor-price restrictions invariably survive later developed advancement-and-fit doctrine. This plenary decision applies that doctrine to the established trial record without deciding every other application of Queensgate or relying on an unestablished factual distinction.

Bigelow reinforces protection of Peoples' lawful information in Stevens's analysis, but no separate interstate-information immunity is adopted. The waived Commerce Clause claim remains outside review.

The challenged restrictions in §§3-8-7, 3-8-8.1 and Regulation 32 cannot constitutionally suppress the truthful price information presented by either plaintiff. Five Justices find inadequate material advancement; seven independently find unreasonable fit. Six adopt the broader Twenty-first Amendment/LaRue treatment, while all nine reject an added presumption for this restraint. Each coalition supplies its own authority.

The decision leaves ordinary Central Hudson/Fox scrutiny in place. Noncontrolling special-care, heightened-review, broader Posadas and categorical anti-ignorance theories do not replace it. No mandatory numerical study, least-restrictive-means standard, universal simultaneous-regulation rule or independent out-of-state advertising immunity follows. False or misleading speech and unlawful promotions retain their separate governing law, and direct alcohol controls are not invalidated. The Court's implementing remand preserves the declaration; the separate requested injunction is not part of its mandate.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Kennedy's noncontrolling Parts IV-A–C.** In IV-A, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, he emphasizes the consumer's interest in accurate commercial information and the special care appropriate when the State suppresses the entire relevant category to influence lawful purchases. That view is distinct from a rule invalidating every such restriction categorically. In IV-B, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg, he would apply more rigorous review to complete truthful-speech suppression unrelated to protecting consumers from deception or comparable injury. It is not announced as universal strict scrutiny for commercial speech. These Justices also adopt the narrower independently sufficient applications in I and II; Stevens's informational objection is not reduced to insufficient evidence alone.

In IV-C, joined by Stevens, Thomas and Ginsburg, Kennedy rejects Posadas's highly deferential permission to choose informational suppression for paternalistic ends, the inference that power to prohibit a product necessarily includes power to suppress truthful promotion, and a freestanding lawful-vice exception. This does not repudiate every Posadas holding. Thomas's join in this treatment does not add a balancing vote, and Souter does not join it. None of these portions commands five votes.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and in the judgment, joined by Breyer.** They join II, III-A and V-A–B. Ordinary Central Hudson/Fox review suffices, with temperance substantial and advancement assumed. Economic prediction and expert concessions deserve genuine weight; they do not decide that the State necessarily fails the advancement prong. The permanent burden on all covered truthful price references nevertheless lacks proportional fit in light of concrete direct controls. Their prior support for supported prophylaxis and finite privacy restrictions does not validate this different burden. They require neither definitive empirical proof nor selection of the least restrictive policy. They distinguish LaRue's licensed-premises conduct and apply subsequent searching doctrine rather than join III-B or IV-C. No new tier, categorical anti-paternalism ground or interstate rule is necessary. Souter joins Part II's independent fit ground, but does not join this separate writing or withdraw his affirmative advancement holding.

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins III-A–B and V-A–B. Existing commercial-speech jurisprudence supports invalidation, but he does not expressly join either the advancement or fit analysis and declines to develop or reinforce the doctrine through a new test here. His doubts about Central Hudson and distaste for governmental paternalism do not themselves supply a replacement constitutional rule. The available historical-practice account is inadequate for that change in this case. He nevertheless joins the broader Twenty-first Amendment treatment and preservation of LaRue's conduct holding, but not the broader Posadas passage. His agreement with the result is not another vote for either sufficient balancing ground.

**Thomas, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins III-A–B, IV-C and V-A–B, and independently regards the asserted interest in keeping lawful purchasers ignorant in order to manipulate their choices as per se illegitimate. Successful informational suppression would not cure that defect. He does not join Central Hudson balancing, an assumption legitimizing that informational objective, a failure-of-proof rationale or the Fox holding. His rule does not decide whether balancing remains appropriate for materially different interests. It reaches Peoples' presented lawful-sale information as well as 44 Liquormart's speech, while leaving unresolved its relationship to Edge when the forum State has prohibited or regulated the underlying domestic transaction. His broader Posadas and Twenty-first Amendment positions do not overrule Edge by implication. His agreement with the implementing remand rests on his categorical ground and does not adopt the Court's balancing analyses.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the constitutional judgment and specified Court portions, and dissenting in part as to remedy.** He joins I, II, III-A and V-A. Ordinary Central Hudson review resolves the case: temperance is substantial, the actual proof does not demonstrate material alleviation, and direct controls reveal inadequate fit. He endorses neither a heightened tier nor a categorical anti-paternalism rule. He does not join the broader LaRue or Posadas treatment. He would expressly enjoin enforcement of the challenged ban against truthful, nonmisleading retail-price advertising, rather than stop at the declaration-preserving remand, and therefore does not join V-B. His injunction would preserve otherwise lawful direct regulation of alcohol sales, prices, taxes, hours and unlawful promotions. False, misleading and transaction-integral advertising remain subject to applicable regulation; his position creates no general immunity for alcohol marketing.

## Holdings

### Manual-task disability requires a substantial limitation of general daily-life function

**Holding and operative rule:** Under original 42 U.S.C. §12102(2)(A), an impairment substantially limits performing manual tasks when it prevents or severely restricts the individual from performing tasks central to most people's daily lives, with permanent or long-term impact. This is an individualized standard requiring considerable functional limitation, not complete incapacity or loss of virtually every manual ability; diagnosis alone is insufficient, and no numerical duration threshold applies.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court's manual-task-standard and application portions, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Eight direct joins establish the rule; Stone-Zsela does not join these portions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that substantial limitation concerns a considerable restriction in the major activity asserted. The ADA's common definition is not confined to employment, so specialized assembly requirements cannot alone define manual-task disability. The original text and contemporaneous guidance concerning nature, severity, expected duration and enduring impact support the stated threshold.

Bragdon requires individual proof and rejects complete inability as an added condition. It does not erase substantiality or prohibit this activity-specific duration requirement. A permanent or long-term functional impact need not mean an incurable diagnosis; the Court sets no numerical minimum. Likewise, identifying central daily-life manual tasks does not restrict every major life activity to something performed each day or displace Bragdon's reproduction holding.

Williams is correct that sustained gripping, reaching and repetition may show important general capacities even if some household tasks remain possible. The standard admits that evidence; it rejects treating a class of demanding occupational tasks as sufficient by category alone. Household and self-care evidence also matters in its actual extent, without creating an automatic defense from the ability to perform some tasks. Ordinary summary-judgment principles govern competing evidence. The Court consults contemporaneous guidance without deciding general binding agency authority.

**Precedent treatment:** Bragdon's individual-proof and no-complete-incapacity principles remain; Sutton and Murphy's distinct occupational-breadth inquiry is not transferred to every activity; Kirkingburg supplies no additional disability method.

### Occupational classification is not a substitute for the claimed activity

**Holding and operative rule:** Inability to perform a class of occupational tasks does not by itself establish substantial limitation in the separate major life activity of performing manual tasks. Workplace evidence remains relevant to the extent it proves limitation of the general manual functions at issue; ordinary household and self-care evidence must likewise be assessed without categorical conclusions.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this severable proposition in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Sutton and Murphy's class-of-jobs inquiry addresses working. A court cannot use that inquiry as a shortcut to establish a different activity. The Sixth Circuit's occupational category therefore does not establish Williams's manual-task disability as a matter of law. The relevant functional evidence must be assessed under the rule governing the particular activity, rather than excluded because it arises at work or treated as dispositive because it concerns a class of job tasks.

**Precedent treatment:** Sutton and Murphy's working rules remain confined to their actual activity; individual functional proof remains necessary.

### Beneficial mitigation does not eliminate the underlying limitation

**Holding and operative rule:** The manual-task inquiry assesses the individual's underlying substantial limitation without treating beneficial correction or medication as eliminating it. Individual proof remains required, and coverage remains distinct from qualification, accommodation and safety; the claimant need not stop treatment.

**Authority:** All nine Justices directly join this severable application of Sutton and Murphy in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Sutton and Murphy settle the mitigation baseline under the original ADA. Their rule identifies the functional condition assessed; it does not automatically establish its substantiality. The manual-task threshold applies to that legally defined underlying condition. Successful management does not remove protection by itself, while evidence still must establish this individual's substantial limitation. The Court therefore rejects neither the governing mitigation rule nor the independent need to prove disability.

**Precedent treatment:** Sutton and Murphy are applied unchanged; their underlying-impairment rule controls the remand.

## Precedent Treatment

- Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc., No. 97-1943, and Murphy v. United Parcel Service, Inc., No. 97-1992: the underlying-impairment rule is applied unchanged; beneficial mitigation does not defeat coverage, and the working-specific class inquiry does not define manual tasks.
- Bragdon v. Abbott, 524 U.S. 624: individualized substantiality and the distinction from complete inability remain; its reproduction and direct-threat holdings are intact.
- Albertson's, Inc. v. Kirkingburg, No. 98-591: its distinct safety-qualification rule remains; it supplies no manual-task or mitigation methodology.

## Law After Decision

The original ADA now requires the stated severe restriction in central daily-life manual tasks and permanent or long-term impact, assessed individually under the existing underlying-impairment mitigation rule. Occupational categories alone do not establish disability; workplace and nonwork functional evidence remain relevant. The lower courts apply those settled rules to the reviewed partial judgment, with other claims retaining their existing procedural status.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the occupational-shortcut and mitigation propositions and in setting aside the partial judgment, but rejects a categorical severe-and-permanent threshold. He would assess individualized substantial limitation of important manual functioning through breadth, frequency, duration and practical effect. These are relevant considerations, not separate mandatory elements. Important work-related functions cannot be excluded because they occur at work, and retained household abilities do not automatically defeat coverage. Expected duration informs substantiality without making permanence indispensable. Sutton and Murphy preserve the underlying-impairment inquiry; the difference between working and manual tasks requires correction of the appellate shortcut, not a restriction to only the most pervasive daily-life limitations. His proposed standard still requires proof rather than diagnosis alone, preserves distinct qualification and accommodation issues, and would not grant Toyota automatic judgment. That broader standard is noncontrolling.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Sixth Circuit for reconsideration of Williams's partial judgment under the controlling standard and ordinary procedural rules. The limited grant does not reopen an independently unreviewed judgment or forfeited coverage branch. Qualification, accommodation, safety and other preserved matters retain their separate elements and existing procedural status.

## Source Notes

Original 42 U.S.C. §12102(2)(A); contemporaneous 29 C.F.R. §1630.2(j); Toyota Motor Manufacturing, Kentucky, Inc. v. Williams, No. 00-1089, 534 U.S. 184, for the functional record and scope of review.

# EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. — 2002-01-15

[Authoritative Record](../records/eeoc-v-waffle-house-inc_merits_2002-01-15.md)

## Event

EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc., No. 99-1823; 534 U.S. 279. Merits decision, January 15, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 2. Argued October 10, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit. The question is whether Baker's employment arbitration agreement prevents the EEOC from seeking individual relief in its own ADA action. Baker experienced a seizure, was discharged and filed a charge; disability discrimination remains alleged. The Fourth Circuit allowed prospective public relief but barred victim-specific remedies. The agency seeks reversal of that restriction.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The restriction on victim-specific relief is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Restriction on victim-specific relief reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would vacate the FAA-based restriction and remand for consideration of any properly available independent state-law contract ground.

All nine reject FAA coverage and agree that actual recovery remains subject to statutory predicates and prevention of duplicate satisfaction. The Commission's public enforcement action continues; the majority permits pursuit of individual remedies without the dissenters' preliminary contract-enforceability inquiry.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer throughout; Scalia and Thomas as to Parts I and III only | Reverses the categorical relief restriction and remands | Part I: FAA exclusion; Part II: independent agency remedies; Part III: ordinary relief conditions; Part IV: reversal without contract prerequisite |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Thomas | Scalia | Joins rejection of FAA premise and ordinary relief conditions; would vacate and return the additional contract issue | Parts I and III joined; Parts II and IV rejected on derivative-remedy ground |

## Holdings

### Part I. The FAA supplies no enforcement ground for this employment agreement

**Holding and operative rule:** Circuit City's section 1 employment-contract exclusion covers Baker's employment arbitration agreement, so the FAA supplies no enforcement command or federal arbitration-policy ground for restricting this EEOC action or its remedies. The exclusion does not itself determine an independent state-law basis for enforcement between Baker and Waffle House.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins control this ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the governing employment exclusion resolves the antecedent FAA question. Circuit City excludes employment contracts within the commerce reach asserted for the Act, including nontransportation employment. Baker's agreement belongs to that category. A general federal preference for arbitration cannot replace statutory coverage. The Fourth Circuit's FAA-based restriction consequently cannot stand. Gilmer concerns a securities-registration agreement and does not supply a contrary answer about this employment contract. Whether another source binds the employee remains distinct from the FAA holding.

### Part II. The Commission's independent action retains its individual-remedy authority

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee's private agreement selecting arbitration does not bind the nonparty EEOC or categorically eliminate victim-specific remedies Congress authorizes in the Commission's own ADA enforcement action. Even if an independent state-law ground makes the promise enforceable against the employee, that fact alone neither makes the Commission a contracting party nor removes those federal remedy categories; this is an independently sufficient ground for rejecting the relief bar.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Stevens's opinion is joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, seven direct votes. Scalia and Thomas withhold assent to this ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the agency enforces its own statutory cause, rather than an assignment of Baker's claim. Section 12117(a) gives the Commission the enumerated Title VII enforcement powers, procedures and remedies. Section 706(f)(1) authorizes its action after the statutory process; section 706(g) authorizes appropriate individual equitable relief; and section 1981a expressly includes the Commission among plaintiffs who may seek the specified damages. Compensation and reinstatement can advance that public enforcement function.

First Options supplies the actual-assent principle, but the statutory enforcement structure completes the answer. General Telephone recognizes the independent public action including individual relief, and Gilmer preserves an agency enforcement role alongside an individual's arbitration obligation. Baker could commit himself to a forum; he did not undertake a promise for the Commission, which never agreed to arbitrate.

The dissent's strongest objection concerns the beneficiary's valid commitments, not direct compulsion of the agency. It would condition individual recovery on a state-law-contract determination. But the statutory permission for appropriate relief does not create a categorical exception whenever an employee chose an arbitral forum. Enforceability against Baker alone does not diminish the agency's enacted remedies. This conclusion decides the live private-contract argument without resolving the contract's state-law enforceability or a hypothetical FAA-covered agreement. An actual adjudication, settlement, release or satisfaction differs from the forum promise presented here and requires its own record.

### Part III. Statutory conditions govern any actual award

**Holding and operative rule:** The EEOC's authority to seek relief does not establish liability or an entitlement to an award: the applicable disability, qualification, unlawful-discrimination, injury, causation and remedy predicates must be proved, and the court must apply each remedy's conditions below. The court must prevent duplicate satisfaction of the same injury when actual overlapping recovery is established; no completed arbitration, settlement, release or prior satisfaction is established here.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part III's ordinary statutory and nonduplication requirements. Scalia and Thomas separately advocate the additional contractual limitation rejected by Part II; their Part III join does not adopt Part II.

**Statutory conditions incorporated in this holding:**

| Remedy provision | Operative conditions and limits |
|---|---|
| Section 706(g)(1) | On the prescribed finding of an intentional unlawful employment practice, the court may enjoin it and order appropriate affirmative action, including hiring, reinstatement, back pay and other equitable relief. Back pay may not accrue earlier than two years before the charge and is reduced by interim earnings or amounts earnable with reasonable diligence. |
| Section 706(g)(2)(A) | Orders requiring union admission or reinstatement, employee hiring, reinstatement or promotion, or back pay are unavailable when the challenged refusal, suspension, expulsion or discharge rested on a reason other than the prohibited discrimination or retaliation. This is the distinct other-reason limitation incorporated with the enforcement provisions. |
| Section 706(g)(2)(B) | For a proved section 703(m) violation where the respondent shows it would have taken the same action without the impermissible motivating factor, the court may grant declaratory relief, limited injunctive relief and fees and costs directly attributable to that claim, but may not award damages or order the admission, reinstatement, hiring, promotion or back pay described in subparagraph (A). That separate trigger does not establish a new ADA mixed-motive or causation rule here. |
| Section 1981a(a) | For this ADA action, compensatory and punitive recovery is available for unlawful intentional discrimination under section 102 or a violation of its section 102(b)(5) accommodation requirements, not disparate-impact damages. For an accommodation claim, damages may not be awarded when the employer proves good-faith efforts, in consultation with the disabled person who informed it of the need, to identify and make an equally effective reasonable accommodation without undue hardship. |
| Section 1981a(b)(1) | Punitive damages require malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights; governmental respondents are excluded. |
| Section 1981a(b)(2) | Compensatory damages under this provision exclude back pay, its interest and other section 706(g) relief. |
| Section 1981a(b)(3) | The combined cap, for each complaining party, on compensatory damages for future pecuniary loss, emotional pain, suffering, inconvenience, mental anguish, loss of enjoyment of life and other nonpecuniary loss, together with punitive damages is $50,000 for more than 14 and fewer than 101 employees; $100,000 for more than 100 and fewer than 201; $200,000 for more than 200 and fewer than 501; and $300,000 for more than 500. The applicable size must be established for each of twenty or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year. |

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the enacted remedial menu remains conditional. Toyota supplies the operative disability rules; a seizure by itself establishes none of their required findings. Section 706(g)'s discretion concerns justified relief on proved facts, and section 1981a separately supplies damages on its own terms. Great-West interprets a different ERISA provision and does not delete this express damages authorization. The distinct limitations in section 706(g)(2)(A), section 706(g)(2)(B) and section 1981a must remain distinct rather than being combined into a new general exception. The employee's conduct can matter to mitigation, actual satisfaction or a properly established judgment or settlement. Without such a record, possible future duplication cannot justify removing every victim-specific remedy at the outset. Employer size and the other damages predicates remain matters for ordinary adjudication.

### Part IV. The categorical restriction is reversed without a contract-enforceability prerequisite

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition on victim-specific relief is reversed, and the action is remanded for adjudication of liability and appropriate statutory relief. The Commission may pursue that relief without first establishing that Baker's private forum promise is unenforceable under state law.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices who join Part II join Part IV; Scalia and Thomas instead support vacatur and a bounded inquiry into any properly available independent contract ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Both the absence of FAA coverage and the Commission's independent statutory authority defeat the restriction as entered. Because the second ground resolves the private forum agreement's asserted effect on the Commission's remedy categories, an antecedent state-law enforcement proceeding is unnecessary. The remand returns the ordinary statutory merits and remedy work under Parts II and III.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams, March 21, 2001:** Applied for the employment-contract exclusion; Baker's employment agreement falls within that exclusion.
- **First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan:** Applied for actual assent; it supports but does not replace the independent statutory-remedy analysis.
- **General Telephone Co. of the Northwest v. EEOC, 446 U.S. 318:** Applied for the Commission's independent public enforcement action, including individual relief.
- **Gilmer v. Interstate/Johnson Lane Corp., 500 U.S. 20:** Applied for the agency's preserved enforcement role; its securities-registration agreement does not govern the employment-contract exclusion.
- **Toyota Motor Manufacturing, Kentucky, Inc. v. Williams, January 8, 2002:** Preserved for the actual disability predicates on remand; the seizure allegation is not their adjudication.
- **Great-West Life & Annuity Insurance Co. v. Knudson, January 8, 2002:** Distinguished as construction of a different ERISA remedy provision; it does not remove expressly authorized ADA damages.

## Law After Decision

The FAA supplies no bar for this excluded employment agreement. Independently, an employee's private forum promise does not remove Congress's individual-remedy categories from the EEOC's own enforcement action, even if the promise binds the employee under an independent source. Liability and every actual award remain governed by their statutory predicates, mitigation rules and nonduplication limits. Concrete adjudication, release, settlement and satisfaction questions await a record establishing them.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Scalia.** They join Part I because Circuit City's governing employment exclusion removes the FAA as a source of enforcement, despite their continuing disagreement with that precedent. They also join Part III's ordinary liability, remedy and nonduplication conditions. The Commission may bring its independent public action and cannot itself be compelled to arbitrate.

They reject Parts II and IV because nonparty assent does not answer whether a court should grant relief for Baker's individual injury in disregard of his own enforceable commitment. Section 706(g)'s requirement of appropriate relief and the distinction between permission to sue and entitlement to a particular remedy, in their view, require respect for a valid beneficiary promise even though broader public relief remains available. First Options addresses actual assent to arbitration, not that distinct remedial question; Great-West similarly preserves attention to the statute authorizing the particular remedy.

The FAA cannot establish enforceability here. They would therefore vacate its categorical restriction and remand to consider any properly available independent state-law ground, the promise's actual scope, and its consequence for individual relief under their view. They presume neither a valid independent ground nor a completed arbitration, and ordinary preservation limits remain. Their position does not compel arbitration of the agency or affirm the existing restriction on a displaced FAA premise.

## Procedure After Action

The action returns through the Fourth Circuit for ordinary adjudication of statutory liability and appropriate relief. The EEOC may seek the authorized individual and public remedies without first adjudicating Baker's state-law forum obligation. The court must apply the stated statutory limitations and prevent duplicate satisfaction if an actual overlapping recovery is proved.
