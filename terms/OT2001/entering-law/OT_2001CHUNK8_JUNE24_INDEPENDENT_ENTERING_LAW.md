## Standards and Tests selection: Objective baselessness before single-suit sham motive

### Objective baselessness before single-suit sham motive

**Governing question:** What instructions govern objective baselessness before single-suit sham motive?

**Current rule:** Under the presented single-lawsuit sham theory, Noerr protection fails only if the action is objectively baseless so no reasonable litigant could realistically expect merits success, and then is shown to use governmental process itself rather than its outcome as an anticompetitive weapon. An objectively reasonable suit ends that inquiry without motive discovery. Even a sham requires ordinary antitrust elements. Fraud, serial proceedings and independent conduct remain unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

### Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

**Governing question:** What instructions govern commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions?

**Current rule:** For truthful, nonmisleading commercial speech about lawful activity, the government must establish real harms, material advancement and a reasonable fit between its chosen restriction and its interests. Where commercial and newspaper racks cause the same physical harms, the commercial category requires justification tied to those harms; benign objectives alone do not make content-selected regulation content neutral. Incremental regulation is not invalid merely because its reduction is small, but alternatives bear on fit without a least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement. In professional solicitation the government must demonstrate advancement in the relevant professional setting, without an invariable empirical-study requirement. Deceptive or coercive solicitation remains regulable. Central Hudson and Fox remain the framework; no general equality of commercial and noncommercial speech or categorical vice exception follows. When the State otherwise makes records available, a condition denying access and prohibiting use because the requester intends truthful, lawful commercial solicitation receives this same review. Absence of a general right to every government record does not remove a speech-selected condition from First Amendment scrutiny; neutral access, retrieval-fee and privacy-redaction rules retain their lawful scope.

Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven. This rejects the City's independent time, place and manner defense.
- Real advancement must be established in the relevant professional setting: Edenfield v. Fane — Real advancement must be established in the relevant professional setting, 1993-04-26. Kennedy’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (8 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

- The intended-speech condition requires commercial-speech review: The intended-speech condition requires commercial-speech review, 1995-04-18. **Present operation effective:** Lanphere & Urbaniak, April 18, 1995, adds the speech-selected records-access trigger; the original advancement-and-fit mechanics remain unchanged.

## Standards and Tests selection: Customer approval for new-category CPNI marketing

### Customer approval for new-category CPNI marketing

**Governing question:** Whether section 222(c)(1) permits affirmative approval for marketing an unsubscribed telecommunications-service category using confidential service-derived customer information.

**Current rule:** Read with the carrier's confidentiality duty, section 222(c)(1) permits the FCC to require an affirmative customer choice by oral, written or electronic means for the challenged use of individually identifiable service-derived CPNI to market an unsubscribed service category. The general statutory text does not unambiguously require that exclusive method for every CPNI use. Its permissibility under Chevron follows independent constitutional review; agency deference does not decide speech validity. On the adopted confidential-service/new-category premises, intelligible notice and flexible affirmative approval continuing until revocation or limitation materially protect against unknowing repurposing and reasonably fit that interest. Less burdensome opt-out is not constitutionally required merely because it is available, and opt-out is not declared generally invalid.

**Authority by component:**

- General approval construction and separate constitutional responsibility: U.S. West, Inc. v. FCC, Public Projection / Holdings / Part I. The marketing condition receives independent commercial-speech review; Part IV. The general approval text permits this construction, with residual claims preserved, February 7, 2000. Part I has all nine joins; Part IV is joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer (seven).
- Confined approval mechanism and fit: same Record, Part II. Affirmative choice materially advances the identified confidentiality interest; Part III. The flexible approval mechanism reasonably fits this use, and Law After Decision / Scope of the approval determination; the same seven join Parts II-III.
- Later regulatory removals and conditional amendments: FCC 99-223, paragraphs 95 and 185-190, released September 3, 1999 and published October 1, 1999; Admitted Source Record, Exact admitted source text.

**Present operation effective:** U.S. West, February 7, 2000, for the construction and confined approval ruling; FCC 99-223 retains its separate conditions.

**Controlling construction:** The category rule permits use to provide or market subscribed local, interexchange and commercial-mobile services. Affiliates supplying already subscribed categories may share when the customer subscribes to more than one such category; that permission is absent otherwise. New-category marketing requires approval, with separate permissions for inside-wiring installation, maintenance and repair, mobile-service health-effects research, and former adjunct-to-basic services. CPNI includes confidential individual calling patterns and billing information; subscriber-list information is excluded and aggregate information has separate treatment. Use required by law or approved by the customer is distinct from use for the originating service or services necessary to its provision, including directories. Original section 222(d) separately permits service initiation, rendering, billing and collection; protection of carrier rights or property and users or other carriers against fraudulent, abusive or unlawful use or subscription; and customer-approved inbound telemarketing, referral or administration during the customer's own call. The last permission does not authorize indefinite later marketing.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** Prior one-time notice must identify the information, specific recipients, purposes, rights, carrier duty, precise approval/denial steps, withdrawal rights and the continuing effect until affirmative revocation or limitation; it must state that refusal does not affect subscribed services. Notice must be comprehensible and nonmisleading. Written notice must be legible, sufficiently large and readily apparent; translating any part requires translating all. Notice may describe tailored-offering benefits and possible compelled disclosure on an affirmative written request, but may not encourage freezing third-party access. Solicitation must be proximate to notice. The carrier bears proof of compliant oral approval, and notice and approval records must be kept at least one year.

**Effective transition:** FCC 99-223 retains affirmative approval, separately removes the winback restriction in paragraph 187 and the separate-document notice restriction in paragraph 188, and makes amended information-collection rules dependent on OMB approval and later effective-date publication under paragraphs 185 and 190. No such later publication is established. Paragraph 189 postpones enforcement of amended section 64.2009(a) and (c) until eight months after release. Publication is not immediate effectiveness of all equipment, information-service or safeguard amendments, and reversal does not revive removed provisions.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is no universal rule for data ownership, all targeted marketing, all internal handling or all CPNI restrictions. Separate equipment/information-service and competitor-tracking provisions are not independently upheld or given invented approval exceptions. Takings and other arbitrary-and-capricious claims remain open; no compliance or sanction is decided.

**Related current rules:** Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions supplies the independent constitutional standard. Wireless-location and emergency subscriber information states the later express statutory requirements rather than treating general approval as permission to disregard them.

## Standards and Tests selection: Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

### Collective Brady materiality and investigating-team responsibility

**Governing question:** Assessment and consequence of suppressed favorable evidence.

**Current rule:** Suppressed favorable evidence is material when its collective lawful uses in the whole record, including defense preparation and presentation, create a reasonable probability of a different result sufficient to undermine confidence in the verdict. No item must independently meet the standard; the defendant need not show that acquittal is more likely than conviction or that remaining evidence is legally insufficient. The prosecutor must learn of favorable evidence held by police acting on the government’s behalf in the case and disclose it when Brady requires. Personal ignorance does not remove it from review. Once a material Brady violation is established, a second duplicative prejudice or harmlessness inquiry cannot defeat relief by reassessing the same nondisclosure. In a capital case, confidence in guilt and confidence in individualized death selection must be assessed separately through all supported lawful disclosure uses. Reliable capital guilt or a surviving eligibility aggravator does not alone resolve death-selection materiality. Revised defense preparation and presentation remain part of each cumulative inquiry, without a second duplicative harmlessness hurdle.

**Authority by component:**

Justice Souter’s opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for the rule, application and conviction-level relief. The dissent accepts collective assessment but disputes materiality on this record.
- The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team: The prosecutor’s responsibility includes the investigating team, 1995-04-19. Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes. The dissent’s consideration of the police-held material does not supply additional votes for an affirmative organizational rule.
- A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing: A completed material Brady violation requires no second duplicative harmlessness showing, 1995-04-19. Souter’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes for this claim-specific ground. The dissent finds no material violation and adopts no independent harmlessness ground.

- Strickler v. Greene — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “The cumulative suppression does not undermine confidence in capital guilt”, 1999-06-17; Stevens Part II, all nine Justices. **Exceptions and limits:** This case-team responsibility does not impute every government office’s knowledge or require unrestricted investigation or open-file discovery. Lawful corroboration, impeachment and investigation-related uses matter collectively; every nondisclosure is not structural error. Independent habeas gates and defense diligence concerning known evidence remain separate.

## Standards and Tests selection: Advance plea-statement waivers for impeachment

### Advance plea-statement waivers for impeachment

**Governing question:** Enforceability of a plea-discussion agreement permitting impeachment of the defendant’s own inconsistent testimony.

**Current rule:** Rules 410 and 11(e)(6) permit a defendant knowingly and voluntarily to agree before plea discussions that statements may impeach the defendant’s own inconsistent trial testimony. Absent an affirmative indication of unknowing or involuntary entry, that limited waiver is enforceable. Supported ignorance, fraud, coercion, involuntariness and agreement-scope objections receive individual review. Inconsistency alone creates no nonconsensual impeachment exception.

**Authority by component:**

- A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable: A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable, 1995-01-18. Justice Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Six Justices adopt the limited waiver rule and its presumption; Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the judgment on a different requirement that the Government prove knowing and voluntary acceptance and is not counted as a join in that presumption.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-18; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Then-Rule 410 and parallel Rule 11(e)(6) protect against use against the participating defendant in civil or criminal proceedings of withdrawn guilty pleas, no-contest pleas, related Rule 11 or comparable state-proceeding statements, and prosecutor plea discussions resulting in no guilty plea or a withdrawn plea. The fairness exception requires another statement from the same plea or discussion already introduced and fairness requiring contemporaneous consideration. The criminal perjury-or-false-statement exception requires oath, an on-record statement and counsel’s presence; ordinary impeachment is not that exception. The waiver holding decides no case-in-chief, derivative, sentencing or other-person-testimony use, no independent perjury-use term and no universal waiver of fundamental protections. Independent constitutional and evidentiary limits remain; the concurrence’s additional Government-proof requirement does not control.

**Holdings navigation:** A knowing and voluntary impeachment waiver is enforceable.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporary residential-access restraint while a warrant is sought

### Temporary residential-access restraint while a warrant is sought

**Governing question:** When officers may restrict unaccompanied entry into a home to preserve evidence during a warrant application.

**Current rule:** Temporary restriction requires the combined justification of particularized probable cause, a reasonable actual destruction risk, restraint reasonably limited to preservation, and diligent pursuit of judicial authorization. Duration, diligence, offense gravity and the substantial home interest matter to reasonableness. Probable cause, disposable evidence, awareness of investigation, refusal of consent, jailability or a stated duration alone creates no automatic exception; refusal does not independently establish exigency.

**Authority by component:**

- Combined preservation justification and execution limits: Canonical Decision Record `Illinois_v_McArthur_merits_2001-02-20.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The particular preservation need and limited execution justify this restraint, 2001-02-20; seven direct joins to Kennedy’s opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Illinois v. McArthur, 2001-02-20.

**Exceptions and limits:** No fixed two-hour allowance, general occupation or search authority, or requirement to exhaust every imaginable alternative follows. Limited observational consent is not general search consent or abandonment of an access objection. A later warrant or discovery does not retrospectively justify the restraint; independent preserved warrant and exclusion issues retain their own law.

**Holdings navigation:** [Illinois v. McArthur, 531 U.S. 326 — Temporary preservation of a home pending a warrant](HOLDINGS.md#illinois-v-mcarthur-531-us-326).

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410 (1993)

Source: state/holdings/first-amendment.md

#### Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1200.

**Decided:** March 24, 1993.

##### The selected commercial category lacks the required fit

**Holding:** When truthful, nonmisleading commercial publications concerning lawful activity and newspapers cause the same asserted physical harms through their newsracks, the City must justify why its selected commercial category reasonably fits its safety and aesthetic interests. Cincinnati has not done so: removing respondents' sixty-two racks while retaining approximately 1,500–2,000 physically similar newspaper racks violates the First Amendment; a small reduction is not invalid merely for being small, and no least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement follows.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven, throughout.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557 (1980): applies the existing commercial-speech test to the accepted classification.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469 (1989): applies reasonable proportional fit and government burden; preserves rejection of least restrictive means.
- Metromedia, Inc. v. San Diego, 453 U.S. 490 (1981): distinguishes the billboard treatment; does not permit an unrelated content distinction without the required fit.

##### The selective prohibition is not content neutral

**Holding:** A newsrack prohibition determined by whether its publication is a commercial handbill is content based when the asserted safety and aesthetic harms do not justify that distinction without reference to the publication's content. Cincinnati cannot sustain this selective ban as a content-neutral time, place and manner restriction merely because its objective is benign; otherwise lawful neutral regulation of newsracks remains available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Court opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Souter: seven. This rejects the City's independent time, place and manner defense.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applies justification without reference to content; a benign objective alone does not validate the selective line.
- Clark v. Community for Creative Non-Violence, 468 U.S. 288 (1984): preserves valid content-neutral time, place and manner regulation.
- Renton v. Playtime Theatres, Inc., 475 U.S. 41 (1986): distinguishes different secondary effects; no such difference justifies this rack classification.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Central Hudson and Fox remain governing. Incremental regulation remains possible; alternatives inform fit but do not impose least restrictive means. No strict-scrutiny rule for all truthful commercial speech, all-sources-at-once duty or commercial/noncommercial equality rule is adopted. Other lawful neutral rack controls remain available.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Sixth Circuit and the injunction against the selective commercial-handbill newsrack prohibition.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island, 517 U.S. 484

Source: state/holdings/first-amendment.md

#### 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island, 517 U.S. 484

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1140.
**Decided:** May 13, 1996.

##### Material advancement and reasonable fit of truthful-price restrictions

**Holding:** Even under ordinary *Central Hudson* review and treating temperance as substantial, Rhode Island has not demonstrated that these truthful-price restrictions directly and materially reduce consumption. The holding concerns this record and restriction, not every possible effect of advertising, prices, or demand; supported prediction remains permissible without a mandatory state-specific numerical study or experimental certainty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five. Stevens affirmatively adopts this independently sufficient conditional application without accepting consumer ignorance as legitimate or ordinary scrutiny as the complete constitutional account.

**Material application:** The State's search-cost/price-dispersion mechanism and opposing experts' concessions of price responsiveness and theoretical plausibility are material but do not overcome the trial finding of no significant consumption effect. General-advertising studies with control problems, missing estimates connecting this restriction's projected price change to consumption, and possible market-share gains without aggregate consumption gains fail to establish material advancement. Constitutional sufficiency is independently assessed without turning appellate economic inference into a new trial finding.

**Independent alternative holding:** Even assuming material advancement, continuously suppressing this category of truthful retail-price information is disproportionate to the temperance objective on this record. Concrete direct price and tax alternatives expose inadequately justified fit; they are neither mandatory replacements nor a least-restrictive-means rule.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Kennedy's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven. O'Connor and Breyer assume advancement; Souter adopts both failures; Stevens's application remains conditional. Scalia's judgment vote and Thomas's categorical theory supply no additional balancing votes.

**Material application:** Interstate-shopping concerns do not explain why the State's indirect price mechanism avoids the same leakage or justify the informational burden. Section 3-8-7 permits merchandise-attached or merchandise-placed price signs or tags inside licensed premises under departmental rules; Regulation 32 forbids references visible from outside Class A premises. Section 3-8-8.1 separately excepts recognized, authorized trade journals. These exceptions and lawful nonprice promotion neither automatically defeat nor establish fit; the challenged category is complete subject to those exact exceptions, rather than a ban on all alcohol speech.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ordinary *Central Hudson/Fox* review remains operative. No majority adopts a special-care or heightened-review tier, categorical anti-ignorance rule, universal alternatives checklist, all-sources-at-once requirement, or independent interstate-information immunity. False or misleading speech, unlawful promotions, and direct alcohol controls retain their own governing law; the Court selects no tax or minimum-price program and decides no antitrust dispute.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Edenfield*, *Ibanez*, *Lanphere*, and *Rubin* require proof responsive to the actual restriction. *Fox* supplies reasonable proportional fit; *Discovery Network* and *Rubin* support concrete comparisons without least-restrictive-means review. *Edge* retains its record-specific meaningful-reduction holding; *Florida Bar*'s invalidation does not prohibit supported prediction. *Midcal* and *324 Liquor* invalidate particular price arrangements, not every state-imposed price control. Subsequent searching scrutiny denies automatic *Posadas* authorization here, but broader repudiation of its paternalism and greater-power reasoning has only four votes. *Queensgate* remains a summary merits disposition confined by *Mandel* to what it necessarily decided, rather than a rule that comparable restrictions invariably survive later developed advancement-and-fit doctrine. *Bigelow* supplies no separate adopted interstate holding.

##### Effect of the Twenty-first Amendment on speech protection

**Holding:** Alcohol-control authority supplies no additional presumption relaxing First Amendment protection for these off-premises truthful-price restrictions. Authority over lawful sales does not itself validate suppression of accurate information, and accepting a license does not surrender speech rights.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-A, unanimous; this agreement does not attribute balancing to Scalia or Thomas.

##### The broader speech rule and LaRue's enhanced-deference rationale

**Holding:** The Twenty-first Amendment does not qualify the First Amendment's protection of speech. *LaRue*'s reliance on the Twenty-first Amendment to enhance deference is disavowed; its holding remains supported by ordinary police powers over the regulated conduct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; six. Stone-Zsela, O'Connor and Breyer do not join this broader rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *LaRue*'s identified rationale loses force, but its conduct holding and *Bellanca*'s conduct result remain intact. The broader rule concerns speech protection, not invalidity of every direct alcohol restriction.

##### Operative relief on both preserved speech claims

**Holding:** Reversal requires implementation of the district court's constitutional declaration against the challenged prohibitions in §§3-8-7 and 3-8-8.1 and Regulation 32; the completed record requires no new merits trial. The Court issues no additional injunction directive.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part V-B, joined by all seven other Associates; eight. Reversal on both plaintiffs' First Amendment claims is unanimous; Stone-Zsela would additionally direct a prospective injunction, which is not the mandate.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the operative October 24 First Circuit judgment and remand to implement the declaration. The paid $400 fine and unappealed administrative order are not reopened; no damages or refund is awarded. Peoples' waived Commerce Clause claim remains outside review. General enforcement authority in §3-8-8 and every other alcohol-advertising rule are not independently invalidated.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States

#### Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-387.
**Decided:** 1999-06-14.

##### Direct and material advancement

**Holding:** Section 1304 cannot be enforced against these Louisiana broadcasters' truthful, nonmisleading advertisements for lawful private-casino gambling because the federal scheme's actual exceptions and operation do not directly and materially advance its asserted gambling-harm reduction and state-policy accommodation interests. Potential reception in a State prohibiting private casinos does not itself make the advertised lawful transactions unlawful.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens joins this application conditionally under the existing framework; Scalia joins the application while retaining broader doctrinal reservations. Thomas supports the judgment on a different ground and adds no balancing vote.

##### Reasonable fit

**Independent alternative holding:** Independently, suppressing these truthful lawful-casino advertisements lacks a reasonable proportional fit with the asserted interests, given the scheme's extensive comparable permissions and available direct regulation of gambling. The Government may pursue genuine incremental policies, but the actual informational burden requires justification; the First Amendment does not require the least restrictive imaginable policy.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The same eight-Justice Court-opinion coalition directly joins this independently sufficient ground, with the same stated reservations.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Central Hudson supplies ordinary lawful/nonmisleading commercial-speech review, substantial interest, material advancement and reasonable fit.
- Fox preserves the distinction between reasonable fit and least-restrictive means.
- Edenfield requires justification responsive to real harms and material alleviation without invariably requiring empirical studies.
- Ibanez tests the actual truthful-information restriction and its responsive justification.
- Rubin applies independent advancement and fit inquiries to a materially contradictory scheme and requires an actual state-accommodation mechanism.
- 44 Liquormart preserves those separate inquiries under ordinary scrutiny; its alcohol-specific rule does not create a gambling exception or a new tier.
- Edge retains its record-specific meaningful-reduction and state-accommodation holding; the different statutory permissions and mechanism here do not reproduce it.
- Posadas is not overruled through this as-applied decision, and no categorical constitutional rule against all vice regulation is adopted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The described lawful private-casino advertising is protected against this federal prohibition because each of two ordinary commercial-speech requirements fails independently. False or misleading advertisements, promotion of unlawful transactions, targeting minors and materially narrower evidence-supported restrictions remain outside the holding. Direct regulation of gambling survives. Other asserted applications require their own justification; neither §1304 nor the FCC restriction is erased in all settings.

**Operative remedy or transition:** **Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The Government may not enforce §1304 against these truthful, nonmisleading advertisements for lawful private-casino gambling. Relief concerning the companion FCC restriction conforms to that same as-applied prohibition. Section 1304 and the rule are not facially invalidated; no damages or replacement gambling policy is ordered.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: U.S. West, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission

Source: state/holdings/first-amendment.md

#### U.S. West, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission

**Docket or dockets:** Tenth Circuit No. 98-9518; judgment below, 182 F.3d 1224. No Supreme Court docket supplied.
**Decided:** February 7, 2000.

##### Whether the marketing-selected use condition requires independent commercial-speech scrutiny

**Holding:** A rule conditioning use of information on its intended use for truthful, lawful commercial solicitation receives commercial-speech review even when the carrier lawfully possesses the information and other marketing remains available. Protecting confidential calling records against unknowing use outside the customer's chosen service relationship is a substantial interest, but the government must independently establish material advancement and reasonable fit.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part I, unanimous; Stevens and Kennedy join only this part.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The information is confidential, individually identifiable service-derived CPNI, including calling patterns and billing information; subscriber lists are excluded and aggregate information receives separate treatment. Corporate or carrier status, lawful custody, an unchallenged statutory baseline and other marketing opportunities do not eliminate speech review. Neither competition nor a new constitutional data-ownership right supplies an alternative sufficient ground.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lanphere and United Reporting supply the speech-selected-use trigger. Went For It preserves both substantial privacy and the need for supported application. Paul P. and Condon remain confined to their different questions.

##### Whether affirmative approval materially advances confidentiality for new-category marketing

**Holding:** On this record, requiring approval before using confidential service-derived CPNI to market an unsubscribed service category directly and materially protects against unknowing repurposing outside the existing relationship. The service-related and distinct research permissions do not defeat that application; this holding neither treats all internal handling as harmful nor validates every collateral CPNI restriction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; seven direct votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The existing-relationship permissions concern marketing within subscribed local, interexchange and commercial-mobile categories and sharing among affiliates that already supply those categories when the customer subscribes to more than one. The distinct permissions cover inside-wiring installation, maintenance and repair; mobile-service health-effects research; and former adjunct-to-basic services. Section 222(d)'s separate permissions cover service initiation, rendering, billing and collection; protection against fraudulent, abusive or unlawful service use and protection of carrier rights and property; and customer-approved inbound telemarketing, referral or administrative services during that customer-initiated call, not indefinite later marketing. Those permissions do not reproduce unconsented new-category marketing. The integrated-firm privacy assessment is counterevidence, not a finding that every later purpose falls within the original relationship. Passive external reception, lack of a recognizable interior image and the equipment’s market availability do not remove that protection; equipment use or investigative purpose alone does not establish a search.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes for this holding and its qualifications. Kennedy and Breyer dissent.

**Material application:** The Agema 210 pattern of unusually warm roof and wall areas revealed hidden domestic operation, including an inference of cultivation lights. Findings that the device was crude, passive and produced no identifiable interior image do not eliminate the information it acquired.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general-public-use condition limits the rule. Ordinary exterior observation does not become a search merely because it aids investigation. The Court decides no all-instrument rule, future technology or open-commercial-premises question.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pinson and Myers are applied without change. Katz and Karo retain protection of concealed domestic information without physical entry. Greenwood’s discarded-property rule and Place’s contraband-confined detection are distinguished.

##### The later entry warrant did not authorize the earlier scan

**Holding:** This search required an advance warrant or a recognized warrant exception. No exception justified the scan on the established record, and the later warrant for physical entry could not retroactively authorize it.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes for this holding and its qualifications. Kennedy and Breyer dissent.

**Material application:** The agents held no scan warrant, and no consent, contemporaneous exigency or other recognized exception is established. Later discovery of contraband creates no antecedent emergency. Corroborative use of the scan does not turn the later entry warrant into prior authorization.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Pinson and Myers retain their separate warrant requirement. Katz and Karo preserve recognized exceptions when their actual predicates exist; none is established for this scan.

##### The preserved derivative-evidence claim requires a lawful determination under the corrected search rule

**Holding:** A conviction affirmance and rejection of a preserved suppression claim that depend on this erroneous no-search premise must be vacated to the extent necessary to decide the claim under the proper search rule. On remand, the court must determine whether the later warrant and evidence remain lawfully supportable without the scan or through a properly available, fact-supported alternative; suppression follows if its predicates are established and no lawful ground defeats exclusion, with conditional-plea consequences governed by Rule 11(a)(2).

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes for this holding and its qualifications. Kennedy and Breyer dissent.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No final untainted-probable-cause, causal-dependence, independent-source, inevitable-discovery or objective-reliance finding is made. Leon requires an available, fact-supported claim of objectively reasonable reliance and excludes knowing or reckless deception, abandonment of judicial neutrality, an affidavit wholly lacking probable-cause indicia and facial deficiency precluding reasonable reliance; its inquiry includes information-supplying officers and assumes execution within scope. The 1992 conduct date alone proves no reliance. Evans concerns qualifying non-law-enforcement court-clerical error. Pinson's affidavit nonchallenge does not govern this preserved claim. For a genuinely forfeited issue, Olano separately requires error, plainness, ordinarily outcome-affecting prejudice and the discretionary fairness, integrity or public-reputation inquiry; intentional waiver remains distinct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The suppression disposition and dependent conviction affirmance are vacated only to the extent necessary for the corrected inquiry. Exclusion follows if its predicates are met and no supported lawful ground defeats it. Rule 11(a)(2) permits conditional-plea withdrawal when the defendant prevails on appeal; the remand itself orders no exclusion, withdrawal, acquittal, release, discovery, new hearing, retrial or sentence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Myers supplies the preserved-claim remand; Pinson’s different preservation posture is distinguished. Karo retains the untainted-warrant inquiry. Leon, Evans and Olano retain their actual predicates and limits; Rule 11(a)(2) retains its conditional-plea consequence.

# United States v. Knights; No. 00-1260 — 2001-12-10

[Authoritative Record](../records/united-states-v-knights_merits_2001-12-10.md)

## Event

United States v. Knights, No. 00-1260. Merits decision, December 10, 2001. Argued November 6, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which affirmed suppression because the residence search pursued ordinary criminal investigation. The question is whether a known probation search condition and individualized reasonable suspicion justify the search notwithstanding that purpose. The written condition permitted searches by probation or law-enforcement officers, and the detective had reasonable suspicion connecting the residence to criminal activity.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument or submission and decision.

## Public Action

Reverse and remand, 9–0.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The categorical investigative-purpose ground for suppression is rejected. The lower courts apply the condition-and-reasonable-suspicion rule; evidence is admissible if no independently preserved defect remains. Any genuinely open scope or factual issue must be resolved on its own record.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supports the complete judgment | Entire opinion |

## Holdings

### The known search condition and individualized suspicion justify this probation search

**Holding and operative rule:** A warrantless search of a probationer's residence within a known, lawful search condition is reasonable when supported by particularized reasonable suspicion of criminal activity in the circumstances presented. Ordinary criminal-investigative purpose does not independently invalidate that combination; neither the condition alone nor reasonable suspicion alone supplies this holding.

**Authority:** The unanimous Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly adopt this rule, its justification, and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the probation relationship and notice of the express condition reduce the privacy interest implicated by this residential search, while individualized suspicion supplies an objective justification for this intrusion. Griffin establishes that lawful probation supervision changes the constitutional setting; it does not make its special-needs rationale the exclusive ground for every probation search. Knights's proposed purpose restriction would invalidate a search supported by this combined justification merely because the officer investigates crime. The Fourth Amendment does not require that result here.

Whren supports examining the objective justification after authority for the intrusion has been established. Its ordinary probable-cause traffic rule does not itself confer residential search power or decide motive doctrine for every search based on reasonable suspicion. Edmond and Ferguson retain their purpose inquiries in their suspicionless and special-needs settings. Scott governs the consequence of an unconstitutional supervisory search, not the antecedent validity of this condition-and-suspicion search; its exclusion rule remains intact. Any ultimate suspicion question properly remaining is independently reviewed under Ornelas, with its protection for historical findings and due weight to grounded contextual inferences.

The holding rests on reasonableness, not complete contractual consent or forfeiture of Fourth Amendment protection. Suspicionless residential searches and blanket consent are outside the question necessarily resolved because individualized suspicion supplies the sufficient ground here.

**Precedent treatment:** Griffin's supervisory-relationship principle is applied without making its rationale exclusive; Whren's objective method is applied within its source-specific limits; Edmond and Ferguson are distinguished; Scott's exclusion rule and Ornelas's review allocation remain operative.

## Precedent Treatment

- Griffin v. Wisconsin, 483 U.S. 868: the lawful supervisory relationship informs diminished privacy; special needs is not the exclusive possible justification.
- Whren v. United States, 517 U.S. 806: objective assessment follows a demonstrated source of search authority; its traffic holding is not generally extended to every reasonable-suspicion search.
- City of Indianapolis v. Edmond, 531 U.S. 32, and Ferguson v. City of Charleston, 532 U.S. 67: purpose remains material in their distinct suspicionless and special-needs settings.
- Pennsylvania Board of Probation & Parole v. Scott, 524 U.S. 357: exclusion for the institution's unconstitutional supervisory search remains unchanged; this decision finds the bounded search reasonable.
- Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690: the division between factual and ultimate legal review is applied unchanged.

## Law After Decision

The Fourth Amendment permits the defined probation-condition and individualized-suspicion combination despite ordinary investigative purpose. Existing scope, execution and exclusion rules retain their separate operation; the decision creates no general suspicionless-search or consent-waiver rule.

# United States v. Arvizu; No. 00-1519; 534 U.S. 266. — 2002-01-15

[Authoritative Record](../records/united-states-v-arvizu_merits_2002-01-15.md)

## Event

United States v. Arvizu, No. 00-1519; 534 U.S. 266. Merits decision, January 15, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 2. Argued November 27, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 232 F.3d 1241, as amended. The district court rejected the stop and voluntary-consent challenges; Arvizu's conditional plea preserved suppression review. The Ninth Circuit held the stop unlawful and treated the ensuing search as tainted. The questions are the proper totality method and whether the supported pre-stop combination establishes reasonable suspicion. The United States seeks restoration of suppression denial; Arvizu seeks exclusion.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment resting on an unlawful stop is reversed, and the case is remanded for the preserved independent consent issues.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The stop was supported by reasonable suspicion. The Ninth Circuit must address the remaining preserved consent-voluntariness and scope issues under their applicable review. The unlawful-stop taint ground falls, but the conviction and complete suppression denial are not finally restored without that review.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Reverses unlawful-stop ruling and returns preserved independent consent issues | All four holdings; Scalia joins the governing Ornelas application while separately retaining his review objection |
| Concurrence | Scalia | No other Justice | Joins opinion and judgment in full | Criticism of inferential deference within independent review; same result under traditional independent review |

## Holdings

### I. Supported observations must be considered in their actual relationship

**Holding and operative rule:** Reasonable suspicion for an investigative stop depends on an objective, particularized inference from the total circumstances known before the seizure; an observation cannot be excluded merely because it has an innocent explanation or would be insufficient alone. Courts must still test each proposed inference's factual basis and actual weight, so neither the number of observations nor an officer's unexplained intuition supplies the required justification.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's opinion is joined throughout by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt all four holdings and their limits; Scalia separately qualifies the review discussion without withholding his application join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that context may make several lawful observations informative together. Terry requires articulable suspicion of wrongdoing; Cortez requires the whole picture and an inference particular to the person or vehicle stopped. Wardlow likewise permits contextual consideration without making location or ambiguous behavior automatically sufficient. Innocence of each isolated act does not compel a conclusion about the connected pattern.

The amended Ninth Circuit opinion acknowledged totality and softened some earlier formulations. It nevertheless categorically discounted the children's posture and waving and separated observations before assessing their relationship. That method removed potentially probative context too early. Arvizu's profiling objection remains substantial: every response cannot become suspicious merely because an officer says experience makes it so. The court must identify supported connections and may assign little or no weight to an asserted factor. The totality rule is not a command that every fact count positively, and the later discovery of marijuana cannot justify the earlier seizure.

### II. Ultimate sufficiency receives independent appellate judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Ornelas, appellate courts independently determine ultimate reasonable suspicion while reviewing historical facts, credibility and genuinely factual subsidiary inferences for clear error, and giving genuine due weight to grounded local and experiential inferences. That contextual weight does not create deference or a presumption of correctness for the officer's ultimate constitutional conclusion.

**Authority:** All nine join this application of the governing Ornelas allocation. Scalia's concurrence preserves his objection to that allocation's inferential formulation without withholding a present join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that review must preserve both the trial court's factual role and the appellate court's responsibility for constitutional sufficiency. Ornelas supplies that division. Local knowledge and experience can explain the significance of an observed route or pattern; they cannot manufacture a fact or insulate the conclusion that the seizure was lawful. Describing what the children did and deciding what constitutional significance their conduct has are not the same task.

Scalia objects that deference to an inferential conclusion can compromise genuinely independent review. The controlling formulation protects genuinely factual subsidiary judgments and gives contextual understanding actual weight while reserving the ultimate sufficiency decision to the reviewing court. It adopts no intermediate standard or numerical formula. The supported pre-stop findings here permit that legal application now; another remand simply to redo the stop comparison is unnecessary.

### III. This connected pre-stop pattern supplied reasonable suspicion

**Holding and operative rule:** The sensor-linked checkpoint-bypass route, final bypass turn, timing context and particular occupant and apparent-cargo observations, assessed together with their ordinary explanations and the agent's actual experience, supplied a particularized basis for this brief investigative vehicle stop. The holding does not require a traffic offense, direct contraband observation or proof that criminal activity was more likely than an innocent explanation, and does not treat any one greeting, vehicle type or neighborhood as independently sufficient.

**Authority:** All nine join this record-specific sufficiency holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the connection among the observations distinguishes this case from a general profile. Sensors tracked travel along a route capable of avoiding an operating checkpoint, and the final turn took the last bypass road. The approaching staffing transition supplied relevant local context. The driver's slowing and stiffness, combined with the children's persistent forward-facing waving and raised knees, supported a limited inference of concealed cargo and an attempted appearance of normality. Neither a greeting nor an acknowledgment of an officer is legally required; the behavior matters only within that route-and-load pattern.

The opposing account has force. The road serves homes, recreation and tourists; a minivan is ordinary family transport; children may wave or rest their feet on luggage; and the shift change was still about forty-five minutes away. The agent had local patrol and alien-stop experience, not a personal history of drug stops. These qualifications reduce the weight but do not eliminate the supported relationship. A similar minivan's drug stop one month earlier does not taint all minivans. Registration to a relative in a broadly described smuggling neighborhood contributes little without a particular connection and is unnecessary to the sufficient combination. The Court presumes neither knowledge of the staffing schedule nor erratic or unlawful driving.

Wardlow's actual insufficiency holding involved a materially thinner departure-and-location record. Here the sensor-tracked bypass and passenger/cargo observations provide the additional individualized connection. The conclusion authorizes the brief stop, not a search of the vehicle or its bags, and rests exclusively on information preceding the seizure.

### IV. The lawful stop removes taint but does not resolve independent consent issues

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's judgment resting on the stop's illegality is reversed, and the case is remanded for proper appellate treatment of the preserved independent consent-voluntariness and scope issues. A lawful stop removes that taint ground but neither establishes voluntary consent nor determines its extent; the claimed exterior-only permission and disputed gun-hand interaction remain subject to their applicable review and preservation limits.

**Authority:** All nine adopt this complete disposition and bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that stop justification and search authorization remain distinct. The district court rejected both the stop and voluntary-consent challenges, and the conditional plea preserved suppression review. The Ninth Circuit's unlawful-stop analysis treated the ensuing search as tainted without deciding the independent consent grounds. Reversing the stop ruling therefore does not finally restore the conviction or conclusively sustain the whole suppression denial. The receiving court must give the trial court's findings their applicable review and address the consent questions actually preserved.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Terry v. Ohio, 392 U.S. 1:** Applied for the objective, particularized reasonable-suspicion threshold and limited investigative-stop authority.
- **United States v. Cortez, 449 U.S. 411:** Applied for assessment of the whole picture and rational, individualized connections among observations.
- **Ornelas v. United States, 517 U.S. 690:** Applied for independent ultimate review, clear-error protection of facts and genuine contextual weight without a police-correctness presumption; no new review standard is adopted.
- **Illinois v. Wardlow, January 12, 2000:** Its contextual method is applied; its actual insufficiency holding is distinguished by the additional sensor-linked bypass and vehicle-specific observations here. Its rejection of categorical location or flight permission remains intact.
- **Florida v. J. L., March 28, 2000:** Its requirement of sufficient grounds before intrusion and rejection of retrospective contraband justification remain effective.
- **United States v. Knights, December 10, 2001:** Distinguished because the probation status and search condition there are absent; its confined holding supplies no ordinary-driver exception.

## Law After Decision

Supported observations capable of innocent explanations retain their actual contextual force but gain no automatic positive weight by accumulation. Independent judicial review determines whether their relationships supply individualized suspicion. This pre-stop combination meets that standard without neighborhood stigma, proof of an observed offense or post-stop contraband. The lawful-stop conclusion leaves the independent authority for the ensuing search to the preserved consent questions.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Scalia, concurring, joined by no other Justice.** He joins the Court's full application of governing law and its reversal and bounded remand. He retains his Ornelas dissent's preference for deferential review and his objection that giving weight to an inferential conclusion can be conceptually inconsistent with independently deciding the ultimate issue. A factual description of the children's waving can receive factual protection; whether it is suspicious cannot be sheltered from constitutional judgment simply by calling it an officer inference. Reversal follows here even under traditional independent review. His join applies Ornelas as governing law without endorsing its original choice of standard, dispensing with innocent explanations or authorizing the subsequent search.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit must give the trial court's findings their applicable review and resolve the preserved consent-voluntariness and scope questions. The reasonable-suspicion issue is settled in favor of the stop. The lawful-stop conclusion does not itself finally restore the conviction or resolve the remaining request for suppression.

Local patrol and alien-stop experience do not establish prior personal drug-stop experience. Registration neighborhood alone supplies no finding that the relative or address was tied to crime.

# United States v. Vonn; No. 00-973; 535 U.S. 55 — 2002-03-04

[Authoritative Record](../records/united-states-v-vonn_merits_2002-03-04.md)

## Event

United States v. Vonn, No. 00-973; 535 U.S. 55. Argued November 6, 2001; decided March 4, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 3. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which vacated the pleas and sentence using harmless-error review confined to the plea proceedings. The questions are whether an unpreserved Rule 11 omission receives Rule 52(b) review and whether relevant information throughout the existing record may inform its effect on substantial rights. The United States seeks correction of the review rules and reinstatement or reconsideration; Vonn seeks to preserve the judgment. Before the pleas, a magistrate twice gave counsel-rights advisements and Vonn acknowledged understanding. The plea judge omitted the trial-counsel warning at both hearings; the prosecutor's attempted correction at the later hearing did not cure it. Defense counsel did not object. A later withdrawal motion rested on a different ground.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

## Holdings

### I. Forfeited Rule 11 error is governed by Rule 52(b)

**Holding and operative rule:** A defendant who forfeits a Rule 11 objection must satisfy Rule 52(b): there must be error that is plain and affects substantial rights, ordinarily with the defendant bearing the effect burden, and correction remains discretionary only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Rule 11(h) does not exempt this plea-taking omission from ordinary forfeiture review; intentional waiver remains distinct.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion has the direct support of Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight votes control; Stevens dissents from this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the plea rule and the general review rule operate together. Rule 11(h) requires disregarding variances that do not affect substantial rights, but does not say that a failure to object has no consequence. Olano supplies the separate requirements and usual defendant burden. Johnson applies that framework to an important constitutional omission without making its importance an automatic exception.

The contrary argument correctly identifies the judge's affirmative personal duty and a defendant's possible inability to recognize an omitted warning. Those concerns establish why the colloquy is mandatory; they do not delete Rule 52(b) for the unpreserved claim. McCarthy enforced an earlier Rule 11 regime, and Rule 11(h) displaced automatic reversal without expressly abolishing plain-error review. McCarthy did not decide the present choice of review standards. The trial-counsel omission therefore remains error despite Vonn's representation and the prosecutor's intervention. His different withdrawal argument did not preserve it. The ordinary effect inquiry and independent discretion requirement apply without requiring actual innocence or treating substantial-rights impact as sufficient by itself.

### II. Relevant portions of the existing record inform the error's effect

**Holding and operative rule:** In assessing the effect of the Rule 11 omission on substantial rights, a reviewing court may consider relevant information throughout the existing record, including earlier judicial rights advisements. Such information bears on actual understanding and effect; it neither cures the judge's omitted personal warning nor conclusively establishes understanding from representation or a signed form alone.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II of O'Connor's opinion, giving direct unanimous authority independent of the division over burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that actual earlier court advisements cannot be excluded merely because they occurred outside the plea colloquy. United States v. Young permits review of the whole record in assessing error, and Olano separates a procedural breach from its effect. McCarthy's personal-colloquy requirement remains mandatory; admitting relevant evidence about consequences does not excuse noncompliance.

The magistrate twice advised Vonn of counsel rights and obtained acknowledgments of understanding. Those events may inform what he understood when pleading, but their timing, content and demonstrated comprehension must be evaluated. The Court supplies no off-record explanation and treats neither counsel's presence nor a form as an irrebuttable cure. A transcript boundary that excludes actual recorded advice would obstruct the inquiry into effect without improving enforcement of the duty itself.

### III. The proper framework must first be applied below

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's use of the wrong burden and its exclusion of relevant existing-record material require vacatur and remand. That court must apply all four Rule 52(b) conditions, including record-specific effect and the independent basis for discretionary correction, before deciding the appropriate appellate relief.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part I. Stevens agrees with vacatur and remand for whole-record consideration but would apply the Government's harmlessness burden.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court of Appeals did not assess this record under the governing framework. Earlier warnings are relevant, not conclusive, so their presence alone cannot support the Government's requested immediate reinstatement. Olano requires the effect inquiry and the distinct judgment about correction to retain separate work. The case returns for that application and any other properly remaining appellate issues. The perjury-warning contention remains undecided because it was not resolved below and falls outside the review questions decided here. The case concerns direct review of this forfeited federal-rule omission, not collateral relief or the consequences of every constitutional plea defect.


## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Olano: applied to Rule 11 forfeiture with its usual substantial-rights burden and independent discretionary condition; no automatic reversal, innocence requirement or comprehensive resolution of its reserved categories follows.
- Johnson v. United States: applied at its actual forfeiture and discretionary-correction scope; its particular intervening-law application becomes no general guilt-based exception.
- McCarthy v. United States: its mandatory personal-colloquy principle remains, while the later Rule 11(h) text precludes importing its automatic-reversal consequence into the present rules.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applied to relevant whole-record review of the error's effect, not as permission to omit the required warning.
- Boykin v. Alabama: its constitutional plea requirements remain distinct; this decision supplies no general constitutional-invalidity or collateral-review rule.


## Law After Decision

Direct review of the forfeited Rule 11 omission uses Rule 52(b) and the relevant existing record. Eight Justices establish the defendant-burden and independent-discretion requirements, and all nine reject a colloquy-only effect inquiry. The judge's personal advice duty remains mandatory. Record-specific effect and justified correction are ordinary applications assigned to the court of appeals under that law.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stevens joins Part II's use of relevant material throughout the official record and agrees that the appellate judgment should be vacated. He rejects Parts I and III's transfer of the effect burden to the defendant. Rule 11 assigns the judge affirmative duties during a procedure in which an uninformed defendant may not recognize the missing advice. Its specific harmless-error provision should therefore leave the Government responsible for showing that the omission was harmless. McCarthy supplies the distinctive judicial obligation; Stevens's Olano dissent distinguishes procedural protections from easily measured outcome injury, and his Johnson join did not decide whether Rule 11(h) controls this separate plea procedure. He would remand for whole-record harmless-error review under Rule 11(h). Earlier recorded advisements may establish actual knowledge under that inquiry, but representation is not itself compliance, and neither automatic invalidation nor invented off-record advice follows from his position.

The appellate judgment is vacated. On remand the Ninth Circuit must consider the relevant existing record under each Rule 52(b) condition and determine the resulting appellate relief. Other properly remaining issues, including the unresolved perjury-warning contention, retain their actual procedural footing. The mandate itself neither reinstates the convictions conclusively nor grants withdrawal of the pleas.

# Thompson v. Western States Medical Center; No. 01-344. — 2002-04-29

[Authoritative Record](../records/thompson_v_western_states_medical_center_merits_2002-04-29.md)

## Event

Thompson v. Western States Medical Center, No. 01-344. Argued February 26, 2002. Decided April 29, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing its February 6, 2001 judgment, 238 F.3d 1090, holding the challenged compounding speech conditions unconstitutional and section 353a nonseverable. The question is whether conditioning the federal compounded-drug exemptions on the prescription-solicitation and particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion restrictions violates the First Amendment. The separate nonseverability ruling was not brought within review.

Licensed pharmacies prepared promotional materials for distribution by mail and at medical conferences. The challenge concerns truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding. Section 503A(a) permits the identified patient's prescription through unsolicited receipt or a prescribing practitioner's approved notation that a compounded product is necessary for that patient. The ordinary route requires compounding by a licensed pharmacist in a State-licensed pharmacy or Federal facility, or by a licensed physician, on the identified patient's prescription from a licensed physician or another practitioner authorized by State law to prescribe. The separate advance-compounding route permits a licensed pharmacist or physician to prepare limited quantities before receiving the patient's valid prescription, based on a history of receiving valid prescriptions for that drug generated solely within an established relationship between the compounder and either that identified patient or the physician or other practitioner who will write the prescription. Section 503A(c) prohibits particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion while permitting promotion of compounding services; the necessity notation does not exempt a message from subsection (c).

Section 503A supplies only exemptions from section 501(a)(2)(B)'s manufacturing-practice requirement, section 502(f)(1)'s adequate-directions requirement and section 505's new-drug-approval requirement. Bulk drug substances must comply with an applicable United States Pharmacopoeia or National Formulary monograph and the United States Pharmacopoeia compounding chapter; if no monograph exists, they must be components of approved drugs or, if neither condition is met, appear on the Secretary's regulatory list. They must be manufactured by a registered establishment, including a foreign establishment registered under section 510(i), and accompanied by valid certificates of analysis. Nonbulk ingredients must comply with an applicable monograph, if one exists, and the compounding chapter.

The compounder may not use a drug product on the Secretary's published list of products withdrawn or removed because the product or a component was found unsafe or ineffective. Regular compounding, or compounding in inordinate amounts as defined by the Secretary, of essentially commercially available copies is barred; a change for an identified patient producing a significant difference determined by the prescribing practitioner is excluded from that copy category. The product must not be one identified by regulation as presenting demonstrable compounding difficulties reasonably demonstrating an adverse effect on safety or effectiveness.

The interstate alternatives are compounding in a State that has entered a memorandum with the Secretary addressing inordinate interstate distribution and providing for appropriate State-agency investigation of complaints about compounded products distributed outside that State, or, absent that memorandum, out-of-State distribution no greater than five percent of the pharmacy's or physician's total prescription orders dispensed or distributed. The statute contains no enacted twenty-percent memorandum ceiling. PET compounded drugs and radiopharmaceuticals are excluded. Mixing, reconstitution and other acts performed according to the manufacturer's approved labeling and other directions consistent with that labeling lie outside the statutory compounding definition. These distinct provisions are not interchangeable exceptions to the challenged promotion rule.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The judgment of the Ninth Circuit is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer, as to consumer-directed promotion; they reserve the distinct physician-directed issue rather than vote to sustain that restriction.

The six affirming Justices sustain the constitutional invalidation of both the prescription-solicitation condition and the particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion condition, including their application to truthful physician-directed information. The dissenters oppose the consumer-directed invalidation under both conditions but express no conclusion on physician-directed validity. All nine participate; the reservation is not nonparticipation.

The Ninth Circuit judgment, including its unreviewed determination that section 353a is nonseverable, remains intact. The judgment does not create an operating compounding exemption with the speech conditions removed.

## Holdings

#### The challenged conditions burden truthful promotion more broadly than their justification supports

**Holding and operative rule.** The prescription-solicitation and particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion conditions of section 503A violate the First Amendment as applied to truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding because the Government has not established a reasonable fit between their informational burden and its asserted distinction between individualized compounding and manufacturing subject to approval. The holding applies the ordinary commercial-speech framework; it neither requires the least restrictive imaginable measure nor prevents restrictions on misleading claims, unlawful transactions, actual manufacturing conduct or narrower commercial-speech measures supported by an adequate justification.

**Authority.** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. These six Justices adopt the complete fit holding, statutory explanation and affirmance. Thomas accepts this sufficient application without endorsing the framework as the full measure of constitutional protection. This is a directly shared rationale, not an aggregation of distinct advancement findings.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that calling drug-specific silence a condition of an exemption does not answer the First Amendment question. Central Hudson and Fox require a proportionate relationship between a lawful commercial-speech burden and the substantial interest it serves. The Court assumes the Government's strongest combined account of drug safety, approval integrity, individualized access and material advancement. The defect is fit.

Product information can enable clinicians and patients to locate an individualized preparation without establishing manufacture for a broad market. Section 503A(a) allows the identified patient's prescription through unsolicited receipt or a prescribing practitioner's approved notation of necessity; its separate advance-compounding route permits a licensed pharmacist or physician to prepare limited quantities before receiving the patient's valid prescription, based on a history of receiving valid prescriptions for that drug generated solely within an established relationship with either that identified patient or the practitioner who will prescribe it. Section 503A(c), however, separately bars promotion of a particular drug, drug class or drug type while allowing service promotion. The necessity notation is not an exception to that separate speech prohibition.

Production, distribution, patient-prescription and commercial-copy controls bear directly on the conduct Congress seeks to distinguish. The Court recognizes the contrary concern: quantity limits can exclude medically needed preparations and may not address every demand-driven use of unapproved drugs. That does not explain why useful truthful product information must carry this broad burden. The Government has not adequately justified this proxy against the available conduct distinctions. The Court selects no production cap and finds no guarantee that every proposed alternative is equally effective. As 44 Liquormart and Lorillard require, the actual speech burden remains a distinct inquiry even when the regulatory objective and some preventive effect are accepted.

**Precedent treatment.** Central Hudson and Fox supply the governing substantial-interest, advancement and reasonable-fit framework; this decision applies their fit requirement without replacing it with strict scrutiny or an absolute alternatives rule. The separate precedent account below states the remaining treatments.

## Precedent Treatment

- 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island: applied for independent examination of the burden on truthful information; its broader separate theories are not converted into a controlling categorical ban.
- Lorillard Tobacco Co. v. Reilly: applied for separate advancement and fit inquiries and attention to the actual lawful-speech burden; this decision rests on fit and assumes the Government's advancement account.
- Edenfield v. Fane: applied for justification responsive to the actual professional setting; the physician audience is part of the covered speech burden.
- Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association v. United States: applied for practical examination of the fit between a selective speech restriction and the regulatory objective; no conclusion that all compounded products are safe follows.
- Lanphere & Urbaniak v. Colorado: its benefit-condition principle remains applicable at its actual scope; selecting speech as the condition does not eliminate ordinary constitutional scrutiny.

## Law After Decision

The reviewed compounding speech conditions fail the existing reasonable-fit requirement. The Court establishes no independent failure of material advancement, categorical commercial-speech immunity or replacement compounding program. The Ninth Circuit's unreviewed nonseverability determination remains part of the affirmed judgment. Substantive drug-safety and manufacturing authority retains its independent scope.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring.** He joins the full fit holding because these restrictions fail even the lesser Central Hudson test. He separately regards suppression of accurate product information to prevent its effect on willing listeners as the mechanism his 44 Liquormart position rejects. Drug safety is legitimate, but that objective does not itself justify withholding accurate information; direct drug controls remain distinct. His concurrence is not judgment-only agreement and does not supply the Court with a broader categorical rule.

**Breyer, dissenting in part, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** The dissent would reverse the constitutional invalidation insofar as it rests on promotion to consumers. The statutory balance preserves medically needed preparations while preventing cumulative risks from a growing market of products that have not undergone approval. Product promotion can generate demand apart from a clinician's prior individualized assessment. Production caps, wholesale restrictions and controls on equipment or advance preparation may either exclude needed treatment or fail to address that demand. General service advertising and continuing physician involvement preserve meaningful channels. Under ordinary reasonable-fit review, the dissent therefore finds the consumer restriction proportionate despite its burden on truthful information. It reserves the distinct question of physician-directed promotion because that issue requires separate consideration; it neither sustains every communication restriction nor announces a benefit-condition exception to the First Amendment.

## Event

United States v. Drayton, No. 01-631. Argued April 16, 2002; merits decision June 17, 2002, on certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit's reversal of suppression denials. The accepted question is whether the February 4, 1999 coordinated drug-and-weapons encounter aboard the bus effected a seizure before the searches. Three officers boarded after the driver checked tickets and left: one observed from the driver's area while two proceeded from the rear. Brown agreed to a bag search and patdown and was arrested; Drayton then underwent a patdown. Brown entered a conditional plea; Drayton was convicted after trial. Independent consent voluntariness and a Terry alternative are outside the question reviewed.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated in submission and decision. No Justice is recused.

## Public Action

The Eleventh Circuit's seizure-based suppression judgment is reversed and the case remanded for lawful further proceedings consistent with the no-seizure determination.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reversed and remanded, 5–4, for both respondents on the seizure-based suppression issue.

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, who would preserve suppression as the product of an unsupported seizure.

The taint-based suppression direction is set aside. Further proceedings must respect Drayton's trial conviction, Brown's conditional plea and any separately preserved issue outside the accepted seizure question. The judgment does not automatically reinstate both convictions or independently uphold consent.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Breyer join throughout; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg join Part I | Reverses and remands | I: objective contextual seizure framework; II: no-seizure application and bounded mandate |
| Dissent | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg | Would affirm seizure-based suppression | Joins governing framework in Part I; rejects application to both respondents and preserves immediate-taint consequence |

## Holdings

### I. Objective freedom to decline governs the bus encounter

**Holding and operative rule:** Police questioning aboard a bus is a seizure only when, considering the whole objectively communicated encounter, a reasonable passenger would not feel free to decline the requests or terminate the interaction. Constraints arising from the passenger's decision to travel are not themselves police restraint; absence of a refusal warning is relevant but neither necessary nor sufficient to establish seizure.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes for the governing framework, with a divided application.

**Controlling explanation:** Bostick adjusts the ordinary freedom-to-leave inquiry to a passenger whose own travel choice already limits movement. The inquiry concerns police-created restraint, not whether a person could conveniently abandon the trip. Robinette rejects an indispensable warning while retaining examination of actual communicated authority. Physical obstruction is not necessary to a seizure, and quiet requests do not prove its absence. Number, position, words and conduct must be considered together. An officer's undisclosed willingness to accept refusal is not an objective assurance, just as an undisclosed coercive purpose would not establish detention. Neither the bus setting nor one isolated circumstance controls.

**Precedent treatment:** Bostick and Robinette are applied without creating a mandatory warning or categorical bus rule; Bond's protection against additional physical intrusion remains distinct.

### II. Neither respondent was seized before the search

**Holding and operative rule:** The coordinated encounter did not objectively compel either Brown or Drayton to cooperate before the challenged searches: the officers used quiet requests, concealed their weapons, left the aisle passable, gave no command to remain and exercised no control over the passengers' tickets. Brown's arrest was relevant to the later request to Drayton but did not, in the circumstances of that request, itself detain him; the seizure-based suppression judgment is reversed.

**Authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by the other four Court-application Justices identified in Opinion Topology; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The officer observing from the driver's area and the colleagues approaching from the rear presented coordinated authority. The Court considers that combined arrangement rather than tallying isolated absences of coercion. It nevertheless conveyed requests capable of refusal: the observer did not block passage, the questioning officer spoke conversationally, weapons remained concealed and the officers did not direct passengers to stay or surrender tickets. The driver, not the police, handled the tickets. An announced drug-and-weapons purpose did not transform those requests into commands.

Brown's arrest after his patdown changed the setting for Drayton, but neither the arrest nor the other officers' positions communicated that Drayton was detained. Cooperation remains a factual circumstance, not a substitute for the objective legal judgment. The Court does not infer freedom from an officer's uncommunicated intent or use the discovered drugs to justify earlier conduct.

The accepted question concerns seizure. Finding no seizure removes the antecedent unlawful-detention ground for suppression but does not decide independent consent voluntariness or an unpresented Terry justification. Arvizu preserves that separation of stop and search questions. The respondents' distinct conviction postures and any independently preserved issues remain for lawful further proceedings.

**Precedent treatment:** Bostick's contextual rule is applied to each respondent; Arvizu preserves the separate consent inquiry. Knights supplies no advance passenger authorization or suspicion for this encounter.

## Precedent Treatment

- Florida v. Bostick, 501 U.S. 429 (1991): applied to objectively communicated freedom to decline in the actual bus setting.
- Ohio v. Robinette, November 18, 1996: applied against an indispensable warning requirement while preserving contextual review and the separation of detention from consent.
- Bond v. United States, April 17, 2000: remains effective on additional intrusion into bus luggage; it does not classify every police request as a seizure.
- United States v. Arvizu, January 15, 2002: applied to preserve the boundary between the reviewed stop or seizure issue and separate consent questions.
- United States v. Knights, December 10, 2001: distinguished; the probation-condition-and-suspicion combination supplies no authority for this passenger encounter.

## Law After Decision

Bostick's contextual test continues unchanged, with this five-Justice no-seizure application to both respondents. The disposition removes the detention-taint basis of suppression and decides neither independent consent nor a new suspicion theory. Police number and positioning remain relevant; neither a passable aisle nor absence of a warning is a categorical rule.

## Separate Writings

**Souter, dissenting, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Ginsburg.** They accept Bostick's objective totality inquiry and reject any indispensable refusal warning. They disagree about the combined message. An officer facing passengers from the front, two colleagues moving from the rear, close requests to seated passengers and announcement of an interdiction operation communicated expected submission before travel resumed. The driver's absence and travel arrangements supplied context, not police seizure by themselves. Quiet voices, concealed weapons and a passable aisle weigh for the Government but do not overcome that coordinated display. Both respondents were already subject to it before Brown's arrest; the arrest intensified the pressure on Drayton. No physical blockade or express command was necessary. Without an adequate independent justification, the continuous restraint was unlawful, and no release or intervening event separated the immediate searches from it. They would preserve suppression on that taint ground and return for the consequences appropriate to Brown's conditional plea and Drayton's trial conviction, without acquittal, an independent consent holding or a new Terry theory.

Brown's conditional plea and Drayton's trial conviction retain their distinct procedural consequences. Independent consent or other issues may be addressed only if otherwise properly preserved and available; this decision resolves no such issue itself.

## Holdings

### I. Limited administrative access does not erase workplace privacy

**Holding and operative rule:** A public employee retains a reasonable expectation of privacy in an individually used office computer where actual access practices do not expose its contents generally. Government ownership, occasional maintenance or document access, and a rule against personal business use do not alone waive that privacy or authorize unrestricted contents searches.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes at this record-specific scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Leventhal used a private office and an individually assigned computer. Occasional technical access and the prohibition on private business did not establish a general practice of inspecting stored contents. Bond distinguishes limited exposure from consent to additional information acquisition; Guest likewise separates retained information privacy from a lawfully justified intrusion. Neither case answers the employer-search question merely by recognizing coverage. The Court does not adopt an absolute privacy rule for every workplace computer or treat government ownership as consent.

**Precedent treatment:** Bond and Guest are applied to the distinction between privacy coverage and justified acquisition, without extending their separate factual holdings to every workplace.

### II. A work-related investigation requires justification at inception and proportionate scope

**Holding and operative rule:** A public employer investigating work-related misconduct may inspect a protected workplace area without a warrant when reasonable grounds support the inquiry at inception and its measures remain reasonably related to that employment objective and not excessively intrusive in light of the suspected misconduct. Each follow-up examination requires its own justification from information lawfully obtained and the continuing work purpose; later discoveries cannot retroactively justify the first intrusion.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by the other six full-opinion Justices; seven direct votes. Scalia does not join this general framework and Stevens does not approve the searches on the merits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court directly adopts Ortega's inception-and-scope framework for the presented internal misconduct inquiry; it does not describe the divided Ortega opinions as a preexisting unified rationale. The standard accommodates legitimate supervision while preserving information privacy. An anonymous complaint is neither automatically sufficient nor automatically irrelevant. Its actual specificity, connection to work and the proposed intrusion matter. Robinette forbids using later discoveries to create earlier authority, while Arvizu requires genuine contextual force rather than automatic weight for every observation. Ordinary police collection, general monitoring and unrelated personal contents are not authorized by this employer-specific holding.

**Precedent treatment:** Ortega's inception-and-scope approach receives seven direct votes here; Robinette and Arvizu retain their independent-justification requirements. Knights supplies no employee-consent fiction.

### III. These four inspections were reasonable

**Holding and operative rule:** The October 25, 1996 file-name inventory and the three limited February and April 1997 examinations of selected program directories and files were reasonably initiated and confined to the actual employment-misuse inquiry. The reviewed individual-capacity Fourth Amendment dismissal is therefore affirmed; the established search record does not include examination of personal tax-return contents.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part III has the seven full-opinion joins plus Scalia's express join in this confined application; eight direct votes. Stevens concurs in the judgment on personal immunity only.

**Controlling explanation:** The letter identified Leventhal by job grade and alleged neglect amid computer-related workplace behavior. It did not directly accuse him of tax work on the office machine. That indirect connection supports a modest file-name inventory, not unrestricted opening of personal documents. The initial inspection neither ran programs nor opened files. The program names lawfully found then justified targeted confirmation in the three later examinations. Permission for outside tax work did not grant permission to use departmental equipment for it; tolerance of nonstandard departmental software did not end inquiry into nondepartmental use.

Repeated inspection is a material concern, but the particular program evidence and confined employment purpose supplied the limit at each step. The Court does not rely on later admissions or an imagined search of client returns. The nonfinal disciplinary suppression ruling supplies no established preclusive adjudication. Because the searches adjudicated here were reasonable, the Court need not decide personal immunity to affirm the search judgment. The ungranted employment-benefit rulings and abandoned claims remain undisturbed.

**Precedent treatment:** Ortega's work-related justification is applied to the actual sequence. Bond and Guest preserve privacy without forbidding every justified employer inspection; no new computer exception or immunity rule is created.

## Event

Christopher v. Harbury, No. 01-394. Argued March 18, 2002; decided June 20, 2002. On certiorari to the D.C. Circuit, which reinstated a claim that official deception prevented access to court. Harbury alleges concealment of her husband's detention and torture in Guatemala while intervention might have saved him. Those allegations receive the procedural assumption appropriate to dismissal; they are not adjudicated facts.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at submission and decision. No recusal or vacancy affects the case.

## Public Action

Reversed and remanded for dismissal of the revived access count, 9–0; surviving tort claims remain for ordinary adjudication.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reversed and remanded for dismissal of the revived access count, 9–0; surviving tort claims remain for ordinary adjudication.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Underlying-action requirement and independent distinct-remedy defect; constitutional cause assumed |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Thomas | None | Rejects the asserted constitutional duty to provide the information sought |

## Holdings

### A retrospective access claim must identify a concrete lost judicial opportunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A claim seeking relief for past obstruction of court access must identify a nonfrivolous underlying judicial action, intentional obstruction causing loss or material impairment of the opportunity to pursue it, and presently available relief for that loss that cannot be obtained through surviving litigation. Ordinary notice pleading requires identification of these substantive predicates, not proof of certain success or a futile prior filing.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, controls for eight Justices. Thomas concurs only in the judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Lewis v. Casey ties access to actual injury to a judicial opportunity; the same need applies when the alleged obstruction has already ended. Without an identifiable underlying action and causally lost relief, a court cannot distinguish a denied legal opportunity from an additional description of the underlying wrong. Harbury's complaint failed to identify the lost cause of action. Swierkiewicz preserves ordinary notice pleading and rejects a requirement to plead evidentiary proof; it does not remove the substantive elements of the claim. The grave allegations must receive their proper procedural assumption, but their gravity cannot identify a missing cause or remedy. The asserted constitutional category and a Bivens damages channel are assumed only to resolve these defects, so their source and availability need not be decided. The actual truth of the allegations, official immunity, foreign-affairs defenses and the surviving torts' elements likewise are not necessary to this disposition.

**Precedent treatment:** Lewis v. Casey is extended from actual hindrance of current litigation to the necessary injury and remedial connection for an asserted lost past opportunity; Swierkiewicz retains ordinary notice pleading.

### The clarified theory also fails to identify distinct available relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Even treating counsel's intentional-infliction theory and lost emergency injunction as part of the pleaded case, this access count identifies no presently attainable remedy unavailable through the surviving tort action. That independently sufficient defect requires dismissal of the revived count.

**Authority:** The same eight Justices join this alternative holding; Thomas's judgment agreement supplies no additional rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The requested emergency intervention could no longer save Harbury's husband when this suit was brought; that relief is unavailable under either theory. The identified intentional-infliction claim against the CIA defendants remains pleaded in counts 18 and 19. If it can be maintained, relief for its demonstrated consequences remains potentially available through that claim, and Harbury identifies no additional presently obtainable access remedy. This is an examination of the actual clarified cause and requested relief, not a presumption that all torts with similar labels compensate identical harm. If the predicate fails for a reason independent of the alleged deception, that failure cannot establish a viable underlying action for access purposes. Intentional deception can impede legal action, but it does not automatically create an additional damages channel for the same alleged consequences. Merely naming the same tort on amendment does not answer the independently adjudicated defect. Ordinary amendment rules remain applicable to any properly sought amendment on its actual allegations; the mandate orders none.

**Precedent treatment:** Lewis requires injury-fitted relief, not a duplicative access label. Malesko supplies no automatic new federal damages channel from an allegation of serious injury.

## Precedent Treatment

- Lewis v. Casey, June 24, 1996: its concrete-injury and remedial-fit principles extend to the asserted retrospective obstruction; the underlying cause and lost relief must be identified.
- Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A., February 26, 2002: ordinary notice pleading remains; substantive identification does not become a heightened evidentiary pleading rule.
- Correctional Services Corp. v. Malesko, November 27, 2001: its confined corporate-custody cause does not decide this different proposed damages channel; the latter is assumed, not recognized, here.

A backward-looking access theory cannot proceed without the identified arguable underlying action, causally lost opportunity and distinct available relief. The present complaint fails both identification and, even as clarified, the distinct-remedy requirement. Recognition of the assumed constitutional damages category remains unnecessary to the judgment.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** The Constitution does not impose the information duty asserted here: officials need not provide national-security information or answer informal requests in the claimed manner. That narrower objection suffices to reject this access count. The affirmative practical-assistance obligation addressed in Lewis concerned persons in custody and does not create this outside-custody duty.
