## Standards and Tests selection: Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

### Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

**Governing question:** What instructions govern forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)?

**Current rule:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of forfeited error only if there is error, it is plain, and it affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, unlike failure timely to assert it. Plain means clear or obvious under current law. The defendant ordinarily must show outcome-affecting prejudice. Even then correction is discretionary and should occur only if the error seriously affects fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings; actual innocence is unnecessary, but substantial-rights impact alone is insufficient. Presence of alternates instructed not to participate supplies neither prejudice nor a presumption without a showing of participation or chilling effect. Initially unsettled trial law, special outcome-independent categories, justified prejudice presumptions and hearing authority remain unresolved. Johnson assumes substantial-rights injury without deciding whether withholding materiality is structural or harmless; its fourth-condition application supplies no general guilt-based exception or innocence prerequisite. When settled law permitted the trial action but intervening controlling law makes it erroneous during direct review, plainness is measured at appellate consideration. A forfeited omission of a genuine jury element proceeds under the distinct Rule 52(b) conditions rather than automatic reversal.

## Standards and Tests selection: Serious risks in prison conditions and deliberate indifference

### Serious risks in prison conditions and deliberate indifference

**Governing question:** What objective danger, official culpability and response govern preventive prison-conditions claims and failure-to-protect liability.

**Current rule:** A prisoner may establish an Eighth Amendment violation through deliberate indifference to involuntary exposure posing an unreasonable risk of serious future harm without waiting for existing disease. For future-health exposure, the objective inquiry includes the seriousness and likelihood of harm and contemporary society’s unwillingness to tolerate the risk. For a substantial danger to inmate safety, an official must actually know facts indicating the risk, actually draw the inference and disregard it unreasonably. Constructive knowledge or negligence is insufficient. Reasonable protective action defeats deliberate-indifference liability even if harm occurs. Actual awareness may be proved circumstantially, including from an obvious risk, but obviousness permits rather than compels the inference. Deliberate avoidance of confirmation may support actual awareness; a specific prisoner warning, identification of the exact assailant or prediction of the precise time is unnecessary. Knowledge cannot be imputed collectively without proof of the individual official’s awareness. Prospective relief considers current danger and awareness through the litigation and whether unlawful disregard is likely to continue; an adequate showing of nonrecurrence may defeat that relief. Later notice can support prospective protection but does not retroactively establish awareness for damages.

**Governing question:** What legal sources an appellate court must consider on a preserved clearly-established-law issue.

**Current rule:** Whether a federal right was clearly established when officials acted is reviewed independently using all relevant precedent, including cases neither cited to nor discovered by the district court. ## Standards and Tests selection: Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

### Circumstantial proof of unconstitutional motive despite qualified immunity

**Governing question:** Proof, discovery and causation when unconstitutional motive is an element of a claim and qualified immunity is asserted.

**Current rule:** Qualified immunity imposes no categorical direct-evidence requirement for unconstitutional motive. The plaintiff must establish the actual constitutional elements, personal responsibility and causation through legally competent direct or circumstantial evidence under ordinary procedural burdens. Specific nonconclusory support, lawful discovery limits and the clearly-established-law defense remain available; the rule abolishes neither all heightened specificity requirements nor a burden supplied by the underlying claim. Ordinary preponderance governs unconstitutional motive in the §1983 retaliation claim; immunity and litigation burden cannot justify a special clear-and-convincing quantum. Courts may decide dispositive legal defenses first and require specific nonconclusory support, but cannot categorically withhold discovery needed to obtain proof until the rejected heightened showing is made. Rule-authorized clarification, replies, sequencing, protective orders, relevant proportional discovery and ordinary summary-judgment duties remain available. Under Mt. Healthy the claimant must establish protected conduct and its substantial or motivating causal role; the defendant may then prove by a preponderance that the same action would have occurred without it. A merely possible lawful explanation does not establish that defense or exclude competent motive evidence.

## Standards and Tests selection: Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

### Qualified-immunity legal appeals and factual sufficiency

**Governing question:** The boundary of immediate review under §1291 when qualified immunity is denied.

**Current rule:** Immediate review may address the separable legal question whether conduct on the relevant assumed facts violates clearly established law, including the legality of a categorical direct-evidence condition. It may not determine only whether pretrial evidence creates a genuine dispute about the official’s participation, or choose between disputed motive, causation or factual accounts. A legal appeal uses the district court’s assumed facts; if not stated, the appellate court may identify likely assumptions favorable to the nonmovant without deciding truth or reweighing sufficiency. An abstract legal contention does not bring the factual dispute within immediate review. Ultimate constitutional voluntariness is not wholly historical merely because its test considers circumstances; a legal coercion defense may be reviewed on proper historical assumptions without selecting the disputed account. An earlier pleading-stage appeal does not by itself exhaust review of a later summary-judgment order: a separable legal immunity question must independently satisfy the collateral-order conditions. This permits neither repetition of an unchanged ruling nor relitigation of matters resolved by the first mandate. Having to defend a different surviving claim does not itself defeat otherwise authorized claim-specific review, but neither supplies missing authorization nor makes that other claim reviewable.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prison-conditions prospective relief

### Prison-conditions prospective relief

**Governing question:** The §3626 scope, necessity findings and preliminary-relief conditions.

**Current rule:** In a civil proceeding arising under federal law concerning conditions of confinement or officials' effects on confined persons' lives, prospective relief must be narrowly drawn, extend no further than necessary to correct the particular plaintiffs' federal-right violation and be the least intrusive necessary means; the court must make those findings and give substantial weight to adverse public-safety and criminal-justice-system effects. Relief requiring or permitting an official to exceed state or local authority or otherwise violate that law also requires that federal law require the state-law violation, necessity to correct the federal violation and absence of any other corrective relief. The statute confers no authority to build prisons or raise taxes and preserves otherwise applicable remedial limits.

Otherwise authorized preliminary injunctive relief must be narrow, no broader than necessary for the identified harm and least intrusive, with substantial safety/system weight and respect for the distinct state-law conditions. It expires ninety days after entry unless the court makes the prospective-relief findings and makes the order final before expiration. An existing permanent decree does not automatically become a preliminary order.

**Present operation effective:** Pub. L. 105-119, §123, 1997-11-26, for the federal-requirement condition; the other statutory conditions and Lewis qualifications retain their original dates.

## Standards and Tests selection: Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays

### Prison-conditions termination motions and automatic stays

**Governing question:** When existing prospective prison relief becomes terminable or stayed under §3626.

**Current rule:** On a party’s or intervener’s motion, prospective prison relief is terminable two years after grant or approval, one year after denial of a termination motion under §3626(b)(1), or, for an order on or before April 26, 1996, two years after that date. Under §3626(b)(2), a defendant or intervener is entitled to immediate termination when the movant establishes that relief was entered without substantive need, narrowness and least-intrusiveness findings, subject to (b)(3)’s preservation condition. Actual sufficient findings require no ritual language, and Rufo’s separate significant-change threshold is not an additional condition. Under amended (b)(3), relief shall not terminate if the court makes written, record-based findings that it is necessary to correct a current and ongoing federal-right violation, extends no further than necessary, is narrowly drawn and is the least intrusive necessary means. Earlier agreed or otherwise lawful judicial modification and termination remain possible.

The court independently determines the current violation and the necessity and scope of every retained duty. Consent decrees are covered; negotiated obligations do not automatically become independent rights, while a necessary corrective remedy may exceed the minimum substantive conduct rule. Material current proof needed for this adjudication must receive a meaningful opportunity for presentation; neither speculation that more evidence might help nor the fact of a termination motion requires a new hearing. Unsupported prospective duties must end, but termination erases neither completed determinations nor payments or other lawful historical effects. It ends the judicial obligations themselves, rather than merely shifting enforcement of identical obligations to another court. An independent State-law agreement or accrued claim requires its own legal basis.

Section 3626(e)(1) requires prompt disposition and permits mandamus to remedy failure to rule promptly. The amended opening of (e)(2) makes a motion under subsection (b) operate as the statutory stay. Its timing clauses specify day thirty for motions under (b)(1) or (b)(2), and day 180 for motions under other law, ending on the final order deciding the motion. The effective date specified in (e)(2)(A) may be postponed for good cause by no more than sixty days; general calendar congestion is not good cause. Except for that specified postponement, an order blocking the stay is treated as refusal to dissolve or modify an injunction and is appealable under §1292(a)(1), regardless of its label or whether termed preliminary or final. For a presented subsection (b)(2) motion, ordinary equitable authority cannot suspend the automatic stay beyond the express good-cause postponement; subsection (e)(4) authorizes review, not substantive power to block the stay. This change to interim prospective law is not categorically legislative adjudication under Article III, while courts retain the actual statutory decision. A supported claim that the timing, including available postponement, denies meaningful adjudication remains open under Article III and applicable due process. No violation, hearing or automatic continued blocking injunction is presumed. Enactment alone supplies no pending motion, elapsed period, stay or termination.

## Standards and Tests selection: After-acquired misconduct and age-discrimination remedies

### After-acquired misconduct and age-discrimination remedies

**Governing question:** The effect of misconduct discovered after an allegedly age-discriminatory discharge.

**Current rule:** Misconduct unknown to the employer at discharge does not erase an ADEA violation or categorically eliminate all relief. The employee retains the liability burden. To limit relief, the employer must prove misconduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone, not merely that discharge would have been permissible. Once proved, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily inappropriate; backpay’s starting measure runs from unlawful discharge to actual discovery, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests.

## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

##### Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority

**Holding:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of a forfeited legal error only if it is plain and affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, while mere failure timely to assert a right does not. Plain means clear or obvious under current law, and the defendant ordinarily must show prejudice affecting the outcome; errors first clarified on appeal, special outcome-independent categories and justified prejudice presumptions are reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: preserves structural/trial-error distinction without deciding every Rule 52(b) category.
- Brecht, April 21, 1993: distinguished; preserved Doyle habeas review does not control forfeited Rule 24(c) error.

##### Correction remains discretionary

**Holding:** Even when plain forfeited error affects substantial rights, correction under Rule 52(b) is permitted, not automatic, and should occur only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Actual innocence is not a necessary condition, but satisfaction of substantial rights alone does not satisfy this additional inquiry.

### Farmer v. Brennan, No. 92-7247

Merits decision, June 6, 1994

#### Chronology and Posture

511 U.S. 825. Argued January 12, 1994; decided June 6, 1994. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit's unpublished summary affirmance of judgment for federal prison officials. The questions are the meaning of deliberate indifference in a failure-to-protect claim and whether the absence of an express warning defeats other possible proof of actual knowledge. Farmer alleges assault after a March 9, 1989 transfer and seeks damages and prospective protection; officials dispute their individual knowledge and responsibility.

Chief Justice Alex-Lamar Stone-Zsela and Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

The judgment is vacated and the case remanded, 9–0.

| Component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Failure-to-protect summary judgment and related discovery ruling | Vacate and remand, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg | No opposing Justice | Reconsider the Rule 56(f) request and judgment using actual awareness and ordinary circumstantial proof; preserve individualized defenses and current-relief review. |

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg | Vacatur and remand | Entire actual-awareness, proof, reasonable-response, application and bounded-remedy opinion |
| Concurrence | Blackmun | No other Justice | Joins the Court and judgment | Preserves objection to Wilson while accepting its application here |
| Concurrence | Stevens | No other Justice | Joins the Court and judgment | Preserves objection to a subjective-motivation condition for cruel confinement |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Thomas | No other Justice | Vacatur and remand only | Applies the unchallenged framework provisionally; does not join the constitutional predicate or remedial reasoning |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Actual awareness and unreasonable disregard of substantial danger

**Controlling proposition:** A prison official violates the Eighth Amendment by disregarding a substantial risk of serious harm only if the official knows facts indicating that risk, actually appreciates the risk, and fails to respond reasonably. A danger that an official merely should have discovered does not establish deliberate indifference; an official who responds reasonably is not liable under this rule even if harm occurs.

**Authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight Justices adopt this rule; Thomas joins only the judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that deliberate indifference requires actual appreciation of serious danger. Estelle separates constitutionally culpable indifference from negligence, and Wilson requires both a serious deprivation and a culpable state of mind in a conditions claim. Hudson v. Palmer recognizes the duty to protect prisoners whom custody prevents from protecting themselves. Those duties do not make officials insurers against every assault. The official must understand the substantial risk, but need not foresee its precise attacker, timing or mechanism when the known danger encompasses the prisoner's situation. The broader proposal to impose liability for whatever a reasonable official would have recognized would replace Wilson's subjective requirement. The opposite proposal to demand purposeful harm would confuse ordinary protective duties with the distinct force setting. Reasonable protective action defeats deliberate indifference even when unsuccessful. The Court does not find that any defendant possessed the required knowledge, had responsibility for a particular placement, caused the assault or lacks a defense. Those questions remain individualized on remand.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: applies its distinction between deliberate indifference and negligence without reopening the accepted conditions framework.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its separate objective and subjective requirements and specifies actual-awareness recklessness for this failure-to-protect claim.
- Hudson v. Palmer, 468 U.S. 517: applies its recognition of custodial protection duties; it supplies no automatic liability from an assault.
- Hudson v. McMillian, February 25, 1992: preserves the nontrivial malicious-beating holding and leaves its unresolved broader force standards untouched; no universal malicious-and-sadistic requirement enters this conditions claim.

##### Circumstantial proof and reconsideration of summary judgment

**Controlling proposition:** Actual awareness may be proved by ordinary circumstantial evidence, including an obvious risk, and does not require an express warning by the prisoner. Obviousness permits but does not compel an inference: a genuine failure to appreciate the risk defeats the knowledge element, whereas deliberately refusing to confirm strongly suspected facts or their obvious inference may establish awareness; summary judgment here must be reconsidered without treating Farmer's silence as dispositive.

**Authority:** The proof-and-remand portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Eight adopt its reasoning; all nine support vacatur and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a subjective rule does not require direct admission of knowledge. Wilson's culpability requirement asks what the official actually understood; ordinary evidence may answer that question. An obvious and longstanding danger, reports, experience or communications may permit a factfinder to reject a disclaimer of awareness. But proof that an official ought to have known remains different from proof that the official did know. Deliberate avoidance of a strongly suspected danger cannot secure immunity from that inference, while an honest failure to draw it remains outside this constitutional rule. Farmer sought discovery about institutional violence and officials' knowledge under Rule 56(f). The district court denied that request and treated the absence of an express safety complaint as decisive. That approach may have foreclosed other material evidence. The Court therefore requires reconsideration of the request and summary judgment under the proper standard whether discovery is ultimately allowed or denied. It neither orders trial nor bars renewed summary judgment on a properly considered record.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: applies its actual culpability requirement without making a prisoner warning an additional element.
- Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97: preserves the negligence boundary while allowing ordinary evidence of deliberate indifference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The transfer officials may contest their knowledge, responsibility, causation and reasonable response. The Court does not combine different defendants' innocent information into collective knowledge, find the alleged assault proved, or decide qualified immunity. The event-date rule is Rule 56(f).

##### Current danger governs preventive relief

**Controlling proposition:** Prospective protection requires an established current substantial danger and knowing unreasonable nonresponse that persists through the litigation and is likely to continue. The court may consider later developments bearing on present danger and knowledge; litigation notice cannot create past damages liability, and officials may defeat prospective relief by showing that the danger or culpable response has ceased and will not recur.

**Authority:** The prospective-relief portion of Souter's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg; eight Justices adopt this rule and the bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that preventive relief concerns present conditions. Helling permits a prisoner to seek protection before serious harm occurs, while retaining Wilson's culpability requirement. The Government's earlier reliance on administrative detention cannot resolve the issue when its later account places Farmer in the general population of a medium-security prison. Neither account establishes a present constitutional danger by itself. Within ordinary procedural discretion, the district court may consider evidence developed during litigation showing both an ongoing substantial risk and the responsible officials' knowing failure to respond reasonably. A filed allegation supplies no finding of danger, and later notice does not establish what officials knew before the assault. Officials remain free to show changed placement, effective protection or cessation unlikely to recur. Any injunction must fit the established danger and officials' lawful responsibilities. The record does not justify a blanket prohibition on all penitentiary placement. The Court preserves the distinct capacities, causation and immunity inquiries and awards neither damages nor an injunction.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Helling v. McKinney, June 18, 1993: applies preventive protection against serious future harm and its direction to examine current conditions.
- Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294: retains the separate culpability requirement for the threatened conditions violation.
- Bivens v. Six Unknown Named Agents, 403 U.S. 388: preserves the presented individual-officer damages channel without creating a Treasury claim.
- Carlson v. Green, 446 U.S. 14: preserves the individual-officer Eighth Amendment damages source; damages entitlement remains unproved.
- FDIC v. Meyer, February 23, 1994: applies the distinction between an individual claim and an unavailable direct agency constitutional damages action.
- Elder v. Holloway, February 23, 1994: preserves review of immunity using all relevant law existing when the conduct occurred; this decision supplies no retroactive notice in 1989.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Terre Haute warden and Bureau Director were sued only in official capacities; the Oxford warden, case manager and two regional officials faced both-capacity claims. Equitable discretion and appropriate internal grievance procedures are not converted into a new mandatory exhaustion holding. No later statutory exhaustion regime is applied.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Wilson remains controlling; its subjective requirement is now specified for this failure-to-protect claim. Helling remains the basis for preventive review. The limited malicious-force rule in Hudson is neither enlarged nor displaced. No precedent is overruled.

Effective June 6, 1994, deliberate indifference in this conditions claim requires actual awareness and unreasonable disregard, with ordinary circumstantial proof and a reasonable-response limit. Absence of a prisoner warning is not dispositive. Prospective relief turns on current danger and conduct; historical knowledge, individual capacities, causation and qualified immunity remain distinct. No defendant is found liable, and no universal mental-state rule for all prison-force claims is adopted.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Blackmun joins the Court because it applies the unchallenged deliberate-indifference framework while preserving workable circumstantial proof and prevention of future harm. He would overrule Wilson's general subjective requirement: objectively inhumane conditions may violate the Eighth Amendment without identifying an official with the required state of mind. That broader position does not change the rule the Court adopts here.

Justice Stevens joins the Court's application but maintains that confinement conditions imposed on a prisoner can constitute punishment without improper subjective motivation. His disagreement with Wilson does not impose a purposeful-malice requirement or prevent his join in the present actual-awareness rule.

Justice Thomas concurs only in the judgment. In his view, not every condition or unauthorized omission is punishment within the Eighth Amendment. Because the parties do not seek overruling Estelle or Wilson, he accepts a cautionary remand within their framework to determine whether Farmer's silence was treated as dispositive. The remand does not declare the officials liable or prevent renewed summary judgment after the proper inquiry. His judgment vote does not join the Court's broader constitutional or prospective-relief explanation.

The case returns for reconsideration of the Rule 56(f) request and the merits judgment. Defendants may assert individualized knowledge, causation, reasonable response and immunity defenses. Current prospective danger and the scope of any lawful protection remain for the lower court. No automatic trial, damages award or placement injunction is ordered.

No general mandatory merits-first rule or authoritative-reliance replacement is adopted. No precedent is overruled. The absence of a common new immunity rationale does not unsettle the existing factual-review limits or the binding remand instruction. Personal immunity remains distinct from constitutional legality, actual participation, institutional responsibility and remedy.

#### Separate Writings

**Souter, announcing the judgment.** Souter considers the merits and immunity inquiries legally distinct: an officer may perceive the facts correctly yet reasonably misunderstand the particular constitutional limit. Harlow and Anderson preserve that legal-error protection; Graham's allowance for reasonable factual perceptions does not itself decide it. General prohibition of excessive force is insufficient by itself, but a materially identical precedent is not indispensable. Hunter and Elder require the proper adverse factual assumptions and the full relevant conduct-date law. Souter would have ordinary lower-court immunity adjudication first determine whether those assumed facts establish a constitutional violation and, if so, whether the particular right was sufficiently established, so that the constitutional rule can develop. He recognizes the limits of the granted review here and assumes a possible violation; he does not require decision of a constitutional issue outside review or an advisory ruling. That sequencing view is his own, not a rule adopted by the judgment majority. Unlike the dissenters, he leaves the first distinct application to the lower court and directs neither immunity nor its denial. Unlike the other concurring Justices, he retains the distinct legal-error rationale in this force setting.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in the judgment.** The Chief Justice accepts the exact existing-law remand while proposing a different personal defense. He would displace Harlow and Anderson only insofar as they protect an official merely because the violated right was insufficiently clearly established, preserving their compatible particularized analysis and lawful summary-judgment requirements. First, the complete constitutional rule would account for urgency, reasonably perceived danger, reasonable factual mistakes and operational discretion. If a violation were established, the official would bear the burden of identifying binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct, genuine reliance on it and a reasonable basis for treating it as valid and applicable. An instruction accurately conveying that authority could implement reliance. Legal silence, lack of a materially identical case, department custom, an unsupported supervisor's assurance and general job authority would not suffice.

In his view, legitimate protection against unexpected legal change concerns affirmative authoritative permission. The existing defense's predictability and litigation-control benefits do not justify withholding an otherwise available remedy from the first claimant establishing a particular application of settled protection. Graham already accounts for the officer's practical circumstances; those same factual allowances should not excuse wrongful force again. On Katz's supported account, an unnecessary shove of an already controlled, unresisting demonstrator violates the force standard without requiring physical injury, while necessary proportionate security removal remains lawful. Actual necessity, resistance, perceptions and participation remain disputed. The record identifies no binding precedent or enactment directly authorizing the alleged unnecessary shove; general security authority is insufficient.

His proposed defense would govern this pending case, with a fair opportunity for bounded submissions on genuinely new reliance elements. An expectation of immunity from unclear law would not preserve the displaced defense; final judgments and ordinary preservation and preclusion rules would remain. The proposal concerns this personal legal-uncertainty defense and materially equivalent claims, not function-specific absolute immunity, sovereign immunity, proper defendants, causation, injury or the availability of a cause of action. Failure of the proposed defense would establish neither a constitutional violation, damages, institutional liability nor a new cause of action; each independent requirement remains. These are separate views: the Court's mandate imposes no reliance burden, transition rule or additional submissions requirement.

**Ginsburg, joined by Stevens and Breyer, concurring in the judgment.** These Justices join the exact vacatur and open application remand while maintaining that Graham already tests the practical choices and reasonable mistakes available to an officer using force. They reject a second overlapping reasonableness shelter for force found objectively unreasonable under that complete standard. That objection is confined to this excessive-force setting; they retain ordinary Harlow protection elsewhere and do not endorse the Chief Justice's general actual-reliance replacement.

The supported unnecessary shared shove of a controlled, unresisting person cannot be dismissed by treating every degree of force as justified by the initial need for security removal. Johnson and Sterling prevent reappraisal of the participation evidence on this appeal. Plakas permits early merits judgment on a properly supported no-violation account, but its immediate armed attack without a contrary material account is absent here. The continuous movement into the van is relevant context, not a factual answer to the alleged unnecessary force. Their separate analysis does not alter their vote for the mandate: neither immunity nor denial, automatic trial, liability or damages is directed.

**Kennedy, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, dissenting.** These Justices would reverse and direct personal damages immunity while assuming a possible constitutional violation. Harlow and Anderson protect reasonable legal error independently of Graham's merits inquiry. Accepting the plaintiff-favorable two-officer account, they regard the carrying and van entry as a continuous urgent removal from a protected event; the particular boundary of this brief nondeadly force was not sufficiently apparent in September 1994. They recognize the strongest contrary point: a controlled, unresisting person's forceful van entry may lack additional demonstrated need. They nevertheless treat this as uncertainty over the degree of force during removal, not a legally unmistakable separate assault after the seizure ended.

Their position does not depend on deciding that Parker acted alone, finding resistance, declaring the force lawful or making injury a prerequisite. Hunter and Sterling preserve the adverse assumed facts; their disagreement concerns the legal conclusion drawn from them. They support an ordinary merits-first approach for the reasons of legal development Souter describes, subject to the actual scope of review. Their agreement with him on that position is not a join in the judgment-announcing opinion and supplies no controlling sequence rule. They reject an open application remand because they would decide the legal defense now.

### United States v. Mead Corp., No. 99-1434

Merits decision, June 18, 2001

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 8, 2000. On writ of certiorari to the Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit. The questions concern the judicial weight of Customs tariff-classification ruling letters and whether the bound-diary provision includes these ring-bound day planners. The Federal Circuit reversed judgment for the United States, rejected both controlling and persuasive weight and held the planners outside the challenged dutiable classification. The January 1993 headquarters letter changed earlier duty-free treatment and offered dictionary and commercial reasons.

All nine Justices participate.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela and Scalia.

The remand requires independent tariff construction with persuasive consideration of Customs' explanation. It directs no particular classification or monetary amount. The two dissenters would instead reverse and sustain the classification, on distinct grounds.

#### Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and II | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Supplies controlling interpretive rules | No controlling deference for this mechanism; independent judicial construction with Skidmore consideration |
| Opinion of the Court, Part III | Kennedy | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacates and remands | Corrected classification inquiry without a predetermined tariff answer |
| Concurrence in Parts I and II and dissent from the judgment | Stone-Zsela | No other Justice | Would reverse and sustain the classification | Independent reading of bound diary; remand only for implementation |
| Dissent | Scalia | No other Justice | Would reverse and sustain the classification | Controlling reasonable-choice treatment for the official agency interpretation |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding and operative rule:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Authority for Holdings I and II:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion have the eight direct supporters identified in the opinion table, including its author. Scalia does not join either proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the power to administer and classify does not make every administrative interpretation controlling law for a reviewing court. Haggar requires actual delegated authority and statutory fit before acceptance of a reasonable choice within genuine ambiguity. Its formally promulgated Customs regulation does not establish the same authority for the different mechanism here. Christensen distinguishes controlling authority from an advisory interpretation's useful reasoning without deciding that all informal actions have the same status.

Customs letters guide particular transactions, may be modified, issue from numerous offices and do not bind unrelated importers as a generally applicable regulation would. Their place in the protest and judicial-review scheme confirms that administrative operation is distinct from delegated interpretive lawmaking. Formal rulemaking or adjudication can be important evidence of delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite. Independent judicial factfinding likewise does not itself defeat lawful deference; Haggar remains intact.

The Solicitor General's brief, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, represents this as Customs' official position. The Court accepts that representation. It identifies whose interpretation is defended, but does not establish that Congress gave this ruling mechanism controlling legal effect. Headquarters' developed reasons and the responsible institution's endorsement strengthen the claim to consideration, not the statutory delegation. The 1993 action remains governed by its own legal regime; later procedures for changing rulings do not supply retrospective authority. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations are outside this grant.

##### II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding and operative rule:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an agency can offer useful reasons without possessing authority to bind the court's answer. Skidmore provides that separate inquiry, and Christensen preserves it when controlling statutory deference is unavailable. Customs addressed dictionary meaning, commercial usage and whether a ring mechanism binds pages. Those reasons bear on the actual tariff language and cannot be discarded by attaching a nonregulatory label to the letter.

Mead correctly points to the change from earlier duty-free treatment. That change may weaken a consistency claim; it does not eliminate every reason the agency offers. Expertise, national responsibility and official endorsement likewise do not automatically make the position persuasive. The court evaluates the explanation on its merits within the enacted tariff boundaries. Neither the fact that Customs changed course nor the fact that it administers a complex scheme decides the tariff question in advance. The distinction preserves meaningful independent judicial interpretation rather than converting persuasion into a lesser form of mandatory deference.

##### III. The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding and operative rule:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with the persuasive consideration required by Holding II. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Authority:** Part III has the seven direct supporters identified in the opinion table, including Kennedy as author. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the appellate judgment cannot stand on an inquiry that wholly excluded the permissible persuasive contribution of Customs' interpretation. The goods are described as ring-bound planners containing calendar and memorandum pages; no missing product fact supplies the reason for return. Nor is there a general entitlement to have a lower court decide every interpretive question first. The complete tariff question is presented, but neither proposed definitive construction commands a majority: independent construction sustaining the category and controlling acceptance of Customs' reasonable construction rest on distinct noncontrolling grounds. The seven-Justice disposition instead requires the corrected judicial inquiry without predetermining its answer.

The arguments remain concrete. Mead contends that removable pages and prospective scheduling are outside bound diaries. Customs answers that rings bind pages and that diaries include dated prospective entries. The reviewing court must decide the statutory meaning, consider the actual agency reasons and implement the resulting classification through ordinary tariff procedures. The disputed tariff meaning is genuinely undecided here for want of a majority construction; assessment calculations following the eventual lawful classification are ordinary implementation, not a second unresolved deference rule.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

#### Law After Decision

Materially equivalent Customs ruling letters do not command controlling Chevron treatment through this mechanism. Courts must independently interpret the tariff while giving the agency's actual reasons the persuasive consideration they earn. Other genuine delegations, including valid regulations governed by Haggar, retain their own authority. Official endorsement does not collapse the delegation and persuasion questions. Neither ultimate classification of these planners commands a majority; the governing judicial inquiry is settled, and the tariff answer returns for decision under it.

#### Separate Writings

Stone-Zsela joins Parts I and II but dissents from the judgment. He would independently classify the goods under the tariff's ordinary words. A diary may organize prospective appointments and daily affairs, rather than only retrospective narrative. Rings fasten pages into a bound volume despite removability; neither sewing, glue nor permanence is required. Calendar, note and appointment pages satisfy that description, and auxiliary telephone pages do not defeat it. Loose unattached sheets remain different from the assembled imported articles. Customs' dictionary and commercial reasons persuade but do not control. The narrower appellate reading adds restrictions absent from the text. With both the question and undisputed product features before the Court, he would sustain the bound-diary classification and remand only for entry-specific judgment, assessment or refund implementation, without fixing an amount.

Scalia dissents on a different ground. Congress's classification assignment, genuine tariff ambiguity and Customs' publicly confirmed official interpretation warrant controlling reasonable-choice treatment in his view. The Solicitor General's brief cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel establishes the official-position condition; the statutory scheme supplies delegation, not those signatures alone. Ring binding and dated calendar and note pages make the classification reasonable despite the residual category. He objects to the Court's less determinate delegation threshold and its substitution of open persuasive review, while declining to treat every employee opinion as authoritative. He would sustain Customs' classification and leave only ordinary assessment implementation. His controlling-deference ground does not join Stone-Zsela's independent textual construction or either Court deference proposition.

The petition presents both questions in substance: [Government petition for certiorari](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/united-states-v-mead-petition-writ-certiorari). The agency mechanism, merchandise and public representation of Customs' official position are reported in [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-533/pdf/USREPORTS-533-218.pdf), including the representation at page 258. That public endorsement is distinct from an original agency-head signature or a regulation. Later procedures for modifying rulings do not govern the legal character of the 1993 action.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Federal Circuit must reconsider the tariff classification through independent statutory construction that evaluates the actual persuasive force of Customs' explanation. Ordinary further proceedings follow within the applicable review structure; no new agency hearing, missing-signature search or future rulemaking is a condition of return. Any resulting assessment or refund depends on the lawful classification and the relevant entries and calculations. No monetary amount is awarded here.

# EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. — 2002-01-15

[Authoritative Record](../records/eeoc-v-waffle-house-inc_merits_2002-01-15.md)

## Event

EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc., No. 99-1823; 534 U.S. 279. Merits decision, January 15, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 2. Argued October 10, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit. The question is whether Baker's employment arbitration agreement prevents the EEOC from seeking individual relief in its own ADA action. Baker experienced a seizure, was discharged and filed a charge; disability discrimination remains alleged. The Fourth Circuit allowed prospective public relief but barred victim-specific remedies. The agency seeks reversal of that restriction.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The restriction on victim-specific relief is reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Restriction on victim-specific relief reversed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would vacate the FAA-based restriction and remand for consideration of any properly available independent state-law contract ground.

All nine reject FAA coverage and agree that actual recovery remains subject to statutory predicates and prevention of duplicate satisfaction. The Commission's public enforcement action continues; the majority permits pursuit of individual remedies without the dissenters' preliminary contract-enforceability inquiry.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Holdings

### Part I. The FAA supplies no enforcement ground for this employment agreement

**Holding and operative rule:** Circuit City's section 1 employment-contract exclusion covers Baker's employment arbitration agreement, so the FAA supplies no enforcement command or federal arbitration-policy ground for restricting this EEOC action or its remedies. The exclusion does not itself determine an independent state-law basis for enforcement between Baker and Waffle House.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins control this ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the governing employment exclusion resolves the antecedent FAA question. Circuit City excludes employment contracts within the commerce reach asserted for the Act, including nontransportation employment. Baker's agreement belongs to that category. A general federal preference for arbitration cannot replace statutory coverage. The Fourth Circuit's FAA-based restriction consequently cannot stand. Gilmer concerns a securities-registration agreement and does not supply a contrary answer about this employment contract. Whether another source binds the employee remains distinct from the FAA holding.

### Part II. The Commission's independent action retains its individual-remedy authority

**Holding and operative rule:** An employee's private agreement selecting arbitration does not bind the nonparty EEOC or categorically eliminate victim-specific remedies Congress authorizes in the Commission's own ADA enforcement action. Even if an independent state-law ground makes the promise enforceable against the employee, that fact alone neither makes the Commission a contracting party nor removes those federal remedy categories; this is an independently sufficient ground for rejecting the relief bar.

**Authority:** Part II of Justice Stevens's opinion is joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, seven direct votes. Scalia and Thomas withhold assent to this ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the agency enforces its own statutory cause, rather than an assignment of Baker's claim. Section 12117(a) gives the Commission the enumerated Title VII enforcement powers, procedures and remedies. Section 706(f)(1) authorizes its action after the statutory process; section 706(g) authorizes appropriate individual equitable relief; and section 1981a expressly includes the Commission among plaintiffs who may seek the specified damages. Compensation and reinstatement can advance that public enforcement function.

First Options supplies the actual-assent principle, but the statutory enforcement structure completes the answer. General Telephone recognizes the independent public action including individual relief, and Gilmer preserves an agency enforcement role alongside an individual's arbitration obligation. Baker could commit himself to a forum; he did not undertake a promise for the Commission, which never agreed to arbitrate.

The dissent's strongest objection concerns the beneficiary's valid commitments, not direct compulsion of the agency. It would condition individual recovery on a state-law-contract determination. But the statutory permission for appropriate relief does not create a categorical exception whenever an employee chose an arbitral forum. Enforceability against Baker alone does not diminish the agency's enacted remedies. This conclusion decides the live private-contract argument without resolving the contract's state-law enforceability or a hypothetical FAA-covered agreement. An actual adjudication, settlement, release or satisfaction differs from the forum promise presented here and requires its own record.

### Part III. Statutory conditions govern any actual award

**Holding and operative rule:** The EEOC's authority to seek relief does not establish liability or an entitlement to an award: the applicable disability, qualification, unlawful-discrimination, injury, causation and remedy predicates must be proved, and the court must apply each remedy's conditions below. The court must prevent duplicate satisfaction of the same injury when actual overlapping recovery is established; no completed arbitration, settlement, release or prior satisfaction is established here.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part III's ordinary statutory and nonduplication requirements. Scalia and Thomas separately advocate the additional contractual limitation rejected by Part II; their Part III join does not adopt Part II.

Great-West interprets a different ERISA provision and does not delete this express damages authorization. Employer size and the other damages predicates remain matters for ordinary adjudication.

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition on victim-specific relief is reversed, and the action is remanded for adjudication of liability and appropriate statutory relief. The Commission may pursue that relief without first establishing that Baker's private forum promise is unenforceable under state law.

### Asset succession alone does not create a new assignment category

A purchaser independently within an enumerated related-person class remains subject to the statute; the holding does not determine an independent contractual obligation or every contested meaning of successor.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, controls the complete statutory rule, its limits and the affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that financial responsibility must arise from the relationships Congress enacted. Section 9701(c)(2)(A) identifies a member of the signatory's controlled group under section 52(a), including the signatory; a trade or business under common control under section 52(b); and a person with a partnership interest or joint venture with a signatory in a coal-industry business employing eligible beneficiaries, except an interest held solely as a limited partner. The successor sentence refers to those listed classes rather than adding all direct successors of an original signatory. Subsection (c)(2)(B) measures the relationship on July 20, 1992, or immediately before an earlier cessation of business. The assignment priorities in section 9706(a) do not enlarge these classes.

Section 9706(b)(2) separately permits a post-enactment assignment transfer to a successor under its conditions, retaining the transferor and related persons as guarantors and requiring notice to the trustees. That provision does not automatically reach this earlier asset purchase. Russello gives effect to Congress's differing language; Ron Pair requires enforcement of the resulting text. The strongest objection is that reaching a related person's successor while excluding the direct successor creates an anomalous financing gap. But an imperfect allocation of statutory liability is not an absurdity authorizing a new liable-person class. Jericol's continuation of the business and agreement assumption do not establish the claimed statutory relationship. The contextual reading required by Chickasaw and J.E.M. does not replace the specific classes with general successor policy.

**Precedent treatment:** Russello and Ron Pair are applied to the differentiated categories; Chickasaw and J.E.M. retain contextual construction without supplying an omitted category.

### Clear statutory limits resolve this assignment theory

**Holding and operative rule:** The definite assignment classes cannot be enlarged by an administrative preference for preserving program revenue. Because that statutory limit resolves these assignments, the Court does not reach the unnecessary constitutional objections or decide an unestablished independent contractual ground.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Mead distinguishes an agency's interpretive authority from the persuasive force of its reasoning; neither permits liability contrary to clear statutory categories. Eastern Enterprises invalidated particular tier-three assessments without a controlling affirmative constitutional rationale. It did not invalidate the Coal Act generally and does not supply a constitutional shortcut here. The Court's statutory judgment resolves the challenged theory while leaving any genuinely independent obligation to its own legal predicates.

**Precedent treatment:** Mead's statutory boundary is applied; Eastern Enterprises is preserved at the scope of its judgment and independently majority-supported property proposition.

## Precedent Treatment

- Russello v. United States, 464 U.S. 16: different language in related provisions supports the enacted distinction.
- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S. 235: the statutory text governs despite a claimed policy anomaly.
- Chickasaw Nation v. United States, November 27, 2001, and J. E. M. Ag Supply, Inc. v. Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc., December 10, 2001: contextual reading preserves each provision's actual function; neither adds this successor category.
- United States v. Mead Corp., June 18, 2001: interpretive authority and persuasion remain distinct, and neither authorizes departure from clear liability conditions.
- Eastern Enterprises v. Apfel, June 25, 1998: the limited assessment judgment and absence of a controlling affirmative constitutional rationale remain; the Coal Act is not invalidated as a whole.

## Law After Decision

Direct asset succession to an original signatory is insufficient by itself for the challenged statutory assignments. Independently qualifying related persons, the separate prospective assignment-transfer mechanism and enforceable contractual obligations remain governed by their own conditions. This is a statutory coverage rule, not a general immunity for asset purchasers or a constitutional invalidation of the program.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by O'Connor and Breyer, dissenting.** The dissent would reverse and remand for application of an inclusive succession rule. Congress allocated existing health-benefit obligations across continuing business relationships. Reading the successor sentence in that integrated structure avoids the anomaly of reaching a related person's successor while exempting the closer direct successor that continued the coal business and assumed its agreement. The contextual treatment of successors in 1 U.S.C. sections 1 and 5, together with the labor-law succession principles illustrated by Wiley, Golden State Bottling and Fall River Dyeing, supports that reading; those authorities do not establish liability for every solvent purchaser.

The dissent recognizes that a differentiated list and a separate prospective transfer provision could reflect compromise. Its answer is that the entire succession structure supplies the context for that list, and the Court's separation defeats the allocation Congress made. Legislative explanations corroborate that construction rather than replacing the text. The dissent does not treat a bargaining discount as a proved fact, impose unrestricted federal common-law liability, or decide the constitutional question. Preserved successor-status and other application issues remain for the proper tribunal on remand.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit's judgment against these assignments stands. The statutory basis asserted in this review cannot support reassignment; any independent obligation must be established on its own terms.

## Holdings

### The named injury restriction bars the compensatory award, not every remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** Original 42 U.S.C. §1997e(e) bars an action by a prisoner confined at filing for mental or emotional injury suffered in custody without the required prior showing of physical injury. That restriction bars the actual distress compensation awarded here even though the underlying wrong is constitutional; it does not erase the violation or compensation-independent remedies.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this statutory holding in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress restricted recovery for a named injury category. Harris v. Garner establishes the confinement-at-filing trigger and the mental-or-emotional-injury limit; it does not convert that limit into immunity from every remedy. The jury found no physical injury, and the $3,650 award compensated mental or emotional harm. The religious source of the right does not exempt that recovery. Carey and Stachura also prevent compensation based merely on an abstract valuation of the constitutional right. The all-damages reading goes too far because it ignores the distinct functions of nominal recognition and punishment, while the contrary compensation argument cannot save a jury award for the expressly barred injury.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris is applied at its actual injury-and-filing scope; Carey and Stachura retain the distinction between a violation and compensable loss.

### Nominal damages recognize the established violation

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1997e(e) does not bar nominal damages recognizing an established constitutional violation without compensable injury. On the preserved violation and lack of compensable injury here, the plaintiff is entitled to one dollar.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Nominal relief does not compensate the mental or emotional injury named in the statute. Carey permits recognition of an established constitutional wrong without inventing injury or valuing the right itself as compensatory loss. The preserved liability verdict therefore requires the one-dollar award even though the distress compensation cannot stand.

**Precedent treatment:** Carey v. Piphus supplies nominal recognition; Stachura's prohibition on abstract-right compensation remains.

### Punitive eligibility survives, but the amount requires a lawful redetermination

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1997e(e) does not categorically bar punitive damages for conduct satisfying Smith v. Wade's evil-motive-or-intent or reckless-or-callous-indifference standard. Punishment remains discretionary even when that culpability is established; an amount expressly tied to invalid compensation must be redetermined independently, preserving separate liability and culpability findings.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this holding and remedy in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** Punitive damages punish culpable conduct and deter its repetition; they do not compensate distress. Smith supplies a distinct remedial predicate rather than a rule that punitive fault must invariably exceed every substantive violation standard. The jury separately made the required culpability finding, which survives the compensation defect. But the instruction expressly relied on the invalid compensatory award in setting the punitive amount. That linkage prevents preserving $42,500 as an independently sound calculation. The corrected proceeding concerns amount, without retrying established liability or punitive culpability. A positive punitive award is not compelled, punishment may not reproduce barred compensation, and no automatic punitive-to-nominal ratio applies. Fees must then be reconsidered in light of the lawful recovery.

**Precedent treatment:** Smith v. Wade's culpability requirement and discretionary punishment are applied; the distinct remedial functions recognized in Carey and Stachura prevent relabeling barred compensation.

### The preserved constitutional objection fails at this remedial scope

**Holding and operative rule:** Applying the physical-injury restriction to this distress award does not deny access to court or due process where the established right remains enforceable through nominal relief and punitive relief on its distinct predicates. This ruling resolves the preserved challenge in these circumstances, without validating every possible application of the statute.

**Authority:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer join this portion of the Opinion of the Court. Stone-Zsela agrees in its disposition only.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute restricts one injury-based recovery rather than withdrawing adjudication of the underlying right. Searles has established liability, receives nominal relief and retains punitive relief subject to its lawful standard. At this actual scope, the constitutional objection therefore supplies no reason to restore barred compensation or upset the preserved remedial distinctions.

**Precedent treatment:** Harris's bounded restriction and Carey and Smith's distinct remedies operate together; no remedy-wide immunity follows.

### United States v. Olano, No. 91-1306

Merits decision, 1993-04-26

#### Chronology and Posture

United States v. Olano; No. 91-1306; October Term 1992. Argued: 1992-12-09; decided 1993-04-26. Source locator: 507 U.S. 725 (1993). Ninth Circuit, 934 F.2d 1425 (1991). On writ of certiorari to that court.

Question: Whether the unobjected-to presence of instructed alternate jurors during deliberations permits reversal under Rule 52(b), and how error, plainness, substantial rights and remedial discretion differ.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority

**Controlling proposition:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of a forfeited legal error only if it is plain and affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, while mere failure timely to assert a right does not. Plain means clear or obvious under current law, and the defendant ordinarily must show prejudice affecting the outcome; errors first clarified on appeal, special outcome-independent categories and justified prejudice presumptions are reserved.

**Authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). This proposition commands a majority of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that failure to object changes the conditions of appellate relief without making a violated rule disappear. Johnson v. Zerbst supplies the distinction between intentional surrender and omission; whether a right can be waived and what participation or procedures waiver requires depend on the particular right. Young reads Rule 52(b) as a limited power, not two disjunctive routes based on either plainness or substantial rights. Its prejudice inquiry ordinarily examines the record, as harmless-error review does, but places persuasion on the defendant who forfeited the objection. This does not decide that every error demands a specific outcome showing. Fulminante preserves the distinction between trial errors and defects affecting the tribunal’s basic structure. Here the Government’s concessions permit the Court to assume error and plainness without resolving whether Rule 24(c) can be waived. They do not concede prejudice. The preserved constitutional error in Brecht arose under a different review posture and does not supply the burden here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458: applies intentional relinquishment; right-specific waiver requirements remain.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies limited plain-error authority and ordinary prejudice requirement.
- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: preserves structural/trial-error distinction without deciding every Rule 52(b) category.
- Brecht, April 21, 1993: distinguished; preserved Doyle habeas review does not control forfeited Rule 24(c) error.

##### Correction remains discretionary

**Controlling proposition:** Even when plain forfeited error affects substantial rights, correction under Rule 52(b) is permitted, not automatic, and should occur only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Actual innocence is not a necessary condition, but satisfaction of substantial rights alone does not satisfy this additional inquiry.

**Authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). This proposition commands a majority of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the word “may” leaves a separate remedial judgment to the reviewing court. Atkinson supplies its governing measure: the effect on the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Young carries that standard into Rule 52(b). Treating every prejudicial plain error as requiring correction would erase the discretion the Rule deliberately retains. The contrary argument that relief must always depend on actual innocence also fails. Innocence can make correction compelling, but serious injury to the administration of justice can exist independently of guilt. The collateral gateway addressed in Sawyer concerns a different procedural obstacle and does not rewrite this appellate rule. These distinctions prevent either an automatic reversal rule or an innocence-only restriction. Because respondents have not satisfied the preceding substantial-rights requirement, the Court does not decide whether this particular intrusion, had it been prejudicial, would warrant the exercise of remedial discretion.

**Precedent treatment:**

- United States v. Atkinson, 297 U.S. 157: applies the independent fairness, integrity and public-reputation standard.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies discretionary, circumscribed correction.
- Sawyer, June 22, 1992: distinguished; capital actual-innocence gateway is not a universal limit on appellate correction.

##### Instructed alternates do not establish prejudice by presence alone

**Controlling proposition:** For these alternates, instructed not to participate, mere presence during deliberations neither establishes prejudice nor warrants a presumption of prejudice under Rule 52(b). Without a specific showing of participation or a chilling effect, respondents have not shown an effect on substantial rights; the per se new-trial ruling must be reversed.

**Authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). This proposition commands a majority of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that deliberative privacy protects against improper influence, and the inquiry must attend to that purpose. Parker and Remmer examine intrusions for prejudice; Smith v. Phillips rejects a new trial merely because a juror encountered a potentially compromising situation. Those authorities do not make this Rule 24(c) violation harmless by definition, but they defeat treating every intrusion as automatically reversible. The alternates heard the same evidence, took the ordinary oath, and received an express direction not to participate. Richardson supports the ordinary assumption that jurors follow instructions. Respondents showed neither actual participation, including nonverbal influence, nor restraint of the regular jurors’ discussion, and the circumstances do not justify presuming either. The Court does not decide the Government’s burden had an objection been preserved. It also reserves what Rule 606(b) would permit at a hearing and whether an appellate court may order such a hearing on plain-error review; none was requested here.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Parker v. Gladden, 385 U.S. 363, and Remmer v. United States, 347 U.S. 227: apply the focus on prejudicial influence, preserving appropriate presumptions in other circumstances.
- Smith v. Phillips, 455 U.S. 209: applies rejection of automatic retrial for every compromising contact.
- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200: applies the ordinary instruction-following presumption, not an irrebuttable cure.
- Zafiro, January 25, 1993: consistent record-specific instruction analysis; no blanket rule that instructions cure intrusions.

#### Current-Law Effect

Before: Rule 52(b) and Atkinson/Young supplied discretionary correction without this complete articulation. After: four separate requirements govern forfeited-error review; ordinary defendant prejudice burden and the no-presumed-prejudice result for instructed alternates apply. Valid waiver, new clarity on appeal, structural categories and Rule 606(b)/hearing authority remain reserved. Brecht’s Chapman rule for preserved Doyle habeas error and Zafiro’s record-specific instruction analysis remain unchanged.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Kennedy joins the Court. He emphasizes that the defendant’s burden is the usual framework, not necessarily the formulation for an error noticed by the court on its own initiative. Rule 24(c) remains independently obligatory; trial judges should not put counsel in the position of resisting a proposed departure from the prescribed jury structure. A preserved objection would place the burden of showing harmlessness on the Government, which could face substantial difficulty. None of those observations permits withholding an objection and demanding automatic reversal later.

Stevens, joined by White and Blackmun, would affirm. Drawing on Vasquez v. Hillery and Gray v. Mississippi, he regards the privacy of deliberations as protecting the tribunal’s integrity independently of measurable verdict prejudice. He nonetheless distinguishes satisfaction of substantial rights from discretionary relief. A reasonable judge could decline reversal after considering the nature of the violation, counsel’s conduct and likely effect; his conclusion is that the Ninth Circuit did not abuse its broad supervisory discretion by granting relief here.
