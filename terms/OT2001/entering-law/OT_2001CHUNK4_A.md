## Standards and Tests selection: Objective appearance of judicial impartiality under §455(a)

### Objective appearance of judicial impartiality under §455(a)

**Governing question:** When judicial or outside conduct requires federal judicial disqualification.

**Current rule:** A judge must disqualify when an informed, detached observer considering relevant conduct and circumstances together would reasonably question impartiality. A reasonable conclusion that a fair and impartial hearing is unlikely suffices, without proof of actual bias or impossibility of fair judgment. Judicial origin is relevant context, not a bar or exceptional-intensity requirement; outside origin is neither necessary nor sufficient. Ordinary adjudicative views, adverse rulings, criticism and trial management alone do not establish improper partiality. Section 455(b)’s particular limits do not automatically narrow §455(a)’s independent appearance protection.

**Authority by component:**

- Single objective appearance standard across sources: Liteky v. United States — Section 455(a) applies the same objective appearance standard regardless of source, 1994-03-07. Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter. These five Justices adopt this rule at the same level of generality. Scalia, O'Connor, Thomas and Ginsburg agree with the judgment but do not join the ordinary-appearance formulation without their additional judicial-source requirement. No narrower-rationale aggregation is necessary.

**Present operation effective:** Liteky v. United States, 1994-03-07.

**Open question:** Section 144 procedure, independent §455(b)(1) claims and the remedy for an established violation remain unresolved. The four-Justice additional judicial-source threshold is not controlling.

**Holdings navigation:** Section 455(a) applies the same objective appearance standard regardless of source.

## Standards and Tests selection: Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

### Equitable vacatur after settlement moots review

**Governing question:** Whether mootness from voluntary settlement warrants vacating a judgment lawfully brought for review.

**Current rule:** Subsequent mootness prevents a merits decision but leaves authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of the judgment and proceeding. Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not establish entitlement to vacatur: the movant must supply a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances can warrant relief, but a settlement term requiring vacatur is not itself such a circumstance. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not command vacatur whenever review becomes moot.

**Authority by component:**

- Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment: Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment, 1994-11-08. Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.
- Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur: Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur, 1994-11-08. Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Present operation effective:** 1994-11-08; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** No heightened proof quantum or exhaustive list of exceptional circumstances is adopted. This settlement rule does not turn loss of review through circumstances outside the parties’ control into automatic vacatur.

**Holdings navigation:** Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment; Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur.

## Standards and Tests selection: Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

### Continuing individual controversy and legally unavailable damages

**Governing question:** Whether an individual former employee's challenge to an employment rule remains live after departure.

**Current rule:** A prospective individual challenge becomes moot after voluntary departure from covered employment when no intent to return, realistic recurrence or surviving claim is established. A general prayer cannot preserve the controversy by producing nominal damages against a State or an official-capacity defendant whom §1983 does not make a damages person. Consent to payment cannot create the missing cause of action, and certification of an unsettled state-law question cannot supply missing Article III jurisdiction.

**Authority by component:**

- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — A continuing personal controversy is absent, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the continuing-controversy and unavailable-damages limits
- Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona — Certification cannot restore the controversy, 1997-03-03; Stevens for all nine supplies the certification boundary

**Present operation effective:** Arizonans for Official English, 1997-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is no categorical express-pleading requirement for nominal damages and does not decide a legally available personal-capacity damages claim. Limited authority to correct lower-court jurisdictional error is distinct from merits authority; judgment vacatur remains equitable under the existing rule. No automatic initiative-sponsor standing or mandatory certification rule is established.

**Holdings navigation:** Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona (1997-03-03) — Federal Courts / Mootness, corrective jurisdiction and equitable vacatur.

## Standards and Tests selection: Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review

### Sequencing nonretroactivity and procedural default on habeas review

**Governing question:** The order of two nonjurisdictional habeas defenses.

**Current rule:** Teague nonretroactivity and state procedural default are distinct nonjurisdictional limits. No inflexible priority requires either always to precede the other; an accepted Teague question may be resolved while disputed default predicates remain reserved. This does not excuse default, permit bypass of an established jurisdictional defect or decide constitutional merits. An appellate court has no mandatory duty to raise an omitted state procedural-default defense; the defense is not a subject-matter-jurisdiction defect.

**Authority by component:**

- Lambrix v. Singletary — I. The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense, 1997-05-12; Stevens with Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven

- An appellate court has no mandatory duty to raise an omitted state procedural-default defense: Trest v. Cain, 1997-12-09; Stevens for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Trest, 1997-12-09, for the no-duty rule; Lambrix, 1997-05-12, retains the sequencing rule.

**Exceptions and limits:** The specific old-rule conclusion for the influential capital recommendation at November 24, 1986 finality is an application, not a universal exception to Teague. Preserved adequate, independent and unexcused state grounds remain enforceable.

**Holdings navigation:** Lambrix v. Singletary (1997-05-12) — The Court may decide the accepted nonretroactivity issue before the unresolved default defense. Trest v. Cain — Habeas procedural default and appellate initiative.

**Open question:** Trest leaves judicial authority to raise an omitted default, the proper exercise of any discretion, intentional waiver or forfeiture, actual default and applicable exceptions unresolved. Any lawful later consideration must address the actual ground with an appropriate opportunity to respond; no universal extra-briefing requirement is imposed.

## Standards and Tests selection: Loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice

### Loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice

**Governing question:** What instructions govern loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice?

**Current rule:** An omitted objection does not establish Strickland prejudice when its lost benefit is a substantive rule subsequently rejected, governing law recognizes no entitlement to that benefit, and the omission otherwise deprived the defendant of no fair or reliable sentencing protection. Ordinary reasonable-probability analysis remains; the usual claim acquires no additional unfairness requirement. Performance is assessed from counsel's perspective at the time. Teague does not require disregarding a subsequent legal correction favorable to the State for this limited prejudice inquiry. No actual-innocence prerequisite or general harmless-error rule follows.

**Authority by component:**

- 1. Loss of a displaced substantive rule is not cognizable prejudice here: Lockhart v. Fretwell — 1. Loss of a displaced substantive rule is not cognizable prejudice here, 1993-01-25. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt the rule and its application to the Collins objection.
- 2. Teague does not preserve the displaced rule for this prejudice inquiry: Lockhart v. Fretwell — 2. Teague does not preserve the displaced rule for this prejudice inquiry, 1993-01-25. O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by White, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; six Justices adopt this answer to the temporal objection.

**Present operation effective:** 1993-01-25; Lockhart v. Fretwell, for the components identified above.

**Holdings navigation:** Lockhart v. Fretwell, 506 U.S. 364 (1993).

## Standards and Tests selection: Mitigation investigation and prejudice in capital sentencing

### Mitigation investigation and prejudice in capital sentencing

**Governing question:** Counsel’s investigative obligation and the prejudice inquiry for omitted lawful capital mitigation.

**Current rule:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make an informed, reasonable decision that further inquiry is unnecessary. A later claim of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to pursue identified records; competent investigation does not require presenting every item it uncovers. Prejudice asks whether the complete supported aggravating and mitigating record creates a reasonable probability of a different lawful result sufficient to undermine confidence, rather than a more-likely-than-not outcome. Ordinary lawful-mitigation claims require no additional fundamental-unfairness showing under Fretwell. Where death requires a unanimous Virginia penalty jury and disagreement requires life, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death is enough; a merely possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy is not.

**Authority by component:**

- Reasonable-probability prejudice and Virginia’s lawful outcome: Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams) — II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia’s unanimity rule, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part I joined by all seven other Associates.
- Informed investigation requirement: Terry Williams — IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part II joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Present operation effective:** Terry Williams, April 18, 2000.

**Related current rules:** Loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice states Fretwell’s separate unavailable-benefit limit. State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254 supplies the independent federal habeas restrictions; a constitutional error finding does not dispense with them.

## Standards and Tests selection: Sentencing prejudice from additional imprisonment

### Sentencing prejudice from additional imprisonment

**Governing question:** Whether deficient representation must cause a minimum duration or number of offense levels of added imprisonment to establish prejudice.

**Current rule:** A sentencing-assistance claim requires deficient performance and a reasonable probability, sufficient to undermine confidence in the result, of a shorter lawful sentence with competent representation. No additional minimum number of months or offense levels applies. An attorney error or changed range alone does not establish the performance and causal-prejudice requirements; an unavailable substantive benefit remains outside cognizable prejudice under Fretwell.

**Authority by component:**

- Ordinary prejudice without duration or offense-level thresholds: Canonical Decision Record `Glover_v_United_States_merits_2001-01-09.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / No minimum duration or offense-level threshold for sentencing prejudice, 2001-01-09; Scalia for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Glover v. United States, 2001-01-09.

**Related current rules:** Loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice preserves Fretwell’s distinct exclusion of a benefit to which substantive law gives no entitlement.

**Holdings navigation:** [Glover v. United States, 531 U.S. 198 — Ineffective assistance at sentencing](HOLDINGS.md#glover-v-united-states-531-us-198).

## Standards and Tests selection: Involuntary antipsychotic medication of an objecting trial detainee

### Involuntary antipsychotic medication of an objecting trial detainee

**Governing question:** Justification for continuing antipsychotic medication over a pretrial detainee's objection and the consequence of unsupported treatment through trial.

**Current rule:** Upon the detainee's objection, the State must establish medical appropriateness and an overriding need sufficient to overcome the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. Clinical acceptability alone is insufficient. Medically appropriate treatment essential to safety remains possible after alternatives are considered. When unjustified compelled treatment creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not establish exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed; explanatory testimony about demeanor does not cure unmeasurable effects on testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel. The combination of unjustified treatment and that supported, unreconstructable trial risk requires a new trial; renewed compelled treatment requires adequate contemporaneous justification.

Where restoration of competency is an actual treatment purpose and no independently adequate safety justification exists, the judge must assess likely effects of the proposed regimen on comprehension, consultation, testimony, demeanor and defense presentation before authorizing medication. The Government must support the preliminary inquiry with available clinical evidence, reasonable alternatives and workable safeguards. Perfect prediction is unnecessary, but postponing the entire inquiry until treatment has occurred is insufficient; actual effects require renewed assessment before trial. Existing effective containment of immediate danger must be addressed as an actual alternative in any claimed safety justification, without making indefinite isolation constitutionally required.

**Authority by component:**
- Justification and alternatives: Riggins v. Nevada, “1. The State must justify antipsychotic medication continued over objection”, May 18, 1992; O'Connor Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter (six).
- Trial-risk consequence at the stated scope: Riggins, “2. This unsupported medication order requires a new trial,” May 18, 1992; O'Connor Part III, same six Justices.

- Safety justification and consideration of actual containment: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Weston_merits_2001-04-17.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The present safety justification does not support unwanted medication, 2001-04-17; all nine join O’Connor’s Part I.
- Preliminary defense-effects inquiry without independent sufficient safety justification: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Weston_merits_2001-04-17.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. The restoration purpose requires a preliminary assessment of likely defense effects on this record, 2001-04-17; seven direct joins to Part II.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. Weston, 2001-04-17, for the required preliminary defense-effects inquiry; Riggins’s underlying liberty rule and trial-risk consequence retain their earlier date.


**Exceptions and limits:** No universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula, documentary ritual or structural-error category is established. Harper's convicted-prisoner procedures are not automatically imported. Kennedy's extraordinary-showing proposal and Stone-Zsela's Chapman formulation are not Court rules.

**Open question:** The complete conditions sufficient to authorize restoration medication, including whether prosecution alone can ultimately justify it, remain unresolved. Weston supplies no majority rule for a materially different order with an independently sufficient safety basis. A competent defendant’s refusal that would produce incompetence and the unpreserved Eighth Amendment sentencing-display theory remain undecided. Immediate emergency treatment and treatment solely for execution are outside Weston’s holding.


**Holdings navigation:** Riggins v. Nevada, 504 U.S. 127 (1992) — Compelled medication and fair trial. [United States v. Weston — Involuntary pretrial medication and advance defense protection](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-weston).

## Standards and Tests selection: Special federal corporations as governmental actors

### Special federal corporations as governmental actors

**Governing question:** First Amendment responsibility of a corporation specially created and governed by the Federal Government.

**Current rule:** A corporation is governmental for First Amendment purposes when the Federal Government creates it by special law to advance governmental objectives and permanently retains power to appoint a majority of its governing directors. A statutory nonagency label cannot defeat that constitutional status, and ordinary managerial discretion does not require a separate external federal command for each challenged act. Charter, subsidy, regulation, governmental property, public benefit, a single appointment, temporary investment or present share ownership alone does not satisfy the combined rule.

**Authority by component:**

- Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental: Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental, 1995-02-21. Justice Scalia's constitutional-status portion, joined by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Eight Justices directly support the same permanent-majority-appointment rule and its application; Justice O'Connor's conditional view of a question she declines to reach is not a join.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-02-21; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule decides constitutional actor status, not the underlying speech claim, forum classification or relief. It supplies no universal agency, immunity, Treasury-liability, tort-liability or charter-jurisdiction classification.

**Holdings navigation:** Special federal creation and permanent majority appointment make Amtrak governmental.

## Standards and Tests selection: Private payment choices and governmental attribution

### Private payment choices and governmental attribution

**Governing question:** Whether statutory permission and public review mechanisms make a private compensation carrier’s initial withholding state action.

**Current rule:** Private carriers’ independent decisions to dispute treatment and withhold payment pending utilization review are not state action merely because the State permits that course, requires compensation coverage and supplies review machinery. Attribution requires an actual governmental basis of responsibility for the challenged choice, such as compulsion, significant encouragement, joint participation or an exclusively governmental function. Screening for proper invocation and assigning a reviewer do not by themselves substantively approve the medical objection or order the initial suspension.

**Authority by component:**

- Choice-specific attribution and limited significance of permission and review: Canonical Decision Record `american_manufacturers_v_sullivan_merits_1999-03-03.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier’s payment choice governmental, 1999-03-03; seven direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-03-03.

**Exceptions and limits:** The legislature’s own act, a public obligor, an official review decision or a distinct proved attribution basis requires its own inquiry. The ruling does not make all acts in the compensation system private.

**Holdings navigation:** [American Manufacturers Mutual Insurance Co. v. Sullivan — Statutory permission does not itself make the private carrier's payment choice governmental](HOLDINGS.md#statutory-permission-does-not-itself-make-the-private-carriers-payment-choice-governmental-1).

## Standards and Tests selection: Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

### Age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness

**Governing question:** What instructions govern age motivation, pension tenure and willfulness?

**Current rule:** ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate and have a determinative influence on the employment decision. Service-based pension interference alone does not establish it; deliberate use of pension status as an age proxy or mixed age and pension motivation may. Age-triggered vesting and disparate impact remain unresolved. A disparate-treatment violation is willful if the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether conduct violated the Act, for individual decisions as well as formal policies. Once shown, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive. A good-faith nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willful. Within the assumed McDonnell Douglas framework, the initial showing must support an inference of age discrimination; replacement by someone at least forty does not defeat it. A substantially younger replacement may support that inference, whereas an insignificant difference standing alone does not. No numerical threshold or indispensable replacement element is imposed when other evidence supplies the inference. Employer production of a lawful reason remains distinct from the employee’s ultimate persuasion burden; rejection of the explanation does not compel liability.

**Authority by component:**

- Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation: Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins — Service-based vesting is distinct from age motivation, 1993-04-20. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, I; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies: Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins — Thurston governs individual acts as well as formal policies, 1993-04-20. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, II; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.
- O’Connor — Parts I–II, replacement status and relative-age inference, 1996-04-01; all nine support protected-group nonexclusion, the positive relative-age proposition and retained burdens; the eight Associates alone adopt insignificant-gap-alone insufficiency.

**Present operation effective:** O’Connor, April 1, 1996, for the replacement inference; Hazen Paper’s causation and willfulness rules survive.

**Holdings navigation:** Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, 507 U.S. 604 (1993); O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., 517 U.S. 308.

**Open question:** Wholesale application of McDonnell Douglas to every ADEA claim, alternative reduction-in-force formulations, particular small-gap records and the force of additional evidence remain unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: After-acquired misconduct and age-discrimination remedies

### After-acquired misconduct and age-discrimination remedies

**Governing question:** The effect of misconduct discovered after an allegedly age-discriminatory discharge.

**Current rule:** Misconduct unknown to the employer at discharge does not erase an ADEA violation or categorically eliminate all relief. The employee retains the liability burden. To limit relief, the employer must prove misconduct so serious that it would in fact have discharged the employee for that reason alone, not merely that discharge would have been permissible. Once proved, reinstatement and front pay are ordinarily inappropriate; backpay’s starting measure runs from unlawful discharge to actual discovery, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances affecting either party’s legitimate interests.

**Authority by component:**

- Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation: Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation, 1995-01-23. O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt this proposition and explanation; it is a direct majority holding.
- Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief: Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief, 1995-01-23. O’Connor’s opinion for the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. All nine adopt the proof requirement and bounded remedial rule.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-01-23; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Actual age motivation, ultimate persuasion and separate willfulness requirements remain unchanged. A litigation assumption of discrimination does not establish liability.

**Holdings navigation:** Later-discovered misconduct does not erase an earlier ADEA violation; Proven independently sufficient misconduct limits appropriate relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change

### Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change

**Governing question:** What instructions govern medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change?

**Current rule:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii) commands suitable retroactive corrective adjustments when aggregate reimbursement for a provider and period proves inadequate or excessive. The Secretary may construe that command as reconciling interim payments to sums payable under lawful reasonable-cost methods and ceilings, without an individualized exception whenever actual costs exceed the ceiling. Authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods remain. Interpretive change can reduce weight without automatically invalidating a considered permissible construction; text, delegation and reasons must be examined. Unsupported litigation positions neither replace required agency explanation nor gain automatic controlling weight. A changed interpretation is not disqualified merely by statutory age or a litigation setting, but unexplained reversal or disregard of legitimate reliance may be arbitrary and capricious. For antecedent transactions lacking clear prior agency guidance, a court may use the agency’s current authoritative statutory interpretation without backdating the regulation’s effective date.

**Authority by component:**

White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- A changed agency interpretation is not automatically disqualified: Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala — A changed agency interpretation is not automatically disqualified, 1993-06-07. White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.
- Smiley — Part III-A, permissible explained change, 1996-06-03; all eight Associates; Stone-Zsela does not join.
- Smiley — Part V, qualified antecedent interpretive use, 1996-06-03; all eight Associates; the absence of clear contrary guidance is independently established in Part III-B.

**Present operation effective:** Smiley, June 3, 1996, for qualified antecedent interpretive use; Good Samaritan’s Medicare rule remains unchanged.

**Holdings navigation:** Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, 508 U.S. 402 (1993); Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., 517 U.S. 735.

## Standards and Tests selection: Controlling weight for a responsible agency's fair regulatory interpretation

### Controlling weight for a responsible agency's fair regulatory interpretation

**Governing question:** Judicial treatment of a responsible agency's interpretation of its own regulation, including a construction presented in an amicus brief.

**Current rule:** The responsible agency's fair and considered interpretation controls unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation. Courts must assess the reading against the operative text, lawful allocation of interpretive responsibility and supported agency treatment. An amicus presentation neither disqualifies the construction nor establishes its correctness. Interpretive authority does not permit amendment of contrary text, unsupported litigation rationalization, automatic authority for an agency lawyer or deference to unproved employer-specific factual assertions.

**Authority by component:**

- Auer v. Robbins — The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds, 1997-02-19; Kennedy Part III, joined by all seven other Associates (eight); Stone-Zsela's replacement proposal is noncontrolling

**Present operation effective:** Auer, 1997-02-19, for the amicus and factual-assertion boundaries.

**Related current rules:** Authoritative interpretive Guidelines commentary preserves its separate statutory and constitutional limits. Medicare educational costs shifted from a related institution remains the distinct substantive reimbursement rule.

**Holdings navigation:** Auer v. Robbins (1997-02-19) — The Secretary's fair and considered regulatory interpretation controls within its legal bounds.

## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation](HOLDINGS.md#trade-court-factfinding-does-not-eliminate-lawful-delegated-interpretation-1). [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).


**Related current rules:** Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation governs independent judicial consideration when controlling force is unavailable.

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

- Required persuasive consideration of tariff-letter reasons: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the Customs-letter application and specified persuasive inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).

## Standards and Tests selection: An agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision

### An agency interpretation necessarily embodied in its decision

**Governing question:** Whether an agency's decision contains its own sufficient statutory ground when later counsel states that ground in more explicit words.

**Current rule:** A reviewing court may recognize an interpretation necessarily embodied in the agency's own decision even when the agency did not use the exact words later employed by counsel. The actual decision must disclose that operative premise. This permits recognition of the agency's ground, not judicial invention of a new ground, missing findings or a substitute policy; appellate rationalizations cannot sustain a rationale absent from the agency decision.

**Authority by component:**
- Discernible-agency-ground boundary: National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., March 25, 1992, holding 2, “The agency's discernible ground”; Kennedy for Stone, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia and Souter, applying Chenery while preserving State Farm.

**Present operation effective:** National Railroad Passenger Corp., March 25, 1992.

**Holdings navigation:** National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp., 503 U.S. 407 (1992) — Passenger-rail acquisition and agency review.

## Copied opening holding: state/holdings/administrative-law.md


#### Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, 508 U.S. 402 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2079.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

##### Corrective adjustments operate within the authorized cost-method system

**Holding:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii) requires suitable retroactive corrective adjustments when, for a provider and fiscal period, aggregate reimbursement produced by the methods proves inadequate or excessive; the Secretary permissibly construes that command as reconciliation of interim reimbursement to the amount payable under lawful reasonable-cost methods and limits. The clause does not require an individualized exception whenever actual costs exceed a ceiling, and authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods remain intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: traditional tools followed by permissible construction of remaining ambiguity applied.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204: unauthorized retroactive rulemaking distinguished; its holding does not decide this clause’s meaning.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, March 9, 1992: text/coexistence principle preserved; the dispute concerns what these provisions require, not implied repeal.

##### A changed agency interpretation is not automatically disqualified

**Holding:** An agency’s change of statutory interpretation may reduce the weight accorded its view, but change alone does not make a considered, otherwise permissible construction unlawful. The Court must examine the text, delegated setting and reasons; an unsupported litigation position cannot substitute for the legally required agency explanation or obtain automatic controlling weight.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The hospitals concede no applicable regulatory exemption or exception. Independent challenges to unlawful reimbursement methods remain available outside this review; no automatic actual-cost escape or replacement of Chevron is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm; no unconditional individualized reimbursement above otherwise valid ceilings or particular additional payment is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- FEC v. Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, 454 U.S. 27: reasonable agency interpretation within the statutory delegation applied.
- INS v. Cardoza-Fonseca, 480 U.S. 421: judicial interpretation and the significance of inconsistent agency positions preserved.
- Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204: unsupported litigation rationalizations receive no automatic controlling weight; change itself is a different issue.


## Copied opening holding: state/holdings/administrative-law.md


#### Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A., 517 U.S. 735

**Docket or dockets:** 95-860
**Decided:** June 3, 1996

##### Does interpretive change or a rule's development during litigation categorically defeat deference?

**Holding:** A lawful, adequately explained interpretation within delegated statutory ambiguity does not lose eligibility for deference merely because it changes or addresses an old statute during litigation. Unexplained reversal or disregard of legitimate reliance may be arbitrary and capricious; the holding independently rejects the categorical inconsistency objection without approving every change.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-A, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight-Justice controlling majority. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment and Parts I, II, IV and VI but does not join Parts III-A, III-B or V. Parts III-A and III-B are independently sufficient answers to the asserted prior-policy barrier; they are not combined into a narrower rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** Good Samaritan permits reasoned change within lawful authority while requiring statutory fit, adequate explanation and attention to legitimate reliance. Neither statutory age nor litigation impetus supplies a categorical exclusion. The OCC formally proposed its interpretation on March 3, 1995, received 112 comment letters, and explained its response and grounds in the final rule published February 9, 1996. That process differs from a position invented by litigation counsel. The Court relies on the agency's stated clarification of uncertainty, not a judicially invented economic or reliance finding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Those procedural facts do not certify compliance with every rulemaking statute. Permission to change an interpretation does not itself authorize retrospective alteration of completed conduct's legal consequences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Good Samaritan permits explained interpretive change while retaining authority, statutory-fit and reliance limits. Bowen continues to reject automatic weight for unsupported litigation rationales.

##### Did the earlier letters establish clear official guidance contrary to this rule?

**Holding:** Independently, the identified 1964 and 1988 letters did not establish clear, settled official guidance contrary to the current interpretation; their existence does not itself make the formal rule an unexplained reversal of a binding prior position. This is a second independently sufficient answer to the claimed prior-policy obstacle.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part III-B, joined by the same seven other Associate Justices; eight-Justice majority. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The 1964 informal automobile-financing statement listed late charges as noninterest items, while the 1988 deputy-counsel response used a home-state-definition approach. Their authority and context differ, and they conflict on whether federal or state law defines the category. They therefore do not establish the clear prior interpretation asserted. The formal rule's explanation that it resolves uncertainty supplies its own reason for a federal definition. Good Samaritan requires attention to agency reasons without treating every informal formulation as binding; Bowen's rejection of unsupported litigation positions does not erase the separately published rule's own explanation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This conclusion concerns these documents' legal force, not a factual finding about any borrower's actual reliance or a general rule that informal agency materials never matter.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Good Samaritan's reason-giving requirement remains; Bowen's bar against unsupported litigation rationales is distinguished from the independently explained formal interpretation.

##### May a court use the current authoritative interpretation for these antecedent transactions?

**Holding:** In determining statutory meaning for antecedent transactions that lacked clear prior agency guidance, a court may use the current authoritative interpretation without backdating the rule's effective date. The materially different case of a later interpretation displacing clear earlier guidance remains reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part V, joined by the same seven other Associate Justices; eight-Justice majority. Stone-Zsela does not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The issue is what section 85 means for these transactions. Part III-B establishes that the cited letters supplied no clear contrary guidance; Part III-A's general allowance for reasoned change would not alone establish retrospective permissibility. Bowen bars unauthorized retroactive legislative rulemaking but does not require a court to disregard current authoritative statutory interpretation in these circumstances. An objection based on displacement of a clear prior interpretation could have substance and is not decided.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The April 1, 1996 effective date remains unchanged. The rule has no special transition or retroactivity clause; no settled judgment is reopened, no actual-reliance finding is made, and no waiver or preservation ruling resolves the objection. The Court gives no universal permission to alter completed conduct's legal consequences.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Bowen's prohibition of unauthorized retroactive legislative rulemaking remains; qualified judicial use of current statutory interpretation without clear prior guidance is distinguished.

##### Does merits deference resolve independent section 43 procedure objections?

**Holding:** Deference to the substantive statutory interpretation does not excuse an established procedural violation or adjudicate every independent rule-validity challenge. Section 43's coverage, advance procedure, final-publication duty and differently scoped exceptions remain operative, without a finding of applicability, compliance, violation, preservation or an exception here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), Part VI, joined by all eight other Justices; unanimous controlling qualification.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 43 covers agency opinion letters or interpretive rules, issued on request or on the agency's initiative, concluding that federal law preempts application to a national bank of state community-reinvestment, consumer-protection, fair-lending or intrastate-branch law, and the specified section 36(f)(1)(A)(ii) determinations. Subsection (a) requires advance Federal Register notice describing the issues and each state law, at least thirty days for written comment, and consideration of comments received. Subsection (b) separately requires publication of the specified final action.

Subsection (c)(1) exempts the entire section where essentially identical preemption issues were previously resolved by courts or addressed in an agency opinion or interpretive rule, or where a response concerns a request with no significant legal basis for a preemption determination. Subsection (c)(2) exempts the entire section for materials prepared for judicial proceedings, submission to Congress or a Member, or intragovernmental use. Subsection (c)(3) exempts only advance subsection (a) procedure: it requires a written necessity determination to avoid a serious and imminent threat to a national bank's safety and soundness, or concerns an opinion or rule connected to acquisition of banks in statutory default or danger of default, or an acquisition receiving FDIC assistance under section 1823(c). Final publication remains required under that exception.

Institutional default requires official custodial appointment. Danger of default requires the specified agency advice or determination of inability to meet demands or obligations, or depletion of substantially all capital, with no reasonable prospect of recovery without federal assistance. Borrower delinquency supplies none of these institutional conditions. Published proposals and received comments do not prove every statutory requirement; interpretive form, later use in litigation, section 93a authority and lack of clear prior guidance prove neither exemption nor compliance. Distinguishing substantive classification from its preemptive consequence does not categorically remove section 43.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No independently dispositive procedural defect is established; missing documentary proof creates no presumption for either party or alternative disposition. The Court does not decide every paragraph of the rule or every potential procedural challenge.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The California judgment is affirmed, 9-0, without agency or evidentiary remand, a special transition rule or newly ordered agency action. The confined section 85 classification and state-law displacement appear under Business and Commercial Law, National-bank interest classification and interstate permission.

#### Christensen v. Harris County, 529 U.S. 576 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1167.
**Decided:** May 1, 2000.

##### II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight

**Holding:** The supported advisory opinion letter lacks the force of law and does not command controlling statutory deference under Chevron. Its interpretation remains entitled to consideration under Skidmore according to the quality of its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force; the specific-consent restriction is unpersuasive for the statutory reasons the Court adopts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II has five votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. Scalia's agreement with the judgment does not support this ground.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The holding concerns the established advisory status and reasoning of this letter, not every informal agency interpretation. No unprovided endorsement, internal delegation or changed policy is assumed.

##### III. Regulatory interpretation cannot turn optional agreement terms into a mandatory condition

**Holding:** Auer does not authorize adding a specific-consent requirement for compulsory use to section 553.23(a)(2)'s unambiguous permission to include compatible preservation, use or cashout terms. The responsible agency may interpret its regulation fairly within the text, but may not amend that permission into the additional prohibition asserted here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has six votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Auer requires a responsible agency's fair and considered reading that is not plainly erroneous or inconsistent with its regulation. This decision imposes no universal rulemaking-format prerequisite and does not decide the Secretary's authority to adopt a valid future rule restricting compulsory use.

**Operative remedy or transition:** These grounds sustain the defense judgment on the categorical FLSA claim without ordering an agreement hearing, agency amendment or new award.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron is distinguished at the threshold of this letter's supported advisory authority; the Court does not treat every agency statement as binding.
- Skidmore remains an independent basis for persuasive consideration, including where controlling statutory weight is unavailable.
- Auer retains fair, responsible interpretation within regulatory text; it permits no amendment of the unambiguous permission at issue.
- NationsBank and Smiley retain their actual delegated and authoritative administrative constructions; their holdings do not automatically give this advisory response the same legal force.
- Boston & Maine preserves an agency's actual discernible ground and forbids judicial invention of its reasoning or authority.
- FDA and U.S. West retain distinct inquiries into statutory authority, permissible interpretation and independent legal constraints; neither eliminates this statute's implementing grant or makes it unlimited.

For the compensatory-time use holding in Christensen, May 1, 2000, No. 98-1167, see Labor and Employment — Public compensatory time and compulsory paid use.

#### United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1434.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** The revisable, transaction-specific letters issue from many offices and do not govern unrelated importers as regulations do. Their place in protest and judicial review shows administration rather than delegated interpretive lawmaking. The Solicitor General's representation, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, establishes Customs' official position but does not establish congressional delegation to this mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Formal rulemaking and adjudication are evidence of delegation, not exclusive prerequisites. A label or independent judicial factfinding does not alone defeat lawful deference. Later ruling-change procedures do not retroactively govern the 1993 action. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations remain outside the grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

##### Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** Customs' dictionary, commercial-usage and ring-binding reasons must be evaluated. Changed treatment may weaken consistency without eliminating all persuasive force; expertise and official endorsement likewise establish neither persuasion nor the answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Skidmore consideration is independent judgment, not a lesser mandatory deference or fixed weight. No choice between bound diaries and the residual duty-free category is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.

##### The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with consideration of the ruling letter's reasons according to their actual persuasive force under Skidmore. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither definitive classification commands a majority. The return rests on exclusion of permissible agency reasoning, not a missing product fact, a general right to first lower-court interpretation or a need for agency action. The distinct noncontrolling textual and deference grounds for sustaining the category do not combine into a Court construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit must independently classify the planners while considering the actual persuasive force of Customs' reasons. Any assessment or refund depends on that classification and entry calculations; no amount, new hearing, signature search or future rulemaking is required.

#### Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams), 529 U.S. 420 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-6615.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure

**Holding:** Section 2254(e)(2) is triggered by deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel in developing the claim's state-court factual basis; an incomplete record alone is insufficient. When triggered, the applicant must show either a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive by this Court on collateral review or a factual predicate previously undiscoverable through due diligence, and must also show by clear and convincing evidence that, but for constitutional error, no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Material application:** Reasonable effort is measured against known leads and available procedures. A generic development request is neither categorically sufficient nor categorically deficient; the public existence of a document is not an informative lead by itself. This post-enactment application falls within section 2254(e)(2).

##### II. The known psychiatric report triggers the statutory bar

**Holding:** Counsel's failure to pursue an identified psychiatric report through reasonably available means, after receiving a transcript repeatedly referring to it, constitutes deficient diligence here. Williams's conceded inability to satisfy section 2254(e)(2)(B) therefore forbids the requested hearing on that report claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Justice Ginsburg's opinion is joined by all seven other Associate Justices and controls with eight votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Failure to pursue the identified report is not a new Brady merits determination or a finding of bad faith, and it is not attributed to the two relationship claims. The conceded failure to satisfy the cumulative innocence condition makes other exception questions unnecessary.

##### III. Unknown relationships do not establish a neglected available lead

**Holding:** The applicant did not fail to develop the juror-relationship and prosecutor-nondisclosure claims where counsel lacked a lead to the concealed relationships, reasonably sought investigative assistance, and the State opposed and the court denied that assistance. The public existence of a divorce record does not by itself establish deficient diligence, so section 2254(e)(2) does not bar factual development of these claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** All eight Associate Justices join Part III of Justice Ginsburg's opinion.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Statutory hearing eligibility does not establish bias, dishonesty, constitutional misconduct or entitlement to a writ. Independent lawful grounds may resolve either claim without immaterial factfinding; remaining hearing and merits predicates still apply.

##### IV. Cause is established only for the specified concealed-fact nonpresentation

**Holding:** The concealed relationships, lack of an informative lead, reasonable unsuccessful investigative efforts and later unavailability of state relief establish cause insofar as default consists of failing to present these two relationship-based claims in the Virginia proceeding. That conclusion does not excuse a different default or establish prejudice; the appropriate prejudice standard and its application remain for the lower courts.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Justice Ginsburg's opinion has the support of all eight Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees with rejection of the lower no-cause ground without joining this reasoning.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Exhaustion, actual independent and adequate state default, section 2254(d)'s state-merits predicate, section 2254(e)(1)'s factual presumption and clear-and-convincing rebuttal burden, and ordinary hearing requirements remain distinct. Section 2254(b)(2) permits denial of an unexhausted claim on the merits but does not require it; unavailable or ineffective state process and express State-counsel waiver have their own conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The report-hearing denial is affirmed, 8–1. The eight-Associate Court sets aside the relationship claims' diligence barriers; all nine support removing those barriers and the specified no-cause ground, although Stone-Zsela seeks further diligence inquiry and joins no cause rationale. Remaining default, prejudice and hearing issues return for adjudication. The separate plea-agreement disposition and identified unreviewed rulings stand; no writ or universal hearing command issues.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Keeney and Townsend retain their deliberate-bypass former-law hearing rule. The later statute changes the consequence of deficient diligence in applications it governs; the Court does not rewrite Keeney's prior holding.
- Lindh is applied for provision-specific temporal reach; this post-enactment application is governed by section 2254(e)(2).
- Coleman remains controlling for an actual adequate and independent state claim default; hearing eligibility does not eliminate it.
- Vasquez retains the distinction between supplementing an exhausted claim and fundamentally altering it into a new claim.
- Weeks preserves the actual state-merits predicate and independent statutory conditions for relief; no general construction of section 2254(d)(1) beyond that holding is supplied here.
- Brady supplies the alleged disclosure duty, but neither that allegation nor the hearing ruling establishes suppression or materiality on the report claim.


## Copied opening holding: state/holdings/federal-courts-habeas.md


#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

##### VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. On this record the state court's nominal ordinary-probability discussion does not furnish an adequate independent ground, so this contrary-rule conclusion supplies a separate statutory basis for the same constitutional review and sentence relief.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland is applied to professionally reasonable investigation and ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice on the entire supported sentencing record; neither deficiency alone nor additional mitigation automatically establishes prejudice.
- Fretwell is applied according to its already controlling limitation: the lost benefit of a rejected substantive rule is distinct from ordinary lawful-mitigation prejudice. No additional unfairness requirement is permitted in this usual claim.
- Lockett is applied to the legal relevance of individual mitigation; it does not require indiscriminate presentation of every item.
- Eddings is applied to lawful consideration of mitigating background; it does not assign that evidence automatic dispositive weight.
- Skipper is applied to the relevance of supported adjustment to confinement; it supplies no finding of lifelong confinement or future nonviolence.
- Lindh and Carriger retain the application-date limits of their respective pre-enactment holdings; this later federal filing falls outside those premises.
- Weeks is applied to the independence of statutory eligibility, actual constitutional error and remedy; its reserved comprehensive statutory construction is resolved only to the scope of the present majority propositions.
- Smith v. Robbins is distinguished at its missing appellate-assistance function; it does not presume prejudice from defective sentencing investigation.
- Flores-Ortega is distinguished at its causal loss of an appeal; contextual performance remains required, and the absence of a probable-appellate-success prerequisite there does not remove actual sentencing-prejudice review here.

#### Liteky v. United States, 510 U.S. 540

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-6921.

**Decided:** 1994-03-07.

##### Section 455(a) applies the same objective appearance standard regardless of source

**Holding:** A federal judge must disqualify when an informed, detached observer, considering the relevant conduct and circumstances together, would reasonably question the judge's impartiality; a reasonable conclusion that a fair and impartial hearing is unlikely suffices, without proof of actual bias or impossibility of fair judgment. Judicial origin is relevant context, neither a bar to disqualification nor an added exceptional-intensity requirement, and outside origin is neither necessary nor sufficient; ordinary adjudicative views, adverse rulings, criticism and trial management do not alone establish improper partiality, while §455(b)'s particular limitations do not automatically narrow §455(a)'s independent appearance protection.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter. These five Justices adopt this rule at the same level of generality. Scalia, O'Connor, Thomas and Ginsburg agree with the judgment but do not join the ordinary-appearance formulation without their additional judicial-source requirement. No narrower-rationale aggregation is necessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Liljeberg v. Health Services Acquisition Corp., 486 U.S. 847 (1988): applied to objective appearance and the independent operation of §455(a); its distinct civil remedial inquiry is preserved.
- United States v. Grinnell Corp., 384 U.S. 563 (1966): its protection of legitimate adjudicative impressions remains effective; its personal-bias discussion does not impose a categorical source bar or mandatory impossibility threshold under §455(a).

##### The reported conduct, considered together, does not require disqualification

#### U.S. Bancorp Mortgage Co. v. Bonner Mall Partnership, 513 U.S. 18

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-714.
**Decided:** November 8, 1994.

##### Mootness ends merits review but leaves authority to dispose of the judgment

**Holding:** When a judgment has been lawfully brought before the Court for review, subsequent mootness bars adjudication of the underlying merits but does not eliminate the Court’s authority under 28 U.S.C. §2106 to make a just disposition of that judgment and the pending proceeding. Section 2106 permits appropriate relief; it does not require vacatur merely because review has become moot.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Walling v. James V. Reuter, Inc.*, 321 U.S. 671 (1944), applies the distinction between inability to complete merits review and authority to make an appropriate disposition of the judgments before the Court.
- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), confirms §2106 dispositional authority when appellate merits review becomes unavailable; it does not make every available disposition mandatory.
- *Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America*, 511 U.S. 375 (1994), is distinguished: jurisdiction over a new settlement-enforcement controversy is different from disposition of a judgment already lawfully under review.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court neither enforces the settlement nor supplies a ruling on bankruptcy priority, plan confirmation or the former automatic-stay dispute. The authority under §2106 to make an equitable disposition of a judgment lawfully before an appellate court differs from authority to reopen a completed judgment after the ordinary review process has ended. This decision does not create a new collateral reopening procedure, displace independently applicable Rule 60 requirements, or make a settlement agreement itself sufficient grounds for vacatur.

##### Voluntary settlement ordinarily does not justify vacatur

**Holding:** A party that voluntarily settles while pursuing review does not ordinarily establish equitable entitlement to vacatur of the judgment it sought to overturn; the movant must show a further sufficient equitable reason. Exceptional settlement circumstances remain possible, but a settlement term calling for vacatur is not itself such a circumstance; Bancorp’s showing establishes none, so vacatur is denied.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *United States v. Munsingwear, Inc.*, 340 U.S. 36 (1950), preserves equitable relief when review is frustrated by happenstance; its actual refusal to erase an unchallenged judgment does not create automatic vacatur after settlement.
- *Karcher v. May*, 484 U.S. 72 (1987), extends its reason for denying vacatur after voluntary abandonment of review to the voluntary settlement here, while preserving its distinct party-capacity setting.
- *Izumi Seimitsu Kogyo Kabushiki Kaisha v. U.S. Philips Corp.*, 510 U.S. 27 (1993), remains a threshold decision that left settlement vacatur undecided. Its separate vacatur discussion supplies no earlier controlling rule.
- *Digital Equipment Corp. v. Desktop Direct, Inc.*, 511 U.S. 863 (1994), remains a collateral-order decision; it does not establish a private right to erase a judgment following settlement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mootness caused by circumstances beyond the movant’s choice, including unilateral action of the prevailing party, remains distinct. No categorical ban on exceptional settlement relief or universal rule of collateral estoppel is adopted. No Rule 60(b) relief or remand to seek it is ordered.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacatur is denied and the Supreme Court proceeding dismissed as moot. The Ninth Circuit judgment remains undisturbed without endorsement of its bankruptcy merits; no other order or underlying Chapter 11 proceeding is vacated or dismissed, and no settlement-enforcement or Rule 60(b) remand is ordered.

#### Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.

**Docket or dockets:** Seventh Circuit No. 96-1136; review of 90 F.3d 1237.

**Decided:** 1997-06-02.

##### Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question

**Holding:** A federal court cannot assume disputed Article III jurisdiction to decide the contested merits of this EPCRA reporting claim. Section 11046(c) describes enforcement and relief without making every statutory liability element jurisdictional; the holding preserves legitimate exceptional threshold sequences and does not prescribe an exhaustive order for all nonmerits issues.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part I, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins, including O'Connor and Kennedy's nonexhaustive sequencing reservation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether EPCRA authorizes suit over reporting violations cured before filing remains undecided. Norton and Avrech retain their distinct settings involving already-controlled merits.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co., No. 98-470, May 17, 1999: resolves the bounded nonmerits sequencing question by permitting straightforward personal-jurisdiction dismissal before a difficult subject-matter inquiry, subject to the stated state-law and statutory limits; the contested-merits prohibition remains unchanged.

##### Requested relief cannot redress this completed informational deprivation

**Holding:** Assuming past informational injury and traceability, Article III requires requested relief likely to repair that injury or prevent a supported continuing or imminent personal injury. When reports were current before suit and no continuing or imminent violation is alleged, a declaration of past lateness, Treasury penalties, monitoring, future report copies and litigation expenses supply no such redress.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six direct joins. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg support dismissal on statutory grounds without joining this constitutional rationale.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes rather than rejects informational injury. Standing is absent at commencement, rather than lost by later cessation. Deterrent penalties may redress a supported threatened injury; annual reporting duties alone do not allege one. Section 11046(f) permits appropriate prevailing-or-substantially-prevailing-party costs on a final order, not independent compensation. A properly presented replacement-information loss claim, monitoring in a live action, recoverable prefiling expenses, associational requirements and statutory temporal reach remain open. Section 11022(f) gives the fire department no transferable automatic inspection entitlement for this plaintiff.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand with directions to dismiss, unanimously as to judgment. No merits relief, standing discovery, amendment entitlement or newly inferred recurrence inquiry is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Bell v. Hood:** Applied to distinguish a substantial federal claim from its ultimate success; a contested statutory element is not automatically jurisdictional.
- **Young v. Northern Illinois Conference:** Applied at that same distinction; its constitutional-defense holding is not expanded into permission to assume Article III.
- **Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife:** Its controlling standing elements and stage-appropriate support remain governing. Its three-Justice absent-agency application is neither enlarged nor needed.
- **Church of Scientology v. United States:** Meaningful partial relief remains enough; no effectual relief of that kind is available on these allegations.
- **United Food & Commercial Workers v. Brown Group:** Congress's representative-enforcement choice does not eliminate personal injury and likely redress.
- **Bennett v. Spear:** Supported pleading-stage causal and remedial links remain sufficient; its ongoing constraint differs from the completed reporting deprivation here.
- **Arizonans for Official English v. Arizona:** Applied for the live-controversy requirement and limited corrective authority to vacate without reaching merits.
- **Norton v. Mathews; Secretary of the Navy v. Avrech:** Their already-controlled merits and distinct jurisdictional settings remain intact; neither decides this unresolved EPCRA dispute.
- **Gwaltney of Smithfield v. Chesapeake Bay Foundation:** Its different statutory wording and enforcement scheme do not settle the merits question the Court leaves open.
- **Meghrig v. KFC Western:** Its RCRA cause-and-remedy limits remain statute-specific; it supplies no automatic EPCRA current-violation rule.

#### Friends of the Earth, Inc. v. Laidlaw Environmental Services (TOC), Inc., 528 U.S. 167 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-822.
**Decided:** January 12, 2000.

##### Whether supported curtailment of local river use establishes personal injury without separate environmental damage

**Holding:** Supported curtailment of identified local recreational and aesthetic use because of reasonable concern about ongoing mercury discharges is a concrete personal injury; Article III does not require separate proof of environmental damage when the asserted injury is this loss of use and enjoyment. General interest in compliance or unexplained subjective concern alone remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The supported current local-use interests and curtailment differ from indefinite distant-travel intentions. Childhood cessation for another reason, limited prior visits and scientific no-harm findings retain their actual weight; none is converted into proof of frequent historical use or automatically negates reasonable present-use loss.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No permit violation injures everyone downstream, no property valuation is decided, and no separate Sierra Club standing determination is needed for the identical request supported by other organizations' injured members.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lujan requires concrete personal interests and stage-appropriate support. Brown Group separates representative enforcement authority from members' personal stakes.


##### Whether Treasury civil penalties can redress threatened personal injury through deterrence

**Holding:** A statutorily authorized civil penalty payable to the Treasury can provide Article III redress when it is likely to deter continuing or reasonably recurring violations affecting the plaintiff's supported personal interests. Payment to the plaintiff is unnecessary for that preventive effect, but punishment of wholly past conduct without a remaining remedial connection, satisfaction from punishment and fees alone are insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** Violations were ongoing at filing, with subsequent mercury exceedances. The statutory penalty can deter recurrence affecting these use interests despite existing governmental enforcement and other litigation exposure; certainty is unnecessary.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Steel Co. is distinguished from its post-cure suit with no continuing or imminent violation alleged. Its express deterrence reservation applies, while its completed-injury limitation remains.


##### Whether compliance and later facility closure establish mootness

**Holding:** A defendant relying on voluntary cessation must make it absolutely clear that the challenged injurious conduct cannot reasonably be expected to recur. Substantial compliance sufficient to defeat an injunction does not automatically satisfy that burden or erase standing that existed at filing.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The lower court must assess actual closure, dismantling, sale, retained permit and consent-agreement limits. The agreement stopped incineration while allowing approved equivalent treatment capacity subject to permits and reconstruction conditions. Neither permit retention nor dismantling alone decides recurrence; alleged reporting problems do not establish resumed mercury discharge.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not newly affirm or increase the $405,800 penalty, adjudicate the preserved diligent-prosecution defense, award fees, reopen the unappealed denial of an injunction, or decide the unpresented Article II question. The dispute over whether the liability ruling itself was declaratory relief does not establish a separate declaratory decree in the record. Fees neither cure missing standing nor alone prevent mootness. Broader survival of a properly initiated penalty claim after proven permanent cessation is reserved by the Court.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the appellate vacatur and dismissal direction. The district penalty judgment returns to ordinary unresolved appellate review, including diligent prosecution, closure and penalty questions, without affirmance of its merits or amount. Ordinary mandate and actual stay rules govern collection; no immediate execution, new injunction, fee award or special stay follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The established voluntary-cessation burden remains distinct from entitlement to prospective equitable relief.

#### Riggins v. Nevada, 504 U.S. 127 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-8466.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### What justification is required to continue antipsychotic medication over a pretrial detainee’s objection

**Holding:** Once a pretrial detainee objects to continued antipsychotic medication, the State must establish medical appropriateness and a need sufficient to override the protected refusal interest, considering reasonable less intrusive means. The unexplained order here supplies neither an essential justification nor findings about alternatives and cannot authorize the compelled treatment through trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this rule and application.

**Material application:** Continued medication became involuntary after objection. Medical appropriateness was accepted, but the unexplained order established neither essential safety need nor the insufficiency of less intrusive means; testimony indicated that competence might continue without Mellaril.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Washington v. Harper, 494 U.S. 210 (1990): applied for the protected refusal interest and requirement of overriding justification and medical appropriateness; its convicted-prisoner procedures do not automatically govern trial medication.
- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): relied on for the State's legitimate trial interest and the possibility of necessary courtroom measures; no comprehensive competency-medication exception is adopted.

##### Whether the unsupported treatment and unreconstructable trial risk require a new trial

**Holding:** When unjustified compelled antipsychotic medication through trial creates a supported, substantial risk of impairing the defense that the record cannot reliably reconstruct, the defendant need not demonstrate exactly how an unmedicated trial would have differed. The risk here extends beyond visible demeanor to testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel; explanatory expert testimony does not cure it, and the convictions and dependent sentence must be vacated for a new trial.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Stone, White, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this record-specific rule and remedy. Kennedy concurs in the reversal and new-trial judgment on a distinct trial-integrity ground.

**Material application:** The supported risk concerned attention, testimony, comprehension and communication with counsel as well as demeanor. Expert explanation cannot restore an altered conversation or response, and an unmedicated trial cannot reliably be reconstructed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No incompetency finding, fixed treatment instruction, categorical medication prohibition, universal proof quantum, strict-scrutiny formula or rule making every medication error structural is adopted. The complete conditions for competency-restoration medication, competency waiver, the Fifth Amendment demeanor theory and an unpreserved Eighth Amendment theory remain undecided. Chapman’s harmless-beyond-a-reasonable-doubt formulation appears only in Stone’s concurrence.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The murder and robbery convictions and dependent death sentence are reversed and vacated, 7–2; the case is remanded for a new trial if the State proceeds. Renewed compelled treatment requires constitutionally adequate contemporaneous justification.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Illinois v. Allen, 397 U.S. 337 (1970): applied by analogy to potentially prejudicial compelled trial measures and the need for an essential justification.
- Estelle v. Williams, 425 U.S. 501 (1976): applied by analogy to effects of compelled trial appearance that cannot be fully reconstructed from a transcript; its particular waiver disposition is not extended.

##### Adequate justification for unwanted antipsychotic medication of a pretrial defendant

#### Foucha v. Louisiana, 504 U.S. 71 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-5844.
**Decided:** May 18, 1992.

##### Whether the insanity acquittal continues to authorize psychiatric custody after the State concedes the end of mental illness

**Holding:** When the State concedes that an insanity acquittee is no longer mentally ill, it may not continue his psychiatric confinement under the existing insanity-acquittee authority solely because he cannot prove that he will be harmless. The original acquittal supports neither a suspended criminal sentence nor indefinite psychiatric custody after its illness predicate disappears.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-A, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this proposition and application.

**Material application:** The conceded absence of present mental illness is not cured by antisocial personality or a doctor’s inability to guarantee safety. An insanity acquittal is not a conviction authorizing punishment.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Jones v. United States, 463 U.S. 354 (1983): applied to the continuing illness-and-danger basis of insanity commitment; initial verdict-based commitment and rejection of an automatic hypothetical-sentence ceiling remain intact.
- O'Connor v. Donaldson, 422 U.S. 563 (1975): applied to the requirement that confinement end when its authorizing basis disappears; the Court does not equate a conditional-release recommendation with a finding of harmlessness.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the required relationship between confinement's nature, duration and purpose.

##### Whether Louisiana may preserve psychiatric custody without new adequate grounds and proceedings

**Holding:** Once the insanity-acquittal basis for holding Foucha as a mental patient has ended, Louisiana cannot preserve that psychiatric custody without constitutionally adequate proceedings establishing the grounds required for it. If the State invokes ordinary civil commitment, it must establish current mental illness and dangerousness by clear and convincing evidence; additional procedure cannot supply an absent substantive ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-B, joined by Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: five of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient ground. Stone does not join this section's separate Vitek-based procedural characterization.

**Material application:** A counseled hearing about danger alone cannot supply the absent illness predicate. Ordinary civil commitment requires clear and convincing proof of present illness and dangerousness.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court makes no finding that Foucha is harmless, adopts no general scrutiny tier or universal dangerousness burden, and leaves materially narrower preventive regimes, the duration of otherwise valid commitment and the scope of state insanity defenses open. Equal protection has only four supporters and supplies no controlling ground.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgment sustaining confinement is reversed, 6–3, and remanded for implementation. Confinement under the challenged insanity-acquittee provision must end; it may not continue indefinitely while the State seeks another rationale. Any independently lawful custody requires its own authority and procedures.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Vitek v. Jones, 445 U.S. 480 (1980): applied to psychiatric institutionalization's distinct liberty deprivation and need for appropriate grounds and procedures.
- Jackson v. Indiana, 406 U.S. 715 (1972): applied to the need for an independently authorized commitment proceeding after the initial basis expires.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418 (1979): applied unchanged to the clear-and-convincing burden for ordinary civil commitment; that burden is not made universal for every preventive custody channel.
- Baxstrom v. Herold, 383 U.S. 107 (1966): relied on for the ordinary protections attending a new civil commitment after the prior detention basis ends; no separate majority equal-protection holding is created here.

##### Whether indefinite psychiatric confinement may rest on failure to prove nondangerousness after mental illness ends

**Holding:** Louisiana's scheme permitting indefinite psychiatric confinement of a no-longer-mentally-ill acquittee because he has not proved nondangerousness violates due process. A legitimate preventive purpose and periodic review do not cure the absence of a carefully limited substantive authority; the Court leaves materially narrower preventive regimes undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** White's Part II-C, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter: six of nine participating Justices adopt this independently sufficient rejection of Louisiana's asserted alternative preventive justification. #### O'Connor v. Consolidated Coin Caterers Corp., 517 U.S. 308

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-354.
**Decided:** April 1, 1996.

**Holding:** The reviewed ADEA merits affirmance must be reconsidered under the corrected replacement rule and ordinary proof and summary-judgment standards. **Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse and remand the ADEA merits affirmance. A genuinely sufficient remaining ground may still support summary judgment. The separate discovery and Rule 59/60 rulings remain intact; Dennis's postjudgment affidavit remains outside the timely record. No trial, hearing, liability, willfulness, damages, backpay or reinstatement is ordered.

# Thomas v. Chicago Park District — 2002-01-15

[Authoritative Record](../records/thomas-v-chicago-park-district_merits_2002-01-15.md)

## Event

Thomas v. Chicago Park District, No. 00-1249; 534 U.S. 316. Merits decision, January 15, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 2. Argued December 3, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Seventh Circuit. The ordinance requires permits for events involving more than fifty people and activities including amplified sound. Applicants seeking expressive events, including marijuana-policy advocacy, challenge its facial standards and procedures. The Seventh Circuit sustained the system. The questions concern the full censorship safeguards, constrained discretion, and timely action and review.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Seventh Circuit judgment rejecting the facial challenge is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Seventh Circuit's rejection of the facial challenge stands, and the permit ordinance remains operative. Properly presented challenges to discriminatory, delayed or content-based administration retain their ordinary availability; no particular permit denial is adjudicated here.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Scalia | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirms the facial judgment | All three holdings, thirteen qualified denial grounds, actual deadlines/review and limits on administration |

## Holdings

### I. Neutral park coordination does not require the entire censorship procedure

**Holding and operative rule:** A genuinely content-neutral permit system for allocating shared park space and coordinating activities need not adopt Freedman's entire procedure for censorship of expressive content, including a requirement that the government initiate judicial proceedings and bear the censorship burden after each denial. Such a system still requires real constraints on discretion, bounded administrative action and meaningful access to review; a logistical label alone does not establish validity.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion is joined throughout by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine directly adopt this and the following two holdings with their qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the safeguards must address the function officials may exercise. Freedman concerns official censorship of acceptable expressive content. Allocating park uses under definite scheduling, capacity and safety rules presents a different recurring risk. Cox recognizes neutral coordination of shared public space; Ward requires actual content independence rather than a benign label. The need to obtain a permit does not itself erase that distinction.

The challengers correctly stress that delay and favoritism can suppress speech even without an express message category. The answer is not to presume good administration, but to examine the authorized criteria and the concrete timing and review protections. Here those protections constrain park management rather than authorize approval of ideas. Requiring the District to commence a judicial case after every scheduling refusal would import a censorship procedure without its predicate. Freedman and FW/PBS remain in force in their own settings. Because the full censorship procedure does not apply, the Court need not decide whether Freedman's expeditious-review requirement there demands prompt commencement of proceedings or a prompt final judicial determination.

### II. The ordinance confines denial to objective management grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** The ordinance's two qualified prior-conduct predicates and eleven further grounds, set out below, sufficiently confine denial to neutral park-management subjects for this facial challenge. It permits denial on a listed ground but does not require denial whenever a ground occurs; permission to excuse a disqualifying circumstance authorizes neither an invented ground nor selective treatment by viewpoint.

**Authority:** The unanimous Court opinion adopts this standards determination and its limits.

**Denial grounds governed by this holding:**

| Ordinance basis | Permitted ground, with its condition |
|---|---|
| Prior conduct | To the extent permitted by law, prior material misrepresentation about the nature or scope of a permitted event, or violation of an earlier permit's terms, by the applicant or the person represented. |
| 1 | An application, including required attachments and submissions, is incompletely filled out or executed. |
| 2 | The application fee was not tendered with the application, or the user fee, indemnification agreement, insurance certificate or security deposit was not tendered within the General Superintendent's prescribed times. |
| 3 | The application contains a material falsehood or misrepresentation. |
| 4 | The applicant lacks legal capacity to contract or to sue and be sued. |
| 5 | The applicant or represented person has unpaid prior damage to District property or another outstanding unpaid District debt. |
| 6 | An earlier fully executed application for the same time and place has been received, and a permit has been or will be granted for uses that do not reasonably allow multiple occupancy. |
| 7 | The intended activity conflicts with a District-organized and conducted program already planned and scheduled for that time and place. |
| 8 | The proposed activity is prohibited by or inconsistent with the park's designated classification and uses under section C.1. |
| 9 | The intended activity presents an unreasonable health or safety danger to the applicant, other park users, District employees or the public. |
| 10 | The applicant has not complied or cannot comply with applicable licensing requirements, District ordinances or regulations governing sales or offers of goods or services. |
| 11 | Law, District code or ordinances, or the General Superintendent's regulations prohibit the intended activity. |

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the grounds must be understood with the receipt-order requirement, written reasons and review. Forsyth, Shuttlesworth and City of Lakewood require definite limits on expressive licensing rather than confidence in official goodwill. These provisions identify the matters officials may assess and permit a reviewing court to identify a refusal resting on some different reason.

The strongest objection concerns the judgment involved in assessing danger, applying cross-references or forgiving defects. But permission to decline denial does not confer permission to invent a disqualifying reason. Nor must every technical defect compel refusal to avoid facial invalidity. The criteria remain neutral management limits; they do not authorize message approval, viewpoint favoritism or an audience-hostility burden. Forsyth's separate rule against hostility-based fees continues to govern any covered fee. This case does not adjudicate an unpresented fee amount or validate every financial condition in every application. A materially different or discriminatory administration remains subject to challenge on its own facts, without a new numerical-pattern prerequisite to proving viewpoint discrimination.

### III. The actual deadlines, reasons and review support the facial judgment

**Holding and operative rule:** The District must decide within fourteen days unless written notice extends the period for fourteen additional days; a denial must state its grounds in writing, propose feasible cures, and suggest alternative times or places when based on an earlier competing application. An applicant has seven days to appeal in writing to the General Superintendent, who must act within seven days, followed by available state common-law certiorari review; together with the objective grounds, these protections sustain the ordinance against the facial challenge.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this procedural-sufficiency holding and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that these actual protections answer the presented structural objection without a universal rule for every neutral permit system. Receipt order governs processing; the bounded decision period prevents open-ended administrative withholding; written reasons identify the decision for challenge; feasible correction and alternative scheduling address curable defects; and administrative and judicial review provide avenues to contest an improper refusal. Content neutrality alone would not cure unrestricted discretion or ineffective review.

No automatic-issuance rule is established on this record, and no fixed court-adjudication deadline is supplied. The Court adds neither. It also does not resolve an actual late application or discriminatory denial absent a proper claim and record. Those are applications of continuing First Amendment constraints, not a license for indefinite delay. The Seventh Circuit's facial judgment is affirmed, leaving the ordinance operative while preserving properly presented claims about actual administration.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Freedman v. Maryland, 380 U.S. 51:** Distinguished as content censorship; its safeguards remain effective in that setting, and the meaning of its judicial-timing requirement is unnecessary to resolve here.
- **FW/PBS, Inc. v. Dallas, 493 U.S. 215:** Its application of censorship-related safeguards to the licensing mechanism there is preserved; it does not make this neutral park procedure identical.
- **Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement, 505 U.S. 123:** Applied for objective limits on licensing and preserved for its independent prohibition of audience-hostility charges; no fee amount is approved here.
- **Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569:** Applied for lawful neutral coordination of shared public space, subject to nondiscriminatory administration.
- **Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781:** Applied for genuine content-independent time, place and manner management rather than acceptance of a stated benign purpose alone.
- **Shuttlesworth v. Birmingham, 394 U.S. 147, and City of Lakewood v. Plain Dealer Publishing Co., 486 U.S. 750:** Applied for narrow, definite licensing standards and meaningful protection against unconfined choice.
- **Hill v. Colorado, June 28, 2000, and Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network, February 19, 1997:** Their actual-mechanism and practical-scope limits remain; neither creates blanket immunity for purportedly neutral restraints.

Genuinely neutral park coordination may use the ordinance's objective grounds, bounded administrative timetable, written reasons, correction and alternative-scheduling provisions, and review without requiring the District to initiate a censorship proceeding after each denial. Permissive forgiveness of a listed defect does not itself create facially unbounded discretion. The same structure remains subject to meaningful review and to properly presented challenges to viewpoint discrimination, actual delay or other unauthorized administration. No automatic-issuance rule or universal court-decision deadline is established.

# Kansas v. Crane — 2002-01-22

[Authoritative Record](../records/kansas-v-crane_merits_2002-01-22.md)

## Event

Kansas v. Crane; No. 00-957. Merits decision, January 22, 2002; argued October 30, 2001. Review of the Kansas Supreme Court's judgment setting aside a sexually violent predator commitment under a complete-inability-to-control construction. The question is what impairment of control due process requires for this form of civil commitment.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court vacates the judgment and remands for application of the serious-difficulty standard under Kansas's prescribed proof burden.

## Judgment & Remedy

Vacated and remanded, 7-2. **Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would reverse the complete-incapacity construction without requiring an additional independent control finding. All nine reject a complete-incapacity requirement.

The Kansas courts must decide whether the verdict and evidence lawfully establish serious difficulty controlling the dangerous conduct, substantially connected to the qualifying present condition, beyond a reasonable doubt. The trial commitment is not automatically restored. If that requirement is not lawfully established, the existing order cannot stand; any new commitment proceeding requires independent lawful authority. A properly preserved punitive-operation claim remains subject to its distinct predicates under Hendricks and Seling.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Vacates and remands | Entire functional-control rule, statutory burden and application remand |
| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas | Would reverse and remand under the statute's genuine condition-and-danger predicates | Rejects complete incapacity and an additional independent control finding |

## Holdings

### Serious functional difficulty, rather than total incapacity, limits this civil commitment

**Holding and operative rule.** Due process permits this form of civil commitment only upon proof of a serious present functional impairment producing serious difficulty controlling the dangerous conduct and substantially connected to the qualifying mental condition and demonstrated danger. Complete inability to control every act is unnecessary, but diagnosis and predicted recidivism alone do not distinguish an eligible person from a dangerous ordinary recidivist; Kansas must establish the required functional connection under its beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden.

**Authority.** Breyer's opinion speaks for Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplying seven votes for the full rule and its application remand.

**Controlling explanation.** Civil confinement needs a substantive basis different from a prediction that a person will commit another crime. Foucha supplies that requirement of lawful present predicates rather than danger alone. A genuine disorder must produce sufficiently serious difficulty controlling the conduct that makes this custody permissible. The dissent's disorder-and-danger formulation does not adequately express that functional boundary when diagnosis and prediction could include ordinary repeated crime. At the other extreme, complete incapacity would exclude serious impairment merely because some capacity to choose remains. Neither liberty's protection nor psychiatric uncertainty requires that unrealistic line. The condition's nature and severity and evidence of actual impairment must be assessed together; clinical categories inform the legal finding without deciding it. Addington recognizes uncertainty in psychiatric judgments but supplies no reduction in Kansas's more protective burden. No mathematical measure, compulsory clinical label, involuntariness of every act or universal jury-instruction formula is required. Personality disorder is neither automatically sufficient nor automatically disqualifying. Hendricks's controlling punishment judgment concerned its own application; its non-dispositive due-process discussion did not settle today's rule. Seling likewise keeps actual punitive operation distinct from substantive civil eligibility. On remand the Kansas courts must evaluate the verdict and evidence under the stated rule. A verdict framed only in statutory diagnosis and likely repetition does not necessarily establish the necessary impairment.

**Limits and questions not reached.** The functional showing remains required for this form of commitment regardless of whether the condition is described as emotional, cognitive or volitional. The Court classifies no particular emotional diagnosis as sufficient or insufficient; clinical terminology does not replace the required functional assessment. A new punishment adjudication, facial redesign of commitment, periodic-review procedures and treatment or alternative-placement requirements lie outside the grant. A preserved operational punishment objection requires Crane-specific facts and the independent Hendricks-Seling inquiry.

## Precedent Treatment

- Foucha v. Louisiana (May 18, 1992): applies its requirement of lawful present substantive custody predicates and its rejection of dangerousness alone after the psychiatric predicate has ended; narrower genuinely justified civil regimes remain possible.
- Addington v. Texas, 441 U.S. 418: preserves its ordinary civil-commitment proof principles and recognition of clinical uncertainty; it does not lower Kansas's reasonable-doubt burden.
- Kansas v. Hendricks / Hendricks v. Kansas (June 23, 1997): retains its as-applied retrospective-punishment holding. Its noncontrolling diagnostic discussion neither establishes today's control rule nor transfers findings from Hendricks to Crane.
- Seling v. Young (January 17, 2001): preserves the cumulative civil-intent and punitive-operation inquiry and its distinct remedial predicates; it supplies no categorical invalidation or approval of Crane's commitment.

## Law After Decision

The serious-difficulty requirement is a new controlling due-process rule for the civil-commitment question presented. Courts must examine actual functional impairment connected to the qualifying condition and dangerous conduct, while retaining the applicable statutory burden. The functional rule governs application on remand; whether the existing verdict and evidence satisfy it is factual and remedial work, not unresolved law. Separate retrospective-punishment claims retain their own demanding proof and temporal requirements.

## Separate Writings

Scalia, joined by Thomas, agrees that due process does not require complete inability to control conduct. He would hold that a genuine present mental abnormality or disorder causally making repeated sexual violence likely supplies the substantive limitation, when proved under Kansas's burden. An additional independent control finding is not constitutionally required. The new serious-difficulty formulation, in his view, gives uncertain guidance and turns descriptions of an individual's condition into a universal extra element. This position neither treats a label or danger alone as sufficient nor allows a high burden to create a missing substantive predicate. It preserves Foucha's ended-predicate rule and Hendricks and Seling's separate review of actual punitive operation. He would reverse the complete-incapacity rule and remand under the statute's genuine condition-and-causal-danger requirements.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to the Kansas courts for the required functional-impairment determination under Kansas's reasonable-doubt burden and for the resulting lawful disposition of the commitment order. Independently preserved objections remain in their proper channels.

## Holdings

### I. The presented platform combination requires an actual competitive inquiry

**Holding and operative rule.** The challenged combination of a principal platform for third-party applications and complementary browser functionality, including its contractual and removal restrictions, must be evaluated under the rule of reason because the existing per se tying category does not reliably resolve the material integration and competitive questions presented. Courts must examine separate demand, conditioning, power, foreclosure and supported efficiencies for the actual restrictions; integration supplies neither immunity nor a presumption of justification, and an adequately supported inquiry may be abbreviated rather than invariably requiring a comprehensive market study.

**Authority.** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion has eight votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens does not join the uniform category direction.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the legal method must fit the combination's actual competitive mechanism. Jefferson Parish and Kodak preserve independent consumer demand and the need to prove tying elements; functional connection alone does not merge separate products. But evidence of separate demand and past industry practice may incompletely measure a platform integration that changes the product. California Dental requires enough inquiry to test a substantial competing explanation, permitting abbreviated analysis when experience and the mechanism make the effects sufficiently clear. Khan and NYNEX likewise require a restraint to fit the category invoked. None supplies general software immunity.

Stevens would retain ordinary per se analysis for separable contractual coercion. That concern identifies conduct the actual inquiry must examine, but the relationship among product definition, interface distribution and each restriction warrants the bounded competitive inquiry here. Distributing useful application interfaces may explain less than compulsory retention of the whole browser. Microsoft must substantiate benefits material to its defense, and an explanation that does not justify a particular restriction cannot shelter it. Plaintiffs must establish net anticompetitive effect in browser competition, considering benefits actually supported for that restriction. Operating-system injury sustaining section 2 does not itself establish browser-market injury under section 1. The original undifferentiated judgment did not undertake that inquiry; its vacatur stands.

**Application and mandate limits.** OEM nonremoval restrictions and omission from the removal utility remain possible tying theories, with any additional relief separately justified. Plaintiffs may test the default-browser override's distinct browser-market effect despite rejection of its section 2 ground on the earlier record. Price bundling requires resolution of a positive browser-attributable price increment, including conflicting competitor-discount evidence, before weighing effects and benefits. Commingled code is not added as an independent trial tying basis. The attempted-monopolization reversal remains: plaintiffs may not renew tying theories dependent on a precise browser-market definition or independent entry barriers they failed to establish for that claim, apart from matters implicit in the tying arrangement itself. That restriction preserves this case's mandate rather than creating a universal antitrust rule.

### II. The undisclosed contacts require disqualification and a fresh remedial proceeding

**Holding and operative rule.** Undisclosed press contacts concerning pending merits, credibility and remedy require disqualification under 28 U.S.C. §455(a) when the circumstances cumulatively cause an informed, detached observer reasonably to question impartiality, without proof of actual bias. The established violation requires reassignment here; the entire June 7, 2000 structural-and-conduct decree remains vacated, and relief must be reconsidered on lawfully established liability and an adequate remedial record.

**Authority.** All nine Justices support Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion, including the common remedial principles and the complete decree disposition.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that publication delayed until judgment did not remove the appearance created by secret outside discussions while adjudication continued. Liteky supplies the cumulative objective standard; judicial origin is context rather than immunity, and outside origin is neither necessary nor sufficient by itself. These contacts concerned the dispute's live subjects and denied the parties an opportunity to raise the issue while the conduct continued. The reported accounts remain assumed true for the disqualification inquiry on the appellate posture, rather than new trial findings about every reported detail.

Liljeberg separately requires attention to injustice to these parties, injustice in other cases and public confidence when determining relief from earlier civil adjudication. Its finality-specific Rule 60(b)(6) prerequisites do not become conditions of this direct appeal. The three interests reject both automatic total vacatur and automatic preservation. All nine agree that the existing decree cannot stand. Apart from disqualification, unresolved remedy-specific disputes without an appropriate hearing, inadequate explanation of the remedy and materially altered liability foundations independently require new remedial proceedings. Neither mandatory divestiture nor immunity from divestiture follows; disputed predictions about division require adjudication at the remedial stage.

### III. Ordinary appellate review does not conclusively resolve the need to revisit compromised liability foundations

**Holding and operative rule.** On the established appearance violation, the new judge must apply Liljeberg's three remedial interests to determine which material factual or credibility foundations cannot meaningfully be separated from the concealed contacts and require fresh determination, and must reconsider the findings and dependent conclusions accordingly. Independently sustainable determinations may remain at their established scope, with appropriate reuse of the record; neither actual-bias proof as to a particular finding nor an automatic all-issues retrial is required.

**Authority.** Part III of Stone-Zsela's opinion has five votes: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer reject this additional-remedy application while accepting the common standard.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the appellate allocation left a material part of Liljeberg's inquiry unanswered. The interviews began before the findings and concerned integration, credibility and the dispute's merits. Rule 52 clear-error review preserves permissible factual choices; it does not independently choose among competing credible accounts or permissible expert inferences. The careful rejection of some liability bases therefore cannot alone establish that every retained foundation may remain binding despite the undisclosed relationship. Anderson and Pullman-Standard preserve this distinction between appellate review and factfinding.

The opposing Justices properly emphasize the innocent plaintiffs' burden, differentiated appellate corrections, limited additional deterrence and confidence restored by replacement of the judge and remedy. Those considerations weigh against indiscriminate repetition. They do not sufficiently justify conclusive insulation of every disputed material foundation reached after the contacts began. Public confidence and the risk of injustice in other cases require the new judge to decide which foundations need neutral reconsideration, without demanding proof of actual bias that section 455(a) does not require. Undisputed matters and independently sustainable legal determinations may remain; prior legal rulings remain pertinent to facts lawfully determined. The remand establishes a tailored inquiry and resulting reconsideration, not presumptive universal taint. Particular findings' binding force is application work under that rule.

# Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co. — 2002-02-19

[Authoritative Record](../records/barnhart-v-sigmon-coal-co_merits_2002-02-19.md)

## Event

Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co., No. 00-1307. Argued November 7, 2001; decided February 19, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit's judgment rejecting the challenged Coal Act assignments. The question is whether asset succession to an original signatory operator, without an independently qualifying statutory relationship, permits assignment under the Coal Act.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Court affirms the judgment against the challenged assignments on the asserted asset-successor theory.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 6–3; the 86 assignments cannot stand on the asserted asset-successor theory.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Stevens, O'Connor and Breyer.

The 86 assignments remain set aside to the extent they rest solely on the rejected direct asset-successor theory. Any independently established statutory relationship or enforceable contractual obligation retains its own force; administration of unassigned beneficiaries remains governed by the Coal Act's lawful mechanisms.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Thomas | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg | Supports the judgment | Entire opinion; every controlling proposition and its stated limits |
| Dissent | Stevens | O'Connor, Breyer | Opposes the judgment | Entire statutory disagreement and contrary remedy |

## Holdings

### Asset succession alone does not create a new assignment category

**Holding and operative rule:** Sections 9701(c)(2) and 9706 do not authorize assigning a signatory operator's beneficiaries to an asset purchaser solely because it directly succeeded to that operator's business. A purchaser independently within an enumerated related-person class remains subject to the statute; the holding does not determine an independent contractual obligation or every contested meaning of successor.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, controls the complete statutory rule, its limits and the affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that financial responsibility must arise from the relationships Congress enacted. Section 9701(c)(2)(A) identifies a member of the signatory's controlled group under section 52(a), including the signatory; a trade or business under common control under section 52(b); and a person with a partnership interest or joint venture with a signatory in a coal-industry business employing eligible beneficiaries, except an interest held solely as a limited partner. The successor sentence refers to those listed classes rather than adding all direct successors of an original signatory. Subsection (c)(2)(B) measures the relationship on July 20, 1992, or immediately before an earlier cessation of business. The assignment priorities in section 9706(a) do not enlarge these classes.

Section 9706(b)(2) separately permits a post-enactment assignment transfer to a successor under its conditions, retaining the transferor and related persons as guarantors and requiring notice to the trustees. That provision does not automatically reach this earlier asset purchase. Russello gives effect to Congress's differing language; Ron Pair requires enforcement of the resulting text. The strongest objection is that reaching a related person's successor while excluding the direct successor creates an anomalous financing gap. But an imperfect allocation of statutory liability is not an absurdity authorizing a new liable-person class. Jericol's continuation of the business and agreement assumption do not establish the claimed statutory relationship. The contextual reading required by Chickasaw and J.E.M. does not replace the specific classes with general successor policy.

**Precedent treatment:** Russello and Ron Pair are applied to the differentiated categories; Chickasaw and J.E.M. retain contextual construction without supplying an omitted category.

### Clear statutory limits resolve this assignment theory

**Holding and operative rule:** The definite assignment classes cannot be enlarged by an administrative preference for preserving program revenue. Because that statutory limit resolves these assignments, the Court does not reach the unnecessary constitutional objections or decide an unestablished independent contractual ground.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** Mead distinguishes an agency's interpretive authority from the persuasive force of its reasoning; neither permits liability contrary to clear statutory categories. Eastern Enterprises invalidated particular tier-three assessments without a controlling affirmative constitutional rationale. It did not invalidate the Coal Act generally and does not supply a constitutional shortcut here. The Court's statutory judgment resolves the challenged theory while leaving any genuinely independent obligation to its own legal predicates.

**Precedent treatment:** Mead's statutory boundary is applied; Eastern Enterprises is preserved at the scope of its judgment and independently majority-supported property proposition.

## Precedent Treatment

- Russello v. United States, 464 U.S. 16: different language in related provisions supports the enacted distinction.
- United States v. Ron Pair Enterprises, Inc., 489 U.S. 235: the statutory text governs despite a claimed policy anomaly.
- Chickasaw Nation v. United States, November 27, 2001, and J. E. M. Ag Supply, Inc. v. Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc., December 10, 2001: contextual reading preserves each provision's actual function; neither adds this successor category.
- United States v. Mead Corp., June 18, 2001: interpretive authority and persuasion remain distinct, and neither authorizes departure from clear liability conditions.
- Eastern Enterprises v. Apfel, June 25, 1998: the limited assessment judgment and absence of a controlling affirmative constitutional rationale remain; the Coal Act is not invalidated as a whole.

## Law After Decision

Direct asset succession to an original signatory is insufficient by itself for the challenged statutory assignments. Independently qualifying related persons, the separate prospective assignment-transfer mechanism and enforceable contractual obligations remain governed by their own conditions. This is a statutory coverage rule, not a general immunity for asset purchasers or a constitutional invalidation of the program.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by O'Connor and Breyer, dissenting.** The dissent would reverse and remand for application of an inclusive succession rule. Congress allocated existing health-benefit obligations across continuing business relationships. Reading the successor sentence in that integrated structure avoids the anomaly of reaching a related person's successor while exempting the closer direct successor that continued the coal business and assumed its agreement. The contextual treatment of successors in 1 U.S.C. sections 1 and 5, together with the labor-law succession principles illustrated by Wiley, Golden State Bottling and Fall River Dyeing, supports that reading; those authorities do not establish liability for every solvent purchaser.

The dissent recognizes that a differentiated list and a separate prospective transfer provision could reflect compromise. Its answer is that the entire succession structure supplies the context for that list, and the Court's separation defeats the allocation Congress made. Legislative explanations corroborate that construction rather than replacing the text. The dissent does not treat a bargaining discount as a proved fact, impose unrestricted federal common-law liability, or decide the constitutional question. Preserved successor-status and other application issues remain for the proper tribunal on remand.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit's judgment against these assignments stands. The statutory basis asserted in this review cannot support reassignment; any independent obligation must be established on its own terms.

26 U.S.C. §§9701(c)(1)–(2), 9706(a), (b)(2); Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co., No. 00-1307; the asset purchase, agreement assumption and 86 challenged assignments described in 534 U.S. 438.

# Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer — 2002-02-20

[Authoritative Record](../records/wisconsin-department-of-health-and-family-services-v-blumer_merits_2002-02-20.md)

## Event

Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer, No. 00-952. Argued December 3, 2001; decided February 20, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Wisconsin Court of Appeals, which required a resources-first Medicaid calculation after rejecting the administrative determination and circuit-court judgment; the Wisconsin Supreme Court declined review. The question is whether section 1396r-5 permits considering anticipated support from the institutionalized spouse before increasing the community spouse resource allowance.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the state judgment requiring resources first and remands for proceedings consistent with the permissible income-first construction.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4; the statute permits the income-first method within its stated limits.**

**Supporting:** Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Scalia.

The Wisconsin Court of Appeals' federal-law restriction on the income-first method is reversed. The matter returns for proceedings and any remaining calculation under the permissible method, using supported income and resource figures and the statute's distinct actual-payment requirements.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Supports the judgment | Entire opinion; every controlling proposition and its stated limits |
| Dissent | Stevens | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia | Opposes the judgment | Entire statutory disagreement and contrary remedy |

## Holdings

### A State may consider prospective support before increasing protected resources

**Holding and operative rule:** At the preeligibility hearing under 42 U.S.C. §1396r-5(e)(2)(C), a State may consider the community spouse's expected posteligibility income, including support that may be transferred under subsection (d)(1)(B), before increasing the community spouse resource allowance. The statute permits income first; it does not require nationwide adoption or independently adjudicate every resources-first program.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, supplies five direct votes for this construction, its statutory qualifications and the reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this hearing anticipates the circumstances in which eligibility and the maintenance allowance operate. Both methods require forecasts. Congress protected a maintenance-income floor while preserving resources for care; it did not guarantee the largest possible resource reserve. Subsection (a)(1) supersedes inconsistent Medicaid provisions, and (a)(3) preserves ordinary methodology except where the section otherwise provides. Neither clause eliminates the need to read the specific provisions together.

Subsection (b)(1) prevents deeming the community spouse's income available to the institutionalized spouse. It does not state the reciprocal prohibition asserted against considering prospective support. Subsection (b)(2) governs posteligibility attribution according to its ownership rules, not the antecedent assessment's exclusive list of potential support. Subsection (c) separately governs resource assessment and later separation. Under (d)(1)(B), a deduction for the community spouse allowance is available only to the extent income is actually made available to or for the benefit of that spouse; transfer remains optional. A forecast neither establishes an actual payment nor commands one.

The contrary reading treats (e)(2)(C)'s resource-increase command as requiring present ownership of every income source considered. But that hearing assesses future maintenance under the statutory arrangement. It may include the contemplated support allowance without deeming ownership or payment already accomplished. Once inadequacy is established under the lawful calculation, the statute requires the resource adjustment. The Court imposes no additional consent, escrow or payment-guarantee prerequisite and makes no finding that a later transfer occurred. Good Samaritan supports considered administration within the statute, not displacement of these specific protections.

**Operative qualifications:** For the separate posteligibility attribution under (b)(2), a governing nontrust instrument may specify the allocation; otherwise income solely payable to one spouse is available to that spouse, jointly payable income is divided equally, and income shared with others follows the spouse's interest or half of the spouses' unspecified joint interest. Trust income follows the governing Medicaid trust provisions and the trust's allocation, or the parallel payment rules when no specific allocation appears. Nontrust income without an ownership instrument is divided equally. The institutionalized spouse may rebut the nontrust payment and no-instrument presumptions by a preponderance of evidence of different ownership. These rules do not turn a forecast into payment. Under (e)(2)(B), if either spouse establishes that exceptional circumstances causing significant financial duress require community-spouse income above the ordinary minimum monthly maintenance needs allowance, an amount sufficient to supply the necessary additional income must replace that allowance. Under (d)(5), when a court orders the institutionalized spouse to provide monthly income for the community spouse's support, the community spouse monthly income allowance must be at least the ordered amount. Those income-allowance adjustments remain distinct from (e)(2)(C)'s resource-increase command; none substitutes another provision's trigger or establishes that its conditions are met here.

**Precedent treatment:** Good Samaritan's permission for considered administration remains bounded by the specific enactment; its holding supplies no universal asset-preservation or administrative-discretion rule.

### Persuasive agency reasoning supports independent statutory construction

**Holding and operative rule:** The agency materials here receive the weight their reasoning earns rather than automatic controlling force. The Court independently concludes that income first is permissible; no new exception to, or abolition of, lawful delegated interpretive authority is adopted.

**Authority:** The same five-Justice Court opinion controls.

**Controlling explanation:** The November 1993 instruction described income first as required. The March 3, 1994 clarification instead permitted income first or another reasonable interpretation pending a final rule. The September 7, 2001 publication remained a proposed rule at this decision. Those different legal forms and dates cannot be collapsed into a binding final mandate. Mead and Christensen distinguish delegated legal force from persuasive reasons, and Skidmore supplies the latter inquiry. The agency's experience and explanation support the coordinated reading the Court independently adopts. Good Samaritan establishes that change alone does not disqualify a considered interpretation; it does not command acceptance of every revised view. The statutory conclusion therefore does not depend on automatically deferring to an amicus submission, guidance or a proposal.

**Precedent treatment:** Mead, Christensen and Skidmore are applied at their actual scope; Good Samaritan's change-of-position rule is preserved, and Chevron remains operative for lawful delegated choices.

## Precedent Treatment

- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, June 7, 1993: considered administration must fit the enactment, and a change of interpretation alone does not establish invalidity.
- United States v. Mead Corp., June 18, 2001: congressional delegation and exercise of interpretive authority remain prerequisites for controlling statutory force; these materials are considered for persuasion.
- Christensen v. Harris County, May 1, 2000: an advisory interpretation's authority and persuasive quality remain distinct; optional conduct is not converted into a compulsory transfer.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co., 323 U.S. 134: the actual reasoning and experience receive the persuasive weight they earn.
- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: its role for lawful delegated choices remains; the independent construction here creates no broader revision.

## Law After Decision

States may use the income-first method in the section 1396r-5(e)(2)(C) resource-allowance hearing while respecting ownership attribution, the optional character of transfer, and the actual-availability condition for a posteligibility deduction. A lawful forecast is not an accomplished transfer. Resource inadequacy, exceptional financial duress and court-ordered support retain their separate statutory treatments.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor and Scalia, dissenting.** The dissent would affirm the resources-first requirement and return the matter for a calculation using supported income and resource figures. Section (e)(2)(C) directs an increased resource allowance when the community spouse's protected income is inadequate. Predicting income from resources legally attributable to that spouse differs from predicting another person's optional payment. Section (d)(1)(B)'s posteligibility transfer does not add a prerequisite that the spouse depend on that payment before receiving the resource protection; denying eligibility can prevent the projected arrangement from beginning.

The dissent recognizes that (b)(1) is not a reciprocal nonattribution rule and that (b)(2) expressly addresses posteligibility attribution. Its point is the scheme's separation of ownership, resource protection and voluntary support, not an invented symmetric prohibition. The Court's answer that every method forecasts future circumstances fails to explain why Congress's resource protection may depend first on another person's voluntary transfer. A future payment might not occur or might diminish; those are objections to the legal method, not findings about Irene's later conduct. Administrative flexibility, conservation of funds and expertise cannot supply the missing compulsory transfer or displace the specific statutory command. The agency's changed view is not invalid merely because it changed, but its reasoning must answer this objection. The dissent asserts no constitutional right to preserve assets and would award no unsupported dollar amount.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Wisconsin courts for proceedings consistent with the permitted income-first method. Remaining factual calculations must respect the distinct statutory attribution and actual-payment rules; the decision fixes no eligibility date or benefit amount.

42 U.S.C. §1396r-5(a)–(f); Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer, No. 00-952, 534 U.S. 473; 66 Fed. Reg. 46763–46768 (September 7, 2001), proposed rule. The reviewed method forecasts support; a later actual transfer is not a finding in this judgment.

# New York v. FERC / Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC — 2002-03-04

[Authoritative Record](../records/new-york-v-ferc-enron-power-marketing-inc-v-ferc_merits_2002-03-04.md)

## Event

New York v. FERC, No. 00-568, and Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC, No. 00-809; 535 U.S. 1. Argued October 3, 2001; decided March 4, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 3. On certiorari to the D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining Order 888. New York challenges FERC's authority over unbundled interstate transmission serving retail purchasers. Enron seeks compulsory extension of the open-access requirements to transmission in bundled retail service. Order 888 found discriminatory wholesale transmission access and required functional unbundling: separate generation, transmission and ancillary-service rates, equal tariff terms for a utility's own wholesale transactions and equal use of electronic transmission information. It also regulated existing independently supplied interstate transmission serving retail purchases. For bundled retail service, FERC both characterized the delivered transaction as a state-regulated retail sale and separately determined that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary to remedy the discrimination it found.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The D.C. Circuit's judgment is affirmed in both dockets. The two affirmances rest on distinct grounds.

## Judgment & Remedy

| No. 00-809: compulsory extension to bundled retail service in this proceeding | Affirmed, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Enron's additional compulsory remedy is denied; the three dissenters would vacate this component and remand for a lawful agency explanation |

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer throughout; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas in Part I | Affirms both dockets | Part I: unbundled interstate transmission; Part II: sufficient scope of this remedy |
| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Thomas | Scalia, Kennedy | Joins Part I and affirmance in No. 00-568; would vacate and remand No. 00-809 | Requires a reasoned bundled-transmission determination under the proper jurisdictional understanding; would not order a tariff |

## Holdings

### I. The transmission grant includes unbundled interstate service for retail purchasers

**Holding and operative rule:** The Federal Power Act's separate grant over transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce includes independently supplied interstate transmission serving retail purchases. The wholesale limitation governs the separate sales grant; it does not narrow the transmission grant, and FERC may regulate the covered transmission's rates, terms and conditions without ordering a utility to begin retail wheeling.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court. That direct unanimous coalition controls the category and the following limits.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 201(b)(1) retains state retail-sale authority and, except as the Act specifically provides otherwise, excludes facilities used for generation, local distribution, intrastate-only transmission or transmission of energy consumed wholly by the transmitter. Its preservation of lawful state authority over hydroelectric exports remains. Section 201(b)(2) applies sections 210, 211 and 212 and their enforcement to the entities those provisions cover; compliance with a section 210 or 211 order does not otherwise subject an entity to general FPA jurisdiction. Section 201(f)'s exclusion of federal, state and local governments, their agencies, authorities and instrumentalities, corporations wholly owned directly or indirectly by one or more of them, and their officials acting in duty remains unless a provision specifically refers to them.

Section 212(g) prohibits orders inconsistent with state retail-marketing-area law. Section 212(h) prohibits an order conditioned on, or requiring, direct transmission to an ultimate consumer. Its separate rule for transmission to or for a resale entity permits the covered exception only when both conditions hold: the entity is a federal power marketing agency, TVA, a State or political subdivision or its agency, a corporation or association that has ever received a Rural Electrification Administration loan to provide electric service under the Rural Electrification Act of 1936, a person with a state- or local-law public electric-service obligation arising independently of contract alone, or a corporation or association wholly owned directly or indirectly by one or more of those listed entities; and it either served that consumer on October 24, 1992 or would deliver all the energy through transmission or distribution facilities it owns or controls. State and local authority over direct-to-consumer transmission remains preserved. Section 212(e) makes use of sections 210, 211, 213 or 212 unnecessary as a substitute for other lawful authority and, except as those sections provide, prevents them from limiting other Commission authority; it does not eliminate the retail-wheeling restrictions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a service does not become a retail sale merely because it transmits energy that the customer purchases at retail. The statute grants authority over interstate transmission separately from wholesale interstate sales. Reading the wholesale limitation into both clauses would insert a restriction Congress did not enact. Section 201(a)'s general policy statement must be read with those operative grants and specific exclusions, not made a repeal of them.

New York's strongest objection is that federal regulation of retail-associated transmission shifts an activity traditionally administered by States. The answer lies in the actual service regulated. The order reaches independent interstate transmission already provided voluntarily or under state direction; it does not require new retail wheeling or transfer retail-rate and local-distribution authority wholesale. State siting, local reliability and retail-service functions remain, and no particular facility classification is decided. The connected grid identifies the interstate facts; it creates no power apart from the text.

Iowa Utilities Board supplies the distinction between an enacted federal assignment and independent limits governing its exercise. Gulf Power likewise separates coverage from a particular regulatory method. Mead requires a real delegation before agency interpretation acquires controlling force; the enacted categories resolve this question independently, without a new deference rule. The statutory boundaries thus answer the allocation objection while sustaining the order's limited transmission coverage.

### II. This proceeding did not compel Enron's additional bundled-service remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** FERC could remedy the wholesale transmission-access discrimination established in this proceeding through the adopted open-access requirements while declining compulsory unbundling of bundled retail service on its independently sufficient determination that this additional remedy was unnecessary. Section 206 does require a lawful prospective determination and order after a hearing and finding that a jurisdictional rate, charge, classification or affecting rule, regulation, practice or contract is unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or preferential; the burden to prove that unlawfulness lies on the Commission or complainant, and the agency may not leave established discrimination uncorrected.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has the direct support of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those six votes control. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would remand this component for corrected agency analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that authority, the trigger for a duty to act and the necessary extent of correction are different questions. Sections 205 and 206 preserve lawful rates, nondiscrimination, filed schedules and mandatory correction when the required finding is made. They do not make every proposed structural extension obligatory merely because the Commission may possess authority to consider it.

The agency did characterize bundled delivered energy as a state-regulated retail sale. That ground cannot be erased by calling the order purely discretionary. But the agency also separately determined that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary to eliminate the discrimination it found. The Court sustains that actual, record-linked remedial determination without adopting a categorical jurisdictional exclusion. The functional-unbundling requirements address the established wholesale-access discrimination; Enron's demand does not show why the findings require the additional restructuring in this proceeding. Counsel's claim of inadequate record support is not substituted for the agency's own explanation.

The opposing Justices contend that the jurisdictional characterization undermines the agency's understanding of the corrective duty. That concern would require remand if the refusal depended on legal powerlessness. Here the independent unnecessary-remedy determination adequately supports the refusal of the particular additional remedy sought. Good Samaritan permits considered administration within a mandatory statutory structure but rejects unsupported litigation rationalizations. MCI forbids dispensing with an enacted obligation, and Blumer distinguishes a mandatory trigger from a permissible means; none requires the Court to prescribe Enron's preferred remedy. Gulf Power's demand to preserve applicable classifications and formulas does not remove the actual independent ground present here.

The holding establishes no permanent bundled-service exemption. A universal allocation of bundled-service jurisdiction beyond the authority needed for this order falls outside the particular remedial demand and is not decided. The demand for compulsory additional unbundling in this proceeding is fully resolved.


## Precedent Treatment

- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board: applied to distinguish an affirmative federal statutory assignment from the independent constraints on its exercise and the functions Congress preserves for States.
- National Cable & Telecommunications Assn. v. Gulf Power Co.: applied to separate coverage from the validity of a particular regulatory method; its actual formula and classification limits remain.
- United States v. Mead Corp.: its actual-delegation requirement remains; independent statutory construction resolves the transmission category without a new general deference holding.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.: its prohibition on replacing an enacted mandatory obligation remains. This order's sufficient remedy does not dispense with section 206's triggered duty.
- Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala: applied to considered administration within statutory commands and to the requirement of actual agency reasons; counsel's litigation assertions receive no substitute status.
- Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer: applied to distinguish permission to use a method from the independent circumstances in which the statute commands action; no general exemption from a corrective duty follows.


## Law After Decision

Independent interstate transmission serving retail purchasers falls within the FPA transmission grant, subject to the retained facilities, entity, state-function and retail-wheeling boundaries. Section 206 remains mandatory upon its stated hearing and unlawfulness finding. This order's separate determination that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary sustains its limited remedy; neither the possession of regulatory power nor Enron's preference makes that additional remedy compulsory here. The Court establishes no permanent bundled-transmission exemption or general allocation of bundled-service jurisdiction.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, joined by Scalia and Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The three join the unbundled-transmission holding and affirmance in No. 00-568. They would vacate the Enron component and require FERC to reconsider under the proper statutory understanding. Interstate transmission does not cease to be transmission merely because a utility sells it with energy for one charge. Section 206's mandatory corrective duty must be assessed without a categorical jurisdictional exclusion. The agency's coexisting unnecessary-remedy statement is real, but insufficiently explained and independent of its contrary account of jurisdiction to justify the refusal. MCI requires compliance with actual statutory obligations; Gulf Power keeps coverage and an adequately reasoned regulatory choice separate; the reasoning requirements preserved in Mead cannot be satisfied by probable power or counsel's substitute explanation. The three would require an agency determination of the relevant discrimination and an appropriate lawful response. They do not find every bundled transaction unlawful, relieve Enron of its burden, or direct immediate adoption of the open-access tariff. Existing wholesale and unbundled-retail requirements would remain while the agency supplied the necessary explanation.

The reviewed open-access requirements continue in force, and the request for compulsory additional unbundling in this proceeding is denied. This merits review concludes without an agency remand. # Edelman v. Lynchburg College — 2002-03-19

[Authoritative Record](../records/edelman-v-lynchburg-college_merits_2002-03-19.md)

## Event

Edelman v. Lynchburg College, No. 00-1072. Argued January 8, 2002; decided March 19, 2002. October Term 2001, chunk 3. On certiorari to the Fourth Circuit. After denial of tenure, Edelman submitted an unverified November 1997 letter within the applicable 300-day period and a verified form on day 313. The Fourth Circuit sustained dismissal because it considered the EEOC's relation-back regulation incompatible with Title VII. Edelman seeks reversal; the College seeks affirmance and preserves the distinct objection that the letter was not a charge. The reviewed question concerns verification timing, not the merits of the discrimination claim.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The judgment is reversed and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reversed and remanded, 9–0.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Fourth Circuit's rule invalidating later verification is reversed. The original letter's status as a charge and the other preserved defenses remain for decision under the lawful relation-back rule.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer | Reverses and remands | Independent statutory construction sustaining later verification of an otherwise sufficient timely charge; Thomas joins with his stated procedural-authority understanding |
| Concurring opinion | Thomas | No other Justice | Joins the Court and judgment | Express procedural delegation, reasonableness, consistency with Title VII and compliance with the Administrative Procedure Act |
| Opinion concurring in the judgment | O'Connor | Scalia | Reverses and remands | Reasonable delegated procedural choice within statutory ambiguity; does not join the Court's independent best-reading rationale |

## Holdings

### A timely charge may receive later verification

**Holding and operative rule:** An otherwise sufficient Title VII charge filed within the applicable filing period may receive its required verification afterward through 29 C.F.R. section 1601.12(b), with the verification relating back to the timely charge. Verification remains mandatory and must protect the employer before it is obliged to answer the substance; the cure does not extend the filing deadline or turn a document that otherwise is not a charge into one.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer, giving seven direct votes for this independent statutory construction. O'Connor and Scalia support the same operative cure and judgment on their distinct delegated-choice ground; that rationale is not added to the Court's reasoning through vote aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that filing and verification are distinct requirements whose performance need not be simultaneous. Section 706(b) requires a written charge under oath or affirmation. Section 706(e)(1) supplies the filing period. Neither provision defines a charge as necessarily verified at the instant of its first timely submission. Prompt filing addresses staleness; verification assures that the complainant adopts the accusation under oath before the employer must respond on the merits.

The College would treat the verification requirement as part of the filing deadline merely by combining the two provisions. That reading adds a simultaneity command that Congress did not state. The regulation instead preserves both obligations: a timely charge initiates the process, and an actual later oath completes the formal requirement. The process is commonly initiated by laypersons, as Love and Commercial Office Products recognize, but ease of filing does not excuse either enacted obligation.

Section 713(a) expressly authorizes procedural rules. This relation-back rule is procedural, reasonable, consistent with the statute and promulgated in conformity with the Administrative Procedure Act. Becker provides a useful analogy for completing a timely filing through an authorized cure; Title VII and this procedural grant, rather than Becker itself, authorize this cure. The independent reading is sufficient without resolving the full degree of administrative deference.

The November letter preceded the applicable 300-day deadline; the verified form arrived on day 313. Those dates make the verification issue concrete but do not decide whether the letter otherwise constituted a charge. That independently preserved question, the subsequent correspondence and the effect of the agency's failure to give notice within ten days require the lower court's analysis on remand. Agency delay is not itself an adjudicated bar or proof that the initial document failed.

## Precedent Treatment

Becker v. Montgomery is applied by analogy to distinguish completion of an existing timely filing from initiation of a late proceeding; its express cure rule is not transplanted into Title VII as independent authority.

Zipes v. Trans World Airlines, Inc. supplies the distinction between the timely-charge condition and subject-matter jurisdiction, without deciding every processing requirement's classification.

Love v. Pullman Co. and EEOC v. Commercial Office Products Co. inform the lay-initiated administrative process; they do not authorize disregard of the actual filing deadline or oath requirement.

United States v. Mead Corp. and Christensen v. Harris County retain their distinctions between delegated legal force and persuasive interpretation. The Court's independent construction makes a broader deference ruling unnecessary; O'Connor and Scalia rely on the actual procedural delegation for their separate ground.

Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit retains its source-based rejection of invented pleading requirements in its own field. Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A. governs the later federal complaint, not what constitutes a timely administrative charge.

EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. retains the distinction between statutory enforcement authority and the actual prerequisites for relief; it supplies no independent verification-timing rule.

## Law After Decision

An otherwise sufficient timely Title VII charge may be verified after the filing period under the lawful relation-back regulation. Seven Justices adopt the independent statutory explanation; two concur in the judgment on a reasonable delegated-choice rationale. Actual verification, timely initiation and charge sufficiency remain separate requirements. The ruling governs verification cure, leaving the underlying employment-practice accrual question to its own statutory analysis.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring.** He joins the Court on the understanding that section 713(a) expressly delegates procedural rulemaking, this regulation is reasonable, Title VII does not prohibit it and its promulgation complies with the Administrative Procedure Act. All four conditions matter. The rule completes verification of an existing timely charge; it neither waives a statutory deadline nor recognizes an unrestricted equitable power to excuse procedural requirements. His qualification accompanies his full join in the Court's explanation, rather than substituting a judgment-only ground.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, concurring in the judgment.** Reading the provisions together most naturally calls for a verified charge within the filing period. But the statute does not expressly define charge in a way that unambiguously compels that reading. Section 713(a)'s procedural delegation therefore permits the EEOC's reasonable alternative, which preserves both timely initiation and later verified accountability. The rule's binding operation and notice-and-comment repromulgation in 1977 distinguish it from the revisable, nonbinding tariff letters addressed in Mead. Commercial Office Products supports deference on this technical agency procedure. The concurrence also questions how recognition of a possible future reasonable agency change can be reconciled with wholly reserving the deference issue. Neither Justice joins the assertion that the current rule is the best independent statutory reading. Both preserve actual verification, the employer's protection before a required merits response and the separate inquiry into whether the initial letter was a charge.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Fourth Circuit for analysis of whether the original letter was a charge, the preserved significance of later correspondence and the agency's ten-day notice failure, and any remaining defenses under the correct verification rule. Ordinary mandate procedure applies.

## Source Notes

Title VII sections 706(b), 706(e)(1) and 713(a); 29 C.F.R. section 1601.12(b); Becker, 532 U.S. 757; Zipes, 455 U.S. 385; Love, 404 U.S. 522; Commercial Office Products, 486 U.S. 107. The original document's independent charge status and the preserved processing issues were not resolved by the verification ruling.

# Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc. — 2002-03-19

[Authoritative Record](../records/ragsdale-v-wolverine-world-wide-inc_merits_2002-03-19.md)

## Event

Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc., No. 00-6029. Argued January 7, 2002; decided March 19, 2002. October Term 2001, chunk 3. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit's affirmance of summary judgment against Ragsdale's FMLA claim. She seeks an additional statutory allotment because Wolverine did not designate her company medical leave as FMLA leave; Wolverine seeks affirmance. She began employment March 17, 1995 and cancer leave February 21, 1996. The district court treated her as eligible at the March 18 extension. Wolverine provided approximately seven months of leave and discharged her September 20; her further leave or reduced-hours request followed September 26. Her physician released her in December. The reviewed question concerns the automatic consequence of absent designation, separately from the underlying notice duty.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Eighth Circuit's judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Affirmed, 5–4. The judgment rejecting Ragsdale's claimed automatic additional leave stands.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, who would sustain the designation condition and remand for application of the rule and the separate statutory requirements for relief.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas | Affirms | Invalidates the challenged automatic no-count consequence; preserves lawful notice regulation and supported harmful-interference remedies |
| Dissenting opinion | O'Connor | Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Would reverse and remand | Treats timely designation as a lawful condition on counting company leave, while retaining individual liability and remedy predicates |

## Holdings

### Missing designation cannot automatically create a new twelve-week allotment without impairment of protected leave rights

**Holding and operative rule:** The Secretary's implementation authority does not permit former 29 C.F.R. section 825.700(a)'s categorical consequence that undesignated qualifying leave never counts against the FMLA entitlement, thereby creating an additional twelve-week allotment without demonstrated impairment of statutory rights. An employee may obtain relief for a notice-related violation when the applicable duty, record and statutory remedy conditions establish harmful interference; invalidating this automatic consequence does not invalidate every notice duty or make all company leave invariably concurrent with statutory leave.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion is joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia and Thomas. Those five direct joins control the rule, its limits and affirmance.

**Operative entitlement and remedy limits:** The Act grants an eligible employee a total of twelve workweeks in a twelve-month period for qualifying family or medical leave. Greater contractual or company rights remain protected. Section 2617 requires a section 2615 violation affecting the employee. Recoverable lost compensation must result from that violation; absent compensation loss, actual monetary loss must directly result and is capped at twelve weeks' wages or salary. Interest is added to the compensation or monetary-loss amount, and liquidated damages equal that amount plus interest. The court may reduce liquidated liability when the employer proves both good faith and reasonable grounds. Appropriate equitable relief, including employment, reinstatement or promotion, is separately available; monetary loss is not an invariable prerequisite to every equitable remedy. These conditions cannot be replaced by the automatic additional-leave rule.

**Regulatory timing and exceptions:** Former section 825.208 ordinarily required designation within two business days after the employer obtained qualifying information, absent extenuating circumstances. The employee need not name the Act but must give a qualifying reason, and inadequate information can require inquiry. Oral notice requires written confirmation by the next payday, or the following payday if the first is less than a week away. An employer with qualifying information that misses timely designation generally may designate only prospectively. Later-acquired information can permit retrospective counting of the qualifying period. Designation after return is barred except for two circumstances. When the employer first learns the reason upon return, retrospective designation with appropriate notice is permitted within two business days of return. An employee seeking retrospective protection from an unaware employer must also give the reason within two business days of return; without timely notice, the employee may not later assert protection for that absence. When the reason is known but qualification or certification, including a second or third opinion, is pending, the employer should provisionally designate and notify at the start of leave or when the reason becomes known. Confirmation makes the designation final; medical certifications failing to confirm a qualifying reason require withdrawal with written notice. Intermittent or reduced-schedule leave ordinarily requires one notice unless circumstances change. No retrospective exception is established on this record.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that useful notice and a lawful sanction for its omission are different questions. Designation can enable an employee to make informed choices about protected leave. But section 2654's implementing grant does not authorize a consequence that treats qualifying leave already received as legally irrelevant regardless of whether the omission impaired those choices. The statutory total and section 2617's employee-specific conditions must still do their work.

The contrary view calls designation a condition on counting company leave rather than an extra entitlement. That characterization does not answer the rule's operation: after an unexcused omission, it confers a new full allotment even where correct notice could not have changed the employee's use of protected leave. Greater-benefit provisions preserve company promises; they do not compel sequential stacking of every company and statutory benefit. Employer generosity likewise does not excuse independently harmful interference.

The regulation's timing and information safeguards mean that not every first-day omission is incurable. They nevertheless do not cure the categorical consequence once it applies. The decision therefore addresses that consequence without treating its actual exceptions as unavailable in every case.

Mead recognizes genuine delegated rulemaking but preserves statutory limits. MCI likewise bars replacement of an enacted obligation. Mourning permits supported prophylactic implementation, not every sanction that might simplify administration. McKennon and Waffle House distinguish a violation from the remedy the particular record permits. These principles leave room for notice enforcement without this automatic additional entitlement.

Ragsdale received more than twelve weeks of leave after the district court's March 18 eligibility date. Her September 26 requests followed the September 20 discharge; her physician released her in December. The record supplies no demonstrated alternative use of protected leave lost because of absent designation. The Court therefore affirms the judgment on the categorical theory presented rather than creating an unsupported alternative claim. The general validity and precise content of other notice duties fall outside this reviewed consequence.

## Precedent Treatment

MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. is applied to require an implementing rule to preserve the enacted statutory scheme; no general hostility to prophylactic regulation is adopted.

United States v. Mead Corp. and Christensen v. Harris County retain the distinction between delegated binding rules, advisory interpretations and statutory validity. A formal rulemaking grant does not authorize contradiction of the statute.

Mourning v. Family Publications Service, Inc. is distinguished at the scope of its authorized prophylactic implementation; it supplies no unlimited power to alter the substantive entitlement and individual relief conditions.

McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co. and EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. are applied at the distinction between a violation and lawful individual relief. Neither supplies a direct FMLA damages formula or makes monetary loss necessary for every equitable remedy.

New York v. FERC retains its separation of statutory authority, a mandatory corrective trigger and a sufficient actual agency remedy. Its FPA holding neither requires nor validates this different automatic counting consequence.

Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer and National Cable & Telecommunications Assn. v. Gulf Power Co. retain their requirements to respect actual statutory predicates and operative classifications; neither supplies a general regulatory exception to the FMLA's conditions.

Raygor v. Regents of University of Minnesota and Young v. United States retain their statute-specific timing holdings. Neither establishes a universal rule against procedural protection or authorizes enlargement of every statutory benefit.

## Law After Decision

The challenged automatic no-count consequence cannot create an additional twelve-week FMLA entitlement without impairment of the employee's protected rights. Applicable notice obligations and claims for actual harmful interference retain their independent scope, as do greater company rights. The majority's rule invalidates this consequence rather than every designation rule, all prophylactic regulation or every equitable remedy without monetary loss.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** The FMLA does not give an employer an unconditional right to count all qualifying company leave retroactively against the protected allotment. Section 2654 permits the Secretary to require timely designation as a condition on that counting. The rule identifies which leave consumes the statutory right, rather than creating a monetary award for every omission. Greater-benefit provisions and the need for usable notice support keeping undesignated company leave distinct from the statutory allotment.

The rule's safeguards matter. Designation follows qualifying information; the ordinary two-business-day period permits extenuating circumstances. Later knowledge and provisional designation pending certification can permit the specified retrospective operation. The dissent does not treat every first-day failure as irreversible or require all company benefits to run sequentially. Nor does it assume that Ragsdale was eligible when she first stopped work in February; the supported eligibility date is the March extension.

The majority's lack-of-prejudice objection, in the dissent's view, confuses the entitlement's lawful definition with the requirements for an actual award. A predictable designation rule makes leave usable without forcing an employee to reconstruct every hypothetical choice. Mourning supports appropriate prophylactic implementation, and Christensen's distinction between an advisory position and a valid regulation leaves this delegated rule open for independent evaluation. MCI's explicit filing duty and Sigmon Coal's missing liable-person category differ from the absence of an unconditional concurrent-counting command here.

The four would reverse and remand to apply the undiminished entitlement and then determine the actual violation and appropriate relief under section 2617. They do not infer earlier work capacity, a medically available reduced schedule, actual monetary loss or automatic reinstatement from the omitted designation. Ragsdale's postdischarge request and December release retain their actual dates. Ordinary employer and employee notice obligations remain distinct, and no paid-leave finding is supplied.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment against the asserted categorical additional-leave theory stands, and this merits review concludes. Ordinary mandate procedure applies. The decision establishes the stated limit on the regulation without reopening an unsupported alternative claim.

29 U.S.C. sections 2612, 2615, 2617, 2619 and 2652–2654; former 29 C.F.R. sections 825.208 and 825.700(a); Eighth Circuit judgment, 218 F.3d 933. Posted-notice penalties under section 2619 are distinct from the individualized designation rule. The record does not establish paid leave, earlier work capacity, a qualifying reduced schedule or an alternative course lost through deficient notice.

# S.S. ex rel. Jervis v. McMullen — 2002-03-26

[Authoritative Record](../records/s-s-ex-rel-jervis-v-mcmullen_merits_2002-03-26.md)

## Event

S.S. ex rel. Jervis v. McMullen; reviewed judgment, 225 F.3d 960 (8th Cir. 2000). Merits decision, March 26, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 3. Submitted on the full merits record. On review of the Eighth Circuit's September 13, 2000 en banc judgment affirming dismissal of the personal substantive-due-process action. The questions concern the actual custodial placement of a child into an allegedly appreciated serious danger and the culpability required after an opportunity to deliberate. The child seeks reinstatement; McMullen, Jacoby and Barnett defend the dismissal and retain their individual defenses. The lower court relied both on the child's exposure before state intervention and on insufficient constitutional culpability. The earlier panel judgment was vacated. March 8, 1996 began full-time residence with the father on an extended visit while the Division retained legal custody; the August 22 judicial order subsequently returned legal custody. The alleged assaults occurred November 15, 1996 and January 15, 1997, after that order.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The categorical dismissal of the reviewed personal claim is vacated, and the case is remanded for further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The action returns for adjudication under the bounded actual-custody and culpability rules. Each official's personal conduct, contemporaneous knowledge, authority, response, causation, supported defenses and attributable injury require separate treatment. The state court's custody order retains its legal effect and must be considered at its actual scope. The excluded claim against Montgomery is not reinstated.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Stone-Zsela | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Vacates and remands | Entire opinion: actual custodial placement, deliberate unreasonable disregard, individualized acts and causal role, judicial-order limits and conduct-date defenses |

All nine join the complete bounded opinion. It does not decide an independent noncustodial claim based on the August request alone.

## Holdings

**Common authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine adopt the actual-custody rule, its culpability requirements, individualized application and preserved defenses below. No broader noncustodial danger doctrine or narrowest-grounds aggregation supplies the decision.

### I. Actual custodial placement is an affirmative act subject to review

**Holding and operative rule:** Officials exercising actual custody over a child may not knowingly use their placement authority to expose the child to a specifically appreciated serious danger through deliberate and unreasonable disregard when they have authority and a real opportunity to respond. Each defendant's knowing participation, authority, unreasonable choice and resulting attributable injury must be established; a prior encounter with the same danger does not categorically erase the official's later affirmative custodial act.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the relevant starting point is the protection and control the State actually exercises when it chooses the placement. City of Florence v. Chipman applies the protection against knowingly using actual restraint to deliver a person into an appreciated serious danger. Youngberg supplies the security principle arising from actual custody. That bounded principle extends to a personally directed protective-custody placement; it is not a general guarantee of competent social services.

DeShaney preserves the rule that officials ordinarily have no constitutional duty to protect a person already in private custody from private violence. Its discussion of returning a child to an earlier danger cannot categorically dispose of a separately culpable exercise of actual custody and placement power. Otherwise an official's knowing choice would disappear from constitutional analysis merely because the child had previously faced danger in that home. The State's actual intervention does not make it a permanent guarantor, but neither can it be treated as never having exercised control.

The complaint alleges state selection and continuation of S.S.'s full-time residence with her father while the Division retained custody. These alleged affirmative acts differ from a request that the State supply protection generally. A biological relationship and a legitimate reunification objective do not by themselves establish that every placement choice was reasonable or legally compelled. Actual control and placement authority are essential; a legal-custody label, nominal supervision or a voluntary private decision alone is insufficient. The rule requires neither removal of every child from danger, indefinite state custody, investigation of every warning nor a guarantee that a reasonable reunification succeeds.

### II. The pleaded deliberate choice can satisfy constitutional culpability

**Holding and operative rule:** A considered custodial placement made with actual appreciation of a specific grave danger and deliberate, unreasonable disregard of a meaningful opportunity to avoid it can constitute the conscience-shocking abuse of custody that substantive due process forbids. On the detailed allegations here, intent to cause the eventual abuse is not indispensable, and the action cannot be dismissed by categorically treating the alleged official choice as negligence.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the demanding actual-custody rule rather than an unrestricted inquiry into whether an outcome is shocking. Daniels excludes negligence. Chipman requires personal awareness, actual authority, opportunity and unreasonable disregard in an affirmative custodial transfer. Lewis's purpose-to-harm rule concerns urgent pursuit decisions made amid instant competing obligations; it does not impose that same requirement on every considered custody decision. Farmer informs actual awareness, permissible inference and reasonable response without transferring prison law wholesale to child protection.

The allegations describe specific warnings of sexual-abuse danger and considered placement decisions, not only a poor prediction followed by harm. If proved, actual appreciation and deliberate unreasonable exposure within available authority can satisfy the required culpability. The assailant's conduct does not establish that the officials themselves acted unconstitutionally, and time to deliberate alone proves neither actual knowledge nor unreasonable choice.

Lawful family-restoration duties and the official's actual alternatives bear on reasonableness. A reasonable professional choice among lawful alternatives remains protected by this substantive rule even if it fails. But a general reunification label cannot settle the contested reasonableness of a particular allegedly knowing exposure at the pleading stage. McMullen's July investigation must be considered as part of her response; its occurrence proves neither that she acted reasonably nor that she appreciated and disregarded the danger. The complaint permits ordinary adjudication under these limits. The rule creates no fixed investigation protocol or constitutional right to the safest or preferred home.

### III. Each placement act, information date and causal contribution must remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Liability requires proof of each official's own knowing use of actual custodial placement authority and injury attributable to that act, assessed with the information and lawful options then available. Earlier physical placement, continued decisions during retained custody, and a later judicial transfer of legal custody cannot be treated as one undifferentiated act; an independent, untainted judicial decision may defeat attribution, while its existence does not establish that every earlier official choice was compelled.

**Controlling explanation:** March 8, 1996 began full-time residence on an extended visit while the Division retained legal custody. The August 22 court order later returned legal custody after McMullen's request. The alleged assaults occurred on November 15, 1996 and January 15, 1997. Because the injuries followed the judicial return, the claimant must establish the causal connection from an actionable earlier official act; the Court does not assume abuse during foster custody or disregard the intervening judicial decision.

The complaint attributes different information and acts to different defendants. McMullen allegedly received the September 1995 warning, continuing-contact reports, the May evaluation, July report and August 13 call. Jacoby allegedly documented warnings, symptoms and reported contact. Barnett allegedly received and approved reports and participated in placement decisions, including the August responsibility decision. These allegations permit individual inquiry, not collective knowledge or liability by supervisory title. Later information cannot establish what an official knew in March, although it may inform a later decision within retained authority.

The lower court must determine the actual order's constraints, each official's personal role and the causal significance of recommendations and implementation. A truthful recommendation, constrained execution, discretionary placement and deliberate deception are distinct functions; deception is not presumed here. Zahrey preserves personal causation and the possible significance of genuinely independent process, not automatic responsibility for every foreseeable consequence. Whether an August request detached from actual custody independently supports a noncustodial danger claim is left open because the order's full constraints and the request's actual relation to custodial implementation are not established. That reservation does not diminish the actual-custody rule governing the pleaded placement and its continuation.

### IV. Ordinary proceedings and conduct-date personal defenses remain

### Hazen Paper Co. v. Biggins, No. 91-1600

Vacatur and remand, 1993-04-20

Argued 1993-01-13; decided 1993-04-20. Reported source: 507 U.S. 604 (1993). First Circuit, 953 F.2d 1405. The case is before the Court on writ of certiorari to that court.

Question: Whether interference with a service-based pension establishes ADEA disparate treatment, and whether Thurston’s willfulness rule governs an individual discharge.

Biggins was 62 and near the ten-year vesting point when discharged. A jury found ADEA liability and willfulness along with separate claims. The district court set aside willfulness; the First Circuit reinstated it and relied heavily on pension interference for age liability. The record also includes age-related remarks, a younger replacement and differing confidentiality requirements. The employer invoked competitive disloyalty. The sufficiency of this remaining evidence requires reconsideration; other claims are not extinguished.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participated in argument and decision.

The Court vacates and remands, 9–0. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support the disposition of ADEA liability and willfulness. Justice O’Connor delivers the Opinion of the Court, Part I on liability, Part II on willfulness and Part III on remand, joined by all eight other Justices. Justice Kennedy concurs, joined by Thomas, emphasizing the unresolved question of disparate impact.

ADEA disparate treatment requires age actually to motivate the employment decision and have a determinative influence; pension interference based only on years of service does not by itself establish that requirement. Liability remains possible when pension status is deliberately used as an age proxy or age and pension status both motivate the decision; age-triggered vesting and disparate impact are not decided.

The holding is adopted in O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, I by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas. It controls as the rule of that 9-Justice majority.

The Court holds that correlation is not the statutory cause. A worker’s years with an employer and that worker’s age are analytically distinct, so avoiding a ten-year service-based pension does not necessarily show action because of age. Aikens and Burdine direct attention to the employer’s actual motivation, which may be established through direct or circumstantial proof. Ingersoll-Rand confirms that pension interference may violate ERISA without thereby establishing an ADEA violation. That distinction creates no pension safe harbor. An employer may deliberately target pension status because it identifies older employees, and age and pension concerns may both play a determinative role. Here the younger replacement, confidentiality demands and comments require reconsideration after removing the mistaken equivalence. The Court neither finds those facts sufficient nor treats every improper explanation as proof of age discrimination. It leaves age-based vesting, disparate impact and the separate non-ADEA claims outside this decision.

The Court applies Aikens, 460 U.S. 711, and Burdine, 450 U.S. 248: actual motivation may be proved circumstantially; no automatic inference from any unlawful motive.

The Court distinguishes Ingersoll-Rand, 498 U.S. 133: ERISA pension interference and age discrimination remain distinct.

The Court preserves McDonnell Douglas, 411 U.S. 792; its legitimate-reason terminology does not make every improper motive an ADEA violation.

An ADEA disparate-treatment violation is willful when the employer knew or recklessly disregarded whether its conduct violated the Act, whether the decision was individual or a formal policy. Once that showing is made, liquidated damages require no additional outrageousness, direct evidence or predominant-age motive; a good-faith, nonreckless mistake about a statutory permission is not willfulness.

The holding is adopted in O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court, II by Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas. It controls as the rule of that 9-Justice majority.

The Court retains Thurston’s knowledge-or-reckless-disregard standard. Nothing in the term willful changes when age discrimination occurs through a single discharge rather than an announced policy. Richland Shoe confirms that mere negligence or awareness that the statute might apply is insufficient. This preserves the two tiers Congress created without adding requirements found nowhere in the governing standard. An employer can intentionally consider age yet nonrecklessly and in good faith misunderstand a statutory defense or exemption, such as a bona fide occupational qualification; an incorrect understanding may leave ordinary liability without willful liability. Conversely, keeping the discriminatory motive private does not justify a stricter evidentiary barrier than an openly discriminatory policy receives. Direct evidence is unnecessary, outrageousness is not an additional element, and age need not be predominant rather than determinative. Because underlying liability must first be reconsidered, the Court awards or removes no liquidated damages itself.

The Court reaffirms Thurston, 469 U.S. 111 and applied to individual disparate-treatment decisions.

The Court applies Richland Shoe, 486 U.S. 128: negligence and mere awareness of statutory relevance are not willfulness.

Age/tenure distinction clarified and Thurston’s individual-decision application confirmed April 20. No later causation doctrine or disparate-impact rule adopted.

Kennedy, joined by Thomas, concurs while emphasizing that Biggins brought disparate treatment alone. He regards importing Title VII disparate-impact doctrine into the ADEA as presenting substantial unresolved objections and understands the Court to decide none of them. The Court’s express reservation supports his join; his skepticism is not a holding. Stone does not join this additional theory.
