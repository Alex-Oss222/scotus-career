## Event

People Who Care v. Rockford Board of Education, School District No. 205; review of Seventh Circuit No. 00-3200, 246 F.3d 1073. Merits decision, June 27, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 8. The Seventh Circuit's April 18, 2001 judgment ordered dissolution of the desegregation decree effective June 30, 2002. The board seeks release; the student plaintiffs seek continued protection against consequences of the adjudicated discrimination. The question is whether the district established the complete release and causal showing and whether the appellate court properly reviewed the retained obligations and findings.

The unappealed 1994 intentional-discrimination determination led to the remedial decree. The district achieved substantial school-assignment integration and undertook extensive expenditures and programs; completion of the remaining educational work, good faith and the causes of achievement and advanced-course disparities remained contested. The appellate court treated assignment integration, expenditure and the plaintiffs' failure to partition residual causes as grounds for termination. Settled liability and previously adjudicated remedy provisions are outside this review. This case concerns release from the existing decree, not university admissions or a general school-finance adequacy right.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

## Public Action

Vacate the direction to terminate the decree effective June 30, 2002, and remand, 9–0.

## Holdings

### I. The complete release showing governs every obligation proposed for termination

**Holding and operative rule:** A school district seeking release from an adjudicated desegregation obligation must establish full and satisfactory compliance, elimination of the relevant vestiges to the extent practicable, and demonstrated good faith toward the whole decree. A court may release a completed domain while retaining supervision elsewhere only after separately assessing whether continued control in that domain is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere; release may not obstruct the remaining remedy, and satisfaction of the prerequisites permits, rather than compels, partial withdrawal.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join the rule, its qualifications and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that release turns on completion of the constitutional remedy. Freeman permits domain-specific withdrawal but requires the district's full showing, attention to whole-decree conduct and an examination of remedial connections. Jenkins applies that rule and distinguishes installing or funding programs from eliminating the injury those programs address. Dowell requires restoration of local control when the required work is complete; it does not substitute age or expense for completion.

Rockford's demonstrated progress in school assignments is substantial evidence for the domains it addresses. It does not alone determine whether retained educational measures still repair consequences of the adjudicated violation. Conversely, a disparity does not itself establish an unfinished constitutional duty. National achievement parity, maximum educational potential and every desirable program goal are not conditions of release. Courts must identify the remaining violation-linked work rather than supervise schools for general improvement.

Good faith is shown by conduct toward the whole decree, not compulsory enthusiasm. Elected officials may criticize the decree; criticism alone neither establishes noncompliance nor excuses it. Completed work and unfinished duties must be evaluated together where their operation connects. The court must decide whether control over a completed domain remains necessary or practicable for compliance elsewhere, assess nonobstruction independently, and define released duties and retained supervision. Rufo's modification rule does not replace this completion inquiry or add a changed-circumstances condition to release from fulfilled obligations.

### II. The district bears the causal showing, with ordinary protection for settled findings

**Holding and operative rule:** The district must show that remaining imbalance or educational conditions for which it disclaims remedial responsibility are not traceable to its adjudicated violation; the students need not establish that violation anew or supply a particular comparative study to preserve the existing burden. Existing findings receive clear-error review, but findings about one domain do not decide unexamined causal and remedial connections in another.

**Authority:** Part II; all nine Justices directly join the causal allocation and review standard.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the request for release carries the causal burden established in Freeman and reaffirmed in Jenkins. Genuinely independent causes do not impose perpetual racial balancing or make the district responsible for every social inequality. But the appellate demand that the plaintiffs partition all remaining achievement differences cannot replace the district's showing concerning the consequences of its own adjudicated discrimination.

The lower court must weigh the actual record. Testimony, records, program operation and sound inferences may establish the relevant causes; a new statistical comparison is neither invariably necessary nor invariably sufficient. Substantial assignment integration and open advanced-course enrollment deserve their supported weight, without automatically resolving every retained educational obligation. Neither general social causes nor a reported absence of newly identified decree violations alone completes the showing.

Freeman protects properly settled findings against appellate replacement absent clear error. It also requires resolution of causal and remedial questions those findings did not answer. The remand applies both requirements. It is not a general retrial of the 1994 liability determination or an instruction to substitute new appellate factual conclusions for supported findings.

### III. The June 30 termination direction must be vacated

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical direction to terminate the decree effective June 30, 2002 is vacated because the appellate reasoning does not establish the complete release and causal showing. On remand the lower courts must apply Parts I and II, identify any supported full or partial release and define the obligations and supervision that remain justified.

**Authority:** Part III; all nine Justices join the disposition and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the termination command rests on an incomplete legal inquiry. Neither duration and expense nor successful assignment integration permits the district's burden to be replaced by a demand for new proof from the students. The displaced June 30 direction supplies no dissolution of the existing decree. The lower courts must make a timely, supported determination of what constitutional work remains and end supervision where that work is complete. The remand imposes no automatic additional supervision period and presumes neither that every retained measure remains necessary nor that every obligation has been fulfilled.

## Precedent Treatment

- Freeman v. Pitts, March 31, 1992: applied in full to permissive partial withdrawal, the district's release and causal burdens, whole-decree good faith, retained-control necessity or practicability, nonobstruction and clear-error review.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, June 12, 1995: applied to distinguish completed violation-linked repair from program installation and spending; its complete Freeman rule governs release.
- Green v. School Board of New Kent County, 391 U.S. 430: the affirmative duty to repair the adjudicated violation continues across unfinished obligations.
- Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1: effective equitable means remain bounded by the violation and the work needed to repair it.
- Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237: temporary supervision ends when the requisite good faith and practicable elimination are established; age and expense do not replace that showing.
- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, January 15, 1992: its distinct institutional-decree modification rule supplies no replacement for completion-based release or added changed-circumstances requirement.
- Ruiz v. United States (Ruiz v. Johnson), March 4, 2002: its PLRA statutory termination inquiry remains distinct from the Freeman/Jenkins school-decree standard.

## Law After Decision

The district bears the complete showing for release, including the causal showing for conditions it disclaims. Completed assignment work is relevant without deciding every remaining educational obligation. Partial release remains discretionary, with a separate inquiry into retained-control necessity or practicability and nonobstruction. The Court changes no governing test; the lower courts must apply the existing rule to the actual retained duties and supported findings.

## Separate Writings

No separate opinion is filed. All nine Justices join the opinion and judgment in full.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Seventh Circuit for application of the complete release and causal standard. The existing decree is not dissolved by the vacated June 30 direction. The receiving courts must make a timely supported determination of completed and unfinished constitutional work, retaining only justified supervision and ending it where the required work is complete. Supreme Court review is concluded subject to ordinary mandate administration.
