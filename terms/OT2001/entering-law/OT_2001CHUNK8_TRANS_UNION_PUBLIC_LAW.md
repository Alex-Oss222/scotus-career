## Event

Trans Union Corp. v. Federal Trade Commission; review of D.C. Circuit No. 00-1141, 245 F.3d 809 and 267 F.3d 1138. Merits decision, June 24, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 8. On review of the denial of relief from the FTC order, including the reported rehearing clarification, on the developed merits record. The question is whether restricting sale of targeted marketing lists compiled from consumer credit records violates the agency's First Amendment rights. Trans Union seeks relief from the restriction; the FTC seeks its enforcement within the FCRA's permissible-use scheme. The lists selected consumers through credit-file attributes and conveyed names and addresses; membership could communicate the selected financial trait. Statutory classification and administrative-law claims outside this First Amendment review are not reopened.

### Statutory setting of the comparison

These are the statute's permissions and duties, not findings that any consumer, reporting agency, recipient or offer complied.

**Consumer direction and financial purposes.** Section 604(a)(2), 15 U.S.C. §1681b(a)(2), permits furnishing in accordance with the consumer's written instructions; silence or failure to opt out is not an instruction. Section 604(c)(1)(A) separately permits consumer authorization for the agency to provide that report to that recipient in a credit or insurance transaction not initiated by the consumer. That clause does not itself require a writing and does not authorize unrelated marketing generally. For the credit and insurance routes, the agency must have reason to believe the recipient intends use for credit extension, account review or collection involving the consumer, or insurance underwriting involving the consumer. Under section 604(a)(3)(F), the agency must have reason to believe that the recipient has a legitimate business need in connection with a consumer-initiated transaction or to review an account for continued compliance with its terms. Section 603(m) excludes a relationship holder's review of the consumer's existing account or insurance policy, or collection of that account, from the unsolicited-transaction definition; it does not create a general new-marketing permission.

**Offers without affirmative authorization.** Section 604(c)(1)(B) permits the unsolicited credit or insurance route only when the transaction consists of a firm offer, the agency has complied with subsection (e), and no effective subsection (e) election excludes the consumer's name and address. Under section 603(l), the offer must be honored if the consumer meets the specific consumer-report criteria used for selection. Further conditions may require application information establishing satisfaction of specific creditworthiness or insurability criteria fixed before selection for deciding whether to extend the offered credit or insurance; verification of continuing satisfaction of selection criteria through a report, application or other relevant creditworthiness or insurability information, or verification of application information to determine satisfaction of the applicable creditworthiness or insurability criteria; and collateral required before selection and disclosed in the offer. These are conditional offers, not guaranteed extensions, and the statute supplies no minimum economic value or favorable-rate requirement.

**Information and use.** Under section 604(c)(2), that route permits only names and addresses, a nonunique identifier used solely to verify identity, and other information that does not identify the consumer's relationship or experience with a particular creditor or other entity. The last category and list membership can disclose a financial trait; the permission is not confined to two address fields. Section 604(c)(3) separately bars furnishing unsolicited-transaction inquiry records except section 609(a)(5)'s disclosure to the consumer, on request with proper identification, of inquiries identifying that consumer during the preceding year. Section 604(f) separately requires an authorized furnishing purpose and a prospective user's general or specific purpose certification under section 607. A permitted transfer does not authorize unrestricted subsequent use.

**Consumer elections and agency duties.** Section 604(e) allows notice through the agency's notification system or its signed election form. Exclusion begins five business days after notice and applies to affiliates. System-only notice lasts two years beginning five business days after notice, unless cancelled sooner; a signed-form election continues until cancellation through the system. The agency must explain the two-year limit after system notice and provide the signed form within five business days if requested during that notice. Its system must include a toll-free telephone number and allow notice with appropriate identification. Publication at least annually in a general-circulation publication for the service area must describe possible file use and give the exclusion address and toll-free number; the original deadline was 365 days after September 30, 1996. A qualifying system and publication maintained for affiliates satisfies those obligations for them; nationwide reporting agencies must maintain the system jointly. These safeguards do not make an election immediately effective, a telephone election perpetual, or general publication proof of individual awareness.

**Separate duties of users.** Section 615(d), 15 U.S.C. §1681m(d), requires each written solicitation based on the firm-offer route to provide a clear and conspicuous statement disclosing use of report information, satisfaction of selection criteria, any applicable selection or further creditworthiness or insurability criteria or collateral conditions that may prevent extension, the right to prohibit unsolicited-transaction use, and how to exercise that right through the system, including its address and toll-free number. Selection, extension and collateral criteria must be retained for three years from the offer. These are separate user duties, not additional express elements of section 604(c)(1)(B); the written-solicitation trigger neither guarantees notice before first disclosure nor covers every oral communication. Federal and state unfair- or deceptive-practice authority remains. Section 615(c)'s distinct liability defense requires proof by a preponderance that reasonable compliance procedures were maintained at the time of the alleged violation; it does not erase those duties or authorize an otherwise impermissible section 604 purpose.

## Holdings

### I. The identified marketing use receives independent speech scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** Restricting sale of these credit-derived lists for commercial targeting requires independent review under the governing commercial-speech standard, even though the agency lawfully possesses the data and can market through other channels. The government must establish a substantial interest, direct material advancement and a reasonable proportional fit; corporate status, payment and lawful possession do not themselves answer those questions or classify every publication as commercial speech.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the sufficient application in Parts I–III; Thomas retains his broader theoretical qualification without withholding an application join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that names and addresses selected through a financial characteristic communicate that characteristic to a buyer. The product here supplies consumer attributes for commercial targeting. Its classification rests on its actual function, not merely the seller's incorporation or profit. U.S. West requires independent speech scrutiny of a marketing-use condition despite lawful custody and alternative channels. Central Hudson and Fox supply the substantial-interest, advancement and proportionality requirements. The Court applies those requirements to this external sale of credit-derived information. Statutory and administrative-law claims outside the granted review, public-concern publication and independently acquired information are not decided.

### II. Preventing unrelated credit-file repurposing materially protects confidentiality

**Holding and operative rule:** Protecting confidential financial information against unrelated commercial repurposing is substantial, and prohibiting the identified nonconsensual sale of credit-derived marketing classifications directly and materially advances that interest. The authorization, financial-purpose and firm-offer permissions, with the actual conditions stated above, do not defeat that conclusion even though permitted prescreening can disclose the same financial trait without affirmative authorization.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies nine direct votes for this confined advancement determination.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this restriction addresses the disclosure constituting the privacy harm itself. A buyer learns the selected characteristic even without receiving an entire credit report. Preventing the unrelated sale therefore protects the information against a further purpose and recipient; no prediction that consumers will refrain from buying products supplies the causal connection.

The firm-offer permission is a real qualification. It can transmit private selection information before individual notice, without affirmative authorization and without guaranteeing completed credit or insurance. The Court therefore does not describe the Act as preventing every unchosen disclosure. Its financial-purpose boundary, conditionally binding offer, information limits, consumer elections and restricted use instead distinguish a constrained financial transaction from unrestricted unrelated targeting. The residual information category is part of that assessment, not treated as harmless.

Discovery Network and Greater New Orleans require this comparison of actual permissions and prohibitions. Their materially inconsistent schemes do not make every partial confidentiality protection invalid. U.S. West recognizes protection against repurposing but does not transfer its different approval mechanism to this statute. The supported credit-source and external-sale setting supplies the direct relationship here; statutory commands supply no finding of actual compliance or universal consumer awareness.

### III. The actual statutory arrangement reasonably fits this application

**Holding and operative rule:** The FCRA restriction on these unrelated credit-derived marketing lists reasonably fits its confidentiality interest when assessed with the separate consumer-direction routes and the defined financial-purpose, conditional-offer, information, election and user-duty limits; the D.C. Circuit's denial of relief on this First Amendment challenge is affirmed. Fox does not require extending a comparable opt-out regime to unrelated marketing merely because it would burden the seller less, and this holding neither declares opt-out generally inadequate nor makes written authorization constitutionally necessary for every information use.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies nine direct votes for the fit application and confined affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the actual distinction is proportionate. Written consumer instructions authorize disclosure at their own scope; recipient-specific authorization separately operates within unsolicited credit or insurance. The alternative financial route combines an offer enforceable when its bounded criteria are met, restricted information and purpose, effective consumer elections, and separate written-solicitation and retained-criteria duties. Those duties have different actors and triggers and are not interchangeable consent exceptions.

Trans Union's proposed extension of opt-out is substantial: the statute itself shows that such a system can protect some privacy interests. Obtaining written instructions for unrelated use imposes a real burden, unlike U.S. West's flexible oral, written or electronic approval. But the proposed extension would expand the purposes and recipients allowed to obtain credit-derived classifications without consumer direction beyond the specifically constrained financial transaction. The credit-source relationship and the external repurposing at issue justify requiring instructions for that expansion. Fox requires reasonable proportionality, not the least burdensome imaginable arrangement.

The comparison includes the actual remaining privacy loss: the initial disclosure can precede individual solicitation notice, elections take five business days, system-only elections expire after two years, and residual information can reveal financial traits. No minimum offer value, actual awareness or universal compliance is assumed. These limits do not erase the financial transaction and information-use boundaries. Lorillard requires examination of the actual burden; Thompson's rejected product-promotion proxy differs from a restriction directly addressing the confidential disclosure itself. Alternative marketing channels mitigate the burden but do not independently validate it. The order remains confined to the challenged lists and does not reach consented uses, independently collected public information or other statutory permissions.

## Precedent Treatment

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557, and Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469: ordinary commercial-speech scrutiny and reasonable proportional fit are applied without a least-restrictive-means command.
- U.S. West, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission, February 7, 2000: independent review and the supported confidentiality principle extend to this external-sale setting; its flexible FCC approval mechanism is not imported into the FCRA's distinct clauses.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410: its requirement of a distinction responsive to actual harm is applied, with permitted prescreening's remaining financial disclosure expressly considered.
- Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States, June 14, 1999: its examination of actual exceptions and separate advancement and fit inquiries is preserved; the constrained financial-use permission supplies the distinction here.
- Lorillard Tobacco Co. v. Reilly, June 28, 2001: separate advancement and fit remain necessary, and alternative channels do not erase the burden on protected expression.
- Thompson v. Western States Medical Center, April 29, 2002: its fit ruling against inadequately justified drug-promotion conditions is distinguished by the direct disclosure mechanism and actual safeguards here; its assumed advancement does not become a new finding.
- 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island, 517 U.S. 484: its independent scrutiny of truthful-information burdens remains; a broader separate objection to paternalistic suppression is not made the Court's rule.
- Reno v. Condon, 528 U.S. 141: its information-market authority holding supplies no independent answer to this speech question.

## Law After Decision

The governing commercial-speech framework sustains the identified restriction because it directly protects against unrelated credit-file repurposing and reasonably fits that interest in light of the actual financial-use permissions. Permitted prescreening can cause a comparable disclosure, but its purpose, offer and information-use constraints distinguish it from unrestricted unrelated targeting. Neither all FCRA applications nor every possible data sale is adjudicated. Statutory compliance remains a separate matter, and opt-out is neither generally inadequate nor constitutionally compulsory here.

## Separate Writings

**Justice Thomas, concurring.** He joins the Court's sufficient confidentiality application and affirmance without treating ordinary commercial-speech scrutiny as the complete measure of First Amendment protection. His 44 Liquormart and Thompson positions reject suppressing accurate product information to prevent willing listeners from acting on it. The present restriction instead addresses repurposing another consumer's confidential financial information. He acknowledges that firm-offer prescreening can disclose a trait without affirmative authorization; the genuine conditional offer and information-use limits, not an absence of speech or inferior corporate rights, supply the relevant difference. The concurrence creates no universal data-ownership rule and does not withdraw any operative join.

## Procedure After Action

The reviewed First Amendment denial of relief is affirmed, leaving the FTC order operative for the identified lists. Supreme Court review of that question is complete; ordinary mandate administration follows.
