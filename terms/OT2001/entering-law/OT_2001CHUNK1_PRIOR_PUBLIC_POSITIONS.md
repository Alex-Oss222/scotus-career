### Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party, No. 95-1608

Merits decision, April 28, 1997

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 4, 1996. On writ of certiorari to the Eighth Circuit, Twin Cities Area New Party v. McKenna, 73 F.3d 196 (January 5, 1996). The court below reversed summary judgment for state officials and held Minnesota's old prohibition unconstitutional as applied to the New Party's nomination of Andy Dawkins, a consenting, supported candidate already seeking the Democratic-Farmer-Labor nomination. The DFL did not object. The grant concerns that minor/major-party combination under the First and Fourteenth Amendments, not the separate two-major-party prohibition. Minnesota Laws 1996, chapter 419, conditionally changed the statute and expressly makes reversal consequential.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.

All nine reach the live controversy. The old prohibition is sustained in this application; the remand must account for chapter 419's express expiration and prior-law revival upon reversal. The completed 1994 election is not reopened, and no particular intervening filing or election is adjudicated.

#### Opinion Topology

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The conditional enactment leaves a live controversy

**Holding and operative rule:** A challenge to the old prohibition remains live where the officials seek reversal of its invalidation and an enacted provision expressly revives that prohibition upon reversal. Chapter 419 supplies that concrete consequence; neither its replacement scheme nor completion of the 1994 election makes this dispute advisory.

**Authority:** Thomas, joined in Parts I and III by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: eight direct joins. Stone-Zsela also votes to reach the merits without an additional chapter-specific ground.

**Controlling explanation:** City of Mesquite distinguishes a genuine end to a challenged restriction from repeal that leaves its recurrence consequential, while keeping mootness separate from entitlement to an injunction. The Minnesota officials continue to contest the same party's asserted right, and chapter 419 conditions revival of the old law on the judgment they seek. That express condition makes adjudication affect legal rights even without finding a present stay or mandate recall. Norman preserves a continuing election challenge despite completion of the immediate election. The new scheme's existence is therefore relevant but not conclusive. Dawkins's earlier consent and the DFL's lack of objection do not establish compliance with every later written-chair-consent requirement. The Court does not decide the independent constitutionality of that replacement scheme or determine which law governed an unexamined intermediate filing.

##### II. This limitation is justified within the complete nomination system

**Holding and operative rule:** Minnesota may prevent this otherwise qualified minor party from placing its consenting nominee on the ballot under its name when he also seeks or holds the other major party's nomination, where the complete system leaves the candidate available and the party able to nominate other candidates, endorse and campaign. In this application the burden is less than severe and proportionate interests in ballot integrity, defined nomination choices and a stable electoral system justify the restriction under Anderson and Burdick.

**Authority:** Thomas, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer: five direct joins in Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** Anderson and Burdick require attention to the actual burden, precise interests and their connection; severe restrictions require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest, while reasonable nondiscriminatory restrictions ordinarily may rest on important regulatory interests. The Party's nomination choice is real association, not an arbitrary protest message, and endorsement is not literally the same legal act. But the rule excludes neither this actual candidate nor the Party itself from meaningful electoral participation. It limits an additional nomination designation within the State's selection machinery. Norman barred unrelated obstacles to authorized party expansion and independently qualified candidacies; it did not require every overlapping nomination of an available candidate. Colorado Republican protects independent campaign expression, a different mechanism from official ballot selection. The State may use general preventive nomination rules to maintain coherent candidacies and stable election administration without proving a past disruption in this particular alliance. Storer supports orderly candidate-selection rules; Jenness and Munro preserve reasonable screening and prevention without a demand for elaborate empirical proof. Candidate consent and qualifying signatures lessen some concerns but do not compel the State to redesign its nomination structure whenever an alliance proposes a workable alternative. This holding does not depend on voter confusion, an asserted incapacity of voters or proof of past fraud. Nor does it approve suppression of competition merely to protect incumbents, pure rational-basis immunity for ballot laws, or every anti-fusion restriction in a more restrictive system.

##### III. Reversal has the enacted transition consequence, without a retrospective election ruling

**Holding and operative rule:** Chapter 419 §10 makes its amendments effective for the 1996 state primary and thereafter, suspends them while the McKenna judgment is stayed or its mandate recalled, and provides that reversal causes the amendments to expire and prior law to revive. This reversal engages that expiration-and-revival condition; the remand must respect it without assuming an unproved stay, recall or continuous intervening statutory regime.

**Controlling explanation:** The enacted condition links the state-law consequence to reversal, not to a finding about an earlier filing. Recognizing that consequence does not adjudicate the replacement provisions' independent validity or establish that their requirements were satisfied in 1994. The existing case asks whether the old ban may be applied to this nomination, so sustaining that application permits a conforming remand. It supplies no basis to reopen the completed election or allocate rights in an unexamined intervening candidacy. Any such distinct question would require its actual filing facts and relevant stay or mandate history.

##### Statutory scope and limits

The challenged 1994 §204B.06 subdivision 1(b) barred an affidavit when another affidavit was on file for any office at the same primary or next general election. Section 204B.04 subdivision 2 barred petition nomination for the same office by a primary candidate, subject to two distinct exceptions. Under §204D.10 subdivision 2, if any major-party primary candidate received ten percent of the average vote for the party's state officers at the last state general election in the relevant district, its highest-vote candidates became nominees; if none met that threshold, none were nominated and all could seek petition nomination under §§204B.07–.09. The relevant state officers were governor, lieutenant governor, secretary of state, state auditor, state treasurer and attorney general. The §204B.13 subdivision 4 nonpartisan-vacancy exception allowed a petition within one week of the vacancy and no later than four calendar days before election; an eligible voter could sign despite voting in the primary or signing another petition. Neither exception applied to Dawkins. The distinct ban on nomination by two major parties is not decided.

The conditional replacement permitted a major-party and one-or-more-minor-party nomination for the same partisan office with written consent of the parties' state chairs; petition nomination also required the candidate's written consent. Losing the major-party primary forfeited the simultaneous minor-party nominations, and a party candidate could not also file as an independent. It retained the nonpartisan-vacancy exception, barred a defeated major-party primary candidate except to fill a §204B.13 vacancy, and repealed the old primary-turnout provision. Its affidavit rule referred to any other office, required petition acceptance and the consent forms, and contained a distinct dual-office exception for a soil-and-water district supervisor outside Anoka, Hennepin, Ramsey and Washington counties and a mayor/council or town-supervisor office in a city or town of no more than 2,500 people wholly or partly within the district. That different-office exception is not fusion. A multiply nominated candidate's name appeared once with party names; major/minor nomination used the major-party position and multiple-minor nomination the first filing party's position. Votes for multiply nominated candidates were excluded from the specified party-status qualification or retention counts. These features neither establish compliance in this case nor receive independent constitutional approval.

The Court recognizes no right to duplicate votes, separate party vote totals or a particular ballot design and adjudicates no independent challenge to consent, counting, format, support or deadline rules.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Anderson v. Celebrezze supplies the actual-burden, precise-interest and fit inquiry.
- Burdick v. Takushi supplies calibrated scrutiny within the complete access system; its protection against forced counting of arbitrary messages does not make this genuine nomination a fictional vote.
- Norman v. Reed retains protection of authorized party expansion and independently qualified candidates, distinguished from this overlapping nomination; its continuing-election principle also supports reaching the controversy.
- Colorado Republican retains its protection for independent party campaign expression and its distinct statutory connection requirement; it does not impose campaign-expenditure scrutiny on ballot designations.
- Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut preserves protected party association; that principle does not itself decide the proportionality of this nomination restriction.
- Eu v. San Francisco County Democratic Central Committee preserves party choice and expression without making every official ballot mechanism compulsory.
- Storer v. Brown supports orderly candidate-selection rules within an election system.
- Jenness v. Fortson preserves meaningful support requirements and reasonable electoral screening.
- Munro v. Socialist Workers Party permits preventive election regulation without proof of actual past disruption, within the calibrated inquiry.
- City of Mesquite v. Aladdin's Castle prevents automatic mootness where repeal leaves a concrete prospect of recurrence, while keeping injunctive entitlement separate.

#### Law After Decision

The old anti-fusion restriction is constitutional in this minor/major-party application. Reversal engages chapter 419's expiration of its amendments and revival of prior law. The governing constitutional method remains contextual Anderson-Burdick review, with no automatic strict scrutiny, universal validity rule, actual-confusion premise or permission to insulate incumbents from competition. The replacement scheme's independent constitutional questions remain undecided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Stevens dissents, joined by Ginsburg throughout and Souter in Parts I–II. A willing, sufficiently supported standard bearer is a central associational choice, and endorsing him or choosing someone else does not replace the Party's legally operative nomination. Even without declaring every such burden severe, they find inadequate fit: no defeated candidate seeks reentry, the candidate consents, the other party does not object, support requirements have been met, and another party designation adds no new individual to the race. Norman, Tashjian and Eu require attention to that actual association; Burdick permits reasonable prevention but does not make every general interest sufficient. Stevens and Ginsburg further reject preserving established parties' competitive position as a reason to exclude this consensual alliance. They would affirm at the as-applied scope, without mandating duplicate votes, separate party totals or a court-designed ballot.

Souter also dissents separately. Less-than-severe burden does not end the inquiry into fit. He agrees that the present interests are inadequate, but does not join a categorical rejection of every stability justification. A demonstrated threat to effective governing and a connection between fusion and that threat might justify a restriction; he leaves that question open. His Norman and Burdick positions preserve support requirements and primary finality, neither of which explains this supported nomination of the primary winner.

Stone-Zsela dissents. The burden is substantial but not severe, and the Eighth Circuit's strict-scrutiny reasoning should not control. Losing the qualified party's legally operative nomination and distinct identity affects effective electoral choice even though endorsement and campaigning remain possible. Applying Anderson-Burdick, he finds the absolute ban insufficiently fitted to this consenting, supported nomination: neutral labels, single counting and access requirements address the identified concerns, while the record supplies no demonstrated confusion, manipulation or splintering sufficient for this burden. Norman protects authorized party identity, and Colorado Republican confirms that shared candidate support does not erase a smaller party's independence without itself deciding fusion. Political stability cannot simply mean protecting established competitors. He reserves a properly supported stability limit and whether the other party's consent may be required. His proposed relief is prospective and leaves lawful design, consent, counting, fraud-prevention, deadlines and support requirements available. He recognizes no right to a separate line, multiple vote credit, general ballot expression or a particular format, and would not reopen 1994.

### Mertens v. Hewitt Associates, No. 91-1671

Merits decision, 1993-06-01

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued 1993-02-22. Decided 1993-06-01. On certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit under §1254(1).

Judgment under review: Ninth Circuit, 948 F.2d 607 (1991).

Question: Whether §502(a)(3)’s appropriate-equitable-relief provision authorizes the requested compensatory monetary recovery from an admitted nonfiduciary alleged knowingly to have participated in a fiduciary breach.

Plan participants alleged that the actuary knowingly participated in conduct causing pension losses. Review follows dismissal; allegations of breach, participation, knowledge and causal loss are not findings. Hewitt’s nonfiduciary status is fixed. The restitution ruling is unchallenged, and additional parties and claims were dismissed without a preserved appeal. The Ninth Circuit’s separate revival of the state malpractice claim is not under review. Hewitt expressly disclaimed the alternative argument that §502(a)(3) supplies no cause of action against a nonfiduciary at all.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas participate at argument (1993-02-22) and decision (1993-06-01).

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The reviewed compensatory award is not appropriate equitable relief

**Controlling proposition:** Section 502(a)(3)’s appropriate equitable relief encompasses traditionally equitable categories of relief, not compensatory damages merely because a court of equity could award them in trust litigation. The requested monetary compensation from this admitted nonfiduciary for the plan’s losses is therefore unavailable under that provision; the Court does not bar every monetary equitable remedy or decide the antecedent, expressly disclaimed cause-of-action question.

**Authority:** Scalia’s Opinion of the Court; Blackmun, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (5 Justices). The named Justices form a direct majority for this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the enacted remedy limitation must retain independent force. Curtis and Terry distinguish compensation for loss, ordinarily legal relief, from traditionally equitable categories. Reading equitable relief to include everything an equity court could award in a trust controversy would deprive that limitation of its work and disregard ERISA’s use of different remedial language elsewhere. Firestone directs consultation of trust law where it fits the statute; it does not authorize replacing the statute’s express limit with the full remedial jurisdiction of historical equity. Clews establishes that trust courts could award money, but that fact does not determine the category Congress selected here. Russell reinforces respect for ERISA’s differentiated enforcement structure without itself settling this question. The participants seek compensation rather than a preserved restitution remedy. The Court rejects that claim while leaving the disclaimed antecedent action question, unreviewed remedies and the separate malpractice disposition untouched; no allegations become findings of liability.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Curtis v. Loether, 415 U.S. 189: traditional legal compensation distinction applied.
- Chauffeurs, Teamsters & Helpers v. Terry, 494 U.S. 558: remedy classification applied; trust forum alone does not decide the statutory category.
- Firestone Tire & Rubber Co. v. Bruch, 489 U.S. 101: trust-law guidance preserved subject to express statutory limits.
- Clews v. Jamieson, 182 U.S. 461: equity’s monetary powers acknowledged but distinguished from the statutory category.
- Massachusetts Mutual Life Insurance Co. v. Russell, 473 U.S. 134: enforcement-structure caution applied; its reserved question is now resolved only within this holding.
- United States v. Burke, May26,1992: its fractured judgment supplies no controlling general remedial-range test.
- Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, February26,1992: appropriate-remedy presumption distinguished because Congress expressly limited this provision to equitable relief.

#### Current-Law Effect

The availability of this compensatory nonfiduciary remedy remained open. After this decision, five Justices reject it under §502(a)(3)’s equitable-relief limit. The Court does not decide whether a cause against the nonfiduciary otherwise exists, does not prohibit every equitable monetary remedy and does not reopen restitution. Stone’s bounded statutory-coverage and restoration proposals remain noncontrolling. Burke’s actual fractured authority and Franklin’s cause/remedy distinction remain unchanged.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Dissent — White; joined by Stevens, O'Connor:** White, joined by Stevens and O’Connor, reads equitable relief in the particular trust-law setting that ERISA incorporates. Firestone makes that background central, and Clews illustrates the traditional power of equity to restore trust losses through a monetary award, including against a knowing participant. The dissent regards the majority’s general legal-versus-equitable categories as an insufficient account of this particular remedy. It argues that the equitable limitation still matters because punitive relief and remedies arising only from the later merger of law and equity were not traditional trust restoration. Russell does not decide the different §502(a)(3) issue. The express redress language and §502(l)’s treatment of knowing participants further support a claim against a participating nonfiduciary without making every professional a fiduciary. This position would remove the categorical dismissal, not establish liability or an amount of recovery. It does not control.

**Dissent — Stone-Zsela; no other joins:** Stone-Zsela would permit traditional monetary restoration for losses caused by actual knowing participation in an ERISA fiduciary’s breach. Firestone supports consulting trust law, and Clews identifies this restorative remedy as equitable; the argument is not that every award once obtainable incidentally in equity becomes available. Russell’s respect for the statutory enforcement structure remains, but does not settle the distinct redress provision presented here. Section 502(a)(3)’s express authority to redress the fiduciary violation supplies the bounded cause; §502(l) offers context without creating an independent private action. Hewitt’s disclaimer is a presentation limit, not a jurisdictional flaw. On remand the participants would still need to prove breach, actual knowing participation and causal loss, and the court would determine the proper amount and recipient within equitable limits. No professional-negligence or punitive claim is authorized. Fixed nonfiduciary status, unchallenged restitution, unappealed parties and claims, and the separate malpractice disposition remain closed. His conclusion on statutory coverage is a dissenting proposition, not an answer supplied by the Court.

### Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc., No. 97-1943

Merits decision, June 22, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 28, 1999; decided June 22. On certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, 130 F.3d 893, which affirmed dismissal under Rule 12(b)(6). The questions concern mitigating measures in the original ADA's actual-disability definition, the proper weight of the EEOC Appendix guidance, and the sufficiency of the pleaded regarded-as-working theory.

The twin applicants allege uncorrected right-eye vision of 20/200 or worse and left-eye vision of 20/400 or worse, corrected vision of 20/20, commercial experience and first-class FAA medical certificates. United requires uncorrected visual acuity of 20/100 or better for the hiring at issue. The complaint alleges actual disability substantially limiting seeing and a perceived disability substantially limiting working. The lower court's assumption that United rejected the applicants from all its pilot positions is maintained for this appeal. Those allegations and assumptions are not adjudicated findings of unsafe flight, employer intent or universal airline qualification.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed in part and vacated in part.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Actual disability — seeing (Parts I–II) | Vacated and remanded, 5–4 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Reassessment without beneficial correction eliminating the underlying limitation; individual substantial limitation remains to be established |
| Regarded as substantially limited in working (Part III) | Affirmed, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | — | Regarded-as-working dismissal stands |

The judgment is affirmed in part and vacated in part. The actual-disability claim returns for reassessment without treating beneficial correction as eliminating the underlying limitation; the applicants must establish individual substantial limitation. Qualification, accommodation and legitimate safety defenses are resolved on their own elements. The regarded-as-working dismissal stands. No automatic hiring, reinstatement, accommodation, damages or new evidentiary proceeding is ordered, and no airline hiring criterion is categorically validated.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Holding 1— Beneficial mitigation does not eliminate underlying-impairment coverage

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the original 42 U.S.C. §12102(2)(A), successful mitigating measures do not erase the underlying impairment: whether it substantially limits a major life activity is assessed without treating beneficial correction or medication as eliminating that limitation. The protections of the Americans with Disabilities Act do not disappear because an individual has successfully treated or mitigated an impairment. The existence of a qualifying disability must be distinguished from the individual’s capacity to perform particular employment duties. Effective treatment does not itself establish that the underlying impairment is outside the statute’s protection. Individual proof of substantial limitation remains necessary; this is not a categorical rule that every eyeglass wearer is disabled.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. All five adopt the rule, individualized-proof requirement and separation of coverage from qualification, accommodation and safety.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute asks whether the underlying impairment substantially limits a major life activity of the individual. A person does not cease to have an impairment because lenses make ordinary activity possible. Making protection disappear when treatment enables work would merge disability with the separate question whether a person can perform the employment duties with accommodation. The original Act separates those inquiries. It still requires a substantial limitation in this individual, not merely a diagnosis or the use of glasses.

Arline’s functional protection supports examining the impairment without confusing coverage with qualification or justified exclusion. Bragdon supplies the individualized sequence of impairment, major activity and substantial limitation; it expressly reserved mitigation, so its recognition of a substantial treated reproductive risk does not resolve this question against the underlying-impairment rule. The twins’ severe uncorrected myopia and dependence on lenses must therefore be assessed without corrected acuity eliminating the underlying limitation. Their individual substantiality remains for reassessment.

The present-function argument properly insists on individual proof, but it treats the effectiveness of an aid as the absence of the limitation the aid addresses. Actual corrected performance belongs in the separate qualification, accommodation and safety inquiries. The Court adopts this construction from the original statute, without making agency deference its ground. The EEOC Appendix’s interpretation does not require a ruling on general definitional authority or the binding force of every regulatory or Appendix statement; Haggar’s actual-authority and statutory-fit requirements remain intact.

##### Holding 2 — The actual-disability claim requires reassessment under the underlying-impairment rule

**Holding and operative rule:** The dismissal of the actual-disability claim is vacated and the claim remanded for reassessment without beneficial mitigation eliminating the underlying visual limitation, with individual proof that the impairment substantially limits seeing. Qualification, reasonable accommodation and legitimate safety defenses must be resolved on their own elements; the remand awards no automatic hiring, reinstatement or accommodation.

**Authority:** Part II of the Court’s opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These five adopt the vacatur and bounded reassessment; O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas dissent from this component.

**Controlling explanation:** The complaint’s allegations of severe uncorrected myopia and continued dependence on corrective lenses must be considered under the statutory rule just stated. Normal corrected seeing does not itself defeat the claim. The Court vacates the dismissal so the lower courts can assess individual substantial limitation under the proper rule; it does not conclusively determine disability or the sufficiency of every allegation.

The FAA certificates and commercial experience concern the applicants’ performance and qualifications; neither establishes the separate disability element nor invalidates every employer criterion. United may contest qualification, accommodation and a supported safety defense, but those distinct inquiries cannot erase the coverage question. Arline supports the separation of disability from qualification and justified exclusion; Bragdon supplies individualized assessment and preserves the distinct objective-risk inquiry within its own setting. No record-of claim, regarded-as-seeing theory, finding of dangerousness or ultimate employment entitlement is supplied.

##### Holding 3 — The alleged perceived restriction is confined to a specialized job category

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming working is a major life activity, a regarded-as-working claim requires an alleged perceived restriction in a class or broad range of jobs; exclusion from the specialized global-airline-pilot category alleged here does not suffice. The pleaded exclusion remains insufficient even under the assumption that United rejected these applicants for all of its pilot positions.

**Authority:** Part III of the Court’s opinion commands all nine Justices: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Each joins both the perceived-class rule and the dismissal application.

**Controlling explanation:** Perceiving an impairment is not necessarily perceiving a substantial limitation of working. The distinction reflected in 29 C.F.R. §1630.2(j) and (l) separates a class or broad range of jobs from a particular specialized occupation. The alleged perception concerns the global-airline-pilot category, while other pilot work provides context for why that category is specialized. The Court does not hold that actual employment elsewhere conclusively proves United's belief, require rejection by every employer, or decide disputed facts about its mental state. The complaint fails because the alleged perceived limitation does not reach the required breadth, even accepting the favorable scope assumption below.

This is a coverage ruling on the theory actually presented. It does not decide whether working invariably qualifies as a major life activity, whether every airline position shares the same qualification, whether United's criterion would be justified against a covered applicant, or whether a different properly presented perceived limitation would satisfy the Act.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **School Board of Nassau County v. Arline:** Applies its functional protection and individualized assessment while keeping disability distinct from qualification and supported exclusion; its actual setting and limits remain intact.
- **Bragdon v. Abbott:** Applies its individualized disability framework and resolves the reserved mitigation question through the underlying-impairment rule. Its reproduction holding, substantial residual-risk application and distinct Title III objective direct-threat rules remain intact.
- **United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. and Chevron:** Preserve actual delegation and statutory fit as conditions of agency deference. The Court rests on its construction of the original ADA and makes no general decision about ADA definitional authority or the binding force of every EEOC regulation or Appendix statement.

#### Law After Decision

Under the original ADA, beneficial mitigating measures do not eliminate an underlying impairment’s substantial limitation for actual-disability coverage. Individual substantiality remains required, and coverage is distinct from qualification, reasonable accommodation and legitimate safety standards. This resolves Bragdon’s express mitigation reservation without automatic coverage for every corrected condition. The actual-seeing claim returns for reassessment under that rule.

Assuming working is a major life activity, the regarded-as theory presented here also fails because the alleged perception concerns a specialized global-airline-pilot category rather than a class or broad range of jobs. Separate coverage branches, individual proof, qualification and lawful defenses retain their distinct elements. No precedent is overruled; no general delegation or airline-safety rule is decided.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Kennedy, joined by O’Connor, Scalia and Thomas, concurring in Part III and dissenting from Parts I–II.** They would assess substantial limitation with the measures the individual actually uses, including beneficial and adverse effects. The statutory requirement concerns this person’s present limitation; treating effective lenses as absent substitutes a hypothetical untreated condition for actual function. This does not erase an impairment or exclude every treated condition: residual limitations and treatment burdens can still be substantial. Bragdon found substantial reproductive risk even with treatment and reserved the general mitigation question. Here the complaint alleges normal corrected seeing and no additional substantial limitation inconsistent with that function. Constant dependence on lenses does not supply the missing present limitation under their construction. The protection and accommodation structure cannot eliminate the threshold Congress separately enacted, and contrary Appendix guidance cannot override that statutory reading under Haggar. They would affirm the actual-disability dismissal without deciding general EEOC power, qualification or safety. They join Part III’s perceived-class rule and regarded-as affirmance.

**Stevens, joined by Breyer, concurring.** They join the Court in full. The agency guidance and legislative explanations persuasively reinforce the underlying-impairment reading because the statute separates protected status from the ability to perform work with assistance. Successful management should not itself defeat protection against impairment-based exclusion. Haggar’s requirements of actual authority and statutory fit do not prevent consideration of that persuasive reasoning, and no conclusion that all Appendix language has binding force is needed. Individual substantiality, the bounded reassessment, the independent regarded-as affirmance and ordinary qualification and safety defenses remain as the Court states them.

#### Record Limits

The governing provisions are the original 42 U.S.C. §§12102(2), 12111(8) and 12112, with the relevant 29 C.F.R. §1630.2 definitions and Appendix guidance. FAA certification does not establish the validity of every employer qualification, and an employer's safety contention is not a finding of actual risk.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The actual-disability portion of the Tenth Circuit’s judgment is vacated and remanded for reassessment under the underlying-impairment rule. The regarded-as-working dismissal is affirmed. Supreme Court merits review is complete, subject to ordinary mandate procedure. The lower courts address individual substantial limitation and any properly presented qualification, accommodation or safety issues on their own elements; no employment award, automatic trial, new-evidence entitlement or amended pleading is directed.

**End of entry: Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc., Merits decision, June 22, 1999.**

### Murphy v. United Parcel Service, Inc., No. 97-1992

Merits decision, June 22, 1999

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 27, 1999; decided June 22, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the Tenth Circuit’s March 11, 1998 order, No. 96-3380, reported at 141 F.3d 1185 (table), affirming employer summary judgment.

Review is limited to petition Questions 1 and 4: assessment of hypertension in its unmedicated state for actual disability and the asserted regarded-as coverage. Questions 2 and 3 concerning DOT justification and qualification or essential functions were presented but not granted. The circuit expressly left those questions undecided.

Murphy had longstanding severe hypertension, took medication, and worked as a mechanic before UPS hired him in August 1994 following issuance of a health card. UPS discharged him October 5 after reviewing elevated readings and asserting DOT noncompliance. Medical testimony, residual symptoms and effects of different regimens were disputed; normal functioning was not conceded. Whether driving was an essential rather than peripheral job function also remained disputed.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Actual disability is assessed without treating successful mitigation as eliminating the underlying limitation

**Holding and operative rule:** Under original 42 U.S.C. §12102(2)(A), successful medication does not erase the underlying impairment: substantial limitation of a major life activity is assessed without treating medication’s beneficial effects as eliminating that limitation. The claimant must still prove an individualized substantial limitation; neither hypertension as a diagnostic label nor use of a prescription alone establishes disability, and coverage remains separate from qualification, reasonable accommodation and legitimate safety standards.

**Authority:** Part I of Ginsburg’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer: five direct joins to the underlying-impairment rule and its individual-proof limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute protects an individual whose impairment substantially limits a major life activity. A person’s successful management of that impairment does not remove the condition that requires the management. Making protection disappear because medication works would make coverage depend on continuing vulnerability, even though the impairment persists. The present-tense and individualized terms require proof about this person’s underlying limitation; they do not require treating beneficial mitigation as its disappearance.

Arline supplies individualized examination of the condition while separating coverage from supported employment and safety judgments. Bragdon requires proof of impairment, a major activity and substantial limitation without demanding utter inability; it reserved general mitigation and therefore does not foreclose this construction. These authorities support an individual inquiry, not automatic coverage for every diagnosis or prescription. Treatment’s success bears on ability to perform essential functions, accommodation and safety under their separate elements. It is not a reason to subtract the underlying impairment at the coverage threshold.

The Court’s ground is statutory construction, not deference to the no-mitigation guidance. Auer, Haggar and NFFE retain their requirements concerning responsibility, text, delegation and statutory boundaries. The Court invalidates no entire body of EEOC regulations, denies no agency all relevant power, and imposes no categorical bar on amicus interpretations. The working regulations used below are assumed valid for this analysis.

##### The actual-disability judgment must be reassessed under the underlying-impairment rule

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the actual-disability judgment rests on counting medication’s beneficial effects to defeat coverage, that judgment is vacated and remanded for individualized reassessment under the underlying-impairment rule. Qualification, essential functions, accommodation and legitimate safety defenses retain their own elements and are addressed below if necessary; the Court orders no automatic hiring, reinstatement, accommodation, certification, waiver or damages award.

**Authority:** Parts I and IV’s actual-disability disposition, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct joins to vacatur and reassessment, without a final finding of actual disability.

**Controlling explanation:** Granted Question 1 contests the legal choice to assess hypertension in its medicated state. The Court’s answer removes the legal premise of the actual-disability judgment and requires ordinary application of the proper rule. It does not decide a separate treated-state sufficiency question or hold that an elevated reading alone proves substantial limitation.

Murphy’s severe untreated hypertension and the medical account of its effects must be assessed individually. The general daily-function testimony, asserted residual limitations and differences among medication regimens retain their evidentiary status. Normal functioning was disputed, possible end-organ injury was not a diagnosed present injury, and effects of stronger or abandoned regimens cannot automatically be assigned to the actual regimen. None of those distinctions supplies a final disability finding here. Bragdon’s individual-proof requirement and Cleveland’s separation of statutory elements support reassessment while leaving necessary remaining elements and defenses for the lower courts. No mandatory trial or new evidentiary proceeding follows.

##### Regarded-as coverage requires a perceived substantial occupational limitation

**Holding and operative rule:** On the working theory presented, an employer must perceive a limitation affecting a class of jobs or a broad range of jobs in various classes, relative to comparable training, skills and abilities and the reasonably accessible geographic area. Knowledge of hypertension, rejection from one particular job or an asserted lawful regulatory motive does not alone resolve that inquiry; complete unemployability is unnecessary.

**Authority:** Part II-A’s general perceived-class framework is joined by all nine Justices. Stone-Zsela, Stevens and Breyer join this framework without joining Part II-B’s application or the affirmance. The pertinent EEOC working regulations are assumed valid.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 12102(2)(C) requires the perceived statutory limitation, rather than perception of an impairment alone. The class-of-jobs and broad-range terms of 29 C.F.R. §1630.2 distinguish the individual’s vocational field from a single particular position. They do not require exclusion from every mechanic job, a prescribed percentage or complete unemployment. A nationwide certification requirement may matter, but its geographic reach does not by itself establish the pertinent occupational class or the employer’s perception.

Coverage and justification remain distinct. Bragdon’s individual approach and Cleveland’s separate statutory inquiries do not make a regulatory explanation a categorical answer to what UPS perceived about Murphy’s capacity for other work. The Court therefore does not adopt the circuit’s shortcut that reliance on DOT requirements itself defeats regarded-as coverage. An erroneous certification judgment likewise would not automatically establish coverage.

##### This showing does not establish the required perceived breadth

**Holding and operative rule:** The evidence that UPS regarded Murphy as unable to obtain the commercial-driving certification required for its particular mechanic position does not, on this record, create a genuine material issue that it perceived him unable to perform a class or broad range of jobs using his skills. The regarded-as determination is affirmed on that evidentiary ground.

**Authority:** Part II-B, joined by O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: six direct joins. Stone-Zsela would return the coverage judgment; Stevens and Breyer make no independent decision of this application.

**Controlling explanation:** Murphy’s mechanical skills, approximately twenty-two years of work not requiring this certification, evidence of alternative mechanic occupations and subsequent mechanic employment place the asserted exclusion in its occupational setting. The record supplies no sufficient additional evidence of a broader UPS perception. The nationwide nature of the certification does not supply that missing showing about work fitting Murphy’s skills and the employer’s understanding of it.

Remaining employment alone is not conclusive. The vocational profile’s totals describe occupational categories, not proved local vacancies, and physicians’ opinions about heavy work are not automatically UPS’s own perception. The conclusion neither requires exclusion from every mechanic position nor holds that commercial driving can never constitute a class of work. Whether UPS correctly understood certification is unnecessary to this coverage result. The Court sustains the judgment on the perceived-breadth proof, not on a categorical regulatory-motive defense.

##### Qualification and DOT defenses are not independently decided

**Rule and scope limit:** Questions 2 and 3 concerning regulatory justification and qualification or essential functions were presented in the petition but were not granted; the circuit also left them undecided. No qualification finding, essential-function determination, categorical DOT defense, entitlement to temporary certification or waiver/exhaustion ruling supplies an independent ground of this decision.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part III’s Supreme Court nonreach and separation of these issues from coverage. That common join concerns Supreme Court nonreach and does not erase the distinct actual-disability vacatur and regarded-as affirmance or Stone-Zsela’s broader proposed remand.

**Controlling explanation:** X-Citement Video permits a respondent to defend an unchanged favorable judgment on a properly supported alternative; permission does not establish these disputed alternatives. Binding safety text and numerical advice remain distinct: §391.41(b)(6) concerns high blood pressure likely to interfere with safe commercial driving; §391.43(f) says further tests may be necessary for consistently elevated readings. An absolute regulatory 160/90 bar cannot simply be assumed from those words. Conversely the advisory criteria and issued health card establish no entitlement to a temporary certificate or final qualification.

The Court decides no new record-of claim or separate interview-inquiry claim. It creates no production obligation or procedural default. The actual-disability remand leaves necessary preserved qualification, accommodation and safety questions for ordinary lower-court proceedings. The regarded-as affirmance stands, and Stone-Zsela’s proposed remand of that route does not become a Court instruction.

Its expressly reserved general mitigation question is resolved here without subtracting medication’s beneficial effects from the underlying limitation; its reproduction application and Title III direct-threat holdings are not overruled or transferred into a decision of this DOT defense. Arline supports individualized examination of impairment while keeping qualification and safety distinct.

Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp. preserves distinct ADA elements and ordinary proof; its SSDI explanation requirement is not triggered. Auer v. Robbins, United States v. Questions 2 and 3 remain outside the grant; the Court announces no independent treated-state sufficiency holding. The lower circuit’s mitigation rule is rejected on the Court’s independent statutory construction; its regulatory-motive shortcut on regarded-as coverage is not adopted. No Supreme Court precedent is overruled.

There is no diagnosis-only coverage, duty to stop treatment or final Supreme Court disability finding. Actual functioning remains relevant to qualification, accommodation and safety. ### FDA v. Brown & Williamson Tobacco Corp., No. 98-1152

Merits decision, March 21, 2000

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 1, 1999. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit, 153 F.3d 155, decided August 14, 1998.

The question is whether the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act authorizes the FDA's asserted drug-and-device regulation of cigarettes and smokeless tobacco as customarily marketed. The agency classified nicotine as a drug and its delivery components as devices, treated the products as combinations, and issued its August 28, 1996 youth-sale, distribution and advertising rule under device authorities. It relied on pharmacological effects, consumer use, manufacturer knowledge and deliberate nicotine-delivery design. The regulated parties did not dispute the agency's factual findings for this appeal; they disputed their statutory consequences.

The FDA acknowledged serious health risks but maintained that abrupt removal could cause greater harm to addicted adults through withdrawal and unsafe substitutes, while youth restrictions could prevent addiction. The courts below disagreed about whether that approach could fit the statute. No new epidemiological finding is made here.

The District Court sustained general authority and device treatment but rejected advertising authority, staying most restrictions while leaving age verification and the under-eighteen sales prohibition in place. Certified interlocutory and injunction appeals brought the case to the Fourth Circuit. That court invalidated the entire August 28 rule for lack of statutory authority and vacated the District Court's separate advertising construction without approving or disapproving it.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5-4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, who would reverse the categorical no-authority judgment and remand for review of the actual regulatory methods and remaining objections.

The Court leaves the rule invalid. Any further proceedings are confined to administrative or severability matters genuinely necessary to implement that judgment; no replacement regulation is ordered. The judgment creates no separate advertising-authority holding, decides no First Amendment claim, and orders no tobacco ban, recall, damages or retrospective liability. Authority over products marketed with therapeutic claims remains outside this decision.

#### Opinion Topology

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela delivered the opinion of the Court, joined throughout by Justices O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Those five Justices adopt both holdings and all their qualifications. Justice Stevens dissented, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The dissent would reverse the categorical no-authority judgment and remand, distinguishing statutory coverage from lawful exercise without exempting the agency from otherwise applicable commands.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The 1997 Act preserves the authority question and specifies the earlier tobacco regime

**Holding and operative rule:** Public Law 105-115, section 422, leaves the question of tobacco authority unchanged and requires any such authority to be exercised under the FDCA as it stood on November 20, 1997. The Act's new mission and regulatory procedures therefore supply neither a new tobacco delegation nor a means to avoid a restriction of that earlier regime.

**Controlling explanation:** The two commands perform different work. Reserving whether authority exists favors neither party's construction; specifying the earlier Act identifies the rules under which any authority operates. Section 501's general effective date, ninety days after November 21, 1997, is subject to other express provisions and excludes sections 111, 121, 125 and 307. That general provision does not override section 422.

Section 406's new mission is not part of the earlier tobacco regime. Nor may the later classification-default, de novo, postmarket, least-burdensome or added clinical-evidence procedures be used here. The earlier provisions retain their own qualified scientific-evidence and reclassification routes; excluding later additions does not erase that existing flexibility or establish a new numerical minimum of clinical studies. The authority question must be decided under the actual statutory system, not by importing only the later provisions favorable to one side.

##### The asserted customary-tobacco regime exceeds the delegated authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Read with its operative safety and marketing requirements and Congress's distinct tobacco enactments, the FDCA does not authorize the asserted drug/device regime for tobacco as customarily marketed. The FDA cannot obtain that power by construing broad coverage terms to include a continuing-market accommodation that the connected statutory requirements do not permit; the challenged rule is invalid on that ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court assumes for this inquiry the force of the agency's intended-use evidence. Section 321's structure-and-function category is not confined to therapeutic advertising, and nicotine's chemical action does not itself answer the classification of a delivery component. But those definitions operate within the statute's duties, not apart from them.

Section 353(g)'s assignment of primary responsibility by a combination's mode of action does not exempt it from otherwise applicable drug obligations. Misbranding, applicable new-drug approval, device safety and conditional cessation duties remain independent of section 360j(e)'s restriction power. Graduated controls permit real choices; they do not confer a general power to dispense with those duties. The agency's accepted danger findings and its proposal to retain customary tobacco through restrictions expose the conflict. A preference for the least harmful available policy supplies no universal exception to the enacted commands.

Congress's cigarette-labeling, advertising, smokeless-tobacco, reporting and youth-access enactments reinforce this reading at their actual scopes. They are not individually express exemptions from all health law. Their combined treatment of customary tobacco, read with the FDCA's operating requirements, does not support the asserted regime. Failed bills, agency disclaimers and congressional silence alone would not decide the question.

The dissent would separate coverage from correction of an unlawful method. Here the claimed customary-tobacco authority itself presupposes an accommodation the connected scheme does not supply. MCI requires an operative statutory obligation to retain its essential force, while Haggar and Chevron require a genuine delegation before deference. Sweet Home and Iowa Utilities Board likewise distinguish broad delegated authority from independent limits. This conclusion turns on the particular enacted scheme, not economic magnitude or a universal major-questions test. Good Samaritan permits reasoned interpretive change within the statute; a changed view alone is not the defect.

##### Operative qualifications and questions left open

The following distinctions limit the statutory reasoning; they are not new enforcement findings:

- **Directions and warnings:** Section 352(f)(1) requires an exemption by regulation when adequate directions are unnecessary for public-health protection. That directions exemption does not waive section 352(f)(2)'s separate necessary warnings concerning pathological conditions, childhood use, unsafe dosage, method or duration. Section 352(j) concerns danger under labeled dosage, manner, frequency or duration; existing tobacco warnings do not automatically establish FDCA compliance.
- **Classification and approval:** Device classes depend on their statutory control and information criteria, including the qualified low-risk route; risk alone does not compel an immediate judicial ban. The earlier scientific-evidence and reclassification procedures remain. No particular classification, new-drug application, grandfather status or approval is adjudicated. Section 321(p)'s pre-June 25, 1938 exception requires the drug's prior-Act status and unchanged labeling representations; the Court does not find those conditions satisfied or unsatisfied here.
- **Restricted devices:** Section 360j(e) permits practitioner-authorization or other sale, distribution or use conditions upon the required determination that harmful potential or necessary collateral measures prevent reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness otherwise. Training or facility limits require their own safe-use determination; lack of specified board eligibility or certification alone cannot exclude a user. Required label statements remain. None of those permissions is a general exemption from another command.
- **Ban proceedings:** Section 360f permits rulemaking on findings of substantial deception or unreasonable and substantial risk. When the Secretary has determined that labeling can correct the problem and provided written notice specifying the change and time allowed, failure to make that change is separately required. Immediate effect additionally requires unreasonable, direct and substantial danger and advance manufacturer notice, followed by prompt notice, an informal-hearing opportunity and expeditious affirmance, modification or revocation.
- **Cessation and recall:** Section 360h(e)(1) requires immediate cessation of distribution and notification and cessation-of-use instructions to health professionals and user facilities after a reasonable-probability finding that a human-use device would cause serious adverse health consequences or death. An informal-hearing opportunity must be provided within ten days of the order, and inadequate grounds require vacatur. Recall requires a further determination, timetable and progress reports; it cannot reach devices held by individuals, and the greater-risk exception concerns recall from user facilities. Those recall limits do not make the initial cessation command elective. Individual risk-notice duties remain. The Court makes none of the required enforcement determinations itself.
- **Other enactments and State law:** The cigarette statutes' labeling and advertising limits and the smokeless-tobacco statute's distinct package/advertisement rules retain their own scopes, including the latter's outdoor-billboard exception. Section 360k requires a relevant federal device requirement and a different or additional State duty in the specified subject relationship. Its exemption procedure requires an application, regulation, notice and oral-hearing opportunity; greater stringency is one route, compelling local conditions without violating federal requirements another. Medtronic therefore prevents treating coverage alone as universal displacement of State youth-access laws. No particular preemption or exemption is decided.

The Court decides no advertising merits, constitutional objection, therapeutic-claim product, biological-license question, particular new-drug refusal ground, international coordination requirement or later classification procedure. It does not pronounce every risky device unmarketable, require therapeutic claims for every drug, create a general prohibition on agency change, or require Congress to use particular words in every delegation. Congress may confer tobacco authority expressly.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.:** Applies statutory construction before deference; agency reasonableness cannot supply a delegation absent from the enacted scheme.
- **United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.:** Preserves the distinction between genuine delegated ambiguity, regulatory validity and application; formal rulemaking alone does not establish authority.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.:** Applies its requirement that a bounded agency power preserve an operative statutory obligation. It creates no universal magnitude-based exception to delegation.
- **Babbitt v. Sweet Home Chapter of Communities for a Great Oregon:** Preserves broad statutory coverage within actual delegated language and concrete limits; it does not authorize disregard of a connected command.
- **AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board:** Preserves separate analysis of general implementing power, particular statutory constraints and unresolved applications.
- **Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala:** Preserves considered interpretive change within the statute; the FDA's former position is not an automatic legal bar.
- **National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Boston & Maine Corp.:** Preserves recognition of the agency's actual discernible ground without supplying missing findings or a judicial replacement policy.
- **Adams Fruit Co. v. Barrett:** Preserves the requirement of an actual congressional delegation; an agency cannot create its own authority.
- **United States v. Bacto-Unidisk:** Its broad protective reading of the FDCA remains, subject to the entire enacted scheme rather than isolated definitions.
- **United States v. Rutherford:** Preserves statutory safety and effectiveness requirements against a judicially created exemption; it is not a present tobacco ban or an adjudication of an application.
- **Medtronic, Inc. v. Lohr:** Preserves the actual federal/state counterpart and subject conditions for device preemption; federal coverage does not automatically eliminate every State rule.
- **United States v. Locke:** Its particular national-vessel and local-waterway allocation remains distinct; overlap alone supplies no general preemption rule here.

#### Law After Decision

Effective March 21, 2000, the FDA lacks the asserted FDCA authority for the August 28, 1996 drug/device regime governing tobacco as customarily marketed. The rule remains invalid. This is a tobacco-specific construction of the connected statutory scheme, not a universal major-questions doctrine, categorical bar on agency reinterpretation, or automatic preemption rule. Section 422 preserves the authority question and the earlier statutory version for exercise of any tobacco power. Therapeutic-claim products, particular advertising and constitutional questions, and independently authorized regulatory subjects remain outside the holding; no new ban or recall follows.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Justice Stevens, dissenting, joined by Justices Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** They would reverse the categorical authority judgment and remand. The structure-and-function definition reaches nicotine deliberately supplied for pharmacological effects, and the delivery-component definition can reach the apparatus used to supply it. Therapeutic advertising is not the only evidence of intended use. The earlier regulatory architecture at least permits that construction within the delegation recognized by Chevron, Sweet Home and Haggar.

They accept that restricted-device authority cannot erase misbranding, applicable approval or cessation duties. Their sufficient disagreement is that an obligation to remove a covered product, if established under the statute's own conditions, limits the FDA's permissible methods rather than proving that the product is outside the protective statute. They do not make section 360h(e)(1) elective merely because other remedies exist, and do not adopt a universal exception for withdrawal or substitute risks. The precise provision must permit consideration of those consequences.

The tobacco-specific statutes address their actual labeling, advertising, reporting and access subjects; they do not establish the categorical FDCA exclusion necessary to sustain the judgment. ### Asgrow Seed Co. v. Winterboer, No. 92-2038

Merits decision, January 18, 1995

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 7, 1994; decided January 18, 1995. On writ of certiorari to the Federal Circuit, reviewing 982 F.2d 486 (1992), which reversed summary judgment for Asgrow, vacated the injunction, and remanded exemption eligibility. The questions concern former §2543's saved-seed protection, §2541(3)'s reproductive-marketing restriction, and consequences for the separately pleaded §2541(6) notice claim. The district court reserved damages and labeling liability.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer participated at argument and decision. No Justice is noted as not participating.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Saved seed means seed retained for the farmer's own planting

**Controlling proposition:** Under former 7 U.S.C. §2543, a farmer satisfying the statutory conditions may sell for reproductive purposes only seed genuinely saved for replanting the farmer's own acreage; intentional multiplication for reproductive marketing does not become exempt merely because the seller primarily grows food or feed crops. Qualifying later disposition of genuinely retained seed remains possible when planting plans change, without a fixed percentage or mechanical ceiling based on the previous year's acreage.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Seven Justices adopt the construction, explanation, and application that these sales fall outside the exemption. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela independently accepts the stated construction but does not join the Court's final application; his separate remand position is not counted as an additional application vote.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the farmer-sales proviso protects a limited category of seed, rather than every crop produced by a person whose principal occupation is farming. The opening sentence reserves infringement through multiplication for reproductive marketing, while the proviso permits sale of such saved seed. Those provisions operate together when the farmer initially retains seed for planting and later changes plans. The proviso's direction to disregard §2541(3) prevents the qualifying later sale from retroactively converting lawful production into infringement; it does not erase the opening reservation for production intended for the planting market. The contrary reading gives the sales proviso work only by depriving saved seed and the reservation of their limiting function. Reasonably anticipated acreage expansion and crop-appropriate retention remain possible, so this construction creates no rigid prior-acreage quota. These reproductive-production sales, greatly exceeding the Winterboers' own planting needs, fail the necessary saved-seed condition. Their asserted food-and-feed business proportion cannot cure that failure, and no finding about disputed state-law compliance is needed to establish it.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: follows its respect for the enacted condition in statutory context, without importing the veterans statute's protective construction or inventing a numerical PVPA condition.

**Operative qualifications:** Saved seed must be produced by the person from seed obtained, or descended from seed obtained, with the owner's authority for seeding purposes. Seller and purchaser must each have as their primary farming occupation growing crops for sale for nonreproductive purposes; the sale must comply with applicable state seed-sale laws. The retained §2541(4) restriction on using the variety to produce, rather than develop, a hybrid or different variety remains separate. Bona fide nonreproductive sales in customary nonreproductive channels retain their independent statutory protection; a purchaser who diverts that seed to planting is deemed to have notice under §2567 that the diversion infringes. That diversion provision is distinct from §2541(6)'s notice claim. No general ruling on a buyer's later authorized-source lineage is adopted.

##### Marketing does not require an advertising or distribution apparatus

**Controlling proposition:** Former §2541(3)'s multiplication as a step in marketing for growing purposes includes multiplication directed toward holding protected seed forth for sale and the preparatory activities of selling; extensive advertising, intermediaries, or coordinated merchandising are not required. The proviso's primary-farming-occupation condition concerns the farmer's business as a whole, not a judicial rule allowing reproductive sale of less than half of each protected variety's crop, and that occupation condition does not dispense with the distinct saved-seed limitation.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Seven Justices adopt these related constructions; Justice Stevens rejects the marketing premise. The Chief's separate opinion rejects an advertising prerequisite and a judicial fifty-percent quota without joining the final application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that ordinary marketing encompasses offering seed for reproductive sale without the elaborate commercial machinery demanded by the Court of Appeals. The statute regulates multiplication as a step in marketing, so its protection reaches production directed toward the later sale as well as the completed transfer separately described in §2541(1). Using different words for the transfer and its preparatory reproductive activity does not require limiting the latter to wholesalers, cooperatives, or advertising campaigns. The enacted context, rather than a preference for plant breeders, supplies that distinction. The lower court's variety-by-variety percentage rule likewise has no textual foundation. Congress described a person's primary farming occupation, not the percentage of each variety marketed for planting. Reading occupation at the whole-business level does not authorize all reproductive sales by that farmer: the seed itself must still satisfy the own-planting condition. Stevens's narrower commercial-distribution reading would preserve the lower judgment, but it would remove ordinary reproductive-sale production from the reserved protection without a statutory basis.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Brown v. Gardner, decided December 12, 1994: applies contextual attention to the enacted terms rather than an additional condition; its separate VA-benefits rule remains unchanged.

##### The former Act governs these transactions

**Controlling proposition:** The 1994 Plant Variety Protection Act amendments do not govern these transactions or supply the meaning of the former saved-seed provision: Pub. L. 103-349 §15 makes the amendments effective April 4, 1995, after this decision, and §14 preserves former-law treatment for specified older certificates and pending applications subject to its express exceptions. The later deletion of the farmer-sales proviso therefore creates no retrospective liability here and does not establish that every later sale involving an older protected variety loses former-law treatment.

**Authority:** Justice Scalia's temporal-text portion, joined by Justices O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer: seven direct opinion votes. The Chief and Justice Stevens also apply former law in their separate writings, without joining every broader temporal rationale of one another or of Landgraf.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Congress's actual temporal directions before considering a general presumption about retrospective consequences. Landgraf and Rivers preserve that starting point, although their broader formulations received different joins. The amendment became law on October 6, 1994 and specified effectiveness 180 days later. The statute therefore has not yet displaced the former law for this January decision, much less retroactively changed the consequences of completed sales. Congress also addressed older certificates and pending applications separately, which forecloses a universal inference that the new farmer-sales restriction immediately reaches all protected varieties. The pendency of this appeal does not change the relevant statutory dates or turn later legislation into conclusive evidence of the prior text's meaning. The Court neither approves nor condemns the new policy. It construes the former Act on its own terms and leaves any future transaction to the provisions that actually govern it, including the transition conditions Congress expressly enacted.

**Precedent treatment:**

- Landgraf v. USI Film Products, 511 U.S. 244 (1994): applies Congress's express temporal direction as the starting point; does not convert Scalia, Kennedy, or Thomas into joiners of its broader general framework.
- Rivers v. Roadway Express, Inc., 511 U.S. 298 (1994): preserves the separation of later legislative change from the law governing completed conduct; the new amendment is not a conclusive interpretation of old text.

**Transition qualifications:** Section 14(a), except as otherwise provided in §14, preserves prior law for varieties with certificates issued before April 4, 1995 and applications pending then. Section 14(b) permits refiling a pending application on or after that date under amended eligibility and protection terms, while §42 uses the original filing date. Section 14(c) requires a notice claiming amended protection to identify that protection and applies §128 sanctions to false or misleading claims or labels. No relevant refiling or transition exception is found.

**Notice and remedial limits:** The seven-Justice Court opinion does not decide whether a sale actually authorized by §2543 must nevertheless satisfy §2541(6), because these sales fail the saved-seed condition. It makes no separate notice-infringement finding or damages award. Section 2541 concerns unauthorized acts in the United States or regulated or affected commerce, before protection expires but after certificate issuance or distribution with §2567 notice, subject to the subchapter's exceptions. Section 2541(6) concerns dispensing propagable protected material without the protected-variety notice under which it was received; failure of the saved-seed defense alone does not establish those elements. Former §2567 permits owners to give public notice by associating a label with seed or affixing it to its container or the variety, stating that unauthorized propagation or seed multiplication is prohibited and, after the certificate issues, adding a protected-variety designation. When an owner-authorized distribution reaches the infringer without that marking, the owner cannot recover damages unless the infringer has actual awareness that propagation is prohibited or the variety is protected; damages then extend only to infringement after such notice. For both damages and an injunction, a court may be lenient concerning disposal of material acquired in good faith through acts before such notice. These are reserved remedial conditions, not findings of marking, actual awareness, good faith, or entitlement to leniency in this case. Actual relief and precise lawful injunction implementation remain for the receiving courts. The adjudicated own-planting failure is not reopened by that limited return.

#### Precedent Treatment and Current-Law Effect

Brown supplies contextual respect for enacted conditions without extending its veterans-specific rule. Landgraf and Rivers support obedience to the express temporal directions, with their actual coalition boundaries preserved. No precedent is overruled, no statutory quota is invented, and the hypothetical notice status of a truly exempt sale remains unresolved by the Court.

Effective January 18, 1995, the former Act protects qualifying later disposition of seed genuinely saved for the farmer's own planting, while reproductive-market production and these sales fall outside that condition. Ordinary marketing needs no advertising apparatus. The farmer's primary occupation concerns the business as a whole, without a crop-specific half-crop safe harbor. The Court establishes no final §2541(6) liability or damages and no rule resolving notice for a truly §2543-exempt sale. The 1994 amendments retain their actual future effective date and qualified grandfather provisions.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela agrees that the Federal Circuit's broad occupation-based exemption must be reversed and that former law controls. Seed genuinely saved to replant the farmer's own acreage may later be sold to qualified farmers when plans change and the statutory conditions are met. Intentional multiplication for reproductive marketing is outside that protected core, but neither high yield alone nor an invented fifty-percent rule establishes infringement. Marketing does not require advertising. The scale and reproductive destination of these sales support applying the multiplication restriction; he would remand for application of the narrower construction and resolution of unresolved infringement and remedial questions, without joining the Court's conclusive application to every challenged sale.

He separately reaches the legal relationship of the exemption to the notice claim. Former §2543 expressly retains §§2541(3) and (4) but does not separately except §2541(6) from its protection. In his view, a sale actually satisfying the correctly construed exemption remains exempt from notice-infringement liability; if it is not exempt, the receiving court must determine §2541(6)'s ordinary elements and lawful relief. Rejection of the broad exemption does not establish absence of notice, satisfaction of every element, or damages. His remand includes that pleaded claim and requires reconsideration of its exemption premise under the narrower rule. He makes no final notice finding, imposes no new notice form, and imports no later amendment backward. This conditional notice proposition is his alone; the Court expressly leaves it undecided.

Justice Stevens would affirm because Congress used marketing in §2541(3) and selling in §2541(1). He reads marketing more narrowly to address commercial distribution through cooperatives, wholesalers, retailers, or comparable extensive coordinated activity, rather than every ordinary farmer sale. Unclear restraints on alienation should not be enlarged by treating those distinct terms as equivalent. He accepts that the Court's own-planting exposition follows if its broad marketing premise is adopted, but rejects that premise. His position is therefore not simply a preference for a different acreage quota or an exemption from every statutory condition. He applies former law and would preserve the Federal Circuit's judgment and eligibility remand; he makes no independent final notice-liability or damages finding.
