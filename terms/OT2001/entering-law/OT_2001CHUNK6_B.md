## Standards and Tests selection: Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal

### Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal

**Governing question:** Effect of a preserved removal error when complete diversity actually exists before trial and through judgment.

**Current rule:** Absent complete diversity at removal, a timely remand motion requires remand under the applicable statutes. A later lawful dismissal of the nondiverse party does not validate the earlier refusal, waive the objection or require extraordinary pretrial review. If actual §1332 diversity arises before trial and continues through judgment, that earlier defect alone does not require vacating the completed federal judgment. Actual jurisdiction at trial and judgment is indispensable; consent, efficiency and finality cannot supply it.

**Authority by component:**

- Earlier statutory duty: Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis — Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis — The erroneous refusal to remand remains error; bounded judgment-preservation rule: Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis — Actual jurisdiction before trial preserves the completed judgment against this earlier defect. O’Connor, all nine Justices, December 10, 1996.

**Present operation effective:** Caterpillar, December 10, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal cure for removal defects, collusive diversity manufacture or collateral review of an entered covered remand is authorized. Independently available fees, sanctions and ordinary merits appeals retain their own requirements.

## Standards and Tests selection: Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

### Compensatory remedies for intentional Title IX discrimination

**Governing question:** Whether remedial silence or Spending Clause notice bars compensatory damages in the established Title IX private action.

**Current rule:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing relief does not itself create a new cause of action. Title IX's established private action permits compensatory damages for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation, and compensable loss. The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar that compensation for intentional breach of Title IX's express nondiscrimination condition.

## Standards and Tests selection: Title VII punitive culpability and managerial attribution

### Title VII punitive culpability and managerial attribution

**Governing question:** When §1981a permits punitive consideration and attributes a manager’s conduct to an employer.

**Current rule:** Punitive eligibility requires intentional discrimination with malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights: at minimum, discrimination in the face of a perceived risk of federal illegality. Egregious conduct may prove culpability but is not an additional indispensable element. Ordinary liability does not automatically establish punitive culpability, attribution or jury submission. The court asks whether evidence and reasonable employee-favorable inferences permit the punitive predicates; if submitted, the jury retains discretion whether to award. For the managerial attribution route, actual entrusted authority and discretion plus action within employment scope control; title alone and an invariant top-management requirement do not.

**Authority by component:**

- Kolstad v. American Dental Association — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Punitive eligibility turns on culpability toward federal rights, not an additional egregious act”, 1999-06-22; O’Connor Part I with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part I also supplies separate submission, discretionary award and amount inquiries.
- Kolstad v. American Dental Association — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Managerial punitive attribution depends on actual authority and scope”, 1999-06-22; O’Connor Part II-A with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. Five direct supporters supply the managerial route.
- Kolstad v. American Dental Association — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Actual good-faith compliance limits managerial vicarious punishment”, 1999-06-22; the same five Justices in Part II-B. Supplies the confined actual-compliance limitation.

**Present operation effective:** Kolstad, June 22, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Managerial vicarious punitive liability does not attach when the discriminatory decision is contrary to meaningful, reasonably designed and actually implemented good-faith Title VII compliance efforts. Paper policy, nonreporting or a good-faith label alone is insufficient. This limit does not remove ordinary liability or compensation or excuse the employer’s own discriminatory policy or independently established wrongdoing. Training, reporting, enforcement and correction may matter without a mandatory checklist.

**Open question:** No majority allocates a universal burden or standard of proof for the compliance limit, makes the managerial route exclusive or decides unused authorization, hiring and ratification routes. No general harassment defense, employee reporting duty or additional clear-and-convincing requirement follows.

**Related current rules:** Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment retains its distinct ordinary-liability rules and unresolved defense questions. Punitive eligibility does not replace statutory caps or independent amount review.

## Standards and Tests selection: Criminal forfeiture, informed waiver and statutory property limits

### Criminal forfeiture, informed waiver and statutory property limits

**Governing question:** Forfeiture under §§848 and 853 following a guilty plea and relinquishment of Rule 31(e)’s special verdict.

**Current rule:** Extent of forfeiture under this scheme is a sentencing determination rather than an element of continuing-criminal-enterprise guilt requiring a Sixth Amendment jury determination. Former Rule 11(f)’s factual-basis inquiry concerns the substantive guilty plea and does not independently require a separate guilt-style inquiry into forfeiture’s extent. A defendant may knowingly and voluntarily waive the statutory forfeiture jury protection and admit supporting facts. Represented affirmative agreement, a signed jury waiver and courtroom assent to ending trial and proceeding to sentence can establish that waiver without an additional forfeiture-specific Rule 11(c) warning or Rule 23(a) writing; competence, signature or silence alone does not establish informed surrender of every issue. A judge must not mislead or leave evident confusion uncorrected. Consent cannot enlarge §853 or transfer an interest beyond the defendant’s lawful power. Section 853(a) reaches violation proceeds; the defendant’s property used or intended in any manner or part to commit or facilitate the violation; and, for CCE, the defendant’s interests in or claims against the enterprise and property or contractual rights affording control. Under §853(p), the defendant’s act or omission must make qualifying property unavailable through inability to locate despite due diligence, transfer/sale/deposit with a third party, placement outside jurisdiction, substantial value diminution, or commingling not divisible without difficulty; then other property of the defendant must be forfeited up to that qualifying value. Section 853(d)’s rebuttable trial presumption requires Government proof by a preponderance of both acquisition during or reasonably after the violation and no likely nonviolation source. An all-assets promise supplies neither predicate.

**Authority by component:**

- Consent and statutory boundaries: Agreement permits waiver but cannot enlarge the forfeiture statute, 1995-11-07; O’Connor Part I-B, all nine.
- Rule 11(f) scope: Former Rule 11(f) does not require a separate forfeiture factual-basis inquiry, same date; O’Connor Part I-A with all Associates (eight).
- Constitutional classification and bounded sufficient waiver: The particular forfeiture determination is not an offense-element jury question; The affirmative agreement and courtroom assent waive the statutory special verdict, same date; O’Connor Parts II-A–B with Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Libretti, 1995-11-07.

**Open question:** The precise extent of an independent factual-assurance duty outside former Rule 11(f) remains unresolved. No general classification of every punishment fact, later Rule 32.2 procedure, automatic asset eligibility or displacement of §853(n) third-party rights follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Libretti v. United States, 516 U.S. 29.

## Standards and Tests selection: Post-enactment interstate child-support failures

### Post-enactment interstate child-support failures

**Governing question:** Elements and temporal limits of prosecution under the original Child Support Recovery Act, 18 U.S.C. §228.

**Current rule:** The government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt a willful failure after October 25, 1992 to pay a court-ordered or State-administratively fixed obligation supporting a child, or child and custodial parent, with the child residing in another State. The amount must be unpaid for longer than one year or exceed $5,000; these are alternatives, and neither substitutes for the different-state condition. Earlier arrears may establish background or past-due status but a completed pre-enactment failure cannot constitute the federal crime. The predicate order must exist and remain operative; legally recognized ability-related and other defenses survive. Upon conviction, §228(c) requires restitution under §3663 equal to the past-due obligation existing at sentencing.

Both prosecutions remain confined to post-enactment failures and proof of every element; conditional restitution: United States v. Mussari / United States v. Schroeder — IV. Each appellate trial remand stands; any restitution remains a separate conditional issue, O'Connor for all nine, 1997-06-30.

**Present operation effective:** Mussari/Schroeder, 1997-06-30, construing the offense effective 1992-10-25.

**Open question:** Precise statutory and constitutional treatment of older arrears in restitution remains undecided; neither the offense-date limit nor the sentencing-balance text alone resolves it. No comprehensive continuing-offense theory is adopted.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

### Commerce connection, mechanism, and class-wide effects

**Governing question:** How courts determine whether a regulated class falls within Congress’s Commerce Clause authority, including when aggregation, a case-specific nexus, or inclusion within a broader federal regime may perform constitutional work.

**Current rule:** The Court first identifies the class Congress actually regulated and the interstate mechanism asserted to connect that class to commerce among the several States. Interstate commerce itself, channels, instrumentalities/persons/things genuinely in commerce, and properly defined economic classes participating in or materially affecting actual interstate markets remain established routes. Congress may also include intrastate conduct when inclusion is reasonably necessary to prevent evasion or material impairment of a valid federal interstate regulatory regime, or when it reasonably identifies, on a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis, a substantial interstate externality generated by the regulated class that individual States cannot reasonably confine or internalize, and the federal prohibition reasonably fits that demonstrated spillover. Aggregate magnitude and substantiality may measure the effect of a class only after a constitutionally sufficient connection has been identified; generalized chains through crime, education, health, insurance, consumption, productivity, public expenditure, or similar downstream consequences cannot themselves create the missing connection.

The Government must identify and support whichever interstate mechanism it invokes with a materially adequate legislative or adjudicative basis. The court independently determines whether the asserted interstate mechanism is constitutionally eligible. Once that mechanism exists, Congress receives substantial latitude on predictive judgment, magnitude, and practical regulatory design, but a court may not invent a market, externality, causal pathway or anti-evasion necessity absent from the relevant record. A reasonable and judicially identifiable fit must exist between the class Congress enacted and the mechanism supporting authority. The limiting-principle inquiry tests whether the asserted mechanism leaves an enforceable distinction between the enumerated commerce power and a general police power; it is not a free-form balance of federal and State interests.

The externality route requires identification of the regulated class; a concrete cross-border ecological, commercial or comparable interstate effect; why that effect is substantial rather than speculative or merely nationally interesting; why the affected States cannot reasonably internalize or control it through ordinary territorial authority; and the reasonable fit of the federal prohibition to that effect. Biodiversity's importance, speculative scientific or future economic value, tourism, development pressure, the statute's comprehensiveness, and the occurrence of similar problems in many States are insufficient by themselves. The rule requires no fixed number of States, incidents, dollars or species, market percentage or scientific threshold.

A court may not save an unambiguous federal offense by inserting an omitted commerce element. A statutory jurisdictional element is relevant only insofar as the connection it requires actually performs constitutional work; the mere existence of jurisdictional words is not itself a safe harbor. Remote historical movement may satisfy a statutory nexus, but it does not by itself supply a constitutional basis for regulating a possession class; Congress cannot evade Lopez merely by adding a jurisdictional element.

For Hobbs Act robbery, direct impairment of the operating assets of an active business participating in interstate commerce supplies the required commercial connection when the enacted commerce element is proved. An individual robbery need not have a substantial effect; aggregation measures this connected class's magnitude. A business label, a victim's potential consumption, or money's eventual interstate use cannot replace the connection. The commerce element remains an element of the offense, not subject-matter jurisdiction.

Under ESA §9(a)(1)(B), the regulated class is the taking of listed wildlife, including habitat-caused injury, without a commercial element. The economic character of particular development projects does not by itself supply a constitutionally sufficient connection fitted to that enacted class; the asserted mechanism for these applications must be identified independently of those commercial features. The distinct statutory requirements for habitat-based harm and conditional permission remain governed by Habitat injury within the Endangered Species Act’s harm prohibition.

The recipient-possession restrictions in §§922(g)(1) and (g)(9) have a distinct fitted connection to the corresponding §922(d)(1) and (d)(9) prohibited-recipient supply restrictions: preventing the disqualified recipients’ acquisition and retention from evading or materially impairing those valid supply restrictions supports inclusion under the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. The enacted interstate conditions remain separately provable offense requirements; historical interstate movement alone is not the constitutional mechanism. Section 922(g)(1) requires neither contemporaneous movement nor an individually substantial or direct interstate effect beyond its retained nexus construction. The prosecution must prove the enacted nexus beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:**

1. Define the enacted regulated class.
2. Identify the asserted interstate-commerce connection.
3. Identify the mechanism connecting the class to interstate commerce.
4. Determine whether the mechanism is constitutionally eligible.
5. Only then use aggregation or substantiality to evaluate class-wide magnitude.
6. Give Congress substantial latitude on prediction and regulatory design within the valid power.
7. Test whether the statutory class reasonably fits the asserted mechanism.
8. Confirm that the theory leaves an enforceable constitutional boundary.

## Standards and Tests selection: Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

### Hostile employment conditions without a psychological-injury prerequisite

**Governing question:** When discriminatory harassment alters employment conditions under Title VII.

**Current rule:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considering all circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and the employee actually perceives as abusive. Serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than merely offensive utterances, and unreasonable interference with work; none is indispensable. Psychological effects may inform subjective abuse. Mere offense without the objective and subjective predicates remains insufficient. The same-sex relationship of harasser and claimant does not exclude Title VII coverage. The claimant must show that the challenged conditions were imposed because of sex through competent direct or circumstantial evidence, without an invariant requirement of sexual desire, a particular orientation or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual language, contact or severity alone does not conclusively establish that causal connection. An all-male workforce is part of the context rather than categorical immunity.

O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. ## Standards and Tests selection: Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment

### Employer attribution of supervisor-created harassment

**Governing question:** When Title VII attributes a supervisor-created hostile environment to the employer without higher-management notice.

**Current rule:** The plaintiff must prove by a preponderance that actual delegated supervisory power materially aided creation or maintenance of an actionable hostile environment. Hiring or firing authority, alter-ego rank, higher-management notice, an express threat, completed personnel action or tangible economic loss is not invariably required; rank, employment or proximity alone is insufficient. Personal purpose or breach of a harassment prohibition does not by itself negate actual aid. Ordinary scope of employment, employer negligence and apparent authority remain separate routes. Apparent authority requires a reasonable belief traceable to the employer's manifestations and reasonable reliance on that appearance, not merely the supervisor's asserted power; it is not an additional element of actual-authority attribution. Prohibitions and correction after notice do not themselves erase earlier authority-aided conduct.

**Authority by component:**

## Standards and Tests selection: Actual federal contractual undertakings and allocated regulatory risk

### Actual federal contractual undertakings and allocated regulatory risk

**Governing question:** Formation, authority, scope and regulatory-risk allocation in a Tucker Act contract claim.

**Current rule:** Section 1491(a)(1) supplies jurisdiction, not a substantive promise: an actual authorized express or implied-in-fact undertaking is necessary, and fairness cannot create an obligation implied only in law. The Government’s ordinary specifications warranty assures performance of the prescribed procurement, but does not itself promise payment of a manufacturer’s third-party tort settlements or defense expenses. Compelled procurement and Defense Production Act §707 protection do not themselves create an indemnity or reimbursement promise. A demonstrated authorized financial undertaking may allocate the risk that valid later legislation prevents the promised treatment. Even assuming public and general sovereign action, ordinary impossibility does not discharge that assumed change-of-law risk. The actual bargain and its qualifications govern; general regulatory permission alone creates no immutable promise.

**Authority by component:**

- Hercules — actual authorized undertaking, specifications-warranty scope and §707 limits, 1996-03-04; Souter, Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg (six).
- Winstar — holdings 1 and 3–5, actual authority, allocated risk and ordinary contract loss, 1996-07-01; all nine support the actual-undertaking requirement; Souter, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer (eight) support the common financial-risk and assumed-risk holdings.

**Present operation effective:** Winstar, July 1, 1996, for allocated regulatory risk; Hercules’s scope and authorization limits remain.

**Exceptions and limits:** Congress retains its legislative power and the promisee must obey valid law. Contractual responsibility creates no injunction, automatic causation, goodwill-balance award, guaranteed recovery duration or takings entitlement. Causation, recoverable loss, mitigation, actual periods, conditions and termination provisions remain independent. A genuine authorized undertaking may cover third-party losses without an express indemnity; neither uncertain damages nor tort immunity destroys an actual contract.

**Open question:** Winstar supplies no majority comprehensive unmistakability framework or substantial-contractual-effect exception to sovereign acts. Its four-Justice threshold account and three-Justice substantial-effect alternative are noncontrolling; the eight-Justice assumed-risk holding does not depend on either.

**Holdings navigation:** Hercules, Inc. v. United States, 516 U.S. 417; United States v. Winstar Corp., 518 U.S. 839 (1996).

## Standards and Tests selection: Process before civil forfeiture seizure of real property

### Process before civil forfeiture seizure of real property

**Governing question:** When the Government may seize real property for civil forfeiture before notice and hearing.

**Current rule:** The Government ordinarily must provide the owner notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard before civil forfeiture seizure of real property; a Fourth Amendment warrant does not satisfy this independent Fifth Amendment duty. To proceed without prior process for exigent circumstances, the Government must show that less restrictive measures, such as recorded notice of the claim, a restraining order or a bond, would not prevent sale, destruction or continued unlawful use. Emergency seizure remains possible on that showing with appropriate subsequent process. The protection includes control and income from rented property, not only physical occupancy.

**Authority by component:**

- Prior process and Government exigency showing: United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property — Notice and a hearing before civil seizure of real property, 1993-12-13. Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Pre-seizure process portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg. These six adopt the same rule, its exigency qualification, and its application.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 1993-12-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** Movable-property exceptions retain their actual justification. No criminal-forfeiture rule, final ownership determination, immunity waiver or automatic compensation remedy follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Notice and a hearing before civil seizure of real property.

## Standards and Tests selection: Withdrawal-liability collection accrual and installments

### Withdrawal-liability collection accrual and installments

**Governing question:** When a withdrawal-liability collection claim accrues under §1451(f).

**Current rule:** Withdrawal creates liability but collection accrues only after assessment, scheduling and demand when a due installment is not paid. Section 1451(f) allows the longer of six years after accrual or three years after actual or constructive knowledge of the cause; fraud or concealment permits suit within six years after discovery. Each missed installment has its own period. A legally effective election to accelerate starts the period for accelerated debt, but a later aggregate demand does not revive already barred installments.

**Authority by component:**

- Collection accrual; separate installments and effective acceleration: Bay Area Laundry and Dry Cleaning Pension Trust Fund v. Ferbar Corp. of California, 1997-12-15; Breyer Parts I–II for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 1997-12-15.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The plan must assess and demand payment as soon as practicable. Payments begin no later than sixty days after demand notwithstanding review or appeal. Payment default requires failure to cure within sixty days after written notice; another plan-defined default must indicate substantial likelihood of inability to pay. Default alone is not an election to accelerate.

**Open question:** Whether a complaint’s alternative full-debt demand itself effects acceleration remains unresolved.

**Related current rules:** Withdrawal-liability installment assumptions and separate collection triggers governs computation and payment; those computational assumptions do not establish collection accrual.

**Holdings navigation:** Bay Area Laundry and Dry Cleaning Pension Trust Fund v. Ferbar Corp. of California — Pension withdrawal-liability collection and limitations.
