# United States v. Vonn; No. 00-973; 535 U.S. 55 — 2002-03-04

[Authoritative Record](../records/united-states-v-vonn_merits_2002-03-04.md)

## Event

United States v. Vonn, No. 00-973; 535 U.S. 55. Argued November 6, 2001; decided March 4, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 3. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which vacated the pleas and sentence using harmless-error review confined to the plea proceedings. The questions are whether an unpreserved Rule 11 omission receives Rule 52(b) review and whether relevant information throughout the existing record may inform its effect on substantial rights. The United States seeks correction of the review rules and reinstatement or reconsideration; Vonn seeks to preserve the judgment. Before the pleas, a magistrate twice gave counsel-rights advisements and Vonn acknowledged understanding. The plea judge omitted the trial-counsel warning at both hearings; the prosecutor's attempted correction at the later hearing did not cure it. Defense counsel did not object. A later withdrawal motion rested on a different ground.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The appellate judgment is vacated and the case remanded for plain-error review using the relevant whole record.

## Judgment & Remedy

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vacatur of the appellate judgment and return for review using the relevant whole record | Vacated and remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | None | The colloquy-only restriction cannot govern the effect inquiry |
| Standard governing the forfeited Rule 11 claim on remand | Rule 52(b), 8–1 | Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stevens | Apply error, plainness, substantial-rights effect and the separate discretionary-correction condition; Stevens would place harmlessness on the Government under Rule 11(h) |

The Court declines the Government's request for conclusive reinstatement here. The court of appeals must make the record-specific application under the correct framework before determining the pleas' and sentence's disposition.

## Holdings

### I. Forfeited Rule 11 error is governed by Rule 52(b)

**Holding and operative rule:** A defendant who forfeits a Rule 11 objection must satisfy Rule 52(b): there must be error that is plain and affects substantial rights, ordinarily with the defendant bearing the effect burden, and correction remains discretionary only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Rule 11(h) does not exempt this plea-taking omission from ordinary forfeiture review; intentional waiver remains distinct.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion has the direct support of Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight votes control; Stevens dissents from this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the plea rule and the general review rule operate together. Rule 11(h) requires disregarding variances that do not affect substantial rights, but does not say that a failure to object has no consequence. Olano supplies the separate requirements and usual defendant burden. Johnson applies that framework to an important constitutional omission without making its importance an automatic exception.

The contrary argument correctly identifies the judge's affirmative personal duty and a defendant's possible inability to recognize an omitted warning. Those concerns establish why the colloquy is mandatory; they do not delete Rule 52(b) for the unpreserved claim. McCarthy enforced an earlier Rule 11 regime, and Rule 11(h) displaced automatic reversal without expressly abolishing plain-error review. McCarthy did not decide the present choice of review standards. The trial-counsel omission therefore remains error despite Vonn's representation and the prosecutor's intervention. His different withdrawal argument did not preserve it. The ordinary effect inquiry and independent discretion requirement apply without requiring actual innocence or treating substantial-rights impact as sufficient by itself.

### II. Relevant portions of the existing record inform the error's effect

**Holding and operative rule:** In assessing the effect of the Rule 11 omission on substantial rights, a reviewing court may consider relevant information throughout the existing record, including earlier judicial rights advisements. Such information bears on actual understanding and effect; it neither cures the judge's omitted personal warning nor conclusively establishes understanding from representation or a signed form alone.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II of O'Connor's opinion, giving direct unanimous authority independent of the division over burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that actual earlier court advisements cannot be excluded merely because they occurred outside the plea colloquy. United States v. Young permits review of the whole record in assessing error, and Olano separates a procedural breach from its effect. McCarthy's personal-colloquy requirement remains mandatory; admitting relevant evidence about consequences does not excuse noncompliance.

The magistrate twice advised Vonn of counsel rights and obtained acknowledgments of understanding. Those events may inform what he understood when pleading, but their timing, content and demonstrated comprehension must be evaluated. The Court supplies no off-record explanation and treats neither counsel's presence nor a form as an irrebuttable cure. A transcript boundary that excludes actual recorded advice would obstruct the inquiry into effect without improving enforcement of the duty itself.

### III. The proper framework must first be applied below

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's use of the wrong burden and its exclusion of relevant existing-record material require vacatur and remand. That court must apply all four Rule 52(b) conditions, including record-specific effect and the independent basis for discretionary correction, before deciding the appropriate appellate relief.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part I. Stevens agrees with vacatur and remand for whole-record consideration but would apply the Government's harmlessness burden.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court of Appeals did not assess this record under the governing framework. Earlier warnings are relevant, not conclusive, so their presence alone cannot support the Government's requested immediate reinstatement. Olano requires the effect inquiry and the distinct judgment about correction to retain separate work. The case returns for that application and any other properly remaining appellate issues. The perjury-warning contention remains undecided because it was not resolved below and falls outside the review questions decided here. The case concerns direct review of this forfeited federal-rule omission, not collateral relief or the consequences of every constitutional plea defect.


## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Olano: applied to Rule 11 forfeiture with its usual substantial-rights burden and independent discretionary condition; no automatic reversal, innocence requirement or comprehensive resolution of its reserved categories follows.
- Johnson v. United States: applied at its actual forfeiture and discretionary-correction scope; its particular intervening-law application becomes no general guilt-based exception.
- McCarthy v. United States: its mandatory personal-colloquy principle remains, while the later Rule 11(h) text precludes importing its automatic-reversal consequence into the present rules.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applied to relevant whole-record review of the error's effect, not as permission to omit the required warning.
- Boykin v. Alabama: its constitutional plea requirements remain distinct; this decision supplies no general constitutional-invalidity or collateral-review rule.


## Law After Decision

Direct review of the forfeited Rule 11 omission uses Rule 52(b) and the relevant existing record. Eight Justices establish the defendant-burden and independent-discretion requirements, and all nine reject a colloquy-only effect inquiry. The judge's personal advice duty remains mandatory. Record-specific effect and justified correction are ordinary applications assigned to the court of appeals under that law.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stevens joins Part II's use of relevant material throughout the official record and agrees that the appellate judgment should be vacated. He rejects Parts I and III's transfer of the effect burden to the defendant. Rule 11 assigns the judge affirmative duties during a procedure in which an uninformed defendant may not recognize the missing advice. Its specific harmless-error provision should therefore leave the Government responsible for showing that the omission was harmless. McCarthy supplies the distinctive judicial obligation; Stevens's Olano dissent distinguishes procedural protections from easily measured outcome injury, and his Johnson join did not decide whether Rule 11(h) controls this separate plea procedure. He would remand for whole-record harmless-error review under Rule 11(h). Earlier recorded advisements may establish actual knowledge under that inquiry, but representation is not itself compliance, and neither automatic invalidation nor invented off-record advice follows from his position.

## Procedure After Action

The appellate judgment is vacated. On remand the Ninth Circuit must consider the relevant existing record under each Rule 52(b) condition and determine the resulting appellate relief. Other properly remaining issues, including the unresolved perjury-warning contention, retain their actual procedural footing. The mandate itself neither reinstates the convictions conclusively nor grants withdrawal of the pleas.

The appellate record includes the earlier judicial warnings and acknowledgments; the trial-counsel omission and the separate perjury-warning contention remain distinct.

# United States v. Cotton; No. 01-687; 535 U.S. 625. — 2002-05-20

[Authoritative Record](../records/united_states_v_cotton_merits_2002-05-20.md)

## Event

**Case:** United States v. Cotton, No. 01-687, 535 U.S. 625.
**Event:** Merits decision.
**Argued:** April 15, 2002.
**Decided:** May 20, 2002.
**Route and posture:** Certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 261 F.3d 397, which vacated the enhanced sentences and directed sentences not exceeding twenty years. Its reasoning treated the operative indictment's quantity omission as jurisdictional and the grand-jury deprivation as warranting correction without regard to evidentiary strength.
**Question:** Whether the omitted quantity allegation removes jurisdiction or requires correction of the enhanced sentences on this unpreserved indictment and jury-quantity claim.
**Record:** The original indictment alleged at least five kilograms of cocaine and fifty grams of cocaine base. The operative superseding indictment altered the conspiracy period, added defendants and alleged only a detectable amount. The jury was instructed that quantity was immaterial and returned no quantity finding. The judge made larger Guidelines quantity findings by a preponderance and imposed five life terms and two thirty-year terms. Respondents disputed credibility and individual Guidelines quantities but did not preserve the indictment or jury-quantity objection at trial. The convictions themselves are not challenged here.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Fourth Circuit's sentence-vacatur judgment and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit's sentence-vacatur judgment and remand, 9–0.
**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Remedy:** The enhanced sentences remain undisturbed on this forfeited claim. The remand implements rejection of the jurisdictional and fourth-condition vacatur grounds; it does not permit a fresh discretionary choice on the same omissions and record. The conspiracy convictions remain intact.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer in full; Scalia except Part II-B | Reverses and remands | Parts I, II-A and III: all nine. Part II-B affirmative plainness: all except Scalia. |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Scalia | No joiner | Supports reversal and remand | Assumes plainness; joins the rest of the Court's opinion. |

## Holdings

### I. The quantity omission does not remove subject-matter jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** Omission of the aggravating drug quantity from an indictment charging a federal offense does not withdraw the district court's subject-matter jurisdiction under 18 U.S.C. §3231. Ex parte Bain is overruled only insofar as it treats this indictment defect as jurisdictional; the prohibition on substantive indictment amendment without grand-jury resubmission, except changes of form, remains.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by every other Justice, supplies nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress gives federal district courts authority to adjudicate federal criminal prosecutions. An error in stating the accusation differs from absence of power over that class of case. Bain's broad language arose when habeas review provided the channel for correcting federal criminal error; it does not establish the present jurisdictional consequence. Lamar v. United States and United States v. Williams distinguish the power to adjudicate from the sufficiency of the accusation. The conspiracy charged here is within §3231 even though the superseding indictment lacks the quantity needed for the enhanced maximum.

The grand jury's constitutional function remains. Neither the original indictment nor later judicial findings may replace the operative superseding indictment. Stirone v. United States and Russell v. United States concern preserved charging objections and do not make this forfeited omission jurisdictional. Complete absence of a charging instrument is outside the question presented.

### II. The constitutional error remains subject to distinct forfeited-error requirements

**Controlling proposition:** The enhanced sentences lack the required quantity allegation and jury determination, and the unpreserved objection is reviewed under Rule 52(b), not treated as an intentional waiver or a jurisdictional nullity. Error, plainness, an effect on substantial rights and a serious effect on the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings remain distinct requirements; substantial-rights effect is assumed here without deciding prejudice or structural classification.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II-A's error, forfeiture and separate-requirements analysis. Part II-B's affirmative intervening-law plainness proposition is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia assumes plainness and does not join Part II-B.

**Controlling explanation:** Apprendi requires the fact authorizing a higher maximum to receive element protections. The superseding indictment alleged a detectable amount, the jury was instructed that quantity was immaterial, and no quantity verdict was returned. Judicial preponderance findings, notice of enhanced exposure and stipulations about physical exhibits do not supply either missing constitutional determination or an admission of the aggravated offense.

Olano distinguishes failure to object from intentional relinquishment and makes discretionary correction independent of substantial-rights injury. Johnson applies that distinction to a genuine jury-element omission. Under Johnson's intervening-law rule, the error is plain when controlling law changes settled contrary trial law before direct review ends and the error is clear at appellate consideration. That is the posture here; the Court does not decide initially unsettled trial law. Assuming the third condition permits resolution at the independent fourth condition without declaring the omission harmless, structural or cured. Preserved error retains its distinct inquiry; the Court does not authorize future omission of required allegations or jury findings.

### III. This record does not warrant fourth-condition correction

**Controlling proposition:** The forfeited quantity error does not seriously affect the fairness, integrity or public reputation of these proceedings in the established circumstances: proof that the conspiracy involved at least fifty grams of cocaine base is overwhelming and essentially uncontroverted at that statutory threshold. The enhanced sentences receive no relief on this claim, even assuming an effect on substantial rights; this is a particular appellate-remedy decision, not a substitute jury finding or a universal overwhelming-evidence exception.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by every other Justice, supplies nine direct votes for the rule, application and reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson permits refusal to correct a forfeited element error on the particular evidentiary and procedural circumstances without erasing the right. Here the jury found the conspiracy, both sides report physical seizures totaling approximately 380 grams of cocaine base, and the record gave notice of enhanced exposure. The supported credibility and defendant-specific objections concern substantially larger Guidelines quantities. They do not make the fifty-gram conspiracy threshold genuinely contestable on this record. The original indictment bears on notice only; it is not substituted for the superseding indictment.

The defendants correctly emphasize that neither grand nor petit jury made the required quantity determination. But genuine constitutional error and substantial-rights injury do not alone satisfy Olano's fourth condition. The decisive threshold proof, the actual objections and the intervening change from settled trial law support leaving the sentences undisturbed on this forfeited claim. The Fourth Circuit erred in treating evidentiary strength as irrelevant merely because the omission concerned the grand jury.

The Court makes no personal quantity admission for any respondent and adopts no general rule of conspiracy-quantity attribution. It does not replace the verdict, determine guilt of an aggravated offense anew or establish an innocence prerequisite. Preserved claims, materially contested threshold evidence and different trial-strategy circumstances require their applicable analysis. The remand implements rejection of these vacatur grounds; it does not reopen discretionary correction of the same omissions on the same record.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Bain, 121 U.S. 1 (1887):** Overruled only insofar as the present indictment defect removes jurisdiction; substantive amendment still requires grand-jury resubmission, with changes of form excepted.
- **Lamar v. United States; United States v. Williams:** Apply their distinction between authority over a federal prosecution and a defect in the accusation.
- **Stirone v. United States; Russell v. United States:** Preserve their charging protections for preserved objections; they do not require jurisdictional treatment or automatic correction here.
- **Apprendi v. New Jersey, 530 U.S. 466 (2000):** Applies the genuine element-protection requirement; strong proof and notice do not supply a missing allegation, verdict or valid element admission.
- **United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993):** Applies its separate forfeiture, plainness, substantial-rights and discretionary-correction requirements; the third condition does not decide the fourth.
- **Johnson v. United States, May 12, 1997:** Applies its intervening-law plainness rule and particular fourth-condition refusal of relief without converting either into a universal evidence exception.
- **United States v. Vonn, March 4, 2002:** Its distinct Rule 11 record inquiry and remand do not require a second appellate exercise of discretion in every forfeited-error case.
- **Neder v. United States:** Preserved-error review remains distinct; this decision supplies no replacement for its actual-verdict inquiry.

## Law After Decision

An omitted aggravating quantity is a real constitutional charging and jury error but does not withdraw §3231 jurisdiction over this federal prosecution. Forfeited claims remain subject to Rule 52(b)'s distinct requirements. This threshold-specific fourth-condition decision leaves the sentences undisturbed without authorizing future element omissions or converting judicial quantity findings into a verdict. Preserved error and materially different contested records retain their own governing analysis.

## Separate Writings

Scalia concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins Parts I, II-A and III, including the genuine constitutional error, forfeiture, assumed substantial-rights injury and denial of correction under the independent fourth condition. He assumes that the error is plain without joining Part II-B's affirmative intervening-law plainness rule. Because the fourth condition is unsatisfied on this record even on that assumption, the broader plainness determination is unnecessary to his vote. He does not treat appellate assessment of the evidence as a jury verdict, find an intentional waiver or adopt a categorical exception to Apprendi.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit's judgment is reversed and the cases remanded for proceedings consistent with rejection of the stated sentence-vacatur grounds. The challenged sentences receive no relief on this claim, and the convictions remain intact.
