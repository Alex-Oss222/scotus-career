# Board of Education of Independent School District No. 92 of Pottawatomie County v. Earls: exact public-law reading copy

Mechanically copied bounded Public Projection. The Record controls. Effective June 27, 2002.

## Event

Board of Education of Independent School District No. 92 of Pottawatomie County v. Earls, No. 01-332. Argued March 19, 2002; decided June 27, 2002. On certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, which reversed summary judgment for the school district and rejected suspicionless urinalysis of the challenged nonathletic competitive extracurricular participants. Earls has standing from her participation; James's separate academic-eligibility standing question need not be decided. The students seek declaratory and prospective relief, and the district seeks reinstatement of its judgment.

Tecumseh adopted the policy in fall 1998. Its implemented population consists of competitive activities sanctioned by the state activities association, including academic teams, band, choir, agricultural and homemaking organizations, and athletics. The granted challenge concerns the nonathletic expansion. Initial and random testing supplement a separate reasonable-suspicion provision. A monitor stands outside the closed stall and can hear production. Written restrictions confine access and use of results, prohibit police use and ordinary academic discipline, and impose counseling and participation consequences. Supported evidence disputes aspects of handling prescription information and results; no finding establishes that another student actually read the prescription information.

After a first positive, participation may continue with proof of counseling within five days of the parental meeting and another test two weeks later. A second positive produces fourteen days' exclusion, four hours of counseling and monthly testing. A third produces exclusion for the rest of the school year or eighty-eight school days, whichever is longer. These are actual participation consequences; the policy does not require withdrawal from ordinary schooling.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The judgment rejecting the challenged nonathletic testing expansion is affirmed, 5–4, under the contextual Fourth Amendment standard.

## Holdings

### I. School responsibility informs reasonableness without erasing the search

**Holding and operative rule:** Compelled urine collection and analysis are searches, and extracurricular participation does not waive Fourth Amendment protection. A claimed protective need beyond ordinary law enforcement must justify the actual program through a contextual assessment of the population, school responsibilities, concrete need and fit, bodily and informational intrusion, and operational safeguards; catastrophe, a numerical epidemic, safety-sensitive duties and exhaustion of alternatives are not universal prerequisites.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Ginsburg's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Vernonia preserves students' protection while sustaining its particular athlete program on combined circumstances. T.L.O. recognizes school supervision without dispensing with reasonableness. Chandler requires actual need and operational fit, not a rigid checklist. The district's protective purpose and exclusion of police use distinguish the ordinary-crime program in Edmond and the coordinated prosecution operation in Ferguson, permitting the special-needs inquiry. They do not themselves answer it. A voluntary activity condition is part of the intrusion's setting, not consent to any search attached to that activity. The Court therefore declines both an automatic school-custody exception and a requirement that each club first develop a proven epidemic.

**Precedent treatment:** Vernonia's search/no-waiver rule is applied; its athlete holding remains contextual. Chandler's actual-need inquiry is applied. T.L.O., Edmond and Ferguson retain their distinct supervisory and enforcement boundaries.

### II. This nonathletic expansion lacks sufficient contextual justification

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, the district's general drug concern and the students' competitive academic or cultural participation do not sufficiently justify suspicionless bodily testing of the challenged nonathletic groups, even with the written collection and use protections. The judgment rejecting this expansion is affirmed; the rule does not bar a differently supported program or disturb the separately sustained athlete program.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five direct votes control.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the relation between the selected activities and the compelled specimen is inadequate. Vernonia's documented athlete-centered concern, athletic safety circumstances and communal privacy expectations were material to its balance. Band, choir and academic competition do not themselves reproduce them. The absence of those features is a contextual difference, not a mandatory athletic-risk checklist.

The district has a real responsibility to protect children and evidence of some drug use. Monitoring outside a closed stall, confined information access, nonpolice use and counseling-centered participation consequences reduce the burden. Those factors receive genuine weight. They do not explain why this nonathletic competitive population should be selected for suspicionless bodily searches merely because the same general drug concern affects all students. General prevention cannot make every educational activity a sufficient testing category.

The opposing position properly emphasizes prevention before a crisis and the limited consequences of refusal. Those principles reject a categorical epidemic requirement, but do not supply the missing relation in this record. The Court applies Vernonia's controlling balance, not a former dissent's alternative necessity rule. An affirmance on this independently sufficient substantive ground permits prospective relief without resolving every implementation allegation.

**Precedent treatment:** Vernonia's athlete application is distinguished while its balancing method is applied. Chandler reinforces the need for actual operational fit without prescribing a least-intrusive-alternative rule.

### III. Written safeguards receive weight without becoming findings of compliance

**Holding and operative rule:** Written confidentiality and use restrictions reduce the asserted intrusion, but supported contrary implementation evidence cannot be resolved for the district merely by assuming perfect compliance at summary judgment. Here the program's insufficient justification independently supports declaratory and appropriate prospective relief even on the district's stronger account of its safeguards, so no independent disclosure claim or automatic trial is created.

**Authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's opinion has the same five direct joins as Part II. The opposing four agree that material implementation disputes require proper evidentiary treatment but seek a different disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Urinalysis can reveal prescription and other medical information as well as illicit-drug results. Access and use therefore matter to the search burden. Vernonia's separate facial prescription ruling allowed consideration of a textually possible confidential method; it did not establish actual confidential practice or authorize intrusive implementation. Ordinary summary-judgment rules govern the evidentiary support and materiality of the present allegations. The Court finds no actual disclosure proved and does not turn an administrative lapse into a freestanding constitutional claim.

Because Part II's conclusion remains the same even crediting the written protection, the disputed operational account need not delay this judgment. Appropriate prospective relief addresses only the challenged nonathletic expansion. Any independently pleaded disclosure claim requires its own preserved source, elements and remedy. Neither that claim nor a replacement testing program is ordered here.

**Precedent treatment:** Vernonia's facial/actual distinction is applied without converting a possible confidential construction into a finding. Ordinary Rule 56 standards retain their separate factual function.

## Precedent Treatment

- Vernonia School District 47J v. Acton: applies search/no-waiver and contextual balancing; distinguishes the documented athlete program and preserves the limited facial prescription holding.
- Chandler v. Miller: applies actual need and operational fit; no epidemic, catastrophe, universal safety duty or exhaustion rule is adopted.
- New Jersey v. T.L.O.: preserves school supervision within constitutional reasonableness.
- Skinner v. Railway Labor Executives' Association and National Treasury Employees Union v. Von Raab: preserve contextual protective-needs analysis; limited collection remains a search.
- City of Indianapolis v. Edmond and Ferguson v. City of Charleston: distinguished by this policy's confined educational and nonpolice operation; their actual enforcement-purpose holdings remain intact.
- United States v. Knights, United States v. Arvizu and United States v. Drayton: retain their distinct adult-supervision, stop and consent settings; none creates student waiver or a general school search exception.
- Leventhal v. Knapek: preserves attention to actual institutional purpose and scope, without resolving school urinalysis by analogy alone.

## Law After Decision

Suspicionless testing of this nonathletic competitive population fails the governing contextual balance. The athlete holding remains effective, as does reasonable-suspicion authority. The new application identifies inadequate fit on this record rather than a categorical prohibition of all nonathletic programs. Written safeguards remain relevant but do not settle actual implementation; the substantive ground independently supports the prospective disposition.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer.** They join the search/no-waiver and contextual standard in Part I but would vacate and remand. School custody and prevention carry substantial weight even without unusual athletic danger or a proven drug epidemic within each club. The implemented competitive program, modest observed collection, limited information, restricted nonpolice use and counseling or participation consequences can together be reasonable. Greater nonathlete privacy is a real counterweight; it does not make these students equivalent to adults outside school supervision. Chandler's unsupported adult candidate program does not dictate invalidity here.

Their substantive approval concerns the defined protective operation, not every extracurricular or all-student testing program. Vernonia's facial prescription construction supplies no finding that this policy operated confidentially. Supported material disputes about prescription handling and access must receive ordinary summary-judgment treatment; unsupported allegations may be rejected on that basis. They therefore withhold unconditional reinstatement of the district's judgment. Their remand would determine the significance of actual safeguards within the search inquiry, without finding another student's access, creating a separate disclosure right or automatically ordering trial.

**Breyer, separately concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Breyer joins Thomas's complete position and emphasizes schools' responsibility to reduce demand, offer counseling and prevent harm before a selected club develops entrenched use. Community deliberation can inform the program's practical acceptability but is not each student's constitutional consent. He acknowledges embarrassment from specimen collection and uncertainty about efficacy. Refusal costs extracurricular participation, not ordinary school attendance. Objective random selection may reduce stigma and selective targeting, but this program adds random testing to suspicion-based testing and therefore does not eliminate individualized selection. These considerations support the bounded program; they do not erase actual confidentiality disputes or broaden the remand into approval of every school search.

## Procedure After Action

The Tenth Circuit judgment is affirmed on the contextual ground stated by the Court. The matter returns for declaratory and appropriate prospective relief against the challenged nonathletic expansion. The separate athlete program and reasonable-suspicion testing remain intact. Confidentiality evidence remains part of the search record, without an independent disclosure judgment, damages award or compulsory trial.
