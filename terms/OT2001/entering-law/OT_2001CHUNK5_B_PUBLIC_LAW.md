### National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co. — 2002-01-16

Section 224's general coverage encompasses qualifying mixed cable-Internet and wireless telecommunications attachments. A gap or dispute about an applicable rate formula does not itself create a jurisdictional exclusion. The statutory formulas, ownership and provider limits, and qualifying state-regulation allocation retain their force. These are new coverage holdings; no Internet-service classification or particular rate is approved.

### United States v. Vonn — 2002-03-04

Direct review of the forfeited Rule 11 omission uses Rule 52(b) and the relevant existing record. Eight Justices establish the defendant-burden and independent-discretion requirements, and all nine reject a colloquy-only effect inquiry. The judge's personal advice duty remains mandatory. Record-specific effect and justified correction are ordinary applications assigned to the court of appeals under that law.

**Current procedural stage:** The appellate judgment is vacated. On remand the Ninth Circuit must consider the relevant existing record under each Rule 52(b) condition and determine the resulting appellate relief. Other properly remaining issues, including the unresolved perjury-warning contention, retain their actual procedural footing. The mandate itself neither reinstates the convictions conclusively nor grants withdrawal of the pleas.

### Mickens v. Taylor — 2002-03-27

A known potential conflict and the judge's neglected inquiry do not themselves establish the adverse effect required for this conflict-based exception to ordinary prejudice. Holloway retains its distinct automatic-relief rule. The Court assumes Sullivan's route is available here without deciding its application to every successive representation. The existing no-adverse-effect determination defeats this claim within the granted question.

### National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co. — 2002-01-16

Thomas, joined by Souter, concurs in the wireless holding but would vacate the mixed-service portion for a reasoned agency determination. If cable-modem access is cable service, subsection (d)'s formula controls; if it is telecommunications service, subsection (e)'s formula controls. Only after resolving those possibilities can residual authority under subsection (b) support the particular regulatory choice. The service supplied by an Internet service provider and the transmission carrying customers to that provider are distinct classification questions. The Commission's treatment of one does not necessarily resolve the other. Permian Basin's demand for a reasoned rate method supports returning that choice to the agency. Probable ultimate jurisdiction and policy favoring broadband deployment cannot supply the missing statutory explanation. This position rejects categorical noncoverage but withholds approval of the existing unexplained agency determination.

These published positions are noncontrolling at the stated scope. [Public opinion account](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK2_PUBLIC_LAW.md#national-cable-telecommunications-assn-inc-v-gulf-power-co-merits-2002-01-16).

### United States v. Vonn — 2002-03-04

[Record](../records/united-states-v-vonn_merits_2002-03-04.md)

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stevens joins Part II's use of relevant material throughout the official record and agrees that the appellate judgment should be vacated. He rejects Parts I and III's transfer of the effect burden to the defendant. Rule 11 assigns the judge affirmative duties during a procedure in which an uninformed defendant may not recognize the missing advice. Its specific harmless-error provision should therefore leave the Government responsible for showing that the omission was harmless. McCarthy supplies the distinctive judicial obligation; Stevens's Olano dissent distinguishes procedural protections from easily measured outcome injury, and his Johnson join did not decide whether Rule 11(h) controls this separate plea procedure. He would remand for whole-record harmless-error review under Rule 11(h). Earlier recorded advisements may establish actual knowledge under that inquiry, but representation is not itself compliance, and neither automatic invalidation nor invented off-record advice follows from his position.

### Mickens v. Taylor — 2002-03-27

[Record](../records/mickens_v_taylor_merits_2002-03-27.md)

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens would reverse the habeas denial and secure a new trial because the State appointed the murder victim's recent lawyer in a capital case without disclosure or the accused's informed acceptance, despite the judge's knowledge. The right to a meaningful lawyer-client relationship, counsel's disclosure obligation and the court's protective duty operate together. This exceptional State-created arrangement warrants presumed impairment under Cronic; Holloway, Sullivan and Wood supply the conflict safeguards, while Swidler preserves continuing confidentiality without proving any particular confidence. His rule concerns this undisclosed capital victim-counsel appointment, not every former-client relationship or ethical breach. He does not require another actual-conflict hearing before recognizing the constitutional entitlement.

**Souter, dissenting.** Souter would reverse and require a new trial under the court's prospective duty to address a sufficiently identified risk before representation unfolds. When the judge already has adequate notice, Holloway's preventive protection should not depend solely on an objection from a potentially conflicted lawyer. The judge must inquire, secure an intelligent waiver or arrange replacement when warranted. Souter distinguishes that prospective duty from later discovery: he reads Wood's retrospective inquiry as requiring actual conflict and adverse effect, rather than supporting an actual-conflict-only remand here. His discussion of possible adverse effects supplies no decision of the factual issue outside the grant and no direction for another hearing.

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by Ginsburg.** These Justices would reverse and secure a new trial on a narrower structural ground. The State supplied the recent criminal lawyer of the murder victim to defend the accused in a capital case; the immediate incompatibility, the capital proceeding's use of the victim's character and the State's creation of the arrangement produce a breakdown in the trial framework. Cronic and Fulminante support relief without reconstructing individual performance effects at that conjunction of facts. Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton reinforces the concern for institutional fairness. They expressly leave the general relationship among Holloway, Sullivan and Wood unresolved and do not join Souter's general known-risk rule or an actual-conflict-only remand.

Each dissent seeks the new-trial remedy through ordinary conditional habeas relief, permitting the State to retry with constitutionally adequate representation. None orders immediate unconditional release, acquittal or a new sentence alone. Their constitutional positions do not adjudicate an independently presented collateral defense on predicates absent from the question decided. Counsel maintained the narrower actual-conflict theory; the dissenters give their own legal answers to the granted conflict question and the requested new-trial remedy.
