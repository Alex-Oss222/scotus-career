# A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc. — 2002-04-23

[Authoritative Record](../records/a_and_m_records_v_napster_merits_2002-04-23.md)

## Event

A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc. Merits decision, April 23, 2002, on full merits submission. Review of the Ninth Circuit judgment, 239 F.3d 1004, entered February 12, 2001, concerning the preliminary injunction entered July 26, 2000 and explained and slightly modified August 10, 2000. The Ninth Circuit affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded. The questions concern the supported direct-infringement and secondary-liability predicates, sampling and space-shifting defenses, the Audio Home Recording Act and §512, the remaining owner-conduct and procedural objections, and the scope of preliminary relief. Napster's searchable directory facilitates music-file exchange; notices and account/index controls are established, but its servers do not possess users' MP3 contents. The review concerns the original restraint and its required correction, not a later replacement order.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit's mixed preliminary disposition is affirmed, and the case is remanded for a conforming injunction and further proceedings.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Court sustains the supported preliminary infringement and secondary-liability predicates and the rejection of the asserted defenses at this record's scope. It affirms the requirement to replace the overbroad injunction with a fitted order and remands for that work. The original July 26, 2000 restraint remains stayed until modification conforming to this judgment. Owners must supply usable identification of works and infringing files; service responses must remain within actual capabilities and applicable statutory limits. These findings remain preliminary. The $5 million security is sustained.

## Holdings

### I. The preliminary fair-use defenses fail under the integrated factors

**Holding and operative rule:** On this supported preliminary record, unrestricted exchange of complete protected creative recordings with unrelated users is unlikely to be fair use under §107. Nontransformative whole-work copying and supported substitution for existing and developing markets outweigh the claimed sampling or relocation purposes; artist-authorized and otherwise lawful files remain lawful.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by all eight other Justices. All nine directly support each of Parts I–VII, including the independent contributory and vicarious grounds and the qualifications below.

**Controlling explanation:** Campbell requires purpose, nature, amount and market effect together, without a dispositive commerciality presumption. The findings include displaced college sales and injury to a developing licensed-download market; this is not merely loss of fees for every unlicensed use. The contrary survey was weighed, not excluded. A plan to buy music later does not erase the present redistribution. Sony's private home time shifting does not establish immunity for public exchanges, and the claimed relocation extends beyond one's private personal devices. The result holds even assuming the owners bear the preliminary burden of likely defeating fair use; the allocation of that burden across all preliminary cases is unnecessary to resolve. The prima facie direct-infringement showing was unchallenged on this appeal; findings remain preliminary.

### II. Substantial lawful capability does not immunize specific ongoing assistance

**Holding and operative rule:** A service can incur contributory liability by knowingly providing material assistance to identified infringement, notwithstanding substantial noninfringing uses of its technology. Sony bars imputing the needed knowledge from capability alone; it does not immunize continuing materially assisting operation after specific knowledge.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The notices identified more than 12,000 infringing files, continued availability followed asserted account terminations, and the search and index functions materially assisted access. Those circumstances support likely knowledge and contribution. General knowledge that copyrighted music circulates does not establish unauthorized copying; lawful capability remains material and present infringement percentages are not an exclusive test. The rule requires no server access to MP3 contents the system does not possess.

### III. Financial benefit and actual supervision independently support preliminary vicarious liability

**Holding and operative rule:** Vicarious liability separately requires a direct financial benefit from infringement and the right and practical ability to supervise it. Attraction of infringing recordings to the user base can satisfy the benefit element without direct transaction fees, while account exclusion and index control support supervision over the service's actual assistance; both elements are supported here at the preliminary stage.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** This is an independently sufficient preliminary ground, not a substitute for contributory knowledge. The architecture both supports limited control and restricts the remedy. A theoretical ability to close the entire service does not create a general duty to inspect inaccessible user files or guarantee prevention of unknown copying.

### IV. The Audio Home Recording Act is confined to its defined coverage

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1008 does not immunize this general-purpose computer service's open redistribution merely because users characterize copying as noncommercial. Its consumer-recording protection depends on the defined devices, media and recording acts.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Under §1001(5), a digital musical recording is a material object containing, in digital form, only fixed sounds and related, relatively minor material, statements or instructions, from which the sounds and material can be perceived, reproduced or communicated directly or with a device. Entirely spoken-word recordings, including speech accompanied by merely incidental sounds, and objects containing computer programs are excluded; instructions constituting the fixed sounds and incidental material or bringing about their perception, reproduction or communication remain permitted. A digital audio recording device must be of a type commonly distributed to individuals for their use, whether separate or part of another machine, have a digital recording function designed or marketed primarily to make private digital audio copied recordings, and be capable of doing so. Professional model products and dictation, answering or other audio equipment designed and marketed primarily to fix nonmusical sounds are excluded. The service cannot detach the exemption from those conditions. This disposes of the asserted exemption without classifying every future dedicated player.

### V. Section 512 independently limits liability and relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Secondary-liability status does not itself defeat a §512 protection. The lower court must determine each invoked service category and condition independently and apply the corresponding injunction limits before imposing relief beyond them; failure under one category does not decide another.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts Part V and the complete operative conditions below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the infringement theories and Congress's service protections perform separate work. Supported likely liability does not answer whether a particular transmission or location-tool function satisfies its statutory conditions. Nor does failure under one category establish another's failure. The contrary demand for immediate unrestricted policing would make the statute's separate notice, knowledge, control and injunction limits disappear before the necessary findings. The preliminary record permits continued consideration of fitted relief, while the court must establish the applicable classification and predicates before imposing relief exceeding a statutory protection. This is affirmative remand work under the governing rules, not a decision that every safe harbor is unavailable.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Secondary-liability status alone does not defeat a safe harbor. The functions in (a)–(d) must be tested separately, and failure under one does not establish failure under another. Subsection (a) concerns automatic user-initiated transmitting, routing or connecting through the provider's system without provider selection of material or recipients except an automatic response, with no content modification. Any intermediate copy must not be maintained in a manner ordinarily accessible to anyone other than anticipated recipients, and must not remain ordinarily accessible to those recipients longer than reasonably necessary for transmission, routing or connection. Subsection (d)'s location-tool protection requires absence of actual knowledge or apparent-infringement awareness, or expeditious removal/disablement upon obtaining it; no directly attributable financial benefit where the provider has the right and ability to control; and expeditious response to a qualifying notice identifying the reference or link sufficiently to locate it. Subsection (i) additionally requires adoption and reasonable implementation of a policy terminating repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances, with subscribers and account holders informed of that policy and accommodation without interference of standard technical measures. Such measures must arise from broad open, fair, voluntary multi-industry consensus, be reasonably and nondiscriminatorily available, and impose no substantial provider cost or system burden.

A qualifying notice must substantially identify the copyrighted work or representative site list, the material or location reference and sufficient location information, provide contact information, bear an authorized signature, state a good-faith belief that the use lacks authorization from owner, agent or law, and state that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that the complainant is authorized to act for the owner of an allegedly infringed exclusive right. Under §512(c)(3)(B), incorporated by (d)(3), a notice that fails substantial compliance may not be considered in determining statutory actual knowledge or awareness of facts making infringement apparent. When a notice delivered to the designated agent substantially supplies the work, material or link location, and contact information but fails other elements, that exclusion applies only if the provider promptly attempts to contact the sender or takes other reasonable steps to assist receipt of a notice substantially satisfying all required elements. The preliminary notices support the underlying knowledge issue, but final satisfaction of each formal notice condition, service category and policy requirement remains for determination on remand.

Section 512(m) does not permit conditioning safe harbors on affirmative monitoring or searching for facts of infringement, apart from qualifying standard technical measures, or on access/removal forbidden by law. Failure to qualify is not affirmative evidence against an independent noninfringement defense under (l). Under (j), a provider protected from monetary relief still may receive only the permitted forms of injunction: for non-(a) conduct, specified-site access restraint, termination of specified infringing accounts, or other necessary relief addressing specified copyrighted material at a particular online location that is least burdensome among comparably effective measures; for (a) conduct, only termination of specified infringing accounts and reasonable steps blocking a specific identified foreign online location. The court must consider cumulative system burden, likely owner harm, technical feasibility and effectiveness, effects on access to lawful material elsewhere, and comparably effective less burdensome means. Notice and an opportunity to appear are required except evidence-preservation or other orders with no material adverse network effect. These limitations must govern if their predicates are satisfied; an unresolved classification is not permission to evade them through a generalized policing command.

### VI. The remaining defenses and preliminary objections do not defeat fitted relief

**Holding and operative rule:** On this preliminary record, the owners' exploration of MP3 businesses does not establish intentional abandonment or permission for this service, and enforcing reproduction and distribution rights does not establish misuse extending copyright to unrelated property. No demonstrated abuse of discretion requires more discovery or an evidentiary hearing, the $5 million security is supported against the competing evidence, and no statutory basis or special circumstance requires a compulsory-royalty substitute.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The defense elements and record-specific review standard govern separately. Express objection to the service defeats the asserted inference of permission; toleration or business exploration alone is neither abandonment nor a license. Security need not equal the claimed enterprise value merely because that value is asserted. Copyright's remedies cannot be replaced with a new license solely for technological novelty. First Amendment interests protect lawful files and independent expression through the remedy's scope, without immunizing the identified infringement. Later materially supported defenses and security evidence remain matters for their own proceedings.

### VII. The replacement injunction must connect usable notice to feasible service action

**Holding and operative rule:** Owners must identify protected works and sufficiently locate infringing files for practical response, and Napster must act within its actual index and account capabilities, including reasonable action against identified repeat infringers. The decree must preserve lawful files and authorized exchange, satisfy the remaining equitable predicates and any applicable §512 limits, and cannot make Napster guarantee prevention of every unidentified infringement.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The original order shifted the full identification/prevention burden onto the service and exceeded the supported controls. Specific knowledge and practical contribution justify effective relief at their proved scope; they do not justify impossible filtering or a blanket technology prohibition. Remand is for a conforming preliminary decree under these rules. The original July 26, 2000 order remains stayed until conformity; neither a later replacement order nor final liability is assumed.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.:** The integrated four-factor inquiry governs; commerciality alone creates no dispositive presumption, and concrete substitution is distinct from mere loss of a fee for unlicensed use. The preliminary outcome does not require resolving the general burden allocation.
- **Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, Inc., 464 U.S. 417:** Substantial noninfringing capability prevents imputed contributory liability from capability alone. Its private time-shifting application does not immunize public redistribution or specific ongoing assistance after knowledge.
- **New York Times Co. v. Tasini:** Its separation of actual statutory rights, use permissions and individualized remedy remains; it supplies neither a general digital-use exemption nor a compulsory license.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc.:** Its demand for a remedy fitted to the proved conduct informs preservation of independent lawful expression; infringement does not justify restraints on unrelated lawful files.

## Law After Decision

Specific knowing material assistance and separately established financial benefit with practical supervision can support preliminary secondary liability for an ongoing service despite substantial lawful capability. Fair use remains contextual; open whole-recording redistribution on this record receives no categorical sampling, relocation or device exemption. Congress's §512 categories and relief limits remain independently enforceable. Effective injunctive relief must connect usable identification to feasible service action while preserving lawful exchange.

## Separate Writings

No separate opinion is filed.

## Procedure After Action

The lower court must frame a replacement preliminary injunction under the stated notice, actual-capability, lawful-use, equity and §512 requirements. The original restraint remains stayed until conformity. Further proceedings retain the ordinary distinction between preliminary findings and final liability; this decision does not presume a replacement order already issued.

# Michigan v. Environmental Protection Agency — 2002-04-23

[Authoritative Record](../records/michigan_v_environmental_protection_agency_merits_2002-04-23.md)

## Event

Michigan v. Environmental Protection Agency. Merits decision, April 23, 2002, on full merits submission. Review of the D.C. Circuit judgment, 213 F.3d 663, entered March 3, 2000, concerning EPA's October 27, 1998 NOx SIP Call. The three questions are whether highly cost-effective controls may inform required reductions after interstate contribution is established, whether statewide budgets unlawfully displace state means choices, and whether Congress sufficiently guides that authorized implementation. EPA first identified contributing States by the magnitude, frequency and relative modeled effects, then used controls estimated at no more than $2,000 per ton to derive reductions. This review concerns the one-hour ozone standard; the agency's eight-hour findings and the corresponding review were stayed. The circuit separately ordered geographic and notice corrections that remain outside these categorical questions.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The D.C. Circuit judgment rejecting the three categorical challenges is affirmed, with its separate geographic and notice dispositions preserved.

## Holdings

### I. This implementation provision permits bounded consideration of cost

**Holding and operative rule:** After EPA establishes the relevant connection between upwind emissions and another State's nonattainment or interference with maintenance, §110(a)(2)(D)(i)(I) permits the availability of highly cost-effective controls to inform the emissions reduction required under that interstate implementation duty. Cost is permitted, not required; it cannot itself establish the interstate nexus, authorize unrelated reductions or replace the enacted air-quality objective.

**Authority:** Part I of Chief Justice Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly adopt this statutory rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The evaluative term significant appears in an implementation assignment with no single enacted numerical cutoff. Congress assigned EPA binding plan-adequacy review, correction of interstate deficiencies and rulemaking under §301, while §110(a)(2)(A) recognizes diverse controls and economic techniques. Mead's actual-delegation threshold is therefore met, and Chevron permits a reasonable implementation choice within the remaining ambiguity; official endorsement or formal procedure alone would not suffice. EPA first identified interstate contribution through magnitude, frequency and relative modeled effects, then assessed highly cost-effective controls. The physical-contribution objection does not defeat that bounded implementation judgment because a demonstrated pollution nexus remains indispensable. Whitman excludes cost from §109 national-standard setting but preserves provision-specific implementation questions; it neither forbids all §110 cost considerations nor independently validates this benchmark. Union Electric's state-plan approval setting is distinct. MCI still forbids replacing a statutory command with administrative preference. The independent explanation of the exact $2,000 cutoff, particular modeling and surviving technical disputes lie outside these questions.

### II. A lawful budget preserves state selection when real means remain

**Holding and operative rule:** A federally authorized statewide emissions budget does not unlawfully displace the States' control choices when actual options among sources and measures remain and the Act supplies lawful federal implementation instead of an unavoidable command to enact a prescribed state program. States need not adopt EPA's illustrative source mix or optional model trading program; the mandatory approval rule protects any submission meeting all applicable requirements.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II of the Chief Justice's opinion at the lawful-budget level. Scalia and Thomas do not thereby approve cost-derived amounts they regard as unauthorized.

**Controlling explanation:** Train supplies initial state source-control choice, which can exceed the constitutional floor. The supported options include stationary or mobile measures, EPA's optional trading program, a state program or no trading. EPA and OTAG found VOC controls inadequate for this long-distance NOx transport problem; that is an attributed finding supporting this pollutant-specific result, not a statutory prohibition on all VOC measures. States can add them for associated local disbenefits. New York and Hodel distinguish federal regulation with a genuine alternative from compelled state lawmaking. Section 110(c)(1) requires a federal plan within two years of a missing/incomplete required submission finding or full/partial disapproval unless the State cures and obtains approval before promulgation. Those triggers are not newly found here. Under §110(k)(3), EPA must approve a compliant whole submission; it may approve a compliant portion and disapprove another, but the revision is not fully compliant until entirely approved. The constitutional alternative cannot erase the statute's greater means-choice protection.

### III. Congress supplied the guidance; agency self-restraint supplies none

**Holding and operative rule:** The interstate provision constitutionally guides the authorized implementation judgment through identified pollutants, a demonstrated interstate relationship, downwind national-standard nonattainment or maintenance, significant-contribution limits and defined plan-adequacy/correction functions. EPA's present geographic restraint or chosen cost ceiling cannot cure a delegation lacking adequate congressional guidance.

**Authority:** Part III is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. These seven directly support constitutional sufficiency and the no-agency-cure application. Scalia and Thomas do not reach this issue.

**Controlling explanation:** Whitman requires legislative criteria and independently rejects agency cure. The statute identifies the subject, purpose and limits; Congress need not select each numerical budget. Permitted cost analysis operates within that task after the nexus is shown, rather than allowing the agency to choose its own unrelated objective. Section 110(k)(5) requires the revision necessary to correct substantial inadequacy, public findings and state notice. EPA may establish a reasonable submission deadline no later than eighteen months after notice; appropriate original-plan requirements apply and dates may be adjusted, except an unelapsed Part D attainment date. Section 301 confers no authority to replace the statutory objective. An unexplained particular figure can fail ordinary review even though Congress's delegation is sufficient. The Court rejects the lower court's reliance on the confined exercise of power as an independent constitutional repair.

**Common statutory boundaries:** Section 110(a)(2)(D) requires adequate plan provisions consistent with Title I prohibiting emissions that significantly contribute to nonattainment in another State or interfere with maintenance by another State of the applicable national primary or secondary ambient air-quality standard. Significant contribution qualifies the nonattainment branch; the separate maintenance branch requires interference with maintenance. The adjacent significant-deterioration/visibility and §§115/126 compliance duties remain in their own settings and are not newly adjudicated here. Section 110(a)(2)(A) calls for enforceable limits and other means or techniques, including fees, marketable permits and emissions-right auctions, with necessary or appropriate schedules. Those methods do not independently authorize replacement of the statutory air-quality objective. Under §110(k)(5), correction follows substantial inadequacy to attain or maintain the relevant national standard, adequately mitigate the identified interstate transport or otherwise comply with the Act. To the extent EPA considers appropriate the finding subjects the State to requirements applicable when the deficient plan was developed and submitted; EPA may adjust applicable dates, but not an unelapsed Part D attainment date. The mandatory federal-plan, approval and notice rules stated above are not findings that any new trigger has occurred or new judicial deadlines.

**Review scope and remedy:** This judgment resolves the three categorical questions for the one-hour ozone standard. The eight-hour findings and their review remain stayed. The independent adequacy of the exact cost cutoff and particular unreviewed technical choices are outside the questions presented. The circuit's Wisconsin, Missouri and Georgia vacaturs/remands and its notice remands concerning the electric-generating-unit definition and the control level for large stationary internal-combustion engines remain intact. The sustained portions remain operative subject to those existing corrections; no additional agency remand is ordered on the three questions decided here.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Whitman v. American Trucking Associations, Inc.:** Section 109's exclusion of implementation cost remains; the distinct §110 implementation question is resolved on its own text. Congress's guidance requirement and the independent no-agency-cure rule govern the constitutional analysis.
- **United States v. Mead Corp. and Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.:** Applied to actual delegated force and a reasonable resolution of genuine implementation ambiguity; formal procedure or institutional endorsement alone supplies neither authority nor validity.
- **Barnhart v. Walton:** Its formal delegated-choice ground confirms permission for reasonable implementation within an actual assignment; it creates no presumption authorizing every cost factor or power from longstanding practice.
- **Train v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 421 U.S. 60:** Its initial state source-control choice is applied; statutory choice remains independently protective even where the constitutional federal alternative exists.
- **Union Electric Co. v. EPA, 427 U.S. 246:** Its rejection of economic infeasibility as a basis for refusing a qualifying state plan does not decide this distinct federal interstate-adequacy question.
- **Arkansas v. Oklahoma:** Authorized federal interstate implementation and supported technical judgments retain their force; they do not supply authority apart from Congress's statutory assignment.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.:** Its prohibition on replacing a mandatory statutory task with agency policy remains binding.
- **AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board and New York v. FERC:** Actual federal assignments, constraints on their exercise and preserved state functions remain distinct; neither decision supplies a general presumption of regulatory power.
- **New York v. United States; Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264; FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742:** Their distinction between lawful federal regulation with genuine state alternatives and compulsory state administration applies. The constitutional floor does not erase greater statutory means-choice protections.

## Law After Decision

Section 110 permits the bounded cost-informed implementation judgment after a demonstrated interstate pollution nexus. It neither mandates cost consideration nor generally imports cost into national-standard setting. States retain genuine selection among compliant source measures and programs, backed by the mandatory approval rule and a lawful federal-plan alternative. Congress supplied sufficient guidance for this assignment; administrative self-restraint cannot supply missing legislative limits. The one-hour review and the circuit's separate corrections define the judgment's reach.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** The enacted criterion concerns amounts of pollution contributing to another State's nonattainment or interfering with maintenance. Cheapness of removal measures expense, not that pollution relationship. Congress's express economic techniques permit ways of meeting an authorized result; they do not authorize EPA to redefine the result through cost. MCI and the limiting-criteria analysis in Iowa Utilities Board require those enacted words to constrain agency choice independently; Chevron supplies no power to replace them. Walton's acceptance of reasonable formal implementation does not help when the disputed choice falls outside the statutory criterion. The two would remand the affected cost determination for agency reconsideration under the pollution standard, without imposing a unique significance number or requiring elimination of every molecule of interstate emissions. They would not preserve the same amount merely by renaming its stage.

They join Part II because Train and the actual alternatives distinguish a lawful statewide result from compelled source selection. That join does not approve the challenged cost-derived amounts. Having resolved the rule on statutory grounds, they do not reach the constitutional sufficiency question. Their existing agreement that agency self-restraint cannot cure missing congressional guidance remains unaffected. The dissent preserves the separate geographic and notice corrections and chooses no judicial replacement budget.

The sustained portions of the budget framework remain operative, subject to the existing Wisconsin, Missouri and Georgia dispositions and the two notice remands. No additional agency remand follows from the three rejected categorical challenges. The separate eight-hour review remains stayed; ordinary mandate governs the reviewed proceeding.

# Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency, No. 00-1167 — 2002-04-23

[Authoritative Record](../records/tahoe_sierra_preservation_council_v_tahoe_regional_planning_agency_merits_2002-04-23.md)

## Event

Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency, No. 00-1167; 535 U.S. 302. Argued January 7, 2002; decided April 23, 2002. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 216 F.3d 764, which rejected the categorical taking determination for two development moratoria. The question is whether the restrictions are compensable solely because they prevented all economic use during their thirty-two-month operation. The district court rejected the owners' Penn Central claim, and they did not appeal that ruling and expressly disavowed that theory. No physical occupation occurred. Later plans and judicial restraints have separate causal and procedural histories outside this question.

## Participation

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment rejecting the preserved categorical claim is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would reverse and remand for the categorical inquiry and actual-period remedy.

Compensation is denied on the temporal-segmentation theory. The abandoned Penn Central ruling remains undisturbed; no remand revives it or determines compensation for separate later periods.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Affirms rejection of the preserved categorical claim | Continuing property, no temporal segmentation and all qualifications |
| Dissent | Thomas | Scalia | Would reverse and remand | Complete present-use deprivation during the actual challenged period; background-law and compensation inquiry |

## Holdings

**A regulatory moratorium is not a categorical taking merely through temporal segmentation.** For a temporary development restriction without physical appropriation, an owner cannot establish Lucas's total-deprivation category merely by defining the relevant property as the exact time during which the restriction bars development. The continuing property interest must be assessed as such; economic impact, expectations, duration and governmental character remain subject to Penn Central when that claim is properly presented, and a temporary label supplies no immunity.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven directly support the rule, preservation limit and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Lucas addresses complete deprivation of the legally relevant property without resolving every temporal denominator. Penn Central, Keystone and Andrus caution against manufacturing totality from a selected burdened strand. First English supplies compensation after a temporary taking is independently established, not the conclusion that every temporary prohibition is a taking. Physical occupation and appropriation of a leasehold are different governmental acts. The claimants' strongest objection—that present use has real value and serial temporary labels could evade compensation—is answered by preserving contextual review and refusing to make the label conclusive. No fixed thirty-two-month safe harbor, environmental exception or numerical duration limit follows. Palazzolo preserves the difference between meaningful remaining use and the distinct contextual inquiry. Here the owners abandoned the rejected Penn Central claim; categorical rejection therefore ends this reviewed claim without its revival.

The relevant restraints are Ordinance 81-5 beginning August 24, 1981, with case-by-case approval available for some single-family homes on Nevada-side Class 1–3 lots, but unavailable on stream-environment-zone lands or California-side parcels, and Resolution 83-21 from August 27, 1983 through April 25, 1984, initially ninety days and then extended. The categorical claim concerns their application to the relevant owners; the ordinance did not impose an identical prohibition on every parcel. Later plan and judicial-restraint periods have separate causal/procedural predicates outside review. No compensation follows on the preserved temporal-segmentation theory.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council:** Its complete-deprivation category and genuine background-law exception remain. The Court rejects using the exact moratorium period alone as the relevant property for this regulatory claim; Lucas did not settle every temporal denominator.
- **Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104:** Its combined economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and governmental-character inquiry remains available for properly preserved claims, but this abandoned theory is not revived.
- **First English Evangelical Lutheran Church of Glendale v. County of Los Angeles, 482 U.S. 304:** Compensation is required for a temporary taking once independently established; it does not make every temporary development bar a taking.
- **Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470, and Andrus v. Allard, 444 U.S. 51:** Applied to the prohibition on manufacturing complete deprivation from a selected burdened strand of a continuing interest.
- **Palazzolo v. Rhode Island:** Its distinction between meaningful remaining ownership and the separate contextual inquiry remains. Neither regulatory notice nor a valid environmental purpose independently defeats compensation, and its unresolved general parcel question is not converted into a contrary temporal rule.

## Law After Decision

A temporary regulatory development restraint does not become a Lucas taking solely by treating its precise duration as a separate property interest. Contextual review remains for properly presented claims, including examination of duration, actual impact, expectations and governmental character. Temporary status is no immunity, while compensation for an independently established temporary taking remains required. This preserved categorical claim is concluded.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** A complete suspension of economically beneficial use for a substantial defined period can take that present use even though ownership and future use remain afterward. Lucas protects the complete-deprivation category, and First English and Lucas's actual-period remedy distinguish whether a taking occurred from how long compensation runs. Later restoration limits compensation; it does not eliminate the earlier complete loss. The majority's whole-interest position recognizes future value, but in the dissent's view that value does not supply usable property during the defined prohibition.

The two would reverse and remand for a sustained complete-deprivation determination, genuine background property or nuisance limitations and compensation for an actual taking within the challenged thirty-two months. They impose neither a numerical minimum duration nor a rule covering every ordinary permit-processing interval. The case-by-case Nevada Class 1–3 single-family approval possibility, unavailable to stream-environment-zone lands and California parcels, requires attention to the owners actually covered. The State must substantiate an antecedent background rule independently prohibiting the use under ordinary proof requirements; a new legislative harm declaration is insufficient. The abandoned Penn Central theory is not restored, and later plans or judicial restraints cannot be attributed to the two enactments through an unproved six-year causal extension. Their remedy concerns the actual established taking period, not presumed permanent value.

## Procedure After Action

The categorical rejection stands. The owners' unappealed contextual ruling is not reopened, and the judgment does not adjudicate separately caused later restraints. This merits review ends subject to the ordinary mandate.

# Universal City Studios, Inc. v. Corley — 2002-04-23

[Authoritative Record](../records/universal_city_studios_v_corley_merits_2002-04-23.md)

## Event

Universal City Studios, Inc. v. Corley. Merits decision, April 23, 2002, on full merits submission. Review of the Second Circuit judgment, 273 F.3d 429, entered November 28, 2001, affirming the August 23, 2000 permanent injunction after a nonjury trial. The questions are whether §1201(a)(2), applied to posting DeCSS and the proved purposeful linking, violates the First Amendment or Copyright Clause, and whether §1201(c)(1) supplies the asserted fair-use trafficking defense. DeCSS defeats effective DVD access protection and yields usable decrypted material; it is available as executable software and source expression. The actual linking restraint rests on separate knowledge, purpose and clear-and-convincing findings. No identified personal movie fair use or public-domain restraint is established.

## Holdings

### I. The separate trafficking prohibition has its own elements and exceptions

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1201(a)(2) prohibits manufacture, importation, offering to the public, provision or other trafficking in a technology, product, service, device, component or part that is primarily designed or produced to circumvent effective access control over a work protected by the Copyright Act, has only limited commercially significant other purpose or use, or is marketed for that circumvention by the responsible person or a concerted actor with that person's knowledge; these are alternative predicates. Section 1201(c)(1) preserves infringement rights and defenses, including fair use, but does not supply a general defense to that independent trafficking prohibition.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine adopt the statutory rule, the application and the precise statutory limits below.

**Controlling explanation:** Effective control requires authorized information, process or treatment in ordinary operation; circumvention includes decryption or bypass without the owner's authority. The identified DeCSS posting meets the designed-circumvention ground, which suffices for this application. Separate infringement and trafficking provisions cannot be collapsed, and (c)(2) leaves contributory/vicarious infringement law separate. The class exemption under (a)(1) does not defend another provision under (a)(1)(E). The nonprofit acquisition permission likewise supplies no (a)(2) or (b) defense. The distinct exceptions retain their own conditions, stated below; this record establishes no entitlement to one.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Under (a)(1), the Librarian's periodic rulemaking may identify a class whose users are or are likely during the succeeding three years to be adversely affected in their noninfringing uses by the individual access prohibition. The published class exemption protects those users as to that class for the ensuing three years; (a)(1)(E) prohibits using that exemption or rulemaking determination as a defense to other provisions, including trafficking. The nonprofit library, archives or educational-institution exemption permits access to a commercially exploited copyrighted work solely to determine in good faith whether to acquire a copy solely for conduct permitted by the Copyright Act, and only when an identical copy is not reasonably available in another form; retention must last no longer than necessary and the accessed copy cannot be used for another purpose. For a library or archives, its collections must be open to the public or available both to affiliated researchers and other persons researching a specialized field. It expressly supplies no (a)(2) or (b) trafficking defense. Reverse engineering under (f) concerns a lawfully obtained right to use a program and elements not previously readily available to the person and necessary for interoperability of an independently created program with other programs; identification and analysis must be for that sole purpose and noninfringing. Necessary tools may be developed and employed for that analysis or interoperability, and information or means may be shared solely to enable that interoperability, without infringement or other illegality. Encryption research under (g) identifies and analyzes flaws or vulnerabilities in encryption applied to copyrighted works to advance knowledge or assist development of encryption products. Qualifying good-faith circumvention requires lawful acquisition of the encrypted copy, phonorecord, performance or display of a published work, necessity for that research, a good-faith effort to obtain authorization before circumvention, and no infringement or other applicable-law violation. Tools may be developed and employed solely for the researcher's own qualifying research and provided to a collaborator for qualifying research or verification. Qualification factors include whether dissemination reasonably advances knowledge or technology development rather than facilitating infringement or other illegality; legitimate study, employment, training or experience in encryption; and whether, with what documentation, and when the researcher informs the copyright owner of the findings. Security testing under (j) requires the owner or operator's authorization and the sole purpose of good-faith testing, investigation or correction of a system flaw; the act must not infringe or violate other applicable law. Qualification factors include whether information is used solely to promote the owner's or operator's security or shared directly with the developer, and whether its use or maintenance avoids facilitating infringement or other illegality, including privacy or security violations. Those are statutory factors rather than categorical prerequisites. Developing, producing, distributing or employing tools is permitted solely for qualifying testing, provided the means do not otherwise violate (a)(2). The findings do not establish that these defendants meet one of those distinct permissions. Their existence is not a factual finding that every legitimate scientific use is accommodated, nor a license to decide unpresented constitutional applications.

### II. The operative circumvention application is content neutral and adequately fitted

**Holding and operative rule:** Executable code can communicate ideas and perform an operation; its expressive character receives First Amendment protection without immunizing the demonstrated unauthorized access-circumvention function. Applied to the working DeCSS tool, §1201(a)(2) advances a substantial interest independent of suppressing expression without a substantially excessive speech burden, and the posting injunction burdens no more speech than necessary to remedy the demonstrated violation.

**Authority:** All nine join this part of Thomas's Opinion of the Court and its exact stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Turner and O'Brien supply the statutory intermediate review; Madsen supplies the distinct injunction fit. The operation defeating effective access protection, rather than an encryption viewpoint or the films' messages, triggers this application. The tool's expressive value remains relevant. Restricting only completed infringement would not protect the distinct access interest Congress enacted. This reasoning does not depend on the proposition that all software is conduct, all scientific discussion is unprotected or a benign purpose conclusively establishes neutrality. The working-tool restraint, rather than abstract reporting, supplies the actual fit.

### III. The proved knowing and purposeful linking satisfies this injunction's limits

**Holding and operative rule:** The linking restraint is sustained where clear and convincing findings establish that the responsible person knows the prohibited material is on the linked site, knows it is unlawfully offered circumvention technology, and creates or maintains the link for the purpose of disseminating it. Those findings connect this restraint to the demonstrated tool-delivery violation and exclude uninformed or merely referential links and mere reporting without the proved dissemination purpose; a reporting label does not exempt a link that satisfies all three findings.

**Authority:** All nine join this part of Thomas's Opinion of the Court and its exact stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** A link communicates reference but can also deliver operational access. Barring posting alone while permitting knowing direct delivery for the same purpose would leave the established violation intact. Collateral protected material on a linked page is a real expressive cost, answered here by the actual purpose, knowledge and tailored scope rather than denied. The three safeguards and proof standard are sufficient for this decree; the Court does not prescribe universal constitutional minima or new statutory elements for all linking cases. The Government's reserved objection to the proof quantum requires no enlargement of this order.

### IV. The presented broader fair-use and limited-times challenges do not defeat this application

**Holding and operative rule:** The defendants identify no personal movie fair use and provide scant evidence of interference with prospective fair users; the record likewise contains no allegation or evidence of a plaintiff withholding a public-domain work or the injunction blocking its copying. These record limits defeat the requested broader constitutional relief here without holding fair use constitutionally irrelevant or approving perpetual control of public-domain works.

**Authority:** All nine join Part IV and these concrete-record grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** A lawful use does not establish an entitlement to every preferred digital copying method on this undeveloped record. A concrete indispensable-lawful-use or public-domain application requires its own preserved facts. The actual injunction neither extends a copyright term nor resolves those distinct applications.

### V. The limited-times contention independently fails preservation

**Holding and operative rule:** Presentation only in a footnote below did not adequately present the limited-times challenge for this merits review; this independent ground also supports declining the abstract claim.

**Authority:** Part V is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight adopt this independent preservation ground. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment and Part IV's concrete-record ground, but not Part V.

**Controlling explanation:** The limited-times contention was not adequately presented through its footnote reference below. That procedural limit independently warrants declining the challenge. It supplies no merits rule permitting technological extension of a copyright term.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC:** Its independent content classification and intermediate scrutiny govern the statutory burden; a benign purpose or technology label does not establish fit.
- **United States v. O'Brien, 391 U.S. 367, and Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781:** Functional regulation may burden expression if its independent substantial interest and adequate fit are established; intermediate scrutiny does not demand a universal least-restrictive-means test.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc.:** Its distinct requirement that a neutral injunction burden no more speech than necessary applies to the demonstrated tool-delivery violation. The actual predicate and confined relief do not erase earlier concerns about restraints on lawful advocacy.
- **Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union and United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc.:** Protection of expression in new media remains; the present function-based application is distinguished from content-selected suppression of protected communicative material.
- **Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition:** Its distinction between protected expression and independently defined unlawful conduct is preserved. It does not immunize the demonstrated access-circumvention operation or supply a new defense absent from §1201.
- **Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.:** Fair use retains its integrated statutory role in infringement litigation; it is not transposed wholesale into a separately enacted trafficking defense.
- **New York Times Co. v. Tasini:** Actual statutory rights and use permissions remain distinct; the value of a digital application alone does not create an unprovided permission.
- **Harper & Row, Publishers, Inc. v. Nation Enterprises, 471 U.S. 539:** The relationship between copyright protection and expression does not eliminate scrutiny of a distinct burden; the undeveloped fair-use record here supports no universal constitutional answer.

## Law After Decision

Section 1201(a)(2) supplies a distinct trafficking prohibition with separate predicates and limited exceptions; fair-use defenses to infringement do not automatically defeat it. Code remains expression, while the demonstrated content-neutral circumvention function and the actual fitted injunction satisfy the governing scrutiny here. The defined linking safeguards suffice for this decree without becoming universal minimum elements. The concrete-record and preservation grounds leave materially different fair-use and public-domain applications to their own preserved facts.
