## Standards and Tests selection: Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

### Forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)

**Governing question:** What instructions govern forfeited error and discretionary correction under Rule 52(b)?

**Current rule:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of forfeited error only if there is error, it is plain, and it affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, unlike failure timely to assert it. Plain means clear or obvious under current law. The defendant ordinarily must show outcome-affecting prejudice. Even then correction is discretionary and should occur only if the error seriously affects fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings; actual innocence is unnecessary, but substantial-rights impact alone is insufficient. Presence of alternates instructed not to participate supplies neither prejudice nor a presumption without a showing of participation or chilling effect. Initially unsettled trial law, special outcome-independent categories, justified prejudice presumptions and hearing authority remain unresolved. Johnson assumes substantial-rights injury without deciding whether withholding materiality is structural or harmless; its fourth-condition application supplies no general guilt-based exception or innocence prerequisite. When settled law permitted the trial action but intervening controlling law makes it erroneous during direct review, plainness is measured at appellate consideration. A forfeited omission of a genuine jury element proceeds under the distinct Rule 52(b) conditions rather than automatic reversal.

- Correction remains discretionary: United States v. Olano — Correction remains discretionary, 1993-04-26. O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

## Standards and Tests selection: Jury determination of criminal materiality

### Jury determination of criminal materiality

**Governing question:** Allocation of materiality when it is an element of the charged offense.

**Current rule:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to decide materiality beyond a reasonable doubt when materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense. The judge states the statutory meaning and legal standard; describing its application as a mixed question cannot remove an element from the jury. Preserved materiality withdrawals and omissions in the financial-deception and tax instructions addressed in Neder are trial errors subject to the Government’s Chapman burden to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Necessary equivalent findings are sufficient but not exclusive: supported whole-record inferences may establish noncontribution only after examination of actual instructions, evidence they directed to the jury, required findings and material record conflicts. Strong, uncontested or overwhelming evidence alone is no automatic substitute where the omission prevents a reliable connection to the actual verdict. Review is count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts.

**Authority by component:**

- The jury decides application of the materiality element: The jury decides application of the materiality element, 1995-06-19. Scalia’s Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine Justices adopt the element-allocation rule.

- Neder v. United States — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements”, 1999-06-10; Thomas Part II, all nine Justices. Supplies application of jury allocation and preserved-error burden.
- Neder v. United States — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “III. Qualified review of the actual verdict”, 1999-06-10; Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Seven direct joins supply classification and qualified inference; Part V supplies the bounded count-specific remand requirement.

**Present operation effective:** Neder, June 10, 1999, for the identified materiality errors and qualified actual-verdict harmlessness; Gaudin’s jury-allocation rule remains effective June 19, 1995.

**Open question:** No universal omitted-element or constructive-amendment classification, no generic tax harmlessness shortcut and no no-contest waiver follows. Sullivan’s vitiated reasonable-doubt rule remains structural; Richardson’s distinct predicate-nonunanimity harmlessness reservation remains unresolved.

## Standards and Tests selection: Firearm-type elements under former section 924(c)(1)

### Firearm-type elements under former section 924(c)(1)

**Governing question:** The treatment of weapon classifications under offense-date, pre-1998 section 924(c)(1).

**Current rule:** The machinegun, destructive-device, designated short-barreled-weapon, silencer and muffler classifications are aggravated-offense elements under the former provision. Each applicable aggravator must be alleged in the federal indictment and, unless validly admitted as an element or subject to a valid jury waiver, proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt. A judge’s weapon finding alone does not authorize the aggravated grade.

**Authority by component:**

- Castillo v. United States; No. 99-658. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, The former statute makes firearm type an aggravated-offense element, 2000-06-05; Scalia with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-05; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** The materially restructured 1998 text remains unconstructed. Particular preservation and error consequences require their own analysis; the statutory ruling creates no universal element-omission remedy.

**Related current rules:** Present-offense facts authorizing a higher sentencing maximum supplies Apprendi’s later, limited constitutional rule; the former-statute construction remains independent.

## Standards and Tests selection: Present-offense facts authorizing a higher sentencing maximum

### Present-offense facts authorizing a higher sentencing maximum

**Governing question:** Which additional present-offense facts must receive constitutional element protections.

**Current rule:** Under the Sixth Amendment jury guarantee as applied to the States and the Fourteenth Amendment’s Due Process Clause, a fact about commission or consequences of the present offense is an element when it is legally necessary to authorize punishment above the maximum supported by the verdict or plea. The fact must be validly admitted as an element or proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt. A sentencing label, strong evidence, notice of possible exposure or unused aggregate punishment on other counts does not supply the missing verdict or admission. Judicial findings may continue to select punishment within existing conviction-based authority.

**Authority by component:**

- Apprendi v. New Jersey; No. 99-478. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, Present-offense facts necessary for a higher maximum require element protections, 2000-06-26; Stone-Zsela with Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six).

**Present operation effective:** 2000-06-26; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Prior-conviction facts are undecided, and no constitutional recidivism exception is created. State charging form and Fifth Amendment grand-jury incorporation, unchanged-maximum minima, general consecutive sentencing, capital eligibility or selection, and materially different systems including general Guidelines classifications remain open. McMillan and Walton are not overruled. No universal structural-error or aggravator-omission harmless-error rule follows; waiver retains its independent conditions.

**Related current rules:** Aggravated unlawful-reentry elements and authorized maximum; Original armed carjacking and cumulative firearm punishment; Firearm-type elements under former section 924(c)(1). Those statute-specific federal charging rules remain independent of this new constitutional rule.

#### United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-1306.
**Decided:** April 26, 1993.

##### Forfeiture, waiver and the limits on plain-error authority

**Holding:** Rule 52(b) permits correction of a forfeited legal error only if it is plain and affects substantial rights; valid intentional relinquishment of a known right extinguishes error, while mere failure timely to assert a right does not. Plain means clear or obvious under current law, and the defendant ordinarily must show prejudice affecting the outcome; errors first clarified on appeal, special outcome-independent categories and justified prejudice presumptions are reserved.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Johnson v. Zerbst, 304 U.S. 458: applies intentional relinquishment; right-specific waiver requirements remain.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies limited plain-error authority and ordinary prejudice requirement.
- Arizona v. Fulminante, 499 U.S. 279: preserves structural/trial-error distinction without deciding every Rule 52(b) category.
- Brecht, April 21, 1993: distinguished; preserved Doyle habeas review does not control forfeited Rule 24(c) error.

**Later-authority backlinks:** Johnson v. United States, May 12, 1997, resolves the intervening-law plainness reservation where settled contrary trial law changes before direct review ends: clarity at appellate consideration suffices. Initially unsettled law, structural classification and ordinary prejudice remain reserved.

##### Correction remains discretionary

**Holding:** Even when plain forfeited error affects substantial rights, correction under Rule 52(b) is permitted, not automatic, and should occur only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Actual innocence is not a necessary condition, but satisfaction of substantial rights alone does not satisfy this additional inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Atkinson, 297 U.S. 157: applies the independent fairness, integrity and public-reputation standard.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applies discretionary, circumscribed correction.
- Sawyer, June 22, 1992: distinguished; capital actual-innocence gateway is not a universal limit on appellate correction.

##### Instructed alternates do not establish prejudice by presence alone

**Holding:** For these alternates, instructed not to participate, mere presence during deliberations neither establishes prejudice nor warrants a presumption of prejudice under Rule 52(b). Without a specific showing of participation or a chilling effect, respondents have not shown an effect on substantial rights; the per se new-trial ruling must be reversed.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority agreement at this level of generality; no Marks inference.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rule 24(c) waivability, errors first clarified on appeal, outcome-independent categories, appropriate prejudice presumptions, the preserved-objection burden, and Rule 606(b) hearing limits remain undecided. Because substantial rights were not shown, discretionary correction of a prejudicial intrusion is not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the alternate-presence new-trial ruling and remand for remaining appellate issues. Gray counts V–VII and Olano counts VI and VIII, reversed for insufficient evidence, remain untouched and are not authorized for retrial.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Parker v. Gladden, 385 U.S. 363, and Remmer v. United States, 347 U.S. 227: apply the focus on prejudicial influence, preserving appropriate presumptions in other circumstances.
- Smith v. Phillips, 455 U.S. 209: applies rejection of automatic retrial for every compromising contact.
- Richardson v. Marsh, 481 U.S. 200: applies the ordinary instruction-following presumption, not an irrebuttable cure.
- Zafiro, January 25, 1993: consistent record-specific instruction analysis; no blanket rule that instructions cure intrusions.

#### United States v. Gaudin

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-514.

##### The jury decides application of the materiality element

**Holding:** When materiality is an element of the charged criminal false-statement offense, the Fifth and Sixth Amendments require the jury to determine it beyond a reasonable doubt. ## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: Johnson v. United States

Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Johnson v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-203.
**Decided:** 1997-05-12.

##### The jury-element violation is reviewed as forfeited error

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the §1623 perjury offense and must be determined by the jury beyond a reasonable doubt; the judge's determination instead was constitutional error. Johnson's failure to object presents forfeiture under Rule 52(b), not an established intentional waiver extinguishing that error.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, unanimous in Parts I and IV.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gaudin supplies the jury obligation for actual materiality elements and Griffith applies it on pending direct review. Wells's different §1014 construction does not eliminate §1623 materiality; Olano separates forfeiture from intentional waiver.

##### Plainness is measured at appellate consideration in this intervening-law setting

**Holding:** When the law at trial settled an issue against the defendant but controlling law changes before direct review ends, the error is plain if it is clear at appellate consideration. This resolves that intervening-law setting without deciding every case in which the law was unsettled at trial or dispensing with Rule 52(b)'s other requirements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's Part II, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight direct joins. Scalia assumes plainness without joining.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano's reservation about error first clarified on appeal is resolved for settled contrary trial law changed before direct review ends, without resolving initially unsettled law.

##### The independent discretionary condition does not warrant correction here

**Holding:** Even assuming an effect on substantial rights, this forfeited materiality error does not warrant correction under Rule 52(b) because, in the established circumstances, it did not seriously affect the fairness, integrity or public reputation of the proceedings. That conclusion rests on the clear materiality of the source-of-funds account in the particular investigation and the intervening change from settled trial law; it is neither a general overwhelming-evidence exception nor a substitute jury verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, unanimous in Parts I and IV.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Substantial-rights impact is assumed; structural classification and ordinary third-step prejudice remain open. There is no innocence requirement or need for a second institutional wrong, no substitute element finding, and no universal overwhelming-evidence exception. Disputed materiality, less decisive proof or trial-strategy effects remain open.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the conviction-sustaining judgment, 9–0, without a new trial, acquittal or sentence calculation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Olano's fourth condition independently governs correction after forfeiture. Gaudin's classification reservation, Sullivan's vitiated-reasonable-doubt rule and Roy's distinct preserved personal-intent habeas inquiry remain intact.

#### Almendarez-Torres v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-6839.

**Decided:** 1998-03-24.

##### Section 1326(b)(2) requires an aggravated-offense allegation before its higher maximum may apply

**Holding:** The prior aggravated-felony conviction specified in §1326(b)(2) is an element of the aggravated unlawful-reentry offense. It must be alleged in the federal indictment and, unless validly admitted as an element of the charged offense, proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt; an admission to the fact of a prior conviction does not cure its omission from this indictment, so the existing conviction permits no sentence above §1326(a)'s two-year maximum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg. These five expressly adopt the statutory rule, its ordinary element consequences and the remedy. Four explain the construction through constitutional avoidance; Stone-Zsela gives a definitive constitutional ground separately. No majority adopts a general constitutional rule governing all facts that increase a statutory maximum.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The rule is statutory. No majority decides a general constitutional rule for maximum-increasing facts or a constitutional prior-conviction exception. The admitted convictions remain valid; neither subsidiary proof questions nor facts that leave the maximum unchanged are decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The enhanced sentence is vacated and the judgment reversed and remanded for sentencing within the two-year maximum on this indictment and plea. The plea is not withdrawn and no acquittal follows. The availability of a properly charged further proceeding is reserved.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hamling v. United States:** Applied to require an indictment alleging each element of the aggravated offense.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applied after statutory classification; its jury-and-proof rule for actual elements remains controlling without deciding all sentencing classifications.
- **McMillan v. Pennsylvania:** Distinguished because its mandatory minimum left the statutory maximum unchanged; its limited holding remains intact.
- **United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra:** Preserved for ordinary sentencing within punishment authorized by the conviction; it does not resolve this higher-maximum classification.
- **Custis v. United States:** Distinguished as a collateral-review decision; the existing limits on attacking prior convictions remain intact.
- **Old Chief v. United States:** Applied to the distinction between element status and the form of adequate prior-conviction proof; no broader compulsory-stipulation rule is created.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: Monge v. California

Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Monge v. California

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-6146.
**Decided:** 1998-06-26.

##### This noncapital recidivist-status determination does not receive offense-acquittal finality

**Holding:** The Double Jeopardy Clause does not bar renewed proof of the serious character of Monge's prior conviction in this noncapital sentencing proceeding after the first proof was insufficient. Here the required personal-conduct qualification determines recidivist status for punishment of the current offense, rather than adjudicating a new offense or a capital acquittal-like determination; neither the availability of trial safeguards nor the increase in the punishment ceiling alone makes this particular determination an offense acquittal.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer; five direct joins to this classification and its limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The classification is confined to this serious-prior qualification. Trial safeguards and a higher ceiling are acknowledged, not ignored. No comprehensive rule for maximum-increasing facts, mandatory minima, unchanged-range findings or prior-conviction procedures is adopted. Independent jury, indictment and due-process claims are not decided; the actual jury waiver is unchanged and relabeling a true offense cannot evade jeopardy.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** DiFrancesco's sentencing/acquittal distinction is applied; Bullington and Rumsey retain their capital protection; Caspari's merits reservation is answered for this case; Almendarez-Torres, Witte and Watts remain within their actual statutory and sentencing limits.

##### Permission to prove the allegation again neither repairs the first record nor directs an enhancement

**Holding:** The California judgment permitting another proof proceeding is affirmed because the disputed adjudication does not receive offense-acquittal protection, not because the State's first evidence was sufficient or its failure excusable. Any renewed proceeding must satisfy applicable law and safeguards; no true finding, automatic doubling, aggregate sentence, or invalidation of the independent prior-prison-term enhancement or drug convictions follows from this judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion; the same five Justices join the complete remedy and its limits.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Renewed proof is permission, not an obligation. Neither personal weapon use nor personal infliction of great bodily injury is found; the prosecutor's assertion is not proof. The seven-year unenhanced maximum is not a fixed aggregate sentence, and the selected term, separate prison-term enhancement and stayed/concurrent counts remain distinct. Independent defenses and sentencing constraints survive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Burks and Hinds retain their mandatory finality consequence once acquittal protection attaches; no exception to those holdings is adopted. Dixon's retained Grady rule remains unchanged and does not independently establish the attachment disputed here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The case returns through the California Supreme Court for further proceedings consistent with the affirmed rejection of the federal double-jeopardy bar. A renewed serious-prior proof proceeding is permitted only as otherwise lawful; it is not commanded. The unsupported first serious-prior finding and doubled term are not reinstated by this decision, and no substitute sentence or release order is entered.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: Jones v. United States

Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Jones v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-6203.
**Decided:** 1999-03-24.

##### Serious bodily injury and death define aggravated carjacking offenses

**Holding:** The version of 18 U.S.C. § 2119 governing the December 1992 offense creates a basic carjacking offense and aggravated offenses when serious bodily injury or death results. Serious bodily injury is an element of the grade carrying a twenty-five-year maximum, and death is an element of the grade carrying a maximum of life imprisonment; the basic offense carries a fifteen-year maximum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six Justices). Separate constitutional theories do not supply an additional Court holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Almendarez-Torres is applied as a statutory element-and-charging analogue, not as a general constitutional maximum-fact holding. Gaudin supplies the significance of identifying an actual element. Monge's bounded double-jeopardy classification is preserved, including its reservation of independent charging and jury questions. Holloway's attention to enacted offense conditions is consistent with this reading, but its construction of later statutory language is not imported into the earlier offense.

##### The existing indictment and verdict authorize only the basic carjacking range

**Holding:** The aggravated injury or death element must be alleged in the federal indictment; absent a valid admission as an element of the charged offense or a valid waiver of jury determination, the Government must submit it to the jury and prove it beyond a reasonable doubt. An ordinary factual admission does not itself cure an indictment that omits the aggravated element. Because injury was neither charged nor established by Jones's verdict, the judge's later injury finding cannot authorize the aggravated range; the aggravated sentence must be vacated, and sentencing on the existing carjacking indictment and verdict may not exceed the fifteen-year basic maximum.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg (six Justices). Separate constitutional theories do not supply an additional Court holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hamling and Gaudin are applied to the aggravated statutory elements. Almendarez-Torres supplies the distinction between admitting a fact and properly charging the aggravated offense. Witte and Watts retain their ordinary within-authority sentencing operation and provide no basis for this increased statutory range. Monge supplies no independent remedy here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court's ground is statutory construction and the protections of the resulting elements. It does not decide a general constitutional rule for all maximum-increasing facts, bare prior-conviction status, mandatory minima within an unchanged maximum, every Guidelines classification or capital sentencing. McMillan's unchanged-maximum holding and Walton's capital rule are not overruled. The death grade is part of the statute's construction, not a finding that Jones caused death.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The sufficient ground is construction of the original carjacking statute and ordinary protections of its aggravated elements. No general constitutional maximum-fact rule, prior-conviction exception, unchanged-maximum minimum rule, universal Guidelines classification or capital-sentencing rule is decided; McMillan and Walton are not overruled.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate the aggravated sentence and remand. Sentencing on the existing indictment and verdict may not exceed the fifteen-year basic maximum. No particular sentence, aggregate calculation, acquittal or authorization of a new aggravated prosecution is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

Almendarez-Torres supplies the statutory aggravated-element and charging analogue; its result remains distinct from a universal constitutional rule. Hamling requires a federal indictment stating the offense's elements, and Gaudin requires the jury's determination beyond a reasonable doubt. Monge retains its noncapital serious-prior double-jeopardy classification and its express reservation of independent charging and jury claims; no renewed-proof remedy is drawn from it here. Witte and Watts retain their within-authority sentencing permission and do not authorize punishment beyond the charged conviction's statutory range. Holloway concerns later statutory language and does not amend the law governing this offense. McMillan and Walton are not overruled, and Custis's limits on collateral attack remain undisturbed.

## Verbatim opening Holdings selection: Neder v. United States

Source: state/holdings/criminal-procedure.md

#### Neder v. United States

**Docket or dockets:** No. 97-1985.
**Decided:** 1999-06-10.

##### I. Materiality in the charged mail, wire, and bank fraud schemes

**Holding:** Materiality is an element of the charged money-or-property schemes under 18 U.S.C. §§1341 and 1343 and the charged bank-fraud schemes under §1344. In these financial-deception schemes, a representation is material when, in its transaction context, it has a natural tendency or capacity to influence the decision it targets; successful deception, actual reliance, actual loss, and status as the sole reason for the transaction are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls unanimously.

##### II. The jury must decide actual materiality elements

**Holding:** The jury must determine beyond a reasonable doubt whether the charged representation is material when materiality is an offense element. A judge may define that requirement but may not withdraw its application because he regards the evidence as conclusive; Neder preserved these objections, and the Government bears Chapman's burden of establishing harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Thomas's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, controls. That agreement on error, preservation, and burden does not establish agreement on every permissible harmlessness method.

##### III. Qualified review of the actual verdict

**Holding:** These materiality withdrawals and omissions are trial errors subject to Chapman review: the Government must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the error did not contribute to the actual verdict. Supported whole-record inferences may establish that conclusion beyond necessary equivalent findings, but only after examination of the actual instructions, the evidence they put before the jury, the findings they required, and material record conflicts; strong evidence alone fails when the omission prevents a reliable connection to that verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Thomas's opinion controls through seven direct joins: Thomas, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Scalia and Ginsburg do not join its classification and broader inference method; their actual-equivalent-findings approach remains separate.

##### IV. The tax verdicts satisfy that inquiry on this charge and record

**Holding:** The Government has established beyond a reasonable doubt that the materiality error did not contribute to the guilty verdicts on tax counts 86 and 87, because the actual charge and verdict establish the knowing, purposeful falsity of the specified annual-income totals and supply a reliable connection to those statements' role in administering the returns. The judgment sustaining those convictions is affirmed against the preserved instructional challenge; a generic false-return verdict or strong evidence alone does not establish the same result in another case.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of Thomas's opinion controls through six direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer. Stone-Zsela, Scalia, and Ginsburg dissent from this application and disposition. Stevens's additional view that the verdict necessarily embraced materiality is not a holding of the Court.

##### V. Initial harmlessness review of the affected fraud convictions

**Holding:** The judgment rejecting materiality under §§1341, 1343, and 1344 is reversed, its affirmance of the affected fraud convictions is vacated, and those convictions are remanded for the court of appeals' first proper harmlessness review. Review must be count-specific unless a demonstrated common finding lawfully covers the relevant counts; an affected conviction that cannot be sustained under Chapman must be set aside, with retrial permissible under correct instructions.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part V-A's bounded mandate has eight direct joins: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Part V-B's direction to apply the qualified actual-verdict method has six: Thomas, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, and Breyer, who also join Part III. Scalia and Ginsburg join V-A only and would limit review to genuine equivalent findings. Stone-Zsela joins the statutory reversal but dissents from the remand for another harmlessness inquiry; he would vacate every affected conviction for a new trial now.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Morissette v. United States:** Applies the borrowed-term principle to fraud's established materiality requirement; statutory silence alone does not discard that content.
- **Durland v. United States:** Preserves coverage of fraudulent future promises while distinguishing that breadth from elimination of materiality or importation of every completed-tort requirement.
- **Kungys v. United States:** Uses its natural-tendency or capacity formulation for the financial deceptions presented here; its civil allocation and burden do not govern the criminal jury question.
- **United States v. Wells:** Preserves the different §1014 construction without an added objective-materiality element; it neither removes materiality from fraud statutes nor automatically cures their instructional error through a purpose-to-influence finding.
- **United States v. Gaudin:** Applies jury determination of each actual materiality element beyond a reasonable doubt; harmlessness review does not authorize withdrawal of that element at trial.
- **Chapman v. California:** Applies the Government's beyond-reasonable-doubt burden to establish that the preserved error did not contribute to the actual verdict.
- **California v. Roy:** Extends its qualified actual-verdict method to the materiality errors presented here; necessary equivalence is sufficient but not exclusive, and its instructional-focus limitation remains essential.
- **Yates v. Evatt:** Applies attention to the actual charge, findings, and evidence considered under the instructions; proof sufficient for a different verdict cannot replace contribution analysis.
- **Sullivan v. Louisiana:** Distinguishes removal of a particular element from invalidation of the reasonable-doubt standard governing all findings; its structural-error rule remains intact.

# United States v. Vonn; No. 00-973; 535 U.S. 55 — 2002-03-04

[Authoritative Record](../records/united-states-v-vonn_merits_2002-03-04.md)

## Event

United States v. Vonn, No. 00-973; 535 U.S. 55. Argued November 6, 2001; decided March 4, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 3. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which vacated the pleas and sentence using harmless-error review confined to the plea proceedings. The questions are whether an unpreserved Rule 11 omission receives Rule 52(b) review and whether relevant information throughout the existing record may inform its effect on substantial rights. The United States seeks correction of the review rules and reinstatement or reconsideration; Vonn seeks to preserve the judgment. Before the pleas, a magistrate twice gave counsel-rights advisements and Vonn acknowledged understanding. The plea judge omitted the trial-counsel warning at both hearings; the prosecutor's attempted correction at the later hearing did not cure it. Defense counsel did not object. A later withdrawal motion rested on a different ground.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

## Public Action

The appellate judgment is vacated and the case remanded for plain-error review using the relevant whole record.

## Judgment & Remedy

| Opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part | Stevens | No other Justice | Agrees with vacatur and whole-record review; opposes the plain-error mandate | Joins Part II; would apply Rule 11(h) with the Government bearing the harmlessness burden |

## Holdings

### I. Forfeited Rule 11 error is governed by Rule 52(b)

**Holding and operative rule:** A defendant who forfeits a Rule 11 objection must satisfy Rule 52(b): there must be error that is plain and affects substantial rights, ordinarily with the defendant bearing the effect burden, and correction remains discretionary only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Rule 11(h) does not exempt this plea-taking omission from ordinary forfeiture review; intentional waiver remains distinct.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion has the direct support of Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight votes control; Stevens dissents from this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the plea rule and the general review rule operate together. Rule 11(h) requires disregarding variances that do not affect substantial rights, but does not say that a failure to object has no consequence. Olano supplies the separate requirements and usual defendant burden. Johnson applies that framework to an important constitutional omission without making its importance an automatic exception.

The contrary argument correctly identifies the judge's affirmative personal duty and a defendant's possible inability to recognize an omitted warning. Those concerns establish why the colloquy is mandatory; they do not delete Rule 52(b) for the unpreserved claim. McCarthy enforced an earlier Rule 11 regime, and Rule 11(h) displaced automatic reversal without expressly abolishing plain-error review. McCarthy did not decide the present choice of review standards. The trial-counsel omission therefore remains error despite Vonn's representation and the prosecutor's intervention. His different withdrawal argument did not preserve it. The ordinary effect inquiry and independent discretion requirement apply without requiring actual innocence or treating substantial-rights impact as sufficient by itself.

### II. Relevant portions of the existing record inform the error's effect

**Holding and operative rule:** In assessing the effect of the Rule 11 omission on substantial rights, a reviewing court may consider relevant information throughout the existing record, including earlier judicial rights advisements. Such information bears on actual understanding and effect; it neither cures the judge's omitted personal warning nor conclusively establishes understanding from representation or a signed form alone.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II of O'Connor's opinion, giving direct unanimous authority independent of the division over burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that actual earlier court advisements cannot be excluded merely because they occurred outside the plea colloquy. United States v. Young permits review of the whole record in assessing error, and Olano separates a procedural breach from its effect. McCarthy's personal-colloquy requirement remains mandatory; admitting relevant evidence about consequences does not excuse noncompliance.

The magistrate twice advised Vonn of counsel rights and obtained acknowledgments of understanding. Those events may inform what he understood when pleading, but their timing, content and demonstrated comprehension must be evaluated. The Court supplies no off-record explanation and treats neither counsel's presence nor a form as an irrebuttable cure. A transcript boundary that excludes actual recorded advice would obstruct the inquiry into effect without improving enforcement of the duty itself.

### III. The proper framework must first be applied below

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's use of the wrong burden and its exclusion of relevant existing-record material require vacatur and remand. That court must apply all four Rule 52(b) conditions, including record-specific effect and the independent basis for discretionary correction, before deciding the appropriate appellate relief.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part I. Stevens agrees with vacatur and remand for whole-record consideration but would apply the Government's harmlessness burden.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court of Appeals did not assess this record under the governing framework. Earlier warnings are relevant, not conclusive, so their presence alone cannot support the Government's requested immediate reinstatement. Olano requires the effect inquiry and the distinct judgment about correction to retain separate work. The case returns for that application and any other properly remaining appellate issues. The perjury-warning contention remains undecided because it was not resolved below and falls outside the review questions decided here. The case concerns direct review of this forfeited federal-rule omission, not collateral relief or the consequences of every constitutional plea defect.


## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Olano: applied to Rule 11 forfeiture with its usual substantial-rights burden and independent discretionary condition; no automatic reversal, innocence requirement or comprehensive resolution of its reserved categories follows.
- Johnson v. United States: applied at its actual forfeiture and discretionary-correction scope; its particular intervening-law application becomes no general guilt-based exception.
- McCarthy v. United States: its mandatory personal-colloquy principle remains, while the later Rule 11(h) text precludes importing its automatic-reversal consequence into the present rules.
- United States v. Young, 470 U.S. 1: applied to relevant whole-record review of the error's effect, not as permission to omit the required warning.
- Boykin v. Alabama: its constitutional plea requirements remain distinct; this decision supplies no general constitutional-invalidity or collateral-review rule.


## Law After Decision

Direct review of the forfeited Rule 11 omission uses Rule 52(b) and the relevant existing record. Eight Justices establish the defendant-burden and independent-discretion requirements, and all nine reject a colloquy-only effect inquiry. The judge's personal advice duty remains mandatory. Record-specific effect and justified correction are ordinary applications assigned to the court of appeals under that law.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stevens joins Part II's use of relevant material throughout the official record and agrees that the appellate judgment should be vacated. He rejects Parts I and III's transfer of the effect burden to the defendant. Rule 11 assigns the judge affirmative duties during a procedure in which an uninformed defendant may not recognize the missing advice. Its specific harmless-error provision should therefore leave the Government responsible for showing that the omission was harmless. McCarthy supplies the distinctive judicial obligation; Stevens's Olano dissent distinguishes procedural protections from easily measured outcome injury, and his Johnson join did not decide whether Rule 11(h) controls this separate plea procedure. He would remand for whole-record harmless-error review under Rule 11(h). Earlier recorded advisements may establish actual knowledge under that inquiry, but representation is not itself compliance, and neither automatic invalidation nor invented off-record advice follows from his position.

## Procedure After Action

The appellate judgment is vacated. On remand the Ninth Circuit must consider the relevant existing record under each Rule 52(b) condition and determine the resulting appellate relief. Other properly remaining issues, including the unresolved perjury-warning contention, retain their actual procedural footing. The mandate itself neither reinstates the convictions conclusively nor grants withdrawal of the pleas.

## Event

**Case:** United States v. Cotton, No. 01-687, 535 U.S. 625.

**Argued:** April 15, 2002.
**Decided:** May 20, 2002.
**Route and posture:** Certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 261 F.3d 397, which vacated the enhanced sentences and directed sentences not exceeding twenty years. **Question:** Whether the omitted quantity allegation removes jurisdiction or requires correction of the enhanced sentences on this unpreserved indictment and jury-quantity claim.
**Record:** The original indictment alleged at least five kilograms of cocaine and fifty grams of cocaine base. The operative superseding indictment altered the conspiracy period, added defendants and alleged only a detectable amount. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Fourth Circuit's sentence-vacatur judgment and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit's sentence-vacatur judgment and remand, 9–0.
**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.
**Remedy:** The enhanced sentences remain undisturbed on this forfeited claim. The remand implements rejection of the jurisdictional and fourth-condition vacatur grounds; it does not permit a fresh discretionary choice on the same omissions and record. The conspiracy convictions remain intact.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer in full; Scalia except Part II-B | Reverses and remands | Parts I, II-A and III: all nine. Part II-B affirmative plainness: all except Scalia. |

## Holdings

### I. The quantity omission does not remove subject-matter jurisdiction

**Controlling proposition:** Omission of the aggravating drug quantity from an indictment charging a federal offense does not withdraw the district court's subject-matter jurisdiction under 18 U.S.C. §3231. Ex parte Bain is overruled only insofar as it treats this indictment defect as jurisdictional; the prohibition on substantive indictment amendment without grand-jury resubmission, except changes of form, remains.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by every other Justice, supplies nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress gives federal district courts authority to adjudicate federal criminal prosecutions. An error in stating the accusation differs from absence of power over that class of case. Bain's broad language arose when habeas review provided the channel for correcting federal criminal error; it does not establish the present jurisdictional consequence. Lamar v. United States and United States v. Williams distinguish the power to adjudicate from the sufficiency of the accusation. The conspiracy charged here is within §3231 even though the superseding indictment lacks the quantity needed for the enhanced maximum.

The grand jury's constitutional function remains. Neither the original indictment nor later judicial findings may replace the operative superseding indictment. Stirone v. United States and Russell v. United States concern preserved charging objections and do not make this forfeited omission jurisdictional. Complete absence of a charging instrument is outside the question presented.

### II. The constitutional error remains subject to distinct forfeited-error requirements

**Controlling proposition:** The enhanced sentences lack the required quantity allegation and jury determination, and the unpreserved objection is reviewed under Rule 52(b), not treated as an intentional waiver or a jurisdictional nullity. Error, plainness, an effect on substantial rights and a serious effect on the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings remain distinct requirements; substantial-rights effect is assumed here without deciding prejudice or structural classification.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II-A's error, forfeiture and separate-requirements analysis. Part II-B's affirmative intervening-law plainness proposition is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Scalia assumes plainness and does not join Part II-B.

**Controlling explanation:** Apprendi requires the fact authorizing a higher maximum to receive element protections. The superseding indictment alleged a detectable amount, the jury was instructed that quantity was immaterial, and no quantity verdict was returned. Judicial preponderance findings, notice of enhanced exposure and stipulations about physical exhibits do not supply either missing constitutional determination or an admission of the aggravated offense.

Olano distinguishes failure to object from intentional relinquishment and makes discretionary correction independent of substantial-rights injury. Johnson applies that distinction to a genuine jury-element omission. Under Johnson's intervening-law rule, the error is plain when controlling law changes settled contrary trial law before direct review ends and the error is clear at appellate consideration. That is the posture here; the Court does not decide initially unsettled trial law. Assuming the third condition permits resolution at the independent fourth condition without declaring the omission harmless, structural or cured. Preserved error retains its distinct inquiry; the Court does not authorize future omission of required allegations or jury findings.

### III. This record does not warrant fourth-condition correction

**Controlling proposition:** The forfeited quantity error does not seriously affect the fairness, integrity or public reputation of these proceedings in the established circumstances: proof that the conspiracy involved at least fifty grams of cocaine base is overwhelming and essentially uncontroverted at that statutory threshold. The enhanced sentences receive no relief on this claim, even assuming an effect on substantial rights; this is a particular appellate-remedy decision, not a substitute jury finding or a universal overwhelming-evidence exception.

**Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by every other Justice, supplies nine direct votes for the rule, application and reversal.

**Controlling explanation:** Johnson permits refusal to correct a forfeited element error on the particular evidentiary and procedural circumstances without erasing the right. Here the jury found the conspiracy, both sides report physical seizures totaling approximately 380 grams of cocaine base, and the record gave notice of enhanced exposure. The supported credibility and defendant-specific objections concern substantially larger Guidelines quantities. They do not make the fifty-gram conspiracy threshold genuinely contestable on this record. The original indictment bears on notice only; it is not substituted for the superseding indictment.

The defendants correctly emphasize that neither grand nor petit jury made the required quantity determination. But genuine constitutional error and substantial-rights injury do not alone satisfy Olano's fourth condition. The decisive threshold proof, the actual objections and the intervening change from settled trial law support leaving the sentences undisturbed on this forfeited claim. The Fourth Circuit erred in treating evidentiary strength as irrelevant merely because the omission concerned the grand jury.

The Court makes no personal quantity admission for any respondent and adopts no general rule of conspiracy-quantity attribution. It does not replace the verdict, determine guilt of an aggravated offense anew or establish an innocence prerequisite. Preserved claims, materially contested threshold evidence and different trial-strategy circumstances require their applicable analysis. The remand implements rejection of these vacatur grounds; it does not reopen discretionary correction of the same omissions on the same record.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Ex parte Bain, 121 U.S. 1 (1887):** Overruled only insofar as the present indictment defect removes jurisdiction; substantive amendment still requires grand-jury resubmission, with changes of form excepted.
- **Lamar v. United States; United States v. Williams:** Apply their distinction between authority over a federal prosecution and a defect in the accusation.
- **Stirone v. United States; Russell v. United States:** Preserve their charging protections for preserved objections; they do not require jurisdictional treatment or automatic correction here.
- **Apprendi v. New Jersey, 530 U.S. 466 (2000):** Applies the genuine element-protection requirement; strong proof and notice do not supply a missing allegation, verdict or valid element admission.
- **United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993):** Applies its separate forfeiture, plainness, substantial-rights and discretionary-correction requirements; the third condition does not decide the fourth.
- **Johnson v. United States, May 12, 1997:** Applies its intervening-law plainness rule and particular fourth-condition refusal of relief without converting either into a universal evidence exception.
- **United States v. Vonn, March 4, 2002:** Its distinct Rule 11 record inquiry and remand do not require a second appellate exercise of discretion in every forfeited-error case.
- **Neder v. United States:** Preserved-error review remains distinct; this decision supplies no replacement for its actual-verdict inquiry.

## Law After Decision

An omitted aggravating quantity is a real constitutional charging and jury error but does not withdraw §3231 jurisdiction over this federal prosecution. Forfeited claims remain subject to Rule 52(b)'s distinct requirements. This threshold-specific fourth-condition decision leaves the sentences undisturbed without authorizing future element omissions or converting judicial quantity findings into a verdict. Preserved error and materially different contested records retain their own governing analysis.

## Separate Writings

Scalia concurs in part and in the judgment. He joins Parts I, II-A and III, including the genuine constitutional error, forfeiture, assumed substantial-rights injury and denial of correction under the independent fourth condition. He assumes that the error is plain without joining Part II-B's affirmative intervening-law plainness rule. Because the fourth condition is unsatisfied on this record even on that assumption, the broader plainness determination is unnecessary to his vote. He does not treat appellate assessment of the evidence as a jury verdict, find an intentional waiver or adopt a categorical exception to Apprendi.

## Procedure After Action

The Fourth Circuit's judgment is reversed and the cases remanded for proceedings consistent with rejection of the stated sentence-vacatur grounds. The challenged sentences receive no relief on this claim, and the convictions remain intact.

The superseding indictment is operative. The physical-evidence account concerns the conspiracy threshold; larger Guidelines disputes are distinct. ## atkins-v-virginia-merits-2002-06-20

## Event

Atkins v. Virginia, No. 00-8452. Argued February 20, 2002; decided June 20, 2002. On certiorari to the Supreme Court of Virginia, which sustained a death sentence after a second penalty proceeding. The question is whether established intellectual disability categorically bars execution. The defense psychologist reported an IQ of 59 and diagnosed disability; the State expert disputed the diagnosis.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at submission and decision. No recusal or vacancy affects the case.

## Public Action

Death-sentence judgment reversed and remanded, 7–2; convictions remain intact.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Disposition:** Death-sentence judgment reversed and remanded, 7–2; convictions remain intact.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Thomas.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer | Categorical restriction, limited overruling and remand |
| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas | Rejects categorical restriction and would affirm against this claim |

## Holdings

### Execution is unavailable for established intellectual disability

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit execution of a person with established intellectual disability: significantly impaired intellectual functioning, substantial adaptive limitations and developmental onset. The State must provide a reliable procedure to determine that status; a score, diagnostic label or courtroom impression alone does not establish it.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer: seven Justices adopt the entire rule, explanation and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The most severe punishment requires culpability proportionate to its severity. Trop supplies the inquiry into developing standards; Coker and Enmund require attention to the punishment's relation to culpability and legitimate penal purposes. The sustained domestic legislative movement since Penry v. Lynaugh supplies objective support: eighteen of thirty-eight death-penalty States enacted restrictions, alongside twelve States without capital punishment. This evidence is directional, not eighteen identical statutes or a mechanical count. Eleven enactments have temporal limits; New York retains a correctional-custody murder exception; Kansas uses a distinct definition. A vetoed Texas bill is not an enactment, and reported low-IQ executions do not establish complete clinical diagnoses. Those qualifications do not erase the consistent movement or infrequency of executing the class. Clinical evidence explains diminished reasoning, judgment and ability to learn from experience, which weaken retributive and deterrent justifications. The same limitations impede assistance to counsel and reliable presentation and can make disability appear aggravating. Individual mitigation therefore does not fully answer the class-wide culpability and reliability problems. Polls, foreign practice and organizational views are unnecessary to this conclusion. Penry's earlier assessment of insufficient consensus no longer sustains categorical permission. Atkins's conflicting expert evidence requires application of the constitutional rule through reliable individual factfinding. The Court establishes no numerical cutoff, proof burden or universal hearing design; those details are not resolved on this disputed record.

**Precedent treatment:** Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 (1989), is overruled only insofar as it permits execution of persons with established intellectual disability. Penry v. Johnson retains its requirement of effective mitigation where death remains lawful.

## Precedent Treatment

- Trop v. Dulles, 356 U.S. 86 (1958): supplies the inquiry into evolving standards, informed by objective domestic evidence.
- Coker v. Georgia, 433 U.S. 584 (1977), and Enmund v. Florida, 458 U.S. 782 (1982): supply categorical proportionality and the relation between culpability and penal purposes.
- Ford v. Wainwright, 477 U.S. 399 (1986): supports implementation of a constitutional death restriction through lawful state procedures; its distinct incompetence rule is not equated with intellectual disability.
- Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 (1989): its categorical permission is overruled to the stated extent; its mitigation protection survives.
- Penry v. Johnson, June 4, 2001: effective consideration of mitigation remains required; that decision did not already establish today's categorical bar.

## Law After Decision

Established intellectual disability removes death as a lawful punishment. Conviction and lawful noncapital punishment remain available. Reliable implementation must evaluate intellectual functioning, adaptive limitations and developmental onset together. Detailed proof allocation, diagnostic procedure, retroactivity in another collateral case and other disability categories are outside the present resolution.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** The domestic record does not establish the objective constitutional rejection required to overturn Penry. Fewer than half of capital-punishment States adopted the asserted rule, several enactments are recent or prospective, and their exceptions and definitions matter. States without capital punishment do not independently decide which offenders may be executed. The majority's independent penal judgment replaces legislative choice and juries' individual assessment; serious crimes and variation within the clinical class weaken a categorical culpability conclusion. Polling, foreign practice and professional submissions neither establish the controlling domestic legal standard nor provide a tested substitute. The dissent would preserve individualized mitigation and affirm against this categorical challenge; it does not resolve the contested diagnosis.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns to Virginia for reliable individualized determination under the categorical rule. If intellectual disability is established, a lawful noncapital disposition must follow. The convictions remain intact.
