# Barnhart v. Walton — 2002-03-27

[Authoritative Record](../records/barnhart_v_walton_merits_2002-03-27.md)

## Event

Barnhart v. Walton, No. 00-1937. Argued January 16, 2002; decided March 27, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 235 F.3d 184. The questions are whether the agency may require twelve months of disabling inability as well as lasting impairment, and whether actual substantial work within that period before a favorable disability determination defeats projected-duration and trial-work theories. The circuit affirmed the October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding but reversed the denial on both legal grounds.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The challenged Fourth Circuit legal rulings are reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower court must implement denial on the reviewed duration and trial-work eligibility grounds. Its affirmed October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding remains undisturbed.

## Holdings

### I. The agency may require twelve months of disabling inability

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the twelve-month branch of the adult disability definitions in 42 U.S.C. §§423(d)(1)(A) and 1382c(a)(3)(A), the agency may require that the impairment cause inability to engage in substantial gainful activity lasting, or expected to last, continuously for at least twelve months. The expected-death branch remains distinct; the Court sustains a permissible delegated construction rather than declaring it the only statutory implementation Congress allows.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part I, is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the formal delegated-regulation ground, the functional-duration rule and its temporal qualification. Part II's additional contextual support is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Scalia does not join that additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the agency's construction fits the integrated definition. The relative clause concerning duration follows impairment, but the defined disability is inability to work because of that impairment. The wording does not unambiguously require benefits for a lasting diagnosis after the disabling inability ends. The agency may read the lasting condition as the condition that produces the described inability. The expected-death alternative concerns the impairment; it does not mean that inability or work itself causes death. Title II's ordinary five-month waiting condition does not compel an impairment-only interpretation, and that waiting rule is not imported into SSI.

Section 405(a), incorporated into SSI by §1383(d)(1), supplies rulemaking authority consistent with the benefit statutes. The agency exercised it in binding implementing regulations and the final trial-work provision, after a proposal and notice and comment. Mead requires actual delegation and its exercise, not expertise or form alone; those predicates are established here. Chevron permits the resulting reasonable choice. The July 2000 final rule became effective August 10, 2000. Smiley permits use of its authoritative interpretation of the preexisting statute for antecedent events without backdating a new legislative condition, where clear contrary prior official guidance is absent. The longstanding duration approach and 1995 proposal supply continuity here; no contrary assurance to Walton is established. The lower court's no-retroactivity statement therefore does not exclude the interpretive use at issue.

**Additional controlling explanation in Part II:** The sustained administrative interpretation and the agency's detailed attention to the connected eligibility provisions further support the reasonableness of its choice. These considerations operate within the established delegation and formal exercise of authority; they create neither power from longevity alone nor a holding that every earlier letter, manual or Social Security Ruling has controlling force. Scalia accepts the sufficient formal-regulation ground while withholding this additional contextual ground.

### II. Actual work before a favorable disability finding may defeat the initial duration claim

**Holding and operative rule:** When work demonstrating substantial gainful activity resumes within twelve months after onset of the disabling impairment and before notice of a determination or decision finding disability, the agency may use that known event to reject an initial claim based on an earlier expectation of twelve-month inability. Section 422(c)'s trial-work protection presupposes qualifying Title II entitlement and does not independently establish a missing initial duration condition.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the permissible timing construction, the qualifications below, and the disposition of Walton's two legal theories.

**Operative qualifications:** A claimant returning to work more than twelve months after onset but before adjudication may qualify for trial-work protection if actual duration and the other entitlement conditions are satisfied; passage of twelve months alone does not cure disabling inability that ended earlier. A claimant returning within twelve months after onset, but after notice finding disability, may qualify even when that notice precedes the benefit-award notice. A disability finding, its notice, an award, payment and statutory entitlement are distinct; neither qualification requires prior payment. The separate exclusion for work during a required waiting period remains separate, and the pre-application exclusion concerns months before application, not permanent disqualification for every earlier work episode.

Trial-work services are distinct from substantial gainful activity. The operative nine service months occur within sixty consecutive months and need not themselves be consecutive. New medical or other evidence independent of protected trial work may establish cessation under the governing rules before nine months; a repeated trial-work period is not created within the same period of disability or entitlement. Ordinary Title II insured status, application, age and the five-month waiting condition, including the qualifying prior-entitlement or cessation route within the statutory sixty-month window, remain separate conditions. The Title II trial-work scheme is not transferred to SSI. Statutory blindness, the separate child definition, genuinely unsuccessful work attempts and distinct reopening or reliance questions concerning a claimant with an existing favorable determination are outside the presented adult eligibility dispute.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute does not fix an earlier moment at which an expectation becomes irrevocable before the initial favorable determination. Expected duration permits prompt awards before twelve months have elapsed; it does not require the agency to disregard actual work already known when initial eligibility is decided. The agency may distinguish establishing disability from deciding whether an entitled person's disability has ceased. A protection against using trial work to establish cessation cannot alone create the initial entitlement it presupposes.

Walton objects that entitlement need not await an award and that adjudication timing can produce unequal results. The first point is correct and is preserved by the two qualifications above. The agency acknowledged the second and reasonably accommodated two statutory purposes: prompt prospective awards and exclusion of demonstrated short-term disability. That explanation does not declare every administrative delay harmless; it answers the claimed requirement to treat an earlier expectation as conclusive here. Good Samaritan preserves reasonable administrative choice within the assigned program, while Smiley preserves qualified interpretive use for antecedent events. The actual statutory grant and rulemaking satisfy Mead; no new generalized deference test or resort to Auer is needed.

Walton's October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding stands. His later closed-period award was superseded on administrative review, and no favorable finding notice preceded his return. The circuit's impairment-only interpretation, accrued-expectation theory and independent unreasonableness conclusion therefore fail. Its challenged legal rulings are reversed, with remand to implement the denial on these eligibility grounds.

## Law After Decision

The agency may administer the adult duration definitions through a twelve-month requirement for disabling inability and use actual substantial work before the relevant favorable finding notice in deciding initial eligibility. The holding sustains that reasonable delegated choice; it does not compel every future lawful implementation. The stated pre-award and post-twelve-month qualifications prevent converting entitlement into prior payment or a universal award prerequisite. Title II trial work retains its own scope and conditions.

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Scalia joins Parts I and III, including the formal delegated-regulation ground, permissible duration and timing constructions, qualifications and reversal. He does not join Part II's additional reliance on sustained administrative interpretation. The formal rule and genuine delegation suffice; duration of an agency practice alone should not confer special controlling weight, and the status of old informal documents cannot be assumed. His position does not demand the Court's independent preferred construction or a new multifactor exception to the governing delegation rules.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief joins the Court's permissible-construction holding and adds that the interpretation also survives independent reading. The lasting impairment must produce the inability that the definition describes; expected duration permits timely decisions, but does not require ignoring actual predecision substantial work. Trial-work protection does not itself create initial entitlement. The agency's longstanding, expert reasons deserve substantial persuasive weight because they fit those enacted distinctions, while its statutory rulemaking assignment and operative regulations separately establish actual delegated authority. This additional ground changes neither the Court's permission into a command nor the stated entitlement qualifications.

# Mickens v. Taylor — 2002-03-27

[Authoritative Record](../records/mickens_v_taylor_merits_2002-03-27.md)

## Event

Mickens v. Taylor, No. 00-9285. Argued November 5, 2001; decided March 27, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the en banc Fourth Circuit, reviewing denial of federal habeas relief. The appointed lawyer had recently represented the murder victim, and the appointing judge knew the relationship but did not inquire or disclose it. The question is whether that omitted inquiry dispenses with a showing that the conflict adversely affected representation. A federal evidentiary hearing resulted in a finding against adverse effect; the separate factual challenge to that finding is outside the granted question.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The denial of habeas relief on the reviewed conflict claim is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, who would reverse and obtain a new trial on the separate grounds stated below.

The conviction and sentence remain undisturbed on this claim. The Court orders no new trial or additional conflict hearing solely because the judge failed to inquire.

## Holdings

### An omitted inquiry does not itself establish the adverse effect necessary here

**Holding and operative rule:** Outside the established circumstances requiring automatic relief, a trial court's failure to inquire into a particular potential conflict it knew or reasonably should have known about does not itself establish a Sixth Amendment violation. A defendant invoking the conflict-based relaxation of ordinary outcome prejudice must at least show that competing obligations adversely affected counsel's performance; that showing is different from proving a reasonable probability of a different verdict under Strickland.

**Authority:** Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas. Those five directly adopt the rule, the confined application and affirmance. The four dissenters support a new trial on distinct grounds and do not establish a common alternative holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the right protects loyal, effective representation rather than imposing automatic reversal as a sanction for a judge's omitted procedure. Holloway requires automatic relief when counsel makes an adequate timely conflict objection to joint representation and the court compels continuation without the required inquiry or protection. That distinct objection-and-compulsion predicate is absent here. Sullivan requires an actual conflict adversely affecting performance before ordinary outcome prejudice is dispensed with. Wood's actual-conflict inquiry does not create a separate rule awarding relief for divided obligations without an effect on representation in this setting. Cronic's exceptional denial-of-assistance principle does not convert this omitted inquiry into a complete loss of counsel.

The contrary positions emphasize that the State selected the murder victim's recent lawyer, the judge knew the particular risk and Mickens lacked the information needed to object. The Court accepts the seriousness of that combination and the duty to inquire. Swidler preserves confidentiality after death, so death does not eliminate the potential conflict. But neither continuing duties nor judicial silence establishes that counsel withheld a defense, altered a choice or otherwise performed differently. The appointment's gravity alone does not establish the structural denial or representation injury needed for the proposed categorical remedy.

The federal court conducted an evidentiary hearing and found no adverse effect. The granted question asks whether that requirement can be dispensed with; it does not reopen the separate factual challenge to the finding. Even assuming Sullivan's relaxed-prejudice route applies to this successive representation, the existing determination defeats this claim. The Court does not decide its application to every category of successive representation, which lies beyond the reviewed question. Holloway retains its established rule, and no ordinary outcome-prejudice burden is added after the predicates of an applicable conflict exception are satisfied.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Holloway v. Arkansas, 435 U.S. 475:** Preserved for compelled joint representation over an adequate timely objection; its automatic remedy is not extended solely because the judge knew a potential conflict.
- **Cuyler v. Sullivan, 446 U.S. 335:** Applied on the assumption favorable to Mickens that its relaxed-prejudice route is available; an actual conflict adversely affecting performance remains necessary here, without additional ordinary outcome prejudice.
- **Wood v. Georgia, 450 U.S. 261:** Its inquiry obligation and actual-conflict requirement do not establish the proposed separate exemption from adverse effect in this setting.
- **Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668:** Ordinary outcome prejudice remains distinct from the adverse-effect requirement under an applicable conflict exception.
- **United States v. Cronic, 466 U.S. 648:** Its exceptional presumed-prejudice circumstances are not established merely by this unperformed inquiry.
- **Swidler & Berlin v. United States:** Post-death confidentiality remains; its survival proves neither a particular confidence nor impairment of this defense.
- **Both Williams v. Taylor decisions:** Constitutional entitlement, concealed-fact cause, factual development and state-merits review remain distinct; the federal hearing does not supply a state merits adjudication.

## Law After Decision

A known potential conflict and the judge's neglected inquiry do not themselves establish the adverse effect required for this conflict-based exception to ordinary prejudice. Holloway retains its distinct automatic-relief rule. The Court assumes Sullivan's route is available here without deciding its application to every successive representation. The existing no-adverse-effect determination defeats this claim within the granted question.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens would reverse the habeas denial and secure a new trial because the State appointed the murder victim's recent lawyer in a capital case without disclosure or the accused's informed acceptance, despite the judge's knowledge. The right to a meaningful lawyer-client relationship, counsel's disclosure obligation and the court's protective duty operate together. This exceptional State-created arrangement warrants presumed impairment under Cronic; Holloway, Sullivan and Wood supply the conflict safeguards, while Swidler preserves continuing confidentiality without proving any particular confidence. His rule concerns this undisclosed capital victim-counsel appointment, not every former-client relationship or ethical breach. He does not require another actual-conflict hearing before recognizing the constitutional entitlement.

**Souter, dissenting.** Souter would reverse and require a new trial under the court's prospective duty to address a sufficiently identified risk before representation unfolds. When the judge already has adequate notice, Holloway's preventive protection should not depend solely on an objection from a potentially conflicted lawyer. The judge must inquire, secure an intelligent waiver or arrange replacement when warranted. Souter distinguishes that prospective duty from later discovery: he reads Wood's retrospective inquiry as requiring actual conflict and adverse effect, rather than supporting an actual-conflict-only remand here. His discussion of possible adverse effects supplies no decision of the factual issue outside the grant and no direction for another hearing.

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by Ginsburg.** These Justices would reverse and secure a new trial on a narrower structural ground. The State supplied the recent criminal lawyer of the murder victim to defend the accused in a capital case; the immediate incompatibility, the capital proceeding's use of the victim's character and the State's creation of the arrangement produce a breakdown in the trial framework. Cronic and Fulminante support relief without reconstructing individual performance effects at that conjunction of facts. Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton reinforces the concern for institutional fairness. They expressly leave the general relationship among Holloway, Sullivan and Wood unresolved and do not join Souter's general known-risk rule or an actual-conflict-only remand.

Each dissent seeks the new-trial remedy through ordinary conditional habeas relief, permitting the State to retry with constitutionally adequate representation. None orders immediate unconditional release, acquittal or a new sentence alone. Their constitutional positions do not adjudicate an independently presented collateral defense on predicates absent from the question decided. Counsel maintained the narrower actual-conflict theory; the dissenters give their own legal answers to the granted conflict question and the requested new-trial remedy.

## Procedure After Action

The habeas denial stands on the reviewed constitutional ground. The Court does not reopen the factual adverse-effect determination or direct another conflict inquiry. The broader successive-representation question remains outside the grant.

# Venetian Casino Resort, L.L.C. v. Local Joint Executive Board of Las Vegas — 2002-04-01

[Authoritative Record](../records/venetian_casino_resort_v_local_joint_executive_board_merits_2002-04-01.md)

## Event

Venetian Casino Resort, L.L.C. v. Local Joint Executive Board of Las Vegas. Merits decision, April 1, 2002. Review of the Ninth Circuit judgment, 257 F.3d 937. The question is whether the replacement sidewalk along Las Vegas Boulevard remains a traditional public forum despite private fee ownership, given its continuing public-passage function and the government agreement replacing the former public sidewalk. The casino sought exclusion of union demonstrators from that route.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate.

## Public Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment is affirmed.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would reverse and remand.

The public-forum judgment for this replacement boulevard sidewalk stands. The casino's requested blanket exclusion based solely on private title is rejected; lawful neutral measures addressing actual obstruction, safety and access remain available.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Both holdings and affirmance in full |
| Dissent | Scalia | Thomas | Rejects governmental attribution; would reverse and remand without reaching forum classification independently |

## Holdings

### I. The replacement public thoroughfare retains constitutional speech protection

**Holding and operative rule:** A sidewalk deliberately substituted for an existing public street route, maintained under a continuing, enforceable public-passage obligation and integrated into that route remains a traditional public forum for peaceful expression. Private fee ownership does not permit a general speech exclusion from that passage; the holding concerns the replacement route, not all privately owned walkways or customer-access areas.

**Authority:** Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. These seven directly adopt the route-specific governmental-attribution and traditional-public-forum rule, its application and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that constitutional responsibility follows the actual public-route arrangement. Government permitted removal of the former public sidewalk while securing a connected replacement, mandatory unobstructed passage, obligations binding successors, and retained protection against hazards. Marsh supplies the limited principle that private ownership cannot defeat constitutional protection of an established governmental public function. Brentwood and Lebron require actual governmental responsibility, rather than an inference from popularity or accessibility alone. Here the government's direct undertaking to preserve its street route supplies that responsibility before forum classification is considered. Grace then supplies the traditional expressive character of an ordinary street sidewalk.

The private-property reservation and future-dedication clause do not disappear. They preserve title, ordinary maintenance and lawful property interests; the Court does not construe the agreement as a present conveyance of title or decide a taking. The reservation itself is bounded by constitutional limits. The obligation to dedicate a further replacement if this walkway is removed, altered or abandoned protects continuity of passage; it does not transform the current mandatory through-route into an invitation the owner may freely withdraw. Lloyd and Hudgens preserve control of genuinely private commercial property, but this passage continues a displaced public street function. The dissent's proposed distinction between access and speech would let the same government-secured street remain compulsory public passage while losing its traditional expressive protection solely through relocated title. The First Amendment does not permit that result on this arrangement.

### II. Forum protection permits lawful neutral protection of passage and safety

**Holding and operative rule:** The casino may not exclude the peaceful demonstration from this replacement sidewalk solely by invoking private title. Traditional-public-forum standards continue to govern: content-based restrictions require a compelling justification and narrow tailoring, while content-neutral time, place and manner restrictions must be narrowly tailored to a significant governmental interest and leave ample alternative channels; viewpoint discrimination is forbidden.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Court opinion adopts this consequence and preserves lawful neutral regulation of actual obstruction, safety and access.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that speech protection and continued pedestrian passage operate together. Perry supplies the governing forum standards; Ward permits genuinely neutral management of time, place and manner without requiring the least restrictive conceivable means, while forbidding burdens substantially broader than necessary. Thomas v. Chicago Park District preserves constrained neutral administration of shared expressive space. Those rules leave room to address actual obstruction and safety; public-forum status does not immunize such conduct. The casino's requested blanket exclusion rests on title and therefore fails at this location. No particular obstruction sanction or casino-interior restriction is presented, so the Court decides neither. The Ninth Circuit's judgment concerning this sidewalk is affirmed.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Marsh v. Alabama, 326 U.S. 501:** Applied to the established public-thoroughfare function; ordinary private commercial property does not thereby become governmental.
- **Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association:** Its requirement of actual governmental responsibility is applied to the government's binding replacement-route arrangement; public benefit or association alone remains insufficient.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.:** Its rejection of dispositive private labels informs the attribution inquiry. Its special-creation and permanent-majority-appointment rule remains sufficient in its own setting, rather than a mandatory template for every public function.
- **United States v. Grace, 461 U.S. 171:** Supplies ordinary street-sidewalk forum protection after governmental responsibility has been established.
- **Lloyd Corp. v. Tanner, 407 U.S. 551, and Hudgens v. NLRB, 424 U.S. 507:** Distinguished because ordinary private shopping property does not continue a displaced government-secured public street route.
- **Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators' Association, 460 U.S. 37, and Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781:** Their traditional-forum and neutral time, place and manner standards remain governing.
- **Thomas v. Chicago Park District:** Preserves lawful, constrained neutral administration; no particular permit rule is adjudicated here.
- **The Lee companion decisions:** Supply no controlling airport classification or combined rationale; their fractured judgments are not converted into authority for this sidewalk.

## Law After Decision

A government-secured sidewalk that substitutes for and continues an existing public street route under an enforceable passage obligation retains traditional-public-forum protection despite private title. This rule applies to that defined public route; it does not make commercial premises generally governmental. Existing content and neutral-management standards govern restrictions on the passage.

## Separate Writings

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justice Thomas. They would reverse and remand because governmental responsibility for this speech exclusion has not been established. Their framework requires attribution before forum classification: Lebron's concrete structural predicate and the limits emphasized in Brentwood do not allow public benefit, traffic regulation or access covenants alone to substitute for governmental control. The agreement expressly preserves present private rights and requires dedication if the present walkway is later removed, altered or abandoned. In their view, that distinction shows that the State secured passage and safety while withholding a present public speech entitlement. The fact that the walkway replaces a former public route makes the contrary argument substantial, but does not supply control over the disputed exclusion. They do not independently classify the forum after rejecting attribution. Their proposed remand would permit ordinary disposition under that attribution rule, rather than direct arrests, award damages or resolve a taking.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment remains in force. Lawful neutral regulation may address actual obstruction and safety under the governing public-forum standards. The decision concerns this replacement boulevard route and extends no access order to the casino interior or unrelated private property.
