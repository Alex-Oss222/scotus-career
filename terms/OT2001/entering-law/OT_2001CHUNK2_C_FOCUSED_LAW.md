## Source: state/holdings/first-amendment.md — Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement

#### Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement, 505 U.S. 123 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-538.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

##### Whether the County's demonstration-fee mechanism is facially invalid for unbounded discretion or audience-hostility pricing

**Holding:** A licensing rule directed at protected demonstrations is facially invalid when neither its text, authoritative construction, nor established administration supplies narrow, objective, definite standards controlling whether and how much officials charge. A challenger need not prove discriminatory administration of its own application, and a maximum fee does not supply the missing standards.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela separately concurs in the judgment on compatible grounds and does not formally join that opinion.

**Material application:** The administrator may select whether to charge, which time and support costs to include and whether to discount them without a binding method or explanation requirement. A $1,000 cap does not constrain those choices, and the finding that the particular $100 assessment reflected administrative work does not cure the facial defect.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Different fees alone do not prove invalidity. Authoritative narrowing construction and established administration may supply constraints; presumed benign discretion does not. Concrete indigency, fixed neutral fees, particular content-independent costs and municipal funding duties remain undecided; separate City and School Board claims are outside this review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm facial relief against enforcement of the County fee mechanism. The County may enact lawful neutral coordination and sufficiently bounded cost rules. No damages, refund amount, fee schedule or mandatory municipal spending formula is awarded.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): retained and distinguished; its authoritative nondiscrimination construction supports flexible lawful fees, not unbounded discretion.
- Shuttlesworth v. Birmingham, 394 U.S. 147 (1969): applied; expressive licensing requires narrow, objective, definite standards.
- Niemotko v. Maryland, 340 U.S. 268 (1951): applied; officials licensing protected expression must act under definite standards.
- City of Lakewood v. Plain Dealer Publishing Co., 486 U.S. 750 (1988): applied; the risk inherent in unbounded expressive licensing supports facial review despite one unobjectionable application.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applied as to authoritative constructions and actual administration; a binding narrowing construction matters, a presumed benign discretion does not.

**Independent alternative holding:** A permit fee that varies with anticipated hostile reaction to a demonstration is content based and violates the First Amendment when justified by recovering the costs of protecting speakers from that reaction. Neither calling those expenses a secondary effect nor imposing a small numerical ceiling makes the charge constitutional.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Souter: an independently sufficient five-Justice holding. Stone-Zsela's separate concurrence agrees with the rule without a formal join.

**Limits, remedy, transition, or precedent treatment:** Audience-hostility pricing independently sustains the same facial protection against enforcement. It does not find the actual $100 assessment retaliatory, fix police expenditures or forbid genuinely content-independent administrative charges. The following precedent treatment applies to this alternative ground:

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781 (1989): applied; the justification must be independent of the message.
- Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988): applied at its supported content-classification level; listeners' hostile responses are not neutral secondary effects.
- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): distinguished and retained; coordination costs do not include a message-dependent hostility premium.
- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991 simulated decision: consistent application of the prohibition on unjustified content-based financial burdens; its separate remedial limits are unchanged.
- Terminiello v. Chicago, 337 U.S. 1 (1949): applied in its protection against suppressing speech because of hostile response; no new police-protection entitlement is inferred.

##### Whether the First Amendment imposes a universal nominal-fee ceiling or makes a small fee necessarily valid

**Holding:** The First Amendment does not make a demonstration fee invalid merely because it exceeds a nominal amount, or valid merely because it is small. Cox's allowance for lawful, nondiscriminatory regulatory cost recovery survives; a fee's lawful purpose, content neutrality, and bounded administration govern the defects presented here.

**Proposition-level authority:** Blackmun's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy and Souter, independently controls both rejections of numerical shortcuts. White's dissent, joined by Scalia and Thomas, and Stone's separate concurrence also reject a universal nominal ceiling; no judgment-only position is converted into a full-opinion join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No permissible dollar amount or comprehensive schedule of recoverable costs is fixed. A fee exceeding a nominal amount must still have a lawful purpose, neutral criteria and bounded administration.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court rejects the court of appeals' amount-only reasoning while affirming the facial relief on the independently sufficient discretion and hostility grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Cox v. New Hampshire, 312 U.S. 569 (1941): retained; flexible, nondiscriminatory regulatory fees are not limited to nominal amounts.
- Murdock v. Pennsylvania, 319 U.S. 105 (1943): distinguished; its nominal-fee reference does not establish an amount-only rule or displace Cox.

## Source: state/holdings/first-amendment.md — Hill v. Colorado

#### Hill v. Colorado, 530 U.S. 703 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1856.

**Decided:** June 28, 2000.

##### 1. The law regulates a specified knowing approach, not all speech near a facility

**Holding:** Colorado section 18-9-122(3) applies to a person who knowingly initiates an approach within eight feet of another person without that person's consent, for passing a leaflet or handbill to that person, displaying a sign to that person, or engaging that person in oral protest, education or counseling, on a public way or sidewalk within a radius of 100 feet from any entrance door to a facility legally licensed, certified, authorized or permitted to administer medical treatment in Colorado. Under the binding state construction, a stationary speaker need not retreat when someone else approaches; the law does not prohibit mere presence, all speech or signs within the area, or consensual communication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A recipient need not be a patient. The decision addresses subsection (3), not the separate knowing-obstruction offense, every permitted local law or the complete referenced civil cause of action. No petitioner's misconduct or liability is found.

##### 2. The actual approach restriction is content neutral

**Holding:** At its authoritative scope, subsection (3) regulates the manner of a close physical approach across subjects and viewpoints for access and treatment-related reasons independent of disagreement with the speaker's ideas. It is therefore reviewed as a content-neutral legislative time, place and manner restriction under Ward, rather than as a rule selecting a disfavored message or an individualized injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Classification requires actual operation and justification, not a benign objective or conduct label. The Court recognizes no general right to avoid unpopular ideas, no medical-area exception to public-forum rights and no independent suppression rationale based on emotional offense.

##### 3. The limited approach rule satisfies Ward's fit and alternative-channel requirements

**Holding:** Taken together, the eight-foot approach distance, 100-foot entrance-area limit, knowledge requirement, consent condition and binding no-retreat construction make subsection (3) narrowly tailored to significant access and medical-treatment interests while leaving ample usable communication channels. The facial challenge therefore fails under Ward, without establishing that every personal-distance rule is valid or foreclosing discriminatory-enforcement and materially broader as-applied challenges.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Parts I-III, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; six direct joins.

**Material application:** The Court recognizes the burden on initiating a quiet exchange and taking a few steps to place a leaflet in a recipient's hand. The permitted channels must be practical, not merely theoretical: speakers can remain stationary and offer literature to passing people, display signs, speak audibly from a lawful distance, request consent, or converse with willing people who approach them. These opportunities are not identical to every preferred encounter, and amplification is not assumed to replace private conversation in every case. Their combined availability satisfies the present inquiry at the defined scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Ward does not require the least restrictive conceivable means. The decision sustains the combination of limits, not eight feet alone; it creates no universal valid distance or mandatory retreat. Madsen's injunction standard and Schenck's invalidation of the moving fifteen-foot restraints remain.

##### 4. The law is not facially vague at its authoritative scope

**Holding:** The knowing speaker-initiated approach, ordinary communicative-purpose terms, consent condition, measurable geographic limits and binding no-retreat construction give adequate notice and constrain enforcement sufficiently to defeat this facial vagueness challenge. This conclusion does not decide every ambiguous encounter or approve punishment outside those elements.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejection of this ancillary claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Knowledge operates with definite elements and the authoritative construction; it is not a universal cure for an undefined offense. Difficult mixed encounters or consent signals remain matters for concrete applications.

##### 5. The facial record does not establish substantial unconstitutional overbreadth

**Holding:** Coverage of all speakers and recipients in the specified public areas around qualifying health-care entrances does not, on this record and at the law's authoritative scope, establish a substantial unconstitutional sweep in relation to its valid applications. The facial overbreadth claim fails, while appropriate challenges to discriminatory enforcement, materially different applications, wide entrances or multipurpose buildings remain open.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejection of this ancillary claim.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Harmless contacts and nonpatients are not excluded from the law or from overbreadth analysis. Neither general coverage alone nor hypothetical difficult settings establishes substantial unconstitutional reach here.

##### 6. Consent to a close approach is not an official speech license

**Holding:** Requiring the approached person's consent for the defined knowing close approach does not create an official licensing system or give that person authority to silence other speech, other willing listeners or stationary advocacy. The consent-based prior-restraint challenge therefore fails at this statutory scope.

**Proposition-level authority:** Breyer's opinion of the Court, Part IV, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg; five direct joins. Stone-Zsela agrees only with rejection of this ancillary claim.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Colorado Supreme Court's judgment is affirmed, leaving the defense summary judgment in place. No new remand, conviction, civil award or prospective injunction issues. Subsection (2)'s knowing obstruction offense remains distinct; subsection (5) permits local laws no less restrictive but receives no blanket constitutional approval; subsection (6) adds civil liability by reference to section 13-21-106.7 without a ruling on every element or remedy. A later challenge to actual discriminatory enforcement or a materially different application must be decided on its own facts and preserved legal grounds.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Ward v. Rock Against Racism supplies content-neutral legislative time, place and manner review: significant interests, narrow tailoring and ample usable channels, without a least-restrictive-means requirement. It is applied to the actual combined limits of this approach rule.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network supplies the limit that a benign objective alone does not cure speech-selective operation. The Court distinguishes this delivery mechanism while retaining that requirement.
- City of Erie v. Pap's A.M. retains its rule against incidental-burden treatment when operation and justification principally target communicative expression. Its adult-entertainment holding does not decide this different, authoritatively confined approach mechanism, and its separate opinions are not combined into a broader rule.
- Madsen v. Women's Health Center retains the more demanding injunction-fit standard and its invalidation of the 300-foot advance-invitation restraint. The smaller statutory approach rule and preserved channels do not revive that injunction or authorize another unsupported restraint.
- Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network retains its invalidation of the particular moving fifteen-foot restraints and its qualified fixed-zone refusal provision. Neither supplies a universal numerical rule, a retreat duty here or a general power to silence an unwilling listener's surroundings.
- Grayned v. City of Rockford supplies ordinary notice and constrained-enforcement requirements without mathematical certainty. It supports Part IV's facial clarity conclusion while preserving concrete application questions.
- Broadrick v. Oklahoma supplies the requirement of substantial unconstitutional reach compared with legitimate operation. Part IV applies it without exempting time, place and manner regulations from overbreadth review.

## Source: state/holdings/first-amendment.md — Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network

#### Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1065.
**Decided:** February 19, 1997.

##### Fixed access restrictions satisfy the governing injunction standard on this record

**Holding:** A content-neutral speech injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the established conduct and serve its corresponding significant interest. These fixed fifteen-foot demonstration restrictions at identified doorways, driveway entrances and parking-access points of covered facilities—all facilities where abortions are performed in the Western District of New York—with nonthreatening initial counseling by no more than two counselors per approached person or group, satisfy that requirement on the supported obstruction and crowding record; fifteen feet is not a universal safe harbor.

The injunction binds responsible parties and persons with notice acting in actual concert; shared views, association or independent advocacy alone do not establish that relationship. This coverage boundary neither adjudicates new nonparty liability nor determines whether a particular speech restriction is sufficiently tailored.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's fixed-access holding is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six Justices. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also join the responsible-party, notice and actual-concert boundary, giving that distinct proposition nine votes.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decree regulates demonstration, not mere presence; the counseling limit is per approached person or group, not two counselors for an entire zone. Substantial temporary-order compliance does not erase supported access interference, and no requirement to disobey a speech-neutral predecessor or exhaust every narrower possibility is adopted. The temporary order already contained personal-distance and counseling restrictions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen supplies provision-specific fit; its distances are not safe harbors. Carroll supplies precision and Claiborne Hardware ties relief to responsible misconduct. Ward's rule for ordinary neutral regulation does not replace the heightened injunction standard.

##### The fixed-zone counseling exception may end after a communicated refusal

**Holding:** Within an otherwise valid fixed access zone, the allowance for nonthreatening counseling by up to two people per approached person or group may end when the recipient objectively communicates verbally or nonverbally that counseling is unwanted. The counselor must end that close encounter and leave the fixed zone, while retaining lawful advocacy outside it; the condition establishes neither silence everywhere nor a mobile personal radius beyond the fixed zone.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; six Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Unexpressed unwillingness does not trigger departure. This condition is an exception to independently valid fixed access relief, not a general unwilling-listener veto or captive-audience rule.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen's protection of peaceful initial approach remains: permission to begin an encounter within a valid corridor differs from the broad advance-permission requirement invalidated there. Boos preserves the distinction between physical harm and offense at communicative content.

##### The moving-person restriction burdens more speech than the record warrants

**Holding:** The injunction's fifteen-foot demonstration boundary moving with each person seeking access to or leaving a covered facility burdens more protected speech than necessary under Madsen and must be struck, even with its initial counseling exception and communicated-refusal qualification. This record-specific holding does not create a right to threaten, touch, obstruct, crowd or coercively pursue anyone, or invalidate every precisely supported separation rule.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The initial counseling and refusal qualifications do not cure uncertainty from moving listeners and overlapping zones. The Court invents neither a travel cap nor an overlap rule to sustain the actual restraint. Precisely supported, materially different conduct relief remains open.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen requires independent fit for the additional moving burden; Carroll supplies precision where contempt enforces it, and Claiborne Hardware preserves the misconduct nexus.

##### The moving-vehicle restriction independently fails

**Holding:** The fifteen-foot demonstration radius moving with vehicles seeking access to or leaving covered facilities likewise burdens more speech than necessary on this record and must be struck. The decision preserves traffic safety and fixed access protection without deciding the validity of a materially different, precisely supported vehicle restriction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen requires a separate vehicle-specific fit; fixed entrance protection and traffic interests do not automatically justify a moving demonstration radius.

##### Removal of the moving predicate also removes the dependent retreat distance

**Holding:** Once paragraph 1(b)'s moving-person and moving-vehicle restrictions are invalidated, paragraph 1(c)'s cross-reference cannot recreate an automatic fifteen-foot moving distance outside valid fixed zones. That dependent operation must be removed on remand; independently lawful conduct prohibitions remain, and the Court neither commands a substitute distance nor decides every cessation condition considered apart from the invalidated predicate.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; eight Justices.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The fixed access and in-zone refusal components are affirmed 6–3; the person and vehicle moving restraints are reversed 8–1; their automatic outside-zone retreat operation is vacated 8–1. Remand requires a conforming preliminary decree, preserving independently lawful unreviewed conduct terms. No replacement distance, travel endpoint, compulsory substitute, contempt judgment, damages or final permanent decree is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Madsen's invalidated-predicate and no-evasion rules require removal of the dependent distance; its refusal to compel a substitute personal zone remains intact.

## Source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Blessing v. Freestone

#### Blessing v. Freestone

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1441.
**Decided:** April 21, 1997.

##### What a claimant must identify before invoking §1983 for a statutory violation

**Holding:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit the claimant, creating a binding obligation and a sufficiently definite individual entitlement for judicial enforcement. A beneficial statute, mandatory administrative duty, or useful remedy does not alone establish that right; qualitative terms are not universally unenforceable merely because they require judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous. Scalia and Kennedy's additional original-meaning reservation withholds no join and supplies no controlling limit.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Wright* and *Wilder* supply defined individual entitlements, not indivisible program promises. *Livadas* separates right, enforcement channel, foreclosure, defendant, and remedy. *Suter* retains provision-specific adjudication; *Pennhurst* requires clear notice without categorical state-plan immunity.

##### Whether Title IV-D creates an individual entitlement to substantial compliance or general administrative adequacy

**Holding:** Title IV-D's aggregate substantial-compliance measure creates no personal right to systemwide compliance. The same theory does not become an individual right when framed as sufficient staffing or satisfactory computer administration as such; genuinely distinct claims under particular provisions require separate assessment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 654(3)'s organizational and staffing duties establish no beneficiary entitlement to a specified staff complement, caseload, or worker. Former and amended §654(16) and related §654a management requirements do not make the administrative system itself an individual entitlement. Aggregate compliance could coexist with a violation of an independently existing family-specific right.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Shortages, backlogs, and accounting defects may prove an established violation or inform fitted relief, but cannot create a right. No distinct standardized-computer, confidentiality, or individual transmission claim is established, and no particular clause of 45 C.F.R. §§303.20 or 307.10 is decided. Regulatory elaboration of an existing right is not categorically excluded.

##### Whether general federal administration forecloses all otherwise existing §1983 claims

**Holding:** An individual federal right is presumptively enforceable through §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that route or provides a sufficiently comprehensive incompatible remedial scheme. Title IV-D audits, corrective plans, and funding sanctions alone do not establish chapterwide foreclosure; the actual provision and any specific incompatible remedy require separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Material application:** The federal oversight tools regulate funding and offer no general family-specific administrative forum. Their detail and significance do not alone show incompatibility with §1983.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Livadas*, *Wright*, and *Wilder* keep foreclosure separate from right creation. *Sea Clammers* and *Smith v. Robinson* retain their incompatible-remedy holdings, distinguished from this general oversight arrangement.

##### What effect state-plan placement has under §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10

**Holding:** A Social Security Act provision cannot be denied enforceability merely because it specifies a state plan or its contents. Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 remove that categorical ground without creating an individual right or changing the preserved conclusion that §671(a)(15) is not privately enforceable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The respective statutes apply to actions pending on October 20 and October 31, 1994 and those filed thereafter, including this action. Other established grounds governing private actions remain except the state-plan ground the legislation removes.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Suter*'s actual §671(a)(15) and (9) rights rulings and separate implied-action rulings remain, together with its preservation of the independent §671(a)(16) claim. The statutory reference imports no broader state-plan immunity.

##### Whether a general prayer for relief eliminates Madrid's particular distribution allegation

**Holding:** Madrid's allegation of nondistribution under former §657(b)(1) requires its own inquiry despite the broad programwide prayer. Each actual claim must be matched to its provision, conduct, remedy, governing version, and operative dates; an obsolete or not-yet-effective duty cannot silently justify an injunction.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The former distribution language is more specific than the aggregate benchmark, but the Court decides no individual right, eligibility, payment fact, defendant, or amount. It concerns the first $50 of periodic monthly support for a month received in that month, plus the first $50 for each prior month received then if the absent parent made that prior payment in the month due, without reducing assistance or affecting eligibility. It does not award a separate $50 for every installment regardless of timing. The rule applies to covered approved-plan collections for fiscal years after September 30, 1976, subject to the distinct foster-care rules in subsection (d).

**Material application:** The foster-care sequence first reimburses current maintenance; qualifying excess up to ordered support goes to the placement agency for the child's interests; the remainder reimburses unrepaid earlier foster-care or family-assistance payments with appropriate federal reimbursement; any balance goes to the agency for the child. The agency may reserve its payments for later needs or make them available to the person providing daily care. No applicable exception or payment fact is established for Madrid.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remand must apply the following distinct statutory dates and qualifications, without inferring an Arizona implementation event or default:

- Pub. L. 104-193 §395(a) generally makes title III changes requiring state-law enactment or amendment under §666 or state-plan revision under §654 effective for periods beginning October 1, 1996; other provisions generally take effect on August 22, 1996. Specifically provided dates and the following separate grace rules remain controlling.
- Under §395(b), the applicable date is the later of the otherwise specified date or the effective date of implementing state legislation, but no later than the first day of the first calendar quarter after the close of the first regular legislative session beginning after August 22, 1996. Each year of a two-year session counts separately. Under §395(c), a State unable to comply without amending its constitution is protected from a noncompliance finding until the earlier of one year after the necessary amendment takes effect or five years after August 22, 1996. Neither rule establishes an Arizona event or compliance fact here.
- The former §654(24) operational-computer deadline was changed from October 1, 1995 to October 1, 1997 by the October 12, 1995 amendment; the old date establishes no present default. The 1996 text separately requires compliance by October 1, 1997 with requirements enacted on or before October 13, 1988, and by October 1, 2000 with requirements enacted on or before August 22, 1996. The latter deadline extends one day for every day the Secretary misses the final-regulations deadline of two years after August 22, 1996. Implementation deadlines remain distinct from enactment and general effective dates.
- Section 654a(g), added by §312(c), takes effect October 1, 1998. Section 312(d)(2) separately permits a State processing receipts through local courts on August 22, 1996 to continue that receipt processing through September 30, 1999, at its option, through the courts already processing those payments by enactment. That narrow exception concerns §654b(b)(1)'s unit receipt-handling requirement; it is not a wholesale extension of every computer-system obligation. No Arizona qualification for it is found, and these future duties are not April 1997 mandates merely because they appear in the compilation.

##### Whether relief may follow general administrative deficiencies without a proved individual violation

**Holding:** Relief requires an actual individual right, an available §1983 channel, a proper defendant, breach, causation, and a fitted lawful remedy. General administrative failure alone supports neither an undifferentiated compliance injunction nor a prescribed statewide staffing or computer redesign; rejection of the broad theory does not require dismissal of every concrete claim.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Vacate and remand for provision-specific adjudication. No specified payment, damages award, structural injunction, or wholesale dismissal is ordered.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No directly implied Title IV-D action is decided. The Eleventh Amendment argument and proposed overruling of *Thiboutot*, neither raised below nor included in the questions granted, are not reached; defendant-specific issues remain subject to their own inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Maine v. Thiboutot* remains undisturbed. *Livadas*'s separate remedial inquiries prevent both an automatic structural decree and categorical rejection of particular claims.

## Source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Alexander v. Sandoval

#### Alexander v. Sandoval, 532 U.S. 275

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1908.
**Decided:** April 24, 2001.

##### Whether Title VI's private action supports prospective enforcement of valid section 602 requirements

**Holding:** Title VI authorizes its statutory beneficiaries to seek prospective compliance by covered recipients with valid, sufficiently definite section 602 requirements implementing the statutory protection against discrimination, including the effects requirement accepted in this case. The action comes from the integrated statute, not the regulation independently; a claimant must establish the actual regulatory elements and obtain relief fitted to the violation, without treating statistical disparity alone as sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct joins to the rule, application, partial overruling and remedy.

**Material application:** The accepted regulatory validity and unchallenged national-origin-effects finding support the reviewed injunction requiring accommodation in Alabama's driver's-license examinations.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Regulatory validity and the correctness of the impact finding are not independently adjudicated. The decision establishes no discriminatory intent, damages remedy for unintentional effects, new section 1983 action, statistical presumption or private administrative-exhaustion requirement. Section 601 retains its intent requirement, and valid administrative enforcement remains available under its own conditions.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the reviewed prospective-action judgment and injunction, 5-4. No damages, funding cutoff or reopening of completed final judgments follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Powell v. Ridge is expressly overruled only insofar as its direct-action prohibition forecloses this prospective Title VI enforcement. Its separate section 1983 holding and completed judgment remain intact. Cannon supplies contextual congressional authorization in the established private-action setting; Guardians and Choate preserve the distinction between statutory intent liability and regulatory effects protection without previously deciding this action. McClellan's statute-specific authorization requirement remains, and Central Bank's received securities action is undisturbed. Franklin keeps authorization separate from relief; Blessing's individual-right inquiry for section 1983 remains distinct.

## Source: state/holdings/civil-rights.md — Saenz v. Roe

#### Saenz v. Roe

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-97; judgment below, Roe v. Anderson, 134 F.3d 1400.
**Decided:** 1999-05-17.

##### Bona fide new citizens receive equal ordinary welfare treatment

**Holding:** A United States citizen who establishes bona fide residence in a State becomes its citizen on equal terms; the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship and Privileges or Immunities provisions forbid lowering that otherwise eligible citizen's ordinary welfare benefits solely because of recent arrival or the former State's benefit amount. California's first-year differential violates that guarantee even if the person never received benefits before moving or moved for reasons unrelated to assistance.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight Justices).

##### Federal permission cannot lower the constitutional floor

**Holding:** Congress may structure and condition federal welfare assistance, but may not authorize a State to violate the Fourteenth Amendment's equal-citizenship guarantee. Section 604(c)'s permission to use prior-State benefit levels therefore cannot sustain California's unconstitutional differential; permission is not a federal command.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight Justices).

**Limits and questions not reached:** Bona fide residence verification and equal eligibility conditions remain enforceable. The Court establishes no national benefit amount or general welfare entitlement and does not reopen Slaughter-House's broader construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the preliminary injunction against California's first-year prior-State ceiling; no permanent decree, damages or fixed benefit amount is entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- The Slaughter-House Cases supply the specific recognition of becoming another State's citizen through bona fide residence; their broader construction is not reopened.
- Shapiro v. Thompson supplies the prohibition on durational welfare penalties; a reduced payment still burdens current equality.
- Dunn v. Blumstein distinguishes actual residence from additional duration.
- Zobel v. Williams rejects ordinary resident-benefit allocation by arrival history.
- City of Boerne v. Flores prevents statutory replacement of constitutional content.
- Lunding and Romer retain their source-specific equality rules; neither is converted into a general judicial guarantee of welfare amounts.

## Source: state/holdings/administrative-law.md — Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala

#### Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala, 508 U.S. 402 (1993)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 91-2079.
**Decided:** June 7, 1993.

##### Corrective adjustments operate within the authorized cost-method system

**Holding:** Section 1395x(v)(1)(A)(ii) requires suitable retroactive corrective adjustments when, for a provider and fiscal period, aggregate reimbursement produced by the methods proves inadequate or excessive; the Secretary permissibly construes that command as reconciliation of interim reimbursement to the amount payable under lawful reasonable-cost methods and limits. The clause does not require an individualized exception whenever actual costs exceed a ceiling, and authorized exceptions and independent challenges to unlawful methods remain intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 467 U.S. 837: traditional tools followed by permissible construction of remaining ambiguity applied.
- Bowen v. Georgetown University Hospital, 488 U.S. 204: unauthorized retroactive rulemaking distinguished; its holding does not decide this clause’s meaning.
- Connecticut National Bank v. Germain, March 9, 1992: text/coexistence principle preserved; the dispute concerns what these provisions require, not implied repeal.

##### A changed agency interpretation is not automatically disqualified

**Holding:** An agency’s change of statutory interpretation may reduce the weight accorded its view, but change alone does not make a considered, otherwise permissible construction unlawful. The Court must examine the text, delegated setting and reasons; an unsupported litigation position cannot substitute for the legally required agency explanation or obtain automatic controlling weight.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas (6 Justices). Direct majority support at this level of generality; no Marks synthesis.

##### States cannot add congressional qualifications through the ordinary ballot

**Holding:** A State may not deny an otherwise constitutionally qualified congressional candidate ordinary ballot access because of prior congressional service when that restriction adds a substantive personal qualification. The exclusive Article I qualifications cannot be supplemented indirectly through the ballot; genuine time, place and manner administration remains distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: six direct votes, in both consolidated dockets.

A remaining write-in opportunity does not save this substantive service-based exclusion. Neutral filing, petition, support and ballot-management requirements and separate state-office provisions remain outside the holding.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Storer, 415 U.S. 724: distinguishes genuine electoral administration from added substantive qualifications; ordinary neutral election rules are preserved.
- Gregory, 501 U.S. 452: distinguishes state control of its own officers from qualification of federal representatives; its actual state-officer protection remains intact.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the Arkansas Supreme Court in Nos. 93-1456 and 93-1828. Section 3 of Amendment 73's ordinary-ballot exclusions after three House terms or two Senate terms remain unenforceable; no state-office term limit or separate redistricting remedy is adjudicated.

## Source: state/holdings/constitutional-structure.md — Lunding v. New York

#### Lunding v. New York Tax Appeals Tribunal

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1462.

**Decided:** 1998-01-21.

##### Article IV does not permit this inadequately justified categorical exclusion

**Holding:** When a State taxes another State's citizens on income earned within its borders, a discriminatory deduction rule must rest on a substantial reason beyond noncitizenship, and the discrimination must bear a close relationship to that reason. Section 631(b)(6)'s categorical exclusion of qualifying alimony from deductions against New York-source income fails that inquiry on the source, personal-expense, initial-computation and family-income justifications offered here; genuine source allocation remains permissible, and identical resident and nonresident tax codes are not required.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter and Breyer; seven Justices.

**Material application:** The complete formula matters: alimony reduces hypothetical resident liability and the income denominator but is denied in the New York-source numerator. Neither personal-expense labeling, the narrower nonresident tax base, a mismatched family-income justification nor another State’s tax choices cures the discrimination.

##### Relief follows the established tax-remedy predicates

**Holding:** The unconstitutional treatment of the challenged assessment requires reversal and remand for lawful state allocation, severability and constitutionally adequate relief. When the State compelled payment before meaningful review, it must provide adequate backward-looking correction; independently, a clearly held-out postpayment remedy cannot be withdrawn after payment, including voluntary payment, subject to ordinary proof and lawful previously announced conditions.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same seven Justices join Thomas's remedial holding in full.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual payment, preservation, amount and lawful announced procedural conditions remain to be established. State allocation and severability choices remain, including lawful refunds, permissible nondiscriminatory retrospective equalization or a lawful combination when correction is required.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No Equal Protection or Commerce Clause claim, federal alimony-qualification dispute, hypothetical recipient-based alternative or duty to apportion every personal expense is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reversed and remanded, 7–2, for lawful allocation and constitutionally adequate relief, without a fixed refund, interest award, exclusive replacement formula or reopening of unrelated final tax years.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Toomer is applied for substantial equality, a substantial independent reason and the close relationship required between that reason and the discriminatory treatment.
- Shaffer retains nondiscriminatory source taxation and justified corresponding deduction limits; it supplies no categorical exception for every deduction labeled personal.
- Travis is applied to distinguish legitimate source limits from inadequately justified unequal personal allowances. Its rejection of presumed offsets remains operative.
- Austin is applied to reject a cure dependent on another State's tax choices; permissible cooperative arrangements remain available.
- Goodwin's reported home-State property and medical-expense setting is distinguished; that different deduction setting does not resolve this alimony provision.
- Friedsam's earlier statutory construction was superseded by § 631(b)(6); it is not revived as the constitutional rule or imposed as the exclusive replacement formula.
- Fulton and Oregon Waste supply analogical support concerning actual tax operation and unsupported offset explanations. Their Commerce Clause tests and Fulton's limited treatment of Darnell remain confined to their fields.

## Standard source: Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure

### Individual statutory rights and congressional foreclosure under §1983

**Governing question:** When an asserted statutory obligation creates an individual right enforceable through §1983.

**Current rule:** The claimant must identify a particular provision intended to benefit that claimant, imposing a binding obligation and defining the individual entitlement sufficiently for judicial enforcement rather than vaguely or amorphously. Text, statutory setting and the asserted entitlement govern; a beneficial statute or mandatory administrative duty alone does not establish a right. An identified federal right is presumptively enforceable under §1983 unless Congress expressly excludes that channel or supplies a sufficiently comprehensive remedial scheme incompatible with it. Right creation and remedial foreclosure remain separate inquiries. Title IX’s individual right against sex discrimination in a covered educational program is not categorically excluded from §1983 enforcement by its administrative funding process or established implied action. The claimant must prove the actual statutory violation, including defendant coverage and recipient-responsibility conditions; the enforcement vehicle enlarges no duty. Title IX also does not displace a §1983 Equal Protection claim independently secured by the Constitution merely because the same school harassment supplies both theories; purposeful constitutional discrimination, state action, defendant-specific or municipal responsibility, causation, remedies and actual preclusion remain separate.

**Authority by component:**

- Blessing v. Freestone — General federal oversight does not categorically displace §1983, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 3 supplies presumptive enforcement and congressional foreclosure
- Blessing v. Freestone — State-plan placement neither defeats nor creates a right, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 4 supplies the §§1320a-2 and 1320a-10 construction
- Blessing v. Freestone — Any remedy must follow the particular proved right and violation, 1997-04-21; unanimous holding 6 supplies the right-and-violation limit on relief

- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “1. Independent constitutional claims remain available”, 1999-06-03; Thomas, all nine Justices. Supplies independent constitutional enforcement.
- Bruneau v. South Kortright Central School District — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “2. The identified statutory right is not categorically foreclosed under section 1983”, 1999-06-03; Thomas with Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. **Present operation effective:** Bruneau, June 3, 1999, for Title IX’s statutory and independent constitutional enforcement relationships; earlier individual-right and actual-foreclosure components retain their dates.

**Controlling construction:** Sections 1320a-2 and 1320a-10 forbid denying enforceability solely because a Social Security Act provision appears in state-plan requirements; they neither create every asserted right nor disturb the preserved result that §671(a)(15) is not privately enforceable. ## Standard source: Private enforcement of Title VI disparate-impact regulations

### Private enforcement of Title VI disparate-impact regulations

**Governing question:** Private prospective enforcement of valid Title VI implementing regulations and the separate section 1983 route.

**Current rule:** Title VI's established private action permits statutory beneficiaries to obtain prospective relief against a covered recipient for violations of valid, sufficiently definite section 602 regulations implementing the statute's protection against discrimination, including valid effects regulations. The source of the action is congressional authorization in Title VI as a whole. A regulation cannot independently create a private action under an otherwise remediless statute. Plaintiffs must establish the actual regulatory elements, coverage and fitted relief; statistical imbalance alone is not enough.

Separately, Powell's section 1983 holding remains operative: section 601's intentional-discrimination protection and section 602's agency directions do not create the broader individual effects right asserted through section 1983. A regulation can elaborate a statutory entitlement but cannot itself supply a missing broader statutory right. That holding concerns right creation before congressional foreclosure and is not displaced by Title VI's direct prospective action.

## Standard source: Equal welfare treatment of bona fide new state citizens

### Equal welfare treatment of bona fide new state citizens

**Governing question:** Whether ordinary welfare benefits may be reduced solely because a citizen recently arrived from another State.

**Current rule:** A United States citizen who establishes bona fide residence becomes a citizen of the State on equal terms. The Fourteenth Amendment’s Citizenship and Privileges or Immunities provisions forbid lowering an otherwise eligible current citizen’s ordinary welfare benefits solely because of recent arrival or the former State’s benefit amount, whether or not that person previously received assistance or moved to obtain it. Congress cannot authorize violation of that equal-citizenship floor through permission to use prior-State benefit levels.

**Present operation effective:** Saenz, May 17, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Bona fide residence verification and genuinely equal prospective eligibility rules remain available. ## Standard source: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. ## Standard source: Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

### Write-in voting within the complete ballot-access system

**Governing question:** Whether a ban on write-in voting violates electoral-choice or independent expressive rights.

**Current rule:** Under the existing Anderson inquiry, the court assesses the character and magnitude of the actual injury against the State's precise interests and the extent to which those interests require the burden. Severe burdens require narrow tailoring to a compelling interest; reasonable, nondiscriminatory burdens ordinarily may be justified by important regulatory interests. When the complete access system imposes only reasonable burdens, a write-in prohibition is presumptively valid and may be sustained by proportionate interests, including primary finality and prevention of evasive candidacies or party raiding. The ballot need not provide an official count and publication of votes for fictional figures or arbitrary protest messages as an independent expressive entitlement.

**Authority by component:**

- Arbitrary-message claim: Burdick, “3. The ballot need not serve as an official tally of arbitrary political messages,” June 8, 1992; same controlling five-Justice opinion, with all nine agreeing in rejection of that component.

**Present operation effective:** Burdick v. Takushi, June 8, 1992, for the write-in presumption and expressive boundary; the underlying Anderson framework is applied unchanged.

**Exceptions and limits:** No absolute validation of all write-in bans follows. Materially more restrictive access systems may impose severe burdens. The rule does not replace strict scrutiny of content-based campaign restrictions on public sidewalks.

## Event

TRW Inc. v. Andrews, No. 00-1045. Argued October 9, 2001; decided November 13, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, which reversed partial summary judgment treating the first two disputed credit disclosures as untimely. The question is whether the then-operative Fair Credit Reporting Act supplies a general discovery extension beyond its express misrepresentation exception.

## Participation

All nine Justices participated.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the Ninth Circuit's general-discovery ruling and remands for further proceedings consistent with the statutory construction.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The first two disclosure claims cannot proceed on the rejected general discovery theory. The mandate supplies no entitlement to reopen the newly presented actual-damages theory, amend to allege misrepresentation, or obtain an accrual or exception hearing. Any request in further proceedings remains subject to the mandate and ordinary preservation, waiver and procedural rules. Independent judgments outside this review retain their force.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Breyer | Supports the judgment | Entire confined statutory opinion |

## Holdings

**No general discovery extension under the governing FCRA.** The version of 15 U.S.C. §1681p governing these claims requires suit within two years from when liability arises. Its express extension permits suit within two years after discovery only when the defendant materially and willfully misrepresented information the Act required it to disclose to the individual and that information was material to establishing that defendant's liability to the individual; ignorance of a violation alone satisfies neither those conditions nor an additional general exception.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer, controls this statutory construction, the exception's application and the limited nonreach below. Scalia and Thomas agree with the judgment on their separately stated ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the specified discovery exception must retain its restrictive operation. A general discovery extension would allow a claimant to bypass its conditions and make it largely redundant. Andrus supports giving an enumerated exception its limiting effect; Menasche requires the enacted parts to perform their assigned work. The words identifying when liability arises do not transform liability into discovered liability. The practical difficulty of detecting identity misuse therefore cannot supply an exception Congress did not enact. Holmberg's fraud and concealment rule and Urie's latent-disease setting do not establish a universal discovery presumption overriding this text. Rotella and Klehr retain their distinct RICO questions and qualifications; they do not rewrite the FCRA. Andrews's complaint does not allege the material and willful misrepresentation of required information specified by §1681p, so that express extension supplies no basis for these claims.

**Precedent treatment:** Holmberg and Urie are distinguished at their stated scopes; Andrus and Menasche are applied to the statutory structure; the distinct Rotella and Klehr rules are preserved.

**Question left undecided.** The Court does not decide Andrews's argument that liability first arose upon actual loss rather than disclosure, because that distinct argument was first presented here and was not raised or briefed below. This nonreach does not resolve the substantive requirements of every willful claim, establish the asserted loss date, or authorize reopening the theory on remand. The confined statutory ruling corrects the general-discovery judgment without deciding a universal federal accrual rule.

## Precedent Treatment

- Andrus v. Glover Construction Co., 446 U.S. 608 (1980): applied for the significance of an expressly enumerated exception.
- United States v. Menasche, 348 U.S. 528 (1955): applied to preserve operative work for the exception's enacted conditions.
- Holmberg v. Armbrecht, 327 U.S. 392 (1946): its fraud/concealment rule is preserved; it does not establish the universal discovery rule urged here.
- Urie v. Thompson, 337 U.S. 163 (1949): its latent-disease setting does not govern this express FCRA structure.
- Rotella v. Wood (February 23, 2000) and Klehr v. A. O. Smith Corp. (June 19, 1997): their distinct RICO accrual, new-injury, unprovable-damage and concealment components remain at their actual scope.

## Law After Decision

The governing version of §1681p has no general discovery extension. Its precise statutory misrepresentation exception remains available when its conditions are met. The Court's ruling does not decide the first-presented actual-loss theory or create a universal accrual rule for federal statutes.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** Stone-Zsela joins the Court's statutory ruling and judgment. He adds that liability arises when the elements necessary for the particular statutory claim exist: rejection of general discovery does not erase the need for an element-specific inquiry or an independently applicable preserved exception. Rotella rejects substitution of a later discovery event for the governing accrual inquiry, but does not make disclosure, discovery and actionable injury interchangeable. Properly preserved questions remain subject to ordinary adjudication. The newly presented theory receives no affirmative permission to reopen merely because the Court leaves it undecided.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** They begin with the traditional rule that a limitations period ordinarily runs when the cause of action is complete and enforceable, rather than when the claimant learns of it. A general discovery presumption does not follow from Congress's failure expressly to reject discovery. Congress can prescribe a different start and did enact a confined exception here. Traditional fraud/concealment protection and the latent-injury setting remain separate rules. They object that leaving the general background uncertain invites uncertainty under other statutes. Their agreement with reversal and remand does not establish that every disclosure completes every FCRA claim or decide the newly raised actual-loss theory.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for proceedings consistent with rejection of the general discovery theory, subject to the stated mandate and ordinary procedural limits. The independent dispositions concerning other claims remain intact.

## Holdings

**IGRA's reporting and withholding direction does not confer the claimed substantive tax exemption.** For Indian gaming conducted under IGRA or an effective Tribal-State compact entered under §2710(d)(3), §2719(d)(1) directs state-equivalent treatment of the Code provisions concerning reporting and withholding of taxes on gaming or wagering winnings. **Authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for the complete construction and both affirmances.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the operative description and its examples together. National Bank of Oregon permits the complete enactment to establish meaning rather than making an isolated textual feature decisive. Here the parenthetical illustrates the class defined by the reporting-and-withholding words; it does not create a second, broader class. The tribes' strongest objection is that this reading deprives the express chapter reference of its apparent substantive work. That is a real textual difficulty, but an inconsistent example cannot expand the enacted subject it purports to illustrate. State-equivalent treatment still governs the reporting and withholding provisions within the defined class. This conclusion rests on text and context, not a claimed power to delete any inconvenient reference, a fabricated drafting sequence, or hostility to protective tribal canons. The state-tax and treaty rules of Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation and Mille Lacs do not supply an independent federal tax exemption.

**Precedent treatment:** National Bank of Oregon is applied for contextual construction; the distinct state-tax and tribal-instrument precedents remain confined to their own predicates. Chapter 35's independent definitions, exclusions and liability-linked provisions remain operative; today's construction rejects the asserted IGRA exemption rather than erasing those provisions.

## Event

J. E. M. Ag Supply, Inc. v. Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc., No. 99-1996. Merits decision, December 10, 2001. Argued October 3, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Federal Circuit. Pioneer asserted utility patents concerning corn varieties against unauthorized seed sellers. The question is whether the Plant Patent Act and Plant Variety Protection Act categorically exclude newly developed plants from utility-patent protection under §101. The accepted defense concerns statutory eligibility and exclusivity, with patent-specific and transaction-specific issues remaining distinct.

## Participation

## Public Action

Affirm, 7–2.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Affirmed, 7–2.** Supporting: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Opposing: Stevens and Breyer. The categorical ineligibility defense is rejected. Patent litigation may continue under the independent requirements of the protection actually invoked.

| Dissent | Stevens | Breyer | Would reverse and remand | Specific plant-regime exclusivity and preservation of seed and research protections |

## Holdings

### The plant statutes permit concurrent utility-patent protection

**Holding and operative rule:** Newly developed plants, including the corn varieties at issue, are not categorically excluded from 35 U.S.C. §101 because the Plant Patent Act or Plant Variety Protection Act also covers the subject. A claimant invoking utility protection must meet that statute's independent requirements; certificate-specific exceptions govern their own statutory right and do not create a general immunity from a valid utility patent.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; seven direct joins support the complete eligibility and coexistence rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's plant-specific protections can operate alongside the broadly expressed utility-patent categories. Chakrabarty rejects a categorical living-subject exclusion and gives effect to human invention within the statutory language. The Plant Patent Act's limited reproduction right and relaxed description requirement address obstacles to protection without declaring that a plant satisfying the more demanding general statute loses that protection. The Plant Variety Protection Act likewise supplies a distinct right with distinct requirements and exceptions.

The contrary argument has practical force: utility rights can restrict seed saving or research that the certificate regime permits. But different rights and eligibility requirements are not an irreconcilable statutory conflict. Neither plant statute expressly makes its protection exclusive, and enactment or recodification does not require exclusion from §101. Each statute retains work; a court may not create a hybrid by importing certificate exceptions into utility-patent law.

Asgrow construes the former certificate saved-seed provision. It does not answer utility-patent exclusivity or supply a universal agricultural exemption. Its former-law limits and the enacted transition remain effective within the certificate regime. Administrative practice and legislative silence are not treated as binding interpretations. The present category-wide answer leaves novelty, nonobviousness, disclosure and other patent requirements independently operative; it establishes neither validity of every asserted claim nor enforceability of every sales restriction.

**Precedent treatment:** Chakrabarty's broad statutory reading is applied; Asgrow remains confined to its certificate setting; Deuel and State Street's independent requirements and limits remain intact.

### Certificate protections retain their own statutory version and exceptions

**Holding and operative rule:** Concurrent utility eligibility does not rewrite the protection supplied by a plant-variety certificate. Former-law saved-seed protection and the amendments effective April 4, 1995 retain their enacted conditions and transition; no specific certificate, seed transaction or exception entitlement is adjudicated here.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Opinion of the Court preserves these boundaries as part of the coexistence rule.

**Controlling explanation:** Under Asgrow, former §2543 permits reproductive sale only of seed genuinely saved for the farmer's own planting, produced from seed obtained with the owner's authority or its descendants. Seller and purchaser must primarily farm for nonreproductive crop sales, and the sale must comply with state seed-sale law. Changed planting plans may permit disposition of genuinely retained seed; intentional multiplication for marketing is not exempt. The independent protection for bona fide nonreproductive sales in customary channels remains; a buyer who diverts seed sold through those channels to planting is deemed to have section 2567 notice that the diversion infringes. Section 2544's exemption for use and reproduction for plant breeding or other bona fide research likewise belongs to that certificate regime.

The 1994 amendments delete the former farmer-sales proviso for the amended regime from April 4, 1995. Section 14(a), subject to its express exceptions, preserves former law for earlier certificates and then-pending applications. Section 14(b) permits refiling under amended terms while retaining the original filing date for §42. Section 14(c) requires identification of amended protection in a notice claiming it and retains sanctions for false or misleading claims or labels. These rules continue to govern the certificate right; none is converted into an exception to a separate utility patent. The Court makes no transaction-specific exception or transition finding.

**Precedent treatment:** Asgrow's saved-seed construction and former-law force are preserved; the admitted amendment's express transition remains unchanged.

## Precedent Treatment

- Diamond v. Chakrabarty, 447 U.S. 303: its broad reading of §101 applies to qualifying human invention rather than a categorical exclusion of living subject matter.
- Asgrow Seed Co. v. Winterboer, No. 92-2038: its former certificate saved-seed construction and transition limits remain; no utility-patent exception is derived from it.
- In re Deuel, January 14, 1997: ordinary supported nonobviousness requirements remain distinct from category eligibility.
- State Street Bank & Trust Co. v. Signature Financial Group, Inc., January 14, 1999: broad category language does not remove genuine statutory limitations or independent proof requirements.

## Law After Decision

Plants meeting the utility statute's requirements may receive utility patents alongside the plant-specific regimes. Each right carries its own requirements and exceptions. The certificate amendments and Asgrow's surviving former-law operation retain their actual reach; the decision supplies no generalized saved-seed or research exemption from utility-patent rights.

## Separate Writings

Scalia concurs and joins the Court in full. Chakrabarty already authoritatively construed the general subject-matter language to reject the supposed ambiguity based on living subject matter. The plant-specific statutes therefore cannot merely be used to clarify that language into the opposite categorical exclusion; a claimed change must satisfy the more demanding inquiry into displacement of the existing rule. This canon explanation supplements rather than narrows the Court's eligibility and coexistence rationale.

Stevens dissents, joined by Breyer, and would treat the plant-specific enactments as the exclusive protection for their covered plant categories. The 1930 Act expressly amended the general patent provision, conditioned protection on asexual reproduction and limited the reproduction right; the later variety statute supplied seed-reproduction protection with saved-seed and research limits. In their view, the utility route bypasses those particular congressional choices, and recodification did not undo them. Different eligibility thresholds do not explain why increased originality should eliminate protections Congress deliberately conferred on agricultural users. Chakrabarty's human-made microorganism lay outside those specialized categories, so the dissent preserves its holding and does not exclude all living inventions or independently qualifying processes. The dissent would reverse on the category defense.

## Procedure After Action

The Federal Circuit's rejection of the categorical defense stands. Remaining patent-specific validity, exhaustion, infringement, competition and contract issues proceed only within their ordinary preserved scope; this decision resolves none of them by the eligibility answer alone.

## Event

Schrader v. Blackwell. Merits decision, December 10, 2001. Review of the Sixth Circuit judgment reported at 241 F.3d 783, reversing relief for the requested Libertarian ballot designation. Schrader qualified for the 1998 congressional ballot through independent nomination; the party had actually nominated him but lacked state recognition. A preliminary injunction permitted the label for that election. The party later regained recognition. The question is whether the First and Fourteenth Amendments permit Ohio to condition the official party designation on recognized-party status. The continuing party plaintiff, recurring recognition requirement and short election cycle keep the dispute reviewable; the earlier filing-deadline objection is outside review.

## Judgment & Remedy

## Holdings

### The recurring controversy remains reviewable

**Holding and operative rule:** Completion of the election and the party's later recognition do not end this challenge to a recognition requirement capable of recurring against the continuing party plaintiff in elections too short for full review. The Court reaches the ballot-designation dispute under the capable-of-repetition exception.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join this threshold proposition in the Opinion of the Court.

**Controlling explanation:** The ongoing party is subject to a periodic recognition requirement and has previously lost and regained recognition. The same challenged designation restriction can therefore recur during an election cycle that ends before full judicial review. Review does not require a new inquiry merely because the 1998 ballot is complete. The earlier filing-deadline objection is outside the accepted issue.

**Precedent treatment:** Timmons's preservation of review in a continuing nomination dispute is applied consistently with its distinct conditional-enactment facts.

### Ohio may retain the official nomination designation within its reasonable recognition system

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Anderson-Burdick, Ohio may condition this requested official party designation on compliance with its neutral party-recognition system where the complete nomination scheme leaves meaningful independent ballot access and opportunities for endorsement and campaigning. The actual burden is less than severe, and coherent nomination categories and reasonable preventive administration proportionately justify this application; empirical proof of prior disruption is not indispensable.

**Authority:** The Opinion of the Court on the merits, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct joins.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that support sufficient to place a congressional candidate on the ballot does not itself establish the separate statewide party status represented by Ohio's official nomination cue. Anderson and Burdick require attention to the actual burden and the fit of the State's interests, rather than treating the designation as immune from review. Timmons permits proportionate regulation of official nomination machinery within a system that leaves meaningful political participation.

Schrader's verified nomination and affiliation make his associational interest real. They do not compel Ohio to redesign its official designation to distinguish affiliation from recognized-party nomination whenever an alternative wording is feasible. Independent access and campaigning reduce the burden; they do not make it disappear. The State may use reasonable preventive categories without waiting for actual disruption.

Norman protected authorized geographic party expansion and independently qualified candidates from disqualification. This candidate remains on the ballot through a functioning nomination route, and the different party-recognition designation serves a distinct purpose. Tashjian and Eu's associational protection does not settle every official ballot format. The Court adopts neither pure rational-basis review nor a presumption validating every affiliation prohibition. More restrictive access systems and unsupported burdens remain subject to the governing calibrated inquiry.

**Precedent treatment:** Anderson and Burdick supply the calibrated framework; Timmons's nomination-system reasoning is applied to this distinct designation burden; Norman is distinguished by the preserved candidate access and different state-status cue; Tashjian and Eu retain their associational protections.

## Precedent Treatment

- Anderson v. Celebrezze, 460 U.S. 780, and Burdick v. Takushi, 504 U.S. 428: the actual burden, precise interests and proportional fit remain controlling; no write-in presumption is transferred to labels.
- Timmons v. Twin Cities Area New Party, No. 95-1608: its contextual nomination-system reasoning supports this bounded application, without universal approval of recognition restrictions.
- Norman v. Reed, 502 U.S. 279: authorized expansion and independently qualified slate access remain protected; the present official status designation is distinguished.
- Tashjian v. Republican Party of Connecticut, 479 U.S. 208, and Eu v. San Francisco County Democratic Central Committee, 489 U.S. 214: associational protection remains, without compelling every requested ballot format.

## Law After Decision

The official party designation may remain linked to Ohio's recognition system in these circumstances. The holding applies burden-and-fit review to a designation restriction; it supplies no categorical approval of every recognition threshold or prohibition of truthful affiliation.

## Separate Writings

Stevens dissents, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg, and would distinguish verified affiliation from the privileges and official representation of recognized-party nomination. Because the candidate has independently qualified and the party actually nominated him, the State must explain why every accurately qualified affiliation cue must be suppressed. Anderson-Burdick requires that fit even when the burden is less than severe; campaign speech does not eliminate the distinct ballot burden. Norman, Tashjian and Eu give weight to the real association and supported standard bearer. Timmons concerned fusion nomination and does not decide this separate informational burden. The dissent would permit the State to verify affiliation and choose accurate wording, and would recognize demonstrated administrative or effective-governing interests rather than impose a universal preferred-label right. It would remand for bounded prospective relief, preserving party-recognition thresholds and leaving the completed election intact.

## Procedure After Action

The reviewed Sixth Circuit judgment stands. The completed election remains intact, and this decision confers no additional recognized-party privileges.
