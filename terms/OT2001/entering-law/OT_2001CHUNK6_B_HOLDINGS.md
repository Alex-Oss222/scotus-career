#### United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit Nos. 95-10479 and 95-10513; district cases CR-95-00009-PGR and CR-95-00010-1-PGR. No separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied.

**Decided:** June 30, 1997.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** The Court affirms the Ninth Circuit reversal of each separate indictment dismissal; neither disposition enters a conviction.

For the domestic-relations limitation in Mussari/Schroeder, June 30, 1997, Ninth Circuit Nos. 95-10479 and 95-10513, see Federal Courts — Domestic-relations boundaries. For post-enactment conduct and conditional restitution, see Criminal Procedure — Interstate support offenses and temporal limits.

##### Whether Congress may punish willful nonpayment of an existing interstate child-support obligation

**Holding:** Congress may punish willful post-enactment failure to pay a legally fixed past-due child-support obligation when the obligor and child reside in different States, because §228 directly protects that cross-border economic payment obligation. The different-state relation is essential to this holding and performs its constitutional work; the holding does not rest on family-policy importance, social costs or generalized economic consequences, and the Court sustains neither regulation of every family debt nor federal creation or revision of the underlying support duty.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts I–IV, unanimous.

**Material application:** Section 228 requires an amount fixed by court order or state administrative process for a child, or child and custodial parent, unpaid longer than one year or exceeding $5,000. Amount and duration are alternative thresholds, neither substituting for different-state residence. The regulated object is the legally owed cross-border payment, without requiring a completed transfer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No flight, travel, voluntary crossing, bank wire, particular instrument, evasion motive or ability finding is added. This is not a rule for all family debt or incidental geographic connections.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Lopez's connection-before-magnitude architecture is applied through a concrete statutory mechanism, not a general interstate-obligation category detached from Lopez; its rejection of remote effects chains and prohibition on supplying missing elements are retained. Harris and Bishop/Stokes retain their direct-instrumentality ground and enacted nexus; Hale retains its anti-evasion/material-impairment ground. None creates an automatic commerce label for every obligation.

##### Whether direct regulation of private obligors commandeers a State

**Holding:** Section 228 commands the private obligor, not a State government, and therefore does not compel state legislation, enforcement, investigation, order revision or assumption of the obligor's liability. Federal power still requires the independent interstate-payment ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts I–IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The existing operative support order remains a predicate; federal tribunals may not create or revise the underlying duty or domestic status.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** New York preserves the distinction between direct private regulation and compulsory state responsibility. Printz/Mack's qualified executive-assistance rule and fiscal mandate concern different implementing means and supply neither commerce power nor a compensation rule here.

#### United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit Nos. 95-10479 and 95-10513; district cases CR-95-00009-PGR and CR-95-00010-1-PGR. No separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied.

**Decided:** June 30, 1997.

For the interstate-payment power and private-regulation holdings in Mussari/Schroeder, June 30, 1997, Ninth Circuit Nos. 95-10479 and 95-10513, see Constitutional Structure — Interstate support-payment obligations. For the domestic-relations boundary, see Federal Courts — Domestic-relations boundaries.

##### Whether conviction may rest on failures before §228's enactment

**Holding:** Federal conviction and punishment must rest on willful failures after §228's October 25, 1992 enactment, with every applicable element proved beyond a reasonable doubt. Earlier arrears may establish lawful background or past-due status, but a completed pre-enactment failure cannot itself constitute the federal offense.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts I–IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Willfulness, capacity to pay, qualifying amount or duration, necessary interstate residence and sentencing facts remain unproved. Lawful ability-related and other defenses survive. No comprehensive continuing-offense theory dispenses with separate proof or notice questions.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The Ex Post Facto Clause supplies the independent criminal prohibition. Landgraf and Rivers reinforce attention to consequences of completed conduct but retain their separate civil scope.

##### What each trial remand and any later restitution determination permit

**Holding:** The Ninth Circuit's judgment reversing each district-court indictment dismissal and remanding each prosecution is affirmed, subject to the post-enactment limit and ordinary proof requirements. Section 228(c) requires restitution under §3663 upon conviction equal to the past-due obligation existing at sentencing; this pretrial decision fixes no amount and does not decide the precise lawful treatment of older arrears in such an order.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts I–IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A first offense permits a fine, up to six months' imprisonment, or both; a subsequent offense permits a fine, up to two years, or both. No prior offense is found. The offense-date limit neither categorically excludes older arrears from eventual restitution nor automatically permits them; that statutory and constitutional issue remains unresolved.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Both indictment dismissals remain reversed and ordinary prosecution may proceed within existing charges and proof limits. No new allegations, conviction, sentence, restitution award, state-order revision or compulsory state administrative act is ordered.

#### United States v. Mussari; United States v. Schroeder

**Docket or dockets:** Ninth Circuit Nos. 95-10479 and 95-10513; district cases CR-95-00009-PGR and CR-95-00010-1-PGR. No separate Supreme Court docket number is supplied.

**Decided:** June 30, 1997.

For the offense-date and restitution limits, see Criminal Procedure — Interstate support offenses and temporal limits.

##### Whether the domestic-relations exception bars these federal criminal prosecutions

**Holding:** The limited domestic-relations exception does not bar §228 prosecutions enforcing an existing operative support order. Federal courts may decide offense elements and lawful defenses but may not create or modify support, custody, parentage or domestic status.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (O'Connor), Parts I–IV, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A cognizable challenge to the existence or continued operation of the predicate order is preserved. Hypothetical interference with a different state proceeding is not decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Ankenbrandt confines the statutory diversity exception to issuing divorce, alimony and child-custody decrees; it creates no general Article III exclusion of family-related disputes.

#### Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-1263.

**Decided:** December 10, 1996.

##### The erroneous refusal to remand remains error

**Holding:** Complete diversity was absent when Caterpillar removed, and Lewis's timely motion required remand under the applicable removal and remand statutes. The later dismissal of the nondiverse party does not make the earlier ruling correct, waive Lewis's preserved objection, or require him to have sought extraordinary pretrial relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No collateral challenge to an entered covered remand is authorized. No late alternative removal argument or independent fee or sanction entitlement is decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Things Remembered and Quackenbush retain the mandatory-remand and covered-remand-review rules at their proper stages. This case concerns an erroneous refusal to remand followed by trial, not review of an actually entered remand protected by section 1447(d).

##### Actual jurisdiction before trial preserves the completed judgment against this earlier defect

**Holding:** When complete diversity actually arises through the nondiverse party's lawful dismissal before trial and persists through judgment, an earlier erroneous refusal of a timely remand motion does not by itself require vacating the completed federal judgment. The rule depends on actual section 1332 jurisdiction during trial and judgment; consent, finality or efficiency cannot cure a judgment entered without that jurisdiction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal cure for every removal defect follows. The decision authorizes no collusive manufacture of diversity, finds no manipulation, and does not extinguish a separately authorized fee or sanction request.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Finn's defect persisting through judgment is distinguished from actual diversity before trial; its bar on jurisdiction by consent survives. Grubbs's completed-judgment distinction is expressly extended beyond its unobjected-removal facts to this preserved objection and actual pretrial cure. Newman-Green retains lawful dismissal, dispensability and prejudice limits without authorizing manufactured trial jurisdiction. Willy preserves independently authorized collateral consequences but supplies no merits jurisdiction or cure for an uncured trial.

##### Restoration against jurisdictional vacatur leaves the ordinary appeal for decision

**Holding:** The Sixth Circuit's jurisdiction-based vacatur and state-court-remand direction are reversed; the district-court judgment is restored against that jurisdictional ground, subject to the unresolved ordinary appeal. The case returns to the Sixth Circuit to decide the five preserved issues it did not reach: prior-accident discovery restrictions, the discovery-sanction request, excluded exhibits, excluded witnesses and the omitted failure-to-warn instruction.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by all seven other Associates; eight Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only with this remand disposition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not finally affirm the defense verdict against the five remaining challenges or decide any of their merits.

#### Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-1971.

**Decided:** 1998-02-24.

##### Federal claim preclusion remains a defense

**Holding:** Under 28 U.S.C. §§1331 and 1441, a prior federal judgment's claim-preclusive effect is a defense and does not transform a complaint presenting only state-law claims into an action arising under federal law. Complete statutory preemption differs: Congress must displace the state claim with the exclusive federal cause that supplies its legal character; the completeness of an asserted judgment defense does not perform that work.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Louisiana mortgage claims may be defeated by bankruptcy judgments without acquiring a federal character. The Court decides neither the mortgage rights nor the bankruptcy orders’ validity or actual preclusive effect; an otherwise available enforcement proceeding needs its own authority.

##### Dependent merits judgments must be vacated

**Holding:** Because this action lacks the asserted original federal jurisdiction and no later jurisdictional cure is established, the dependent federal merits judgments must be vacated and the whole action returned to state court. A different merits ground for one respondent does not cure the same jurisdictional defect.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Scalia), unanimous.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The entire action must return to state court. Vacatur includes the Bank and Fountainbleau Storage Associates preclusion judgments and the Browns’ differently reasoned failure-to-state-a-claim judgment. The state court may apply governing federal preclusion law.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Moitie's ordinary claim-preclusion rule is preserved; its footnote is not extended into a general removal exception for prior federal judgments.
- Peacock supplies the independent-authority requirement for a later action while preserving ordinary enforcement within its proper sphere.
- City of Chicago is distinguished because substantial federal claims appeared on the complaints' face there; supplemental jurisdiction has no comparable original anchor here.
- Caterpillar is distinguished because actual jurisdiction arose before trial there; finality alone cannot save this action's merits judgments.
- Matsushita preserves judgment recognition without equating that effect with adjudicatory power.
- Richards preserves constitutional limits on binding parties; it supplies no removal authority and no finding about these petitioners' binding status.
- Baker preserves the distinction among recognition, execution and new duties in another forum; that distinction creates no universal removal grant.

#### Hercules, Inc. v. United States, 516 U.S. 417

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-818.

**Decided:** March 4, 1996.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** Hercules and Thompson's distinct procurement contracts and consolidated appellate claims; both petitioners' specifications-warranty claims are decided, and Thompson alone has the preserved separate contextual-indemnity claim.

##### The necessary contractual source of a Tucker Act reimbursement claim

**Holding:** A §1491(a)(1) contract claim requires an express or implied-in-fact undertaking the Government was authorized to make. The Tucker Act supplies jurisdiction, not the substantive payment promise, and fairness alone cannot create an obligation implied only in law.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six Justices. Stevens does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Uncertain prospective breach damages do not invalidate an otherwise authorized warranty. No actual absence of an appropriation, delegation or required approval is found in these contracts.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sutton applies actual-authority limits to implied and express undertakings; Hatzlachh preserves genuine contract remedies despite the absence of tort relief, without supplying a missing promise.

##### Whether the specifications warranty alone covers these settlements and tort-defense expenses

**Holding:** The implication arising from government specifications assures satisfactory performance of the procurement undertaking; without more it does not promise reimbursement of these manufacturers' veterans' tort settlements or expenses defending those claims. Both petitioners' settlement claims and their separately considered defense-expense claims fail on that contractual-scope ground.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six Justices. Stevens does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This is not a rule ending all contractual damages at delivery, requiring express indemnity for every third-party loss or categorically barring such expenses. Different demonstrated authorized duties remain possible. The Federal Circuit's equation of warranty success with a successful government-contractor defense and resulting causation conclusion is unnecessary and undecided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Spearin retains its actual specifications undertaking and ordinary breach remedies. Ryan Stevedoring and Weyerhaeuser permit foreseeable third-party consequences of proved service duties without an express indemnity; those duties are not established merely by procurement specifications. Boyle's tort defense remains unchanged; Kania's distinct nonprosecution setting is not adopted as a universal expense bar.

##### Whether Thompson establishes indemnity through compulsory procurement and statutory protection

**Holding:** Thompson's combined reliance on DPA compulsion, specifications, alleged government control and superior knowledge and §707 protection does not establish the asserted authorized implied-in-fact promise to pay third-party settlements and defense costs. Section 707 itself supplies no affirmative reimbursement promise.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Souter), joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg; six Justices. Stevens does not participate.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 101's production command and §707's specified liability protection are distinct from governmental assent to reimbursement. Genuine authorized indemnities remain enforceable. The full §707 immunity boundary, defective specifications, relative knowledge, actual breach, contract-specific authority, causation and damage allocation are not determined. Hercules's allegations do not revive an unpreserved separate indemnity theory, and no independent superior-knowledge, purchaser-use, good-faith or takings cause is newly decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm both petitioners' warranty settlement and separate expense denials and Thompson's contextual indemnity denial, 6–2. Summary judgments remain; no damages inquiry, payment, further merits remand or contract-litigation fees are ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sutton's authority boundary and actual-contract limits govern; X-Citement Video and Lebron permit defense of the existing favorable judgment on an alternative ground without new factual findings or unpresented independent claims.

#### United States v. Winstar Corp., 518 U.S. 839 (1996)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 95-865; consolidated Glendale, Winstar and Statesman disputes.
**Decided:** July 1, 1996.

##### What actual undertaking and jurisdiction the contract claim requires

**Holding:** The Tucker Act, 28 U.S.C. §1491(a)(1), supplies contract jurisdiction but creates no substantive promise. A claimant must establish an actual authorized express or implied-in-fact undertaking, not an obligation imposed only for fairness; review of the certified interlocutory partial-liability judgment does not require a final damages award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Part I, unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court of Appeals found Winstar’s agreement express; the implied-in-fact alternative and takings claims are not independently decided.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Hercules supplies the actual-duty requirement; Romein distinguishes regulation from a contractual undertaking.

##### Whether the transaction writings promise the particular accounting and capital treatment

**Holding:** The integrated Glendale, Winstar and Statesman writings establish express promises of the specified accounting and capital treatment and allocate the financial risk of its withdrawal, subject to the actual contractual qualifications. Statesman’s capital-credit undertaking is distinct from its goodwill allowance; neither regulation alone nor general assistance policy creates the promises.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Parts II–IV and VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** The operative terms are distinct:

- **Glendale:** The November 19, 1981 Supervisory Action Agreement preserves contemporaneous Board/FSLIC resolutions and letters rather than superseding them. Resolution 81-710 requires a satisfactory independent accountant's opinion describing goodwill, justifying purchase accounting and substantiating amounts and amortization, with compliance with Memorandum R-31b. The approved accountant treatment allocates $18 million over twelve years and $716.666 million over forty years, straight line. The lower judgment found the accounting condition satisfied; the Court does not turn that finding into a concession or treat every dollar as forty-year goodwill.
- **Winstar:** The integrated agreement, Resolution 84-363 and July 13, 1984 letter permit straight-line amortization of purchase-accounting intangibles over a period not exceeding thirty-five years for regulatory reporting. The maximum is permission, not a required period or a promised damages term. Those accounting forbearances are not acceptable in financial statements required to follow GAAP. Ongoing reserve/net-worth records and regulatory reporting remain required. The Net Worth Maintenance obligation concerns the required capital level, while the agreement's accounting terms define what counts toward it; a change in level is not identical to withdrawal of a promised component.
- **Statesman goodwill:** The March 11, 1988 agreement and Resolution 88-169 permit straight-line amortization not exceeding twenty-five years, subject to a satisfactory accountant's analysis within ninety days describing intangibles and substantiating amounts and methods. The lower judgment found the condition satisfied. Approximately $25.8 million of acquisition goodwill is distinct from the capital credit.
- **Statesman capital credit:** The express $26 million credit consists of $21 million of the FSLIC contribution and $5 million debenture principal. It is credited to the regulatory capital account and constitutes regulatory capital under the identified Insurance Regulation §561.13 definition. Its amount and scope do not borrow the goodwill undertaking's twenty-five-year maximum.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No universal insurance against later regulation, perpetual exemption or finding of insolvency causation. The separate maximum periods are permissions, not mandatory amortization or damages periods.

##### How accounting hierarchy, regulatory reservations and termination terms qualify those promises

**Holding:** The incorporated specific benefits retain contractual significance alongside general assistance terms and ordinary regulatory evolution. The whole integrated agreement controls; neither its long accounting permission nor its shorter ordinary assistance term automatically erases the other, and no defined termination event is presumed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Parts II–IV and VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** The following actual terms qualify the identified undertakings and subsequent relief:

**Accounting hierarchy and reserved powers.** Winstar §10 begins with GAAP for going-concern savings-and-loan computations except where the agreement, applicable regulations or contemporaneous Board approval requires otherwise. Ambiguity is resolved consistently with the regulations and approval; when those conflict, the Board's transaction resolution or action controls. The section also includes subsequent Board/FASB clarification, interpretation and amendment, with Board requirements controlling conflicts with FASB. Both priority and change language remain operative.

The July 13 letter reserves statutory powers except enforcement of its enumerated waived requirements and their authorizing statutory provisions insofar as waived. The referenced benefits concern direct net-worth credit for the FSLIC cash contribution; the amortization just described; loan-discount recognition by a level-yield method assuming contractual payments for fifteen years and repayment of the balance at that point, or another level-yield method described in R memoranda or other accounting documents acceptable under GAAP; and dividend limits. The dividend waiver permits sixty, seventy and sixty percent of net income for fiscal years beginning July 1, 1985, 1986 and 1987 respectively, while forbidding dividends that reduce net worth below the level §563.13 requires for institutions insured at least twenty years. These ancillary terms identify the reservation's scope and add no separate damages claim. Powers outside those waivers remain reserved; the exception is neither an unconditional cancellation right nor surrender of Congress's authority.

Statesman §17 similarly preserves agreement/approval exceptions to GAAP, resolves ambiguity by the regulations and transaction approval, and gives that approval priority over conflicting regulations. Its changing-accounting language refers to Board changes, amendments, clarifications and interpretations, including the proposal at 52 Fed. Reg. 39112, its staff, FASB and the stated AICPA-successor reference; Board interpretation controls a conflict with FASB or AICPA. Real-estate valuation separately follows the proposed regulations at 52 Fed. Reg. 39070, those regulations as finally adopted by the Board, or any successor regulations then in effect. It is not simply Winstar's clause under another name.

**Compliance, duration and termination.** Glendale's assistance agreement ordinarily terminates after ten years, when FSLIC payment obligations cease. Appointment of a receiver or other official to liquidate Glendale, or termination of Glendale's or its successor's FSLIC-insured status, terminates the agreement and all FSLIC obligations at that event. Contemporaneous resolutions and letters remain preserved; modifications require written agreement by the parties or successors. The agreement requires no unlawful action or inaction. The particular forward accounting benefit is construed with, not equated to, the ordinary assistance/payment term.

Winstar §13(a) ordinarily ends the agreement after two years except as otherwise specifically provided, or at a different date agreed in writing. Appointment of a receiver to liquidate the resulting association, or cessation of its insured-account status, separately terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations at that event. Section 15 requires no unlawful action or inaction. Section 13(b) concerns limited continuing indemnity for the liabilities/claims and related or prosecution litigation expenses it identifies, conditioned on litigation at termination and continued observance of the agreement's conditions, covenants, cooperation and best-efforts duties, with recoveries payable over under its referenced limits. It is not a general continuation of every contractual claim; this judgment does not decide an interaction with a receiver event or find a qualifying indemnity claim.

Statesman §20 requires the acquiring association and its subsidiaries, affiliates and service corporations materially to comply with applicable public laws, regulations, orders and restrictions governing property and business, but does not itself require compliance while applicability is contested in good faith. The proviso establishes neither an actual contest nor an exemption from valid public law. Section 19 ordinarily supplies a five-year term or a different written agreed date, with its specific-exception language and separate §16 sale provision. Appointment of a receiver to liquidate the acquiring association or cessation of its insured-account status terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations. Its lawful-action term remains. FIRREA's enactment within the five-year period defeats only an assertion that ordinary expiration had already eliminated every undertaking; it decides neither other triggers nor recoverable duration.

Statesman §19(b) directs distribution/payment of the Special Reserve Account's net credit or debit at termination under the agreement. Section 19(c)'s identified liability/claim and related litigation indemnities require litigation at termination and continuing covenants, cooperation, reporting and best efforts; recoveries must be paid over under the applicable limitations, and continuation remains subject to the liquidation/insurance terminating proviso. Section 19(d) requires action on reports, applications, requests and other submissions actually received by termination that require FSLIC approval, consent or action, with cooperation and final-accounting treatment. None is a general survival clause or a finding of an eligible claim or pending submission in this case.

**Statesman's separate sale conditions.** During seven years, a defined Sale by the acquiring association of any of its own assets, or of capital stock of a subsidiary or affiliate formed to conduct its operations or hold its assets, requires prior written approval and prompt payment of an agreed percentage of net proceeds, capped at fifty percent. In calculating net proceeds, an appropriate return of the acquirer's investment is allocated in proportion to the assets sold relative to total assets. Sale aggregates sales consummated or agreed during ninety days with an aggregate sales price of $20 million. An unapproved defined Sale during five years terminates the agreement and FSLIC obligations immediately after consummation notwithstanding §19; approval lies in FSLIC's sole discretion. The exclusion concerns transfers to an acquirer affiliate of stock or assets and liabilities of Federated Financial, Combined Mortgage Corporation, Homestead Mortgage Corporation or Perpetual Financial Corporation, not every affiliate transaction. Seven-year approval/proceeds duties and the five-year termination consequence remain distinct. No such Sale, approval, excluded transfer or termination is found.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No general indemnity clause preserves every claim. No liquidation, insurance termination, defined Sale, qualifying transfer, contest, indemnity claim, pending submission or damages cutoff is found merely because its clause exists.

##### Whether actual assistance authority permits financial undertakings while preserving legislation

**Holding:** The transaction-effective contracting and assistance powers, exercised through the actual resolutions and approvals, authorize the identified financial undertakings. Separate authority to prevent Congress from changing law is unnecessary for ordinary monetary responsibility because these contracts confer no regulatory veto; general agency mission alone would not cure an unauthorized promise.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Parts II–IV and VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** Former §1725(c)(3) authorized FSLIC contracting and transaction-effective §1729(f) authorized conditional assistance. Glendale authority covered default prevention/restoration and merger or liability assumption; contributions and guarantees were limited to reasonably necessary liquidation savings unless continued operation was found essential to adequate community savings or home-financing services. Actual resolutions supplied the required determinations and approvals. Winstar and Statesman likewise depend on effective powers and approvals.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Congress may change capital law and institutions must obey it. No hypothetical appropriation/approval defect, equitable right to payment, regulatory injunction or inferred general assistance warranty.

##### Whether withdrawal breaches the promises and assumed risk defeats sovereign-acts discharge

**Holding:** Withdrawal of the promised treatment breaches these financial undertakings. Even assuming public and general sovereign action, sovereign-acts protection and ordinary impossibility do not discharge the change-of-law risk the contracts allocated to the Government; this sufficient ground preserves legislative authority and imposes financial responsibility within the actual bargain.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Parts II–IV and VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** FIRREA’s goodwill limits and OTS’s distinct §567.1(w) capital-credit treatment prevent the promised performance. The latter treatment is regulatory, not statutory wording expressly naming capital credits. Horowitz does not improve the sovereign’s position over a private promisor on assumed risk.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No majority selects a complete unmistakability framework: Souter/Stevens/O’Connor/Breyer’s limited inapplicability ground has four votes, Scalia/Kennedy/Thomas’s application-and-satisfaction ground three, and Stone-Zsela supports the narrower actual risk allocation. The substantial-contractual-effect sovereign-acts alternative has Souter/Stevens/Breyer only and is not an independent alternative holding. No majority rule excludes all monetary claims from unmistakability or classifies all FIRREA as non-general.

##### What partial liability establishes about causation and relief

**Holding:** Partial contractual liability is affirmed for all three transactions, including Statesman’s separate goodwill and capital credit, with contract-specific causation, compensable loss, mitigation, applicable contractual periods and damages left for ordinary adjudication. Breach does not establish every consequence or a nominal goodwill/capital-credit award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter’s Parts II–IV and VII, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; eight Justices.

**Material application:** Each institution must prove recoverable loss under its own bargain and lawful defenses. One component cannot borrow another’s amount or duration; any properly proved termination or other qualifier remains material.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No automatic insolvency causation, guaranteed usability of the whole goodwill balance, fixed recovery period, damages amount, takings entitlement or compelled specific performance against valid law.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Partial liability affirmed, 8–1, and cases remanded for genuinely remaining contract proceedings; no mandatory trial or hearing. Ginsburg would vacate present liability affirmance for bounded contract-by-contract reconsideration under unmistakability, not enter Government judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hercules:** Its actual-authorized-duty requirement controls. The Court distinguishes the absent reimbursement undertaking there from the specific integrated promises here, while preserving its limits on fairness-based liability and automatic damages.
- **Sutton and Merrill:** Actual governmental authority remains necessary; neither the Tucker Act nor equitable appeal supplies missing assent or authority. No particular approval defect is found here.
- **Romein:** Regulation governing a relationship is not itself an immutable contractual obligation. These holdings rest on the additional transaction writings and incorporated promises.
- **Cisneros:** The obligation is enforced with its real qualifications, not enlarged into an unconditional payment or regulatory guarantee.
- **United States v. Texas:** An existing obligation is distinct from a mechanism governing its enforcement. It supplies no damages amount or formula in this case.
- **Lynch and Perry:** Genuine government contractual obligations remain legally significant despite sovereign status. This decision preserves legislation and applies the financial undertakings actually made; it does not adopt a general power to enjoin legislative change.
- **Horowitz:** Public/general sovereign treatment does not itself answer ordinary assumed-risk impossibility. The eight-Justice holding rests on allocated risk, not the additional three-Justice substantial-effect theory.
- **Bowen:** Its retained-power and promise-construction principles remain. The Court does not convert the statutory arrangement there into a rule cancelling every express financial-risk undertaking here.
- **Merrion, United States Trust and Home Telephone:** Retained governmental powers and the particular commitments at issue must be distinguished. No majority adopts a universal rule making the sovereign-construction doctrine irrelevant to all money claims.
- **OPM v. Richmond:** Authority and appropriations limits are not erased by monetary relief; no unsupported defect or general equitable right to payment is found.

No earlier decision is overruled. The additional unmistakability and substantial-effect explanations have only the joins stated in the topology; they do not independently alter controlling doctrine.

#### Mobil Oil Exploration & Producing Southeast, Inc. v. United States / Marathon Oil Co. v. United States, 530 U.S. 604 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 99-244 and 99-253.

**Decided:** June 26, 2000.

##### 1. The leases contain a clear continuing process undertaking that the Government breached

**Holding:** These leases incorporate a continuing obligation to provide the specified Outer Continental Shelf Lands Act (OCSLA) exploration-plan approval process, including approval, after necessary modifications, of a compliant plan within thirty days of submission unless serious harm to life (including aquatic life), property, minerals, national security or defense, or the marine, coastal or human environment cannot be avoided by modification and requires disapproval. The Government's refusal to provide that approval because of the Outer Banks Protection Act's (OBPA) new conditions breached the undertaking; independent permits and state coastal-consistency requirements do not make the federal promise disappear.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, Part I, unanimous.

**Material application:** The leases' term, conditional operating rights and incorporation provisions establish an authorized continuing federal process undertaking. The incorporated sources include OCSLA, sections 302-303 of the Department of Energy Organization Act, existing regulations and the specified limited future regulations; the general catchall does not transfer every later statutory change to the lessees.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Future regulations for waste prevention, resource conservation and correlative rights remain incorporated. Sections 9-10 require approved plans and compliance; section 13 preserves OCSLA suspension and cancellation, and separately suspension on the Defense Secretary's recommendation during war or a national emergency declared by Congress or the President, with just compensation. Section 19 reserves ungranted rights: other geological and geophysical exploration and other-mineral operations may not unreasonably interfere with or endanger lease operations; easements may serve other lands' work or product shipment. A defense designation requires the Defense Secretary and presidential approval; surface operations then require Defense concurrence. A resulting suspension also suspends rentals and royalties, extends the term and carries constitutionally required compensation. None supplies a blanket no-liability reservation. OCSLA permits suspensions at the lessee's request in the national interest, for proper development or transportation facilities and separate suspensions for threatened serious, irreparable or immediate harm to life, property, minerals or the marine, coastal or human environment; neither predicate is newly found merely because further environmental study is useful. A qualifying suspension extends the affected lease or permit for an equivalent period, except when the suspension results from gross negligence or willful violation of the lease, permit or applicable regulations. Approval remains conditional on compliance, necessary modifications and the specified serious-harm exception. Yet Interior's completed evaluation did not supply the legal approval withheld under the new Act. North Carolina made actual objections here; operations still require a lawful state-consistency resolution and separate discharge and drilling permissions. No concurrence or permit entitlement is found.

##### 2. The statutory replacement of the promised process was material repudiation

**Holding:** OBPA's prohibition and new prerequisites materially withdrew an essential part of the bargained-for federal process and constituted repudiation, rather than an ordinary permissible delay under the incorporated terms. That repudiation applies to the four submitted-plan leases and anticipatorily to the same promised future process on the fifth lease, without inventing a submitted plan or missed deadline on that tract.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, Parts II-V; eight Justices, with Stevens joining Part I only.

**Material application:** The Act barred the relevant approvals and drilling until the later of October 1, 1991 or forty-five days of continuous congressional session after the required report and certification. Continuity is broken only by adjournment of Congress without a further date, and recesses when either House adjourns for more than three days to a day certain are excluded from the count. The Secretary must consider the scientific panel and explain disagreements, but the panel has no absolute veto. The Court does not treat the gate as forty-five calendar days or an indefinite ban, or attribute every later delay to it.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The same repudiation reaches the fifth lease anticipatorily despite the absence of a submitted plan for it. Congress retains regulatory power; the decision establishes financial responsibility for the particular assumed risk, not a rule that every delay or unfavorable enactment is total repudiation.

##### 3. Restitution does not require proof that the project would have earned profits

**Holding:** After this material repudiation, the injured lessees may elect restoration of consideration paid for the promised performance without proving that all independent approvals would have been obtained or profitable production would have occurred. Independent regulatory obstacles bear on the actual bargain and any cognizable benefit accounting, but do not automatically defeat restitution by making expectation profits unprovable.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, Parts II-V; eight Justices, with Stevens joining Part I only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Independent state objections and permits remain relevant to the actual bargain and accounting. The Court guarantees no profitable production and finds no statutory cancellation or entitlement to OCSLA cancellation compensation.

##### 4. The supplied continued-performance defense does not bar restoration

**Holding:** Urging the repudiating party to perform, submitting the requested plan, pursuing independent approvals or seeking extensions does not by itself surrender the injured party's restitution election. Acceptance of significant substituted performance may matter, but the documented acts here do not establish acceptance of the withheld federal process as fulfilled or a waiver of restoration.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, Parts II-V; eight Justices, with Stevens joining Part I only.

**Material application:** The companies' submission, unsuccessful override efforts and MOU-related extensions kept the original opportunity alive; they did not obtain the approval process the Government had repudiated. The 1992 restitution suit asserted restoration rather than relinquishing it. Administrative work and additional lease time are not denied; they remain relevant at their lawful scope without being treated as performance of the withheld undertaking. The record also distinguishes earlier rent-free suspensions from the June 1995 letter's rent-bearing, five-year-capped suspension. No later renewal, continuous rent exemption or payment history through decision is assumed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no unproved retraction, settlement, abandonment, forfeiture of a preservation objection or attribution of every act by one petitioner to the other. It does not reopen entitlement merely to decide an unsupported election theory.

##### 5. The monetary remand is confined to genuine restoration and amount questions

**Holding:** The petitioners' restitution entitlement requires return of proved bonus consideration with legally required restoration of corresponding rights and any established lawful credit or offset. The Federal Circuit must resolve its unreached, properly preserved calculation objections on the actual record; recovery of rentals, profits or interest does not follow automatically, and interest requires independent authority.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, Parts II-V; eight Justices, with Stevens joining Part I only.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Court of Federal Claims separately denied rental recovery and, after a benefits hearing, found no cognizable offset established. The Federal Circuit never reached the Government's monetary challenges because it rejected liability. Reversal therefore permits review of genuinely remaining objections; it does not erase the existing benefits determination or command a new evidentiary hearing regardless of what remains disputed. Approximate descriptions of payments cannot substitute for the actual judgment and supported calculations. No decree compels a regulatory approval, invalidates North Carolina's objection, awards speculative production profits or converts OBPA's later repeal into performance at the promised time.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. Winstar Corp. supplies actual-undertaking and assumed-risk responsibility while preserving sovereign regulatory power and separate remedy analysis. Its noncontrolling broad sovereign-acts and unmistakability theories are not made controlling here; the particular continuing promise is clear even under the requirement to identify one unmistakably.
- Roehm v. Horst supplies anticipatory-repudiation principles and the distinction between urging performance and surrendering a restoration election. It is applied to the essential future process, not every prospective administrative delay.
- United States v. Behan supplies the distinction between recovery of value after prevented performance and proof of anticipated profits; independent obstacles to profitable production do not erase the restitution claim established here.
- Pence v. Langdon supplies the distinction between preservation of a restoration right and conduct inconsistent with that election; the Court rejects waiver from the documented acts without assuming an unproved acceptance of significant substitute performance.

#### Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools, 503 U.S. 60 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 90-918.
**Decided:** February 26, 1992.

##### Whether compensatory damages are available in the established Title IX private action for intentional discrimination

**Holding:** When a cognizable federal statutory cause of action exists, courts may award appropriate relief absent clear congressional direction limiting it; recognizing that remedy does not create a new cause of action. In Title IX’s established private action, compensatory damages are available for proved intentional discrimination, subject to proof of recipient responsibility, causation and compensable loss.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices control the remedial rule and application. Scalia and Thomas concur only in the judgment on a narrower statutory ground.

**Material application:** Franklin’s allegations concern completed sexual harassment and abuse while attending a federally funded school, with alleged official knowledge and inaction. After her departure, backpay and prospective student relief could not compensate the pleaded injury. Congress’s 1986 reference to legal and equitable remedies and subsequent preservation of private enforcement supply no direction barring compensation.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Allegations are not findings. No new cause of action, final recipient-attribution rule, punitive remedy, or liability for unintended discriminatory effects is established; the source of Congress’s constitutional power need not be selected.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical damages dismissal and remand for ordinary adjudication of liability, attribution, causation and compensable loss. No amount is awarded or prospective school-supervision order entered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Cannon v. University of Chicago*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established Title IX action and is not reopened. *Bell v. Hood*, 327 U.S. 678, supplies appropriate relief absent congressional limitation. *Davis v. Passman*, 442 U.S. 228, separates authority to sue from remedy. *J. I. Case Co. v. Borak*, 377 U.S. 426, opposes limiting relief merely because a statute does not enumerate remedies. *Guardians Association v. Civil Service Commission*, 463 U.S. 582, supplies intentional-discrimination remedial support without a single unified majority rationale. *Consolidated Rail Corp. v. Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, supplies analogous monetary-relief support.

##### Whether Spending Clause notice categorically bars compensation for the intentional Title IX violation alleged

**Holding:** The Spending Clause notice principle does not categorically bar compensatory relief for an intentional violation of Title IX’s express prohibition of sex discrimination within its established private action. Ambiguous funding conditions, unintentional effects and the separate requirements for attributing misconduct to a recipient are not resolved.

**Proposition-level authority:** White’s opinion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy and Souter; seven Justices reject the separate funding-notice ground for dismissal.

**Material application:** The enacted nondiscrimination condition and established private action defeat a categorical notice objection to compensation for the pleaded intentional discrimination. This conclusion does not establish when the school is responsible for the teacher’s conduct.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The notice objection does not sustain dismissal; compensatory relief may be sought on remand subject to proof of the violation and resulting loss.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman*, 451 U.S. 1, retains its fair-notice rule for funding conditions but does not bar relief for the intentional violation alleged. *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57, informs the identification of sex-based harassment without importing a Title VII attribution formula. *Darrone*, 465 U.S. 624, defeats a categorical monetary-remedy objection based on spending legislation. *Cannon*, 441 U.S. 677, supplies the established enforcement premise.

#### McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Commission

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-986.
**Decided:** April 19, 1995.

##### Compelled identification on these political leaflets requires exacting scrutiny

**Holding:** A content-defined requirement that an individual identify herself on ordinary political issue leaflets burdens core political expression, including the choice to speak anonymously. The State must satisfy exacting scrutiny by showing narrow tailoring to an overriding state interest; viewpoint neutrality or the election setting alone does not lower that standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices; Thomas concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Talley v. California, 362 U.S. 60: applies protection of anonymous handbills to this individual political leaflet; the Court does not establish immunity for fraudulent communications.
- Burson v. Freeman, May 26, 1992: applies the independently majority-supported scrutiny distinction; its fractured polling-place validity judgment does not settle this leaflet requirement.
- Anderson v. Celebrezze, 460 U.S. 780: distinguishes the election-administration setting; it does not reduce scrutiny for every election-related speech restriction.

##### The asserted voter-information interest does not justify this identification demand

**Holding:** A general interest in helping voters evaluate a source does not justify requiring this ordinary individual to place her name and residence or business address on her independently produced, truthful political leaflet. The Court leaves candidate finance, campaign committees, organizational spending, and other materially distinct disclosure settings for separate analysis.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices; Thomas concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Talley v. California, 362 U.S. 60: applies protection for the ordinary author's choice of anonymous publication.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1: distinguishes campaign-finance reporting and its specific interests; those disclosure holdings remain undisturbed.
- First National Bank of Boston v. Bellotti, 435 U.S. 765: distinguishes corporate-advertising source information; its observation does not resolve this private individual's leaflet.

##### Fraud prevention does not justify this application to truthful independent advocacy

**Holding:** Although preventing electoral fraud is a weighty interest, Ohio may not impose this categorical identification requirement on truthful, independently produced individual issue leaflets without the necessary narrow fit to that interest. The enforcement order and $100 fine must be set aside; properly tailored fraud, impersonation, and materially different disclosure rules remain available.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stevens), joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven Justices; Thomas concurs in the judgment only.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Talley v. California, 362 U.S. 60: applies anonymous-publication protection while distinguishing measures directed at fraud.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1: leaves candidate-finance disclosure and corruption-related interests intact rather than treating them as dispositive of this ordinary leaflet.
- First National Bank of Boston v. Bellotti, 435 U.S. 765: leaves materially different corporate source-disclosure questions outside the individual sanction decided here.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Ohio's judgment is reversed, 8–1, and the enforcement order and $100 fine must be set aside. No damages or invalidation of every disclosure provision is ordered; candidate, committee, organizational and other materially different disclosure settings remain outside this application.

#### Libretti v. United States, 516 U.S. 29

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-7427.
**Decided:** November 7, 1995.

##### Consent, factual admission and the statutory bounds of criminal forfeiture

**Holding:** A defendant may knowingly and voluntarily waive the forfeiture jury protection and admit facts supporting forfeiture, but consent cannot enlarge §853's punishment or transfer an interest beyond the defendant's lawful power of disposition. An admission may establish a statutory predicate; an all-assets agreement cannot replace a predicate Congress requires.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I-B, unanimous on these common propositions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Lawful acquisition alone does not immunize property: §853(a)(1) reaches directly or indirectly obtained proceeds, (a)(2) the defendant's property used or intended in any manner or part to commit or facilitate the violation, and (a)(3) for a continuing criminal enterprise his interests, claims and property or contractual rights supplying control over it. Under §853(p), the defendant's act or omission must make qualifying property unavailable by inability to locate it despite due diligence, third-party transfer/sale/deposit, location beyond jurisdiction, substantial diminution, or commingling not divisible without difficulty; then other defendant property must be forfeited up to its value. Section 853(d)'s rebuttable presumption at trial requires the Government to prove by a preponderance both acquisition during the violation or within a reasonable time afterward and the absence of a likely source other than the covered violation. An all-assets promise proves neither predicate. No asset-by-asset eligibility or invalidity is found here.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Godinez distinguishes competence from knowing voluntary choice; Olano intentional relinquishment from inaction; Mezzanatto's limited consent holding does not authorize an unlawful sentence or waiver of every protection.

##### Former Rule 11(f) and a separate forfeiture factual-basis inquiry

**Holding:** Former Rule 11(f) requires a factual basis for the substantive guilty plea, not a separate guilt-style factual-basis inquiry into the extent of forfeiture imposed as sentence under §§848 and 853. That procedural construction neither authorizes punishment exceeding the statutes nor requires acceptance of a defective agreement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part I-A; all eight Associates join. Stone-Zsela affirms on separate statutory-record and informed-waiver grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Rules 7(c)(2) and 31(e)'s distinct notice and special-verdict safeguards do not turn each property interest into a CCE guilt element. The precise extent of any independent inquiry duty outside Rule 11(f) remains open. No later Rule 32.2 procedure applies.

##### The particular forfeiture determination and the Sixth Amendment

**Holding:** Under the §§848 and 853 scheme reviewed, the extent of forfeiture following conviction is a sentencing determination, not an element of CCE guilt requiring a Sixth Amendment jury determination. This does not place every sentence-affecting fact outside the jury guarantee.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-A, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg reserve the constitutional source of the forfeiture jury protection; their reservation does not displace the majority.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Gaudin remains fully effective for enacted offense elements. McMillan, Cabana and Spaziano supply the bounded guilt/punishment distinction. Austin and Alexander establish forfeiture's punitive character in their settings, which does not itself import every guilt-trial safeguard. No Excessive Fines claim is decided.

##### Waiver of the Rule 31(e) special verdict by affirmative agreement and courtroom assent

**Holding:** Libretti's represented agreement to forfeit identified interests, signed jury waiver and express courtroom assent to ending trial and proceeding to sentence waive the Rule 31(e) special verdict on this record. Former Rule 11(c) requires no additional forfeiture-specific advisement, and Rule 23(a) requires no new forfeiture-specific writing for this statutory sentencing verdict.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; five direct votes. Stone-Zsela, Souter and Ginsburg separately uphold informed relinquishment but do not join this broader sufficient-waiver formulation; Stevens does not independently decide the application.

**Material application:** Affirmative represented assent, the agreement's actual admissions and scope, express explanation that trial would end, and earlier jury-forfeiture references support waiver. Neither competence, a signature nor silence alone establishes knowing surrender of every sentencing issue.

**Limits and questions not reached:** A judge may give an explicit special-verdict explanation and must not affirmatively mislead or leave evident confusion uncorrected. Coercion, voluntariness, understanding and agreement-scope challenges retain their distinct predicates. Private legal advice is not assumed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Godinez, Olano and Mezzanatto preserve informed intentional choice within the protection actually surrendered, without automatic waiver from a plea or statutory classification alone.

##### Rejection of a wholesale new factual-basis proceeding on the existing record

**Holding:** The trial evidence, sentencing materials, §853-referenced agreement and the judge's response to Libretti's preserved objection support rejecting the demanded wholesale new forfeiture factual-basis proceeding. The Court leaves the precise independent duty outside Rule 11(f) unresolved and makes no new asset-by-asset §853(a) or (p) adjudication.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III, joined by Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; five direct votes. Scalia and Thomas withhold this additional appraisal, Stone-Zsela supplies a separate record-limited ground, and Stevens would remand for statutory assurance.

**Material application:** The later April order's adverse Wyochem/Porter substitute-property finding, reserved tracing questions and third-party return orders survive at their actual scope. The pending appeal defeated defendant-specific reopening, not separate §853(n) jurisdiction. The $8,416.23 General Chemical check designated as substitute property is distinct from the Columbia IRA and Porter's $1,333.45 claim; designation alone proves no §853(p) predicate. A conditional exclusion for an employer pension/profit-sharing interest inalienable by law or another's valid spendthrift trust does not establish that either the check or IRA qualifies.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm on the reviewed questions, 8–1; no wholesale new hearing, automatic return or reopening of valid third-party relief. Section 853(n) remains available to a person other than the defendant asserting a legal interest: petition within thirty days of final publication or receipt of notice, whichever is earlier; a nonjury hearing; proof by a preponderance of either a right vested in the petitioner rather than the defendant or superior when the acts occurred, or good-faith purchaser-for-value status with reasonable lack of cause to believe the property forfeitable at purchase. On that determination the court must amend the order. It is not a defendant's postjudgment-reconsideration route, and general innocent-owner terminology does not replace those criteria.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** The procedural affirmance preserves the common statutory-authority limit and does not adopt the appellate view that agreement creates forfeiture power. The dissent's additional demanded assurance is not an independent Court holding.

#### Harris v. Forklift Systems, Inc., 510 U.S. 17

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1168.

**Decided:** 1993-11-09.

##### Hostile employment conditions do not require psychological injury

**Holding:** Sex-based harassment violates Title VII when, considered in all the circumstances, it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to alter employment conditions and create an environment a reasonable person would find hostile or abusive and that the employee actually perceives as abusive; serious psychological injury, treatment, diagnosis, and lost productivity are not prerequisites. Relevant circumstances include frequency, severity, physical threat or humiliation rather than a merely offensive utterance, and unreasonable interference with work, but no listed consideration is indispensable; psychological effects may inform subjective abuse, while offensive conduct that does not meet the objective and subjective requirements remains insufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; O'Connor and those eight Justices supply nine votes for the complete rule and reversal with remand. Direct unanimous authority; no fractured-opinion inference.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- *Meritor Savings Bank, FSB v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986): applies and clarifies its severe-or-pervasive alteration-of-conditions rule; its extreme examples do not impose a psychological-injury element. Its employer-responsibility questions are not enlarged here.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Mere offense remains insufficient. Meritor's employer-responsibility issues and Hicks's persuasion rules are not altered; proposed EEOC regulations and a sex-specific reasonable-person formulation are not decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Sixth Circuit is reversed and the case remanded for application of the objective and subjective standard without an injury prerequisite; liability, constructive discharge and damages are not decided.

#### Oncale v. Sundowner Offshore Services, Inc.

**Docket or dockets:** Fifth Circuit No. 95-30510; 83 F.3d 118 identifies the judgment below.
**Decided:** March 19, 1997.

##### Whether Title VII excludes harassment by a person of the employee's own sex

**Holding:** Title VII's protection against discriminatory employment conditions includes same-sex harassment. The employee must still plead and ultimately prove discrimination because of sex and every other element of the asserted claim; common sex supplies neither immunity nor liability.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Meritor* supplies coverage of discriminatory abusive conditions, extended here to same-sex actors without eliminating any statutory element.

##### Whether sexual desire or an actual opposite-sex comparator is necessary to establish discrimination

**Holding:** Competent direct or circumstantial evidence can establish discrimination because of sex without proof of desire, a particular sexual orientation, or an actual opposite-sex coworker. Sexual content, contact, or the gravity of conduct does not alone conclusively establish that causal element.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Sex-directed hostility, selective treatment, discriminatory statements, threats, and surrounding circumstances may support the inference. These examples create neither exclusive proof channels nor a compulsory checklist.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court finds no participant's sexual orientation or motive and decides no independent sexual-orientation coverage question. Personal hostility unrelated to sex is outside this cause of action.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Hicks* preserves ultimate persuasion and permissible circumstantial inference; it does not impose a compulsory desire showing or comparator. *Brown v. Polk County* and *Nabozny* retain their distinct statutory and constitutional scopes.

##### Whether a single-sex workplace changes the hostile-environment standard

**Holding:** Sex-based conduct must be severe or pervasive enough to produce an objectively hostile or abusive workplace in context, and the employee must actually perceive it as abusive. Workforce composition informs context but creates no exemption; psychiatric injury and measurable productivity loss are unnecessary.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Material application:** Workplace custom cannot automatically recast forced physical humiliation or rape threats as harmless camaraderie. The allegations remain unproved, and their gravity does not establish unwelcome treatment, sex causation, or attribution.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No single circumstance, diagnosis, treatment, medical proof, or universal reasonable-woman formulation is required.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Harris* supplies the contextual severe-or-pervasive inquiry and its separate objective and subjective requirements, without an economic or psychiatric injury prerequisite.

##### Which attribution and remedial issues survive removal of the categorical bar

**Holding:** Employer attribution, if reached, requires application of *Andrade*: actual delegated supervisory authority must materially aid creation or maintenance of the unlawful environment. A supervisory title alone does not establish that connection, and lack of higher-management notice alone does not defeat this agency route; negligence and a preserved quid-pro-quo theory remain distinct.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Thomas), unanimous.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The agency route does not invariably require a completed employment action or economic loss. A quid-pro-quo claim separately requires a supported employment benefit or detriment conditioned on sexual participation. No strict employer liability, new affirmative defense, individual-coworker rule, or finding about Lyons's statutory status is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Reverse the categorical ground as to affected Sundowner and Lyons claims and remand under ordinary procedure. Pippen and Johnson's separate not-an-employer dismissal remains outside review, without endorsement of a new immunity rule. Liability, factual sufficiency, constructive discharge, discovery, trial entitlement, and available relief for the 1991 conduct remain undecided; properly presented independent defenses survive.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Andrade* supplies the authority-aided agency rule and preserves negligence as another route. The lower-court *Garcia* same-sex exclusion cannot govern after this decision.

#### United States v. James Daniel Good Real Property, 510 U.S. 43

**Docket or dockets:** No. 92-1180.

**Decided:** 1993-12-13.

##### Notice and a hearing before civil seizure of real property

**Holding:** Before seizing real property for civil forfeiture, the Government must ordinarily give the owner notice and a meaningful opportunity to be heard; a Fourth Amendment warrant does not itself satisfy this independent Fifth Amendment requirement. To bypass prior process for exigent circumstances, the Government must show that less restrictive measures, such as notice of the pending claim recorded against the property, a restraining order, or a bond, would not prevent sale, destruction, or continued unlawful use; emergency seizure remains possible on that showing with appropriate subsequent process.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Pre-seizure process portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, Souter, and Ginsburg. These six adopt the same rule, its exigency qualification, and its application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Soldal v. Cook County: applied to preserve the independent operation of constitutional protections; its possessory-seizure rule remains intact.
- Mathews v. Eldridge: applied to the process required before this deprivation; no new general balancing formula replaces it.
- Connecticut v. Doehr: applied for the value of notice and an opportunity to contest a prejudgment property deprivation.
- Fuentes v. Shevin: applied for the value of notice and an opportunity to contest a prejudgment property deprivation.
- Calero-Toledo v. Pearson Yacht Leasing Co.: distinguished because movable property and exceptional governmental needs can justify delayed process; that exception remains available where its justification exists.

##### Mandatory administrative promptness does not supply an unstated dismissal sanction

**Holding:** Noncompliance with the distinct reporting and commencement duties in 19 U.S.C. §§1602–1604 does not require dismissal of a forfeiture action filed within §1621's five-year limitations period. Section 1602 requires immediate reporting by customs agents to customs officers; §1603(b) requires prompt reporting to the United States attorney when the specified seizure or discovery requires proceedings by that office; and §1604, for recovery requiring district-court or Court of International Trade proceedings, requires those proceedings to be commenced forthwith when the prescribed probable basis for a fine, penalty, or forfeiture appears, subject to the Attorney General's preserved determination, after inquiry, that such proceedings probably cannot be sustained or that public justice does not require institution or prosecution, in which event the facts are instead reported to the Treasury Secretary for direction. These remain mandatory duties within their stated conditions, and the ruling does not extinguish an independently supported constitutional-delay claim or other source-authorized relief.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion for the Court, Statutory timing portion, joined by Stone, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine adopt this construction and reversal of the contrary ground below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Brock v. Pierce County: applied to distinguish a mandatory timing duty from an unstated loss of governmental authority.
- United States v. Montalvo-Murillo: applied to reject an automatic substantive remedy that the timing provision does not prescribe; its distinct hearing duty is not diluted.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The notice holding concerns civil real-property forfeiture, not criminal forfeiture or ultimate forfeiture liability. Statutory promptness remains mandatory; an independently supported constitutional-delay claim or otherwise authorized relief is not extinguished.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The hearing ruling is affirmed, 6–3, and the statutory-dismissal ground reversed, 9–0; the case is remanded. The timely forfeiture action may proceed lawfully. Restoration, accounting or other consequences of the interim seizure require an independently authorized basis; no award, automatic dismissal, return of title or sovereign damages is ordered.
