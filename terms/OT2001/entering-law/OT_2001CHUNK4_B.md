## Standards and Tests selection: Factual knowledge and depiction boundaries under §§2252 and 2256

### Factual knowledge and depiction boundaries under §§2252 and 2256

**Governing question:** The knowledge and definition requirements governing the reviewed child-pornography handling offenses.

**Current rule:** Under 18 U.S.C. §§2252(a)(1)–(2), the Government must prove beyond a reasonable doubt the applicable knowing handling act, knowledge of the depiction’s sexually explicit character and knowledge that the performer was a minor. Knowledge of the statute, illegality or the precise legal age definition is unnecessary. The under-eighteen definition does not become substantially overbroad merely from difficulty estimating ages near eighteen or different adult-treatment rules for older adolescents. The complete category of lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area retains a sexual-conduct limit: mere bodily visibility or nudity is insufficient, and substituting “lascivious” for “lewd” does not itself create facial vagueness or substantial overbreadth.

## Standards and Tests selection: Cable-carriage rules and review of legislative predictions

### Cable-carriage rules and review of legislative predictions

**Governing question:** How the First Amendment reviews content-neutral cable-carriage requirements.

**Current rule:** Cable operators’ editorial choices receive ordinary First Amendment protection, without the broadcast-scarcity rationale. Content classification examines what the law selects and why. The general full-power must-carry provisions at issue are content neutral and receive intermediate scrutiny: they must advance substantial interests unrelated to suppressing expression and burden no substantially greater amount of speech than necessary. The least restrictive alternative is not required. Preserving broadcast availability, diverse information sources and fair competition are substantial interests, but identifying them does not prove the law satisfies the test. Legislative predictive judgments receive substantial deference while courts independently determine whether reasonable inferences rest on substantial evidence and support the claimed harms and response.

## Standards and Tests selection: Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

### Speech burdens in a content-neutral injunction

**Governing question:** How a court tailors a content-neutral injunction regulating speech-related conduct.

**Current rule:** A content-neutral injunction must burden no more speech than necessary to serve the significant governmental interest supporting the remedy. This inquiry is more demanding than the ordinary content-neutral legislative time, place and manner rule, without becoming a universal least-restrictive-means test. Each restriction needs a remedial connection to the established interest and conduct. Public access, medical quiet and residential privacy may support relief, but neither those interests nor prior misconduct justifies territorial restrictions or advance-permission conditions broader than necessary. Removing an invalid restriction also removes its operative predicate; it cannot be restored by relabeling the same conduct under another clause. Within an otherwise valid fixed access zone, an exception permitting nonthreatening initial counseling by up to two people per approached person or group may end when the recipient objectively communicates, verbally or nonverbally, that counseling is unwanted. The counselor must end that close encounter and leave the fixed zone, while retaining lawful advocacy outside it; this supplies neither universal silence nor a mobile personal radius.

O'Connor's Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five. Stevens's individualized equitable account and the strict-restraint dissent are not joins in this standard.

## Standards and Tests selection: Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

### Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

**Governing question:** Facial review of indecency restrictions on a general-purpose communications network.

**Current rule:** A content-based criminal restriction fails the First Amendment when uncertain, substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the government does not establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. Knowledge that a minor is present, other available channels and scienter do not by themselves cure uncertain content categories or adult-access burdens. Age-verification defenses must afford workable protection on the actual record; cost, loss of anonymity and lawful adult exclusion remain material. A severability clause permits removal of a discrete unlawful textual branch but does not authorize judicial creation of a minor-only offense or an absent value exception.

**Authority by component:**
- Overbreadth, uncertainty and narrow fit: Reno v. ACLU, Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — I. The challenged content restrictions violate the First Amendment; textual severance boundary: Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — II. Textual severance preserves obscenity law; judicial rewriting cannot save the remaining restrictions, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer (eight), 1997-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** Reno v. ACLU, 1997-06-26.

**Open question:** Precisely drawn future controls with effective nonexclusionary age assurance, parental-approval questions, every Internet service's classification and extraterritorial scope remain open. The holding creates no independent Fifth Amendment rule and leaves obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation and direct harmful contact to their own predicates.

**Holdings navigation:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union (1997-06-26) — First Amendment / Internet content restrictions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Content-selected restrictions on protected adult cable programming

### Content-selected restrictions on protected adult cable programming

**Governing question:** The justification required for government restrictions directed at the sexual content of protected adult cable speech.

**Current rule:** Strict scrutiny requires a narrowly tailored restriction serving a compelling interest; the Government bears the ultimate justification burden. Once a concrete, plausibly effective arrangement imposing less restraint on speech is presented, the Government must show why its comparative inadequacy warrants the broader adult burden. Child protection and parental assistance do not by themselves establish necessity. Assessment concerns the actual medium, speech burden and supported implementation problems, with serious consideration but not conclusive deference to legislative predictions.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc.; No. 98-1682. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, I. Congress's content-selected cable restriction requires strict scrutiny, 2000-05-22; Ginsburg with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-05-22; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Neither scientific certainty nor a perfect alternative is required. A paper alternative alone does not establish practical effectiveness. The rule creates no statutory publicity duty, automatic restoration of an invalid restriction, universal blocking-device rule or prohibition of supported narrower regulation; obscenity remains distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal tax liens on the power to accept or redirect inherited property

### Federal tax liens on the power to accept or redirect inherited property

**Governing question:** Whether a state-law disclaimer defeats a federal lien on an already existing power over inherited value.

**Current rule:** A legally effective state-created power to accept inherited value or direct it to someone else by disclaimer is a right to property reached by 26 U.S.C. 6321. State law determines what control exists; federal law classifies that control for the lien. State relation back cannot erase a power that already existed for federal lien purposes.

**Authority by component:**

- Existing inheritance control and federal classification despite relation back: Drye v. United States, Public Projection / Holdings / Federal lien law reaches the taxpayer's existing power to accept or redirect inherited value, December 7, 1999; Souter for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Drye v. United States, December 7, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule creates no inheritance interest absent under state law. Disclaimers completed before legally cognizable control, prior expectancies, spendthrift interests, valuation, recipient liability and competing priority remain unresolved. Irvine's gift-tax reasonable-time analysis is not a lien condition; Congress's specific priority protections remain.

## Standards and Tests selection: Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

### Complete deprivation of economically beneficial land use

**Governing question:** Compensation for a land-use restriction eliminating all economically beneficial use of the relevant property.

**Current rule:** Complete deprivation effects a taking requiring just compensation unless background state property or nuisance law already excluded the prohibited use from the owner's title. After the owner establishes complete deprivation, the State must identify and substantiate a background limitation that independently prohibits the use in the property's circumstances. A new legislative declaration that the use is harmful is insufficient. Apply ordinary adjudicative proof requirements, without a special heightened quantum. Changed knowledge may affect application of genuine nuisance law; legislative labeling cannot substitute for an antecedent title limit. Compensation concerns any actual taking period, not an assumed permanent loss.

**Authority by component:**

- Categorical compensation, background-law exception and allocation: Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, “Complete economic deprivation requires compensation unless background ownership law already foreclosed the use”, June 29, 1992; Scalia, Stone, White, O'Connor and Thomas.

**Present operation effective:** *Lucas*, June 29, 1992.

**Exceptions and limits:** Penn Central's contextual analysis remains for partial deprivation. No percentage shortcut, parcel-definition rule, automatic valuation, right to anticipated profits or finding that every environmental restriction is invalid follows. Valid authority to regulate remains distinct from responsibility to compensate.

**Holdings navigation:** Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council, 505 U.S. 1003 (1992) — Complete economic deprivation of land.

**Current rule:** Postenactment acquisition does not itself extinguish the claimant's own challenge or transform the regulation into background property law. ## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation](HOLDINGS.md#trade-court-factfinding-does-not-eliminate-lawful-delegated-interpretation-1). [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).


**Related current rules:** Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation governs independent judicial consideration when controlling force is unavailable.

## Standards and Tests selection: National air-quality standards and implementation costs

### National air-quality standards and implementation costs

**Governing question:** The criteria for primary and secondary national ambient air-quality standards under Clean Air Act section 109(b).

**Current rule:** Exclude economic implementation costs when setting either standard. Primary standards must furnish the public-health protection requisite under section 108's scientific pollutant-effects criteria with an adequate margin of safety. Secondary standards protect public welfare against known or anticipated adverse effects associated with the pollutant's presence under those criteria; they do not inherit the primary safety-margin requirement. Implementation expense cannot be relabeled a pollutant effect to enter that balance.

**Authority by component:**

- Standard-setting criteria and separation from implementation: Canonical Decision Record `Whitman_v_American_Trucking_Associations_merits_2001-02-27.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. Implementation costs do not set primary or secondary national standards, 2001-02-27; Kennedy; nine direct supporters of the primary operative rule and eight of the secondary rule.

**Present operation effective:** February 27, 2001.

**Exceptions and limits:** This is not a zero-risk command or a prohibition on costs where a different provision authorizes them. Section 109(d)(2)(C)(iv)'s advice about health, welfare, social, economic and energy effects of attainment strategies does not change the section 109(b) criteria. Periodic review returns to those statutory criteria. No particular concentration, scientific methodology or indirect health-effects claim is resolved.

**Holdings navigation:** [Whitman v. American Trucking Associations, Inc. / American Trucking Associations, Inc. v. Whitman, 531 U.S. 457 — National ambient standards and ozone implementation](HOLDINGS.md#whitman-v-american-trucking-associations-inc--american-trucking-associations-inc-v-whitman-531-us-457-2).

## Standards and Tests selection: Congressional guidance and agency self-limitation

### Congressional guidance and agency self-limitation

**Governing question:** Constitutional delegation and a proposed administrative cure for the absence of legislative guidance.

**Current rule:** Congress must supply a constitutionally sufficient governing principle. Section 109(b)(1) does so by identifying the pollutant subject, scientific criteria, requisite public-health protection and adequate safety margin; requisite protection means sufficient protection without exceeding what protection requires, and no unique numerical answer is necessary. If Congress has supplied no sufficient principle, an agency cannot cure the defect by choosing its own limit. Choosing a reasoned scientific method within an existing delegation is different from supplying a missing legislative policy.

**Authority by component:**

- Adequate guidance: Canonical Decision Record `Whitman_v_American_Trucking_Associations_merits_2001-02-27.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. The primary-standard delegation supplies adequate congressional guidance, 2001-02-27; all nine Justices.
- Independent no-cure rule: Canonical Decision Record `Whitman_v_American_Trucking_Associations_merits_2001-02-27.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. An agency cannot cure a genuinely standardless delegation, 2001-02-27; all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** February 27, 2001.

**Exceptions and limits:** The received intelligible-principle doctrine remains. Neither scientific uncertainty nor the need for judgment alone proves standardless power. The Court does not decide a separate constitutional challenge to secondary standards, approve a particular scientific methodology, or require economic balancing as a cure.

## Standards and Tests selection: Integrated fair use of commercial parody

### Integrated fair use of commercial parody

**Governing question:** How §107 applies to claimed parody and its borrowing and market effects.

**Current rule:** Consider together purpose and character, the copyrighted work’s nature, the amount and substantiality taken, and effect on the potential market or value. Commercial parody may qualify without a dispositive presumption of unfairness. Parody’s particular borrowing justification requires reasonably perceptible criticism directed at the original; satire directed elsewhere lacks that justification merely as satire but may establish fair use under the factors. Transformation informs purpose without absorbing the other factors. Requesting or being denied permission does not defeat an otherwise fair use. Creative character remains relevant, though it contributes little to separating fair from unfair parody, which ordinarily uses recognizable expression. Quantity and qualitative importance must reasonably relate to the critical purpose; recognizable or central material is not excessive solely because it is central. Repetition and potential substitution remain relevant, with no fixed allowance. For transformative parody, commerciality alone creates no market-harm presumption. Examine substitution for the original and legitimate potential derivatives, including widespread comparable conduct; lost demand caused by criticism or loss of licensing the critical attack itself is not cognizable substitution. Supported nonparody derivative markets remain relevant.

#### Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC, 512 U.S. 622

**Docket or dockets:** No. 93-44.

**Decided:** 1994-06-27.

##### Cable editorial expression receives ordinary First Amendment protection

**Holding:** Cable operators' editorial selection and cable programmers' expression are protected speech, and compulsory carriage burdens those interests. Cable's physical transmission capacity and market power do not justify importing broadcasting's spectrum-scarcity standard or reducing review of this expressive burden to rational-basis review.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts II-A and II-B as relevant to this proposition, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stevens recognizes protected cable expression but does not join these complete portions and is not counted for their formulation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Leathers v. Medlock, 499 U.S. 439: applies its recognition of cable speech and press; its approval of a broad content-neutral tax does not resolve compulsory carriage.
- City of Los Angeles v. Preferred Communications, Inc., 476 U.S. 488: applies its protection of editorial selection and attention to cable's characteristics; it had left the precise standard open.
- Red Lion Broadcasting Co. v. FCC, 395 U.S. 367: confines its scarcity rationale to the broadcasting setting; it supplies no diminished cable standard here.

##### Content classification depends on what the law selects and why

**Holding:** A law burdening protected expression because of its content ordinarily must be necessary and narrowly drawn to serve a compelling interest; a burden justified without reference to content is subject to the appropriate intermediate review. A benign purpose or absence of hostility does not neutralize a content-based operation, while a distinction among speakers or media does not by itself establish content discrimination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part II-B, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg: eight Justices. Stevens does not join that portion; his agreement with the case-specific classification below is distinct.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Simon & Schuster, Inc. v. Members of the New York State Crime Victims Board, December 10, 1991: applies its actual content-based escrow holding; neutral collection remedies remain outside that holding.
- Arkansas Writers' Project, Inc. v. Ragland, 481 U.S. 221: applies its scrutiny of differential burdens dependent on a publication's content.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781: applies justification without reference to content as the classification inquiry.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, March 24, 1993: applies its rejection of benign-purpose neutrality for an unexplained content distinction; its commercial-speech fit holding remains bounded.
- Leathers v. Medlock: applies its distinction between permissible neutral differential treatment and censorial or content-based burdens.

##### The general full-power carriage requirements are content neutral

**Holding:** The general commercial and noncommercial full-power must-carry obligations regulate cable's distribution bottleneck and preservation of independently available broadcast service without selecting the stations' individual messages; intermediate scrutiny therefore governs those obligations. This classification does not decide the distinct low-power local-programming criteria, the market-adjustment provision, or a materially different content-based administration, and creates no general right to compel private editorial publication.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Parts II-C and II-D, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg reject this application.

**Material application:** The classification addresses the general full-power core of §§4–5, whose commercial and noncommercial eligibility, capacity, duplication, signal-quality and channel-position provisions remain distinct. It does not adjudicate the separately reserved low-power programming criteria or market adjustments, authorize an editorial-balance mandate for another medium, or confer both must-carry and retransmission-consent entitlement at once.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Miami Herald Publishing Co. v. Tornillo, 418 U.S. 241: distinguishes compelled reply and interference with newspaper editorial judgment; its protection is not displaced.
- Pacific Gas & Electric Co. v. Public Utilities Commission, 475 U.S. 1: distinguishes its fractured compelled-access reasoning concerning speech-responsive access and attribution; no unanimous categorical rule is attributed to it.
- Leathers v. Medlock: applies its allowance for justified neutral distinctions between media; the cable-specific burden still requires constitutional scrutiny.
- Red Lion Broadcasting Co. v. FCC: supplies no basis for the classification; broadcast scarcity is not extended.

##### Intermediate scrutiny requires advancement and a proportionate speech burden

**Holding:** A qualifying content-neutral carriage requirement must further an important or substantial governmental interest unrelated to suppressing expression and must not burden substantially more speech than necessary to further that interest. The Government need not establish that the law is the least restrictive conceivable alternative, but the actual expressive burden and relevant alternatives bear on whether its fit is sufficient.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. This common standard does not establish common agreement about whether the existing record satisfies it.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- United States v. O'Brien, 391 U.S. 367: applies the important-interest, nonsuppression and incidental-burden requirements to this content-neutral carriage burden.
- Ward v. Rock Against Racism: applies its requirement against substantially excessive burdens while preserving its rejection of least-restrictive-means review.

##### Preserving broadcast availability, diverse sources and fair competition are substantial interests

**Holding:** Preserving free local broadcast service, maintaining diverse sources of information and promoting fair competition in television distribution are important content-neutral interests supporting consideration of the general full-power carriage scheme under intermediate scrutiny. Their importance does not itself prove threatened harm, advancement, adequate fit or the scheme's final constitutionality.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion, Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens and Souter: five Justices. The separate opinions disputing the neutrality of the Act's localism and programming preferences do not join this proposition.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Leathers v. Medlock: applies the distinction between a medium's economic characteristics and selection of protected ideas.
- United States v. O'Brien and Ward v. Rock Against Racism: apply the requirement of a substantial nonsuppressive interest without treating that element as proof of advancement or tailoring.

##### Predictive judgments receive substantial deference and remain subject to review

**Independently majority-supported proposition:** In reviewing Congress's predictive judgments relevant to the carriage scheme, a court must give substantial deference while independently assuring that Congress drew reasonable inferences from substantial evidence. Congress need not compile the formal evidentiary record required of a court or agency; this general principle neither decides whether the evidence here suffices nor joins the four-Justice application requiring additional proof.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Part III-B, supported by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun and Souter, together with Stevens's express agreement in his concurrence at this limited level of generality: five Justices. Stevens does not join Part III-B, its real-harm formulation as a complete passage, or its record-insufficiency application. This is direct agreement on the stated proposition across writings, not a Marks inference or a fifth join in the plurality portion.

**Current force:** Controlling at this express five-Justice level of agreement only; no majority adopts Part III-B's present-record insufficiency application.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Landmark Communications, Inc. v. Virginia, 435 U.S. 829: applies the judiciary's continuing responsibility to review an expressive burden despite legislative findings.
- Sable Communications of California, Inc. v. FCC, 492 U.S. 115: applies the refusal to make congressional findings conclusive; its strict-scrutiny setting does not impose that standard here.

##### Authority of the remand on evidentiary sufficiency

**Authority of the judgment:** The summary judgment is vacated and the case remanded, 5–4, by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, Kennedy and Souter. No controlling rationale exists under Marks for the present-record insufficiency conclusion: only Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Kennedy and Souter adopt it; Stevens agrees in remand without adopting it. The separate grounds do not establish a logical subset. The six controlling propositions above retain only their separately stated support and scope.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Final constitutionality of the general scheme remains open. The district court must consider the separate low-power criteria in the first instance and may address the market-adjustment provision; neither is independently upheld or judicially severed. The initial home-shopping transition clause does not establish current FCC treatment. The remand orders no replacement quota, damages, blanket injunction against the Act or particular interim relief. Any interim relief requires its own properly presented legal basis. The legal standards and the judgment govern; the four-Justice explanation of why further proof is needed remains identified as such.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The summary judgment sustaining the challenged carriage scheme is vacated and the case remanded, 5–4, under the controlling intermediate framework. Final constitutionality remains open. The district court must consider low-power eligibility in the first instance and may consider market adjustments; any interim relief needs its own properly presented basis. No new channel quota, damages or blanket injunction against the Act is ordered.

**Holding:** The reviewed full-power duties burden no substantially greater amount of cable editorial discretion than necessary to protect the accepted interests on this developed facial record. Intermediate scrutiny requires an adequate fit, not the least restrictive imaginable arrangement; the supported predictive inquiry permits final affirmance without further trial here.

Systems with more than 12 channels carry eligible local commercial signals up to one-third of usable activated capacity. This is not a combined commercial-and-educational ceiling. Substantially duplicative commercial signals already represented in carriage and additional same-network affiliates need not be carried; voluntary duplicates and multiple affiliates count toward the obligation. When eligible stations exceed the required number, the operator selects among them, but cannot substitute low power for full power and must choose the elected network's affiliate with the closest city-of-license reference point to the principal headend.

Section535 adds separate educational duties. Systems with 12 or fewer channels carry one qualified local educational signal and may carry more. Systems with 13–36 carry at least one and need not carry more than three, subject to continued carriage; they may carry more. If no qualified local station is present, the former category must import one qualified educational signal and the latter at least one. For that importation, the smallest systems need not remove programming actually provided on March 29, 1990 but must use the first available channel. All qualified local educational stations carried on that date retain carriage unless operator and station give written waiver consent. Systems above36 channels, including those expanding beyond36, carry requesting qualified local educational stations; a system required to carry three need not add stations substantially duplicating another qualified local educational station requesting carriage. This is not a general three-station cap. A13–36-channel system already carrying a State-network educational affiliate need not add another same-network affiliate whose programming substantially duplicates the carried affiliate.

Commercial distant signals require indemnification of increased copyright liability. A commercial station below the specified headend levels (−45dBm UHF; −49dBm VHF) is excluded if it does not agree to bear the costs of delivering a good-quality or baseband signal. Educational carriage is not required without delivery of a good-quality or baseband signal; its distant-signal indemnity exception concerns adding a signal and expressly does not erase the continued-carriage rule. These are actual programming obligations, including primary video, audio and captions. Commercial carriage includes technically feasible program-related blanking-interval or subcarrier material; educational carriage includes technically feasible program-related ancillary material necessary for disability access, education or language purposes. Other ancillary material remains discretionary. Positioning and availability duties also constrain operation; the law does not merely reserve empty channels. No new dispute over ancillary material, positioning, payment, notice or enforcement is decided.

No blanket exemption of legislative facts from summary-judgment rules follows. More burdensome future legislation and supported system-specific displacement challenges remain open. The commercial one-third cap does not combine commercial and educational duties; the educational scheme creates no universal three-station ceiling. No dispute over ancillary programming, positioning, payment, notice, or enforcement is decided.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the reviewed full-power facial judgment, leaving its duties enforceable. No replacement quota, Act-wide injunction, damages, takings disposition, or religious-claim disposition issues. The separate low-power and market-modification classifications remain outside the judgment.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *O'Brien* requires a substantial nonsuppressive interest and fitted burden. *Ward* requires adequate tailoring while preserving concrete alternatives as relevant evidence, without a least-restrictive-means command.

##### I. Congress's content-selected cable restriction requires strict scrutiny

**Holding:** A governmental restriction selecting protected adult cable programming by its sexual content must satisfy strict scrutiny: it must be narrowly tailored to a compelling interest, with the Government bearing the ultimate burden of justifying its means. A merely theoretical alternative is insufficient; actual adult burdens and supported implementation concerns matter.

##### II. The Government has not justified this adult burden against the noticed household alternative

**Holding:** Section 505 cannot impose its complete-blocking or sixteen-hour time-channeling burden on the protected programming at issue because the Government has not established that household-request blocking with adequate notice is comparatively inadequate enough to justify that burden. This is a developed-record failure of necessity, not a finding that adequate notice already occurred, that household blocking is flawless, or that every narrower child-protection measure is invalid.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct joins. Scalia and Breyer dissent from this application.

**Material application:** The alternative is household-request blocking with adequate notice identifying the affected channels, understandable audio/video bleed, possible child exposure without parental knowledge, free blocking and the telephone request method. It includes repeated notice at reasonable intervals and special mailed notice to prior blocking recipients after the adult channel changes. Billing inserts, preview channels and advertisements elsewhere are examples, not cumulative compulsory media. No precise interval, installation deadline, repair protocol or automatic replacement rule is established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The child-risk finding remains; low distributed-device figures during unproved notice establish neither informed refusal nor failed adequately publicized blocking. Delay, malfunction, channel changes and operator costs matter without becoming universal failure findings. No completed notice experiment, guaranteed technology, cost-free installation or digital conversion is assumed.

**Holding:** The declaration and injunction against §505 are affirmed at the adjudicated scope, while §504's distinct requested-blocking command remains effective. The affirmance creates no Supreme Court notice order, statutory publicity command or new authority over nonparty operators, and leaves unreviewed collateral lower relief only at its actual scope and procedural status.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 504, codified at §560, requires a cable operator, upon a subscriber's request and without charge to that subscriber, to fully scramble or block each channel's audio and video so that a nonsubscriber does not receive it. It has no independent indecency or sexually oriented channel trigger. Subsection (b) supplies the same scrambling definition. This command took effect February 8, 1996. Subscriber-selected blocking and §505's default/time restriction are distinct; compliance with one does not automatically satisfy the other. Free service to the subscriber does not mean zero operator equipment or installation cost. Section 504 itself contains no notice program.

Equal protection, vagueness, obscenity enforcement and evidence-supported narrower measures are not decided. The lower court’s announced contractual-notice direction is neither a proved implementation event nor a new Supreme Court notice-power holding; inadequate notice does not automatically restore §505.

- **FCC v. Pacifica Foundation:** Preserves its confined contextual broadcast permission. It does not automatically sustain this cable restriction or withdraw protection from nonobscene adult programming.

FCC:** Applies the distinction between private editorial permission and Congress's integral compulsory restriction. **Holding:** The public portion of the 36-foot clinic clearance, with its retained south shoulder, east shoulder subject to the five-foot setback and no-embankment rule, and prompt-crossing exception, burdens no more speech than necessary on this record to secure driveway access and traffic after narrower obstruction commands failed. **Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part III-A, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Souter and Ginsburg: five on geographic fit. Stevens adds a vote to leave the arrangement intact only against the categorical challenge; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the expressive exclusion.

**Holding:** The north/west extension of the 36-foot exclusion cannot suppress otherwise lawful expression on adjoining private land without a demonstrated connection between that full exclusion and the access or operational harm to be remedied. **Holding:** Paragraph (4)'s noise restriction is valid in its medical-operation context from 7:30 a.m. through noon, Monday–Saturday, during surgical procedures and recovery periods, as to the listed and other sounds within earshot of patients inside the clinic. ## Copied holding: AT&T Corp. v. Iowa

#### AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 97-826, 97-829, 97-830, 97-831, 97-1075, 97-1087, 97-1099 and 97-1141; including California respondents in the AT&T, MCI and FCC petitions.
**Decided:** 1999-01-25.

##### I. National implementing rules coexist with state determinations

**Holding:** Section 201(b) authorizes FCC rules implementing the local-competition obligations added to the Communications Act by §§251–252. That authority includes national standards governing the statutory tasks while state commissions retain the negotiations, arbitrations, individual rates and other determinations Congress assigned to them.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Parts I–II are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices control the general-authority and associated remand propositions; O'Connor, Thomas and Breyer dissent on their distinct allocation grounds.

##### II. The rural-exemption and agreement rules require review within the correct allocation

**Holding:** The categorical absence-of-federal-authority grounds for vacating Rules 51.405 and 51.303 and displacing otherwise lawful federal rules from state implementation cannot stand. Unresolved substantive objections return for review with the actual statutory rural-exemption, agreement-approval and state-law preservation limits intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six-Justice coalition controls Part II. The remand does not adopt a new rule resolving the returned substantive objections.

##### III. A network element need not be a detachable physical object

**Holding:** Section 153(29)'s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment, as well as the physical facilities or equipment themselves. Eligibility under that definition does not establish that §251(d)(2) requires unbundled access to every eligible function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Those eight supply a controlling majority; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on the independent definition component.

##### IV. Necessity and impairment must perform independent limiting work

**Holding:** In selecting required network elements under §251(d)(2), the FCC must give independent limiting effect to necessity for proprietary elements and impairment for network elements generally, considering realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent's network. Rule 51.319 is set aside because the adopted comparison and impairment standard did not perform that task.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IV is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, giving the complete rule seven votes. Souter dissents from invalidation.

##### V. The Act imposes no freestanding own-facilities prerequisite

**Holding:** A requesting telecommunications carrier need not own some local facilities merely to qualify to seek unbundled access under §251(c)(3). Its entitlement to particular elements remains governed by §251(d)(2) and the lawful conditions of access and combination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part V is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the independent own-facilities prerequisite, without a ground.

##### VI. Existing combinations need not be dismantled

**Holding:** Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prohibits an incumbent from separating requested network elements already combined in its network unless the requesting carrier asks for separation. Unbundled availability and valuation do not require physical destruction of an existing combination.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Scalia's Parts VI–VII; those parts control the existing-combination and individual-arrangement rules with their stated limits.

**Holding:** Section 252(i) permits Rule 51.809's access to an individual interconnection, service or network-element arrangement in an approved agreement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions. The rule preserves proof of greater provision costs or technical infeasibility and limits availability to a reasonable period after public filing.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same nine-Justice coalition joins Part VII and controls this rule and its qualifications.

## Copied holding: United States v. Mead

#### United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1434.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** The revisable, transaction-specific letters issue from many offices and do not govern unrelated importers as regulations do. Their place in protest and judicial review shows administration rather than delegated interpretive lawmaking. The Solicitor General's representation, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, establishes Customs' official position but does not establish congressional delegation to this mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Formal rulemaking and adjudication are evidence of delegation, not exclusive prerequisites. A label or independent judicial factfinding does not alone defeat lawful deference. Later ruling-change procedures do not retroactively govern the 1993 action. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations remain outside the grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

##### Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** Customs' dictionary, commercial-usage and ring-binding reasons must be evaluated. Changed treatment may weaken consistency without eliminating all persuasive force; expertise and official endorsement likewise establish neither persuasion nor the answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Skidmore consideration is independent judgment, not a lesser mandatory deference or fixed weight. No choice between bound diaries and the residual duty-free category is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.

##### The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with consideration of the ruling letter's reasons according to their actual persuasive force under Skidmore. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither definitive classification commands a majority. The return rests on exclusion of permissible agency reasoning, not a missing product fact, a general right to first lower-court interpretation or a need for agency action. The distinct noncontrolling textual and deference grounds for sustaining the category do not combine into a Court construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit must independently classify the planners while considering the actual persuasive force of Customs' reasons. Any assessment or refund depends on that classification and entry calculations; no amount, new hearing, signature search or future rulemaking is required.


## Copied holding: New York v. United States,

#### New York v. United States, 505 U.S. 144 (1992)

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563.
**Decided:** June 19, 1992.

**Companion or consolidated matters:** No. 91-543, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-558, County of Allegany, New York v. United States et al.; No. 91-563, County of Cortland, New York v. United States et al. The same component-specific relief applies in all three dockets.

For the ripe-review holding in *New York v. United States*, June 19, 1992, Nos. 91-543, 91-558 and 91-563, see Federal Courts — Ripeness of future statutory obligations.

##### Whether the Waste Act's general responsibility clause independently commands state regulation

**Holding:** Read as a whole, the 1985 Low-Level Radioactive Waste Policy Amendments Act's general state-responsibility language describes the objective implemented through its monetary, access and take-title mechanisms and adds no independently enforceable command to regulate. That plausible construction avoids serious structural problems without contradicting Congress's arrangement.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The construction does not erase the express compulsory title and liability terms or authorize judicial rewriting; those terms receive their own constitutional review.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to identify the structural problem with a freestanding state-legislative command.
- Gregory v. Ashcroft, 501 U.S. 452 (1991): applied; displacement of the usual federal balance requires statutory clarity.
- Edward J. DeBartolo Corp. v. Florida Gulf Coast Building & Construction Trades Council, 485 U.S. 568 (1988): applied; a plausible construction avoiding serious constitutional problems is preferred, without judicial rewriting.

##### Whether the Act's surcharge collection and milestone-conditioned distributions are lawful federal monetary inducements

**Holding:** Congress may authorize the Act's interstate waste surcharges, collect a share for a segregated federal spending program, and condition distributions on explicit, disposal-related milestones. On this record these conditions are authorized inducements; segregation of the funds and state choice whether to qualify do not remove the program from the spending power.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-A of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** The program serves safe disposal, supplies clear notice of the disposal conduct and deadlines earning distributions, and relates the conditions to that expenditure. No independent constitutional prohibition breached by satisfying those conditions is identified. The federal tax on interstate commerce is not independently challenged.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Dole's general-welfare, notice, relatedness, independent-constitutional-limit and noncompulsion requirements remain. No numerical coercion threshold or authority to condition unrelated programs is adopted.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the monetary incentives in all three dockets. Lawful surcharge collection and distributions remain operative; the monetary 1993 take-title-or-forfeit alternative is not the compulsory 1996 mechanism severed below.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied; general welfare, clear notice, relatedness, independent constitutional limits, and the boundary against compulsion remain in force.
- Wyoming v. Oklahoma, January 22, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished on the decisive express-congressional-authorization ground; unilateral state discrimination remains invalid.
- Prudential Insurance Co. v. Benjamin, 328 U.S. 408 (1946): applied; Congress may authorize otherwise impermissible interstate-commerce burdens.
- Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman, 451 U.S. 1 (1981): applied as Dole's notice requirement; the milestones unambiguously state what earns a distribution.

##### Whether Congress may authorize higher access charges and exclusion of waste from jurisdictions missing statutory disposal deadlines

**Holding:** Congress may conditionally authorize sited States and regional compacts to increase access charges and exclude waste from jurisdictions missing the Act's deadlines. These provisions do not compel an unsited State to legislate, spend, or administer a federal program because the consequences of its refusal fall on waste generators seeking disposal, and the State remains free to choose its own regulatory response.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III-B of O'Connor's opinion on this incentive, joined by White, Blackmun, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: eight Justices. Stone-Zsela separately agrees; no formal join is attributed to him.

**Material application:** Declining the milestones exposes private waste producers to increased costs and eventual loss of outside disposal access; it does not itself transfer obligations to the State or require an appropriation. Political pressure from affected residents is distinct from a legal command, and New York remains free to regulate in its own manner.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The decision supplies no federal disposal fallback, no duty on sited States to accept New York waste and no permission for discrimination beyond Congress's express authorization.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The judgments are affirmed as to the access incentives in all three dockets. The authorized higher charges and exclusion remain operative.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied by analogy to a lawful alternative borne outside the unwilling State government; the actual federal fallback in Hodel is not fabricated here.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): applied in its cooperative-regulation scope; incentives do not establish unlimited power to direct legislation.
- Northeast Bancorp, Inc. v. Board of Governors, 472 U.S. 159 (1985): applied; express congressional authorization removes the otherwise applicable interstate-commerce restraint.
- Fort Gratiot Sanitary Landfill v. Michigan Department of Natural Resources, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; unauthorized origin-based private-waste exclusion remains impermissible.
- Chemical Waste Management v. Hunt, June 1, 1992 simulated decision: distinguished; its invalid unauthorized discriminatory surcharge is not approval or disapproval of the express federal choices here.

##### Whether Congress may require a State to regulate private waste or assume ownership, possession and liability, and whether state officials' consent cures the command

**Holding:** Congress may not use its ordinary commerce authority to make an unwilling State choose between regulating private waste generators under federal instructions and accepting their waste into state ownership and possession with associated liability. Combining two unauthorized commands does not create a lawful option, and state officials' advocacy for the statute does not enlarge Congress's powers or estop this noncompacting State from challenging the command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The compulsory 1996 mechanism leaves no lawful way for New York to decline federal program responsibility: the State must arrange disposal under federal instructions or take private generators' waste and assume liability for all damages directly or indirectly incurred through failure to take prompt possession. New York joined no compact.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual compact duties, direct regulation of state market activity, and distinct constitutional bases for state-court duties and judicial enforcement of federal law remain reserved. Garcia's generally applicable regulation of state activities survives; traditional-governmental-function immunity is not revived.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The compulsory title, possession and associated liability mechanism is declared invalid in each docket; the severability holding below determines the surviving Act.

**Material judgment:** The take-title component is reversed, 6–3: Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support invalidation; White, Blackmun and Stevens would affirm. O'Connor's five-Justice opinion supplies the controlling rationale independently of Stone's concurrence.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): clarified and extended; its legislative-compulsion boundary applies to mandatory state ownership/liability offered as the alternative to state regulation.
- FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742 (1982): distinguished and retained; federal-law adjudication and limited consideration in a preemptible field do not authorize an unavoidable duty to run this program.
- Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority, 469 U.S. 528 (1985): distinguished, not overruled; generally applicable regulation of state activity is not the issue decided.
- South Carolina v. Baker, 485 U.S. 505 (1988): distinguished and retained; compliance with federal rules governing state activity does not decide compulsory state regulation of private actors.
- Buckley v. Valeo, 424 U.S. 1 (1976): applied by structural analogy; governmental consent does not enlarge constitutionally assigned powers.
- INS v. Chadha, 462 U.S. 919 (1983): applied by structural analogy; assent to legislation does not cure its departure from the constitutional allocation of authority.
- Testa v. Katt, 330 U.S. 386 (1947): distinguished; the Supremacy Clause's duties for state judges remain intact.
- West Virginia ex rel. Dyer v. Sims, 341 U.S. 22 (1951): distinguished in the asserted compact/estoppel setting; this Court decides no duty under a compact New York actually joined.
- National League of Cities v. Usery, 426 U.S. 833 (1976): not revived; Garcia's rejection of traditional-governmental-function immunity remains effective.

##### Whether the remaining monetary and access incentives deny New York a republican form of government

**Holding:** Assuming without deciding that this Guarantee Clause claim may be heard, the surviving monetary and access incentives do not deny New York a republican form of government because they preserve its legislative choices and officials' accountability to its voters. The Court decides neither general Guarantee Clause justiciability nor a separate Guarantee objection to the already invalidated take-title command.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's Part V, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas, supplies five votes for the assumption-and-merits rationale. Stone-Zsela separately rejects the claim after finding this limited institutional claim justiciable. White, Blackmun, and Stevens vote to reject the challenge as part of their affirm-all disposition but do not join Part V.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court assumes this claim is justiciable without deciding that issue. Stone's affirmative decision to hear the limited institutional claim has no other join and creates no controlling justiciability rule. No separate Guarantee ground is decided for the already invalidated take-title command.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The remaining Guarantee Clause challenge is rejected in all three dockets. The surviving incentives leave New York's legislative choice and elected officials' accountability intact.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Luther v. Borden, 48 U.S. 1 (1849): left intact in its rival-government recognition setting; no blanket justiciability or nonjusticiability rule is announced here.
- Baker v. Carr, 369 U.S. 186 (1962): retained; the Court does not resolve the general political-question boundary.
- South Dakota v. Dole, 483 U.S. 203 (1987): applied to the continuing choice under conditional spending; no general Guarantee Clause justiciability rule is inferred.
- Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264 (1981): applied to the distinction between an authorized conditional arrangement and compulsory state regulation; it supplies no blanket Guarantee Clause justiciability rule.

##### Whether the invalid compulsory 1996 take-title mechanism is severable from the remainder of the Act

**Holding:** An invalid provision is severed when the remainder remains fully operative and it is not evident that Congress would have rejected that remainder independently. The compulsory 1996 title, possession, and associated liability mechanism is severable here; the remaining financing and access incentives stay in force, without a court-created replacement program or a duty on other States to accept New York's waste.

**Proposition-level authority:** O'Connor's opinion of the Court, joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, and Thomas: five of nine, controlling. Stone-Zsela agrees separately without a formal join.

**Material application:** The remaining financing and access provisions function independently and advance disposal capacity. Absence of a severability clause creates no presumption against severance; the Act's objective is capacity, not the invalid sanction for its own sake.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The retained 1993 monetary take-title-or-forfeit alternative remains distinct from the invalid compulsory 1996 mechanism. The Court creates no federal substitute or duty on other States to accept waste.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Sever the compulsory 1996 title, possession and associated liability mechanism and remand all three dockets for corresponding declaratory relief. The remaining financing and access arrangements stay in force; no damages, disposal site or federal administrator is ordered.

**Material judgment:** Severance and remand are supported by Stone, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas, 6–3; White, Blackmun and Stevens would uphold the entire Act.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Alaska Airlines, Inc. v. Brock, 480 U.S. 678 (1987): applied without changing the severability standard or treating silence as nonseverability.
- Reagan v. Farmers' Loan & Trust Co., 154 U.S. 362 (1894): applied; the failure of an enforcement device need not defeat the independently operative statutory purpose.
- United States v. Jackson, 390 U.S. 570 (1968): applied; an invalid enforcement measure may be severed while preserving an independently functioning scheme.

#### Loving v. United States, 517 U.S. 748

**Docket or dockets:** No. 94-1966.
**Decided:** June 3, 1996.

##### Did Congress authorize the challenged presidential limits on military capital murder punishment?

**Holding:** UCMJ Articles 18 and 56 authorize the President to restrict the availability of the death punishment Congress selected for Article 118(1) and (4); Article 36 supplies additional statutory confirmation and procedural authority. Together they authorize the challenged RCM 1004 restrictions, but not new capital offenses, death under Article 118(2) or (3), or punishment exceeding or contradicting Congress's choice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Holding I, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (eight Justices). Thomas concurs in the judgment only and supplies no join to this or the following three holdings.

**Material application:** Withholding death from part of an already capital class is a punishment limit under Articles 18 and 56 even though it is not a numerical maximum. Congress defined the capital murders and death-or-life punishment; these factors neither add an offense nor enlarge its penalty. Article 36 supports implementing procedures: generally recognized federal criminal-trial principles apply so far as the President considers practicable; regulations may neither contradict nor be inconsistent with the Code, must be uniform insofar as practicable, and must be reported to Congress. Calling a factor procedural does not independently establish substantive authority.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Whether Article 36 alone would suffice remains undecided. The assignment permits neither creation of civilian crimes nor vague or otherwise unconstitutional capital factors. The decision uses the governing pre-1990 Article 36 and pre-1991 RCM 1004; it determines no actual transmission or reporting violation.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Neal v. United States*, 516 U.S. 284, retains the boundary between statutory punishment and valid delegated sentencing rules: these restrictions implement rather than replace Congress's punishment choice.

##### Must Congress additionally name capital aggravating factors expressly to authorize these restrictions?

**Holding:** The enacted punishment-limiting authority suffices without an additional formula expressly naming capital aggravating factors. Article 106a's later express espionage provisions neither withdraw that authority nor make it inapplicable to these murder offenses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Holding II; the same eight Justices directly join.

**Material application:** Articles 18 and 56 expressly assign punishment limitations. More explicit treatment of a distinct offense in Article 106a, including its additional presidential factor under Article 36, does not repeal that assignment. Existing authorization also did not expire when additional narrowing became constitutionally necessary on the assumption used in this case; an otherwise authorized use remains within the grant when a later constitutional requirement makes it necessary.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Actual enacted authority remains essential. Executive practice, congressional silence and usefulness cannot substitute for it. The Court resolves no general criminal-law clear-statement question beyond the challenged assignment.

##### Is this military capital-sentencing assignment constitutionally nondelegable or inadequately bounded?

**Holding:** Congress may assign the President identification of narrowing factors for military capital sentencing when Congress defines the capital offenses and punishment, supplies intelligible legal boundaries, and confines implementation to the President's assigned military-administration role. Congress's offense selection, death-or-life alternatives, express punishment-limiting assignment and consistency restraints adequately bound this delegation even assuming that criminal and capital delegations require greater specificity than the ordinary standard.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Holding III; the same eight Justices directly join.

**Material application:** For this authority question, the Court assumes that these military murders require additional eligibility narrowing and that the three factors would adequately narrow if Congress enacted them. Neither Article I's military rulemaking clause nor the assumed capital requirement categorically requires Congress personally to prescribe each limiting factor. The President's executive and Commander-in-Chief responsibilities reinforce the assigned military role without enlarging statutory authority. The enacted limits and that actual role suffice without an additional express factor-selection principle; executive safeguards or panel unanimity could not cure absent congressional authority.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court does not decide whether criminal delegations generally require enhanced specificity, whether additional eligibility narrowing is indispensable throughout military capital proceedings, whether Article 118 alone supplies all necessary narrowing, or whether independent Article II authority could support factors without congressional delegation. No rule for newly created actors, unrelated executive functions, wholesale civilian capital-doctrine incorporation, or military immunity from structural review follows.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** *Mistretta v. United States*, 488 U.S. 361, supplies guided sentencing implementation with Congress retaining legislative responsibility; its conditional capital discussion did not decide these factors. *Touby v. United States*, 500 U.S. 160, supplies review of an actual criminal delegation without resolving its open general specificity question; its drug-scheduling criteria are not a universal checklist. *Weiss v. United States*, 510 U.S. 163, and *Ryder v. United States*, 515 U.S. 177, retain constitutional review of military adjudicator arrangements and their respective appointment, tenure and defect-specific remedial limits.

##### What relief follows from the rejection of the presented authority challenges?

**Holding:** The statutory-authorization, additional-clear-authorization, categorical-nondelegability and inadequate-guidance objections establish no error and furnish no ground to disturb the Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces' judgment. The affirmance rests on that absence of error, without harmless-error review or adjudication of unpresented individual-factor and collateral claims.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, Holding IV; the same eight Justices directly join. Thomas separately supports the judgment alone.

**Material application:** The panel found premeditated murder during robbery, another Article 118 murder in the same case, and actual perpetration of the felony-murder killing under the authorized rule. Sustaining the rule's source does not independently adjudicate every factor's validity, every sentencing safeguard, or every underlying conviction claim. Congress's offense boundaries constrain delegation without necessarily satisfying the separate assumed eligibility-narrowing requirement.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The constitutional need for a service connection in capital military offenses remains open; no new prerequisite is imposed. Survival of an aggravator does not replace correction of a separately established injury.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The convictions and sentence remain undisturbed against these authority challenges; no resentencing, replacement life sentence, new guilt trial, acquittal, release or execution is ordered. Article 71 independently requires a final judgment as to legality through applicable military and Supreme Court review routes where review has not been waived or withdrawn. Death cannot be executed without presidential approval; the President may commute or remit it but may not suspend it. Article 71(c)(2)'s separate waiver-or-withdrawal route concerns dismissal or punitive discharge, and subsection (d)'s suspension power excludes death. The Court supplies no presidential approval, execution date or determination that every execution prerequisite is complete.

**Material judgment:** Affirmed, 9–0; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer support the judgment. The common rationale has eight direct supporters; Thomas's judgment vote is not a ninth rationale join.

##### Commercial parody and the integrated statutory inquiry

**Holding:** Section 107 requires the four fair-use factors to be considered together; commercial parody may qualify, and neither profit motive nor a parody label settles the inquiry or creates a dispositive presumption of unfairness. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

##### The original song remains protected creative expression

**Holding:** The published original song's creative nature weighs within §107's second factor and is not erased by the claim of parody, but it contributes little to distinguishing fair from unfair parody because parody ordinarily must use recognizable expressive works. Neither publication nor creativity categorically determines fairness, and the four factors remain part of one inquiry.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: its attention to creative character remains; its first-publication concern does not determine this published-song parody, and no general unpublished-work rule is decided.

##### The lyrical reference is justified; the extent of musical borrowing remains open

**Holding:** Under §107's third factor, both the quantity and qualitative importance of the borrowing must reasonably relate to the critical purpose; taking a recognizable opening or the original's core is not excessive solely because it is central. The brief identifying lyrical reference followed by substantially different critical lyrics takes no more than the parody reasonably requires, but whether the repeated musical borrowing is excessive remains for further consideration with purpose and potential substitution; no fixed allowance or sampling violation is established.

**Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. Kennedy's separate caution does not withdraw his full join. Direct unanimous authority; no Marks inquiry.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: qualitative substantiality remains important, but its application to valuable excerpts is distinguished where recognizable borrowing serves criticism; no automatic permission to take the whole or repeat the core follows.

**Holding:** For this transformative parody, commerciality alone does not raise a presumption of market harm; §107 requires examination of substitution for the original and legitimate potential derivatives, including the effect of widespread comparable conduct. **Proposition-level authority:** Souter's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this proposition and its stated limits. **Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Sony, 464 U.S. 417: commercial market-harm language is confined to its context; no universal presumption follows for this transformative use.
- Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: protection of legitimate existing and potential derivative markets remains; criticism injury and lost permission for criticism are distinguished from substitution.

**Holding:** Fair use remains an affirmative defense, and defendants seeking summary judgment must establish it on a record permitting judgment as a matter of law; rejection of the appellate commerciality and harm presumptions does not shift that burden. The Sixth Circuit's adverse fair-use judgment is reversed and remanded without reinstating defense summary judgment, because justified musical taking and the potentially material nonparody derivative market remain unresolved; no final infringement, royalty, damages, or injunction ruling follows.

Requesting or being refused permission supplies no veto over otherwise fair use. No first-publication claim, bad faith from the disputed release/request sequence, independent sampling violation or constitutional immunity is decided.

##### What relationship to the collective work section 201(c) requires

**Holding:** Absent an express transfer supplying different rights, §201(c) permits reproduction and distribution of a contribution as part of the particular collective work, a recognizable revision of that work, or a later collective work in the same series. Source identification alone does not permit separately offering the contribution in a new compendium; the actual presentation must retain the contribution's relationship to the collective work, regardless of medium.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; the same seven directly adopt all three questions and their product-specific applications.

**Material application:** The compiler's selection and arrangement copyright coexists with the contributor's separate copyright. A revision can change format, layout or contents and need not preserve every item or exact typography. Electronic medium and search functionality alone are neutral. But bibliographic attribution supplies history, not present membership in the collective work, and cannot authorize an otherwise detached contribution.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 201(d) transferability remains undecided because the uses exceed the substantive privilege even if transferable. Fair use was not asserted; public display is not adjudicated. Actual licenses and genuine electronic revisions remain possible on their own terms.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Stewart v. Abend preserves distinct ownership of underlying material without deciding this revision privilege. Campbell and Quality King require attention to the specific statutory right and limitation; neither supplies an unasserted fair-use or first-sale reproduction defense.

##### Whether NEXIS, New York Times OnDisc and General Periodicals OnDisc satisfy that relationship

**Holding:** NEXIS and New York Times OnDisc reproduce and distribute the articles as separately retrieved text outside a perceptible revision of their source issues; General Periodicals OnDisc preserves particular page images but likewise separates the article pages from the original issue as a collective whole. None of these proved uses is privileged by §201(c), and the contrary summary-judgment defense fails for each product.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; the same seven directly adopt all three questions and their product-specific applications.

**Material application:** The first two products deliver individual text articles rather than a perceptible revised issue. GPO retains photographs, captions, advertisements and other matter on particular page images, but omits a navigable presentation of the issue as a whole. Its page context receives separate consideration. Unlike continuously reproduced microform, user retrieval of detached articles or possible assembly of issue contents does not establish that the publisher presents the article within a revision. An intact intermediate transfer does not validate different downstream reproductions.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The Court requires neither physical page-turning nor an immutable facsimile. It decides the proved products and uses, not all digital archives, later interfaces or unpresented articles; no library exception, compilation-originality determination or contributory-liability rule is adopted.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Stewart's separate-ownership principle and Campbell's and Quality King's right-specific approach apply to the demonstrated reproduction and distribution uses; archival usefulness creates no additional statutory privilege.

##### What the affirmed liability judgment requires and leaves for relief

**Holding:** The Second Circuit's judgment rejecting the §201(c) defense and directing judgment for the authors on the challenged uses is affirmed. Damages and equitable relief remain for the lower courts under their applicable statutory and equitable requirements; an appropriate injunction may issue, but neither automatic deletion of all freelance material nor a compulsory license follows from this judgment.

**Proposition-level authority:** Kennedy's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg; the same seven directly adopt all three questions and their product-specific applications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Newsday's contract defense was waived below and Time's was rejected; neither is pressed here or reopened. Whitford's written agreement remains background, without a new construction or a finding that all authors lacked contracts. No license entitlement or right to judgment on every unreviewed issue follows.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The directed author judgment on the challenged uses stands. Lower courts apply the actual damages and equitable standards, including appropriate attention to archival public value, without enlarging the privilege or compelling a license. No fixed damages, willfulness determination, universal injunction, wholesale deletion mandate or new deadline is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Campbell and Quality King preserve the distinction between the settled statutory question and individualized relief under the remaining lawful record.

### Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., No. 92-1292

Merits decision, 1994-03-07

Argued November 9, 1993; decided March 7, 1994. On writ of certiorari to the United States Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit, 972 F.2d 1429, which reversed the defendants' summary judgment, 754 F.Supp. 1150, and held the use unfair. The questions concern commercial parody under §107, the relation between borrowing and criticism, and the proof of market effect.

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Associate Justices Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg participated at argument and decision. All nine were seated at both milestones; no recusal or nonparticipation is established.

The Court reverses the Sixth Circuit's adverse fair-use judgment and remands for the integrated statutory inquiry. It does not reinstate summary judgment for 2 Live Crew or decide ultimate fair use.

Reversal and remand, 9–0: Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine reject the categorical commerciality and market-harm treatment, adopt the narrow lyrical application, and leave musical extent and the potentially material nonparody derivative market for further consideration. No Justice opposes this disposition. No final infringement, royalty, damages, or injunction determination is made.

Souter delivers the opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, and Ginsburg. All nine join every stated holding and remand limit. Kennedy also concurs, joined by no other Justice; his full join remains intact.

Section 107 requires the four fair-use factors to be considered together; commercial parody may qualify, and neither profit motive nor a parody label settles the inquiry or creates a dispositive presumption of unfairness. The work must reasonably permit perception of original-directed criticism to claim parody's particular justification for borrowing; satire directed elsewhere lacks that justification merely as satire but may still establish fair use under the actual factors, and requesting or being denied permission does not defeat an otherwise fair use.

The Court holds that the character of the use must be examined rather than inferred from its sale. Section 107 expressly includes commerciality within purpose and character, alongside the remaining statutory considerations. Sony's home-copying analysis and Harper & Row's protection of first publication cannot be detached from the uses and substitution those cases examined. A new work that comments on an earlier work may employ its expression for a different critical purpose. The reported contrast between the original's romantic presentation and the new lyrics reasonably permits perception of such criticism here; that conclusion does not certify taste or ultimate fairness. Recognizable borrowing may be needed to identify criticism's object, while borrowing for unrelated satire requires its own justification. Transformation informs purpose and character but is neither indispensable to every fair use nor a substitute for amount and market analysis. The owner's refusal to license criticism gives it no statutory veto over an otherwise fair use. No independent constitutional immunity is adopted.

Sony Corp. v. Universal City Studios, Inc., 464 U.S. 417: its contextual statutory inquiry is applied; its commerciality language does not establish a universal presumption against sold criticism, and its home-time-shifting result remains intact. Harper & Row, Publishers, Inc. v. Nation Enterprises, 471 U.S. 539: distinguished as commercial appropriation in a protected publication market; its four-factor method remains binding, without a categorical rule against commercial parody.

The published original song's creative nature weighs within §107's second factor and is not erased by the claim of parody, but it contributes little to distinguishing fair from unfair parody because parody ordinarily must use recognizable expressive works. Neither publication nor creativity categorically determines fairness, and the four factors remain part of one inquiry.

The Court does not strip the original song of the protection afforded expressive composition. Harper & Row recognizes the importance of a work's character in the fair-use inquiry, and the original here falls close to copyright's creative core. But that observation cannot perform the same work in every setting. Parody commonly must confront expressive material recognizable to its audience; the source's creativity therefore does little by itself to separate lawful criticism from unlawful appropriation. Treating it as an automatic barrier would negate the critical-use inquiry the statute requires. Treating it as irrelevant would make the opposite mistake by excusing courts from considering an enumerated factor. The Court preserves both the original's protected character and the need to evaluate how it is used. Because the song was published, no claim concerning control of first publication is resolved. The statutory amendment providing that unpublished status alone does not bar fair use remains effective but does not decide this dispute.

Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: its attention to creative character remains; its first-publication concern does not determine this published-song parody, and no general unpublished-work rule is decided.

Under §107's third factor, both the quantity and qualitative importance of the borrowing must reasonably relate to the critical purpose; taking a recognizable opening or the original's core is not excessive solely because it is central. The brief identifying lyrical reference followed by substantially different critical lyrics takes no more than the parody reasonably requires, but whether the repeated musical borrowing is excessive remains for further consideration with purpose and potential substitution; no fixed allowance or sampling violation is established.

The Court holds that a parody may need the audience to recognize the work it criticizes. Harper & Row makes qualitative importance relevant, but its concern with taking valuable expression does not eliminate the need to ask why it was taken. A rule forbidding the original's recognizable core would often prevent criticism from identifying its object. Here the short lyrical reference performs that identifying function and is followed by a markedly different critical account; its extent is reasonably related to that purpose. This favorable application to the lyrics does not excuse every musical use. The recurring bass riff and broader musical borrowing require their own assessment of how much serves identification or criticism and how much may substitute for protected expression. Recognition supplies a reason for some taking, not a numerical license or blanket permission for repetition. The Court leaves that musical inquiry open and does not find an independent sampling violation. Ultimate fair use still requires the integrated statutory balance.

Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: qualitative substantiality remains important, but its application to valuable excerpts is distinguished where recognizable borrowing serves criticism; no automatic permission to take the whole or repeat the core follows.

For this transformative parody, commerciality alone does not raise a presumption of market harm; §107 requires examination of substitution for the original and legitimate potential derivatives, including the effect of widespread comparable conduct. Harm because criticism diminishes demand and foregone licensing of the critical attack itself are not cognizable substitutive injury, but a supported nonparody rap-derivative market remains relevant and this record proves neither its injury nor its absence.

The Court holds that the fourth factor protects copyright markets without granting control over criticism. Harper & Row protects an owner's valuable publication and derivative markets; Sony examines the likely economic consequences of the use rather than dispensing with proof in every setting. A critical parody can reduce demand because its audience accepts the criticism, and that effect differs from supplying a competing substitute. Nor can an owner establish a protected market merely by asserting a lost license for the attack itself. Yet the presence of criticism does not eliminate every ordinary derivative market. A nonparody rap adaptation may compete in a legitimate market distinct from the market for this critical work. Defense affidavits concerning the original's audience and popularity do not settle that separate question. The lower court therefore erred in presuming injury from commerciality, but correction of that error does not establish a complete absence of harm. The Court requires a record-based market inquiry and leaves the ultimate balance open.

Sony, 464 U.S. 417: commercial market-harm language is confined to its context; no universal presumption follows for this transformative use. Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: protection of legitimate existing and potential derivative markets remains; criticism injury and lost permission for criticism are distinguished from substitution.

Fair use remains an affirmative defense, and defendants seeking summary judgment must establish it on a record permitting judgment as a matter of law; rejection of the appellate commerciality and harm presumptions does not shift that burden. The Sixth Circuit's adverse fair-use judgment is reversed and remanded without reinstating defense summary judgment, because justified musical taking and the potentially material nonparody derivative market remain unresolved; no final infringement, royalty, damages, or injunction ruling follows.

The Court corrects legal shortcuts but does not replace missing evidence with a presumption favoring the defense. Harper & Row establishes the affirmative nature of fair use and permits appellate determination when sufficient facts support it. Here the original-directed critical purpose and the limited lyrical taking resolve important parts of the analysis. They do not answer how much recurring music is reasonably justified or whether that taking substitutes in an ordinary derivative market. The defendants' showing about the original's audience cannot do the work of an undeveloped inquiry into such a market. Conversely, the copyright owner cannot prevail merely because the parody was sold or permission was refused. Further consideration must combine all four statutory factors under the rules stated today, retaining the defendants' burden and the summary-judgment posture. The Court therefore reverses the appellate determination without restoring the district court's final defense judgment. It decides no amount, license obligation, or equitable remedy and leaves unappealed collateral claims undisturbed.

Harper & Row, 471 U.S. 539: applied to preserve the fair-use defense burden and the requirement of sufficient facts for a conclusive judicial determination.

Sony's time-shifting result and contextual statutory analysis remain; its commerciality and harm language does not establish the rejected universal presumptions for this critical use. Harper & Row retains its protection of creative expression, qualitative substantiality, legitimate markets, and the affirmative-defense burden; its first-publication and substitutive-excerpt setting does not decide every commercial parody. Feist's originality rule is unchanged. No independent constitutional exemption supplies the decision.

Effective March 7, 1994, §107 requires an integrated assessment of actual critical purpose, the protected work's nature, the relation between taking and purpose, and cognizable substitution. Transformation informs purpose and character but does not absorb the other factors. The Court finds reasonably perceptible original-directed criticism and a justified limited lyrical reference; the repeated music and possible nonparody rap market remain unresolved. The original's published creative character remains relevant, though of little help in separating fair from unfair parody. Neither a license refusal nor an allegation of parody decides the defense. Satire remains eligible to make its own statutory showing. Defendants retain their affirmative-defense and summary-judgment burdens.

Kennedy concurs, joined by no other Justice, and joins the Court's opinion in full. A parody claim must identify a reasonably apparent critical relationship to the original; altered lyrics or an asserted humorous purpose cannot automatically justify using valuable music as a sales device. He emphasizes examination of how the borrowing serves that criticism and remains cautious about ultimate fairness on this record. His concern is compatible with the Court's limited finding that criticism can reasonably be perceived and with its favorable lyrical application. It supplies neither a refusal to join nor a heightened mandatory test beyond §107, and it does not foreclose satire from making its own fair-use showing.

**Source notes.** The [Sixth Circuit opinion and dissent, 972 F.2d 1429](https://static.case.law/f2d/972/cases/1429-01.json), provide the reported music/lyric comparison, summary-judgment posture, competing market evidence, disputed release chronology, and unreviewed claims. The [official report, 510 U.S. 569, predecision account at 571–574 and reported record in the appendices](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep510/usrep510569/usrep510569.pdf), supports dates and the described materials. The [official §107 text and amendment history](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-1994-title17/html/USCODE-1994-title17-chap1-sec107.htm) verify the October 24, 1992 unpublished-work amendment; this case involves a published song. Original recordings and trial affidavits were not independently reviewed; no sampling finding or conclusive derivative-market fact is supplied.

The case returns for proceedings consistent with all four statutory factors. The extent of justified musical borrowing and evidence of a legitimate nonparody rap-derivative market require further consideration. The Court does not direct a final fair-use or infringement judgment, an award, a compulsory license, or an injunction. The unappealed state-claim preemption determination and denial of leave to add a Lanham Act claim remain outside review. The initial release/request sequence is disputed; no bad faith is found from it, and the sampling allegation is not adjudicated.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Breyer, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Breyer joins the operative cost rules and separation of standard setting from implementation, but reaches them through the whole structure and the reported 1970 sponsor and Senate materials and later 1977 and 1989 materials showing a technology-forcing choice. He rejects deriving the result from isolated section 109 words or a general presumption that rational cost consideration always needs unusually clear authorization. Ordinarily he would construe genuine ambiguity to allow attention to seriously disproportionate adverse consequences; Congress displaced that choice here. Scientific discretion can consider severity, numbers affected, distribution, uncertainty, contextually trivial risks and comparative health consequences. That discussion supplies no implementation-cost exception, zero-risk mandate or finding that a particular technology will emerge. He retains his direct joins to the other holdings and the bounded-execution characterization.

**Justice Stevens, joined by Souter, concurring in part and in the judgment.** The statutory limits and scientific criteria sufficiently confine EPA, and an agency could not cure an absence of legislative policy by choosing one itself. They join both propositions and every judgment component. Their disagreement is with describing authorized rulemaking as inherently nonlegislative: in their view it is legislative rulemaking that Congress may constitutionally delegate when sufficiently bounded. Their characterization does not enlarge the agency's authority or validate a standardless assignment.

**Justice Thomas, concurring.** Thomas joins the Court's holdings under existing delegation precedent but reserves whether an intelligible principle always satisfies Article I. His prior military-specific position in Loving supplies no general civilian-delegation theory. No party has presented reconsideration of the governing standard, so he does not demand it here or withhold the present joins.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Stevens, joined by Justice Breyer, dissents. They would recognize the demonstrated archival uses as revisions only when both conditions are met: each article expressly identifies its source collective work, and substantially the rest of that collective work is readily accessible to the reader of the individual file at the same time. The publisher's editorial selection and issue association must remain available. A detached article with only a source label does not qualify.

In their view the files corresponding to an issue can constitute its revision, and aggregation of those revisions into a searchable archive does not destroy that status. The library and microform analogies demonstrate why a reader's selection of one item need not change the character of the collection. GPO's images add page context, but their conclusion does not depend on images alone. They regard the accessible editorial contents and express issue association as sufficient; the Court instead requires a perceptible collective presentation these interfaces lack. Neither side permits alteration of the contribution itself or use in an unrelated anthology merely because its former source is identified.

The dissent preserves the author's ownership and the compiler's limited privilege under §§103(b) and 201(c). Stewart's different 1909 Act dispute does not erase the modern statutory separation; Campbell and Quality King likewise require giving the actual statutory permission its full effect. Archive completeness and clearance costs reinforce their construction rather than create a free-standing public-benefit exemption or prove inevitable deletion.

On §201(d), they reject complete nontransferability: a publisher may farm out printing or distribution of its own collective work or qualifying revision. That does not permit assignment authorizing the contribution's inclusion in a wholly different collective work. Because the Court and the court of appeals did not decide transferability, they decline further resolution. They would reverse the §201(c) construction, vacate the dependent liability disposition and return for consistent statutory proceedings, without reopening the unpressed contract defenses or guaranteeing final publisher judgment on every other preserved issue. They order no fixed license, universal archive remedy or revision of unpresented agreements.
