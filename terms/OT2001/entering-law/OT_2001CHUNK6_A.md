## Standards and Tests selection: Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

### Punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review

**Governing question:** What instructions govern punitive forfeiture and Excessive Fines review?

**Current rule:** Forfeitures to the sovereign under §881(a)(4) and (a)(7), and punitive criminal RICO forfeiture, are subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Civil labeling, in rem form and concurrent remedial aims do not remove the covered statutory forfeitures' partly punitive character. Coverage and lawful conviction or statutory connection do not decide excessiveness. The court must separately assess the exaction in relation to its offenses; the property-offense connection may matter but review is not limited to instrumentality alone. No fixed ratio, mandatory exhaustive factor list, incorporation holding or automatic return follows. The gross-disproportionality inquiry assesses the offense’s gravity without converting the case’s lawful-funds, limited-harm and sentencing findings into a mandatory checklist or its dollar amounts into constitutional ceilings. The distinct due-process defense for a separately held automobile interest is stated in ‘Reasonable prevention and a separately held automobile interest’; other constitutional innocent-owner questions remain unresolved. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish criminal punishment for Double Jeopardy, which uses the independent civil-intent/clearest-proof inquiry. Alexander’s independent expressive-burden inquiry remains distinct. Punitive criminal forfeiture under §982(a)(1) is a fine and violates the Clause when grossly disproportionate to the gravity of the offense. Statutory connection alone does not answer that question, and instrumentality is no categorical prerequisite excluding all currency-reporting forfeitures. The appellate constitutional proportionality determination is reviewed independently; underlying supported factual findings receive clear-error review.

**Authority by component:**

- These statutory forfeitures are covered fines: Austin v. United States — These statutory forfeitures are covered fines, 1993-06-28. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas (9 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- Historical in rem form does not negate punishment: Austin v. United States — Historical in rem form does not negate punishment, 1993-06-28. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (5 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- Remand leaves excessiveness distinct from statutory connection: Austin v. United States — Remand leaves excessiveness distinct from statutory connection, 1993-06-28. Blackmun’s Opinion of the Court, this proposition; Stone-Zsela, White, Blackmun, Stevens, O’Connor, Souter (6 Justices). Direct majority support at the stated level of generality; no Marks aggregation.
- 3. Punitive criminal forfeiture requires Excessive Fines review: Alexander v. United States — 3. Punitive criminal forfeiture requires Excessive Fines review, 1993-06-28. Souter's Part III, joined by Stone-Zsela, White, O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas; six Justices adopt coverage and remand. Blackmun, Stevens and Kennedy accept the alternative excessiveness remand without joining Part III or adopting a proportionality formula.

- Limited resolution of innocent-owner protection: A qualified owner defense applies to this nuisance forfeiture, 1996-03-04; Stone-Zsela with Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer (five).
- Distinct constitutional classification: II. Excessive Fines coverage does not itself establish Double Jeopardy punishment, 1996-06-24; O’Connor with Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (five).

## Standards and Tests selection: Primary securities fraud through source deception

### Primary securities fraud through source deception

**Governing question:** When concealed securities use of entrusted nonpublic information violates §10(b).

**Current rule:** A person acting with scienter who owes a source loyalty and confidentiality, feigns fidelity and secretly trades using entrusted material nonpublic information commits deception connected with the transaction when trading consummates that concealed disloyalty. The source need not be the trading counterparty. Duty, deception and the securities-transaction connection are indispensable; superior information alone creates no market-wide duty. Full disclosure of the trading plan to every person owed the relevant duty removes this theory's deception, but disclosure to one principal may leave another duty unsatisfied and does not legalize conduct under other law.

**Authority by component:**
- Primary source-deception theory: O'Hagan, United States v. O'Hagan — I. Deceptive use of entrusted information in securities trading can be primary §10(b) fraud, Stevens with O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer (six), 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** United States v. O'Hagan, 1997-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule supplies neither status-based liability nor a criminal mental-state shortcut from Central Bank's private accessory rule. Every confidential relationship and every trial objection is not resolved; source participation in the transaction is not a Court-adopted extra requirement.

**Holdings navigation:** United States v. O'Hagan (1997-06-25) — I. Deceptive use of entrusted information in securities trading can be primary §10(b) fraud.

## Standards and Tests selection: Knowing false best-execution undertakings in securities transactions

### Knowing false best-execution undertakings in securities transactions

**Governing question:** Primary deception when a broker accepts an uninstructed customer order while intending not to perform its represented execution undertaking.

**Current rule:** A broker-dealer that accepts a customer order without price instructions while knowingly intending to disregard the best-execution undertaking it represents can commit primary fraud when the representation is materially false and the other private-action elements are proved. Breach of care, inferior execution, fiduciary breach or broker status alone is insufficient. The customer must prove the actual undertaking, material deception and culpability, legally sufficient reliance, causation and actual loss. Execution at the national best bid and offer or through SOES supplies no categorical safe harbor when genuine evidence supports knowing disregard of reasonably available better terms. Contemporary reasonable availability includes order size, trading characteristics, speed, clearing costs, access expense and difficulty, as well as price; later law or technology does not itself establish earlier obligations or feasibility.

**Authority by component:**

- Knowing undertaking and independent private-action elements; contemporary execution inquiry: Canonical Decision Record `merrill_lynch_v_kravitz_merits_1998-11-12.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / A knowingly false execution undertaking can be primary securities fraud; The disputed contemporary execution evidence precludes the categorical summary judgment, 1998-11-12; unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-11-12.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal venue, best-possible-price guarantee or search of every quotation is imposed. Custom is relevant but not conclusive immunity for a knowingly false undertaking. General primary-fraud recklessness is unresolved; the separate assistance standard is not imported.

**Holdings navigation:** [Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. Kravitz — A knowingly false execution undertaking can be primary securities fraud](HOLDINGS.md#a-knowingly-false-execution-undertaking-can-be-primary-securities-fraud-1); [Merrill Lynch, Pierce, Fenner & Smith, Inc. v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Knowing sham sales of securities options

### Knowing sham sales of securities options

**Governing question:** Whether selling an actual option while secretly intending never to honor it is primary securities deception.

**Current rule:** A seller who obtains value for an actual option while concealing a contemporaneous intention not to honor the option deceives the purchaser in connection with that security’s sale. An established oral option is not excluded merely for lack of writing, and its purchase satisfies the purchaser-or-seller requirement. The private plaintiff must still establish materiality, required culpability, legally sufficient reliance, causation and recoverable loss. Later nonperformance, negligence or commercial unfairness alone establishes neither the initial deception nor those remaining requirements.

**Authority by component:**

- Option acquisition, concealed initial intent, oral form and independent private-action requirements: Canonical Decision Record `Wharf_Holdings_Ltd_v_United_International_Holdings_Inc_merits_2001-05-21.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Deception at the sale of the option, 2001-05-21; Thomas’s unanimous opinion.

**Present operation effective:** Wharf, May 21, 2001.

**Open question:** Treatment of an agreement unenforceable under state law remains outside the decision; state enforceability is not adopted as an invariable additional §10(b) element. Foreign-transaction limits and independent state punitive remedies are not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** [Wharf (Holdings) Ltd. v. United International Holdings, Inc., 532 U.S. 588 — Securities fraud in an option sale](HOLDINGS.md#wharf-holdings-ltd-v-united-international-holdings-inc-532-us-588).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal claim preclusion as a removal defense

### Federal claim preclusion as a removal defense

**Governing question:** Whether an earlier federal judgment makes a state-law complaint removable under §§1331 and 1441.

**Current rule:** A prior federal judgment’s claim-preclusive effect is a defense and does not convert a complaint presenting only state-law claims into a federal cause. Complete statutory preemption instead requires Congress to displace the state claim with an exclusive federal cause supplying its legal character. A conclusive judgment defense or an artful-pleading label supplies no such cause. Without original jurisdiction or an actual later cure, dependent federal merits judgments must be vacated and the action returned to state court.

**Authority by component:**

- Federal claim preclusion remains a defense; dependent judgment correction: Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana, 1998-02-24; Scalia for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-02-24.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not decide actual preclusion or foreclose otherwise authorized enforcement proceedings. Moitie’s claim-preclusion rule remains; its footnote supplies no general removal exception.

**Related current rules:** Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal requires actual jurisdiction before trial, not efficiency or finality alone.

**Holdings navigation:** Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana — Federal-question removal and claim preclusion.

## Standards and Tests selection: Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal

### Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal

**Governing question:** Effect of a preserved removal error when complete diversity actually exists before trial and through judgment.

**Current rule:** Absent complete diversity at removal, a timely remand motion requires remand under the applicable statutes. A later lawful dismissal of the nondiverse party does not validate the earlier refusal, waive the objection or require extraordinary pretrial review. If actual §1332 diversity arises before trial and continues through judgment, that earlier defect alone does not require vacating the completed federal judgment. Actual jurisdiction at trial and judgment is indispensable; consent, efficiency and finality cannot supply it.

**Authority by component:**

- Earlier statutory duty: Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis — Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis — The erroneous refusal to remand remains error; bounded judgment-preservation rule: Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis — Actual jurisdiction before trial preserves the completed judgment against this earlier defect. O’Connor, all nine Justices, December 10, 1996.

**Present operation effective:** Caterpillar, December 10, 1996.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal cure for removal defects, collusive diversity manufacture or collateral review of an entered covered remand is authorized. Independently available fees, sanctions and ordinary merits appeals retain their own requirements.

**Holdings navigation:** Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis (1996-12-10) — The erroneous refusal to remand remains error; Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis (1996-12-10) — Actual jurisdiction before trial preserves the completed judgment against this earlier defect.

## Standards and Tests selection: Intervention before a shareholder’s derivative-settlement appeal

### Intervention before a shareholder’s derivative-settlement appeal

**Governing question:** Whether an otherwise nonparty shareholder may appeal a derivative settlement solely after appearing and objecting under Rule 23.1.

**Current rule:** Notice, shareholder interest and a heard objection under Rule 23.1 do not themselves create appellate party status. An otherwise nonparty shareholder must properly become a party through intervention to appeal the derivative settlement judgment. The opportunity to assist the settlement court by objection differs from authority to continue the corporate litigation on appeal. Review by an actual party and an appropriate appeal from an actual intervention denial remain available under their own conditions.

**Authority by component:**

- Derivative-settlement party status and retained intervention review: Canonical Decision Record `california_public_employees_retirement_system_v_felzen_merits_1999-01-20.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / 1. Rule 23.1 objection does not itself confer the party status needed for this appeal; 2. Intervention review and nonparty due-process protection remain distinct, 1999-01-20; five direct joins.

**Present operation effective:** 1999-01-20.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal prohibition of all nonparty appeals or general duty to intervene to preserve an independent claim follows. Due-process limits on binding genuinely unrepresented persons remain, without creating an appellate entitlement, late-intervention right or collateral remedy by themselves.

**Holdings navigation:** [California Public Employees' Retirement System and Florida State Board of Administration v. Paul Felzen et al. — 1. Rule 23.1 objection does not itself confer the party status needed for this appeal](HOLDINGS.md#1-rule-231-objection-does-not-itself-confer-the-party-status-needed-for-this-appeal-1); [California Public Employees' Retirement System and Florida State Board of Administration v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

### Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants

**Governing question:** The due-process prerequisite to claim or issue preclusion against a nonparty.

**Current rule:** Identical interests, vigorous advocacy, resources, public importance and fiscal reliance alone do not authorize binding absent persons' own legal rights. Binding requires constitutionally sufficient consent, control, genuine privity, adequate class or representative litigation, or another recognized relationship or special remedial arrangement consistent with due process. Constitutionally inadequate representation is not independently cured by notice, opportunities to object or opt out; notice alone imposes no general duty on strangers to intervene. Issue preclusion, like claim preclusion, requires a lawful basis to bind the person. Genuine representative public-right adjudication and use of precedent in a later case remain distinct from extinguishing a stranger's personal claim.

**Authority by component:**

- Representation independent of notice and exclusion rights: Matsushita — Constitutional representation and unresolved review questions limit the remand, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part IV-A; eight Associates reserve complete collateral-review scope in IV-B.
- Nonparty binding and direct personal tax claims, including the issue-preclusion alternative: Richards — Holdings II–IV, June 10, 1996; Stone-Zsela for all nine.

**Present operation effective:** Richards, June 10, 1996; Matsushita supplies the retained representation component.

**Open question:** The Court assumes without deciding whether adequate representation may cure absent notice in some representative proceedings. It neither requires individual notice in every such action nor constitutionalizes every federal class-action requirement. The broader adequacy of officials' representation of constituents, ordinary state-law-error review and Matsushita's collateral-review boundary are not resolved.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367; Richards v. Jefferson County, 517 U.S. 793.

## Standards and Tests selection: Certification of settlement-only classes

### Certification of settlement-only classes

**Governing question:** Rule 23 prerequisites and the order of decision for a proposed settlement-only class.

**Current rule:** A settlement-only class must satisfy Rule 23(a) and the applicable Rule 23(b) requirements, including typicality, adequate representation and (b)(3) predominance. With no trial proposed, trial-management difficulties need not be examined. The bargain and settlement's actual operation inform the remaining requirements but do not relax them; Rule 23(e) fairness and settlement desirability do not substitute. Notice and opt-out rights cannot cure materially conflicting bargaining interests inadequately protected by common representatives and counsel. Common exposure or a common payment mechanism does not eliminate material governing-law, injury, causation, defense and claim differences for predominance. A nonmerits certification defect logically antecedent to class-dependent standing questions may be resolved first, without assuming jurisdiction to adjudicate anyone's substantive claim; any individual action that proceeds must independently satisfy Article III.

**Authority by component:**
- Antecedent certification and settlement requirements: Amchem, Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — I. Certification may be resolved before the particular class-dependent standing questions and Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — II. Settlement does not replace Rule 23's requirements, Thomas with all seven other participants (eight; O'Connor absent), 1997-06-25.
- Representation and predominance boundaries: Amchem, Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — III. The undivided representation is inadequate on this record and Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — IV. Individual claims and governing-law differences defeat predominance, Thomas with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** Amchem, 1997-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal subclass architecture or categorical bar on nationwide, mass-tort, issue, future-claim or settlement classes is adopted. Constitutional notice and reserved collateral-review questions remain open.

**Related current rules:** Certification of multistate damages classes supplies the common Rule 23(b)(3) showing and trial-jury constraints; this entry supplies the distinct settlement-only application. Article III power before disputed statutory merits forbids assumed jurisdiction for substantive merits, without prohibiting the logically antecedent nonmerits certification resolution here.

**Holdings navigation:** Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor (1997-06-25) — I. Certification may be resolved before the particular class-dependent standing questions; Amchem Products, Inc. v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Mandatory limited-fund settlement classes

### Mandatory limited-fund settlement classes

**Governing question:** What supports contested Rule 23(b)(1)(B) certification on a limited-fund rationale.

**Current rule:** The asserted limit must exist independently of the parties’ negotiated ceiling. Proponents must demonstrate an ascertained maximum insufficient for aggregate claims. The traditional characteristics—a definitely ascertained inadequate fund, devotion of the whole qualifying fund to those claims and equitable treatment of claimants sharing the relevant theory of recovery—are presumptively necessary; proponents must justify a departure. Settlement desirability and predicted eventual depletion do not establish their own maximum or replace applicable Rule 23(a) and (b)(1)(B) requirements.

**Authority by component:**

- Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “2. An independent maximum and the qualified traditional limited-fund framework”, 1999-06-23; Thomas Part II-A with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven votes supply independent limit and aggregate insufficiency; Part II-B with O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg supplies the six-vote presumptive characteristics and justified-departure burden. Stone-Zsela’s stronger whole-fund condition is not counted.

**Present operation effective:** Ortiz, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Rule 23(b)(3) predominance is not imported. No previously segregated asset, certain insolvency, mechanical equality overriding valid priorities or exclusive bankruptcy route is invariably required. Materially conflicting present/future or insurance allocation interests retain the independent-representation requirement; fairness review does not replace protection in negotiation.

**Open question:** The full range of justified departures and whether unliquidated mass-tort claims can ever satisfy the category remain unresolved. No universal exhaustion probability, opt-out right, whole-company retention prohibition or replacement agreement is adopted.

**Related current rules:** Certification of settlement-only classes retains Amchem’s applicable-prerequisite and independent-representation rules. Ortiz applies their bounded logically antecedent nonmerits sequence without assuming jurisdiction to decide individual substantive claims.

**Holdings navigation:** [Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp. — 2. An independent maximum and the qualified traditional limited-fund framework](HOLDINGS.md#2-an-independent-maximum-and-the-qualified-traditional-limited-fund-framework).

## Standards and Tests selection: State-created liberty in prison discipline

### State-created liberty in prison discipline

**Governing question:** When substantive prison law creates a protected liberty interest.

**Current rule:** A State creates protected prison liberty when substantive law meaningfully restricts officials’ authority to impose a significant deprivation. Mandatory words alone are insufficient: courts examine the genuine constraint in the whole disciplinary scheme and whether the restraint is meaningful rather than routine. This is a sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule; state codification is unnecessary for directly protected constitutional liberty.

**Authority by component:**

- Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty: Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty, 1995-06-19. Breyer’s Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg; five Justices adopt this sufficient, nonexclusive state-entitlement rule.

**Present operation effective:** 1995-06-19; the component authorities identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Other direct constitutional, unexpected-restraint and sentence-duration protections are not displaced. A protected interest and required process need their own determinations. No new multifactor test, categorical rejection of punitive constraints or automatic liability follows.

**Holdings navigation:** Substantive state constraints and meaningful prison liberty.

## Standards and Tests selection: Protected silence concerning disputed sentencing facts

### Protected silence concerning disputed sentencing facts

**Governing question:** Whether a plea waives self-incrimination protection and whether silence proves punitive facts at sentencing.

**Current rule:** A guilty plea does not itself waive the privilege concerning disputed sentencing facts while testimony presents a genuine risk of additional criminal punishment. The plea and actual voluntary admissions remain usable, and an accepted plea does not convert limited admissions into consent to compelled testimony about reserved facts. A sentencing court may not draw an adverse factual inference from protected silence to determine the circumstances and details of the crime; the Government must prove disputed facts through lawful affirmative evidence. Once sentence is fixed, conviction final and no further adverse criminal consequence remains, the privilege has no remaining incriminating risk to protect, but finality alone does not eliminate another genuine exposure.

**Authority by component:**

- Mitchell v. United States — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “I. The plea did not waive privilege concerning reserved quantity”, 1999-04-05; Breyer Part I, all nine Justices. Part I supplies continuing privilege and actual-waiver limits.
- Mitchell v. United States — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “II. Protected silence cannot prove the crime’s circumstances at sentencing”, 1999-04-05; Breyer with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Part II supplies the no-inference rule; Part III requires the Government to establish beyond a reasonable doubt that constitutional error did not contribute to the affected sentencing determination, not merely that other sufficient evidence exists.

**Present operation effective:** Mitchell, April 5, 1999.

**Open question:** Remorse, actual acceptance-of-responsibility claims and immunity-backed testimony remain undecided. The rule creates no acceptance-reduction entitlement or new proof allocation for such a reduction and classifies no sentencing fact as an element.

**Holdings navigation:** [Mitchell v. United States — The plea did not waive privilege concerning reserved quantity](HOLDINGS.md#the-plea-did-not-waive-privilege-concerning-reserved-quantity-1); [Mitchell v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Self-incrimination privilege despite professed innocence

### Self-incrimination privilege despite professed innocence

**Governing question:** Whether a witness’s denial of wrongdoing defeats an otherwise supported privilege against self-incrimination.

**Current rule:** A witness may claim the privilege despite denying wrongdoing when the actual circumstances give reasonable cause to fear incrimination from truthful answers, including an evidentiary link. The court must identify real exposure; the witness’s fear alone and remote or imaginary possibilities are insufficient. Privilege does not depend on an admission or preliminary finding of guilt.

**Authority by component:**

- Real exposure and no innocence-based forfeiture: Canonical Decision Record `Ohio_v_Reiner_summary-disposition_2001-03-19.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Reasonable exposure survives a witness's assertion of innocence, 2001-03-19; reasoned per curiam holding; no public tally or individual lineup.

**Present operation effective:** Ohio v. Reiner, 2001-03-19.

**Exceptions and limits:** The federal privilege, authority to grant state transactional immunity and the accused’s independent trial protections are separate inquiries. Recognition of privilege establishes neither innocence nor the validity or necessity of an immunity grant.

**Holdings navigation:** [Ohio v. Reiner, 532 U.S. 17 — Testimonial exposure and professed innocence](HOLDINGS.md#ohio-v-reiner-532-us-17).

## Standards and Tests selection: Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat

### Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat

**Governing question:** When health or safety risk permits a covered provider to exclude a person.

**Current rule:** Exclusion requires a significant risk to others’ health or safety that cannot be eliminated by modification of policies, practices or procedures or auxiliary aids or services. The provider must make an individualized reasonable judgment using current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence, addressing nature, duration, severity, probability of injury and reasonable mitigation. The assessment uses information reasonably available to the provider and profession when service is refused, not a subjective sincerity test; neither absolute safety nor confirmed past transmission is universally required. Sincere professional belief receives no special legal deference. Reasonable public-health judgments receive special but nonconclusive weight subject to credible scientific challenge, only within the source’s supported role, date and scope.

**Authority by component:**

- Holding 3: unanimous objective method; Holding 4: five direct votes for founded nonconclusive public-health weight: Bragdon v. Abbott, No. 97-156. — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** Private professional policy is not automatically an official public-health assessment; neither an inferred official conclusion nor later information substitutes for the actual relevant source. An alternative hospital offer alone supplies no independent defense or duty to accept it; service outside professional competence is not required and objectively justified precautions remain permissible.

**Holdings navigation:** Bragdon v. Abbott — ADA disability and direct-threat assessment in public accommodations.

## Standards and Tests selection: Mitigating measures in actual ADA disability

### Mitigating measures in actual ADA disability

**Governing question:** Whether actual-disability coverage under original §12102(2)(A) is assessed with measures the individual uses.

**Current rule:** Successful mitigating measures do not erase the underlying impairment. Whether it substantially limits a major life activity is assessed without treating the beneficial effects of correction or medication as eliminating that limitation. The individual still must prove a substantial limitation of a major life activity; a diagnosis or the use of lenses or a prescription alone does not establish disability, and no corrected or treated condition is automatically covered. Actual functioning with treatment remains relevant to qualification, reasonable accommodation and legitimate safety standards, which are resolved on their own elements. No duty to stop treatment follows.

**Authority by component:**

The same-day decisions independently resolve Bragdon’s general mitigation reservation.

**Present operation effective:** Murphy and Sutton, June 22, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Record-of and regarded-as branches remain separate. No general invalidation of EEOC authority or every regulation, new episodic-impairment threshold, qualification ruling or safety defense follows.

**Related current rules:** Actual disability and substantial limitation of reproduction retains Bragdon’s individual reproductive-risk application; Objective assessment of an ADA direct threat remains distinct from coverage.

## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation](HOLDINGS.md#trade-court-factfinding-does-not-eliminate-lawful-delegated-interpretation-1). [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).


**Related current rules:** Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation governs independent judicial consideration when controlling force is unavailable.

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

- Required persuasive consideration of tariff-letter reasons: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the Customs-letter application and specified persuasive inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).

## Standards and Tests selection: SSDI representations in ADA qualification proof

### SSDI representations in ADA qualification proof

**Governing question:** How an earlier total-disability representation affects an ADA employment plaintiff’s qualification showing.

**Current rule:** Applying for or receiving SSDI neither automatically estops an ADA plaintiff nor creates a special adverse presumption. ADA qualification asks whether the person can perform essential job functions with or without reasonable accommodation, a different inquiry from SSDI. A plaintiff confronting an apparently inconsistent earlier total-disability representation must explain it sufficiently for a reasonable juror, accepting the earlier statement as true or made in good faith, still to find qualification at the relevant time. The plaintiff retains the ordinary qualification burden; good faith alone, a bare contradiction or an unexplained statutory label does not satisfy it.

**Authority by component:**

- Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “SSDI does not create an automatic bar or special adverse presumption under the ADA”, 1999-05-24; Stevens, all nine Justices. “An apparent inconsistency still requires an adequate explanation” supplies the reasonable-juror explanation requirement and ordinary burden.

**Present operation effective:** Cleveland, May 24, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The explanation must address material factual inconsistency, including relevant time, functions and accommodation. A sufficient explanation guarantees neither trial nor success. General judicial-estoppel law, purely factual contradictions, benefits awards and other statutory relationships remain undisturbed.

**Holdings navigation:** [Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp. — SSDI does not create an automatic bar or special adverse presumption under the ADA](HOLDINGS.md#ssdi-does-not-create-an-automatic-bar-or-special-adverse-presumption-under-the-ada-1); [Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp. — An apparent inconsistency still requires an adequate explanation](HOLDINGS.md#an-apparent-inconsistency-still-requires-an-adequate-explanation).

## Standards and Tests selection: Federal driving standards and experimental ADA waivers

### Federal driving standards and experimental ADA waivers

**Governing question:** Whether the ADA requires accepting an experimental waiver instead of an otherwise applicable substantive federal vision criterion.

**Current rule:** Where the federal commercial-driving vision criterion remains substantive and the waiver program is an experiment collecting evidence for possible revision, the ADA permits an employer to insist on the ordinary criterion rather than participate in that experiment. The employer need not justify the Government’s standard afresh or independently prove direct threat merely to invoke that program-specific entitlement. Experimental permission is not a completed determination that the substantive criterion has become unnecessary.

**Authority by component:**

- Albertson’s, Inc. v. Kirkingburg — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “The experimental waiver did not require the employer to abandon the ordinary federal vision standard”, 1999-06-22; Kennedy Part I, all nine Justices. The following holding, “Reliance on this federal criterion does not require an independent direct-threat finding”, supplies the bounded defense clarification.

**Present operation effective:** Albertson’s, June 22, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** No general license to disregard substantive federal exemptions, mandatory exclusion of waiver holders, unsafe-driving finding or approval of unsupported private standards follows. A regulation making an exemption equivalent to ordinary qualification and other waiver systems remain unresolved. The nonessential disability-method discussion supplies no natural-compensation rule.

**Holdings navigation:** [Albertson’s, Inc. v. Kirkingburg — The experimental waiver did not require the employer to abandon the ordinary federal vision standard](HOLDINGS.md#the-experimental-waiver-did-not-require-the-employer-to-abandon-the-ordinary-federal-vision-standard-1); [Albertson’s, Inc. v. Kirkingburg — Reliance on this federal criterion does not require an independent direct-threat finding](HOLDINGS.md#reliance-on-this-federal-criterion-does-not-require-an-independent-direct-threat-finding).

## Standards and Tests selection: Perceived occupational breadth under the original ADA

### Perceived occupational breadth under the original ADA

**Governing question:** What perceived limitation supports a regarded-as-working claim.

**Current rule:** On a working theory under the original ADA, the employer must perceive restriction in a class of jobs or broad range of jobs in various classes relative to comparable training, skills and abilities and the reasonably accessible geographic area. Knowledge of an impairment, rejection from one particular position or a specialized job category, and a claimed regulatory motive do not alone resolve the inquiry. Complete unemployability, exclusion from every position in a vocation or a prescribed percentage is unnecessary. Actual other employment and nationwide reach of a certification rule do not conclusively establish the employer’s perception.

**Authority by component:**

- Murphy v. United Parcel Service, Inc. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Regarded-as coverage requires a perceived substantial occupational limitation”, 1999-06-22; Part II-A, all nine Justices. Supplies the perceived-class framework on the assumed regulatory definitions.
- Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Holding 3 — The alleged perceived restriction is confined to a specialized job category”, 1999-06-22; Stone-Zsela Part III, all nine Justices. Supplies the specialized-category limit while assuming working is a major life activity.

**Present operation effective:** Murphy and Sutton, June 22, 1999.

**Open question:** Whether working invariably is a major life activity and the general validity or deference owed all EEOC definitions remain unresolved. Coverage supplies no qualification, business-necessity or direct-threat finding.

**Holdings navigation:** [Murphy v. United Parcel Service, Inc. — Regarded-as coverage requires a perceived substantial occupational limitation](HOLDINGS.md#regarded-as-coverage-requires-a-perceived-substantial-occupational-limitation); [Sutton v. United Air Lines, Inc. — Holding 3 — The alleged perceived restriction is confined to a specialized job category](HOLDINGS.md#holding-3--the-alleged-perceived-restriction-is-confined-to-a-specialized-job-category).
