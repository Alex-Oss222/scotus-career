# June 17, 2002 entering law — current-term public fields

### United States v. Knights — 2001-12-10

The Fourth Amendment permits the defined probation-condition and individualized-suspicion combination despite ordinary investigative purpose. Existing scope, execution and exclusion rules retain their separate operation; the decision creates no general suspicionless-search or consent-waiver rule.

The FAA supplies no bar for this excluded employment agreement. Independently, an employee's private forum promise does not remove Congress's individual-remedy categories from the EEOC's own enforcement action, even if the promise binds the employee under an independent source. Liability and every actual award remain governed by their statutory predicates, mitigation rules and nonduplication limits. ### United States v. Arvizu — 2002-01-15

Supported observations capable of innocent explanations retain their actual contextual force but gain no automatic positive weight by accumulation. Independent judicial review determines whether their relationships supply individualized suspicion. This pre-stop combination meets that standard without neighborhood stigma, proof of an observed offense or post-stop contraband. The lawful-stop conclusion leaves the independent authority for the ensuing search to the preserved consent questions.

### Lee v. Kemna — 2002-01-22

The adequate-state-ground doctrine now expressly reaches this exceptional application of Missouri's continuance requirements. Useful, regularly applied procedural requirements remain enforceable, but their ordinary legitimacy does not make an exorbitant application adequate. Removal of this particular default barrier leaves the due-process merits, remaining lawful habeas restrictions and entitlement to relief for their own adjudication.

The four-of-seven exception is not a statutory safe harbor from substantive-sham scrutiny. This arrangement's accepted lack of both nontax business purpose and pretax profit potential supports the disallowance. Transactions with meaningful economic substance, mixed findings or an affirmative statutory incentive remain governed by their own legal and factual predicates.

### Guest v. Leis — 2002-02-27

From February 27, 2002, restricted bulletin-board subscriber information receives the stated personal, account-specific inquiry; entrustment alone is not a categorical forfeiture. Six Justices support that rule for confidential credentials and five support its stated broader identifying-record scope. The eight-Justice objective immunity holding preserves the existing personal defense and its conduct-date inquiry. A newly recognized constitutional application does not itself defeat that defense, and personal immunity supplies no institutional defense.

Exact controlling propositions, proposition-level authority, qualifications, precedent treatment and remedy: [exact public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK3_PUBLIC_LAW.md#guest-v-leis-merits-2002-02-27). [Record](../records/guest-v-leis_merits_2002-02-27.md).

**Current procedural stage:** The Sixth Circuit judgment is affirmed on the reviewed individual-capacity subscriber claims. The affected institutional and official-capacity subscriber claims return for personal privacy, actual acquisition, subscriber-specific lawful warrant scope, defendant capacity, applicable source of liability, policy or authorized-decision attribution, causation and preserved defenses under the stated rules. The other adjudicated branches remain undisturbed. The remand does not itself require a trial where an independently sufficient legal or undisputed factual ground resolves a claim.

### Young v. United States — 2002-03-04

The stated three-year lookback excludes a prior bankruptcy stay that actually prohibited collection of the covered tax debt. Motive is immaterial to that legal disability. The tax's substantive burden and statutory priority categories remain unchanged, and other deadlines retain their own text and structure.

Exact controlling propositions, proposition-level authority, qualifications, precedent treatment and remedy: [exact public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK3_PUBLIC_LAW.md#young-v-united-states-merits-2002-03-04). [Record](../records/young-v-united-states_merits_2002-03-04.md).

**Current procedural stage:** The First Circuit's nondischargeability judgment is affirmed. The prior stay is excluded from the lookback, and the surviving liability may be collected through otherwise lawful procedures. The reviewed dischargeability issue is concluded.

### United States v. Craft, No. 00-1831 — 2002-04-17

Michigan entireties restrictions do not defeat federal attachment to the taxpayer's existing beneficial rights considered together. The lien follows those actual rights, while their valuation, competing priorities, enforcement authority and the nondebtor spouse's independent interests retain separate legal work. The rule does not turn a bare future expectancy into property or assume identical marital-property law in every State.

Exact controlling propositions, proposition-level authority, qualifications, precedent treatment and remedy: [exact public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK4_PUBLIC_LAW.md#united-states-v-craft-merits-2002-04-17). [Record](../records/united_states_v_craft_merits_2002-04-17.md).

**Current procedural stage:** Further proceedings determine valuation and lawful treatment of the escrow under the rights-specific attachment rule. The excluded transfer theory and the unrelated cross-appeal remain outside this review; the Court does not adopt either former lower-court remedial calculation as the attachment value.

### Compaq Computer Corp. v. Commissioner — 2002-05-28

From May 28, 2002, profit analysis of a dividend transaction with established ownership and foreign tax liability must treat the gross dividend and the legally available credit consistently. This demonstrated market arrangement satisfies substance and purpose, and its reviewed sham-dependent adjustment and penalty cannot stand. The decision adds that application to the existing substance doctrine without replacing it with a nominal-profit threshold, an ownership-only safe harbor or a universal test for cases with different economic findings. Independent statutory qualification requirements retain their force. The 1997 holding-period rule supplies no governing standard for this 1992 transaction.

### National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan — 2002-06-10

Known discrete acts retain separate filing periods despite related timely conduct. One hostile environment is timely as a practice when a contributing act falls within the window, subject to actual continuity, substantive proof, existing attribution law and distinct remedial limits. The two-year backpay ceiling does not revive stale discrete liability. Traditional equity and background evidence retain their separate functions. Concealed-act discovery timing remains undecided because the record presents known discrimination.

### United States v. Bollin — 2002-06-10

Section 853(e) cannot restrain an untainted potential substitute asset solely because §853(p) may later make it forfeitable. The rule governs a continuing §853(e) order as well as its original statutory basis; a request made after conviction does not eliminate the property limitation. Actual substitute forfeiture and protection of property already ordered forfeited remain governed separately by §§853(p) and 853(g). State exemption law cannot defeat authorized federal forfeiture, and federal supremacy cannot create an omitted grant. The present IRA restraint must be removed absent a distinct valid order; the broader appellate-counsel claim and the unpreserved intermediary issue remain outside the holding.

### United States v. Farmer — 2002-06-10

A supported, specific claim that continuing civil seizure prevents use of potentially legitimate assets for trial counsel triggers a prompt focused hearing. The initial substantial showing remains distinct from the preponderance showing for release. The defendant bears the confined burden on both legitimacy and actual need, subject to the government's opportunity to contest resources and show probable cause of taint. Successful proof requires access to the legitimate funds needed unless an independently established lawful ground prevents release. This rule does not shift the ultimate forfeiture burden or convert every seizure into a counsel violation. The hearing entitlement has nine direct votes, the Mathews explanation eight votes excluding Scalia, and the complete release allocation eight votes excluding Stone-Zsela.

Exact public holding, authority, treatment and remedy: [public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK6_PUBLIC_LAW.md#united-states-v-farmer-merits-2002-06-10).

### EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. — 2002-01-15

**Justice Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Justice Scalia.** They join Part I because Circuit City's governing employment exclusion removes the FAA as a source of enforcement, despite their continuing disagreement with that precedent. They also join Part III's ordinary liability, remedy and nonduplication conditions. The Commission may bring its independent public action and cannot itself be compelled to arbitrate.

They reject Parts II and IV because nonparty assent does not answer whether a court should grant relief for Baker's individual injury in disregard of his own enforceable commitment. Section 706(g)'s requirement of appropriate relief and the distinction between permission to sue and entitlement to a particular remedy, in their view, require respect for a valid beneficiary promise even though broader public relief remains available. First Options addresses actual assent to arbitration, not that distinct remedial question; Great-West similarly preserves attention to the statute authorizing the particular remedy.

The FAA cannot establish enforceability here. They would therefore vacate its categorical restriction and remand to consider any properly available independent state-law ground, the promise's actual scope, and its consequence for individual relief under their view. They presume neither a valid independent ground nor a completed arbitration, and ordinary preservation limits remain. Their position does not compel arbitration of the agency or affirm the existing restriction on a displaced FAA premise.

These published positions are noncontrolling at the stated scope. [Public opinion account](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK2_PUBLIC_LAW.md#eeoc-v-waffle-house-inc-merits-2002-01-15).

### United States v. Arvizu — 2002-01-15

**Justice Scalia, concurring, joined by no other Justice.** He joins the Court's full application of governing law and its reversal and bounded remand. He retains his Ornelas dissent's preference for deferential review and his objection that giving weight to an inferential conclusion can be conceptually inconsistent with independently deciding the ultimate issue. A factual description of the children's waving can receive factual protection; whether it is suspicious cannot be sheltered from constitutional judgment simply by calling it an officer inference. Reversal follows here even under traditional independent review. His join applies Ornelas as governing law without endorsing its original choice of standard, dispensing with innocent explanations or authorizing the subsequent search.

### Lee v. Kemna — 2002-01-22

Kennedy, joined by Scalia and Thomas, would affirm because Rule 24.10 retained a substantive function apart from the written-motion form. Earlier alibi descriptions and earlier attendance did not necessarily establish what the witnesses would then say, whether they could be obtained within a reasonable time, or the current diligence and other facts needed to evaluate the delay. A focused contemporaneous presentation also creates a reviewable record. A known procedural ground may support appellate affirmance even when the trial judge relied on scheduling; an uncited requirement is not thereby useless. Osborne involved repetition of a legal objection already foreclosed, not the initial support for a factual request. The dissent objects that searching earlier portions of the record for substantial compliance can displace the State's actual presentation rule. It does not hold that every formally regular rule is adequate in every application, and relies on no finding about why the witnesses left or whether their alibi was true.

These published positions are noncontrolling at the stated scope. [Public opinion account](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK2_PUBLIC_LAW.md#lee-v-kemna-merits-2002-01-22).

### Guest v. Leis — 2002-02-27

[Record](../records/guest-v-leis_merits_2002-02-27.md)

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Confidential access credentials have an exclusion function materially different from ordinary provider records. She would vacate the categorical dismissal only at that scope and require a claimant-specific showing on remand. Names, addresses and other routine identifying information deliberately conveyed for administration ordinarily fall within Miller and Smith; expected confidentiality alone does not distinguish them. Ferguson and Denius require attention to what was entrusted and the actual use, but do not establish the broader account rule adopted by five Justices. She joins objective personal immunity and rejects a requirement of actual authoritative reliance.

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The three would affirm the subscriber-information judgment on the merits as well as on personal immunity. Miller and Smith do not depend on public dissemination: they address information voluntarily conveyed to a provider, often with an expectation of limited use. Calling the records digital or credential-related does not establish a constitutional distinction from that settled rule. Kyllo's protection of concealed home information does not control operational information held by a different person. Each claimant still must establish a personal interest and an intrusion; the existing equipment and private-message dispositions are distinct. The three join Part II's independent objective immunity ground.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the full privacy rule and both institutional remands but would also vacate personal immunity. After an actionable violation is established under the complete substantive rule, including its reasonable-factual-mistake allowances, he would require the official to identify binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct, prove genuine reliance and show reasonable grounds for treating the permission as valid and applicable. An instruction accurately transmitting the authority may prove reliance; silence, unsettled law, departmental practice, general investigative authority or an ungrounded supervisor or prosecutor assurance would not. Knowing material falsification, concealed disqualifying facts or manufactured authority would defeat reasonable reliance. So would disregard of the authority's apparent limits, apparent unlawfulness under controlling constitutional principles, or a factual mistake already rejected under the substantive merits rule. The protection would include an unexpected change in controlling law that the official could not reasonably anticipate.

He would overrule Harlow and Anderson only insofar as insufficiently clear law independently excuses a proven violation, disapprove Wilson to that extent and retain Elder's consideration of relevant law for the new inquiry. Section 1983's remedial commitment, in his view, warrants that change while concrete reliance protects legitimate official decisiveness. He acknowledges an additional factual inquiry and litigation cost; ordinary pleading and summary-judgment procedures would remain. A valid warrant may defeat the constitutional claim, but its existence alone does not prove actual reliance, and later litigation citations cannot establish the official's 1995 knowledge. A prior judgment granting personal immunity would not itself constitute authority permitting the underlying conduct. He would allow the officials to make the newly required showing on remand. His proposal would not reopen final judgments, change the plaintiff's other elements, create an institutional remedy, displace an independent statutory defense or decide a separate absolute immunity.

### United States v. Craft, No. 00-1831 — 2002-04-17

[Record](../records/united_states_v_craft_merits_2002-04-17.md)

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Stevens and Scalia.** Section 6321 reaches property belonging to the taxpayer; it cannot create a distinct individual estate from inseparable marital ownership. Michigan gives the husband neither unilateral withdrawal nor a power to sell, partition or redirect the estate. Drye and National Bank of Commerce involved those actual powers over value, and therefore do not settle this case. Joint enjoyment and a veto on conveyance are meaningful benefits, but remain incidents of the marital entity's ownership rather than separately owned assets. The longstanding collection treatment supports this statutory caution; it does not replace the inquiry. The dissent does not make a creditor-exemption label conclusive or declare all restricted, nontransferable interests unreachable. It would affirm the attachment ruling without deciding a different tenancy, completed severance or separate income right.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** Entireties ownership also gives institutional security to the nondebtor spouse, including a spouse whose domestic contribution yields no separate income. Removing that protection through a new federal property classification counsels particular statutory caution. This additional ground neither finds that Mrs. Craft held that domestic role nor creates a general marital-assets exemption. Stevens joins the common statutory dissent, but not this additional writing.

### Bell v. Cone; No. 01-400; 535 U.S. 685. — 2002-05-28

Justice Stevens joins the correct statutory and prejudice framework but dissents from the applications and judgment. He would affirm the Cronic-based sentence-only conditional writ. In his view, the combined failure to present an individualized case for life and answer the prosecution's request for death eliminated the relevant capital-sentencing function. He acknowledges the mercy opening, references to existing experts, favorable military cross-examination and protective objections; he considers them insufficient to constitute meaningful adversarial testing in the proceeding as a whole. His rule is not that every omitted witness or waived closing is structural, and he makes no finding that later illness establishes trial incapacity.

If Cronic is rejected, Stevens would also leave the ordinary tactical claim open on remand. He would require examination of the asserted legal misunderstandings, the performance ground's independence from the erroneous prejudice inquiry and its relationship to the disputed restrictions on mitigation development. Pritchett's limited support for expecting appellate correction does not, in his view, excuse surrender of a meaningful penalty defense. He accepts the State's independent-ground argument as substantial but would not foreclose ordinary appellate review on this record. He finds neither Strickland deficiency nor actual prejudice as an alternative present holding. His primary remedy preserves the existing 180-day structure for a new lawful penalty proceeding; the convictions and unused alternative sentence grounds are unaffected.

### National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan — 2002-06-10

O'Connor concurs in part and dissents in part. In Part I of her separate opinion, joined by Breyer alone, she expresses the view that a notice or reasonable-discovery rule should apply to discrete acts, while recognizing that this known-discrimination record does not require deciding its operation. That observation creates no controlling discovery exception. Scalia and Kennedy do not join it.

In Part II, joined by Scalia and Kennedy, she would limit hostile-environment liability to occurrences within the charge window while permitting older conduct to establish the character, severity or discriminatory cause of timely conditions. Their position limits liability for the older occurrences; exclusion of compensation follows from that limit rather than from a damages-only cap. They accept harassment as a collective wrong and do not demand that each incident independently meet the severe-or-pervasive standard. Harris's totality inquiry does not itself erase the deadline once a known actionable injury is complete. They reject Amtrak's suggestion that early knowledge extinguishes a later new violation, but would reverse the broader environment revival as well as discrete revival. Preserved evidentiary and instructional objections still require appellate consideration; they do not seek automatic restoration of the defense verdict. Breyer does not join Part II.

### United States v. Farmer — 2002-06-10

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Justice Scalia joins Parts I and III: immediate review, the supported hearing threshold and its application, the defendant's confined preponderance burden, and the conditional release consequence. He does not join Part II's Mathews rationale. His sufficient ground is the distinction between preliminary seizure and the meaningful later adversary opportunity in this continuing civil-seizure setting. His Good position accepted the sufficiency of preliminary procedures on that different record; it did not authorize a warrant to eliminate the later contest altogether. Monsanto left this hearing question open. The prolonged civil custody, particular claim of legitimate property, immediate trial-counsel need and government's conditional acceptance of a hearing support the focused opportunity here. He rejects an open-ended balancing license to redesign all criminal restraint procedures; he agrees that the hearing neither decides the indictment nor authorizes spending proved proceeds.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins Parts I and II and the order requiring a prompt hearing. Farmer's specific sworn showing meets the threshold; the time-sensitive defense interest, concrete risk of error and value of bounded testing satisfy Mathews. He agrees that Farmer must establish actual need for the identified funds by a preponderance. He dissents from Part III because, once taint is materially contested, he would require the government to justify continued restraint through the applicable probable-cause connection to forfeiture or another independently valid basis. Probable cause of an offense does not establish the taint of every asset. In his view, requiring the owner to disprove the government's case through inaccessible information improperly allocates that part of the hearing. He would affirm the hearing entitlement with modified instructions and require release of the needed amount lacking that support. He does not claim access to proved forfeitable proceeds or extend this counsel-focused appeal to living expenses, interest or final forfeiture merits. His allocation is noncontrolling.
