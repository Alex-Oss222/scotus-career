## Controlling holdings

### A State may delegate its preserved motor-vehicle safety authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 14501(c)(2)(A) preserves a State's ability to authorize political subdivisions to exercise its safety regulatory authority with respect to motor vehicles. A local rule must actually fall within that delegated safety authority; an economic restriction does not qualify merely because the municipality calls it safety regulation.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer, controls for seven Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** The general preemption clause expressly covers state and local laws relating to motor-carrier prices, routes or services. The safety exception preserves an identified state power. Mortier explains that a State ordinarily exercises retained regulatory power through authorized subdivisions; naming the State does not alone forbid that internal allocation. The contrary inference from Congress's separate references to subdivisions elsewhere is substantial but not conclusive: the exceptions preserve different subjects, and a preserved state authority is not naturally restricted to personal exercise by state officials. The statute's separate protection for vehicle size or weight and hazardous-cargo route controls, and for minimum financial responsibility or insurance, retains its distinct scope. These provisions do not transform all towing regulation into safety regulation. Economic deregulation remains effective because the court must examine what each ordinance actually regulates; Section 31141's federal supervisory process likewise remains operative. The Sixth Circuit stopped at categorical institutional exclusion and did not decide the necessary ordinance-specific questions. It must examine Ohio's authorization and the actual safety character of each challenged requirement, together with any independent federal conflict.

**Precedent treatment:** Wisconsin Public Intervenor v. Mortier, June 21, 1991, supplies the ordinary state-delegation principle; the federal statute still controls the scope of saved regulation and actual preemption.

## Precedent treatment

## Separate Writings

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by O'Connor.** Congress expressly named States and political subdivisions in the broad prohibition and in selected exceptions but named only the State in this safety reservation. That difference should control. Mortier involved a statute lacking the same express institutional contrast, so ordinary delegation cannot erase the choice made here. The dissent would affirm the categorical exclusion of independent municipal regulations under this exception. It does not forbid local enforcement of valid state rules or purport to invalidate every generally applicable traffic law.
