## Controlling holdings

### I. State immunity from private judicial suit does not bar this Commission process

**Controlling proposition:** State immunity from private judicial suit does not prevent the Federal Maritime Commission from administering the Shipping Act through this statutory complaint adjudication against a state-operated port. Private initiation, a request for personal reparations and court-like safeguards do not transform the Commission's determination of the regulatory controversy into an independent private judicial suit.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author in that five-Justice coalition. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this tribunal rule and propose a different remand. Shared opposition to the existing final dismissal is not agreement on the controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Commission applies the maritime statute, reviews the ALJ and issues its own administrative order through the enacted scheme. Congress's use of complaints and adjudicative safeguards is a method of national regulatory administration. Compulsory answer, discovery, hearing and potential individual relief impose real burdens; the Court does not deny them or rely merely on an executive label. Their presence does not change the actual federal statutory function into the private court action to which the asserted immunity attaches.

California establishes public-terminal coverage and the particular corrective regulatory power it sustained, not an answer to this distinct immunity question. Seminole's judicial-function holding concerned a court's conclusive determination; it does not classify every agency determination of public obligations as a private judicial suit. Waffle House recognizes an agency's independent statutory role, but this complaint remains privately initiated. The Commission's own-motion review did not convert it into a government-filed action.

The absence of immediate execution is not alone decisive, and the Court does not suppose that an eventual order is either unenforceable or automatically collectible. Those questions belong to their actual later proceedings. The holding concerns this Commission process, not every administrative tribunal. Because the immunity asserted does not bar this process, a separate Shipping Act abrogation determination is unnecessary.

**Precedent treatment:** California v. United States, 320 U.S. 577 (1944), retains its public-terminal and corrective-order holdings without an assumed private initiator or implied immunity ruling. Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, March 27, 1996, retains its conclusive-judicial-function holding. EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc., January 15, 2002, supplies an agency-role distinction without converting this private complaint into the agency's own enforcement action.

**Controlling proposition:** The Commission may adjudicate the complaint's requests under the Shipping Act's applicable coverage, violation and remedy provisions, without an immunity-based severance of reparations from prospective compliance. **Agency-boundary ground.** The Commission may adjudicate this statutory maritime complaint against the state arm without treating the administrative proceeding itself as a private judicial suit barred by sovereign immunity. This conclusion does not convert the complainant into the United States, make the complaint a government-filed action or decide immunity from a later private judicial collection action.

The supported explanation is confined to this Commission process. California establishes federal authority to regulate public terminal operations and the described corrective order; it does not answer the distinct immunity issue. The distinction rests on the nature and statutory control of this federal administrative function, not merely the name of the branch, the absence of immediate execution, the political cost of noncompliance or a general denial that adjudication burdens States. Waffle House identifies the independent character of an agency's own action, but this complaint is not that action. ## Event

Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority, No. 01-46, 535 U.S. 743. Merits decision, May 28, 2002. Argued February 25, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit.

The question is whether state sovereign immunity prevents the Commission from adjudicating a private Shipping Act complaint against a state-operated port. South Carolina Maritime Services sought berths for the M/V Tropic Sea on five occasions and alleges discriminatory application of the port's policy against vessels principally devoted to gambling, compared with Carnival vessels offering gambling. It seeks reparations, cessation of the alleged discrimination and Commission pursuit of interim judicial relief. The port's state-arm status is uncontested. The ALJ dismissed on immunity grounds; the Commission reviewed that ruling on its own motion and reversed, although the complainant had not appealed. The Fourth Circuit directed dismissal. The United States supports reversal as a respondent.

## Holdings

### I. State immunity from private judicial suit does not bar this Commission process

**Controlling proposition:** State immunity from private judicial suit does not prevent the Federal Maritime Commission from administering the Shipping Act through this statutory complaint adjudication against a state-operated port. Private initiation, a request for personal reparations and court-like safeguards do not transform the Commission's determination of the regulatory controversy into an independent private judicial suit.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, including the author in that five-Justice coalition. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reject this tribunal rule and propose a different remand. Shared opposition to the existing final dismissal is not agreement on the controlling rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Commission applies the maritime statute, reviews the ALJ and issues its own administrative order through the enacted scheme. Congress's use of complaints and adjudicative safeguards is a method of national regulatory administration. Compulsory answer, discovery, hearing and potential individual relief impose real burdens; the Court does not deny them or rely merely on an executive label. Their presence does not change the actual federal statutory function into the private court action to which the asserted immunity attaches.

California establishes public-terminal coverage and the particular corrective regulatory power it sustained, not an answer to this distinct immunity question. Seminole's judicial-function holding concerned a court's conclusive determination; it does not classify every agency determination of public obligations as a private judicial suit. Waffle House recognizes an agency's independent statutory role, but this complaint remains privately initiated. The Commission's own-motion review did not convert it into a government-filed action.

The absence of immediate execution is not alone decisive, and the Court does not suppose that an eventual order is either unenforceable or automatically collectible. Those questions belong to their actual later proceedings. The holding concerns this Commission process, not every administrative tribunal. Because the immunity asserted does not bar this process, a separate Shipping Act abrogation determination is unnecessary.

**Precedent treatment:** California v. United States, 320 U.S. 577 (1944), retains its public-terminal and corrective-order holdings without an assumed private initiator or implied immunity ruling. Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, March 27, 1996, retains its conclusive-judicial-function holding. EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc., January 15, 2002, supplies an agency-role distinction without converting this private complaint into the agency's own enforcement action.

### II. Neither state status nor commercial activity supplies the omitted legal steps

**Controlling proposition:** The port's ordinary commercial operations and its defensive challenge establish no consent to suit; the Court's administrative-forum holding rests on the Commission's statutory function, not constructive waiver. The existing rule permitting unmistakably authorized State exposure within valid Article I power remains controlling, but this decision finds no affirmative or negative Shipping Act abrogation rule.

**Authority:** The five-Justice Court opinion states these limits. All four dissenters likewise reject supplied consent and the categorical Article I incapacity premise, but their proposed authorization remand remains distinct from the Court's agency-boundary ground.

**Controlling explanation:** College Savings Bank rejects fictional consent from ordinary commerce, and Lapides concerns actual authorized voluntary removal, an act not supplied here. Florida Prepaid and Garrett permit the separate inquiry into unmistakable authorization and enumerated power; Alden rejects the categorical state-forum barrier to its expressly authorized remedy. The Fourth Circuit's categorical Article I premise therefore cannot govern. Substantive coverage, authority for a particular private proceeding and actual waiver remain different questions. The Court need not decide the particular Shipping Act authorization alternative because its tribunal holding permits the complaint to proceed. Nor does Verizon Maryland create a connected officer defendant in this administrative complaint. Both requested forms of administrative relief may be adjudicated on the controlling institutional ground; no prospective-only immunity exception is necessary.

**Precedent treatment:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999, Alden v. Maine, June 23, 1999, and Board of Trustees v. Garrett, February 21, 2001, retain their actual legislative-power and enforcement holdings. College Savings Bank, June 23, 1999, rejects constructive commercial consent. Lapides v. Board of Regents, May 13, 2002, remains confined to authorized removal; Verizon Maryland, May 20, 2002, preserves the distinct connected-officer route.

### III. Administrative adjudication and later enforcement remain separate

**Controlling proposition:** The Commission may adjudicate the complaint's requests under the Shipping Act's applicable coverage, violation and remedy provisions, without an immunity-based severance of reparations from prospective compliance. Any later judicial enforcement, collection or interim injunction must independently satisfy its own statutory, jurisdictional and immunity requirements; this decision establishes no substantive violation or award.

**Authority:** The five-Justice Court opinion controls the administrative remand. The four dissenters share the later-proceeding limits but would first require the separate authorization determination before the State may be compelled to defend this complaint.

**Controlling explanation:** Commission investigation begun on its own initiative is distinct from own-motion review of the ALJ in a private case. The Act also separates governmental or authorized private judicial enforcement of a nonreparation order, private judicial collection of reparations, Commission interim-injunction litigation and the complainant's separate interim judicial route. The Attorney General's nonreparation enforcement role does not authorize collection of the private reparation award. A Commission finding's specified evidentiary force does not automatically establish judicial jurisdiction or defeat immunity. No penalty for disobedience is before the Court.

Ordinary adjudication must apply the operative enactment to the particular conduct and requested relief. The 1998 amendments generally took effect May 1, 1999, while preserving suits filed before that date and suits concerning earlier conduct filed within one year afterward. The earlier enactment and amendments must remain distinct where coverage or an enhanced reparation depends on them. The Commission must determine actual carrier and terminal predicates, the discrimination allegation and lawful relief, with ordinary administrative review. The Court leaves immunity from a later independent private collection action outside this question, as well as different proceedings permitting full new judicial review without required prior participation.

**Precedent treatment:** California's public-terminal construction informs coverage without deciding these voyage-specific predicates or private enforcement. College Savings Bank and the governing legislative-authorization cases retain their work in a later proceeding to which their conditions apply.

## Law After Decision

A state-operated port cannot demand dismissal of this Shipping Act administrative complaint merely because a private complainant initiated it and seeks reparations.
