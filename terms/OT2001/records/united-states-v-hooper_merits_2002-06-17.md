## Controlling holdings

### The protected interest must exist at the statutory time

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Section 853(n)(6)(A), a third-party claimant must prove by a preponderance of the evidence a legal interest that invalidates forfeiture in whole or part because, at the time of the acts giving rise to forfeiture, it was vested in that claimant rather than the defendant or was superior to the defendant's interest. That timing requirement governs both alternatives; assuming the spouses' community ownership, interests arising only through receipt of the criminal proceeds satisfy neither here.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, controls for the coalition stated in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** State law identifies a claimed property interest; the federal forfeiture statute determines its protection and priority. Section 853(a)(1) expressly reaches directly or indirectly obtained criminal proceeds and property derived from them. Subsection (c) makes federal title relate back to commission of the acts giving rise to forfeiture once forfeiture is lawfully adjudicated, subject to the actual third-party protections; it does not bypass the ancillary proceeding. The spouses' asserted interests arise with the very proceeds the crimes produced. Immediate community vesting upon receipt cannot make those interests antecedent to the acts generating the property. The strongest contrary argument invokes innocent ownership, but innocence does not supply the statute's missing time or purchase predicate. Otherwise a later gratuitous transfer could defeat the carefully distinct purchaser protection. Bennis concerns a separately held lawful interest exposed to another's later misuse, not an interest generated with criminal proceeds. The court already separated legitimate assets, demonstrating why provenance matters item by item. The judgment can therefore stand on federal priority without deciding the disputed California title interpretation or treating marriage as guilt.

**Precedent treatment:** Bennis is distinguished by the lawful preexisting property; Buena Vista by its different civil owner defense; Lester by its untainted substitute property. Their protections retain their own predicates.

### The separate purchaser protection remains distinct and successful statutory proof requires relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 853(n)(6)(B) separately protects a claimant who proves by a preponderance of the evidence that the claimant was a bona fide purchaser for value of the relevant interest and, at purchase, was reasonably without cause to believe the property subject to forfeiture. Proof of either statutory route requires amendment of the forfeiture order to reflect the protected interest; innocence alone supplies neither route, and no purchaser-for-value claim is asserted here.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, controls for the coalition stated in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** The vested-or-superior route protects interests satisfying its temporal condition; the purchaser route protects a different class under its value and reasonable-notice conditions. Combining them into a general innocent-spouse exception would remove requirements Congress retained. Conversely, failure on these proceeds claims does not authorize taking every lawful asset a spouse owns. The protected legitimate-property interests remain intact. Buena Vista preserves meaningful adjudication of the defense available under the civil statute; it does not transfer that statute's donee protection into the criminal ancillary categories. Bollin requires authority for the actual property and order, which the proceeds provision supplies here. Farmer protects meaningful review of a supported legitimate-assets claim; these claimants used the statutory hearing and challenge the priority result. No procedural-due-process or purchaser theory is presented, so neither is adjudicated.

**Precedent treatment:** Buena Vista's civil eligibility and lawful-adjudication requirements are maintained. Bollin and Farmer preserve actual statutory authority and meaningful adjudication without creating a right to retain proved proceeds.

## Operative statutory rule

The temporal requirement applies to both the vested-interest and superior-interest alternatives. Assuming the spouses' community interests exist under California law, interests arising only through receipt of the proceeds produced by the offense satisfy neither alternative on this record.

Section 853(n)(6)(B) separately protects a bona fide purchaser for value of the relevant right, title or interest who, at purchase, was reasonably without cause to believe the property subject to forfeiture. That is a separate route, not a free-standing innocence exception and not merely the timing test of subsection (A). No purchaser-for-value claim is asserted here. When a claimant proves either statutory route, amendment of the forfeiture order to reflect that determination is mandatory, not discretionary.

Section 853(a)(1) expressly includes proceeds obtained directly or indirectly from the offense and property derived from those proceeds. Section 853(c)'s relation-back operation supports priority after forfeiture is lawfully adjudicated, subject to the actual third-party protections. ### The protected interest must exist at the statutory time

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Section 853(n)(6)(A), a third-party claimant must prove by a preponderance of the evidence a legal interest that invalidates forfeiture in whole or part because, at the time of the acts giving rise to forfeiture, it was vested in that claimant rather than the defendant or was superior to the defendant's interest. That timing requirement governs both alternatives; assuming the spouses' community ownership, interests arising only through receipt of the criminal proceeds satisfy neither here.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, controls for the coalition stated in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** State law identifies a claimed property interest; the federal forfeiture statute determines its protection and priority. Section 853(a)(1) expressly reaches directly or indirectly obtained criminal proceeds and property derived from them. Subsection (c) makes federal title relate back to commission of the acts giving rise to forfeiture once forfeiture is lawfully adjudicated, subject to the actual third-party protections; it does not bypass the ancillary proceeding. The spouses' asserted interests arise with the very proceeds the crimes produced. Immediate community vesting upon receipt cannot make those interests antecedent to the acts generating the property. The strongest contrary argument invokes innocent ownership, but innocence does not supply the statute's missing time or purchase predicate. Otherwise a later gratuitous transfer could defeat the carefully distinct purchaser protection. Bennis concerns a separately held lawful interest exposed to another's later misuse, not an interest generated with criminal proceeds. The court already separated legitimate assets, demonstrating why provenance matters item by item. The judgment can therefore stand on federal priority without deciding the disputed California title interpretation or treating marriage as guilt.

**Precedent treatment:** Bennis is distinguished by the lawful preexisting property; Buena Vista by its different civil owner defense; Lester by its untainted substitute property. Their protections retain their own predicates.

### The separate purchaser protection remains distinct and successful statutory proof requires relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 853(n)(6)(B) separately protects a claimant who proves by a preponderance of the evidence that the claimant was a bona fide purchaser for value of the relevant interest and, at purchase, was reasonably without cause to believe the property subject to forfeiture. Proof of either statutory route requires amendment of the forfeiture order to reflect the protected interest; innocence alone supplies neither route, and no purchaser-for-value claim is asserted here.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer, controls for the coalition stated in Opinion Topology.

**Controlling explanation:** The vested-or-superior route protects interests satisfying its temporal condition; the purchaser route protects a different class under its value and reasonable-notice conditions. Combining them into a general innocent-spouse exception would remove requirements Congress retained. Conversely, failure on these proceeds claims does not authorize taking every lawful asset a spouse owns. The protected legitimate-property interests remain intact. Buena Vista preserves meaningful adjudication of the defense available under the civil statute; it does not transfer that statute's donee protection into the criminal ancillary categories. Bollin requires authority for the actual property and order, which the proceeds provision supplies here. Farmer protects meaningful review of a supported legitimate-assets claim; these claimants used the statutory hearing and challenge the priority result. No procedural-due-process or purchaser theory is presented, so neither is adjudicated.

**Precedent treatment:** Buena Vista's civil eligibility and lawful-adjudication requirements are maintained. Bollin and Farmer preserve actual statutory authority and meaningful adjudication without creating a right to retain proved proceeds.

## Precedent Treatment

- United States v. 92 Buena Vista Avenue, February 24, 1993: its civil owner-defense eligibility and opportunity to prove title remain; the criminal statute has different third-party conditions.
- Bennis v. Michigan, March 4, 1996: protects the qualifying separately held lawful automobile interest when the owner proves nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention; the present proceeds are outside that category.
- United States v. Lester, 85 F.3d 1409 (9th Cir. 1996): its untainted substitute-property setting is distinguished from proceeds.
- United States v. Bollin, June 10, 2002: actual statutory authority remains necessary for the particular property and order; Section 853(a)(1) supplies proceeds authority here.
- United States v. Farmer, June 10, 2002: meaningful focused review of a supported legitimate-assets claim remains; it supplies no entitlement to proved proceeds.
- United States v. Craft, April 17, 2002: state property interests and federal consequences remain distinct; no general marital exemption follows.

## Law After Decision

An after-arising community interest in these proved proceeds fails the vested-or-superior protection at the statutory time. Antecedent qualifying interests and qualifying purchases for value retain their separate protections, and successful statutory proof requires amendment of forfeiture. Independently lawful spousal property remains outside this confined judgment.
