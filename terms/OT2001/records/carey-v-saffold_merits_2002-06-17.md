## Controlling holdings

### Timely ordinary state review includes its intervals

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 28 U.S.C. §2244(d)(2), a properly filed state collateral application remains pending during timely continuation through the State's ordinary review process, including the interval before a timely filing at the next available level. California's successive-level original petitions qualify when they perform that review function; an abandoned or completed process, an unreasonable gap or an unrelated later attack does not receive interval exclusion merely because another collateral petition is possible.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by the other five members of the six-Justice coalition identified in Opinion Topology; all six adopt both holdings.

**Controlling explanation:** Pending describes a review process awaiting completion, not only a paper physically awaiting one judge's order. A contrary construction would consume the federal period during steps ordinarily needed to complete state review and encourage premature federal filings. California's original-petition terminology does not change that practical function. The dissent correctly insists that a properly filed application, not a merely possible future claim, is required. The Court's rule retains that requirement and confines interval protection to timely continuation of the ordinary course; extraordinary or unrelated filings do not qualify automatically. Artuz distinguishes filing conditions from claim-relief bars and leaves pendency separate. Duncan requires state rather than federal review. Boerckel explains the ordinary state-review round, without making every extraordinary remedy part of it.

**Precedent treatment:** Artuz and Duncan are applied without alteration; Boerckel supplies ordinary-review context. The previously open interval question is resolved at this functional, timely-continuation scope.

### State timeliness and the actual federal calculation remain necessary

**Holding and operative rule:** An alternative merits discussion does not establish timely state filing or override a valid independent state determination that the relevant interval was unreasonable. The Ninth Circuit must determine the California order's actual meaning, the applicable state timing standard, the supported reasons for this interval and the exact elapsed time, then recalculate the federal limitation without excluding any interval that fails the governing requirements.

**Authority:** The same six-Justice Court opinion adopts this rule and bounded remand; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas also reject a conclusive merits-language inference but do not join the Court's functional-pendency rule or resulting mandate.

**Controlling explanation:** The California Supreme Court referred both to the merits and lack of diligence. Its merits phrase therefore cannot alone resolve whether the four-and-one-half-month interval complied with California law. A clear state determination of untimeliness defeats interval exclusion even if merits were also considered. Where the order's meaning is uncertain, the federal court must identify the actual state ground and relevant timing circumstances; certification or another lawful clarification may be used if needed, but is not automatically required. Neither a fixed nationwide deadline nor an assumption that this interval is necessarily reasonable or necessarily late supplies the answer. Proper filing, pendency and reasonable state timing remain distinct. Artuz does not turn an actual filing deadline into a relief bar. Duncan forecloses counting federal habeas pendency as state review and preserves independently available commencement or equitable grounds only on their own requirements. Unpresented equitable tolling and the conviction's constitutional merits are outside this decision.

**Precedent treatment:** Artuz's filing-deadline-exception reservation remains; Duncan's independent timing rules remain. Lee's adequate-state-ground inquiry and Young's bankruptcy tolling do not determine statutory pendency here.
