## Controlling holdings

### I. Armstrong's confined Rule 16 source holding governs this request

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 16(a)(1)(C)'s defense-preparation materiality branch does not authorize discovery to prepare this selective-prosecution challenge to the decision to prosecute; Armstrong's holding remains confined to that claim rather than every affirmative defense unrelated to guilt. The distinct intended-use and defendant-origin branches are not established on this record, and protective or sanction powers cannot create an otherwise missing disclosure duty.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's per curiam opinion has six direct joins: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela and Breyer join only Parts II–III; Stevens concurs in the judgment on his separate ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the existing source rule applies to the requested inquiry. Armstrong distinguishes preparation of the defense against the prosecution's criminal case from an independent constitutional attack on the Government's selection decision. Its confined construction of the materiality branch controls here. It neither excludes every guilt-unrelated defense nor supplies immunity from constitutional review.

The three Rule 16 triggers remain disjunctive. The requested information is not shown to be material the Government intends to use in its evidence in chief or material obtained from or belonging to Bass. The distinct exclusions and protections stated in the statutory setting do not create a disclosure obligation and do not make aggregate information, selection criteria and internal deliberations interchangeable categories. A protective order may regulate an authorized inquiry; reducing the request or imposing a sanction cannot establish its original source.

Breyer reads the preparation branch to include material support for a genuine selective-prosecution defense. Armstrong resolved that construction at the confined scope stated here. In any event, Part II supplies a separate sufficient ground: this proffer lacks the comparative connection required for the requested inquiry. No alternative discovery source is established or rejected categorically by this decision.

### II. The attainable preliminary standard is not met by this aggregate showing

**Holding and operative rule:** Before obtaining discovery on this indirect racial-selection theory under an available lawful source, a defendant must present some credible evidence tending to show discriminatory effect and purpose, including a reliable inference of different treatment of relevantly similarly situated persons of another race at the challenged federal selection stage; this is less than a prima facie merits case. Grounded statistics, circumstantial comparisons and a specifically justified alternative when comparator names cannot reasonably be obtained may suffice without perfect identity, universal naming or a fixed matching checklist, but Bass's aggregate charging and completed-plea figures lack the required effect comparison.

**Authority:** Part II has eight direct joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens agrees that the present order fails through his distinct supported-pattern and bounded-discretion analysis, not by joining this entire rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Armstrong requires an attainable, grounded inference, not completed proof of discrimination. Its treatment of Wayte and Wade preserves constitutional review while requiring a meaningful preliminary connection. Its application of Kimberlin and Klinger permits statistical and circumstantial comparison tied to the challenged treatment; neither identity nor individual names are universal requirements.

The national charging totals describe selected defendants without connecting racial distribution to a relevantly comparable eligible population or persons passed over. An appropriate denominator or matched-person evidence could perform that work, but neither technique is mandatory. Official provenance establishes the source of the counts, not comparability of the unmeasured groups. McCleskey requires connection to the challenged decision without categorically excluding statistics; Yick Wo preserves inference from a sufficiently supported discriminatory pattern.

Local capital-eligible charging and the Attorney General's later death authorization are distinct decisions. The national authorizer makes nationwide evidence potentially relevant, but does not turn charging totals into a comparison of authorization decisions. The Court imposes no local-only rule. Likewise, completed bargains combine offers and acceptance. Bass was offered a bargain and declined it. The reported acceptance disparity therefore does not establish that he was denied an offer, without proving equal terms or equality throughout the system.

Information asymmetry is substantial. Armstrong answers it through grounded alternative proof, not a presumption that records are readily available. Discovery may develop a supported inference; the Government's possession of useful data, the official national scale, or a less intrusive request cannot alone create the missing comparative connection. The effect defect suffices. The Court makes no independent purpose-deficiency holding, no finding that comparators do not exist and no ultimate determination of discrimination or neutrality. Brown's rule governing deliberate racial classification in police investigation supplies neither an absent disclosure source nor that missing inference.

### III. The unsupported order cannot sustain its dependent death-notice sanction

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining the discovery order is reversed, and the refusal-based exclusion of the death notice loses its unsupported predicate and must be set aside. Ordinary pretrial proceedings continue; a later request remains possible only under an available lawful source, a sufficiently grounded preliminary showing and warranted proportionate protective conditions, without any directed new inquiry.

**Authority:** Part III has the same eight direct joins as Part II. Stevens supplies the ninth reversal vote on his separate ground and agrees that the dependent sanction falls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a sanction enforces an existing obligation rather than creating one. Once the current discovery demand fails, the Government's refusal of that demand cannot support exclusion of its death notice. Armstrong supplies the distinction between removing an unsupported order and guaranteeing renewed discovery. Carlisle preserves supervision within governing legal limits.

Nothing necessary to decide this order requires a newly directed evidentiary step. Ordinary proceedings allow a genuinely different later request to be evaluated under its own source, evidentiary foundation and protective limits. That possibility is a limit on the decision's reach, not a command to collect evidence or hold a hearing. Failure of the present request does not establish neutral prosecution or foreclose all constitutional scrutiny. The proper sanction for refusing a valid discovery order and the remedy for proved discrimination are different questions outside this disposition.

### Issue 1: A preliminary comparison can be inferential but must be grounded

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Apply the discovery standard rather than a completed-merits burden; identify the challenged selection stage and the source of disclosure authority.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this statistical presentation supports the inference required by United States v. Armstrong.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the governing rule, including its express allowance for grounded statistical and alternative evidence and its prohibition on treating access difficulty as proof of the missing connection.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The presented aggregate charging counts and plea-outcome figures do not establish the required reliable inference that relevantly comparable defendants of another race received more favorable treatment at the challenged decision. A numerical disparity without the relevant eligible comparison is insufficient, although national, circumstantial, and non-name-specific proof can satisfy the threshold in an appropriate case.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding is record-specific. It creates no local-only rule, named-comparator prerequisite, or presumption that racial disparities are harmless. The central Attorney General’s role may make properly framed national evidence relevant.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The charging totals describe who entered the federal system, not the racial distribution and legally material characteristics of people eligible but not charged. Plea outcomes also combine whether an offer was made with whether a defendant accepted it; Bass’s declined offer matters to that inference. The information asymmetry warrants allowing grounded alternative forms of proof, as the United States v. Armstrong already does. It does not turn every unequal total into evidence of differential treatment of similarly situated people. The lower court crossed that line on the present showing. Armstrong’s preliminary evidentiary rule is decisive; it requires a grounded inference at the challenged selection stage without imposing the final merits burden. National data are potentially apt to the Attorney General’s centralized decision, but data about selected cases cannot identify comparable unselected cases simply by aggregation. The narrow record holding is appropriate because the defect is the missing relevant comparison, not a categorical inability of statistics to establish it.

* **Question Stone Resolves:** What follows when the discovery demand lacks the required foundation.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply United States v. Armstrong’s controlling ordinary-remand rule, rather than its separate noncontrolling proposal for a directed staged inquiry.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An unsupported discovery demand cannot sustain the dependent death-notice exclusion. Reversal leaves a later adequately supported request possible under its own lawful source and does not entitle the Government to avoid all constitutional scrutiny.

## 2. United States v. Bass

### Components and controlling baseline

**B1 — Legal source and general discovery threshold.** Armstrong's controlling Rule 16 holding excludes preparation of this selective-prosecution claim from the defense-materiality branch, without deciding every unrelated defense or every other lawful discovery source. Its separately controlling preliminary standard requires some credible evidence tending to show discriminatory effect and purpose, including a reliable inference that relevantly similar persons of another race could have been selected but were not. Discovery needs less than prima facie merits proof; names, perfect identity and a fixed checklist are not universal requirements. Grounded statistics, circumstantial comparisons and specifically justified alternatives to unavailable comparator names remain permissible. The source and threshold inquiries cannot be collapsed.

**B2 — This defendant's showing at the challenged selection stages.** National evidence is not categorically irrelevant: the Attorney General's central authorization role supplies a reason national comparisons may matter. But local decisions to charge capital-eligible crimes, central decisions to authorize death, offers of pleas and defendants' acceptance of offers are distinct acts. The supplied survey does not identify a sufficiently comparable eligible population or a reliable comparison to persons passed over at the relevant decision. Bass received and rejected an offer; disparity in plea outcomes cannot alone establish that he was denied that opportunity. These gaps defeat the current discovery showing without proving that comparators do not exist or that any decision was race-neutral.

**B3 — Existing sanction and further proceedings.** Reverse the Sixth Circuit's discovery judgment and set aside the dependent order excluding the death notice. Return for ordinary pretrial proceedings. A later request may be considered if a lawful source and the required evidentiary connection are established, subject to appropriate scope and protections; the mandate neither commands renewed discovery nor bars it categorically. The remedy for refusal of a valid order and the remedy for proved discrimination remain undecided.

Brown's rule concerns deliberate racial selection in police investigation. It neither supplies a federal criminal discovery source nor transforms outcome disparities into proof that the prosecutorial choice expressly used race. Armstrong preserves statistical proof and equal-protection review while requiring the comparison relevant to the actual choice. Its insufficiency ruling is a discovery decision, not a merits acquittal of discrimination.

### Adversarial paths and their consequences

**Path B-A: Reverse the current order and sanction, preserving a later supported request.** The missing relevant comparison is decisive even under the attainable Armstrong standard. Its strongest objection is informational asymmetry: the Government controls the selection data, and an absolute demand for named comparators would make review illusory. The answer is the actual rule's flexibility, not an assumption that information is readily available. A justified alternative comparison or grounded inference can suffice; this supplied aggregate does not connect the disparity to differential treatment of relevantly comparable persons. The Court must not rewrite B-A as a prohibition on national evidence, a names requirement, or a final finding of neutrality.

**Path B-B: Sustain discovery because an official nationwide disparity and central authorization justify inquiry.** Official data and a common national decisionmaker are stronger foundations than speculation; they can matter at a preliminary stage even if they would not prove discrimination. The strongest objection is that reliable collection of counts does not make the selected groups comparable or distinguish different selection stages. An authorization decisionmaker does not itself supply the missing eligible population or differential-treatment inference. The offered plea further weakens use of acceptance rates to show denial of a plea opportunity to Bass. A smaller request or protective order changes burden, not the missing connection or source. Seven Associates accept the Armstrong standard itself; Stevens reaches the same present-order result through his narrower discretionary analysis below.

**Path B-C: Set aside the order but direct a new staged inquiry or stage-specific factual reconsideration.** This would preserve the constitutional concern and avoid approving the existing demand. Its strongest objection is that mandatory new inquiry still needs a lawful procedural reason; a request's failure and the Government's possession of useful data alone establish none. The supplied record identifies the relevant stage distinction and the evidentiary defect without an unresolved finding necessary to decide the current order. All eight favor ordinary remand permitting a later lawful request rather than a mandated new evidence-gathering process. Breyer and Stevens retain distinct reasons and will not join a remand that forecloses such a request.

## Event

United States v. Bass, No. 01-1471. Merits decision, June 28, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 8. On review of the Sixth Circuit's judgment, 266 F.3d 532, sustaining the District Court's discovery order. The United States seeks reversal and removal of the resulting death-notice restraint. Bass seeks dismissal of the notice or discovery concerning alleged racial selection in federal capital charging. The questions concern the source and preliminary evidentiary basis for that discovery and the consequence of refusing the current order.

A federal grand jury in the Eastern District of Michigan charged Bass, who is Black, with offenses including the intentional firearm killings of two people. The Government filed notice of intent to seek death. The District Court ordered selection discovery and excluded the death notice after the Government declined to comply; a divided appellate panel sustained the discovery order. Bass relied on the Justice Department's 2000 national survey of capital-eligible charges and plea outcomes. The aggregate figures did not identify the relevantly comparable eligible population or defendants passed over at the pertinent decision. Bass was offered a plea bargain and declined it. Under the protocol in place since 1995, local United States Attorneys decide capital-eligible charges and submit those charged cases for the Attorney General's separate death-authorization decision.

### Rule 16 setting

Rule 16(a)(1)(C) concerns books, papers, documents, photographs, tangible objects, buildings or places within the Government's possession, custody or control. On a defendant's request, inspection and copying or photographing depend on one of three alternatives: materiality to preparation of the defense, intended use by the Government in its evidence in chief, or acquisition from or ownership by the defendant. Those triggers remain distinct.

Paragraph (a)(2) excludes internal attorney or agent reports, memoranda and other Government documents connected with investigation or prosecution, except the disclosures provided by (a)(1)(A), (B), (D) and (E) concerning defendant statements, prior criminal records, examinations and tests, and expert testimony. Government witness statements are separately governed by 18 U.S.C. §3500; no witness-statement production question is resolved here. Paragraph (a)(3) preserves the separate grand-jury disclosure provisions of Rules 6, 12(i), 26.2 and (a)(1)(A). No grand-jury exception is invoked as the source here, and none is decided. These provisions do not establish that every requested aggregate, criterion or deliberative document belongs to the same category.

Rule 16(d)(1) permits protective relief on sufficient showing, including an ex parte written showing whose full text must be sealed and preserved if relief is granted. Paragraph (d)(2) permits discovery, a continuance, exclusion and other just responses to noncompliance. These powers regulate an existing obligation; their existence is not a finding that a duty, exception, privilege or sanction predicate is established here.

## Holdings

### I. Armstrong's confined Rule 16 source holding governs this request

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 16(a)(1)(C)'s defense-preparation materiality branch does not authorize discovery to prepare this selective-prosecution challenge to the decision to prosecute; Armstrong's holding remains confined to that claim rather than every affirmative defense unrelated to guilt. The distinct intended-use and defendant-origin branches are not established on this record, and protective or sanction powers cannot create an otherwise missing disclosure duty.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's per curiam opinion has six direct joins: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela and Breyer join only Parts II–III; Stevens concurs in the judgment on his separate ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the existing source rule applies to the requested inquiry. Armstrong distinguishes preparation of the defense against the prosecution's criminal case from an independent constitutional attack on the Government's selection decision. Its confined construction of the materiality branch controls here. It neither excludes every guilt-unrelated defense nor supplies immunity from constitutional review.

The three Rule 16 triggers remain disjunctive. The requested information is not shown to be material the Government intends to use in its evidence in chief or material obtained from or belonging to Bass. The distinct exclusions and protections stated in the statutory setting do not create a disclosure obligation and do not make aggregate information, selection criteria and internal deliberations interchangeable categories. A protective order may regulate an authorized inquiry; reducing the request or imposing a sanction cannot establish its original source.

Breyer reads the preparation branch to include material support for a genuine selective-prosecution defense. Armstrong resolved that construction at the confined scope stated here. In any event, Part II supplies a separate sufficient ground: this proffer lacks the comparative connection required for the requested inquiry. No alternative discovery source is established or rejected categorically by this decision.

### II. The attainable preliminary standard is not met by this aggregate showing

**Holding and operative rule:** Before obtaining discovery on this indirect racial-selection theory under an available lawful source, a defendant must present some credible evidence tending to show discriminatory effect and purpose, including a reliable inference of different treatment of relevantly similarly situated persons of another race at the challenged federal selection stage; this is less than a prima facie merits case. Grounded statistics, circumstantial comparisons and a specifically justified alternative when comparator names cannot reasonably be obtained may suffice without perfect identity, universal naming or a fixed matching checklist, but Bass's aggregate charging and completed-plea figures lack the required effect comparison.

**Authority:** Part II has eight direct joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens agrees that the present order fails through his distinct supported-pattern and bounded-discretion analysis, not by joining this entire rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Armstrong requires an attainable, grounded inference, not completed proof of discrimination. Its treatment of Wayte and Wade preserves constitutional review while requiring a meaningful preliminary connection. Its application of Kimberlin and Klinger permits statistical and circumstantial comparison tied to the challenged treatment; neither identity nor individual names are universal requirements.

The national charging totals describe selected defendants without connecting racial distribution to a relevantly comparable eligible population or persons passed over. An appropriate denominator or matched-person evidence could perform that work, but neither technique is mandatory. Official provenance establishes the source of the counts, not comparability of the unmeasured groups. McCleskey requires connection to the challenged decision without categorically excluding statistics; Yick Wo preserves inference from a sufficiently supported discriminatory pattern.

Local capital-eligible charging and the Attorney General's later death authorization are distinct decisions. The national authorizer makes nationwide evidence potentially relevant, but does not turn charging totals into a comparison of authorization decisions. The Court imposes no local-only rule. Likewise, completed bargains combine offers and acceptance. Bass was offered a bargain and declined it. The reported acceptance disparity therefore does not establish that he was denied an offer, without proving equal terms or equality throughout the system.

Information asymmetry is substantial. Armstrong answers it through grounded alternative proof, not a presumption that records are readily available. Discovery may develop a supported inference; the Government's possession of useful data, the official national scale, or a less intrusive request cannot alone create the missing comparative connection. The effect defect suffices. The Court makes no independent purpose-deficiency holding, no finding that comparators do not exist and no ultimate determination of discrimination or neutrality. Brown's rule governing deliberate racial classification in police investigation supplies neither an absent disclosure source nor that missing inference.

### III. The unsupported order cannot sustain its dependent death-notice sanction

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining the discovery order is reversed, and the refusal-based exclusion of the death notice loses its unsupported predicate and must be set aside. Ordinary pretrial proceedings continue; a later request remains possible only under an available lawful source, a sufficiently grounded preliminary showing and warranted proportionate protective conditions, without any directed new inquiry.

**Authority:** Part III has the same eight direct joins as Part II. Stevens supplies the ninth reversal vote on his separate ground and agrees that the dependent sanction falls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a sanction enforces an existing obligation rather than creating one. Once the current discovery demand fails, the Government's refusal of that demand cannot support exclusion of its death notice. Armstrong supplies the distinction between removing an unsupported order and guaranteeing renewed discovery. Carlisle preserves supervision within governing legal limits.

Nothing necessary to decide this order requires a newly directed evidentiary step. Ordinary proceedings allow a genuinely different later request to be evaluated under its own source, evidentiary foundation and protective limits. That possibility is a limit on the decision's reach, not a command to collect evidence or hold a hearing. Failure of the present request does not establish neutral prosecution or foreclose all constitutional scrutiny. The proper sanction for refusing a valid discovery order and the remedy for proved discrimination are different questions outside this disposition.
