### Temporary instructional handling is not institutional maintenance

**Holding and operative rule:** Under 20 U.S.C. §1232g(a)(4)(A), student-related information must also be maintained by the institution or someone acting for it in the relevant recordkeeping capacity to constitute an education record. Classmates' temporary exchange and grading of papers, followed by immediate reporting of marks during this instructional exercise, does not itself satisfy that condition; teacher direction alone does not make the peer grader an institutional recordkeeper.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer, supplies nine direct votes for this coverage rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that identifying a student's performance and maintaining an institutional record are separate statutory requirements. Falvo's privacy concern is real, but disclosure of educational information does not eliminate the maintenance condition. The opposing agency theory relies on the teacher's direction and the student's production of a score. Those facts describe instructional participation; they do not establish institutional record custody. Immediate reporting of the mark does not turn the preceding temporary handling into maintenance.

FERPA's access and custody provisions illuminate recordkeeping without requiring a central repository, permanent storage, one custodian or a particular location. The sole-possession exclusion in section 1232g(a)(4)(B)(i) retains its separate work and is not erased by an invented central-file limitation. Nor does student possession invariably defeat coverage: a student may perform an actual institutional recordkeeping function in a different setting. The maintained-record definition, rather than a general preference for peer grading, decides this practice. A teacher's retained gradebook and papers collected for institutional retention present different conduct outside this coverage question.

**Precedent treatment:** The provision-specific approach in Blessing is preserved; TRW's treatment of independent statutory limitations is consistent with giving maintenance its own function.

**Holding and operative rule:** The Court assumes, without deciding, that the asserted section 1983 enforcement route is available and rejects this claim because the challenged temporary process falls outside FERPA's education-record definition. That assumption creates no holding that FERPA generally confers a private action.

**Authority:** The same unanimous Court opinion controls the scope of decision and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The federal claim supports jurisdiction, and private statutory enforceability is a separate, nonjurisdictional inquiry. No affirmative relief requires recognition of the disputed action when the pleaded practice fails the coverage condition. This complete adverse coverage disposition leaves the separate enforcement question undecided because its answer is unnecessary to the presented coverage question. Later institutional retention must be assessed separately rather than resolved by extending this holding.

**Precedent treatment:** Blessing and Sandoval retain their distinct statute-specific holdings; neither is enlarged or displaced.
