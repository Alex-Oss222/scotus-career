### Limited monetary relief under the two statutes

**Controlling proposition:** IRCA does not categorically withdraw the Board's NLRA §10(c) authority to award limited pre-discovery backpay for an established antiunion discharge, even where the worker lacked employment authorization and obtained the job through false verification documents. The Board may remedy that independent labor violation through a retrospective monetary award that orders no unlawful employment, denies reinstatement and ends the ordinary lost-work remedy at actual discovery of the employment bar; the award supplies neither work authorization nor immunity from separate sanctions.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Their five votes establish this rule and enforcement of the actual limited order.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 10(c)'s affirmative remedial authority remains bounded by the NLRA and other applicable enactments. Its separate bar for an actual discharge for cause is not established by a reason discovered only after an antiunion discharge. Section 1324a separately prohibits covered knowing hiring or fee-based recruitment/referral, requires employment verification, and prohibits knowing continued employment of an unauthorized worker. Authorization is employment- and time-specific, turning on permanent-resident status or statutory or Attorney General authorization. Good-faith verification can defend the knowing-hire charge; it does not permit knowing continuation after discovery. No earlier immigration-law violation by Hoffman is adjudicated here.

Section 1546(b) prohibits use, for employment verification, of identification known or reasonably understood not to have been lawfully issued for its possessor, false identification with that knowledge standard, or a false attestation. Its operative prohibition predates the 1988 hiring; the later five-year penalty does not govern the original act. Section 1324c applies only to violations on or after November 29, 1990, not the original tender; a later violation requires its own applicable act, knowledge, purpose and time predicates. Later benefit/application amendments and the 1996 deletion of the birth-certificate document category do not apply retroactively.

Both document statutes retain exceptions for lawfully authorized investigative, protective or intelligence activity of federal, state or state-subdivision law-enforcement agencies or federal intelligence agencies. Section 1324c(b) separately preserves activity authorized by 18 U.S.C. chapter 224; section 1546(c) separately preserves activity authorized by title V of the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970. The latter provision's State definition includes the District of Columbia, commonwealths, territories and possessions. No such activity is implicated here. Neither statute generally criminalizes the worker's mere performance of work.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 10(c) supplies the monetary authority, while IRCA supplies distinct employment, verification and fraud restrictions. Compensation for completed unlawful retaliation does not direct the forbidden future employment. Southern Steamship requires accommodation of another federal prohibition, not abandonment of every monetary labor remedy; this order commands no prohibited hiring or work. Sure-Tan's departed workers and unlawful-reentry problem do not establish a universal bar for the worker who remained here. ABF rejects automatic remedial forfeiture from serious misconduct while preserving actual statutory and remedial limits. McKennon likewise separates the unlawful decision from later-discovered disqualification. Its actual-would-discharge predicate and ordinary pre-discovery measure, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances, belong to its after-acquired-evidence setting; the analogy does not itself construe IRCA.

The dissent's strongest objection is that replacement wages value an employment opportunity federal law made unavailable and may encourage further fraud. The Court accepts the original fraud and the real continuing-use evidence. Neither mitigation duties nor an award authorize false documentation. But the identified prohibitions do not withdraw this bounded retrospective authority. Denying reinstatement and ending accrual at actual discovery give the employment restriction practical effect; independent sanctions retain their full force. Removing every backpay consequence would also reduce the cost of targeting this workforce for antiunion discharge. That consequence supports the statutory accommodation; deterrence alone supplies no remedial power.

The Board confronted the known disqualification and fraud, found reasonable interim efforts and adopted this limited accommodation. A particular preserved calculation, mitigation or explanation defect remains reviewable on its actual basis. None is demonstrated here merely by disputing the statutory choice or the reach of the continued-use inference. No additional formula, evidentiary hearing or explanation remand is required. The award remains restorative within §10(c), not a general punitive authority or automatic entitlement for every unauthorized worker.

**Application and remaining calculation:** Enforcement covers the order described in Judgment & Remedy, including the remaining January 1–June 14, 1993 period; this decision supplies no new total. Ordinary calculation and mitigation requirements remain operative. The employer's specified initial answers did not challenge the method, but it contested calculations and periods at the March 4, 1993 hearing; the Board found its method valid and reasonable. Its no-exception observation concerned receipt of evidence and original fraudulent acquisition of the Hoffman job, not a newly established tender at a named interim employer. The continuing statutory objection, the administrative contest and the Board's effort finding remain distinct. The Court relies on no blanket waiver and finds neither every interim job lawful nor every job fraudulently obtained.

### Precedent treatment

- ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB is applied to preserve reviewable remedial discretion and reject automatic forfeiture for misconduct. It does not protect relief contrary to another statute, misconduct defeating a remedial predicate, or arbitrary or inadequately explained relief.
- McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co. supplies an analogy separating an unlawful decision from later-known disqualification. Its actual-would-discharge requirement and ordinary pre-discovery backpay measure, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances, remain intact; its ADEA rule does not decide the NLRA/IRCA interaction.
- Sure-Tan, Inc. v. NLRB retains labor coverage and the duty to accommodate immigration law. Its departure, reentry and legal-availability setting is distinguished from this limited monetary order for a worker who remained here.
- Southern Steamship Co. v. NLRB remains a limit on remedies conflicting with independent federal prohibitions. It does not bar this award, which directs no prohibited employment.
- New York v. FERC / Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC preserves review of an actual agency-authority or explanation defect. The Board's identified accommodation is the legal choice presented, not an unexplained alternative requiring an additional remand.

### Resulting law

An established antiunion discharge may carry the limited pre-discovery backpay consequence upheld here despite the employee's lack of authorization and original verification fraud. Labor coverage, the actual employer violation and the legality of a particular remedy remain separate questions. The award must stay within the governing monetary authority and ordinary remedial predicates; it neither guarantees all unauthorized workers backpay nor orders future employment. Immigration verification duties, knowing-employment prohibitions and independently applicable document sanctions remain enforceable. Actual calculation and mitigation objections require their own preserved factual and legal basis; neither blanket forfeiture nor an unsupported new administrative inquiry follows from the statutory dispute.

### Separate writing

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** The dissent would reverse enforcement of this backpay category because it replaces earnings from an employment opportunity federal law made unavailable and Castro obtained through fraudulent verification. Section 10(c)'s public remedial purpose does not permit the Board to restore that lost-work premise in conflict with the independent federal employment scheme. The payment need not itself constitute hiring, and IRCA need not expressly repeal backpay, for that remedial conflict to matter.

Southern Steamship requires the Board to respect the independent prohibition. Sure-Tan's legal-availability limit remains significant despite its different departure facts; the dissent's sufficient ground is the operative immigration scheme and the established original fraud and lack of authorization, not that precedent's sentence alone. McKennon's later-discovered employer ground does not authorize compensation premised on employment forbidden by another enactment. Its actual-would-discharge requirement therefore does not resolve this different statutory bar.

The discovery cutoff and denial of reinstatement prevent future employment, but do not remove the defect in replacement wages for the earlier unauthorized position. The majority's concern that employers may exploit this workforce is substantial. The established labor violation, cease-and-desist and notice obligations, and other lawful remedies nevertheless cannot supply authority for this conflicting wage-replacement category. Nor can a public deterrence objective alone overcome the remedial limit.

The present-tense testimony supports continued-use concerns, but the dissent needs no finding that every interim job required new false documents, that the Board induced fraud, or that a particular post-1990 document violation occurred. It does not apply section 1324c to the original hiring. Its proposed reversal preserves NLRA coverage, the employer's violation, other lawful relief and independent immigration enforcement. It decides neither earned wages nor every remedy involving an unauthorized worker. It would return only for implementation of the backpay bar, not a new mitigation hearing or an additional punitive-remedy holding.

### Historical dissent and its substantive position

The historical dissent accepts the labor violation and the seriousness of the immigration restrictions but finds no statutory withdrawal of the limited monetary remedy. Section 10(c) protects effective enforcement as well as compensation. It argues that removing backpay can reduce the cost of antiunion discrimination against unauthorized workers and thereby undermine the immigration scheme's effort to remove the employment incentive. The competing assertion that possible backpay induces unlawful entry is, in its view, too speculative. The House Report and relevant agencies' positions support its accommodation argument, without changing the actual prohibitions on verification fraud or knowing employment.

The dissent applies ABF's rejection of automatic remedial forfeiture for serious employee misconduct and questions why administration of the other statute by a different agency requires a contrary result when the responsible immigration authorities support the order. It distinguishes Fansteel and Southern Steamship by the employee wrongdoing that supplied the actual good cause for discharge there; here the antiunion discharge itself is the violation, and later discovery does not rewrite its motive. It reads Sure-Tan in the departure/reentry context and notes that the Court does not treat that decision's sentence as independently conclusive. The present money order requires no unlawful reentry, reinstatement or new fraud. It also emphasizes the Board's ability to tailor the award, the actual discovery cutoff, and deference to a reasoned lawful agency accommodation.

The final H2 position retains that substantive framework, with actual authorization, statutory limits and the source qualifications now explicit. It does not turn the historical dissent's deference argument into a license to override an established federal prohibition, make deterrence alone a source of remedial authority, or announce that no interim document-use evidence exists. An order requiring no future fraud and evidence supporting earlier continuing use are compatible propositions.

## Corrected primary-record and temporal reconciliation

**Continuing use is supported, but its precise reach remains disputed.** The historical Court recites postdischarge fraudulent procurement as an admission and describes acquisition of the driver's license and Social Security card as admitted. The predecision ALJ and Board texts are more precise: they report present-tense use of the borrowed certificate and false identification for work, while the ALJ describes acquisition of the additional documents as apparently resulting from the certificate. The first supports a continued-use inference through the hearing; the second is the ALJ's inference about procurement. Neither supplied recital identifies a named interim employer, a dated later tender or a job-by-job finding. The petitioner argues continued use; the respondent disputes a job-specific tender and suggests possible alternative routes. Neither counsel's account alone settles those propositions. The final handoff preserves the actual evidence and inference without asserting its absence or inventing a particular transaction.

**The corrected account is not a merits reversal of an administrative finding.** It identifies the terms and limits of the source that the model may use. No group finds that later fraud did not occur. H1 requires only established lack of authorization and original procurement fraud, not a proved later tender, Board-induced fraud or every-job violation. H2 considers the continued-use evidence real but finds the limited monetary accommodation lawful without treating a specific unestablished event as a separate defective award premise. A genuinely material, properly presented particular remedy challenge would require its own actual record and analysis; the absence of the complete recovered transcript supplies no default, concession or new hearing mandate.

**The award and cutoff remain separate.** The primary Board order states $66,951 plus the specified interest, less required federal and state withholding. Its note 12 explains that the stated amount computes backpay through the last quarter of 1992. January 1 through June 14, 1993 is identified separately as the remaining period. June 14 is the discovery cutoff, not proof that the stated figure is a final total through that date. No new total, interest calculation, finding of additional entitlement or enlargement of accrual is supplied by this reconciliation. H2 enforces the actual limited Board order with its own terms; H1 disallows the disputed backpay category and returns only for implementation of that conclusion.

**No blanket waiver resolves the calculation or mitigation issue.** The Board's no-exception statement concerns admission of evidence and the original fraudulent acquisition of the Hoffman job. Separately, specified initial answers did not dispute the calculation method; the employer then contested calculations and periods at the March 4, 1993 hearing, and the Board found its method valid and reasonable. It also rejected the legal argument that lack of authorization prevented reasonable mitigation, finding actual efforts at interim work. Those distinct matters cannot be collapsed into an unchallenged finding about every later tender or a universal forfeiture of objections. The continuing statutory challenge neither erases the effort finding nor proves legal procurement of each job. The final model identifies no separate preserved defect requiring a calculation or explanation remand, rather than assuming all such defects waived.

**The statutes retain their actual dates.** Section 1324a's knowing-hire, verification and knowing-continuation framework predates the 1988 hiring. Section 1546(b)'s specified false-identification and attestation prohibition likewise supplies the relevant preexisting document-use restriction; its later five-year maximum is not the original-hiring penalty. Section 1324c was enacted November 29, 1990 and applies prospectively from that date; its later amendments do not govern the original 1988 tender. The historical Court's combined reference to these provisions cannot establish an earlier section 1324c violation. A later violation would require its own temporal, knowledge, act and statutory-purpose predicates, which this handoff does not newly adjudicate. The 1996 document-list changes do not retroactively explain the wrongfulness of another person's certificate in 1988.

The government-activity exceptions remain as stated in the statutory supplement: lawfully authorized investigative, protective or intelligence activities of the identified federal, state or subdivision law-enforcement agencies or federal intelligence agencies, and the distinct authorization clauses: section 1324c(b) also preserves activity authorized under 18 U.S.C. chapter 224, while section 1546(c) preserves activity authorized under title V of the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970 (18 U.S.C. note preceding section 3481). Nothing in the supplied facts implicates such an exception. No group makes a criminal conviction or sentencing determination. Mere performance of work is not universally criminalized; the statutory-conflict theory identifies the employment and verification scheme, not a literal claim that a backpay check itself hires the employee.

## Event

Hoffman Plastic Compounds, Inc. v. NLRB, No. 00-1595. Merits decision. Argued January 15, 2002; decided March 27, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the en banc United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, which enforced the Board's limited backpay order.

## Holdings

### Limited monetary relief under the two statutes

**Controlling proposition:** IRCA does not categorically withdraw the Board's NLRA §10(c) authority to award limited pre-discovery backpay for an established antiunion discharge, even where the worker lacked employment authorization and obtained the job through false verification documents. The Board may remedy that independent labor violation through a retrospective monetary award that orders no unlawful employment, denies reinstatement and ends the ordinary lost-work remedy at actual discovery of the employment bar; the award supplies neither work authorization nor immunity from separate sanctions.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Their five votes establish this rule and enforcement of the actual limited order.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 10(c)'s affirmative remedial authority remains bounded by the NLRA and other applicable enactments. Its separate bar for an actual discharge for cause is not established by a reason discovered only after an antiunion discharge. Section 1324a separately prohibits covered knowing hiring or fee-based recruitment/referral, requires employment verification, and prohibits knowing continued employment of an unauthorized worker. Authorization is employment- and time-specific, turning on permanent-resident status or statutory or Attorney General authorization. Good-faith verification can defend the knowing-hire charge; it does not permit knowing continuation after discovery. No earlier immigration-law violation by Hoffman is adjudicated here.

Section 1546(b) prohibits use, for employment verification, of identification known or reasonably understood not to have been lawfully issued for its possessor, false identification with that knowledge standard, or a false attestation. Its operative prohibition predates the 1988 hiring; the later five-year penalty does not govern the original act. Section 1324c applies only to violations on or after November 29, 1990, not the original tender; a later violation requires its own applicable act, knowledge, purpose and time predicates. Later benefit/application amendments and the 1996 deletion of the birth-certificate document category do not apply retroactively.

Both document statutes retain exceptions for lawfully authorized investigative, protective or intelligence activity of federal, state or state-subdivision law-enforcement agencies or federal intelligence agencies. Section 1324c(b) separately preserves activity authorized by 18 U.S.C. chapter 224; section 1546(c) separately preserves activity authorized by title V of the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970. The latter provision's State definition includes the District of Columbia, commonwealths, territories and possessions. No such activity is implicated here. Neither statute generally criminalizes the worker's mere performance of work.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 10(c) supplies the monetary authority, while IRCA supplies distinct employment, verification and fraud restrictions. Compensation for completed unlawful retaliation does not direct the forbidden future employment. Southern Steamship requires accommodation of another federal prohibition, not abandonment of every monetary labor remedy; this order commands no prohibited hiring or work. Sure-Tan's departed workers and unlawful-reentry problem do not establish a universal bar for the worker who remained here. ABF rejects automatic remedial forfeiture from serious misconduct while preserving actual statutory and remedial limits. McKennon likewise separates the unlawful decision from later-discovered disqualification. Its actual-would-discharge predicate and ordinary pre-discovery measure, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances, belong to its after-acquired-evidence setting; the analogy does not itself construe IRCA.

The dissent's strongest objection is that replacement wages value an employment opportunity federal law made unavailable and may encourage further fraud. The Court accepts the original fraud and the real continuing-use evidence. Neither mitigation duties nor an award authorize false documentation. But the identified prohibitions do not withdraw this bounded retrospective authority. Denying reinstatement and ending accrual at actual discovery give the employment restriction practical effect; independent sanctions retain their full force. Removing every backpay consequence would also reduce the cost of targeting this workforce for antiunion discharge. That consequence supports the statutory accommodation; deterrence alone supplies no remedial power.

The Board confronted the known disqualification and fraud, found reasonable interim efforts and adopted this limited accommodation. A particular preserved calculation, mitigation or explanation defect remains reviewable on its actual basis. None is demonstrated here merely by disputing the statutory choice or the reach of the continued-use inference. No additional formula, evidentiary hearing or explanation remand is required. The award remains restorative within §10(c), not a general punitive authority or automatic entitlement for every unauthorized worker.

**Application and remaining calculation:** Enforcement covers the order described in Judgment & Remedy, including the remaining January 1–June 14, 1993 period; this decision supplies no new total. Ordinary calculation and mitigation requirements remain operative. The employer's specified initial answers did not challenge the method, but it contested calculations and periods at the March 4, 1993 hearing; the Board found its method valid and reasonable. Its no-exception observation concerned receipt of evidence and original fraudulent acquisition of the Hoffman job, not a newly established tender at a named interim employer. The continuing statutory objection, the administrative contest and the Board's effort finding remain distinct. The Court relies on no blanket waiver and finds neither every interim job lawful nor every job fraudulently obtained.

## Precedent Treatment

- ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB is applied to preserve reviewable remedial discretion and reject automatic forfeiture for misconduct. It does not protect relief contrary to another statute, misconduct defeating a remedial predicate, or arbitrary or inadequately explained relief.
- McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co. supplies an analogy separating an unlawful decision from later-known disqualification. Its actual-would-discharge requirement and ordinary pre-discovery backpay measure, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances, remain intact; its ADEA rule does not decide the NLRA/IRCA interaction.
- Sure-Tan, Inc. v. NLRB retains labor coverage and the duty to accommodate immigration law. Its departure, reentry and legal-availability setting is distinguished from this limited monetary order for a worker who remained here.
- Southern Steamship Co. v. NLRB remains a limit on remedies conflicting with independent federal prohibitions. It does not bar this award, which directs no prohibited employment.
- New York v. FERC / Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC preserves review of an actual agency-authority or explanation defect. The Board's identified accommodation is the legal choice presented, not an unexplained alternative requiring an additional remand.

## Law After Decision

An established antiunion discharge may carry the limited pre-discovery backpay consequence upheld here despite the employee's lack of authorization and original verification fraud. Labor coverage, the actual employer violation and the legality of a particular remedy remain separate questions. The award must stay within the governing monetary authority and ordinary remedial predicates; it neither guarantees all unauthorized workers backpay nor orders future employment. Immigration verification duties, knowing-employment prohibitions and independently applicable document sanctions remain enforceable. Actual calculation and mitigation objections require their own preserved factual and legal basis; neither blanket forfeiture nor an unsupported new administrative inquiry follows from the statutory dispute.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** The dissent would reverse enforcement of this backpay category because it replaces earnings from an employment opportunity federal law made unavailable and Castro obtained through fraudulent verification. Section 10(c)'s public remedial purpose does not permit the Board to restore that lost-work premise in conflict with the independent federal employment scheme. The payment need not itself constitute hiring, and IRCA need not expressly repeal backpay, for that remedial conflict to matter.

Southern Steamship requires the Board to respect the independent prohibition. Sure-Tan's legal-availability limit remains significant despite its different departure facts; the dissent's sufficient ground is the operative immigration scheme and the established original fraud and lack of authorization, not that precedent's sentence alone. ABF addressed misconduct that did not render the underlying employment federally unavailable and preserves only lawful remedial discretion. McKennon's later-discovered employer ground does not authorize compensation premised on employment forbidden by another enactment. Its actual-would-discharge requirement therefore does not resolve this different statutory bar.

The discovery cutoff and denial of reinstatement prevent future employment, but do not remove the defect in replacement wages for the earlier unauthorized position. The majority's concern that employers may exploit this workforce is substantial. The established labor violation, cease-and-desist and notice obligations, and other lawful remedies nevertheless cannot supply authority for this conflicting wage-replacement category. Nor can a public deterrence objective alone overcome the remedial limit.

The present-tense testimony supports continued-use concerns, but the dissent needs no finding that every interim job required new false documents, that the Board induced fraud, or that a particular post-1990 document violation occurred. It does not apply section 1324c to the original hiring. Its proposed reversal preserves NLRA coverage, the employer's violation, other lawful relief and independent immigration enforcement. It decides neither earned wages nor every remedy involving an unauthorized worker. It would return only for implementation of the backpay bar, not a new mitigation hearing or an additional punitive-remedy holding.

## Procedure After Action

The en banc judgment enforcing the limited Board order is affirmed. The $66,951 through the last quarter of 1992 remains payable with the order's specified interest and required withholding. Calculation of the separately remaining January 1–June 14, 1993 period proceeds within the existing Board framework, subject to applicable ordinary remedial requirements. This affirmance orders no additional explanation or evidentiary remand, no reinstatement and no postdiscovery accrual based on unauthorized continued employment. Other lawful labor relief and independent immigration proceedings retain their proper routes.
