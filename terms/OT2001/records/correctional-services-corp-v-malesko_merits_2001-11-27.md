## Event, record and chronology

Correctional Services Corp. v. Malesko, No. 00-860. Argued October 1, 2001; decided November 27, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, which reversed dismissal of the constitutional damages claim against the private operator of a federal halfway house. The question is whether a private federal custodial corporation may answer for an Eighth Amendment injury caused by its own unconstitutional policy, practice or authorized decision.

Malesko alleged that a heart condition supported an elevator accommodation, that an employee required him to use the stairs, and that he suffered a heart attack and fall in 1994. The allegations establish the context, not corporate attribution, deliberate indifference or medical causation. The district court extended Meyer's agency rule to the corporation; the Second Circuit permitted the claim. The time-barred employee claims have independent force and are not revived. The cause-of-action question must be answered before ordinary examination of whether the actual pleading identifies the corporation's own culpable conduct.

The opening law is effective through June 28, 2001. Uncoordinated actions released on this date use the same entering-law baseline; their sequential authorship count does not make one decision entering law for another.

Individual actions, state tort remedies, prospective relief and contractual oversight retain their actual scope and reinforce remedial restraint; the dissent does not need to assume that tort relief exactly duplicates every constitutional injury. Limiting the proposed action to corporate policy narrows liability but still adds the disputed defendant. The dissent's objection concerns creation of that cause, not a finding that the company complied with the Constitution or a grant of sovereign status to every contractor.

**Scalia, joined by Thomas, dissenting separately.** They join O'Connor's dissent and add that the Court should not extend implied constitutional damages beyond the recognized Bivens, Davis and Carlson settings even when a proposed extension can be analogized to their reasoning. That distinct objection reaches the Court's claim of a sufficiently close custodial analogy: accepting the analogy does not, in their view, authorize another judicially created damages action. Existing recognized causes remain operative. They do not rely on treating Sandoval as a controlling general abandonment of statutory implication, because its actual statutory holding supplies no such premise. Their separate ground neither decides private employee liability nor overrules the recognized constitutional actions.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns for application of the confined cause to the actual pleading and, if the claim proceeds under ordinary rules, proof of its elements. The independent employee-limitations dispositions retain their force.
