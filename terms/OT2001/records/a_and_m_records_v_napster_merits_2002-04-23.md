### I. The preliminary fair-use defenses fail under the integrated factors

**Holding and operative rule:** On this supported preliminary record, unrestricted exchange of complete protected creative recordings with unrelated users is unlikely to be fair use under §107. Nontransformative whole-work copying and supported substitution for existing and developing markets outweigh the claimed sampling or relocation purposes; artist-authorized and otherwise lawful files remain lawful.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by all eight other Justices. All nine directly support each of Parts I–VII, including the independent contributory and vicarious grounds and the qualifications below.

**Controlling explanation:** Campbell requires purpose, nature, amount and market effect together, without a dispositive commerciality presumption. The findings include displaced college sales and injury to a developing licensed-download market; this is not merely loss of fees for every unlicensed use. The contrary survey was weighed, not excluded. A plan to buy music later does not erase the present redistribution. Sony's private home time shifting does not establish immunity for public exchanges, and the claimed relocation extends beyond one's private personal devices. The result holds even assuming the owners bear the preliminary burden of likely defeating fair use; the allocation of that burden across all preliminary cases is unnecessary to resolve. The prima facie direct-infringement showing was unchallenged on this appeal; findings remain preliminary.

### II. Substantial lawful capability does not immunize specific ongoing assistance

**Holding and operative rule:** A service can incur contributory liability by knowingly providing material assistance to identified infringement, notwithstanding substantial noninfringing uses of its technology. Sony bars imputing the needed knowledge from capability alone; it does not immunize continuing materially assisting operation after specific knowledge.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The notices identified more than 12,000 infringing files, continued availability followed asserted account terminations, and the search and index functions materially assisted access. Those circumstances support likely knowledge and contribution. General knowledge that copyrighted music circulates does not establish unauthorized copying; lawful capability remains material and present infringement percentages are not an exclusive test. The rule requires no server access to MP3 contents the system does not possess.

### III. Financial benefit and actual supervision independently support preliminary vicarious liability

**Holding and operative rule:** Vicarious liability separately requires a direct financial benefit from infringement and the right and practical ability to supervise it. Attraction of infringing recordings to the user base can satisfy the benefit element without direct transaction fees, while account exclusion and index control support supervision over the service's actual assistance; both elements are supported here at the preliminary stage.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** This is an independently sufficient preliminary ground, not a substitute for contributory knowledge. ### IV. The Audio Home Recording Act is confined to its defined coverage

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1008 does not immunize this general-purpose computer service's open redistribution merely because users characterize copying as noncommercial. Its consumer-recording protection depends on the defined devices, media and recording acts.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Under §1001(5), a digital musical recording is a material object containing, in digital form, only fixed sounds and related, relatively minor material, statements or instructions, from which the sounds and material can be perceived, reproduced or communicated directly or with a device. Entirely spoken-word recordings, including speech accompanied by merely incidental sounds, and objects containing computer programs are excluded; instructions constituting the fixed sounds and incidental material or bringing about their perception, reproduction or communication remain permitted. A digital audio recording device must be of a type commonly distributed to individuals for their use, whether separate or part of another machine, have a digital recording function designed or marketed primarily to make private digital audio copied recordings, and be capable of doing so. Professional model products and dictation, answering or other audio equipment designed and marketed primarily to fix nonmusical sounds are excluded. The service cannot detach the exemption from those conditions. This disposes of the asserted exemption without classifying every future dedicated player.

### V. Section 512 independently limits liability and relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Secondary-liability status does not itself defeat a §512 protection. The lower court must determine each invoked service category and condition independently and apply the corresponding injunction limits before imposing relief beyond them; failure under one category does not decide another.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts Part V and the complete operative conditions below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the infringement theories and Congress's service protections perform separate work. Supported likely liability does not answer whether a particular transmission or location-tool function satisfies its statutory conditions. Nor does failure under one category establish another's failure. The contrary demand for immediate unrestricted policing would make the statute's separate notice, knowledge, control and injunction limits disappear before the necessary findings. The preliminary record permits continued consideration of fitted relief, while the court must establish the applicable classification and predicates before imposing relief exceeding a statutory protection. This is affirmative remand work under the governing rules, not a decision that every safe harbor is unavailable.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Secondary-liability status alone does not defeat a safe harbor. The functions in (a)–(d) must be tested separately, and failure under one does not establish failure under another. Subsection (a) concerns automatic user-initiated transmitting, routing or connecting through the provider's system without provider selection of material or recipients except an automatic response, with no content modification. Any intermediate copy must not be maintained in a manner ordinarily accessible to anyone other than anticipated recipients, and must not remain ordinarily accessible to those recipients longer than reasonably necessary for transmission, routing or connection. Subsection (d)'s location-tool protection requires absence of actual knowledge or apparent-infringement awareness, or expeditious removal/disablement upon obtaining it; no directly attributable financial benefit where the provider has the right and ability to control; and expeditious response to a qualifying notice identifying the reference or link sufficiently to locate it. Subsection (i) additionally requires adoption and reasonable implementation of a policy terminating repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances, with subscribers and account holders informed of that policy and accommodation without interference of standard technical measures. Such measures must arise from broad open, fair, voluntary multi-industry consensus, be reasonably and nondiscriminatorily available, and impose no substantial provider cost or system burden.

A qualifying notice must substantially identify the copyrighted work or representative site list, the material or location reference and sufficient location information, provide contact information, bear an authorized signature, state a good-faith belief that the use lacks authorization from owner, agent or law, and state that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that the complainant is authorized to act for the owner of an allegedly infringed exclusive right. When a notice delivered to the designated agent substantially supplies the work, material or link location, and contact information but fails other elements, that exclusion applies only if the provider promptly attempts to contact the sender or takes other reasonable steps to assist receipt of a notice substantially satisfying all required elements. The preliminary notices support the underlying knowledge issue, but final satisfaction of each formal notice condition, service category and policy requirement remains for determination on remand.

Section 512(m) does not permit conditioning safe harbors on affirmative monitoring or searching for facts of infringement, apart from qualifying standard technical measures, or on access/removal forbidden by law. Failure to qualify is not affirmative evidence against an independent noninfringement defense under (l). Under (j), a provider protected from monetary relief still may receive only the permitted forms of injunction: for non-(a) conduct, specified-site access restraint, termination of specified infringing accounts, or other necessary relief addressing specified copyrighted material at a particular online location that is least burdensome among comparably effective measures; for (a) conduct, only termination of specified infringing accounts and reasonable steps blocking a specific identified foreign online location. The court must consider cumulative system burden, likely owner harm, technical feasibility and effectiveness, effects on access to lawful material elsewhere, and comparably effective less burdensome means. Notice and an opportunity to appear are required except evidence-preservation or other orders with no material adverse network effect. These limitations must govern if their predicates are satisfied; an unresolved classification is not permission to evade them through a generalized policing command.

**Holding and operative rule:** On this supported preliminary record, unrestricted exchange of complete protected creative recordings with unrelated users is unlikely to be fair use under §107. Nontransformative whole-work copying and supported substitution for existing and developing markets outweigh the claimed sampling or relocation purposes; artist-authorized and otherwise lawful files remain lawful.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by all eight other Justices. All nine directly support each of Parts I–VII, including the independent contributory and vicarious grounds and the qualifications below.

**Controlling explanation:** Campbell requires purpose, nature, amount and market effect together, without a dispositive commerciality presumption. The findings include displaced college sales and injury to a developing licensed-download market; this is not merely loss of fees for every unlicensed use. The contrary survey was weighed, not excluded. A plan to buy music later does not erase the present redistribution. Sony's private home time shifting does not establish immunity for public exchanges, and the claimed relocation extends beyond one's private personal devices. The result holds even assuming the owners bear the preliminary burden of likely defeating fair use; the allocation of that burden across all preliminary cases is unnecessary to resolve. The prima facie direct-infringement showing was unchallenged on this appeal; findings remain preliminary.

### II. Substantial lawful capability does not immunize specific ongoing assistance

**Holding and operative rule:** A service can incur contributory liability by knowingly providing material assistance to identified infringement, notwithstanding substantial noninfringing uses of its technology. Sony bars imputing the needed knowledge from capability alone; it does not immunize continuing materially assisting operation after specific knowledge.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The notices identified more than 12,000 infringing files, continued availability followed asserted account terminations, and the search and index functions materially assisted access. Those circumstances support likely knowledge and contribution. General knowledge that copyrighted music circulates does not establish unauthorized copying; lawful capability remains material and present infringement percentages are not an exclusive test. The rule requires no server access to MP3 contents the system does not possess.

### III. Financial benefit and actual supervision independently support preliminary vicarious liability

**Holding and operative rule:** Vicarious liability separately requires a direct financial benefit from infringement and the right and practical ability to supervise it. Attraction of infringing recordings to the user base can satisfy the benefit element without direct transaction fees, while account exclusion and index control support supervision over the service's actual assistance; both elements are supported here at the preliminary stage.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** This is an independently sufficient preliminary ground, not a substitute for contributory knowledge. The architecture both supports limited control and restricts the remedy. A theoretical ability to close the entire service does not create a general duty to inspect inaccessible user files or guarantee prevention of unknown copying.

### IV. The Audio Home Recording Act is confined to its defined coverage

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1008 does not immunize this general-purpose computer service's open redistribution merely because users characterize copying as noncommercial. Its consumer-recording protection depends on the defined devices, media and recording acts.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Under §1001(5), a digital musical recording is a material object containing, in digital form, only fixed sounds and related, relatively minor material, statements or instructions, from which the sounds and material can be perceived, reproduced or communicated directly or with a device. Entirely spoken-word recordings, including speech accompanied by merely incidental sounds, and objects containing computer programs are excluded; instructions constituting the fixed sounds and incidental material or bringing about their perception, reproduction or communication remain permitted. A digital audio recording device must be of a type commonly distributed to individuals for their use, whether separate or part of another machine, have a digital recording function designed or marketed primarily to make private digital audio copied recordings, and be capable of doing so. Professional model products and dictation, answering or other audio equipment designed and marketed primarily to fix nonmusical sounds are excluded. The service cannot detach the exemption from those conditions. This disposes of the asserted exemption without classifying every future dedicated player.

### V. Section 512 independently limits liability and relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Secondary-liability status does not itself defeat a §512 protection. The lower court must determine each invoked service category and condition independently and apply the corresponding injunction limits before imposing relief beyond them; failure under one category does not decide another.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts Part V and the complete operative conditions below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the infringement theories and Congress's service protections perform separate work. Supported likely liability does not answer whether a particular transmission or location-tool function satisfies its statutory conditions. Nor does failure under one category establish another's failure. The contrary demand for immediate unrestricted policing would make the statute's separate notice, knowledge, control and injunction limits disappear before the necessary findings. The preliminary record permits continued consideration of fitted relief, while the court must establish the applicable classification and predicates before imposing relief exceeding a statutory protection. This is affirmative remand work under the governing rules, not a decision that every safe harbor is unavailable.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Secondary-liability status alone does not defeat a safe harbor. The functions in (a)–(d) must be tested separately, and failure under one does not establish failure under another. Subsection (a) concerns automatic user-initiated transmitting, routing or connecting through the provider's system without provider selection of material or recipients except an automatic response, with no content modification. Any intermediate copy must not be maintained in a manner ordinarily accessible to anyone other than anticipated recipients, and must not remain ordinarily accessible to those recipients longer than reasonably necessary for transmission, routing or connection. Subsection (d)'s location-tool protection requires absence of actual knowledge or apparent-infringement awareness, or expeditious removal/disablement upon obtaining it; no directly attributable financial benefit where the provider has the right and ability to control; and expeditious response to a qualifying notice identifying the reference or link sufficiently to locate it. Subsection (i) additionally requires adoption and reasonable implementation of a policy terminating repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances, with subscribers and account holders informed of that policy and accommodation without interference of standard technical measures. Such measures must arise from broad open, fair, voluntary multi-industry consensus, be reasonably and nondiscriminatorily available, and impose no substantial provider cost or system burden.

A qualifying notice must substantially identify the copyrighted work or representative site list, the material or location reference and sufficient location information, provide contact information, bear an authorized signature, state a good-faith belief that the use lacks authorization from owner, agent or law, and state that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that the complainant is authorized to act for the owner of an allegedly infringed exclusive right. Under §512(c)(3)(B), incorporated by (d)(3), a notice that fails substantial compliance may not be considered in determining statutory actual knowledge or awareness of facts making infringement apparent. When a notice delivered to the designated agent substantially supplies the work, material or link location, and contact information but fails other elements, that exclusion applies only if the provider promptly attempts to contact the sender or takes other reasonable steps to assist receipt of a notice substantially satisfying all required elements. The preliminary notices support the underlying knowledge issue, but final satisfaction of each formal notice condition, service category and policy requirement remains for determination on remand.

Section 512(m) does not permit conditioning safe harbors on affirmative monitoring or searching for facts of infringement, apart from qualifying standard technical measures, or on access/removal forbidden by law. Failure to qualify is not affirmative evidence against an independent noninfringement defense under (l). Under (j), a provider protected from monetary relief still may receive only the permitted forms of injunction: for non-(a) conduct, specified-site access restraint, termination of specified infringing accounts, or other necessary relief addressing specified copyrighted material at a particular online location that is least burdensome among comparably effective measures; for (a) conduct, only termination of specified infringing accounts and reasonable steps blocking a specific identified foreign online location. The court must consider cumulative system burden, likely owner harm, technical feasibility and effectiveness, effects on access to lawful material elsewhere, and comparably effective less burdensome means. Notice and an opportunity to appear are required except evidence-preservation or other orders with no material adverse network effect. These limitations must govern if their predicates are satisfied; an unresolved classification is not permission to evade them through a generalized policing command.

### VI. The remaining defenses and preliminary objections do not defeat fitted relief

**Holding and operative rule:** On this preliminary record, the owners' exploration of MP3 businesses does not establish intentional abandonment or permission for this service, and enforcing reproduction and distribution rights does not establish misuse extending copyright to unrelated property. No demonstrated abuse of discretion requires more discovery or an evidentiary hearing, the $5 million security is supported against the competing evidence, and no statutory basis or special circumstance requires a compulsory-royalty substitute.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The defense elements and record-specific review standard govern separately. Express objection to the service defeats the asserted inference of permission; toleration or business exploration alone is neither abandonment nor a license. Security need not equal the claimed enterprise value merely because that value is asserted. Copyright's remedies cannot be replaced with a new license solely for technological novelty. First Amendment interests protect lawful files and independent expression through the remedy's scope, without immunizing the identified infringement. Later materially supported defenses and security evidence remain matters for their own proceedings.

### VII. The replacement injunction must connect usable notice to feasible service action

**Holding and operative rule:** Owners must identify protected works and sufficiently locate infringing files for practical response, and Napster must act within its actual index and account capabilities, including reasonable action against identified repeat infringers. The decree must preserve lawful files and authorized exchange, satisfy the remaining equitable predicates and any applicable §512 limits, and cannot make Napster guarantee prevention of every unidentified infringement.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The original order shifted the full identification/prevention burden onto the service and exceeded the supported controls. Specific knowledge and practical contribution justify effective relief at their proved scope; they do not justify impossible filtering or a blanket technology prohibition. Remand is for a conforming preliminary decree under these rules. The original July 26, 2000 order remains stayed until conformity; neither a later replacement order nor final liability is assumed.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.:** The integrated four-factor inquiry governs; commerciality alone creates no dispositive presumption, and concrete substitution is distinct from mere loss of a fee for unlicensed use. The preliminary outcome does not require resolving the general burden allocation.
- **Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, Inc., 464 U.S. 417:** Substantial noninfringing capability prevents imputed contributory liability from capability alone. Its private time-shifting application does not immunize public redistribution or specific ongoing assistance after knowledge.
- **New York Times Co. v. Tasini:** Its separation of actual statutory rights, use permissions and individualized remedy remains; it supplies neither a general digital-use exemption nor a compulsory license.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc.:** Its demand for a remedy fitted to the proved conduct informs preservation of independent lawful expression; infringement does not justify restraints on unrelated lawful files.

## Law After Decision

Specific knowing material assistance and separately established financial benefit with practical supervision can support preliminary secondary liability for an ongoing service despite substantial lawful capability. Fair use remains contextual; open whole-recording redistribution on this record receives no categorical sampling, relocation or device exemption. Congress's §512 categories and relief limits remain independently enforceable. Effective injunctive relief must connect usable identification to feasible service action while preserving lawful exchange.
