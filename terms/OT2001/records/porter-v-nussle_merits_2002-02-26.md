### Exhaustion covers individual incidents of prison life

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1997e(a) requires a confined prisoner to exhaust actually available administrative remedies before bringing a federal action about prison life, whether it concerns general circumstances or a particular episode and whether it alleges excessive force or another wrong. A responsive process must be exhausted even when it cannot award the damages sought; the statute does not deem an actually obstructed or nonresponsive process available.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices, supplies nine direct votes for the rule, its application and the stated remedial conditions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that prison conditions describes the subject of the action, not the number of prisoners harmed or the duration of the alleged wrong. McCarthy v. Bronson treated isolated misconduct as a matter of confinement under analogous magistrate-referral language. That context supports the enacted prison-life category here, although Bronson construed a different statute. Booth requires the responsive administrative opportunity Congress made mandatory, rather than an exception based on the particular judicial remedy requested.

Nussle's proposed distinction between enduring conditions and an officer's assault has no stable basis in this provision. The Eighth Amendment's different culpability rules for force and ordinary conditions answer a merits question; they do not define this exhaustion command. Administrative review can address individual misconduct, investigate and preserve evidence without immunizing violence or promising a monetary award. The allegation's seriousness does not itself supply a statutory exception.

Nussle's action concerns prison life and therefore falls within the provision. Actual availability remains a statutory predicate, and an allegation of reprisal is not a judicial finding of either obstruction or accessibility. The separate screening rule in section 1997e(c)(2) permits dismissal before exhaustion of a facially frivolous or malicious claim, a claim failing to state a claim, or one seeking money from an immune defendant. That permission neither compels such dismissal nor entitles a prisoner to bypass exhaustion for a viable claim. The prison-life category remains distinct from a challenge to the fact or duration of custody.
