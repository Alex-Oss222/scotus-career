### I. The separate trafficking prohibition has its own elements and exceptions

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1201(a)(2) prohibits manufacture, importation, offering to the public, provision or other trafficking in a technology, product, service, device, component or part that is primarily designed or produced to circumvent effective access control over a work protected by the Copyright Act, has only limited commercially significant other purpose or use, or is marketed for that circumvention by the responsible person or a concerted actor with that person's knowledge; these are alternative predicates. Section 1201(c)(1) preserves infringement rights and defenses, including fair use, but does not supply a general defense to that independent trafficking prohibition.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine adopt the statutory rule, the application and the precise statutory limits below.

**Controlling explanation:** Effective control requires authorized information, process or treatment in ordinary operation; circumvention includes decryption or bypass without the owner's authority. The identified DeCSS posting meets the designed-circumvention ground, which suffices for this application. Separate infringement and trafficking provisions cannot be collapsed, and (c)(2) leaves contributory/vicarious infringement law separate. The class exemption under (a)(1) does not defend another provision under (a)(1)(E). The nonprofit acquisition permission likewise supplies no (a)(2) or (b) defense. The distinct exceptions retain their own conditions, stated below; this record establishes no entitlement to one.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Under (a)(1), the Librarian's periodic rulemaking may identify a class whose users are or are likely during the succeeding three years to be adversely affected in their noninfringing uses by the individual access prohibition. The published class exemption protects those users as to that class for the ensuing three years; (a)(1)(E) prohibits using that exemption or rulemaking determination as a defense to other provisions, including trafficking. The nonprofit library, archives or educational-institution exemption permits access to a commercially exploited copyrighted work solely to determine in good faith whether to acquire a copy solely for conduct permitted by the Copyright Act, and only when an identical copy is not reasonably available in another form; retention must last no longer than necessary and the accessed copy cannot be used for another purpose. For a library or archives, its collections must be open to the public or available both to affiliated researchers and other persons researching a specialized field. It expressly supplies no (a)(2) or (b) trafficking defense. Reverse engineering under (f) concerns a lawfully obtained right to use a program and elements not previously readily available to the person and necessary for interoperability of an independently created program with other programs; identification and analysis must be for that sole purpose and noninfringing. Necessary tools may be developed and employed for that analysis or interoperability, and information or means may be shared solely to enable that interoperability, without infringement or other illegality. Encryption research under (g) identifies and analyzes flaws or vulnerabilities in encryption applied to copyrighted works to advance knowledge or assist development of encryption products. Qualifying good-faith circumvention requires lawful acquisition of the encrypted copy, phonorecord, performance or display of a published work, necessity for that research, a good-faith effort to obtain authorization before circumvention, and no infringement or other applicable-law violation. Tools may be developed and employed solely for the researcher's own qualifying research and provided to a collaborator for qualifying research or verification. Qualification factors include whether dissemination reasonably advances knowledge or technology development rather than facilitating infringement or other illegality; legitimate study, employment, training or experience in encryption; and whether, with what documentation, and when the researcher informs the copyright owner of the findings. Security testing under (j) requires the owner or operator's authorization and the sole purpose of good-faith testing, investigation or correction of a system flaw; the act must not infringe or violate other applicable law. Qualification factors include whether information is used solely to promote the owner's or operator's security or shared directly with the developer, and whether its use or maintenance avoids facilitating infringement or other illegality, including privacy or security violations. Those are statutory factors rather than categorical prerequisites. Developing, producing, distributing or employing tools is permitted solely for qualifying testing, provided the means do not otherwise violate (a)(2). The findings do not establish that these defendants meet one of those distinct permissions. Their existence is not a factual finding that every legitimate scientific use is accommodated, nor a license to decide unpresented constitutional applications.

Tools may be developed and employed solely for the researcher's own qualifying research and provided to a collaborator for qualifying research or verification. Qualification factors include whether dissemination reasonably advances knowledge or technology development rather than facilitating infringement or other illegality; legitimate study, employment, training or experience in encryption; and whether, with what documentation, and when the researcher informs the copyright owner of the findings. Security testing under (j) requires the owner or operator's authorization and the sole purpose of good-faith testing, investigation or correction of a system flaw; the act must not infringe or violate other applicable law. Qualification factors include whether information is used solely to promote the owner's or operator's security or shared directly with the developer, and whether its use or maintenance avoids facilitating infringement or other illegality, including privacy or security violations. Those are statutory factors rather than categorical prerequisites. Developing, producing, distributing or employing tools is permitted solely for qualifying testing, provided the means do not otherwise violate (a)(2). The findings do not establish that these defendants meet one of those distinct permissions. Their existence is not a factual finding that every legitimate scientific use is accommodated, nor a license to decide unpresented constitutional applications.

### II. The operative circumvention application is content neutral and adequately fitted

**Holding and operative rule:** Executable code can communicate ideas and perform an operation; its expressive character receives First Amendment protection without immunizing the demonstrated unauthorized access-circumvention function. Applied to the working DeCSS tool, §1201(a)(2) advances a substantial interest independent of suppressing expression without a substantially excessive speech burden, and the posting injunction burdens no more speech than necessary to remedy the demonstrated violation.

**Authority:** All nine join this part of Thomas's Opinion of the Court and its exact stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Turner and O'Brien supply the statutory intermediate review; Madsen supplies the distinct injunction fit. The operation defeating effective access protection, rather than an encryption viewpoint or the films' messages, triggers this application. The tool's expressive value remains relevant. Restricting only completed infringement would not protect the distinct access interest Congress enacted. This reasoning does not depend on the proposition that all software is conduct, all scientific discussion is unprotected or a benign purpose conclusively establishes neutrality. The working-tool restraint, rather than abstract reporting, supplies the actual fit.

### III. The proved knowing and purposeful linking satisfies this injunction's limits

**Holding and operative rule:** The linking restraint is sustained where clear and convincing findings establish that the responsible person knows the prohibited material is on the linked site, knows it is unlawfully offered circumvention technology, and creates or maintains the link for the purpose of disseminating it. Those findings connect this restraint to the demonstrated tool-delivery violation and exclude uninformed or merely referential links and mere reporting without the proved dissemination purpose; a reporting label does not exempt a link that satisfies all three findings.

**Authority:** All nine join this part of Thomas's Opinion of the Court and its exact stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** A link communicates reference but can also deliver operational access. Barring posting alone while permitting knowing direct delivery for the same purpose would leave the established violation intact. Collateral protected material on a linked page is a real expressive cost, answered here by the actual purpose, knowledge and tailored scope rather than denied. The three safeguards and proof standard are sufficient for this decree; the Court does not prescribe universal constitutional minima or new statutory elements for all linking cases. The Government's reserved objection to the proof quantum requires no enlargement of this order.

### IV. The presented broader fair-use and limited-times challenges do not defeat this application

**Holding and operative rule:** The defendants identify no personal movie fair use and provide scant evidence of interference with prospective fair users; the record likewise contains no allegation or evidence of a plaintiff withholding a public-domain work or the injunction blocking its copying. These record limits defeat the requested broader constitutional relief here without holding fair use constitutionally irrelevant or approving perpetual control of public-domain works.

**Authority:** All nine join Part IV and these concrete-record grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** A lawful use does not establish an entitlement to every preferred digital copying method on this undeveloped record. A concrete indispensable-lawful-use or public-domain application requires its own preserved facts. The actual injunction neither extends a copyright term nor resolves those distinct applications.

### V. The limited-times contention independently fails preservation

**Holding and operative rule:** Presentation only in a footnote below did not adequately present the limited-times challenge for this merits review; this independent ground also supports declining the abstract claim.

**Authority:** Part V is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight adopt this independent preservation ground. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment and Part IV's concrete-record ground, but not Part V.

**Controlling explanation:** The limited-times contention was not adequately presented through its footnote reference below. That procedural limit independently warrants declining the challenge. It supplies no merits rule permitting technological extension of a copyright term.

## Precedent Treatment

- **Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC:** Its independent content classification and intermediate scrutiny govern the statutory burden; a benign purpose or technology label does not establish fit.
- **United States v. O'Brien, 391 U.S. 367, and Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781:** Functional regulation may burden expression if its independent substantial interest and adequate fit are established; intermediate scrutiny does not demand a universal least-restrictive-means test.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc.:** Its distinct requirement that a neutral injunction burden no more speech than necessary applies to the demonstrated tool-delivery violation. The actual predicate and confined relief do not erase earlier concerns about restraints on lawful advocacy.
- **Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union and United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc.:** Protection of expression in new media remains; the present function-based application is distinguished from content-selected suppression of protected communicative material.
- **Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition:** Its distinction between protected expression and independently defined unlawful conduct is preserved. It does not immunize the demonstrated access-circumvention operation or supply a new defense absent from §1201.
- **Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.:** Fair use retains its integrated statutory role in infringement litigation; it is not transposed wholesale into a separately enacted trafficking defense.
- **New York Times Co. v. Tasini:** Actual statutory rights and use permissions remain distinct; the value of a digital application alone does not create an unprovided permission.
- **Harper & Row, Publishers, Inc. v. Nation Enterprises, 471 U.S. 539:** The relationship between copyright protection and expression does not eliminate scrutiny of a distinct burden; the undeveloped fair-use record here supports no universal constitutional answer.

## Law After Decision

Section 1201(a)(2) supplies a distinct trafficking prohibition with separate predicates and limited exceptions; fair-use defenses to infringement do not automatically defeat it. Code remains expression, while the demonstrated content-neutral circumvention function and the actual fitted injunction satisfy the governing scrutiny here. The defined linking safeguards suffice for this decree without becoming universal minimum elements. The concrete-record and preservation grounds leave materially different fair-use and public-domain applications to their own preserved facts.
