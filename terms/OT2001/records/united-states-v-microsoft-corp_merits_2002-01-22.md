## Controlling holdings

### I. The presented platform combination requires an actual competitive inquiry

**Holding and operative rule.** The challenged combination of a principal platform for third-party applications and complementary browser functionality, including its contractual and removal restrictions, must be evaluated under the rule of reason because the existing per se tying category does not reliably resolve the material integration and competitive questions presented. Courts must examine separate demand, conditioning, power, foreclosure and supported efficiencies for the actual restrictions; integration supplies neither immunity nor a presumption of justification, and an adequately supported inquiry may be abbreviated rather than invariably requiring a comprehensive market study.

**Authority.** Part I of Stone-Zsela's opinion has eight votes: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens does not join the uniform category direction.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the legal method must fit the combination's actual competitive mechanism. Jefferson Parish and Kodak preserve independent consumer demand and the need to prove tying elements; functional connection alone does not merge separate products. But evidence of separate demand and past industry practice may incompletely measure a platform integration that changes the product. California Dental requires enough inquiry to test a substantial competing explanation, permitting abbreviated analysis when experience and the mechanism make the effects sufficiently clear. Khan and NYNEX likewise require a restraint to fit the category invoked. None supplies general software immunity.

Stevens would retain ordinary per se analysis for separable contractual coercion. That concern identifies conduct the actual inquiry must examine, but the relationship among product definition, interface distribution and each restriction warrants the bounded competitive inquiry here. Distributing useful application interfaces may explain less than compulsory retention of the whole browser. Microsoft must substantiate benefits material to its defense, and an explanation that does not justify a particular restriction cannot shelter it. Plaintiffs must establish net anticompetitive effect in browser competition, considering benefits actually supported for that restriction. Operating-system injury sustaining section 2 does not itself establish browser-market injury under section 1. The original undifferentiated judgment did not undertake that inquiry; its vacatur stands.

**Application and mandate limits.** OEM nonremoval restrictions and omission from the removal utility remain possible tying theories, with any additional relief separately justified. Plaintiffs may test the default-browser override's distinct browser-market effect despite rejection of its section 2 ground on the earlier record. Price bundling requires resolution of a positive browser-attributable price increment, including conflicting competitor-discount evidence, before weighing effects and benefits. Commingled code is not added as an independent trial tying basis. The attempted-monopolization reversal remains: plaintiffs may not renew tying theories dependent on a precise browser-market definition or independent entry barriers they failed to establish for that claim, apart from matters implicit in the tying arrangement itself. That restriction preserves this case's mandate rather than creating a universal antitrust rule.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that publication delayed until judgment did not remove the appearance created by secret outside discussions while adjudication continued. Liteky supplies the cumulative objective standard; judicial origin is context rather than immunity, and outside origin is neither necessary nor sufficient by itself. These contacts concerned the dispute's live subjects and denied the parties an opportunity to raise the issue while the conduct continued. The reported accounts remain assumed true for the disqualification inquiry on the appellate posture, rather than new trial findings about every reported detail.

Liljeberg separately requires attention to injustice to these parties, injustice in other cases and public confidence when determining relief from earlier civil adjudication. Its finality-specific Rule 60(b)(6) prerequisites do not become conditions of this direct appeal. The three interests reject both automatic total vacatur and automatic preservation. All nine agree that the existing decree cannot stand. Apart from disqualification, unresolved remedy-specific disputes without an appropriate hearing, inadequate explanation of the remedy and materially altered liability foundations independently require new remedial proceedings. Neither mandatory divestiture nor immunity from divestiture follows; disputed predictions about division require adjudication at the remedial stage.

### III. Ordinary appellate review does not conclusively resolve the need to revisit compromised liability foundations

**Holding and operative rule.** On the established appearance violation, the new judge must apply Liljeberg's three remedial interests to determine which material factual or credibility foundations cannot meaningfully be separated from the concealed contacts and require fresh determination, and must reconsider the findings and dependent conclusions accordingly. Independently sustainable determinations may remain at their established scope, with appropriate reuse of the record; neither actual-bias proof as to a particular finding nor an automatic all-issues retrial is required.

**Authority.** Part III of Stone-Zsela's opinion has five votes: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer reject this additional-remedy application while accepting the common standard.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the appellate allocation left a material part of Liljeberg's inquiry unanswered. The interviews began before the findings and concerned integration, credibility and the dispute's merits. Rule 52 clear-error review preserves permissible factual choices; it does not independently choose among competing credible accounts or permissible expert inferences. The careful rejection of some liability bases therefore cannot alone establish that every retained foundation may remain binding despite the undisclosed relationship. Anderson and Pullman-Standard preserve this distinction between appellate review and factfinding.

The opposing Justices properly emphasize the innocent plaintiffs' burden, differentiated appellate corrections, limited additional deterrence and confidence restored by replacement of the judge and remedy. Those considerations weigh against indiscriminate repetition. They do not sufficiently justify conclusive insulation of every disputed material foundation reached after the contacts began. Public confidence and the risk of injustice in other cases require the new judge to decide which foundations need neutral reconsideration, without demanding proof of actual bias that section 455(a) does not require. Undisputed matters and independently sustainable legal determinations may remain; prior legal rulings remain pertinent to facts lawfully determined. The remand establishes a tailored inquiry and resulting reconsideration, not presumptive universal taint. Particular findings' binding force is application work under that rule.

## Precedent Treatment

- Jefferson Parish Hospital District No. 2 v. Hyde, 466 U.S. 2: retains the independent-demand and ordinary tying elements; its categorical inference is distinguished for the platform combination presented.
- Eastman Kodak Co. v. Image Technical Services (June 8, 1992): preserves evidence-based separate demand and rejection of categorical economic assumptions; the present decision grants no integration immunity or final liability finding.
- California Dental Association v. FTC (May 24, 1999): applies its proportionate competitive inquiry and requirement to test a material explanation; no universal full-market study is imposed.
- State Oil Co. v. Khan (November 4, 1997): applies its category-fit principle without extending its separate vertical maximum-price holding.
- NYNEX Corp. v. Discon (December 14, 1998): applies its requirement to examine the actual competitive mechanism rather than extend a per se category by label.
- Liteky v. United States (March 7, 1994): applies the single cumulative objective appearance standard, without actual-bias or exceptional-intensity requirements; its reserved civil-remedy question is resolved here at the stated scope.
- Liljeberg v. Health Services Acquisition Corp., 486 U.S. 847: applies all three remedial interests on direct appeal without transplanting finality-specific Rule 60(b)(6) prerequisites or adopting automatic wholesale vacatur.
- Anderson v. Bessemer City, 470 U.S. 564, and Pullman-Standard v. Swint, 456 U.S. 273: preserve the distinction between clear-error review and fresh findings; ordinary appellate examination does not itself redo material credibility or inferential choices.

## Law After Decision

The presented platform/browser combination receives a restriction-specific rule-of-reason inquiry, with existing tying categories preserved outside this setting and the case's unpresented-claim limits retained. Section 455(a)'s appearance rule remains objective. Civil relief from an established violation depends on all three Liljeberg interests; on this record they require a new judge to assess and reconsider material liability foundations where meaningful independence cannot be established, beyond replacement of the remedial decree. The factual competitive balance, individual findings' binding status and appropriate relief are remand applications of those rules.

## Separate Writings

Stevens concurs in Parts II and III and dissents from Part I's uniform direction. Jefferson Parish and Kodak preserve the established category when independent demand and a separable forced tie satisfy its elements. Genuine functional integration may warrant fuller competitive inquiry, but useful distribution of interfaces does not make compulsory retention of a complete browser an innovative design. His California Dental position likewise requires examination of the actual restraint. He would distinguish contractual and removal restraints that fit the conventional category from integrated functionality; he would neither reinstate the entire undifferentiated trial judgment nor decide ultimate tying liability.

O'Connor, joined by Scalia, Thomas and Breyer, concurs in Parts I and II and dissents from Part III. Under all three Liljeberg interests, they find the existing appellate allocation sufficient. The plaintiffs did not participate in the misconduct; repeating completed adjudication can impose serious costs; appellate review differentiated sustainable liability from rejected theories; and reassignment plus replacement of the entire remedy materially protect deterrence and public confidence. They expressly account for interviews beginning before the findings. The misconduct's pronounced remedial expression supports their line without making all earlier contacts irrelevant. They adopt neither an actual-bias prerequisite nor an automatic rule that clear-error review cures disqualification. Their disagreement concerns the sufficiency of this particular allocation, not the governing appearance standard or a belief that the appellate court conducted a new trial.
