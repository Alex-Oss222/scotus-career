## Judgment, controlling law and continuity

**Reversed and remanded, 8–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Not participating:** Breyer.

The preliminary injunction must be vacated insofar as it rests on the categorical tenant-knowledge requirement or the three additional categorical constitutional grounds rejected in Parts III–V. Any remaining or renewed preliminary protection requires its own supported legal ground and satisfaction of the ordinary preliminary-relief requirements. The named tenants' actual removability remains for the authorized proceedings; the mandate does not direct their eviction.

### I. Statutory authority and the actual lease

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1437d(l)(6) requires a lease ground permitting termination for qualifying activity by the tenant, a household member, a guest or another person under the tenant's control, without an additional actual-or-constructive-knowledge requirement. Inclusion of that ground is mandatory; termination in an eligible case remains discretionary and requires the actual activity, relationship and lease predicates.

The threatening-criminal-activity branch concerns other tenants' health, safety or peaceful enjoyment. The separate drug-related branch covers activity on or off the premises without importing the first branch's threat requirement. That later statutory wording does not replace an older lease's on-or-near limitation. Under the applicable 1997 guest definition, the person must be in the leased unit with a household member's consent. Control does not require constant ability to prevent conduct; a stranger's remote association does not establish a covered relationship. Drug-related activity means illegal manufacture, sale, distribution, use, or possession with intent to manufacture, sell, distribute or use a controlled substance.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all eight participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress specified the actors, conduct and consequence without adding a tenant mental state. MCI and Christensen require respect for the enacted boundary rather than administrative or judicial amendment. Household membership and guest access supply a tenancy connection without imputing a personal crime to the tenant. The general prohibitions on unreasonable terms and termination without serious or repeated violation or other good cause remain operative, but cannot erase the specific ground. The tenants' strongest reading combines those provisions, control, housing purposes and constitutional avoidance into a knowledge condition. That reading rewrites the particular clause; ignorance also does not establish every constitutional predicate. Conversely, a mandatory lease clause does not make every permitted termination compulsory. The allegations here must still satisfy the actual lease, including its geographical terms, and the applicable definitions. This independent construction resolves the statutory question in both dockets without treating agency discretion as authority beyond the statute.

### II. Due process and the defined Bennis protection

**Controlling proposition:** Permission to terminate a conditioned public tenancy for covered third-party conduct is not facially invalid merely because the tenant lacks personal knowledge or criminal fault. Protected tenancy interests, required process and independently established substantive defenses remain enforceable; civil form, lease assent and optional leniency do not defeat them.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all eight participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** A prospective household condition protecting other residents differs from personally convicting the tenant. Bennis nevertheless prevents statutory permission or agency grace from answering every property claim. It protects a separately owned interest in an ordinarily lawful automobile when the claimant proves actual ownership, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention. Shared lawful use does not defeat that defense; omniscience, constant monitoring and prevention at any cost are not required. Culpable entrustment, known misuse and omitted reasonable precautions remain outside it. The claimant receives a meaningful opportunity to prove the ordinary burden, without a heightened quantum; established protection requires preservation or restoration of the interest. Otherwise valid forfeiture does not automatically require compensation. The injunction's ignorance condition does not establish those predicates, and Bennis did not adjudicate a continuing public tenancy. That difference defeats a universal innocence immunity here. But a landlord label cannot foreclose a supported analogy before the actual lease interest, prevention facts and deprivation are established. The individual analogical question remains open because this preliminary record does not supply those necessary facts, not because optional mitigation substitutes for constitutional protection. Required notice and a meaningful opportunity to contest the agency's case remain distinct from the existence of a substantive defense.

### III. First Amendment association

**Controlling proposition:** Household or guest status and resulting economic pressure alone do not establish a First Amendment tenant-knowledge condition. An association claim requires protected expressive activity or relationship and a material governmental burden upon it; no particular expressive predicate is established by this categorical challenge.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment rejecting this independent injunction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The clause addresses specified criminal conduct and occupancy relationships, not an identified viewpoint, compelled message or political membership. Hurley protects an actual expressive presentation against compelled alteration. Dale requires actual expressive impairment in its access-law setting and preserves direct speech, genuine message-bearing roles and intimate association; its organizational formulation is not an exclusive test for every tenant. Nor must expression always take the form of spoken advocacy. Lyng distinguishes an indirect economic consequence of a household benefit condition from a substantial constitutional burden while recognizing that subtle interference can be actionable. The tenants' strongest argument is practical pressure to expel associates, potentially concealing retaliation. That can matter in an actual expression-targeted application, but does not supply the missing expressive connection here. The Court neither finds that these tenants have no expression nor creates a benefits exception to the First Amendment. A properly supported expressive application remains subject to its own constitutional inquiry.

### IV. Intimate association

**Controlling proposition:** Family and genuinely intimate relationships receive protection independently of any expressive message. The statutory omission of tenant knowledge does not itself establish direct and substantial interference with a protected living arrangement or trigger strict scrutiny; the specified relationships rationally connect this categorical housing condition to the legitimate protection of safe, drug-free housing and other tenants' enjoyment.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by the same seven Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment rejecting this independent injunction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute authorizes termination for specified conduct; it does not command relatives to separate. Optional continued occupancy excluding a responsible member is not compulsory family dissolution. The strongest contrary analogy concerns an actual choice between shelter and severance of a protected relationship. The record does not establish each relationship's kind and strength, compulsory severance or a particular direct burden requiring further justification. Displacement and family pressure are not therefore deemed nonexistent. Troxel protects actual parental direction from replacement by a judge's preferred arrangement, but supplies no majority rule demanding universal strict scrutiny or a harm showing for every governmental family effect. Shahar protects enduring intimacy beyond civil marriage and requires the actual governmental interest rather than a label. Neither establishes unconditional entitlement to this dwelling despite covered misconduct. This ruling does not dilute the standard applicable when infringement of an established fundamental parental right is actually shown; Thomas's agreement rests on the missing infringement predicate, not rejection of his scrutiny position. A properly demonstrated direct and substantial burden must receive its own constitutional justification.

### V. Excessive Fines

**Controlling proposition:** Statutory permission to end a conditional public tenancy does not itself establish a punitive extraction, and tenant ignorance or economic loss alone does not make every termination a fine. A supported application must identify the legal interest actually taken and an operation extracting it at least partly as punishment; if that threshold is established, excessiveness requires a separate gross-disproportionality inquiry.

**Authority:** Part V, joined by the same seven Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment rejecting this independent injunction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Recovery of possession under a lawful occupancy condition and prospective cessation of assistance differ from Austin's drug-property forfeitures and Bajakajian's criminal currency exaction. The strongest tenant argument nevertheless identifies a valuable leasehold deliberately forfeited to punish another's conduct. Austin makes civil form, remedial purpose and noncash form insufficient answers. Government ownership of the building does not prove that a punitive taking of a tenant's interest is impossible. The present categorical authorization and supplied application findings establish no particular punitive extraction; that is not an adverse factual judgment about every tenant's interest. If one is established, lawful authority and an offense nexus do not settle excessiveness. Bajakajian requires identification of the actual offense, gravity and exaction under its gross-disproportionality rule. No adjudicated value or proportionality findings support deciding an actual forfeiture here. Ursery, Kurth Ranch and Hudson concern distinct Double Jeopardy classifications; their clearest-proof or accounting approaches do not become the fines threshold. The Court adopts no new ratio, exclusive instrumentality test, universal factor list, fixed leasehold value or remedial formula. State incorporation remains expressly undecided because this categorical claim fails without resolving it. No alternative holding approves an otherwise established excessive fine.

### VI. Remedy, discretion and operative process

**Controlling proposition:** Reversal requires removal of the preliminary injunction insofar as it rests on the rejected categorical grounds and remand for actual lease application and properly presented individual claims under their own predicates. Any retained or renewed preliminary relief must satisfy the ordinary requirements on a supported independent ground; actual eviction proceeds only through an authorized forum with applicable proof and process.

**Authority:** Part VI, joined by all eight participating Justices. The following procedural and discretionary terms qualify the remand; none is a finding that an individual predicate has been satisfied.

**Operative qualifications:** The 1997 regulations preserve these distinct requirements:

- **Discretion:** Section 966.4(l)(5) permits consideration of all circumstances, including seriousness, family participation and effects on uninvolved family members. In appropriate cases remaining members may continue occupancy subject to exclusion of members who engaged in the activity. A member who used illegal drugs may be required to demonstrate successful treatment as a residence condition. These are options, not compulsory mitigation, mandatory severance or a universal prevention defense.
- **Ordinary grievance coverage:** Sections 966.51(a)(1) and 966.53(a) cover individual tenant disputes about agency action or inaction contrary to the lease or agency regulations that adversely affects rights, duties, welfare or status. Section 966.51(b) separately excludes tenant disputes not involving the agency and class grievances; the procedure is not a forum for initiating or negotiating group policy changes. That agency-procedure limit does not bar this federal suit or adjudicate an individual defense.
- **Conditional judicial exception:** Section 966.51(a)(2) permits an agency actually to elect exclusion of an individual termination or eviction grievance only where HUD has determined that the jurisdiction requires a pre-eviction court opportunity with the defined safeguards. The grievance must involve criminal activity threatening other residents' or agency employees' health, safety or peaceful enjoyment, or drug-related criminal activity on or near the premises. Employees are included in this exception's first category; the second retains the older geographical limit. The actual court procedure must be covered by HUD's determination. The exception removes the agency grievance hearing, not the qualifying court opportunity. HUD need not use notice-and-comment procedures for its determination but must publish the list of covered procedures and make its supporting legal analysis available for public inspection and copying.
- **Court safeguards:** Section 966.53(c) requires adequate notice of termination and eviction grounds, the right to representation by counsel, opportunity to refute agency evidence through confrontation and cross-examination and to present affirmative legal or equitable defenses, and a merits decision. The counsel right is not a guarantee of appointed counsel.
- **1997 notice requirements:** Section 966.4(l)(3) requires written notice of fourteen days for nonpayment, a reasonable period considering seriousness not exceeding thirty days for threats to other residents' or agency employees' health or safety, and thirty days otherwise. It must state specific grounds, reply rights, relevant-document access and any required grievance-request right. A legally required notice to vacate may be combined with or run concurrently with that notice. Where grievance opportunity is required, termination awaits expiration of the request period and, on timely request, completion of the process even if the local notice to vacate has expired. An elected lawful exclusion requires notice of the absence of a grievance entitlement, the chosen qualifying court procedure and HUD determination, and which precise criminal-activity category applies.
- **Separate statutory notice:** Section 1437d(l)(4) requires adequate written notice no shorter than the applicable period: a reasonable period not exceeding thirty days when other tenants, agency employees or persons in the immediate vicinity face a health or safety threat, or for drug-related or violent criminal activity or a felony conviction; fourteen days for nonpayment; and thirty days otherwise, except a shorter state or local period where that last category permits it. These statutory terms do not silently replace or merge with the 1997 regulatory requirements. Section 1437d(l)(7)'s notice of pre-hearing or pretrial access to directly related documents, records and regulations also remains operative notwithstanding state law.
- **Administrative route and documents:** Eviction without court action under section 966.4(l)(4) requires local authorization, a due-process administrative hearing and the opportunity for a pre-eviction agency grievance hearing. The judicial exception cannot erase those safeguards. Section 966.4(m) requires a reasonable opportunity, upon the tenant's request before the hearing or trial, to inspect directly relevant documents held by the agency and copy them at the tenant's expense. Failure to provide that required access bars proceeding with eviction.

**Controlling explanation:** Statutory cause, discretionary choice and lawful implementation do different work. The contrary automatic-eviction position would erase the actual lease, the enumerated procedural conditions and the individual defenses preserved by Parts II–V. Conversely, optional mitigation cannot become a required hearing in every case or defeat an independently established constitutional protection. The actual actor, activity, relationship, older lease geography and procedural route must be established. No HUD determination, agency election, notice compliance or grievance-request timing is inferred from the drug allegation alone. Ordinary adjudication may resolve remaining issues on the appropriate actual record without a compulsory new evidentiary hearing in every case. The tenants' ADA and state-law claims are outside this review. The individual constitutional reservations rest on missing application predicates, while application of the settled statutory and procedural rules is ordinary remand work.

## Defined commitments and common disposition

### R1. Statutory cause, actual coverage and agency choice

Section 1437d(l)(6) makes qualifying activity by the specified tenant, household member, guest or other controlled person cause for termination without an additional requirement that the tenant knew or should have known. The clause requiring lease inclusion is mandatory; use of termination in an eligible case is permissive. The relationship and criminal-activity predicates must actually exist. The control language does not make constant supervision of every listed person's behavior a condition of statutory authority, and it does not cover a stranger through a merely remote association. The first branch covers criminal activity threatening other tenants' health, safety or right to peaceful enjoyment of the premises. The separate drug-related branch covers activity on or off the premises; the first branch's threat requirement is not silently added to it, and the later statutory geographical term does not rewrite an older actual lease. A general condition of personal tenant fault cannot be inserted into either by borrowing criminal liability rules.

Section 1437d(l)(2)'s prohibition of unreasonable terms, (l)(5)'s serious-or-repeated-violation or other-good-cause requirement, and the notice and document provisions remain operative. They do not erase the more specific listed-person ground. The strongest contrary path treats the general provisions, control language, housing purpose and constitutional avoidance together as a knowledge condition. That path fails because it rewrites the specific ground and treats mere ignorance as resolving every constitutional predicate. The alternative overbroad path, mandatory eviction whenever statutory cause exists, also fails: Congress required a lease ground, not its use in every case.

### R2. Due process, Bennis and individual defenses

Termination authority without personal tenant knowledge is not for that reason alone incompatible with due process. Government may impose lawful conditions concerning the conduct of a household occupying its housing and protect other residents without personally convicting the tenant. That proposition does not deny a protected tenancy interest or eliminate required process. Neither the civil designation, a signature on a lease nor discretionary mitigation defeats an independently established constitutional protection.

Bennis protects a separately owned interest in an ordinary lawful automobile when the claimant proves actual ownership, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all that could reasonably be expected to prevent misuse. Shared lawful use alone does not defeat the defense; constant monitoring, omniscience and prevention at any cost are not demanded. Culpable entrustment, known misuse and omitted reasonable precautions remain outside the protection. The ordinary claimant burden receives a meaningful opportunity for proof, without an invented heightened quantum. Once established, the protection requires preservation or restoration of the interest; equitable grace cannot replace it. A valid forfeiture otherwise does not automatically require compensation. These rules remain binding despite current nonparticipation by one earlier joiner.

The injunction's ignorance condition does not establish those predicates and Bennis did not adjudicate a continuing public tenancy. All seven distinguish that interest and the prospective household condition from the automobile forfeiture. All seven reject a universal innocence immunity, but do not aggregate their disagreement about possible future extension into a universal rule that Bennis can never matter to an individually demonstrated housing deprivation. The individual analogical question is reserved because the actual lease interest, prevention facts and challenged application have not been established at the necessary scope, not merely because a narrower opinion is preferable.

### R3. First Amendment association

The First Amendment supplies no categorical tenant-knowledge condition merely because the statute refers to household members or guests and may create economic pressure concerning them. A claimant must identify protected expressive association and a material burden attributable to the challenged governmental application; the supplied record establishes no particular expressive activity, message or expressive relationship impaired by the no-knowledge clause. This is an evidentiary limit, not a finding that these tenants engage in no protected expression. The rule addresses specified criminal conduct and occupancy relationships, not an identified viewpoint, compelled message or membership in a political organization.

Hurley protects an actual private expressive presentation from compelled alteration. Dale requires actual expressive impairment in its access-law setting and preserves direct speech, genuine message-bearing roles and intimate association; its organizational message-role formulation is not mechanically imposed as the exclusive test for all tenants. Lyng distinguishes an indirect economic effect of a benefit condition from a substantial constitutional burden and expressly recognizes that subtle interference can be actionable. No general benefits exception to the First Amendment follows. The strongest contrary path emphasizes pressure to expel a guest and the possibility of indirect retaliation; those matters could support an actual claim but do not establish the missing expressive predicate or turn every guest relationship into protected expression. Thus this categorical claim does not independently sustain the injunction. A properly presented expression-targeted application is not adjudicated away.

### R4. Fourteenth Amendment intimate association

Protected family and other genuinely intimate relationships require a distinct inquiry; their protection does not depend upon proof of an expressive message. The statute's authorization of termination for specified criminal activity, without tenant knowledge, does not by itself directly and substantially forbid a protected living arrangement or require relatives to separate. On the categorical theory supplied, protecting safe, drug-free housing and the other tenants' enjoyment is a legitimate objective, and the specified household, guest and control relationships rationally connect the authorized condition to it. Automatic strict scrutiny is not triggered by the personal-knowledge omission alone. The optional permission to continue occupancy without a responsible family member is not a statutory command to dissolve a family.

That conclusion does not treat displacement, homelessness or pressure on a family as nonexistent. The record does not establish the kind, strength or precise burden of each asserted intimate relationship, a compulsory severance, or a particular application whose direct and substantial effect requires further constitutional justification. Troxel protects actual parental direction against displacement merely by a judge's preferred arrangement; it supplies no majority-adopted universal strict-scrutiny or harm test for every governmental effect on a family. Shahar protects an actual enduring intimate relationship even outside civil marriage and rejects reliance on labels instead of the real governmental interest. Neither decision creates an unconditional entitlement to this dwelling irrespective of covered criminal activity. The strongest tenant path analogizes an actual expulsion-or-separation choice to a direct family restriction; that path is preserved for supported application, without assuming its predicates or adopting a universal no-fault defense now.

### R5. Excessive Fines

The statute's permission to terminate a conditional right to occupy government housing is not itself a punitive payment to the sovereign. The categorical attack supplies no sufficient basis to equate every such termination with confiscation of tenant-owned property as punishment. Government recovery of possession under a lawful occupancy condition and prospective cessation of assistance have a different operation from the statutory drug-property forfeitures in Austin or the criminal currency exaction in Bajakajian. The no-knowledge feature and the possibility of economic loss alone do not establish a fine. This ground rejects an alternative categorical basis for the injunction; it does not hold that every measure called an eviction necessarily falls outside the Clause.

Austin forecloses the argument that civil form, a remedial purpose or a noncash transfer alone defeats coverage. A leasehold can be a valuable legal interest; describing the building as government-owned does not establish that a particular punitive taking of a tenant's interest is impossible. A supported claim must identify the interest actually taken and how the challenged operation extracts it at least partly as punishment. A regulatory label or the mere fact that the loss is valuable does not answer that inquiry in either direction. No such particular punitive extraction is established by the categorical statutory theory and supplied application findings. That is not a final adverse factual finding about every tenant's interest or actual termination.

If an actual application establishes a covered punitive forfeiture, lawful statutory authority and a nexus to an offense do not end the separate constitutional excessiveness inquiry. Bajakajian's gross-disproportionality rule requires the actual offense, gravity and exaction to be identified; the Court adopts no new ratio, exclusive instrumentality rule, universal factor checklist, fixed leasehold value or remedy. The record supplies no adjudicated values or proportionality findings that could decide an actual punitive forfeiture. Ursery, Kurth Ranch and Hudson address distinct Double Jeopardy classifications and do not impose their clearest-proof or accounting tests as the fines threshold. The Court can reject the categorical injunction ground without resolving state incorporation; incorporation remains expressly undecided, neither assumed settled nor rejected. No alternative holding approves an otherwise established excessive fine.

### R6. Remedy and remaining proceedings

The lower courts must apply the statutory holding to the actual lease, alleged act, actor and relationship; assess any properly preserved individual constitutional claim under its own predicates; and apply the ordinary preliminary-relief requirements to any remaining request. The older lease and 1997 regulation's on-or-near-premises limitation is not silently replaced by the later statutory on-or-off-premises language. A visitor's status must satisfy the applicable definition, including the regulation's consent-of-a-household-member element for a guest. Facts supporting a legal control relationship are distinct from constant ability to prevent all conduct.

Section 966.4(l)(5) gives discretion to consider all circumstances, including seriousness of the offense, family members' extent of participation and eviction's effects on family members not involved. In appropriate cases it permits continued occupancy by remaining family members on a condition excluding family members who engaged in the proscribed activity. A family member who engaged in illegal drug use may be required to show successful completion of treatment as a condition of residing in the unit. Those options are not mandatory mitigation, automatic severance or a universal reasonable-prevention defense. Required notice must state the grounds and applicable response, document-access and hearing rights. When a grievance hearing must be afforded, tenancy cannot terminate before the request period expires or, if timely requested, before completion of that process. An agency relying on an authorized grievance exclusion must establish its application and decision to exclude the grievance; its notice must identify the judicial eviction procedure, HUD's determination that the procedure provides basic due process, the absence of a grievance entitlement and the applicable criminal-activity basis. No such exclusion is presumed from the allegation alone. Eviction through an administrative action without a court judgment requires authorization by local law, a due-process administrative hearing and the opportunity for the required pre-eviction grievance hearing. Under section 966.4(m), the tenant has a reasonable pre-hearing or pretrial opportunity, on request, to examine agency-held documents directly relevant to termination or eviction and copy them at the tenant's expense; failure to make those requested documents available bars proceeding with eviction. A preserved defense cannot be denied merely because mitigation is discretionary, and a claim's factual predicate cannot be inferred merely because eviction is severe.

The remand neither directs an additional evidentiary hearing in every case nor treats incomplete source retrieval as litigant default. It permits resolution of properly presented remaining issues on the appropriate actual record. The federal injunction cannot remain on the rejected categorical grounds; any renewed or retained protection needs its own supported legal and preliminary-relief basis. No tenant is adjudged removable and no state judgment is automatically entered or vacated by this handoff.

## Complete R6 qualification

Under the 1997 section 966.51(a)(1), the grievance procedure ordinarily applies to an individual tenant's dispute with the public housing agency about action or inaction contrary to the tenant's lease or agency regulations that adversely affects the tenant's rights, duties, welfare or status.

The section 966.51(a)(2) exception requires a HUD determination that the jurisdiction's law requires an opportunity for a court hearing with the defined basic due-process safeguards before eviction. Once that determination exists, the agency may exclude an individual termination or eviction grievance involving either:

- Criminal activity threatening other residents' or agency employees' health, safety or right to peaceful enjoyment of the premises; or
- Drug-related criminal activity on or near the premises.

This is permission to exclude, not mandatory exclusion. The agency must actually elect the permissible exclusion. The first category includes agency employees, unlike the separately worded statutory termination ground; the second retains the 1997 on-or-near limitation. Neither is silently replaced by the later statute's drug-activity coverage on or off the premises. The judicial eviction procedure used must be one covered by HUD's determination. The exception dispenses with the agency grievance hearing through that qualifying court route; it does not dispense with the court opportunity itself.

Section 966.53(c)'s basic safeguards are adequate notice of the grounds for termination and eviction; the tenant's right to representation by counsel; an opportunity to refute the agency's evidence, including confrontation and cross-examination of witnesses and presentation of affirmative legal or equitable defenses; and a decision on the merits. Representation by counsel is not rewritten as a guarantee of appointed counsel. HUD need not use notice-and-comment rulemaking for its determination, but must publish the list of covered judicial procedures and make the supporting legal analysis available for public inspection and copying.

The separate notice and route requirements remain operative. Under the 1997 section 966.4(l)(3), notice is 14 days for nonpayment, a reasonable period considering seriousness not exceeding 30 days for threats to other residents' or agency employees' health or safety, and 30 days otherwise. Notice identifies specific grounds, reply rights, relevant-document access and any required grievance-request opportunity. A legally required notice to vacate may be combined with or run concurrently with the lease-termination notice. Where a grievance opportunity is required, termination must await expiration of the request period and, upon a timely request, completion of the grievance process, even if a state or local notice to vacate has expired. Where the agency permissibly elects exclusion, notice must state the absence of a grievance entitlement, identify the qualifying judicial procedure and HUD determination, and identify which of the two criminal-activity categories applies. These 1997 regulatory terms do not merge with the separately supplied statutory notice provisions; the actual legally applicable requirements must be satisfied.

Administrative eviction without a court action remains a different route: the jurisdiction must authorize it, a due-process administrative hearing is required, and the agency must afford the pre-eviction grievance opportunity. The judicial-procedure exclusion cannot remove those administrative-route safeguards. Section 966.4(m) still requires a reasonable opportunity, on request before the hearing or trial, to examine agency-held documents directly relevant to termination or eviction and copy them at the tenant's expense. If the agency fails to make those documents available as required, it may not proceed with eviction.

Section 966.51(b) separately excludes disputes between tenants that do not involve the agency and class grievances from the agency procedure. Its statement that the procedure is not a forum for initiating or negotiating group policy changes creates no jurisdictional bar to this federal suit and determines no individual defense.

## Holdings

### I. Statutory authority and the actual lease

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1437d(l)(6) requires a lease ground permitting termination for qualifying activity by the tenant, a household member, a guest or another person under the tenant's control, without an additional actual-or-constructive-knowledge requirement. Inclusion of that ground is mandatory; termination in an eligible case remains discretionary and requires the actual activity, relationship and lease predicates.

The threatening-criminal-activity branch concerns other tenants' health, safety or peaceful enjoyment. The separate drug-related branch covers activity on or off the premises without importing the first branch's threat requirement. That later statutory wording does not replace an older lease's on-or-near limitation. Under the applicable 1997 guest definition, the person must be in the leased unit with a household member's consent. Control does not require constant ability to prevent conduct; a stranger's remote association does not establish a covered relationship. Drug-related activity means illegal manufacture, sale, distribution, use, or possession with intent to manufacture, sell, distribute or use a controlled substance.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all eight participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress specified the actors, conduct and consequence without adding a tenant mental state. MCI and Christensen require respect for the enacted boundary rather than administrative or judicial amendment. Household membership and guest access supply a tenancy connection without imputing a personal crime to the tenant. The general prohibitions on unreasonable terms and termination without serious or repeated violation or other good cause remain operative, but cannot erase the specific ground. The tenants' strongest reading combines those provisions, control, housing purposes and constitutional avoidance into a knowledge condition. That reading rewrites the particular clause; ignorance also does not establish every constitutional predicate. Conversely, a mandatory lease clause does not make every permitted termination compulsory. The allegations here must still satisfy the actual lease, including its geographical terms, and the applicable definitions. This independent construction resolves the statutory question in both dockets without treating agency discretion as authority beyond the statute.

### II. Due process and the defined Bennis protection

**Controlling proposition:** Permission to terminate a conditioned public tenancy for covered third-party conduct is not facially invalid merely because the tenant lacks personal knowledge or criminal fault. Protected tenancy interests, required process and independently established substantive defenses remain enforceable; civil form, lease assent and optional leniency do not defeat them.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all eight participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** A prospective household condition protecting other residents differs from personally convicting the tenant. Bennis nevertheless prevents statutory permission or agency grace from answering every property claim. It protects a separately owned interest in an ordinarily lawful automobile when the claimant proves actual ownership, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention. Shared lawful use does not defeat that defense; omniscience, constant monitoring and prevention at any cost are not required. Culpable entrustment, known misuse and omitted reasonable precautions remain outside it. The claimant receives a meaningful opportunity to prove the ordinary burden, without a heightened quantum; established protection requires preservation or restoration of the interest. Otherwise valid forfeiture does not automatically require compensation. The injunction's ignorance condition does not establish those predicates, and Bennis did not adjudicate a continuing public tenancy. That difference defeats a universal innocence immunity here. But a landlord label cannot foreclose a supported analogy before the actual lease interest, prevention facts and deprivation are established. The individual analogical question remains open because this preliminary record does not supply those necessary facts, not because optional mitigation substitutes for constitutional protection. Required notice and a meaningful opportunity to contest the agency's case remain distinct from the existence of a substantive defense.

### III. First Amendment association

**Controlling proposition:** Household or guest status and resulting economic pressure alone do not establish a First Amendment tenant-knowledge condition. An association claim requires protected expressive activity or relationship and a material governmental burden upon it; no particular expressive predicate is established by this categorical challenge.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment rejecting this independent injunction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The clause addresses specified criminal conduct and occupancy relationships, not an identified viewpoint, compelled message or political membership. Hurley protects an actual expressive presentation against compelled alteration. Dale requires actual expressive impairment in its access-law setting and preserves direct speech, genuine message-bearing roles and intimate association; its organizational formulation is not an exclusive test for every tenant. Nor must expression always take the form of spoken advocacy. Lyng distinguishes an indirect economic consequence of a household benefit condition from a substantial constitutional burden while recognizing that subtle interference can be actionable. The tenants' strongest argument is practical pressure to expel associates, potentially concealing retaliation. That can matter in an actual expression-targeted application, but does not supply the missing expressive connection here. The Court neither finds that these tenants have no expression nor creates a benefits exception to the First Amendment. A properly supported expressive application remains subject to its own constitutional inquiry.

### IV. Intimate association

**Controlling proposition:** Family and genuinely intimate relationships receive protection independently of any expressive message. The statutory omission of tenant knowledge does not itself establish direct and substantial interference with a protected living arrangement or trigger strict scrutiny; the specified relationships rationally connect this categorical housing condition to the legitimate protection of safe, drug-free housing and other tenants' enjoyment.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by the same seven Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment rejecting this independent injunction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute authorizes termination for specified conduct; it does not command relatives to separate. Optional continued occupancy excluding a responsible member is not compulsory family dissolution. The strongest contrary analogy concerns an actual choice between shelter and severance of a protected relationship. The record does not establish each relationship's kind and strength, compulsory severance or a particular direct burden requiring further justification. Displacement and family pressure are not therefore deemed nonexistent. Troxel protects actual parental direction from replacement by a judge's preferred arrangement, but supplies no majority rule demanding universal strict scrutiny or a harm showing for every governmental family effect. Shahar protects enduring intimacy beyond civil marriage and requires the actual governmental interest rather than a label. Neither establishes unconditional entitlement to this dwelling despite covered misconduct. This ruling does not dilute the standard applicable when infringement of an established fundamental parental right is actually shown; Thomas's agreement rests on the missing infringement predicate, not rejection of his scrutiny position. A properly demonstrated direct and substantial burden must receive its own constitutional justification.

### V. Excessive Fines

**Controlling proposition:** Statutory permission to end a conditional public tenancy does not itself establish a punitive extraction, and tenant ignorance or economic loss alone does not make every termination a fine. A supported application must identify the legal interest actually taken and an operation extracting it at least partly as punishment; if that threshold is established, excessiveness requires a separate gross-disproportionality inquiry.

**Authority:** Part V, joined by the same seven Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment rejecting this independent injunction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Recovery of possession under a lawful occupancy condition and prospective cessation of assistance differ from Austin's drug-property forfeitures and Bajakajian's criminal currency exaction. The strongest tenant argument nevertheless identifies a valuable leasehold deliberately forfeited to punish another's conduct. Austin makes civil form, remedial purpose and noncash form insufficient answers. Government ownership of the building does not prove that a punitive taking of a tenant's interest is impossible. The present categorical authorization and supplied application findings establish no particular punitive extraction; that is not an adverse factual judgment about every tenant's interest. If one is established, lawful authority and an offense nexus do not settle excessiveness. Bajakajian requires identification of the actual offense, gravity and exaction under its gross-disproportionality rule. No adjudicated value or proportionality findings support deciding an actual forfeiture here. Ursery, Kurth Ranch and Hudson concern distinct Double Jeopardy classifications; their clearest-proof or accounting approaches do not become the fines threshold. The Court adopts no new ratio, exclusive instrumentality test, universal factor list, fixed leasehold value or remedial formula. State incorporation remains expressly undecided because this categorical claim fails without resolving it. No alternative holding approves an otherwise established excessive fine.

### VI. Remedy, discretion and operative process

**Controlling proposition:** Reversal requires removal of the preliminary injunction insofar as it rests on the rejected categorical grounds and remand for actual lease application and properly presented individual claims under their own predicates. Any retained or renewed preliminary relief must satisfy the ordinary requirements on a supported independent ground; actual eviction proceeds only through an authorized forum with applicable proof and process.

**Authority:** Part VI, joined by all eight participating Justices. The following procedural and discretionary terms qualify the remand; none is a finding that an individual predicate has been satisfied.

**Operative qualifications:** The 1997 regulations preserve these distinct requirements:

- **Discretion:** Section 966.4(l)(5) permits consideration of all circumstances, including seriousness, family participation and effects on uninvolved family members. In appropriate cases remaining members may continue occupancy subject to exclusion of members who engaged in the activity. A member who used illegal drugs may be required to demonstrate successful treatment as a residence condition. These are options, not compulsory mitigation, mandatory severance or a universal prevention defense.
- **Ordinary grievance coverage:** Sections 966.51(a)(1) and 966.53(a) cover individual tenant disputes about agency action or inaction contrary to the lease or agency regulations that adversely affects rights, duties, welfare or status. Section 966.51(b) separately excludes tenant disputes not involving the agency and class grievances; the procedure is not a forum for initiating or negotiating group policy changes. That agency-procedure limit does not bar this federal suit or adjudicate an individual defense.
- **Conditional judicial exception:** Section 966.51(a)(2) permits an agency actually to elect exclusion of an individual termination or eviction grievance only where HUD has determined that the jurisdiction requires a pre-eviction court opportunity with the defined safeguards. The grievance must involve criminal activity threatening other residents' or agency employees' health, safety or peaceful enjoyment, or drug-related criminal activity on or near the premises. Employees are included in this exception's first category; the second retains the older geographical limit. The actual court procedure must be covered by HUD's determination. The exception removes the agency grievance hearing, not the qualifying court opportunity. HUD need not use notice-and-comment procedures for its determination but must publish the list of covered procedures and make its supporting legal analysis available for public inspection and copying.
- **Court safeguards:** Section 966.53(c) requires adequate notice of termination and eviction grounds, the right to representation by counsel, opportunity to refute agency evidence through confrontation and cross-examination and to present affirmative legal or equitable defenses, and a merits decision. The counsel right is not a guarantee of appointed counsel.
- **1997 notice requirements:** Section 966.4(l)(3) requires written notice of fourteen days for nonpayment, a reasonable period considering seriousness not exceeding thirty days for threats to other residents' or agency employees' health or safety, and thirty days otherwise. It must state specific grounds, reply rights, relevant-document access and any required grievance-request right. A legally required notice to vacate may be combined with or run concurrently with that notice. Where grievance opportunity is required, termination awaits expiration of the request period and, on timely request, completion of the process even if the local notice to vacate has expired. An elected lawful exclusion requires notice of the absence of a grievance entitlement, the chosen qualifying court procedure and HUD determination, and which precise criminal-activity category applies.
- **Separate statutory notice:** Section 1437d(l)(4) requires adequate written notice no shorter than the applicable period: a reasonable period not exceeding thirty days when other tenants, agency employees or persons in the immediate vicinity face a health or safety threat, or for drug-related or violent criminal activity or a felony conviction; fourteen days for nonpayment; and thirty days otherwise, except a shorter state or local period where that last category permits it. These statutory terms do not silently replace or merge with the 1997 regulatory requirements. Section 1437d(l)(7)'s notice of pre-hearing or pretrial access to directly related documents, records and regulations also remains operative notwithstanding state law.
- **Administrative route and documents:** Eviction without court action under section 966.4(l)(4) requires local authorization, a due-process administrative hearing and the opportunity for a pre-eviction agency grievance hearing. The judicial exception cannot erase those safeguards. Section 966.4(m) requires a reasonable opportunity, upon the tenant's request before the hearing or trial, to inspect directly relevant documents held by the agency and copy them at the tenant's expense. Failure to provide that required access bars proceeding with eviction.

**Controlling explanation:** Statutory cause, discretionary choice and lawful implementation do different work. The contrary automatic-eviction position would erase the actual lease, the enumerated procedural conditions and the individual defenses preserved by Parts II–V. Conversely, optional mitigation cannot become a required hearing in every case or defeat an independently established constitutional protection. The actual actor, activity, relationship, older lease geography and procedural route must be established. No HUD determination, agency election, notice compliance or grievance-request timing is inferred from the drug allegation alone. Ordinary adjudication may resolve remaining issues on the appropriate actual record without a compulsory new evidentiary hearing in every case. The tenants' ADA and state-law claims are outside this review. The individual constitutional reservations rest on missing application predicates, while application of the settled statutory and procedural rules is ordinary remand work.

## Precedent Treatment

- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co. is applied to preserve the enacted boundary of agency authority; it authorizes neither an added knowledge requirement nor authority beyond the actual statute.
- Christensen v. Harris County is applied to distinguish statutory or regulatory permission from an extra compulsory condition.
- Bennis v. Michigan remains binding in its defined innocent-interest category, including reasonable-prevention proof and required protection. A conditioned public tenancy is distinguished; a supported individual analogy remains open on the missing lease-interest and prevention facts.
- Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston is applied to actual expressive presentation and compelled alteration, not to guest status alone.
- Boy Scouts of America v. Dale is applied for actual expressive impairment and its preserved direct-speech, genuine message-role and intimate-association protections. Its organizational formulation and noncontrolling stronger advocacy requirement do not become universal tenant tests.
- Lyng v. International Union, UAW is applied to distinguish indirect household economic consequences from a substantial protected burden while preserving its recognition of actionable subtle interference.
- Troxel v. Granville preserves actual parental choice; its fractured grounds do not establish universal strict scrutiny or a harm test for every family effect.
- Shahar v. Bowers is applied to actual enduring intimate relationships beyond civil marriage and the real governmental interest; it supplies no unconditional right to this tenancy.
- Austin v. United States preserves coverage of at least partly punitive property extraction despite civil form or remedial purpose. It does not make every loss of occupancy a fine.
- United States v. Bajakajian preserves the distinct punitive-extraction threshold and gross-disproportionality inquiry; no exclusive instrumentality rule or new valuation formula is adopted.
- United States v. Ursery, Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch and Hudson v. United States concern different Double Jeopardy classifications. Their tests do not displace Austin's separate fines inquiry.

## Law After Decision

Public-housing leases must permit termination for the statute's defined third-party conduct without a universal tenant-knowledge condition. Actual enforcement remains discretionary within the governing statute, actual lease and operative process. Ignorance alone supplies none of the four categorical constitutional objections rejected here. Due process, expressive and intimate association, and fines protections remain enforceable when their distinct application predicates are established. Bennis's defined property protection and Austin/Bajakajian's separate coverage and proportionality rules retain their force. The remedial ruling removes the invalid categorical injunction grounds while preserving supported individual proceedings; it creates no general innocence immunity or blanket constitutional approval of every eviction.
