## Event, chronology, and review scope

The Court considers the filed direct appeal under 28 U.S.C. § 1253 on the papers, without oral argument or plenary review. The scheduled event and congressional-only scope are established event premises. No additional docket, grant, filing, or submission condition attaches to this disposition.

The three-judge Eastern District of Texas court adopted Plan 1151C for thirty-two congressional seats on November 14, 2001, and directed final judgment under Rule 54(b). It had received evidence October 22–November 1 and heard argument November 2. Texas had gained two congressional seats, and neither legislation nor completed state proceedings supplied a timely operative replacement for the malapportioned thirty-seat plan. The jurisdictional statement was filed February 12, 2002, the appeal was docketed February 19, motions to affirm were filed March 21, and replies followed April 2.

The appeal challenges rejection of the proposed additional Latino-opportunity arrangement, including the treatment of registered-voter majorities, electoral performance, comparative geography, and the adequacy of the findings. Appellants seek correction of the congressional replacement arrangement for asserted dilution. Their contention that Latino registered-voter majorities imply citizen-voting-age majorities is not an accepted factual premise. Their performance evidence and objection to the findings are actually presented; the appeal is not reduced to demographic arithmetic or treated as an established sub-fifty-percent case identical to Valdespino.

The lower opinion links its rejection to a majority requirement while also identifying compactness and totality deficiencies. The question resolved is whether those explanations identify independently sufficient lawful grounds under the governing capacity rule. The Court finds that they do not sufficiently disclose the disposition of the actual disputed proof under that rule. Its remedy reaches only the rejection of the appealed congressional arrangement and the districts it concerns. The November 28 Texas House and Senate judgments and their separate appeals are outside this event.

## Controlling holdings

### Capacity and independently sufficient grounds

**Holding and operative rule:** A preserved Section 2 district-formation claim by one protected group may satisfy the first Gingles condition in a lawful, geographically compact district either through a numerical majority or rigorous proof of dependable crossover support sufficient to elect the group's preferred candidates. Numerical exclusivity cannot defeat that supported alternative, but cohesion, majority bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat the group's preferred candidates absent special circumstances such as an unopposed minority candidate, causation, and unequal opportunity under an independent totality inquiry remain necessary; an erroneous numerical screen warrants relief only when material to the disposition, and a genuinely independent sufficient lawful ground remains effective.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer. These five directly adopt the complete rule, its application, and the limited mandate. The Court applies Valdespino's existing rule without changing its scope or any statutory burden.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 2 protects actual electoral opportunity and operates on its own statutory elements. Valdespino permits one protected group to prove dependable ability to elect through support from other voters; population arithmetic, an isolated success, or influence alone does not establish that capacity. Gingles and Growe supply the remaining causal conditions, with attention to special circumstances in the voting evidence. De Grandy requires a distinct totality inquiry and rejects both maximization and a conclusive proportionality defense. Voinovich limits judicially compelled districts to relief for a proved violation. A timely lawful state plan has priority under Growe, but necessary federal replacement does not exempt the resulting map from Section 2.

The congressional appeal presents electoral-performance evidence, comparative geography, and an objection to the adequacy of the findings. The lower opinion links its rejection to a majority requirement without sufficiently identifying an independent resolution of that disputed material under the governing rule. Appellees identify substantial contrary evidence about marginal districts, turnout, possible loss of existing opportunity, and geographic corridors. That evidence could support a lawful judgment; their appellate explanation cannot substitute for sufficiently identifiable findings by the trial court. Rule 52(a) does not require discussion of every exhibit, but the explanation must permit review under the correct legal standard.

The selected electoral results do not conclusively establish dependable capacity, and geometric scores do not determine legal compactness. Testimony about Plan 1099C cannot establish the same facts about Plan 1123C without support. Nor does the separate finding against common cohesion of distinct minority groups resolve one group's dependable outside support. The limited remand requires the lower court to resolve the appealed claim under the existing inquiry and explain any independent sufficient ground, preserving supported findings and the plaintiffs' burden.

**Material limits:** The Court determines the adequacy of the stated grounds for appellate review, not whether the disputed proof establishes the statutory elements. Ordinary procedures govern supplementation; the remand does not require a new trial. Distinct-group coalition entitlement, influence-only entitlement, and a universal population or statistical formula lie outside the single-group question presented. The separate legislative judgments lie outside the appeal.

### Remedy for the deficient explanation

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the majority-linked explanation does not sufficiently identify a lawful ground independently disposing of the appealed additional Latino-opportunity arrangement, the congressional judgment is vacated only insofar as it rejects that arrangement. The remand requires application of both capacity routes and the remaining statutory conditions with sufficiently identified findings, while Plan 1151C remains operative unless lawfully modified and any 2002 election already conducted under Plan 1151C is left undisturbed.

**Authority:** The same five Justices join the Court's application and mandate. This is a direct majority holding, not an inference from agreement on a result.

**Controlling explanation:** Valdespino distinguishes a material error in the capacity rule from independent findings and procedural rulings that remain valid. The defect here requires a reviewable application to the actual congressional claim, not automatic rejection of every finding made after trial. Growe preserves priority for timely lawful state action and permits necessary federal replacement; Voinovich permits a federal court to compel districts only as relief for an established violation. Those principles leave the operative replacement map in force during the bounded remand and require any further remedy to rest on the violation proved and lawful election administration. The remand is confined to the appealed arrangement and the districts it concerns, preserves supported findings, and allows supplementation only through ordinary procedure. It mandates neither an additional district nor a general reopening of the trial; the separate House and Senate judgments are unaffected.

- Growe v. Emison, February 23, 1993: applied for the districting causal framework and priority for timely lawful state action. Its distinct-group reservation and necessary federal replacement authority remain intact.
- Voinovich v. Quilter, March 2, 1993: applied to distinguish a State's permissible choices from court-compelled relief for a proved violation. Judicial authorship supplies no exemption from Section 2.
- Johnson v. De Grandy, June 30, 1994: applied for independent totality, rejection of maximization, and the absence of a proportionality safe harbor. Local deprivation cannot be excused by aggregate opportunity elsewhere.

## Law After Decision

The single-group capacity rule remains unchanged: dependable functional capacity may satisfy the first condition, while every remaining statutory condition and unequal opportunity must still be established. The Court holds that the stated grounds do not sufficiently disclose an independent lawful disposition of this appealed arrangement. The lower court must apply the settled rule to the disputed evidence and identify its dispositive findings. The decision establishes no new population denominator, crossover formula, compactness measure, or constitutional condition on Section 2. The existing distinct-group and influence-only reservations remain outside the question decided.

## Separate Writings

**O'Connor, dissenting; Kennedy joins Part I only.** Part I regards the trial court's practical-effectiveness and geographic findings as sufficient when read with the record. The competing testimony concerns actual electoral opportunity, including marginality, turnout, the proposed electorate, and the possible loss of effective districts. Registration majorities do not answer those questions, and a geometric comparison does not by itself establish lawful compactness. Rule 52(a) requires an intelligible factual basis, not discussion of every exhibit. Although the findings are brief, they permit review and sustain the judgment. This conclusion requires neither a numerical safe harbor nor rejection of Valdespino. Voinovich supplies the distinction between state districting choices and federally compelled districts; De Grandy supplies the need for proof beyond the necessary conditions.

In Part II, O'Connor also accepts the lower court's contextual totality determination as a sufficient ground for rejecting the requested additional district. That is an assessment of the actual opportunity shown, not a conclusive defense based on statewide proportionality. Kennedy does not join this further account; his agreement is confined to the independent practical-effectiveness and geographic grounds in Part I.

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** Consistent with their positions in Holder v. Hall and De Grandy, they conclude that Section 2 does not authorize the asserted judicial allocation of districts to remedy this form of vote dilution. That antecedent statutory ground is sufficient to affirm. They accept the court's authority to replace an unconstitutionally malapportioned plan, but they do not join either capacity route or a finding that these plaintiffs failed the governing dilution framework. The actual electoral-performance evidence does not answer their statutory objection.

## Procedure After Action

The direct congressional appeal concludes with partial vacatur and remand to the three-judge Eastern District of Texas court. That court must apply the governing capacity and remaining statutory requirements to the appealed additional arrangement, preserve supported findings, and identify any sufficient independent lawful ground. Ordinary procedural rules govern permissible supplementation. Plan 1151C remains operative unless lawfully modified, and any 2002 election already conducted under Plan 1151C is left undisturbed. The mandate reaches no separate House or Senate judgment and requires neither an additional district nor a general reopening of the trial.
