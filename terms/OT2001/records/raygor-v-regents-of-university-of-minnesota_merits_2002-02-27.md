### Issue 1: Supplemental tolling and a State’s immunity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The state limitations judgment presents an independent federal question. The Court may resolve tolling while preserving the finality of the earlier federal case.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress’s reference to a claim asserted under subsection (a) includes a related claim dismissed because the State did not consent to its adjudication in federal court.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Kimel to reject the historical jurisdictional premise; propose a construction of §1367(d) that does not equate tolling with abrogation.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** Section 1367(d) preserves a related state-law claim timely asserted in a federal action supported by original jurisdiction even when sovereign immunity requires dismissal of that supplemental claim. The provision does not itself authorize a federal merits judgment against the State; it preserves the time for a state action that the State otherwise permits.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the tolling of properly related claims and the specified postdismissal interval. It creates no new state cause of action, waiver of a substantive defense, or route around the State’s independent immunity in an otherwise unauthorized forum.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The enacted term concerns assertion of a claim and the protection against losing it while the federal tribunal determines its authority. Dismissal on immunity grounds does not mean that the related claim was never asserted. Congress’s Article III implementing authority, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, permits a reasonable procedural protection against forfeiture caused by presenting related claims in an authorized federal action. The State’s distinct power to insist on its own authorized forum survives. The constitutional and statutory clarity rules for a treasury judgment do not answer this different procedural measure. Text and federal procedural structure operate together, with the wording asserted under subsection (a) decisive. Kimel is reaffirmed for the lawful federal ADEA channel; the abandoned federal judgment is not reopened. Tolling preserves time in a state forum the State otherwise permits, while abrogation would authorize a merits judgment against a nonconsenting sovereign. Because these are different legal effects, the clear statement required for treasury liability cannot be imported to defeat the specific tolling instruction. The replacement construction governs that procedural effect and leaves independent state immunity and valid claim defenses intact.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The exact state filing arithmetic and any independent finality defense depend on the record below; no new federal reopening theory is presented.

### Supplemental tolling does not cover this nonconsenting State's immune state-law claims

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1367(d) does not toll the limitations period for a state-law claim against a nonconsenting State that falls outside subsection (a)'s supplemental adjudicative grant because of sovereign immunity. A valid federal claim within original jurisdiction does not itself authorize the separate state-law claim or bring that immune claim within the linked tolling provision.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion is joined throughout by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Those five direct joins control the statutory rule and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory reference to subsection (a) limits which asserted claims receive subsection (d)'s protection. Pennhurst preserves a State's immunity from federal adjudication of its state-law duties without consent. Vermont Agency distinguishes constitutional power from affirmative statutory authorization to proceed against a sovereign. Subsection (a)'s general supplemental grant contains no unmistakable authorization for this nonconsenting State-defendant claim, and subsection (d) supplies no independent affirmative indication that Congress included it despite that exclusion.

The employees correctly distinguish tolling in a competent state forum from a federal merits judgment. But that difference does not eliminate the cross-reference Congress enacted. The word asserted does not make every related filing a claim selected by subsection (a). The ordinary protection lasts while a covered claim is pending and for thirty days after dismissal, unless state law provides longer tolling; those periods do not enlarge the class covered. The additional provision for other same-action claims voluntarily dismissed at or after dismissal of a covered claim does not govern these involuntary dismissals.

Kimel authorizes the federal ADEA action. Schacht accordingly prevents immunity on the supplemental claim from erasing the independent federal jurisdiction. Neither decision supplies consent to adjudicate the state claim. The Regents asserted immunity in their answers, and subsequent discovery, mediation and scheduling did not relinquish that express defense. College Savings Bank likewise rejects consent inferred merely from ordinary activity.

This statutory ground sustains the state limitations judgment without treating the federal ADEA claim as unauthorized or reopening its final dismissal. The Court need not decide Congress's constitutional power to enact a differently specified tolling rule: statutory coverage resolves the presented claim. The holding supplies no general rule excluding every dismissal outside subsection (c), and no general presumption against federal laws affecting States.

The controlling question is whether section 1367(d)'s protection for a claim asserted under subsection (a) includes a supplemental state-law claim against a nonconsenting State dismissed on immunity grounds. An independently authorized federal ADEA claim, immunity from federal adjudication of a state-law claim, and a limitations rule for a competent state court are distinct.

The operative register provisions are **Express federal ADEA actions against state employers**, effective January 11, 2000 (Kimel; general Article I operation June 23, 1999), and **Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim**, effective June 22, 1998 (Schacht). Kimel supplies the authorized federal claim; Schacht prevents immunity on one claim from erasing the entire action's independent federal jurisdiction. Florida Prepaid requires power and unmistakable authorization separately. College Savings Bank supplies no constructive consent from ordinary activity. Alden distinguishes review of a new state judgment from reopening a terminated federal proceeding.

Subsection (a) requires an action within original jurisdiction and a same-case-or-controversy relationship. Subsection (b)'s diversity-only limitations do not govern this ADEA action. Subsection (c) permits declining jurisdiction for a novel or complex state-law issue, substantial predominance, dismissal of all original-jurisdiction claims, or exceptional circumstances with other compelling reasons; immunity is not converted into a discretionary subsection (c) ground. Subsection (d) protects the relevant claim while pending and for thirty days after dismissal unless state law provides longer tolling; its separate category of other voluntarily dismissed same-action claims is not the basis of these involuntary dismissals.

### Two lawful paths and strongest objections

**R1 — exclusion and affirmance.** Read subsection (d)'s cross-reference as preserving claims eligible for subsection (a)'s supplemental adjudicative grant, rather than independently extending a State's exposure through claims that grant does not unmistakably authorize against it. Generic all-other-claims language does not overcome the sovereign-defendant restriction. The strongest objection is that asserted describes filing, not adjudicability, and state-forum tolling neither orders federal adjudication nor creates a new cause. The R1 answer is limited: Congress linked this protection to subsection (a), and the state-law claim's immunity remains intact despite the federal claim's valid jurisdiction. Neither a general Article I prohibition nor the false proposition that the whole action lacked federal jurisdiction is necessary.

**R2 — coverage and reversal/remand.** Read asserted under subsection (a) to encompass the related state claim actually brought alongside a competent federal claim, even though immunity ultimately prevents its adjudication. The enacted protection preserves a competent state forum; it does not abrogate federal-forum immunity. The strongest objection is that a claimant cannot obtain a statutory consequence merely by asserting an unauthorized State-defendant claim, and state limitations can define the State's consent. The R2 answer distinguishes newly authorized adjudication or damages from a procedural consequence attached to a related claim in an independently proper federal case. Congress expressly specified pending-period and postdismissal protection without an immunity-dismissal exclusion. No State consent to federal adjudication is inferred.

**Bounded correction path.** Vacatur solely to correct the mistaken whole-action ADEA premise is lawful if that classification is indispensable to the state judgment. It is not necessary for a Justice who resolves the preserved state-claim coverage question independently under R1 or R2. No modeled Justice remands merely to reconsider a proposition the present statutory ruling resolves.

## Holdings

### Supplemental tolling does not cover this nonconsenting State's immune state-law claims

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1367(d) does not toll the limitations period for a state-law claim against a nonconsenting State that falls outside subsection (a)'s supplemental adjudicative grant because of sovereign immunity. A valid federal claim within original jurisdiction does not itself authorize the separate state-law claim or bring that immune claim within the linked tolling provision.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion is joined throughout by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Those five direct joins control the statutory rule and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory reference to subsection (a) limits which asserted claims receive subsection (d)'s protection. Pennhurst preserves a State's immunity from federal adjudication of its state-law duties without consent. Vermont Agency distinguishes constitutional power from affirmative statutory authorization to proceed against a sovereign. Subsection (a)'s general supplemental grant contains no unmistakable authorization for this nonconsenting State-defendant claim, and subsection (d) supplies no independent affirmative indication that Congress included it despite that exclusion.

The employees correctly distinguish tolling in a competent state forum from a federal merits judgment. But that difference does not eliminate the cross-reference Congress enacted. The word asserted does not make every related filing a claim selected by subsection (a). The ordinary protection lasts while a covered claim is pending and for thirty days after dismissal, unless state law provides longer tolling; those periods do not enlarge the class covered. The additional provision for other same-action claims voluntarily dismissed at or after dismissal of a covered claim does not govern these involuntary dismissals.

Kimel authorizes the federal ADEA action. Schacht accordingly prevents immunity on the supplemental claim from erasing the independent federal jurisdiction. Neither decision supplies consent to adjudicate the state claim. The Regents asserted immunity in their answers, and subsequent discovery, mediation and scheduling did not relinquish that express defense. College Savings Bank likewise rejects consent inferred merely from ordinary activity.

This statutory ground sustains the state limitations judgment without treating the federal ADEA claim as unauthorized or reopening its final dismissal. The Court need not decide Congress's constitutional power to enact a differently specified tolling rule: statutory coverage resolves the presented claim. The holding supplies no general rule excluding every dismissal outside subsection (c), and no general presumption against federal laws affecting States.

## Precedent Treatment

Pennhurst State School & Hospital v. Halderman is applied to preserve the State's unwaived immunity from federal adjudication of its state-law duties.

Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens is applied for the independent requirement of statutory sovereign-defendant authorization; it supplies no general constitutional prohibition on procedural rules affecting States.

Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents is reaffirmed: the express federal ADEA action against state employers remains authorized. It does not authorize supplemental state-law adjudication.

Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht is applied to separate the immune claim from the action's valid federal jurisdiction.

College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board is applied to reject inferred consent from participation consistent with an expressly retained immunity defense.

Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank retains its distinction between constitutional power and unmistakable authorization. Today's statutory holding does not narrow its Article I rule.

Alden v. Maine retains its distinction between review of a new state judgment and reopening a terminated federal case. Only the former is before the Court.

Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs concerns equitable tolling in its own statutory setting; it does not independently place these immune supplemental claims within section 1367(d).

## Law After Decision

Section 1367(d)'s linked tolling protection excludes the specified nonconsenting State's immune supplemental state-law claims. The independent federal ADEA channel remains valid, and immunity remains claim-specific. The decision rests on statutory coverage, leaving constitutional power to enact a differently specified tolling rule undecided because that issue is unnecessary to this judgment.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Breyer, dissenting.** Section 1367(d) protects a related claim actually asserted alongside an authorized federal controversy even when immunity prevents a federal merits judgment on that claim. The filing-related word asserted does work distinct from maintainable. The pending-period and thirty-day protection, subject to longer state tolling, preserve an otherwise available state forum rather than create a new cause, impose liability or waive immunity. The subsection (a) reference supplies original-jurisdiction and relatedness predicates; it need not make eventual adjudicability a further condition absent from the dismissal protection. Pennhurst therefore survives this reading. Kimel supplies the valid federal ADEA anchor, while Schacht keeps the supplemental defense claim-specific. Vermont Agency's exclusion from a sovereign-defendant action does not answer this different procedural effect. The dissent would reverse the categorical exclusion, leaving actual filing arithmetic and remaining independent defenses for the state court and leaving the federal judgment closed.

**Stone-Zsela, separately dissenting.** Congress's authority to implement Article III, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, permits this reasonable protection against forfeiture while a federal tribunal determines its authority over related claims. The clear statement needed to authorize a treasury judgment cannot be transferred without analysis to a rule preserving time in a state forum the State otherwise permits. Text and procedural structure support coverage: immunity dismissal does not erase the fact that the claim was asserted with a valid federal controversy. The Chief Justice would apply the enacted pending-period and thirty-day protection, subject to a longer state period, while preserving the State's distinct forum immunity, substantive defenses and any independent finality defense. His position supplies no new state cause of action, no authority to reopen the federal dismissal and no automatic finding that these state filings were timely. His proposed construction and remand do not command a majority.

## Procedure After Action

The state limitations judgment stands, and this merits review concludes. The final federal dismissal remains undisturbed. Ordinary mandate procedure applies.
