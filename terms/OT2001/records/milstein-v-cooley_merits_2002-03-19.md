## Controlling law of the decision

### I. Investigation, personal accusation and public statements

**Holding and operative rule:** Absolute prosecutorial immunity does not protect ordinary investigative creation of evidence, a personal crime report made as the accusing witness or purported victim, detective work on the reported offense, or public press statements. The official's actual function controls; later advocacy does not transform these distinct acts into protected legal preparation, and a personal accusing-witness function does not invariably require an oath.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine adopt the rule and its application to the four functions described here.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that prosecutorial office does not confer one immunity on an entire prosecution. Buckley separates ordinary evidence gathering and press activity from genuine preparation and presentation of the State's case. Fletcher distinguishes supplying evidence personally from presenting it as counsel. These distinctions protect the advocate's role without absorbing every act that assists a prosecution.

Milstein alleges that the prosecutors recruited a witness to recant defense testimony and falsely accuse him. That alleged creation of evidence is investigation, rather than evaluation of existing evidence for advocacy. Their subsequent crime report allegedly made them the complaining witnesses or supposed victims. Its evidentiary function, rather than an invented oath, controls. This does not mean every unsworn charging paper is a witness statement. The further investigation between that report and the grand-jury proceeding likewise allegedly performed a detective's function. Its timing supports the account but supplies no universal boundary at indictment or probable cause. Finally, the challenged public statements perform the press function Buckley places outside absolute immunity.

The prosecutors cannot obtain absolute protection for these acts merely because the alleged evidence later supported a prosecution. Conversely, an allegation that protected advocacy was dishonest cannot alone turn it into investigation. The pleaded functions must remain distinct, and the allegations remain unproved. Lack of absolute immunity for a press statement does not make defamation alone a constitutional injury.

### II. Legal advocacy retains absolute immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A prosecutor retains absolute immunity for seeking an indictment as an advocate, preparing and filing legal charging and arrest-warrant submissions without a distinct personal evidentiary undertaking, and arguing a motion concerning counsel. Alleged malice, misleading advocacy or knowing use of false evidence does not withdraw that immunity; the Court retains the existing rule and rejects an additional exception for knowing material factual falsification within an otherwise protected advocacy function.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, providing eight direct votes for the complete rule. Stone-Zsela joins Part II-C, protecting opposition to counsel's reappointment, and the protection of ordinary lawful advocacy, but not Parts II-A and II-B insofar as they retain absolute protection for knowing material false advocacy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Imbler's protection for initiating prosecution and presenting the State's case, Burns's protection for legal presentation in court, and Fletcher's express separation of advocacy from a personal factual certification. Obtaining the indictment is the advocacy function pleaded here, despite the allegation that the prosecutors misled the grand jury about their neutrality. The holding does not classify every grand-jury-related act as advocacy.

Directing a subordinate to sign a criminal complaint and pursuing an information and arrest warrant likewise concern the legal charging submission as pleaded. No personal oath by the prosecutors or the subordinate is alleged. A signature alone cannot be converted into Fletcher's sworn evidentiary certification; the initial investigative manufacture of evidence remains a distinct, unprotected act. Appearing to oppose reappointment of counsel is also advocacy, and alleged retaliation does not change that function.

The proposed knowing-material-falsity exception addresses serious injuries, but would change immunity from suit, not merely identify functions already excluded. Imbler deliberately accepts that some meritorious claims will lack damages redress to protect independent advocacy. An allegation of deliberate falsity can subject an honest prosecutor to costly inquiry into knowledge, proof and causation before its falsity is established. A narrow ultimate liability rule and ordinary discovery management do not preserve the same protection from that inquiry. Criminal sanctions and professional discipline remain meaningful safeguards. Fletcher retained this precise boundary, and the costs inherent in it do not justify withdrawing that settled protection here.

### III. Further proceedings preserve the separate elements and defenses

**Holding and operative rule:** The claims based on investigative fabrication, the personal crime report, detective work and press statements may proceed under ordinary civil rules, while the dismissals resting on indictment advocacy, legal charging submissions and the counsel motion stand. A claimant must still establish the applicable constitutional injury, personal responsibility, culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury; the expressly reserved qualified-immunity defense remains for adjudication under the law governing that issue.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part III's rules for the reinstated claims and its reservation of qualified immunity. Eight Associates join its preservation of the indictment and charging-submission dismissals; Stone-Zsela dissents from that remedial limit. All nine preserve the counsel-motion dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** Fletcher separates an act's immunity classification from constitutional liability and personal defenses. Buckley likewise leaves those independent requirements for the proper proceedings after an absolute-immunity ruling. The Ninth Circuit expressly reserved qualified immunity, and this review of absolute immunity does not decide that separate legal defense. Its determination must use the law actually governing the issue when adjudicated, including any applicable intervening controlling decision, while keeping conduct-date notice distinct from later law.

Crawford-El preserves ordinary proof and lawful procedural screening; it authorizes neither a special heightened evidentiary burden nor a circular requirement to prove knowledge before obtaining discovery necessary to prove it. Those rules govern the surviving claims without substituting case management for immunity on the protected functions. The reversal of Milstein's conviction does not itself establish knowing falsity or an actionable deprivation. The lower courts must adjudicate the surviving claims and supported defenses act by act under these rules.

## Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and dissents in part; no other Justice joins. He agrees that investigative fabrication, personal accusation, detective work and press statements lack absolute immunity. He also preserves immunity for the counsel motion and ordinary advocacy outside his proposed exception. He would vacate the remaining dismissal insofar as it categorically protects knowing material factual fabrication or deliberate false presentation causing an actionable deprivation.

His exception requires knowing fabrication of a materially false fact, deliberate material falsification of an evidentiary record, or knowing presentation of such false facts; an independently actionable constitutional deprivation; the official's own responsibility; and the governing causal connection. Materiality concerns the fact's significance in authorizing or sustaining the deprivation. Error, disputed inference, adverse credibility, mistaken testimony, erroneous legal judgment, malicious motive, reversal of conviction and an unfavorable prosecution outcome do not suffice. The claimant retains ordinary proof burdens, and loss of immunity proves none of these elements.

He would partially overrule Imbler and Fletcher, and Burns to the same extent, because the categorical advocacy bar defeats redress for deliberate manufacture of the factual premise constitutional process must test. He acknowledges Imbler's common-law account and the especially strong continuity owed a statutory precedent. He does not claim that historical immunity already contained the exception or that congressional silence enacted it.

The strongest opposing interest belongs to honest prosecutors: easy accusations of deliberate falsity may be costly to disprove and chill independent advocacy. Criminal sanctions and discipline also supply real deterrence. Stone-Zsela accepts that his proposal increases discovery and litigation costs and weakens protection from suit; saying that no one may legitimately falsify evidence does not answer those costs. At this defined boundary, he gives greater weight to adjudicating a deliberately manufactured constitutional injury. Institutional planning around immunity is a real reliance interest, but no property title, completed transaction or final judgment must be undone. The change would govern this and future open cases without reopening final judgments.

Crawford-El permits clarification, dispositive legal rulings, sequenced relevant discovery and ordinary summary judgment. It does not permit a new heightened evidentiary quantum or a requirement to prove knowledge before obtaining discovery necessary to prove it. These safeguards reduce costs without eliminating them. The claim remains act-specific, not a general dishonesty tort or entitlement to sue for every malicious prosecution. The counsel motion does not become actionable merely because the prosecutors allegedly wanted a conviction.

He would remand for that bounded inquiry. Qualified immunity remains reserved and must be decided under the law actually governing it, including applicable intervening decisions; his proposed absolute-immunity change neither grants nor rejects that defense. Constitutional elements, personal participation, materiality, causation, damages and remaining defenses require independent adjudication. No separate witness immunity, independent constitutional privilege, municipal-liability rule, new interlocutory appeal or mandatory discovery or trial program is resolved.
