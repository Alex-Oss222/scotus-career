## Controlling holdings

### I. The right belongs to the individual

**Holding and operative rule:** The Second Amendment protects an individual's keeping of an ordinary pistol independently of enrollment in an organized militia. That coverage remains subject to a restriction justified under the governing burden-sensitive framework; a civil protective order does not itself remove the individual from the guarantee.

**Authority:** Souter's Part I, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Love establishes the individual holder and Hale separates coverage from justified regulation. Wright directs courts to examine the practical burden, and Napier applies that framework to a civil protective-order disability. Those decisions resolve coverage here. The Court therefore applies the existing personal right without adopting a new collective-right account or treating recognition of coverage as a complete defense to regulation.

**Precedent treatment:** Love, Hale, Wright and Napier are applied to personal coverage; none is displaced.

### II. The statute preserves alternative order predicates

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 922(g)(8) requires an order issued after a hearing of which the person received actual notice and at which the person had an opportunity to participate; the order must restrain harassment, stalking or threats against that person's intimate partner or a child of either person, or other conduct placing the partner in reasonable fear of bodily injury to the partner or child. The order must also either find a credible threat to the partner's or child's physical safety, or expressly prohibit using, attempting or threatening physical force against the partner or child reasonably expected to cause bodily injury. The express finding in subsection (C)(i) is not an element of the alternative in (C)(ii); statutory satisfaction remains distinct from the constitutionality of the resulting federal disability.

**Authority:** Souter's Part II, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress made (A) and (B) cumulative conditions and (C)(i) and (C)(ii) alternatives. An interpretation requiring both alternatives would alter that structure. The September 14 order followed notice and an opportunity to participate and prohibited bodily injury and threats of imminent bodily injury. The August ex parte order is not the charged predicate. Napier rejects an indispensable criminal-conviction condition but does not authorize every civil-order application. The absence of the statutory danger formula thus supplies neither a statutory defense under (C)(ii) nor categorical constitutional invalidity. The federal commerce element and the knowledge requirement retain their separate operation.

**Precedent treatment:** Napier is applied to the alternative statutory predicates and absence of a categorical criminal-conviction requirement.

### III. This protective adjudication supports the possession disability

**Holding and operative rule:** A force-prohibiting domestic-relations order entered after actual notice and meaningful opportunity to contest specific violent-threat evidence may supply a constitutionally sufficient civil danger predicate when the applicable law requires a real, nonspeculative protective necessity, the order addresses that threatened injury, and the federal disability depends on the operative order. On this record the restriction is a substantial regulation under Wright and satisfies intermediate scrutiny: it serves the important objective of preventing armed domestic injury, substantially relates to that objective, and does not unnecessarily or effectively eliminate protected activity beyond the justified individual disqualification.

**Authority:** Souter's Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer; these six Justices adopt the complete rule, application and affirmance. Stone-Zsela, Scalia and Thomas do not join.

**Controlling explanation:** Napier upheld a civil violence-and-recurrence predicate. Its principle extends here to an actual protective adjudication of threatened injury; completed assault and a verbal danger formula are unnecessary. The hearing included testimony that Emerson threatened to kill his wife's friend and communicated that threat to her. It did not establish that he threatened to kill her. The resulting injury restraints were entered under Texas law requiring actual protective necessity. That combined legal and procedural basis supplies the predicate, without inventing an express finding or treating unrebutted testimony as a criminal admission.

Preventing armed escalation is an important objective. A command not to injure leaves access to firearms intact, while a public-carry restriction would not address private domestic encounters. Intermediate scrutiny requires neither inevitable violence nor earlier gun use. The specific protective adjudication and continuing order dependence limit the disability; a safety label, temporary duration or the availability of guns to other people would not suffice. Modification and mandamus review were available, although direct interlocutory appeal was not. The order lasted until the divorce decree or further order; no fixed short duration or guaranteed restoration is assumed.

The strongest objection identifies the difference between authority to enjoin threats and justification for every-possession disarmament. The actual threat proceeding, protective legal standard and operative bodily-injury restraints provide that link here. Constitutional review of the additional federal burden remains distinct from routine collateral retrial of the divorce case. Later unadjudicated allegations supply no justification. Wright's prohibition on general abolition of meaningful ordinary defensive home possession remains; it does not forbid this justified individual protective disqualification.

**Precedent treatment:** Napier is extended from adjudicated violence and recurrence to the defined threat-order predicate; Wright's intermediate-scrutiny requirements and categorical home boundary remain intact. Fraternal Order of Police's official-use holding is not made a rule for every private-possession case.

### IV. The asserted ignorance-of-law ground does not support dismissal

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Amendment does not require dismissal of this possession charge for lack of fair warning where the defendant previously signed a federal form specifically identifying the restraining-order disability, later knew of the operative order and its contents, and allegedly continued possessing the firearm more than sixty days after the order. The earlier warning is a concrete circumstance prompting inquiry, not conclusive proof of later recollection or a substitute for the Government's proof of every enacted offense element.

**Authority:** Souter's Part IV is joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with reversal of this dismissal ground in the judgment only and adopts no additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lambert addressed wholly passive conduct without knowledge or circumstances prompting inquiry. The signed firearm-purchase form supplied a particular warning here, although it preceded the injunction and Emerson's answer was correct when made. His later knowledge of the order and its contents distinguishes the asserted blanket ignorance-of-law theory from Lambert. Staples separates knowledge of regulated facts from general awareness of illegality. Bryan construes a distinct willfulness requirement and does not rewrite this possession offense. The divorce judge's failure to provide an additional warning is not constitutionally dispositive on these facts. This pretrial ruling does not establish guilt or dispense with factual knowledge required at trial.

**Precedent treatment:** Lambert is distinguished by the specific warning and known operative order; Staples and Bryan retain their offense-specific mental-state rules.

**Precedent treatment:** Napier is extended from adjudicated violence and recurrence to the defined threat-order predicate; Wright's intermediate-scrutiny requirements and categorical home boundary remain intact. Fraternal Order of Police's official-use holding is not made a rule for every private-possession case.

### IV. The asserted ignorance-of-law ground does not support dismissal

**Holding and operative rule:** The Fifth Amendment does not require dismissal of this possession charge for lack of fair warning where the defendant previously signed a federal form specifically identifying the restraining-order disability, later knew of the operative order and its contents, and allegedly continued possessing the firearm more than sixty days after the order. The earlier warning is a concrete circumstance prompting inquiry, not conclusive proof of later recollection or a substitute for the Government's proof of every enacted offense element.

**Authority:** Souter's Part IV is joined by all seven other Associate Justices; eight direct votes. Stone-Zsela agrees with reversal of this dismissal ground in the judgment only and adopts no additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Lambert addressed wholly passive conduct without knowledge or circumstances prompting inquiry. The signed firearm-purchase form supplied a particular warning here, although it preceded the injunction and Emerson's answer was correct when made. His later knowledge of the order and its contents distinguishes the asserted blanket ignorance-of-law theory from Lambert. Staples separates knowledge of regulated facts from general awareness of illegality. Bryan construes a distinct willfulness requirement and does not rewrite this possession offense. The divorce judge's failure to provide an additional warning is not constitutionally dispositive on these facts. This pretrial ruling does not establish guilt or dispense with factual knowledge required at trial.

**Precedent treatment:** Lambert is distinguished by the specific warning and known operative order; Staples and Bryan retain their offense-specific mental-state rules.

## Precedent Treatment

- Love v. Pepersack, December 11, 1995: applied for individual coverage independent of organized-militia service.
- Hale, February 12, 1996: applied to distinguish personal coverage from a justified restriction.
- United States v. Wright, October 5, 1998: its graduated framework remains controlling; this individual protective disqualification receives substantial-regulation review, with actual fit and anti-elimination limits.
- Fraternal Order of Police, October 7, 1998: preserved at its conviction-based official-use scope; it does not decide every private-possession restriction.
- United States v. Napier, June 1, 2001: extended to the defined threat-only protective adjudication; its civil-process principle, intermediate-scrutiny classification and rejection of an indispensable conviction remain.
- Lewis v. United States, 445 U.S. 55 (1980): its conviction-disability and collateral-attack limits do not foreclose constitutional review of the additional burden of this civil-order disability.
- Lambert v. California, 355 U.S. 225 (1957): distinguished by the actual warning source and knowledge of the order.
- Staples v. United States, May 23, 1994, and Bryan v. United States, June 15, 1998: applied within their distinct factual-knowledge and willfulness holdings; neither supplies a universal knowledge-of-the-specific-law requirement.

## Law After Decision

Section 922(g)(8)'s force-prohibition alternative can rest on the defined threat-order adjudication without an express credible-threat sentence or completed assault. The six-Justice holding extends Napier at that scope; it does not validate every divorce injunction or replace Wright with general reasonableness. The operative hearing, actual protective legal standard, targeted injury restraint, federal burden and continuing order dependence remain material. Ordinary collateral relitigation and constitutional review of the additional federal restriction remain distinct. Statutory satisfaction does not conclusively establish constitutionality, and rejection of the present pretrial defenses does not establish criminal guilt.

## Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins individual coverage and the statutory alternatives. An actual civil danger adjudication may suffice without a criminal conviction or a particular formula, but complete private-possession disarmament burdens the core of the right. He would overrule Napier's intermediate-scrutiny classification to the extent it avoids strict scrutiny for that complete deprivation. Wright, in his view, requires a compelling protective interest, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective, materially less restrictive alternative.

Preventing lethal domestic violence is compelling. The hearing, specific threat, bodily-injury restraint and Texas protective standard matter, but do not conclusively establish the fit of every-possession prohibition. He would return the constitutional application for assessment of actual individualized risk, duration, review mechanisms and supported narrower conduct or access restrictions. Such alternatives may be inadequate if continued firearm access preserves the danger; that must be assessed rather than assumed. Existing materials may suffice, with additional proceedings only if needed. If the Government makes the required showing the prosecution may proceed subject to all elements; otherwise the unconstitutional application must be dismissed. The remand would not automatically retry the divorce dispute. He agrees in the judgment rejecting the independent Fifth Amendment dismissal ground without joining its reasoning.

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** They join coverage, the statutory alternatives and the fair-warning holding. Their Napier position requires actual justification of the full possession burden even where a civil order lawfully restrains violence. The hearing and Texas protective standard support a serious affirmance argument, but do not themselves complete that federal constitutional inquiry. They would vacate the arms application for restriction-specific review under Wright and Napier, considering the actual protective basis and operation of the entire disability. They do not require completed violence, a criminal conviction or the (C)(i) words, and do not adopt a rule that every individual restriction requires strict scrutiny. Nor do they find the hearing defective, disbelieve the wife or require automatic additional evidence. Ordinary further proceedings are permitted as needed; their proposed return is not immediate dismissal, acquittal or firearm restoration.

## Procedure After Action

The Fifth Circuit's reversal of dismissal remains effective and the remaining possession count returns to the district court for prosecution, subject to proof of every enacted element and otherwise available defenses. The four dismissed counts remain dismissed. No new civil hearing or automatic retrial of the divorce proceeding is required. The constitutional ruling concerns this operative order and its identified protective adjudication.
