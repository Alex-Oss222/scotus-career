## Controlling holdings

### I. This exceptional application of the continuance rules is inadequate to bar federal review

**Holding and operative rule.** A normally valid state procedural rule does not bar federal review when its application is exorbitant in the particular circumstances. Here the combined unexpected disappearance of subpoenaed witnesses who had attended trial, substantial prior presentation of the alibi and anticipated return, brief requested delay, and the trial judge's stated scheduling impediments make the additional written and repeated showings inadequate as a federal-review bar; the holding creates no general oral-motion or emergency exemption.

**Authority.** Souter's opinion speaks for Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, six Justices, on the complete rule and application.

**The distinct state requirements.** Missouri Rule 24.09 requires a written motion and affidavit unless the adverse party consents to an oral application. Rule 24.10 separately requires materiality and diligence; the witness's identity and residence if known, or diligence to learn them; reasonable grounds to expect attendance or testimony within a reasonable time; the particular expected facts and absence of another available person who could prove them as fully; and absence not procured, consented to or contrived by the applicant, with good faith rather than vexation or delay. If an affidavit is considered insufficient, the court must permit amendment. The oral-application exception does not erase these information requirements, and opponent consent is not inferred from silence.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that adequacy depends on the rule's operation as well as its ordinary legitimacy. Coleman preserves genuinely independent and adequate state grounds. Osborne distinguishes useful presentation from pointless repetition after a matter has been adequately brought before the judge. Ford protects fair notice and regular application, but neither regularity nor the absence of an identical earlier case conclusively answers this exceptional application. The Court respects Missouri's construction of its rules and decides the separate federal question.

The State has legitimate interests in current information about diligence, materiality and likely availability, as the dissent stresses. Here subpoenas, earlier attendance, prior alibi descriptions and counsel's explanation of the expected local presence supplied the substantial practical basis for the short request. The emergency made a contemporaneous sworn explanation of the unexplained disappearance unavailable. The trial judge's stated scheduling impediments would not have been cured by a perfectly written motion. Those facts work together: earlier testimony descriptions alone, a scheduling remark alone or an unexpected absence alone would not establish the same result. The holding neither declares literal compliance nor requires trial judges to announce every procedural defect. It holds that the additional formal demand, in this particular setting, does not adequately justify withholding federal consideration.

### II. Removal of the default barrier returns the distinct constitutional claim

**Holding and operative rule.** With this state ground inadequate, Lee need not additionally establish cause and prejudice to overcome that ground; the federal court must adjudicate his continuance claim under due process and any independently applicable habeas restriction. The state appellate court's procedural ruling does not become an adjudication of the federal claim's merits under 28 U.S.C. §2254(d) merely because it resolved the appeal.

**Authority.** The same six Justices join the bounded remand and its separation of adequacy from entitlement to relief.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that access to federal consideration and a right to relief are different inquiries. Coleman concerns excuses for an adequate default; it does not require such an excuse after the ground has been found inadequate. Trest confirms that default is a defense with actual predicates rather than a subject-matter-jurisdiction defect. The underlying due-process inquiry asks whether denial of the delay arbitrarily deprived Lee of a meaningful opportunity to present his defense in the actual trial circumstances. It requires examination of the missing testimony's disputed significance under the applicable habeas law. The relatives' account that an officer directed them to leave remains an allegation, and their anticipated alibi is not a finding. The separate gateway rulings are not reversed by eliminating this inadequate ground. The constitutional merits and entitlement to relief remain for adjudication because the federal courts did not reach them.
