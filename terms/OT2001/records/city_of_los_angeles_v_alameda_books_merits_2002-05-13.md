## Event

City of Los Angeles v. Alameda Books, Inc., No. 00-799. Argued December 4, 2001. Decided May 13, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing its judgment, 222 F.3d 719, filed July 27, 2000 and amended August 28, 2000, sustaining summary judgment, a declaration and an injunction for the businesses. The question is whether the City's concentration evidence can support the asserted noncommunicative harm underlying its combined-use prohibition. The district court's preserved expressive-targeting ground remains distinct; the court of appeals assumed neutrality and held the evidence insufficient.

Los Angeles's 1977 study linked concentrations of adult establishments to neighborhood crime but counted bookstore/arcade combinations as single establishments. Each plaintiff's retail and viewing operations occupies under 3,000 square feet, with one entrance and one supervising employee. The premises are physically undivided and the revenues are distinguished only internally. Neither plaintiff violates the separate one-thousand-foot adult-business or five-hundred-foot school, park or religious-institution distance rule. Those facts do not by themselves establish the actual effects of patron concentration.

Section 12.70(C) separately prohibits establishing or maintaining multiple adult businesses in the same building, structure or portion, and increasing an adult business's floor area in premises containing another adult business. Each defined adult-business category counts separately even when operated in one commercial enterprise. The combined-use amendment allowed combinations to continue until March 10, 1985, except a business already required to discontinue under the earlier transfer provision. That earlier provision required discontinuance within five years of a transfer allowed during the original ordinance's two-year transfer period if the business remained within the prohibited adult-business or school, park or religious-institution distance. After March 10, 1985, pre-September 1, 1978 uses could continue; if none qualified, one use could continue, with the earliest established taking priority in a dispute.

Continuation to March 10, 1987 could be granted for a written lease made before March 10, 1982 and extending beyond March 10, 1985, or undue financial hardship from pre-March 10, 1982 investment in real property, improvements or stock. An uncorrected violation of the governing zoning chapter after notice barred continuation. The verified application required prescribed supporting information, without a fee; a decision was due within seventy-five days unless the applicant and administrator agreed to an extension. A hearing could be set if necessary. The determination could not take effect until fifteen days after a copy of the written determination was mailed to the applicant; a timely appeal stayed proceedings on the challenged action pending determination. These terms establish no qualification of either plaintiff.

The separate exception for a site within five hundred feet of an A or R zone requires that no compliant site be reasonably available elsewhere. A reasonably available site must satisfy applicable zoning, be available for use, purchase or rent, have adequate street access, lighting and sidewalks, and be at least five hundred feet from uses that are or may become obnoxious or offensive through odor, dust, smoke, noise, gas, fumes, cinders, refuse or water-carried waste. Massage parlors and sexual-encounter establishments are excluded. The applicant must identify its current or proposed site and supply supporting data and the fee. A hearing and decision are required within sixty days; approval is required when the conditions are met, and denial requires findings identifying the deficiency. An appeal must be decided within thirty days after expiration of the appeal period. This is a residential-zone exception, not an exception to every combined-use prohibition.
