## Law entering the event

Section 2719(d)(1) directs state-equivalent application of the Code provisions concerning reporting and withholding of taxes on gaming or wagering winnings for the covered operations, and expressly names chapter 35 among its parenthetical references. Section (d)(2) supplies a priority rule unless another law specifically cites the subsection; that does not decide (d)(1)'s substantive scope. Chapter 35 imposes substantive wagering excise and occupational taxes. Section 4402(3) exempts a state-conducted sweepstakes, wagering pool or lottery when the wager is placed with the conducting state agency or its authorized employee or agent. Section 4411 has its own liability-linked occupational-tax terms; its reduced rate for state-authorized wagers is distinct. Section 4421 definitions and exclusions remain separate. National Bank of Oregon (June 7, 1993) reads the whole enactment without giving a general power to rewrite it. Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation (June 14, 1995) and Legal incidence of taxes on Indian-country tribal sales (effective that date) concern their state-tax and treaty components, not a federal tax exemption. Mille Lacs (March 24, 1999) preserves instrument-specific tribal interpretive protection. No displaced post-divergence enactment predicate is involved.

## Law of the decision

**IGRA's reporting and withholding direction does not confer the claimed substantive tax exemption.** For Indian gaming conducted under IGRA or an effective Tribal-State compact entered under §2710(d)(3), §2719(d)(1) directs state-equivalent treatment of the Code provisions concerning reporting and withholding of taxes on gaming or wagering winnings. Its parenthetical chapter 35 reference does not extend that defined subject to chapter 35's substantive wagering excise and occupational taxes. Section 2719(d)(2) makes the subsection govern notwithstanding other law unless that law specifically cites the subsection; this priority applies only within (d)(1)'s scope.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplies six direct votes for the complete construction and both affirmances.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reads the operative description and its examples together. National Bank of Oregon permits the complete enactment to establish meaning rather than making an isolated textual feature decisive. Here the parenthetical illustrates the class defined by the reporting-and-withholding words; it does not create a second, broader class. The tribes' strongest objection is that this reading deprives the express chapter reference of its apparent substantive work. That is a real textual difficulty, but an inconsistent example cannot expand the enacted subject it purports to illustrate. State-equivalent treatment still governs the reporting and withholding provisions within the defined class. The Indian-law canon resolves genuine remaining ambiguity; it does not require the opposite construction after the whole clause fixes that scope. This conclusion rests on text and context, not a claimed power to delete any inconvenient reference, a fabricated drafting sequence, or hostility to protective tribal canons. The state-tax and treaty rules of Oklahoma Tax Commission v. Chickasaw Nation and Mille Lacs do not supply an independent federal tax exemption.

**Precedent treatment:** National Bank of Oregon is applied for contextual construction; the distinct state-tax and tribal-instrument precedents remain confined to their own predicates. Chapter 35's independent definitions, exclusions and liability-linked provisions remain operative; today's construction rejects the asserted IGRA exemption rather than erasing those provisions.
