### Issue 1: Materiality cannot become a license for distinct knowing falsehoods

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The question permits review of the legal dismissal ground; it does not permit a finding that the challenged statements were false or knowingly published as false.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether protected accusations elsewhere eliminate the need to examine the challenged statement.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Propose a narrow clarification of the constitutional boundary between materiality and actual malice. The constitutional fault requirement remains unchanged.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A challenged statement may be dismissed when it creates no materially false defamatory meaning in the publication’s context. It may not be immunized merely because it is subsidiary to other serious statements for which actual malice was not proved. If it conveys a materially distinct defamatory accusation, the required fault inquiry concerns that accusation; other protected criticism does not supply a substitute finding.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding protects contextual assessment and excludes trivial differences. It does not presume additional actionable injury from every sentence or weaken the public-figure burden.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The defendants correctly insist that words must be read in context and that a legally immaterial difference cannot support recovery. Their broader proposed immunity does not follow. Protection of one accusation rests on the constitutional limits applicable to that accusation, including the plaintiff’s evidentiary failure. It does not establish the truth or innocent publication of another. The remand should determine whether the Vancouver statements actually add a distinct defamatory assertion, and, if so, whether the Church can meet the stringent fault requirement. That preserves vigorous criticism without transforming unrelated protected content into a defense to a proven knowing falsehood. Stone reaffirms New York Times v. Sullivan’s actual-malice burden and Masson’s distinction between material falsity and a constitutional incremental-harm immunity. Constitutional fault and statement-specific materiality carry decisive weight; a general balance of reputational harm does not replace them. The correction is broad enough to govern this recurring dismissal ground, but leaves ordinary contextual truth, identification, and summary-judgment rules intact.

## Law of the decision

**Contextual materiality does not create automatic immunity for a distinct accusation.** A challenged statement may be dismissed when, read in context, it conveys no materially false defamatory meaning. But the First Amendment does not immunize a materially distinct factual accusation merely because it is subsidiary to other serious accusations for which actual malice was not proved; the required fault inquiry concerns the distinct accusation itself.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls the complete rule and bounded remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court preserves the demanding protection New York Times v. Sullivan supplies: a public figure must prove a materially false defamatory assertion of and concerning it, published with knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard, with clear-and-convincing proof of actual malice. Context remains essential. Masson permits immaterial differences to be excluded from actionable defamation, but rejects a constitutionally compelled incremental-harm defense and keeps meaning, fault and injury distinct. The strongest defense of subsidiary-meaning immunity is that a supported general portrayal leaves an additional accusation with no meaningful defamatory work. Ordinary contextual analysis may establish that premise in a particular case; protection of one accusation does not itself establish it for another. Nor does absence of actual malice about one assertion prove the publisher's belief about a separate assertion. The Vancouver statements concern a source of funds and particular takeover, stock and property conduct capable of adding a distinct charge. The panel's reliance on the article's general portrayal did not resolve all their separate elements. Statement-specific adjudication is therefore required through the ordinary procedures, including an adequately supported alternative summary-judgment ground.

**Precedent treatment:** Sullivan's fault burden remains unchanged; Masson's separation of materiality, fault and incremental injury governs. The Second Circuit's Herbert subsidiary-meaning rule is rejected only insofar as it supplies the independent constitutional immunity described here.

**Limits and remaining adjudication.** Ordinary state rules governing injury, causation and damages retain the role Masson left them. The of-and-concerning defense and other undecided statement-specific elements remain for the lower courts because the panel did not resolve them and the record does not compel their resolution here. Nominal damages do not eliminate the conceded public-figure fault requirement. The distinct libel-proof-plaintiff doctrine lies outside the question decided.
