## Controlling holdings

#### I. The BVI corporation is a subject of the United Kingdom

**Holding and operative rule:** A corporation organized under British Virgin Islands law is a subject of the United Kingdom under 28 U.S.C. §1332(a)(2) because the territory's corporate and governmental authority rests under United Kingdom sovereignty. That established relationship at commencement satisfies the foreign-subject category without treating the territory as an independent foreign state; other statutory jurisdictional requirements remain independently necessary.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. All nine adopt the rule and its reversal and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute's alternative reference to subjects reaches the allegiance of this organized entity to the recognized sovereign whose authority sustains the legal system creating it. The British Virgin Islands is not an independent recognized foreign state. It is a territory under United Kingdom sovereignty, and incorporation under its law therefore establishes the relevant corporate relationship to that sovereign.

Traffic Stream correctly insists that alienage jurisdiction does not cover every foreign person and cannot arise from convenience. But equating subjecthood with full modern citizenship would omit the alternative Congress enacted. Individual immigration, passport and nationality distinctions do not determine the allegiance of a corporation formed under this dependent territorial government. Diplomatic and commercial purposes confirm this reading; they do not independently confer jurisdiction. No universal rule for every associated territory or every natural person's nationality follows.

Caterpillar requires actual jurisdiction rather than finality or consent. Rivet likewise requires the statutory jurisdictional source instead of a convenient substitute. Those requirements are met by the relationship existing when the action began, not by the forum clause, the completed debt litigation or a later nationality enactment. Verizon Maryland preserves the separation between jurisdiction and ultimate relief; its federal-question rule does not supply the alienage basis here.

The sovereign facts are established, so another factual inquiry is unnecessary merely because their legal significance is contested. The erroneous dismissal is reversed and the remaining appellate issues return below. The Court does not decide the financing merits anew, determine another corporation's principal place of business, or resolve the separate reach of §1332(c) to alien corporations. None is needed to answer the category question presented.

**Precedent treatment:** Caterpillar's actual-jurisdiction requirement and Rivet's statutory-source requirement are applied; Verizon Maryland's distinction between forum and ultimate entitlement is preserved without extending its §1331 holding.
