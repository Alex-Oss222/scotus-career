## Controlling procedural account

### This writ does not warrant decision of the substantive questions

**Controlling procedural conclusion:** The writ is dismissed as improvidently granted. This favorable-merits vehicle does not warrant discretionary resolution of the statutory and immunity questions, despite the separately denied dismissal motions; the Court decides no categorical constitutional bar on appeals by prevailing parties.

**Authority:** The per curiam opinion of the Court supplies the confined threshold explanation. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in the dismissal on the separate ground stated in his opinion. The dismissal leaves the Seventh Circuit's judgment undisturbed and adopts none of its telecommunications or immunity reasoning.

**Controlling explanation:** The commissioners' regulatory order prevailed. Their jurisdiction and immunity motions were separately postponed and denied after the merits, and the lower court treated the favorable merits determination as binding on them. The lost opportunity for interlocutory immunity review and the denial of requested dismissal are real procedural objections; the Court does not reduce every unsuccessful motion to a stray unfavorable sentence.

The present request nevertheless principally seeks review of the jurisdiction and immunity bases of the completed favorable adjudication. The binding merits sustain the commissioners' order and impose no adverse substantive change to it. The Court identifies no distinct corrective component comparable to the unnecessary patent-validity adjudication removed from a favorable decree in Electrical Fittings. That decision did not require certain future preclusion or a pending later lawsuit. Roper likewise recognizes a continuing stake supporting confined collateral review; it does not restrict all such stakes to money. Forney permits review of actual unawarded relief and reserves wholly favorable relief and requests merely for another explanation.

These distinctions make the commissioners' argument serious, but they do not compel discretionary resolution of the broad substantive questions in this vehicle. The Court leaves open the proper treatment of a distinct adverse collateral adjudication presenting an appropriate continuing stake and corrective remedy. It does not decide the section 252(e)(6), State-consent or prospective-officer questions, and supplies no decision on reciprocal compensation.
