* **Open Point:** Whether Congress’s detailed insurance-loan provisions nevertheless affirmatively protect this arrangement, and whether the accepted absence of nontax purpose and pretax return is sufficient for disallowance without a universal test for all tax-favored investment.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The courts below treated the borrowing arrangement as a substantive sham notwithstanding real policies and actual payments. Winn-Dixie argues that Congress deliberately drew an objective four-of-seven line and that applying an additional judicial restriction nullifies the permitted exception. It also disputes a rule that would condemn ordinary tax-motivated investment. The Commissioner answers that §264(c)(1) merely removes one disallowance and does not confer deductibility on borrowing engineered to produce a tax loss without independent economic purpose. The dispute does not require condemning every transaction with a tax motive or denying Congress’s power to confer intentional incentives.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could hold that satisfying §264(c)(1) leaves the ordinary substantive-sham inquiry intact and that this undisputed arrangement fails it. Reversal could treat the exception as affirmative authorization, requiring allowance subject to any independently preserved condition. A narrower remand could correct an erroneous legal formulation if the Tax Court had equated any tax motive with sham, but the accepted findings are materially stronger than that. The rule’s scope should distinguish this negative-pretax-return borrowing from economically substantive insurance and congressionally intended tax incentives. No penalty finding or later-year liability should be added.
