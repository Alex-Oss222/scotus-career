### Issue 1: Judicial inquiry and conflict-related prejudice

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Williams v. * **Other Governing Law:** The Sixth Amendment guarantees effective assistance. Strickland v. * **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law supplies ordinary prejudice and established exceptions. ## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The circuit required adverse effect despite the assumed inquiry failure. Mickens argues that the court’s own appointment and neglected inquiry place the case within a protective exception and that continuing duties to the victim compromised loyalty. The warden, supported by the United States, argues that a potential conflict and procedural failure do not prove deficient representation or trigger automatic relief. The warden must acknowledge the real continuing duties arising from the prior representation; Mickens must identify why the specific inquiry failure warrants a remedy independent of its effect on assistance.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could hold that inquiry failure alone does not dispense with adverse effect, leaving the existing factual determination dispositive within the granted scope. Reversal could recognize a properly defined automatic rule for this judicially created conflict or require a renewed hearing under a different burden. A limited remand might address whether the earlier hearing used the correct adverse-effect inquiry. Broad conclusions about all successive representations or all ethics violations are unnecessary. A new constitutional rule on collateral review would also require attention to applicable nonretroactivity limits; a procedural breach alone is not a writ.

Confidentiality survives the victim's death under Swidler, making the potential conflict real, but that does not identify a resulting impairment. The granted question concerns dispensing with adverse effect; the separate factual challenge to the adverse-effect finding was not granted. On that limited question, the existing finding defeats relief even assuming Sullivan governs successive representation. No new categorical rule for all successive conflicts is needed, and no harsher prejudice requirement is added.

This claim fails the minimum adverse-effect requirement even assuming that rule applies. Separate challenges to the factual finding are also outside the reviewed question.

### Issue 1: Duration and trial work

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Smiley and Good Samaritan preserve reasoned agency choice within an actual statutory assignment. * **Open Point:** Whether the statute permits the agency’s linked-duration construction and its distinction between anticipated inability and an actual predecision return to substantial work.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Fourth Circuit read the duration modifier as attaching only to impairment and treated the earlier expectation as sufficient to support trial-work protection. Walton argues that the agency adds an unstated requirement and defeats a statutory incentive to try working. The Commissioner must identify delegated authority rather than rely on program size alone; Walton must explain how a trial period presupposing entitlement creates it.

* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A life sentence carrying no ordinary parole eligibility is grossly disproportionate for this first conviction of delivering 0.238 grams of cocaine base on the record established here. Permanent incarceration for this first conviction involving 0.238 grams produces an inference of gross disproportionality; the available intra- and interjurisdictional comparisons confirm the exceptional mismatch.
