### Issue 1: Pending grievances and lawful consideration of earlier discipline

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling holding resolves the Board’s use of pending disciplinary actions. The prior decisions do not turn contractual grievance pendency into either conclusive validity or conclusive invalidity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate the existence of authority to consider information from the evidentiary and procedural safeguards governing its use. Review the actual administrative decision, including prejudice to the final penalty.
* **Other Governing Law:** The civil-service adverse-action and judicial-review provisions, the governing collective agreement, and the Board’s disciplinary-review rules supply the operative framework.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** An agency may explain a progressive penalty through the employee’s established disciplinary history, but a later invalidation can affect the premise of that explanation. Neither proposition decides how extensive collateral review must be.
* **Open Point:** Whether grievance pendency alone excludes earlier discipline and what disposition follows for the penalty review. The adequacy of the Bolling standard was not preserved; it is not an additional merits question for decision.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit treated the unresolved grievance process as making reliance on earlier discipline impermissible. Its rule avoided the risk that a removal would rest on penalties later overturned, without deciding the independent sufficiency of each penalty. **Postal Service:** Neither the civil-service framework nor ordinary administration requires the employer to suspend progressive discipline until every contractual challenge ends. Meaningful Board review can protect the employee while allowing the agency to manage continuing misconduct. Its proposed general rule permits consideration, subject to review and correction. **Gregory:** A nominal opportunity to contest an old penalty is insufficient if the Board then treats it as presumptively reliable and applies only narrow collateral review. Grievance rights could become practically useless if removal occurs first. The overturned warning supplies a concrete problem beyond theoretical delay. **Alternative grounds and limits:** Rejecting the categorical rule does not establish that the Board’s particular standard satisfies every statutory or constitutional requirement. Conversely, the reversal of the warning does not itself prove that no lawful removal penalty remains. The court must identify the penalty’s dependence on the invalid action.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Pendency path:** Reject exclusion based solely on a pending grievance, vacate the Federal Circuit judgment, and remand for the unresolved review and penalty issues. **Plausible Holding Scope:** Pendency is not a categorical bar; no approval of every form of collateral review follows. **Pendency-exclusion path:** Affirm the categorical exclusion only if the governing review statutes make final contractual adjudication a prerequisite to consideration. **Plausible Holding Scope:** That would resolve the question presented without reaching the unpreserved validity of Bolling review. **Completed-reversal path:** If the properly preserved arbitral reversal independently defeats the penalty reasoning, set aside the Board’s decision and require a new penalty determination. **Plausible Holding Scope:** Relief follows the actual invalid predicate and demonstrated dependence, not an across-the-board rule for pending grievances.

### Issue 1: Pendency is not invalidity

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The categorical legal premise is reviewable; contested factual and preservation questions remain with the appropriate tribunal.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether an unfinished grievance necessarily prevents consideration of earlier discipline.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt a new, limited construction of the personnel-review framework. No controlling decision already answers the question.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** An agency and the Board may consider earlier discipline while a grievance is pending, provided the employee receives a meaningful opportunity to challenge the predicate and its material use. Pendency alone does not invalidate the action. A completed reversal must receive the effect required by the governing agreement and review framework; the resulting penalty cannot rest on a premise that has lawfully disappeared.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This resolves the categorical pendency rule. It does not establish that a particular deferential review formula always provides meaningful review.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The civil-service statute permits parallel grievance and adverse-action channels; it does not make the speed of the former a categorical condition of the latter. Statutory structure carries decisive weight. The agency retains its statutory burden to justify removal and its penalty, while judicial review under §7703(c) respects lawful administrative factfinding and judgment. These are different burdens, so deference cannot establish that every collateral-review formula is valid. Gregory did not preserve a challenge to that formula. The completed expungement presents a different matter from mere pendency: a penalty may require reconsideration when one of its legal predicates disappears, through the available reopening and review mechanisms. Vacatur therefore answers the categorical question without deciding an unpreserved procedural challenge or pretending the warning remains valid.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The adequacy of Bolling’s particular collateral-review standard was not raised in the Federal Circuit or sought here and is outside the preserved question. The consequences of Gregory’s failure to notify the Board and the penalty’s dependence on the expunged warning require the remaining procedural and record application.

### Issue 1: Express exception and ordinary accrual

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Rotella separates discovery of an injury from later discovery of a complete pattern in its own statute. It does not establish a universal discovery rule for every federal action.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Read the enacted limitation rule, its express exceptions, and the elements making liability actionable together. Concealment, delayed injury, and delayed knowledge are not interchangeable.
* **Other Governing Law:** The version of the Fair Credit Reporting Act governing these disclosures required suit within two years from the date liability arose, with an express discovery extension for a defendant’s specified willful misrepresentation.
* **Material Other Authority:** The statute’s language and structure govern. General equitable principles may inform a properly preserved tolling claim but cannot silently replace a specified statutory exception.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Rotella rejects waiting for later pattern knowledge where an actionable injury is known. Its treatment of injury and concealment cannot be imported as a different statute’s comprehensive accrual rule.
* **Open Point:** Whether the express exception precludes a general discovery extension and whether liability arose only upon a later legally necessary injury.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Ninth Circuit applied a generally presumed discovery rule to federal limitations unless Congress clearly displaced it. **TRW:** Congress expressly selected one discovery exception. Making discovery generally sufficient would deprive the restrictive exception of meaningful work and extend stale claims beyond the enacted design. Its general rule measures the statutory period from liability, subject to the listed exception. **Andrews:** An undiscovered disclosure associated with identity theft may cause injury before a victim reasonably can identify the wrong, making an occurrence rule unusually harsh. The phrase liability arises can encompass completion of a cause of action rather than the earliest challenged act. Her strongest narrower position is that the particular claims were not actionable until the required injury existed. **Limits and alternatives:** A textual rejection of general discovery does not require treating every disclosure as an immediately complete claim. A genuine concealment or willful-misrepresentation theory needs its own pleaded and preserved basis. Hardship alone cannot establish an unstated exception, while the exception’s presence alone does not settle every element of accrual.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Textual-exception path:** Reject general discovery, reverse the appellate judgment, and remand for remaining accrual or exception questions. **Plausible Holding Scope:** No across-the-board discovery rule under the then-operative provision; no universal rule that the first act necessarily starts time. **Discovery path:** Treat liability as ordinarily discoverable liability while preserving a separate function for the express exception; affirm the appellate timeliness approach. **Plausible Holding Scope:** This statute’s liability-arises language would carry that construction, not every federal limitation. **Injury-specific path:** Resolve a properly preserved dispute about completion of injury before choosing a broad discovery rule. **Plausible Holding Scope:** The period starts when the particular statutory claim becomes complete, with relief limited to the claims whose dates are established.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Potential power does not itself displace exercised authority

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach statutory coverage and displacement; preserve remaining factual and penalty issues.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What exercise by another federal agency triggers the exclusion.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Adopt a new application of the statute’s actual-exercise language.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The other-agency exclusion requires that another federal agency actually exercise statutory authority to prescribe or enforce standards affecting the working conditions at issue. Possession of potential authority over an uninspected vessel, or investigation after a casualty, does not by itself exclude OSHA. A barge within the Act’s geographic and employment coverage may be a workplace; any displacement extends only as the actual governing regulation supports.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule does not require duplicative enforcement where Coast Guard standards genuinely cover the conditions, and does not equate uninspected status with absence of all Coast Guard regulation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The statutory choice of exercise matters. Replacing it with potential jurisdiction would create regulatory gaps whenever an agency could regulate but has not done so. Mallard Bay’s coordination concern is legitimate where real standards overlap, but cannot supply regulations absent from the record. The relevant contrast is therefore between the working conditions actually covered and those merely within theoretical power. The explosion’s gravity does not expand OSHA’s authority; the statute and actual regulatory coverage do. The actual-exercise language is decisive and creates a statutory boundary, not a free-floating balance of agency expertise. Stone distinguishes comprehensive Coast Guard regulation of inspected vessels from the limited regulation of this uninspected barge, and resolves the alternative workplace objection from §4(a)’s geographic coverage. Mead’s delegation threshold cannot confer displacement merely because an agency possesses expertise. The rule governs overlapping authority at the relevant working-condition level; individual citation and penalty issues need not be decided to restore the lawful enforcement channel.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Any unresolved violation facts, penalty issues, and different Coast Guard regulatory settings remain open.
