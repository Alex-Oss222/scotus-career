## 74. Bell v. Cone

### Components and controlling source distinctions

**B1 — Governing review and presumed prejudice.** The July 1, 1997 federal application is governed by amended section 2254. The constitutional rules available at the relevant state merits adjudication include Strickland and Cronic. Later Williams supplies the actual statutory review construction, while Shelton, Cotton, Mickens and Flores-Ortega retain their distinct predicates; none retrospectively creates a new duty at the September 19, 1986 state adjudication. The clean supplement supplies the intermediate appellate merits reasoning of November 4, 1987. No later source is made part of counsel's April 1982 legal knowledge.

Cronic's complete-failure rule does not apply merely because counsel presented no new sentencing witnesses and waived closing. At this sentencing counsel used an opening to identify mitigation and request mercy, referred to the experts' evidence already before the same jury, obtained favorable military-service evidence on cross-examination and successfully objected to harmful prosecution evidence. Those are actual advocacy, not a presumption arising from counsel's physical presence. Particular mistakes in selecting or omitting advocacy remain subject to Strickland. All eight Associates reject the Sixth Circuit's complete-abandonment basis for the conditional writ.

**B2 — The reviewed ordinary tactical claim.** The separate question is whether the state rejection of the asserted failure to present available mitigation and make a final argument was an objectively unreasonable application of Strickland. A failure of that gateway on a sufficient performance ground can defeat this limited claim without adopting the district court's erroneous added Fretwell requirement or deciding prejudice. It cannot decide every distinct investigation allegation or every other sentence ground.

The source supports a real, bounded performance alternative. The state court credited reliance on experts whose evidence remained before the jury, the mother's ineffective appearance, interviews of other potential witnesses, the absence in that reviewed record of a showing that their omitted testimony would have helped, and the tactical choice to prevent the stronger prosecutor's unanswered rebuttal. Those source findings and conclusions must retain their actual scope; they are not a finding that every possible mitigation source was investigated adequately.

Cozzolino admits evidence pertinent to a statutory aggravator or a mitigating proposition actually raised. Present criminal proclivities did not rebut the troubled-childhood explanation there; later defense proof could make premature introduction harmless only if it actually supplied rebuttal relevance. Thus fear of any and all bad-acts evidence would be legally mistaken, but Cozzolino does not immunize every statement by every proposed character witness. The supplied record establishes counsel's stated concerns and the state's tactical findings, not a conclusive new witness-specific admissibility ruling.

Pritchett, decided before trial, supplies an actual reason to expect a new sentencing hearing when one of two aggravators fails and the court cannot assume the jury's weighing of the remaining one. It did not guarantee reversal whenever any aggravator failed. Cone's 1984 direct appeal later distinguished that setting and applied harmless error. The latter decision cannot prove that counsel should have anticipated its distinction in 1982. His hope for appellate error also was not the sole stated reason for waiving argument: the reproduced testimony separately identifies prior mitigation, the identity of the rebuttal prosecutor and the actual sentencing presentation. The commitments below do not endorse deliberately allowing error as invariably competent practice.

**B3 — Prejudice and independent district grounds.** The district court both stated ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice and demanded additional fundamental unfairness or unreliability. Actual Williams rejects that additional requirement for ordinary lawful mitigation. Its performance and closing-strategy discussions are separate reasons, but the phrase treating strategy as unreviewable is not the governing rule: informed professional reasonableness remains reviewable. Seven Associates independently sustain the limited state performance adjudication under the correct standard; Stevens would leave that determination to the Court of Appeals on remand. None adopts the added Fretwell burden or holds that performance labels automatically control.

**B4 — Remaining claims and remedy.** The Sixth Circuit pretermitted other sentence grounds. The district's investigation/development restriction rests on distinct default decisions whose adequacy, independence and asserted cause are not resolved here. The supplied respondent remand argument raises the previously-determined bar, its alleged novelty and unavailable investigative funding; these are arguments, not findings that the default is inadequate or excused. Lee does not decide Tennessee's application. No claim is defaulted because the operator lacks a source. No new hearing, funding entitlement, actual incapacity, further witness testimony or mitigation prejudice is presumed.

### F1. Actual narrowing and the patentability trigger

A claim amendment gives rise to prosecution-history estoppel when it actually narrows relevant claim scope to obtain the patent by satisfying a requirement of patentability. The trigger is not confined to avoiding prior art: a genuinely narrowing amendment addressing a disclosure requirement can perform the same surrender function. A voluntary amendment is subject to the same inquiry; neither its voluntary label nor a change in wording establishes or defeats estoppel by itself.

The relevant inquiry is amendment- and limitation-specific. Stoll's recited section 112 rejection, added sealing-ring structure, magnetizable-sleeve limitation and accompanying prior-art remarks must be considered distinctly. Carroll's canceled claim and added sealing-ring claim arise from a different reexamination history. This commitment construes no unprovided statutory phrase and makes no new examiner finding. It does not finally determine that every identified alteration narrowed the pertinent scope for the same reason.

**Authority and treatment:** All eight Associates' Warner-Jenkinson joins supply the existing patentability-surrender principle; Exhibit Supply supplies actual prosecution surrender as retained there. The proposed holding clarifies the kinds of patentability-related narrowing that can trigger it. It does not overrule the element-specific equivalents doctrine or treat a formal amendment as sufficient without narrowing.

### F2. Explanation of an otherwise unexplained amendment

Warner-Jenkinson's existing rule remains: when required narrowing lacks a disclosed legally sufficient reason, the patentee seeking the disputed equivalent must explain it. After fair consideration, inadequate explanation supports the rebuttable presumption of a patentability-related reason and estoppel at the affected limitation. A supported nonpatentability explanation must be considered.

That purpose inquiry precedes and is distinct from F3's inquiry into the surrendered scope. Older records receive contextual consideration; nonexistent contemporaneous formulations cannot be demanded and silence is not misconduct. These qualifications neither shift the explanation burden to SMC nor guarantee a hearing, reopening or repeated evidentiary opportunity. No broader automatic purpose presumption for every voluntary textual change is adopted.

### F3. Surrender scope and the competing alternatives

Once a qualifying patentability-related narrowing is established, the patentee presumptively surrendered the disputed equivalent at the amended limitation. The patentee may overcome that presumption by showing a concrete, objectively supportable basis in the prosecution record, considered in the specific infringement setting, why the scope relinquished to obtain the patent did not include that variation. The court must enforce actual surrender and cannot permit a purported equivalent to erase a material claim element.

The new scope presumption does different work from F2's presumption about why an unexplained amendment was made. It is not an irrebuttable complete bar, and the absence of a persuasive rebuttal has the operative consequence of foreclosing that asserted equivalent at the affected limitation. A supported basis for rebuttal removes that estoppel barrier only; it does not itself establish technical equivalence or liability.

**Preferred path and strongest objection:** The government's intermediate proposal provides a definite starting rule while allowing the prosecution to establish a more limited concession. SMC correctly emphasizes competitors' reliance on amendments and the applicant's control of the claim. That objection supports placing the concrete burden on the patentee; it does not establish that a narrowing addressing one patentability problem necessarily relinquishes every nonliteral way of satisfying the amended limitation.

**Complete-bar alternative:** Affirmance under a conclusive rule would reduce uncertainty but would require changing Warner-Jenkinson's preserved relation between actual surrender and nonliteral scope. The record supplies no reason to make that change when a burdened, objective surrender inquiry can protect the public notice interest. All eight reject this path.

**Unrestricted flexible-bar alternative:** A remand with no scope presumption would preserve contextual inquiry but inadequately specify the consequence of the applicant's actual narrowing. The applicant, who seeks scope beyond the narrowed words, should supply the concrete prosecution basis. All eight reject an unburdened appeal to overall similarity or fairness; they retain the specific rebuttal route stated above.

No closed exception list is adopted. Unforeseeability, the relationship between the reason for narrowing and the accused variation, or limitations of claim expression could matter only insofar as a properly supported showing establishes the relevant absence of surrender under this rule. None is an automatic safe harbor. This record establishes no date when either accused substitute became available and no successful rebuttal for either patent. No rule concerning every foreseeable but unclaimed substitute without narrowing is announced.

### F4. Decisionmaker and factual boundaries

The court determines claim meaning and the legal effect of prosecution surrender before an asserted equivalent may establish liability. Markman includes genuinely disputed technical usage and credibility embedded in construction within that judicial task; it does not convert external infringement facts into judicial construction. Any legally available disputed factual equivalence retains ordinary factfinder treatment. No universal appellate review standard, mandatory construction hearing or complete allocation of every equivalents question is adopted.

The no-bad-intent rule remains. The analysis concerns claim scope and surrender, not whether Festo or SMC behaved blameworthily. Graver Tank's protection against insubstantial substitutions survives within the claim-specific boundaries retained by Warner-Jenkinson.

## 76. In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation

### Question, components and operative commitments

The review concerns the Third Circuit judgment affirming the March 29, 2000 federal restriction on the Lowes' retained Florida litigation. The Lowes remained in the settlement for two eligible policies and excluded two others through the policy-specific election expressly offered in the notice. The federal order reaches motion practice, discovery, presentation of evidence and other action involving the facts of released transactions. The Florida action is stayed and inactive subject to ordinary reopening; its retained claims have not been dismissed.

The following propositions define the scope to which the individual rows refer:

- **P1 — Enforcement jurisdiction.** Incorporation of the release and express retention of jurisdiction permit the district court to enforce the settlement within Kokkonen's rule. This answers the postdismissal-jurisdiction objection; it does not satisfy section 2283 or determine what was released.
- **P2 — Relitigation exception and the actual release.** The incorporated release extinguishes claims and relief within its lawful scope, including a genuinely indirect effort to obtain relief on a released transaction. It does not, consistently with the express policy-specific exclusions, turn every fact common to a released policy and an excluded policy into a released claim or a conclusively determined issue. Section 2283 therefore does not permit this blanket evidentiary restraint solely because proof overlaps. A supported injunction against an actual released claim or inconsistent determination remains available; the subject and use of the proposed proof must be connected to that protected adjudication, rather than identified by association alone.
- **P3 — Aid of jurisdiction.** Ongoing administration of a complex settlement is relevant but does not establish that the retained state claims or their overlapping proof seriously impair the federal court's ability to administer or dispose of its case. No such necessity sufficient for this order's breadth is established by the supplied reasons. Retained jurisdiction, settlement size and a general concern about future claims do not replace the statutory showing. The All-Writs Act does not remove section 2283's independent restriction.
- **P4 — Disposition and remedy.** Reverse the Third Circuit insofar as it sustains the categorical restriction and remand to remove that restriction. The settlement and release remain operative; any further request for protection must identify the actual released claim or issue, its lawful preclusive scope, the conduct requiring restraint and the applicable section 2283 exception. The Florida court applies its own reopening and evidentiary rules. The remand grants neither automatic admission of a particular item nor automatic recovery on any retained count.

The proposed rule is a statutory and release-specific answer to the order under review, not a categorical rule that settlement obligations can never affect evidence. The express exclusions and the distinction between adjudicated claims and evidence perform the necessary legal work. The Lowes' independent constitutional notice and vagueness objections need not be resolved to remove the present restraint on these sufficient statutory grounds. The Court does not use those objections to reopen certification or the settlement's completed approval.

## 77. Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority

### Questions, record and distinct components

This review concerns the private Shipping Act complaint against the admitted state arm, not a Commission-initiated enforcement proceeding. The Commission's own-motion review of the ALJ's dismissal does not change the complaint's initiator. The complaint seeks reparations, cessation of alleged discrimination and Commission pursuit of an interim judicial injunction. No violation, award, waiver, officer substitution or independent enforcement action has been established.

The judgment below ordered dismissal after extending immunity to private administrative adjudication and treating Article I as incapable of sustaining this enforcement against a State. Actual Florida Prepaid and Garrett make the latter categorical premise unavailable. The two issues must therefore be separated even by a Justice who supports extending immunity to this tribunal.

The separable components are:

- **F1 — Tribunal boundary:** Whether the State's immunity from private judicial suit also bars this private complaint before the Commission.
- **F2 — Legislative authorization and power:** If immunity reaches the proceeding, whether the operative statute unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to this particular adjudication and remedy, and whether that enforcement falls within valid enumerated power. Current law permits that inquiry; it does not settle its Shipping Act application.
- **F3 — Actual consent:** Ordinary port operations are not consent. No authorized voluntary removal, express waiver or other established submission supplies consent here.
- **F4 — Prospective-only alternative:** Whether the cease-and-desist request survives even if the monetary component encounters immunity. The government's administrative distinction must be addressed; it is not already a judicial Young claim against an identified connected officer.
- **F5 — Later proceedings:** Commission investigation on its own initiative, governmental enforcement, private judicial enforcement of nonreparation orders, private reparation collection and the two interim-injunction channels retain their separate predicates.
- **F6 — Disposition:** Reversal on the agency-boundary ground, or vacatur with a remand under the controlling legislative-authorization framework. Neither supplies liability or an award.

### Operative grounds available for joins

**Agency-boundary ground.** The Commission may adjudicate this statutory maritime complaint against the state arm without treating the administrative proceeding itself as a private judicial suit barred by sovereign immunity. Private initiation and a reparation request do not by themselves remove an adjudication from the Commission's public regulatory function: the agency determines the statutory violation, reviews the ALJ's determination and issues its own order through the enacted scheme. This conclusion does not convert the complainant into the United States, make the complaint a government-filed action or decide immunity from a later private judicial collection action.

The supported explanation is confined to this Commission process. California establishes federal authority to regulate public terminal operations and the described corrective order; it does not answer the distinct immunity issue. The distinction rests on the nature and statutory control of this federal administrative function, not merely the name of the branch, the absence of immediate execution, the political cost of noncompliance or a general denial that adjudication burdens States. Waffle House identifies the independent character of an agency's own action, but this complaint is not that action. Seminole's judicial-function holding establishes that a court's conclusive determination can remain judicial despite later remedial steps; it does not classify every executive adjudication of statutory public obligations as a private court suit. Later judicial proceedings remain subject to their own immunity, authority and remedy requirements.

**Private-compulsory-adjudication ground.** Immunity protects a State from this private litigant's compulsory adjudication before an ALJ and Commission, subject to valid displacement under the actual legislative-power rules. The pleadings, demanded response, civil discovery, adversarial hearing, determination of individual liability and personal reparation request do legal work comparable to a private suit. The agency's review and inability to execute its own order do not eliminate the adjudicative burden or the order's specified later evidentiary consequences. The conclusion is not an immunity from substantive maritime duties, federal investigation or government enforcement.

This ground does not sustain the judgment below by itself. Florida Prepaid and Garrett independently permit Congress, within enumerated power, to authorize the State's particular exposure unmistakably. A Justice adopting this ground returns the separate authorization question for consideration under that controlling rule instead of reinstating the displaced categorical Article I prohibition. That return is a decision about the scope of this review and the lower court's erroneous legal premise, not a declaration that an unavailable research document establishes a litigant's failure.

**Authorization reserved on the returned path.** General coverage and statutory exposure to a particular private proceeding are distinct. The original person and terminal definitions, the complaint and investigation provisions, the separate enforcement provisions, California's public-terminal construction and the actual statutory continuity all bear on that distinction. The clear-authorization requirement is not reduced to a demand for a single formula; nor does the word person, public-terminal coverage or federal regulatory power alone satisfy it. The lower judgment's categorical constitutional premise prevented the inquiry from doing its required work. The returned proceeding must identify the operative statute and actual relief, assess unmistakable authorization, and apply the valid Article I framework. No present affirmative or negative abrogation holding is joined by these commitments.

The source-version limit does not prevent deciding F1. The original and 1998 texts are separately supplied; the 1998 changes generally operate from May 1, 1999 with their savings clause, while the government appendix retains older cross-references. The commitments decide no version-sensitive violation, enhanced reparation, transition fact or particular award. The actual complaint and conduct dates must govern any later conclusion that depends on them. They are not invented or converted into untimeliness.

### G1. Competing methods and the selected rule

**Seven-Associate commitment:** When section 406(b)'s favorable-judgment and court-representation predicates are satisfied, the court begins with the lawful contingent-fee agreement and independently determines whether the requested fee is reasonable for the actual court representation, within the statutory ceiling. The agreement does not carry a presumption that the requested percentage is reasonable, and the attorney seeking payment bears the burden of establishing a reasonable award. A court may not replace the agreed arrangement at the outset with an exclusive hours-times-rate measure merely because that measure is usual in adversary fee-shifting cases.

Section 406(b) governs compensation paid out of the successful claimant's benefits under an existing representation arrangement. Its reasonable-fee requirement supervises that arrangement; it does not prescribe an hourly formula. The 25 percent limit fixes the outer boundary without fixing the award. Dague's categorical rule remains intact under its two environmental fee provisions, and Blanchard's separation of the private bargain from an adversary's statutory obligation remains intact. Neither already settles this distinct claimant-funded method. The present inference from those limits and section 406(b) is new to this question, not an earlier announced seven-Justice rule.

**Strongest objection and response:** The Commissioner emphasizes the same reasonable-fee language, judicial responsibility and protection of disability benefits. A percentage can increase because of a claimant's earnings, dependents or accumulated benefits, without a corresponding increase in legal work. Those concerns defeat automatic contract enforcement and a presumption for the maximum. They do not require treating every contingent return above an hourly figure as unreasonable; such a rule would deny the contingent arrangement a role without statutory language selecting that result. G2 supplies the necessary individual review, rather than assuming that all contingent contracts or all requested fees are sound.

**Scalia's competing commitment:** The court should determine a reasonable fee through reasonable hours multiplied by a reasonable market rate, subject to the statutory maximum and ordinary substantiation and nonduplication requirements. It should not add a multiplier for the risk of nonpayment. Dague's reasoning supports that method because a judicial reasonable-fee award need not reproduce all private payment economics. The source of payment changes who bears the fee, not the court's duty to value compensable services. The percentage cap retains work as an additional ceiling and supplies no statutory direction to begin at a contract percentage.

Scalia recognizes that Dague reserved different compensation schemes. His present extension rests on section 406(b)'s own judicial-determination language and claimant-protection operation, not on a claim that Dague's holding automatically governs. He answers the contract-first path by finding its protection against routine maximum awards less definite than an objective lodestar with the enacted ceiling. He would reject an EAJA-rate substitution: a reasonable market rate under section 406(b) and an EAJA limitation are different inquiries.

**Other available narrower method:** A rule treating the contract merely as evidence within an otherwise controlling lodestar would not correct the seven Associates' identified error: it would still substitute hourly valuation for review of this claimant-funded bargain. Conversely, presumptive enforcement of 25 percent would not preserve the attorney's burden or the statutory reasonableness decision. None of the eight adopts that presumptive-maximum alternative. O'Connor's earlier Dague market position supplies no mandatory multiplier here, and Stevens's join in the other Dague dissent supplies no automatic amount.

### G2. Independent review, proof and lawful reductions

All eight agree that counsel must substantiate the requested statutory fee and that the court must make an independent, explained reasonableness determination. Neither a signed agreement, a favorable judgment, the customary use of a percentage nor compliance with the numerical ceiling proves reasonableness by itself. The seven agreement-method Associates adopt the following implementation; Scalia does not join it as the governing method.

The court evaluates the actual services and results under the agreement. Demonstrated substandard representation, attorney-caused delay that inflated the benefit accumulation, or compensation disproportionate to the representation may justify reducing the request. These are grounds of statutory reasonableness, not findings about either lawyer here and not a mandatory numerical formula. The court may require and consider time records and rate information as an aid; it must explain why the actual request is or is not reasonable in this setting. A high effective hourly rate alone does not establish unreasonableness, and no preselected multiplier or universal hourly limit follows.

The ordinary contingent bargain may account for the possibility of no payment, but the decision creates no separate right to a risk premium or formula reimbursing counsel for unrelated unsuccessful representations. Nor does the lawyer satisfy the burden merely by asserting that all Social Security litigation is risky. The source's statement that no individual matter was argued unusually risky is preserved without treating it as a concession that no reasonable contingent compensation can exceed lodestar.

The court must address each reviewed representation separately. This commitment fixes no fee, permissible multiplier, market rate, delay finding, professional deficiency or universal list of required factors. It neither adopts all of the lower court's Kerr factors nor overrules an unpresented compensation rule. Buckhannon's distinct eligibility doctrine does not erase section 406(b)'s express favorable-judgment requirement; its separated reasonable-amount inquiry supplies an analogy at that scope only.

### G3. Statutory conditions and separate EAJA operation

All eight retain the following statutory boundaries, independently of the method disagreement:

- The court must have rendered the required favorable judgment to a claimant represented before it by an attorney. The permitted fee is for that court representation and cannot exceed 25 percent of the statutorily defined total past-due benefits due by reason of the judgment. A contract cannot authorize an award above that ceiling or substitute for the court's allowance.
- Certification is permitted out of the benefits, not as an additional government fee, and remains subject to any applicable subsection (d). Section 406(b)'s restriction on other payable or certified fees for the same court representation remains subject to the specific EAJA saving provision. Section 406(a)'s administrative-representation procedures and dollar limits are not imported into this method, and no aggregate administrative-and-court cap question is decided.
- Where subparagraph (b)(1)(B) applies under its favorable-judgment transition, continued benefits described in section 423(g) or (h) are excluded from the base; the benefit amount is determined before the applicable section 1320a-6(a) reduction. The 1994 addition applies to favorable judgments made after 180 days after August 15, 1994. No representation-specific date or benefit input is invented.

## 79. South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection

### Question, components and distinct paths

The Third Circuit reversed the preliminary injunction's section 1983 basis. The residents originally pleaded both intentional discrimination under section 601 and a direct effects theory under section 602; the later amendment added section 1983. The original theories were not shown to have been abandoned. The injunction was suspended on June 15, 2001. The present question includes both the asserted section 1983 right and the separate prospective Title VI route.

- **S1 — Section 1983, current-law path.** Powell's separate holding rejects the asserted broader individual effects right under section 1983. Section 601's intentional-discrimination right and section 602's agency instructions do not themselves create that additional individual right; regulatory elaboration cannot supply one that the statute lacks. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas adopt this ground and would retain the rejection of the section 1983 basis.
- **S1-A — Section 1983, supported reconsideration path.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain their published Powell position: the identified beneficiaries, statutory nondiscrimination protection, express implementation authority and a valid, sufficiently definite implementing requirement can together define the individual entitlement, with section 1983 supplying the enacted remedy. They would reject the categorical section 1983 bar and reconsider Powell to that extent. They do not treat every regulation or program-administration command as a right. This is their proposed legal change, not entering law or a controlling proposition of this handoff.
- **S2 — Direct prospective Title VI route.** Sandoval independently authorizes beneficiaries to obtain prospective compliance with valid, sufficiently definite section 602 implementing requirements from the proper covered defendant. The retained direct claim must receive that treatment. Section 7.35(b)'s criteria-or-methods rule requires the actual prohibited effect, including its alternative concerning defeat or substantial impairment of program objectives for protected persons. Section 7.35(c)'s site-selection provision has its own purpose-or-effect terms and is not automatically a pleaded, proved claim merely because it is in the copied regulation. Coverage, the particular requirement's validity and definiteness, causation and fitted relief remain actual adjudicative questions.
- **S3 — Jurisdiction, defendant and the distinct intentional claim.** The asserted substantial federal controversy is distinct from eventual entitlement. Under Verizon Maryland and Young, an actually connected officer may face prospective relief for an alleged continuing federal violation within the enacted remedy's limits; Commissioner Shinn is named, but his required connection and the relief's proper scope must be applied to this record. The remand does not resolve NJDEP's direct amenability by assuming consent. The original section 601 intentional-discrimination claim retains its separate intent requirement and actual procedural footing.
- **S4 — Disposition and remedy.** Vacate the appellate judgment and remand for consideration of the retained direct prospective claim under S2, the distinct intentional claim and the ordinary defendant and equitable inquiries under S3. The eight Associates support this disposition, but divide on S1 versus S1-A as specified below. Vacatur itself does not revive the previously suspended preliminary injunction; operative preliminary relief must rest on a valid route and the requisite findings. The existing findings retain their actual status for ordinary review; the Court requires no new hearing or wholesale reopening merely because the source excerpts do not reproduce every finding.

The S1 division is real. The common direct-action remand does not turn the four proposed section 1983 supporters into adopters of the contrary rule. Nor does agreement to apply Sandoval turn its four former dissenters into adopters of the rejected regulatory-right theory under section 1983.
