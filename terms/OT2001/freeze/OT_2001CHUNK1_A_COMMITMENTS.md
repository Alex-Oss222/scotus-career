## 2. TRW Inc. v. Andrews — November 13, 2001

**Provisional disposition:** All eight Associates reject a general discovery rule under the then-operative FCRA §1681p, reverse the Ninth Circuit's contrary legal judgment, and remand. They do not reinstate partial summary judgment automatically or determine that the first disclosure necessarily completed every statutory claim. A preserved liability-arises/injury argument remains distinct from discovery and is available for ordinary consideration on remand. No exception or tolling entitlement is awarded.

**Sufficient rule:** The provision requires suit within two years after liability arises, subject to its express exception: a defendant must have materially and willfully misrepresented information the FCRA required it to disclose to the individual; the information must be material to establishing liability to that individual; qualifying suit may be filed within two years after discovery of that misrepresentation. General ignorance of a violation does not satisfy those predicates or create a parallel automatic discovery extension. Determining when liability arose requires the elements of the particular actionable claim, not an assumption that disclosure, injury and discovery are identical dates.

**Entering authority:** The authenticated statutory text, enacted in 1970, controls. Civil RICO limitation, new injury and concealed discovery operates through Rotella (February 23, 2000) and Klehr (June 19, 1997), preserving its statute-specific limits. Rotella's unanimous refusal to await pattern discovery on a complete claim and known injury does not adopt a universal accrual rule. Nothing in the proposed FCRA holding changes the distinct new-injury, unprovable-damages or diligent-concealment components of those RICO cases.

## 4. Brown v. City of Oneonta — November 13, 2001

**Provisional disposition:** All eight Associates vacate the equal-protection dismissal insofar as it rests on a categorical exemption for victim-description investigations and remand for application of the governing common substantial-justification standard to the actual racial selection, scope and burdens. They preserve independently final or ungranted matters, existing Fourth Amendment dispositions, personal-participation limits, and ordinary immunity and remedy inquiries. No damages or blanket liability judgment follows.

**Controlling-rule proposal:** A police decision intentionally selecting persons by race is an intentional racial classification even when a victim supplied the racial feature and the officers lack racial hostility. The source and reliability of the description bear on justification; they do not make deliberate official selection disappear. In this setting, the common standard requires an important lawful investigative objective, a substantial evidence-supported relation between the actual selection and that objective, and justified coverage and burdens, including a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives offering comparable investigative value with materially less racial disadvantage. Use of a specific victim's description is not categorically forbidden; generalized suspicion, convenience or racial stereotyping is insufficient.

**Operative application and limits:** Investigation of the identified violent attack is an important lawful objective. Race was one part of the reported identification, together with apparent youth, male sex and an injured hand; the campus-directed trail also matters at its actual evidentiary weight. The claimed wider sweep of nonwhite persons, including a woman, requires examination of the government's actual selection rather than categorical validation. A consensual encounter can impose a racially selected burden even if it is not a seizure. Conversely, membership in the described group does not establish a particular encounter, coercion, injury, defendant's responsibility or compensation. September 1992 clearly established law remains the immunity date; later decisions cannot retroactively supply notice.

**Entering authority:** Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications, underlying rule Peña June 12, 1995; present operation Eisenberg May 30, 2000 for its distinct K–12 component. Engineering Contractors (1998) and City of Dallas (1999) establish that all eight Associates subsequently applied the common standard as governing precedent, including those who preferred strict scrutiny. Eisenberg expressly preserves that binding force. This proposal extends the justification principle to deliberate investigative selection; it does not pretend that competitive-opportunity cases had already decided every investigative use of race. Lesage separates process injury and consequential relief.

## 6. Chickasaw Nation v. United States, No. 00-507 — November 27, 2001

**Identity:** One petition covers Chickasaw and Choctaw; both reviewed tax judgments remain distinct applications. Use the neutral preparation's control-verified docket correction. No unrelated docket belongs in the decision.

**Provisional disposition:** Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm rejection of the asserted IGRA exemption in both tribal matters. O'Connor and Souter would reverse that construction and remand for application of the specific state-equivalent exemption to qualifying operations, with ordinary taxpayer-specific computation and no blanket tribal tax immunity or predetermined refund.

**Restrictive-description rule:** Section 2719(d)(1) directs state-equivalent treatment for the described reporting and withholding provisions concerning gaming/wagering winnings. Its illustrative parenthetical reference to chapter 35 does not extend that defined subject to substantive wagering excise and occupational taxes. The strong contrary argument is that this reading leaves an expressly named chapter without its apparent work; the six accept that awkwardness rather than use an inconsistent example to expand the operative description. They need not announce a general power to correct congressional mistakes. Subsection (d)(2)'s priority rule operates only within the scope established by (d)(1).

**Express-reference/canon rule:** O'Connor and Souter give independent force to the explicit inclusion of chapter 35. At minimum, naming a chapter of substantive wagering taxes while describing reporting and withholding produces a genuine conflict remaining after ordinary reading; the Indian-law canon resolves that ambiguity in favor of the tribes. State-equivalent treatment carries the actual §4402(3) exemption for a state-conducted sweepstakes, pool or lottery and qualifying placement with the conducting agency or its authorized employees/agents. Section 4411 then follows its own liability-linked terms. Neither Justice would infer a general exemption for every tribe, member, game or federal tax.

## 8. Church of Scientology International v. Behar — November 27, 2001

**Provisional disposition:** All eight Associates vacate the judgment insofar as it dismisses the three Vancouver statements, numbered 5–7 below, by transferring the absence of actual malice for other statements through a subsidiary-meaning rule. Remand for the ordinary statement-specific issues and any properly available alternative grounds. Leave unrelated statement determinations and ungranted amendment matters undisturbed. No finding of falsity, defamatory meaning, identification, actual malice or damages is made, and no trial or fresh discovery is guaranteed.

**Sufficient rule:** A public figure must establish a materially false defamatory assertion of and concerning it, published with knowledge of falsity or reckless disregard, with constitutionally sufficient proof of actual malice. An article's independently protected criticism does not supply an automatic constitutional defense to a distinct challenged factual accusation simply because it supports the same unfavorable overall view. Context can show that no materially different defamatory meaning exists; that inquiry cannot treat a separate potentially false charge as true, harmless or nonmalicious solely because the publisher lacks actual malice about other charges. Masson's reservation of state tort rules for injury, causation and damages remains intact.

**Application and source limits:** The Vancouver material concerns a particular exchange as a source of church funds and particular takeover/stock/property conduct. Those are factual assertions capable of adding a distinct accusation, even within an article characterizing the organization as financially predatory. The appellate court invoked the general characterization rather than resolving all relevant statement-specific elements. Its panel did not decide the independent of-and-concerning defense; a single judge's footnote cannot be treated as its alternative holding. Discovery's focus on actual malice does not authorize this Court to resolve deferred falsity issues. Prior actual-malice dismissals remain their own judgments, not transferable mental-state findings. Nominal damages do not remove the conceded public-figure fault requirement; the limited grant supplies no separate amendment entitlement.
