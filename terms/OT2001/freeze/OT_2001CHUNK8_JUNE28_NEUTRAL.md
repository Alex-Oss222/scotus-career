### Issue 1: Authority and the preliminary showing for selection discovery

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Armstrong holds that Rule 16’s defense-preparation materiality provision does not authorize this type of selective-prosecution discovery. * **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The discovery showing is below a prima facie merits case. * **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment equal protection; the event-date Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure 16 and 17; the actual federal charging and death-authorization framework.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** United States v. Armstrong assumes an appropriate discovery opportunity may exist without establishing an independent source; supervision and sanctions do not create the missing duty. A bare racial numerator without the relevant comparison did not satisfy that case.
* **Open Point:** Whether these statistics reliably connect racial treatment to the challenged charging or death decision, and whether the request identifies a lawful source for any resulting inquiry.

### Issue 2: Consequences of an unsupported demand

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Armstrong sets aside an unsupported discovery order and its dependent dismissal, with ordinary proceedings on remand. It neither directs renewed inquiry automatically nor bars a later properly supported request.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** A sanction dependent on a disclosure obligation fails when the obligation has not been lawfully established. Maintain the distinction between failure at the discovery threshold and a merits ruling that discrimination did not occur.
* **Other Governing Law:** The District Court’s death-notice order and ordinary pretrial jurisdiction.
* **Material Other Authority:** Protective measures and staged production can regulate an otherwise valid inquiry; they cannot cure a complete absence of authority or the required evidentiary connection.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** No holding authorizes automatic capital punishment, decides guilt, or fixes the remedy for a proved selective-prosecution violation.
* **Open Point:** The appropriate correction of the death-notice order and the scope of further ordinary proceedings.
