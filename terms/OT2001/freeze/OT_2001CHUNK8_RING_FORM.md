# Ring: form and propositions before assignment

The plenary, argued merits decision warrants one signed lead opinion with distinct parts. It expressly resolves the capital boundary reserved in Apprendi, displaces Walton's contrary allocation, and provides a qualified error-review mandate. It remains separate from Harris because eligibility and the State's preserved harmlessness argument present different questions. These choices precede consideration of author counts.

Part I requires the jury to find a present-offense aggravating fact necessary to authorize death beyond a reasonable doubt, subject to valid element admission and the existing lawful jury-waiver boundary. It overrules Walton only as to contrary necessary-aggravator allocation. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg support the eligibility rule. The actual case has neither an admission nor a jury waiver; no new waiver rule is adopted.

Part II applies qualified Neder review, placing the burden on the State to establish noncontribution to the actual verdict beyond a reasonable doubt; it permits supported whole-record inferences subject to actual-instructions, required-findings, material-conflict and no-new-credibility constraints. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas support this complete method and conditional sentence consequence. Scalia and Ginsburg permit only genuine equivalent jury findings and withhold this broader method.

Part III reverses the appellate death-sentence affirmance and remands, preserving the conviction and leaving the State's actual harmlessness contention for proper application below. Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg join that bounded appellate judgment. Breyer supplies a seventh reversal-and-remand vote independently on Eighth Amendment jury-selection grounds, without joining the Sixth Amendment or broader harmlessness rationale. Stone-Zsela instead reverses the judge-only constitutional ruling, vacates the appellate affirmance and remands for the same initial qualified harmlessness inquiry; his approved formal vacatur is not counted as reversal of the affirmance. O'Connor would affirm while seeking reconsideration of Apprendi and retaining Walton. Neither proposed appellate disposition itself unconditionally vacates the death sentence.

The partial joins and distinct formal dispositions require a topology table and component judgment presentation. Stone-Zsela joins Parts I and II, with a separate concurrence in part and dissent in part preserving his appellate-vacatur remedy; he does not join Part III. No combination of Breyer's rationale with the Sixth Amendment opinion creates additional law. No fallback supplies a new rationale. The form and governing propositions are fixed independently of authorship or counts. This initial-draft review corrects Stone's formal disposition before a fresh assignment; no frozen non-Stone commitment changes.
