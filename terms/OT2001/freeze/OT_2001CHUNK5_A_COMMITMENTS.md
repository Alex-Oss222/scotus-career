## 65. Verizon Communications Inc. v. FCC

### Questions and operative alternatives

**Pricing: sustain the challenged methodology.** Reverse the Eighth Circuit's invalidation of §51.505(b)(1), retain the rejection of an exclusively historical-cost requirement, and remand for implementation of the methodology judgment. Section 252(d)(1) permits the Commission to measure the cost of supplying an element through forward-looking efficient technology and configuration, anchored to existing wire-center locations. It does not require reproduction of the incumbent's embedded investment. The binding rulemaking authority is established by AT&T; Mead and Barnhart require real delegation and its exercise, both present here. Permission to include reasonable profit is not a separate statutory command to reimburse all past costs. The rule's capital cost, economic depreciation and reasonable common-cost components make the price a cost estimate rather than a zero-cost access entitlement.

The strongest objection is that an entrant rents actual incumbent assets at a price computed from a network the incumbent does not possess, potentially rewarding continued leasing and undermining facilities investment. The sustaining answer is limited: Congress left room to choose the economic cost measure, and the retained wire centers plus element and common-cost recovery furnish a rational connection to supplying the service. Neither the wisdom of a different transition policy nor a hypothesized loss proves the adopted measure unlawful. Actual confiscation remains a separate rate-result question.

The sustaining commitments include the rule's actual cost qualifications: total element long-run incremental cost plus a reasonable allocation of forward-looking common costs; current efficient technology and the lowest-cost configuration at existing wire-center locations; forward-looking capital cost and economic depreciation; an element-specific stand-alone-cost ceiling; and total common-cost allocations equaling the forward-looking common costs of the network, excluding retail costs. Embedded, retail and opportunity costs and revenues subsidizing other services are excluded from this methodology. The incumbent must substantiate rates by a compliant cost study before the state commission. Rates outside the proxy ranges or above proxy ceilings require full and fair effect to the prescribed methodology in a proceeding with notice, comment opportunity and a written reviewable factual record containing the cost study. These conditions govern implementation without converting permitted reasonable profit into a guarantee of every historical expenditure.

**Pricing: retain the partial vacatur on reasoned implementation grounds.** Affirm the result setting aside §51.505(b)(1), but require agency reconsideration of its efficient-network benchmark instead of a judicial command to reimburse embedded costs. Forward-looking costs are permissible; the distinct objection is that this mandatory combination of best available technology, lowest-cost configuration and incumbent-specific access lacks a sufficient answer to the effect on replacement investment and competitive facilities construction. AT&T's preserved necessity/impairment limits and Breyer's public explanation objection make this a supported alternative. The strongest counterargument is that §252 rejects traditional rate-based proceedings, the FCC supplied competition and investment reasons, and courts do not select their preferred economic model. This path must identify the particular inadequacy in those reasons, not substitute historical cost or an essential-facilities doctrine.

**Constitutional component.** Reject the requested facial confiscation-based invalidation or saving construction. There is no adjudicated complete rate and financial record establishing a confiscatory end result. The incumbent cost-study obligation and state rate process remain operative. Hope and Duquesne, as supplied by the lower opinion, address the consequences of the rate rather than an entitlement to one methodology; the simulated AT&T ripeness holdings likewise distinguish a method or general announcement from an actual rate injury. This is not a declaration that every TELRIC rate is constitutional.

**New combinations: restore the challenged statutory implementation.** Reverse the categorical invalidation of §51.315(c)–(f). AT&T Part XI already rejects exclusive assignment of every physical combination task to the requester. Providing nondiscriminatory access in a manner that permits combination can include compensated functions needed to assemble authorized elements; paragraph (d) also implements connection with requester-held elements. The rule does not select the elements to which access must be given. Section 251(d)(2)'s independent necessity and impairment requirements therefore remain prior access constraints, not conditions displaced by technical feasibility.

Paragraph (c) requires both technical feasibility and no impairment of other carriers' access or interconnection. Paragraph (d) independently requires technical feasibility for combining unbundled elements with requester-held elements. Paragraph (e) places proof of claimed infeasibility under (c)(1) or (d) on the incumbent before the state commission; paragraph (f) separately places proof of claimed other-carrier impairment under (c)(2) on that incumbent. Those burdens make the stated limitations usable where the incumbent controls the network information. They do not establish a new element entitlement. Section 251(c)(3)'s just, reasonable and nondiscriminatory rates and terms and §252's cost framework govern compensation. The explicit (c)(2) condition is not inserted into (d).

The strongest objection is that a command covering every requested feasible combination can supply assembly services beyond what is necessary for nondiscriminatory access. Compensation and useful competition cannot alone create statutory authority. The sustaining answer is the actual delegated access duty, read with the statutory limits on available elements and lawful terms; the rule governs functions necessary to combine those elements rather than an entitlement to an unrestricted network design. No entrant-incapability prerequisite is added, and no source is said to establish one.

**New combinations: corrected-standard remand without final restoration.** Vacate the Eighth Circuit's categorical exclusive-task judgment and remand for actual rule-specific review of the affirmative duties and proof allocations. This accepts the controlling Part XI construction while declining to treat it as approval of (c)–(f). The lower court rested on the already-rejected exclusive-task premise. Its opinion does not independently examine whether the full reach of the duties and burdens is a reasonable, adequately explained implementation. The contrary argument is that the statute, rule conditions and nondiscrimination rationale suffice for the Court to resolve the presented facial statutory objection now. This alternative requires no new hearing, new proof threshold, or predetermined invalidation; it preserves the existing Rule (b) protection and returns the actual remaining rule questions under the correct framework.

## 66. Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union

### Reach, alternatives and governing rule

**Reviewed component:** Whether the contemporary-community-standards feature independently warrants the Third Circuit's categorical facial conclusion. The preliminary posture leaves live alternative grounds; it does not authorize a final declaration that all of COPA is valid. All eight accept reachability of this pre-enforcement speech challenge and distinguish statutory commercial purpose from commercial advertising.

**Path to vacatur and remand.** Vacate the appellate judgment insofar as its categorical community-standards rationale supplies the basis, remand for the whole-statute overbreadth and tailoring questions, and preserve the existing preliminary injunction during that consideration. Geographic variation is a serious burden, but its presence alone does not establish substantial unconstitutional reach without evaluating the offense's complete definition and operation. Unlike the CDA categories in Reno, COPA expressly includes whole-material, specified sexual-content, prurience and serious-value limitations, together with separate knowing-commercial-posting requirements and defenses. Miller supplies the framework's relevance; Pope separates serious value from local majority acceptance. Neither establishes that every harmful-to-minors provision on the Web is constitutional.

The strongest contrary case is concrete, not merely abstract: the unchallenged preliminary findings say speakers cannot geographically exclude visitors and verification burdens cost, anonymity and adult access. Thus local prosecution may force a national speaker to conform to restrictive communities. Those facts must remain operative on remand. The answer supporting vacatur is that the lower appellate court's isolated-feature rule does not determine the coverage produced by all conjunctive elements, the proper construction, or the comparative effectiveness and burdens of alternatives. An ordinary objective serious-value inquiry does not itself prove complete narrow tailoring. Reno and Playboy keep the ultimate justification burden on the Government for protected adult speech.

**Path to affirmance.** Affirm the preliminary injunction because a federal criminal standard varying by community, applied to an inseparable national distribution system on the established technological facts, materially chills adult expression outside the community that finds it harmful to minors. Serious value for minors is not the measure of an adult's full constitutional protection. The age-verification defense shifts costs and access burdens to the speaker without eliminating the underlying national restriction. This path is not a judgment that Congress can never protect minors online; it treats the actual geographic reach and adult burden as enough for the present preliminary relief.

Its strongest counterargument is that constitutional obscenity doctrine already tolerates community variation, Congress added narrower elements than the CDA, and substantial overbreadth cannot be presumed from one phrase. The affirming answer is that conventional addressed distribution and obscene-to-adults material do not eliminate the distinct national burden on nonobscene adult Web expression, and the developed preliminary findings show that burden here.

**Construction boundary:** No common national-community construction is adopted in this handoff. Such a construction cannot be invented solely to save the law. The remand position permits consideration of a construction supported by ordinary interpretation and the whole statute; it does not order a national standard or hold that a local standard is automatically lawful. The complete statutory defenses and privacy restrictions remain as written. Section (c)(1)'s good-faith access-restriction defense, (c)(2)'s separate cause-of-action protection and (d)'s disclosure/security duties and exceptions are not interchangeable cures.

## 67. Lapides v. Board of Regents of University System of Georgia

### Components, competing rules and remedy

**Waiver rule supported for this controversy:** A State that authorizes its representative voluntarily to remove an action to federal court relinquishes its federal-forum immunity from state-law claims for which it has already permitted adjudication in its own courts. The legal consequence follows from the actual federal litigation choice; it does not depend on a separate state enactment empowering counsel to announce an abstract waiver. Reverse the Eleventh Circuit's contrary immunity determination and remand for the lawful jurisdictional and claim-specific proceedings that remain.

Clark and Gardner establish that actual voluntary invocation of federal adjudication can carry the corresponding obligation to submit to adjudication. College Savings rejects consent manufactured from ordinary commerce but preserves actual submission; Florida Prepaid Part IV repeats that distinction. Schacht deliberately leaves removal waiver open. Raygor concerns litigation consistent with an expressly retained defense in a forum the State did not choose by removal, so it does not compel immunity here. Georgia's express state-court-only tort waiver means that the legislature did not waive federal-forum immunity simply by enacting the GTCA. It does not make the later authorized choice of federal court ineffective for federal procedural purposes.

**Strongest contrary path:** Affirm because Georgia expressly reserved federal immunity and its Attorney General lacks separate state-law authority to surrender it; defense in a forum chosen for federal questions is different from filing an affirmative claim. Ford Motor Co. supplies the state-authority concern and the demanding consent tradition makes convenience an inadequate substitute. The waiver response is that choosing the federal tribunal is itself the material act, unlike ordinary defense of a suit brought there by another party. A State may retain its state substantive defenses, but cannot obtain removal of the same permitted tort claims and invoke only the tribunal's federal character to bar their adjudication. This treats removal as a voluntary litigation submission, not as a penalty for conducting lawful ordinary activity.

**Scope alternative tested:** A universal rule waiving every conceivable immunity for every removed claim would extend beyond the state-permitted tort claims that provide the operative controversy. It would encompass claims whose underlying immunity was never surrendered. No Associate needs that extension here. Conversely, limiting the rule to a State filing its own affirmative claim would preserve the inconsistent tribunal positions presented by deliberate removal and would not adequately explain the voluntary-submission distinction.

**Separate federal cause and jurisdiction:** Will's rule that the State is not a §1983 damages person remains separate from forum immunity. Removal waiver does not cure that statutory defect. Failure of that claim on its merits does not by itself establish absence of a substantial federal question at removal, and the individually pleaded claims must be treated on their own terms. The remand requires examination of the actual jurisdictional anchors and proper treatment of the remaining claims. If original jurisdiction is absent, the applicable remand requirement governs. If original jurisdiction existed but the federal claims are dismissed, supplemental retention or remand follows the ordinary statutory rules. No automatic compulsory retention follows from the waiver holding, and no fictional §1983 merits trial is ordered against the State.

## 68. Voting Integrity Project, Inc. v. Keisling

### Distinct statutory components and alternatives

**Congressional elections:** Affirm the judgment rejecting preemption under then-2 U.S.C. §§1 and 7. Those provisions fix the legally completed selection of the Senator or Representative on the federal election day; they do not require every contributing act of ballot preparation and casting to occur on that day. Oregon permits continuing participation through election day, requires timely ballot receipt, and makes no earlier final legal selection. Foster's rule against a consummated October selection therefore remains intact. The size of the early-voting share is not a statutory substitute for the time of final selection.

**Presidential electors:** Independently affirm under then-3 U.S.C. §1. Appointment of the electors occurs through the State's completed selection on the federal day, not through each voter's earlier transmission of a ballot. Foster's congressional holding provides a pertinent completion distinction but did not already decide this statute. The statute's appointment language and the separately enacted presidential absentee provisions support reading advance participation consistently with the common appointment day. The quoted §1973aa-1(d) requirement concerns qualified residents absent from their district or unit on election day; subsection (g) permits less restrictive practices. Those provisions are not recast as an express, unrestricted authorization of every early-voting system for every federal office.

**Strongest contrary path:** Reverse as to federal contests because the statutes select a single day, the individual's vote is a decisive electoral act, and Congress considered and rejected continued multiday polling. This path takes the text and reported legislative history seriously. Its difficulty is that it treats any early voter act as the completed election while leaving Congress's permitted and required absentee voting unexplained. Distinguishing all universal mail ballots from all absentee ballots solely by the voter's inconvenience would add a qualification absent from the timing enactments and inconsistent with the less-restrictive-practices provision's role. A court cannot erase the congressional date, but neither should it extend the date command from selection to every step without statutory support.

**Counterargument to affirmance tested:** Calling an election a process could conceal an early final result or leave election day as a ceremonial formality. The supported Oregon facts avoid that problem: voting and receipt remain legally available through the federal deadline, and no earlier result becomes final. The rule depends on that legal operation. It does not authorize an earlier consummated election merely because certification is delayed. The old multiday-polling debates are relevant context, but their rejection does not eliminate the operative distinction reinforced by later absentee legislation and Foster's actual rule.

**Remedy:** Affirm the defense summary judgment against this statutory preemption claim. Preserve the receipt-by-election-day system and election-day deposit opportunity as described; enter no replacement calendar or ballot procedure. Post-election receipt, a legally final early selection, fraud, secrecy and unequal-treatment claims are outside the supplied questions and record. The holding does not transform subsequent counting or certification into an extension of the ballot-receipt period.
