# Atkins v. Virginia — opinion form

Event: 2002-06-20.

Form: Signed opinion of the Court on the categorical constitutional rule and its application to a disputed status predicate. One governing coalition supports the rule and remand; the disagreement concerns the substantive constitutional boundary, warranting a signed merits explanation..

### Execution is unavailable for established intellectual disability

**Holding and operative rule:** The Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments prohibit execution of a person with established intellectual disability: significantly impaired intellectual functioning, substantial adaptive limitations and developmental onset. The State must provide a reliable procedure to determine that status; a score, diagnostic label or courtroom impression alone does not establish it.

**Authority:** @AUTHOR@'s opinion, joined by @JOINS@: seven Justices adopt the entire rule, explanation and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The most severe punishment requires culpability proportionate to its severity. Trop supplies the inquiry into developing standards; Coker and Enmund require attention to the punishment's relation to culpability and legitimate penal purposes. The sustained domestic legislative movement since Penry v. Lynaugh supplies objective support: eighteen of thirty-eight death-penalty States enacted restrictions, alongside twelve States without capital punishment. This evidence is directional, not eighteen identical statutes or a mechanical count. Eleven enactments have temporal limits; New York retains a correctional-custody murder exception; Kansas uses a distinct definition. A vetoed Texas bill is not an enactment, and reported low-IQ executions do not establish complete clinical diagnoses. Those qualifications do not erase the consistent movement or infrequency of executing the class. Clinical evidence explains diminished reasoning, judgment and ability to learn from experience, which weaken retributive and deterrent justifications. The same limitations impede assistance to counsel and reliable presentation and can make disability appear aggravating. Individual mitigation therefore does not fully answer the class-wide culpability and reliability problems. Polls, foreign practice and organizational views are unnecessary to this conclusion. Penry's earlier assessment of insufficient consensus no longer sustains categorical permission. Atkins's conflicting expert evidence requires application of the constitutional rule through reliable individual factfinding. The Court establishes no numerical cutoff, proof burden or universal hearing design; those details are not resolved on this disputed record.

**Precedent treatment:** Penry v. Lynaugh, 492 U.S. 302 (1989), is overruled only insofar as it permits execution of persons with established intellectual disability. Penry v. Johnson retains its requirement of effective mitigation where death remains lawful.
