# Alameda Books: full historical source verification

Source-only verification for reconciliation, not an independent commitment or an assignment. The operator read all of the official 535 U.S. 425–466 text, including every writing and footnote, from the saved UTF-8 official text in contiguous segments. No Stone position was consulted.

Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-535/pdf/USREPORTS-535-425.pdf. Saved text: [official text](../sources/chunk4-control/alameda-official.txt).

- Historical disposition: reverse and remand, five to four. O'Connor plurality joined by Rehnquist, Scalia, Thomas; Scalia concurrence; Kennedy concurrence in judgment. Souter dissent joined throughout by Stevens and Ginsburg, and by Breyer in Part II only. These are comparator facts, not automatically adopted votes or authorship.
- The decision concerns respondents' summary judgment on the substantial-interest evidentiary issue. The prior spacing ordinance was assumed valid. The study found a correlation between concentrations and crime, not a demonstrated property-value relationship. Single combined bookstores/arcades were treated as single establishments by the study. Each respondent occupied less than 3,000 square feet and one entrance; no current violation of the prior spacing rule was alleged.
- O'Connor accepts reasonable inferences from the concentration study to multiple operations under one roof. Evidence must fairly support the municipality's actual rationale. A plaintiff can cast direct doubt by exposing that evidentiary failure or furnishing contradictory evidence; the city must then renew support with supplemental evidence. Neither conclusive proof nor an exclusive causal explanation is required at the initial stage. This leaves trial challenges open.
- The plurality does not decide the content-neutrality issue the court of appeals assumed. It declines to overhaul Renton, outside the question presented, and does not decide reliance on Hart Book Stores or whether the city consulted that outside study before enactment. The court of appeals' no-ban determination was not cross-petitioned. The plurality accepts as unobjectionable Kennedy's rule against reducing speech to reduce secondary effects, treating it as the difference between a time/place/manner regulation and a ban.
- Kennedy calls the ordinance content based, but allows intermediate scrutiny for genuine zoning that reduces external harms while leaving the quantity and accessibility of speech substantially intact. A city cannot justify proportionate harm reduction by suppressing speech or audience. The premise must be dispersal rather than closure, with significant harm reduction and little speech loss. Common experience and the study suffice initially; contrary proof at trial could defeat the ordinance. His hypothetical patron counts are illustrations, not record findings.
- Scalia joins the plurality's application of existing doctrine and separately maintains that the business of pandering sex can constitutionally be suppressed. That separate proposition is not a five-Justice holding.
- Souter Part I, with Stevens and Ginsburg but not Breyer, explains content-correlated regulation and favors O'Brien's formulation, empirical safeguards against disguised viewpoint suppression, and an evidence-sensitive role for common experience. Part II, joined also by Breyer, finds no evidentiary link between these isolated traditionally combined businesses and crime, or between splitting them and reducing crime. It distinguishes relocation from compelled division, notes doubled overhead and potential closure, and says experiments cannot replace the required factual connection.
- All nine dissent footnotes were read. Footnotes 1 and 7 leave challenges to the original concentration premise unaddressed; 2 discusses O'Brien; 3 gives the commercial-speech evidentiary analogy; 4 explains existing Los Angeles open-booth/lighting rules and why Hart's sanitation evidence is not necessarily relevant; 5–6 distinguish concentrated businesses and isolated establishments; 8 disputes Kennedy's patron illustration and notes the commercial uncertainty of free booths; 9 rejects compartmentalizing motive from evidence when the city relies only on the study.

The historical opinion does not establish later trial findings, validity in every application, new factual studies, or a final permit entitlement. Reconciliation must test any proposed departure against the relevant Justice's actual grounds and the present neutral record.
