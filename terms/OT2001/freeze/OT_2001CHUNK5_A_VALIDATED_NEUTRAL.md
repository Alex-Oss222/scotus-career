## 65. Verizon Communications Inc. v. FCC

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Verizon Communications Inc. v. FCC
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 467; Nos. 00-511, 00-555, 00-587, 00-590, and 00-602.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-13. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Telecommunications Act permits the FCC to prescribe forward-looking network-element rates based on efficient current technology and configuration, and whether it permits rules requiring compensated combinations of network elements for entrants.
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** On remand in the consolidated regulatory challenge, the Eighth Circuit sustained forward-looking costing in principle but invalidated the FCC methodology insofar as it used an efficient hypothetical network rather than the incumbent's actual network. It also invalidated additional combination rules. Incumbents and federal and entrant parties seek review of different components.
* **Relief Requested:** Incumbents seek invalidation of the methodology and assembly duties; the FCC and entrants seek restoration of the challenged rules.
* **Material Record:** The Act requires incumbent local exchange carriers to provide network elements to entrants. The FCC methodology estimates total element long-run incremental cost using the most efficient currently available telecommunications technology and lowest-cost configuration while retaining the incumbent's existing wire-center locations. It adds a reasonable allocation of forward-looking common costs. Incumbents argue that actual sunk investment and the costs of an entire replacement network cannot be recovered under that benchmark. The challenged combination rules impose the distinct duties and proof rules stated in Issue 2 below, subject to applicable statutory rates and terms. This is review of a methodology and regulatory duties, not adjudication of a particular incumbent's complete rate order or an established confiscatory loss.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The prior simulated decision in AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board establishes FCC implementation authority and preserves a separate inquiry into the legality of the specific rules. Do not reopen those resolved propositions. An as-applied takings challenge requires an actual rate and financial record; this facial methodology dispute supplies no finding that every lawful rate generated by the method is confiscatory.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument occurred October 10, 2001. The merits event is May 13, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The five consolidated historical dockets are one listed matter. Verizon Maryland and Mathias concern separate later judicial-review disputes.
* **Participation Matter:** All nine participate at argument and decision; no independently established exclusion is supplied.

### Issue 1: Pricing authority and methodology

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board recognizes the FCC’s general implementation authority, including pricing rules, while leaving this methodology unresolved. It does not authorize the agency to erase the statute’s cost and nondiscrimination requirements. Section 252(d)(1)(B) permits a reasonable profit; the word may is not a separate mandatory-profit command.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify delegated authority and statutory limits independently; then assess the reasoned exercise of authority within those limits. A national rule may coexist with state implementation. Economic disagreement does not itself prove absence of delegated power.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Telecommunications Act of 1996 provisions in the official 2000 Code: 47 U.S.C. §§251(c)(2)–(3), 251(d)(2), 252(d)(1), and the applicable review provisions; original Rules 51.505 and 51.315 are copied in the source supplement. Section 252(d)(1) requires just and reasonable, nondiscriminatory rates based on the cost of providing the interconnection or element, determined without reference to a rate-of-return or other rate-based proceeding, and permits a reasonable profit.
* **Material Other Authority:** Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council supplies the received statutory-administration framework, subject to any authenticated intervening simulated change. FPC v. Hope Natural Gas Co. informs the distinction between methodology and unconstitutional rate results.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The FCC may prescribe a national pricing methodology. Section 252(d)(1)(A)(i) requires cost to be determined without reference to a rate-of-return or other rate-based proceeding. Neither proposition settles whether every forward-looking network assumption is permissible.
* **Open Point:** Whether efficient technology with existing wire centers reasonably measures the cost of supplying network elements and whether the present facial record establishes constitutional confiscation.

### Issue 2: Additional combination duties

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board preserves existing combinations and the independent necessity-and-impairment inquiry. Its Part XI also holds that §251(c)(3) does not unambiguously assign every physical combination task exclusively to the requesting carrier, and vacates the prior invalidation of (c)–(f) for corrected-standard reconsideration without approving those duties. The present question therefore concerns actual statutory relation, conditions, burdens and agency reasons; an exclusive-task reading already rejected by that holding cannot be treated as undecided entering law.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Functional network elements are not limited to physical pieces. An implementation rule must remain connected to the statutory access duty and its limiting conditions; competitive access does not itself authorize every requested service.
* **Other Governing Law:** Sections 251(c)(3), 251(d)(2) and 252 preserve authorized-element access, just and reasonable nondiscriminatory terms, lawful costing and the statutory access criteria. The exact original §51.315 text is in the source supplement. Paragraph (c), upon request, requires functions necessary to combine unbundled elements even when not ordinarily combined, only if technically feasible and not impairing other carriers' access to unbundled elements or interconnection. Paragraph (d), separately, requires functions to combine unbundled elements with elements the requester possesses in any technically feasible manner. Paragraph (e) places proof of infeasibility for a denial under (c)(1) or (d) on the incumbent before the state commission; (f) separately places proof of other-carrier impairment for a denial under (c)(2) on that incumbent. The explicit (c)(2) condition is not textually repeated in (d); applicable statutory nondiscrimination limits remain distinct. These provisions do not establish that every element is available, that the entrant is actually incapable of combining, or that a particular configuration or rate is approved.
* **Material Other Authority:** The prior simulated network decision is the principal authority; no later historical telecommunications decision supplies missing simulated law.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Incumbents cannot sabotage the lease of existing combinations. Whether they must perform new assembly work is distinct.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statute permits bounded, compensated combination work necessary to make authorized access usable.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Eighth Circuit distinguished prospective costing of the incumbent’s existing network from constructing a hypothetical efficient network. It treated the latter as insufficiently tied to the statutory cost of providing the leased element. It read §251(c)(3) as assigning combination to the requesting carrier, sustaining the vacatur of (c)–(f). That reported lower rationale must be tested against the prior controlling Part XI, which rejects unambiguous exclusive task allocation; it is not itself current Supreme Court law.

**Incumbents' position.** Congress required access to their facilities, not a price based on facilities no one has built. A benchmark that excludes actual investment can discourage replacement and require uncompensated access. They also argue that supplying unbundled elements does not require assembling a competitor’s network.

**FCC and entrants' position.** Historical monopoly costs would entrench incumbent inefficiency and defeat competition. Current technology, fixed existing wire centers, shared costs, and reasonable profit together create an economic estimate rather than a fictional free network. The Act can require work needed to make the access obligation effective, with compensation under the applicable statutory rate-and-terms provisions. A suggested limit based on work the entrant cannot perform is an analytical possibility unless the actual order or a supported agency construction supplies it; the original rule text alone does not state that limit.

**Limits and unreached matters.** The federal position must preserve statutory prerequisites for which elements are obtainable. Upholding a price methodology cannot decide every actual rate or eliminate a supported confiscation claim. Upholding assembly rules must retain each paragraph's actual feasibility condition, the separate (c)(2) protection, the (e)–(f) proof duties, and statutory nondiscrimination; those are not interchangeable conditions.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain the methodology while leaving actual rates subject to statutory and constitutional review, or invalidate unsupported efficient-network assumptions and return the rule to the agency. A facial constitutional ruling for incumbents would require more than the possibility of an inadequate future rate. The combination rules can be considered separately: restoration must apply each paragraph's actual conditions and statutory compensation framework; a supported invalidation or corrected-standard remand would leave existing-combination protection intact. A categorical exclusive-task rationale is already foreclosed by the prior controlling holding, but particular duties and burdens remain open.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A statutory holding may permit forward-looking efficient-cost models anchored to the existing wire-center layout without requiring that model in every regulatory scheme. A narrower invalidation could target assumptions that lack reasoned cost support rather than mandate historical-cost reimbursement. A combination holding may recognize authority for work necessary for usable access while retaining the distinct text, proof duties and statutory terms identified above; alternatively the Court may require further agency consideration if the actual justification cannot sustain the particular duty. None of these paths establishes a general right to an entrant’s preferred network design.

**Uncertainty:** No carrier-specific complete rate record establishes an actual taking. The source materials must be distinguished between the original order, the prior simulated decision, and the challenged remand judgment.

## 66. Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 564; No. 00-1293.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-13. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Child Online Protection Act is facially unconstitutional because its definition of material harmful to minors incorporates contemporary community standards for commercial material on the World Wide Web.
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit affirmed a preliminary injunction against enforcement, treating the community-standards feature as independently fatal. The district court had relied on additional First Amendment defects.
* **Relief Requested:** The Government seeks reversal of the community-standards basis for the injunction; the challengers seek continued protection against enforcement.
* **Material Record:** COPA regulates knowing commercial World Wide Web communications available to any person under seventeen, with knowledge of the material's character, in interstate or foreign commerce. Its harmful-to-minors definition covers obscenity or the conjunctive three-part definition: prurient appeal or pandering, patent offensiveness as to the enumerated sexual subject matter, and lack of serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value, with the statutory whole-material and minors qualifications. The contemporary-community-standards phrase appears expressly in the prurience clause. The complete elements, exclusions, defenses and privacy qualifications are in the incorporated official text; a commercial-purpose communication is not automatically commercial advertising. Speakers challenged the law before enforcement. The Web allows geographically dispersed access, making a local-community benchmark potentially restrictive for a national publisher. The record also raises technological, financial, privacy, and adult-access burdens of verification. The appellate judgment did not finally resolve every definition, defense, technological claim, or less-restrictive alternative. The requested review therefore concerns a particular rationale for an interim injunction, not a final conviction or a complete trial record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Facial overbreadth and chilling injury permit review of a criminal speech restriction before prosecution where the plaintiffs show the necessary exposure. The preliminary-injunction posture matters: rejecting one legal reason does not establish the statute’s validity or require dissolution of an injunction supported by another adequate ground. No concession about the Government's precise Supreme Court prayer is independently verified in the permitted source files; the remedial proposition here rests on the preliminary posture and the unresolved lower grounds, not on an inferred concession.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument occurred November 28, 2001. The supplied decision event is May 13, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Free Speech Coalition concerns virtual and promoted-as-child depictions under separate criminal definitions. COPA concerns commercial web material harmful to minors and the challenged community-standards component, with different elements, defenses and adult-access burdens.
* **Participation Matter:** All nine participate at the applicable argument or submission milestone and decision; no independently established exclusion is supplied.

### Issue 1: Community standards and national Internet speech

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union rejects broad criminal suppression of protected adult Internet speech under a poorly bounded minors-protection rule. It does not hold that every use of community standards in an Internet statute is unconstitutional.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the regulated expression, the protected adult speech burden, the statutory limiting elements, and practicable alternatives. The medium’s actual characteristics matter, but neither a new medium nor protection of minors supplies an automatic constitutional exemption.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment; official 47 U.S.C. §231 as printed in the 2000 Code and copied in the source supplement; preliminary-injunction and substantial-overbreadth requirements. Under §231(e)(2), profit need not occur and the activity need not be the sole or principal business, but a regular trade-or-business course with a profit objective and knowing posting or solicitation is required. Section (b)'s carrier, access-provider, locator and specified passive-intermediary exclusions are limited to the stated capacities, and its deletion exception preserves qualifying good-faith restrictions. Section (c)(1) is an affirmative defense for good-faith restriction by a listed credit/debit or adult-access method, age-verifying digital certificate, or other reasonable feasible available-technology measure. Section (c)(2)'s protection from a cause of action for otherwise lawful good-faith protective activity is separate; it is not a substitute for (c)(1)'s terms. Section (d) restricts disclosure and requires protection against unauthorized access, subject to consent and its separate necessary-business/communication and court-order exceptions. The original text controls every qualification.
* **Engine §2 enactment-predicate check:** COPA was enacted to address the concerns underlying Reno's invalidation of the CDA. The supplied lower opinion, paragraphs 21–26 and 58–59, quotes the contemporaneous committee reports to that effect. The complete governing Reno entry likewise invalidates the CDA indecency and patently-offensive branches for uncertain, substantially overbroad adult-speech burdens and inadequate defenses/tailoring, while leaving more precise future controls open. That relevant legislative predicate is present here. The supplied sources establish no displaced invalidation or contrary validating rule requiring a user selection on whether COPA exists. This conclusion concerns the enactment's availability only; it supplies no presumption of COPA's constitutionality.
* **Material Other Authority:** Miller v. California provides the obscenity framework from which Congress drew portions of COPA. Ginsberg v. New York concerns material harmful to minors, without deciding nationwide Web geography. Their standards must not be detached from their actual subjects.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Protected adult speech cannot be broadly reduced to what is suitable for children. The presence of a community standard does not itself establish how broadly COPA operates when its other elements and defenses apply.
* **Open Point:** Whether this feature alone justifies facial invalidation and whether a permissible construction can avoid an uncontrolled national lowest-common-denominator restriction.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Third Circuit regarded the borderless Web as making a local-community definition intolerable, because speakers could not reliably confine access to permissive communities. It treated that defect as enough to affirm without disposing of all other grounds.

**Government’s position.** Community standards appear within a narrowed commercial-Web statute with multiple elements and age-verification defenses. Geographic variation alone does not prove substantial overbreadth. The Government seeks rejection of the categorical rationale and further review of the actual statute, not a universal rule that child protection outweighs adult expression.

**Challengers' position.** A national speaker cannot avoid exposure in restrictive jurisdictions, so local standards predictably suppress lawful adult material everywhere. Verification imposes costs and anonymity burdens. COPA’s contextual elements do not necessarily solve those burdens, and less restrictive user-side measures may protect children more effectively.

**Limits and alternative grounds.** Even if community standards are not independently fatal, the full definition, age range, defenses, and alternative protections remain material. A national standard is a possible construction only if the statutory text and ordinary interpretation support it; a court cannot simply rewrite the offense.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reject the categorical community-standards ground and remand while retaining the injunction for consideration of the unresolved grounds. It may affirm if this feature, properly construed with the whole statute, independently establishes substantial unconstitutional reach. A merits declaration that COPA is wholly valid would exceed the reason actually reviewed and the incomplete adjudication below.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow holding may establish that geographic variation alone does not facially invalidate a carefully defined Internet offense. It leaves proof of adult burdens, verification feasibility, and less restrictive means open. An affirmance could establish that a national medium combined with unavoidable exposure to restrictive local standards creates substantial protected-speech suppression under this statute. Either rule must account for the statute’s commercial scope and additional elements; neither constitutionalizes every Internet business model or makes all nationwide regulation impossible.

**Uncertainty:** The district court made findings after five days of testimony, accepted by the court of appeals as unchallenged for clear error: publishers could not geographically exclude Web visitors with then-existing technology; operative verification systems imposed costs, reorganization and anonymity burdens; filtering was both over- and underinclusive. These are findings on the preliminary record, not proof about every future technology. The appellate court left the further issues in note 19 unresolved, including commercial scope, content breadth, defense burdens and placement, criminal penalties, chat rooms/discussion groups/links, foreign access, the whole-material unit, and age differences among minors. Its notes 16 and 24 express skepticism about parental filtering as a governmental alternative; that lower reasoning must be assessed under the actual governing Reno and Playboy holdings, not adopted as a binding exclusion of user-side measures.

## 67. Lapides v. Board of Regents of University System of Georgia

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lapides v. Board of Regents of University System of Georgia
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 613; No. 01-298.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-13. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a State that voluntarily removes an action to federal court may invoke sovereign immunity against state-law claims for which it has waived immunity in its own courts.
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The district court treated Georgia’s removal as a waiver of the asserted federal-forum immunity. The Eleventh Circuit reversed that determination. Lapides seeks reversal of the appellate immunity judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Lapides seeks rejection of the State’s immunity defense after removal; Georgia seeks preservation of that defense.
* **Material Record:** A university professor alleged that state officials placed allegations of sexual harassment in his personnel files in violation of state law and his federal rights. He sued in Georgia court, asserting state tort claims and federal civil-rights claims against the Board and individual officials. The State joined in removing the action and then asserted immunity from federal adjudication. Georgia had waived immunity from the relevant tort claims in its own courts. The asserted federal damages claim against the State also confronts the separate rule that a State is not a person under the federal civil-rights statute. Allegations against individual officials cannot simply be merged with the Board’s immunity defense. No adjudicated tort liability or damages amount follows from the removal issue.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The viable state-law claims and the State’s own litigation act provide the concrete controversy. Waiver of immunity does not create a federal cause of action or decide original jurisdiction. Failure on a federal claim's merits is not automatically absence of a substantial federal question; Steel Co. and Schacht keep the inquiries distinct. If federal claims are dismissed, the district court must separately consider lawful treatment of supplemental claims; if original jurisdiction is absent, the applicable remand command remains. The authority of the State’s litigation representative to remove is not a license to invent an unauthorized private waiver.

* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate listed brief.

### Issue 1: Voluntary removal and immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board rejects consent manufactured from ordinary commercial activity. Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht separates an immunity defense to a claim from removal jurisdiction over the action and leaves the waiver question open. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank and Alden v. Maine do not recognize the historical categorical Article I immunity barrier.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Consent, statutory authorization, constitutional power, forum jurisdiction, and the merits of a cause of action remain separate. Actual litigation conduct differs from an implied waiver inferred merely from participation in regulated commerce.
* **Other Governing Law:** Removal and supplemental-jurisdiction statutes; the applicable Georgia tort waiver; the federal civil-rights statute; the constitutional limits on federal adjudication.
* **Material Other Authority:** Clark v. Barnard and Gardner v. New Jersey concern voluntary invocation of federal adjudication. Will v. Michigan Department of State Police addresses the separate absence of a statutory damages defendant in a suit against the State.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The registers reject a fictional commercial waiver and prevent one immune claim from destroying jurisdiction over an otherwise removable action. They do not determine the effect of the State’s deliberate removal here.
* **Open Point:** Whether voluntary removal relinquishes a federal-forum immunity defense to otherwise permitted state tort claims, and the scope of that relinquishment.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Eleventh Circuit distinguished the State’s choice of forum from an authorized waiver of sovereign immunity. The district court treated removal followed by an immunity objection as inconsistent positions concerning the same adjudication.

**Lapides’s position.** A State cannot choose federal adjudication of claims it permits in state court and then deny the chosen tribunal authority solely because it is federal. The requested rule attaches to an actual litigation choice, not presumed consent to regulation.

**Georgia’s position.** State waiver rules determine who may surrender immunity, and a litigation representative’s removal should not enlarge substantive liability or eliminate sovereign protections beyond authorized terms. The State also points to defects in the federal claim.

**Limits and alternatives.** Lapides need not establish a universal waiver of all state immunity. Georgia’s objection to an invalid federal damages cause remains available. The Court can resolve the forum inconsistency without deciding immunity from claims Georgia has not permitted in its own courts or the personal defenses of individual officials.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would preserve Georgia’s immunity defense despite its removal. Reversal on waiver would remove that federal-forum immunity ground and return the case for the jurisdictional and other proceedings lawfully remaining. It would not compel retention of supplemental claims after federal claims are dismissed, create a cause of action, settle substantive defenses, or establish liability. The Court may dispose of the invalid State-directed federal damages claim separately; that would not automatically answer the state-law removal-waiver issue.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A waiver rule can be confined to a State’s authorized voluntary removal of claims for which it already accepts adjudication in its own courts. A broader rule covering every removed claim would require attention to claims whose substantive immunity was never surrendered and is not necessary here. A no-waiver rule would permit the State to preserve an immunity defense while using removal, but would need to explain why its voluntary invocation differs from other litigation submissions to federal adjudication.

**Uncertainty:** The effect on supplemental jurisdiction depends on which federal claims remain. The packet does not resolve the professor’s allegations or imply that waiver alone establishes a remedy.

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Foster v. Love holds that a State cannot consummate the final legal selection of federal officeholders before the federally prescribed election day. Its rule does not make every preliminary electoral act unlawful.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Ninth Circuit recognized substantial textual and legislative-history force in the Project's single-day reading, including Congress's rejection of a proposed continuation of multiday voting. It distinguished Foster's early final legal selection, and treated Congress's express presidential absentee-voting provision and permission for less restrictive practices, together with the longstanding absentee practice, as the decisive basis for reading the statutes harmoniously. Its reasoning was not merely that elections are processes. The complete governing Foster entry controls the scope of that precedent here.

**Project’s position.** An individual makes the decisive choice when casting a ballot, and a common federal day loses force if most votes are cast earlier. Congress could have expressly authorized Oregon’s universal mail system if it intended such a departure. The requested rule treats advance voting itself as the prohibited election.

**State’s position.** Elections are processes. Ballot preparation, absentee voting, and other advance steps have long preceded the chosen date without electing an officeholder early. The system leaves the legally operative choice open until the election-day deadline. Its rule preserves Congress’s common completion date while allowing States to administer how qualified voters participate.

**Necessary limits.** The State cannot use advance voting to make an earlier result legally final or cancel the federally required election. The Project’s concerns about late developments influencing early voters do not by themselves establish statutory preemption.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would leave Oregon’s system in force against this statutory claim. Reversal would require relief directed to advance voting in federal elections, with attention to lawful implementation rather than automatic invalidation of all state elections. A constitutional judgment on access, secrecy, fraud, or equality would exceed the issue and record.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court may hold that advance ballot casting is permissible when the State does not consummate the federal election before the uniform date and timely receipt is required by that date. This covers comparable early and absentee voting without deciding post-election receipt or early final certification. The competing interpretation would require all decisive voter acts on the federal date, a rule whose implications for absentee voting cannot be avoided by calling Oregon’s system unusual.

**Uncertainty:** The inquiry assumes the described receipt and final-selection rules. Proof that a particular procedure legally fixes the winner before election day would present a different application.

 §231. Restriction of access by minors to materials commercially distributed by means of World Wide Web that are harmful to minors 

 (a) Requirement to restrict access 
 (1) Prohibited conduct 
 Whoever knowingly and with knowledge of the character of the material, in interstate or foreign commerce by means of the World Wide Web, makes any communication for commercial purposes that is available to any minor and that includes any material that is harmful to minors shall be fined not more than $50,000, imprisoned not more than 6 months, or both. 
 (2) Intentional violations 
 In addition to the penalties under paragraph (1), whoever intentionally violates such paragraph shall be subject to a fine of not more than $50,000 for each violation. For purposes of this paragraph, each day of violation shall constitute a separate violation. 
 (3) Civil penalty 
 In addition to the penalties under paragraphs (1) and (2), whoever violates paragraph (1) shall be subject to a civil penalty of not more than $50,000 for each violation. For purposes of this paragraph, each day of violation shall constitute a separate violation. 
 (b) Inapplicability of carriers and other service providers 
 For purposes of subsection (a) of this section, a person shall not be considered to make any communication for commercial purposes to the extent that such person is— 
 (1) a telecommunications carrier engaged in the provision of a telecommunications service; 
 (2) a person engaged in the business of providing an Internet access service; 
 (3) a person engaged in the business of providing an Internet information location tool; or 
 (4) similarly engaged in the transmission, storage, retrieval, hosting, formatting, or translation (or any combination thereof) of a communication made by another person, without selection or alteration of the content of the communication, except that such person's deletion of a particular communication or material made by another person in a manner consistent with subsection (c) of this section or section 230 of this title shall not constitute such selection or alteration of the content of the communication. 
 (c) Affirmative defense 
 (1) Defense 
 It is an affirmative defense to prosecution under this section that the defendant, in good faith, has restricted access by minors to material that is harmful to minors— 
 (A) by requiring use of a credit card, debit account, adult access code, or adult personal identification number; 
 (B) by accepting a digital certificate that verifies age; or 
 (C) by any other reasonable measures that are feasible under available technology. 
 (2) Protection for use of defenses 
 No cause of action may be brought in any court or administrative agency against any person on account of any activity that is not in violation of any law punishable by criminal or civil penalty, and that the person has taken in good faith to implement a defense authorized under this subsection or otherwise to restrict or prevent the transmission of, or access to, a communication specified in this section. 
 (d) Privacy protection requirements 
 (1) Disclosure of information limited 
 A person making a communication described in subsection (a) of this section— 
 (A) shall not disclose any information collected for the purposes of restricting access to such communications to individuals 17 years of age or older without the prior written or electronic consent of— 
 (i) the individual concerned, if the individual is an adult; or 
 (ii) the individual's parent or guardian, if the individual is under 17 years of age; and 

 (B) shall take such actions as are necessary to prevent unauthorized access to such information by a person other than the person making such communication and the recipient of such communication. 
 (2) Exceptions 
 A person making a communication described in subsection (a) of this section may disclose such information if the disclosure is— 
 (A) necessary to make the communication or conduct a legitimate business activity related to making the communication; or 
 (B) made pursuant to a court order authorizing such disclosure. 
 (e) Definitions 
 For purposes of this subsection, 1 the following definitions shall apply: 
 (1) By means of the World Wide Web 
 The term “by means of the World Wide Web” means by placement of material in a computer server-based file archive so that it is publicly accessible, over the Internet, using hypertext transfer protocol or any successor protocol. 
 (2) Commercial purposes; engaged in the business 
 (A) Commercial purposes 
 A person shall be considered to make a communication for commercial purposes only if such person is engaged in the business of making such communications. 
 (B) Engaged in the business 
 The term “engaged in the business” means that the person who makes a communication, or offers to make a communication, by means of the World Wide Web, that includes any material that is harmful to minors, devotes time, attention, or labor to such activities, as a regular course of such person's trade or business, with the objective of earning a profit as a result of such activities (although it is not necessary that the person make a profit or that the making or offering to make such communications be the person's sole or principal business or source of income). A person may be considered to be engaged in the business of making, by means of the World Wide Web, communications for commercial purposes that include material that is harmful to minors, only if the person knowingly causes the material that is harmful to minors to be posted on the World Wide Web or knowingly solicits such material to be posted on the World Wide Web. 
 (3) Internet 
 The term “Internet” means the combination of computer facilities and electromagnetic transmission media, and related equipment and software, comprising the interconnected worldwide network of computer networks that employ the Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol or any successor protocol to transmit information. 
 (4) Internet access service 
 The term “Internet access service” means a service that enables users to access content, information, electronic mail, or other services offered over the Internet, and may also include access to proprietary content, information, and other services as part of a package of services offered to consumers. Such term does not include telecommunications services. 
 (5) Internet information location tool 
 The term “Internet information location tool” means a service that refers or links users to an online location on the World Wide Web. Such term includes directories, indices, references, pointers, and hypertext links. 
 (6) Material that is harmful to minors 
 The term “material that is harmful to minors” means any communication, picture, image, graphic image file, article, recording, writing, or other matter of any kind that is obscene or that— 
 (A) the average person, applying contemporary community standards, would find, taking the material as a whole and with respect to minors, is designed to appeal to, or is designed to pander to, the prurient interest; 
 (B) depicts, describes, or represents, in a manner patently offensive with respect to minors, an actual or simulated sexual act or sexual contact, an actual or simulated normal or perverted sexual act, or a lewd exhibition of the genitals or post-pubescent female breast; and 
 (C) taken as a whole, lacks serious literary, artistic, political, or scientific value for minors. 
 (7) Minor 
 The term “minor” means any person under 17 years of age.
