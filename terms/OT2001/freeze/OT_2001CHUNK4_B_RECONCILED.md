### Component rules and boundaries

**Appearance definition — five full joins.** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm invalidation of §2256(8)(B) for substantial overbreadth. A criminal definition may not suppress substantial nonobscene depictions made without exploiting an actual minor merely because they appear to depict a minor in sexually explicit conduct. Ferber's production and continuing victim injuries do not arise merely from an invented image or an adult performance. Miller's obscenity requirements cannot be replaced by apparent age, and the definition does not preserve serious literary, artistic, political or scientific value. Making an image realistically photographic does not itself supply an exploited child. Actual exploitation, obscenity, solicitation and other independently unlawful conduct retain their own elements.

The strongest contrary case is that realistic substitutes aid grooming, stimulate demand, sustain exchange markets and complicate proof that real children were depicted. The five recognize those interests but find insufficient fit between those predicted harms and this suppression of substantial protected expression. The mere possibility of unlawful later use is not itself production injury; uncertainty in an actual-child prosecution does not justify transferring that category's reach to all protected substitutes. This is not a demand for scientific certainty or a finding that grooming never happens. No independent affirmative-defense burden holding is needed.

**Presentation definition — five full joins, O'Connor separate agreement, Thomas judgment-only.** The same five would affirm invalidation of §2256(8)(D) for substantial overbreadth. Ginzburg allows consideration of pandering within an obscenity inquiry; it does not make the underlying nonobscene work contraband merely because another person describes it as child pornography. This definition operates through connected handling and possession offenses and is not enacted as an independent unlawful-offer offense limited to its intentional promoter. The five reject rewriting it to add those limits. O'Connor separately agrees in this invalidation because the Government supplies no compelling justification adequately explaining this presentation category's protected reach; the adult-performer defense also falls away when the work is presented as depicting minors. Thomas agrees in the judgment without a full principal-opinion join. No Justice's disposition on (D) is inferred from his or her position on (B).

**Thomas's reservation.** Thomas would affirm both invalidations but does not join the categorical suggestion that protecting enforcement against unprotected speech can never justify a restriction affecting otherwise protected expression. The enforcement difficulty is the Government's strongest contention, but the current predictive premise does not demonstrate the technological obstacle needed to support this breadth. If technology later makes proof of real-child production practically impossible, an appropriate affirmative defense or another narrowly drawn restriction may present a different constitutional question. He neither requires such future legislation to use one particular defense nor approves a current presumption shifting an actual-child element. His assent remains judgment-only to the principal opinion; the five's broader explanation cannot be attributed to him through his remedy vote.

**O'Connor's divided appearance disposition.** O'Connor would affirm (B)'s invalidation as applied to youthful-looking actual adults but reverse the facial invalidation insofar as it prevents enforcement against wholly computer-generated realistic images virtually indistinguishable from actual-child images. The limited adult-performer defense excludes possession and therefore does not remove the substantial protected reach affecting adult performers. The virtual application instead directly implicates Congress's predictions concerning the effectiveness of real-child enforcement. Under the narrow realistic-image construction, cartoons, sculptures and other obviously unreal depictions are outside that rationale. She would not require Congress to wait for the predicted proof failure to occur before acting; on this construction the child-protection and enforcement interests suffice, and substantial protected reach relative to that application's legitimate sweep has not been established. Broadrick and application-severability principles permit preserving that application while invalidating the adult-performer reach. She does not create a new categorical class of unprotected speech, extend the defense to possession, or conclude that every apparent-age image is constitutional to ban. Her vagueness conclusion is confined to the realistic-image construction.

Her strongest contrary argument is that even realistic nonobscene images harm no child in production and can have serious expressive value. She answers through the narrow application, compelling enforcement interest and Congress's permissible prediction, rather than denying First Amendment protection as a category. Her earlier Reno emphasis on severing a textually supportable application is directly relevant; the five disagree with her constitutional assessment of this remaining application, not with her desire to preserve genuine adult access.

**Scalia's narrower sustaining disposition.** Scalia would reverse invalidation of both definitions. He construes sexually explicit conduct to require the defined actual or realistically simulated hardcore conduct, rather than mere sexual suggestion, and reads apparent minority to require realistic images virtually indistinguishable from images of actual minors. The serious films invoked against the statute cannot be assumed to depict that defined conduct merely because their plots involve minors and sex. He accepts O'Connor's virtual-image enforcement rationale while declining her adult-performer and presentation invalidations. For (D), he would confine liability to knowing pandering of a depiction as child pornography, reading the offense's scienter together with the definition and excluding innocent downstream possession. Ginzburg makes the actor's deliberate presentation relevant to this narrower transaction. He supplies no legislative-history rationale from the paragraph he declined to join historically.

The strongest objection is that these limitations alter a definition used in connected offenses rather than interpret an offense limited to the promoter, and that the statute lacks a serious-value exception. Scalia regards the narrowed conduct, apparent-age and knowing-pandering readings as permissible interpretations of enacted terms; the five regard the presentation limitation as rewriting the offense and reject his breadth assessment. His X-Citement textual objection is not abandoned: no absent affirmative defense is inserted and §2252A(c) is not expanded. His prior simulated Playboy premise prevents treating all nonobscene adult material as unprotected; his sustaining judgment rests on the narrow construction and sufficiently close child-enforcement connection, not an obscenity-only shortcut. This is a genuine interpretive disagreement, not an invented change in record facts.

**Statutory qualifications applicable to every row.** A minor is under eighteen. Sexually explicit conduct means actual or simulated intercourse (genital-genital, oral-genital, anal-genital or oral-anal, whether same or opposite sex), bestiality, masturbation, sadistic or masochistic abuse, or lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area; mere nudity does not itself satisfy the last category. Section 2252A(c) applies only to (a)(1)–(4), requires actual performers who were adults when the material was made, and requires that the defendant did not advertise, promote, present, describe or distribute it as depicting minors. It does not protect wholly generated images or ordinary possession under (a)(5). Section 2252A(d) independently applies to possession of fewer than three images when the defendant promptly and in good faith, without retaining or allowing anyone other than law enforcement access to any image or copy, takes reasonable steps to destroy each image or reports it and affords law-enforcement access to each image. Reasonable destruction steps are the statutory condition; successful destruction is not an additional requirement. That defense is not a right to keep protected expression. The unchallenged (C) category concerns an identifiable actual person recognizable by face, likeness or another distinguishing characteristic, who was a minor when the depiction was created, adapted or modified or whose image as a minor was used; proof of the person's actual identity is unnecessary. No row adjudicates (C)'s validity.

## 56 — United States v. Craft — April 17, 2002

### Historical comparison and provisional correction

The official 535 U.S. 274 opinion authenticates argument on January 14, 2002 and decision on April 17, 2002. Its application involved an existing unilateral power to accept or redirect value; neither that holding nor Stevens's separate Palazzolo claimant-injury distinction supplies a changed rule treating this marital estate as separate individual property. Stevens's provisional reliance on the majority's plausible federal-classification argument was an alternative legal choice, not a concrete changed premise explaining abandonment of his historical statutory dissent. His row is corrected to no attachment.

The remaining five attachment rows and Scalia/Thomas no-attachment rows are confirmed by the same intact premises. The attachment side does not rely simply on a state exemption being ineffective; it identifies actual individual benefits. The contrary side does not treat every state label as conclusive; it argues that federal law cannot create the antecedent ownership the statute requires. The longstanding collection treatment invoked by that dissent corroborates its reading but does not replace the statutory inquiry. Neither a current income amount, a later death, nor a new fraudulent-transfer fact is imported from the comparator.

**Separate position preserved:** Scalia, with Thomas, additionally stresses the institutional protection an entireties estate provides a nondebtor spouse, including a spouse whose domestic work does not produce a separate income. Removing that protection through a new federal classification is a reason for statutory caution. Stevens joins the statutory no-attachment reasoning without joining this additional account. This is a distinct permissible separate-writing function, not an assignment, a finding about this wife's work, or a general exemption for all marital assets.

### Competing rules and remedies

O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the Sixth Circuit's attachment ruling and remand. Stevens, Scalia and Thomas would affirm that ruling. All eight preserve the distinction among attachment, valuation, priority, sale authority, nondebtor ownership and the excluded fraudulent-transfer theory.

**Attachment rule supported by five.** After demand and nonpayment, §6321 reaches a taxpayer's existing state-created rights that together amount to property or rights to property, even when the State protects the marital estate from ordinary separate creditors and requires the other spouse's consent to disposition. Michigan gives this taxpayer present use and enjoyment, exclusion of strangers, survivorship and a necessary role in a joint conveyance and its proceeds. Federal classification is not controlled by the state-law label that the marital estate is indivisible. No one of those incidents is made a universal sufficient test; their existing economic substance together supports attachment. The lien reaches the taxpayer's rights before the conveyance to his wife. The absence of unilateral alienation limits the interest and its possible value, but does not erase its present benefits.

Drye applies the state-definition/federal-classification division. National Bank of Commerce illustrates that local creditor restrictions do not settle the federal lien question; Bess separates the reachable economic right from other interests in the same arrangement. Drye's unilateral accept-or-redirect power is powerful evidence of property, not an exclusive requirement silently added to §6321. Rodgers concerns enforcement and the nondebtor's protections; it supplies neither a half-value rule nor permission to take the wife's interest. Romani preserves specific priority rules after attachment and is not an entireties exemption.

**No-attachment rule supported by three.** Section 6321 attaches property belonging to the delinquent person; it does not create an individual property estate from rights belonging to an inseparable marital ownership. On Michigan's terms the husband cannot unilaterally withdraw, sell, partition or redirect the estate. Drye and National Bank of Commerce concerned actual unilateral powers over value already held by the taxpayer. Joint enjoyment and a veto on conveyance do not establish such separate ownership here. Federal law may disregard a creditor exemption after identifying a taxpayer's property, but may not manufacture the antecedent individual asset. This ground would affirm the reviewed attachment judgment without deciding what another tenancy, a completed severance or a separately owned income right would entail.

**Alternative-path testing.** The attachment path's strongest difficulty is identifying a real individual interest without collecting what belongs to the wife. Its answer is a lien on the existing bundle only, with separate valuation and statutory enforcement safeguards; no automatic half-share is recognized. The no-attachment path's strongest difficulty is that the taxpayer enjoys substantial present economic benefits and can prevent their disposition, so treating the state unity label as dispositive risks letting state collection policy defeat federal classification. Its answer must rest on Michigan's actual inseparability and lack of unilateral extraction, not on the bare immunity of the property from ordinary creditors. The three no-attachment Justices retain that narrower account; they do not deny every restricted or nontransferable interest can be federal property.

## 57 — Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency — April 23, 2002

### Historical comparison and bounded dissent

The official 535 U.S. 302 opinion authenticates argument on January 7, 2002 and decision on April 23, 2002. ### Competing categorical rules

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm rejection of the categorical claim. Scalia and Thomas would reverse that rejection and remand for the complete-deprivation inquiry, background-law defenses and the actual compensable period. Neither side reopens the abandoned Penn Central theory.

**Rule supported by six.** A temporary development restraint does not establish a categorical taking merely because it prevents all economically beneficial use during the interval selected by the claimant. For this regulatory moratorium claim the relevant continuing ownership may not be sliced into the precise period of the restriction to manufacture total deprivation. Lucas addresses complete deprivation of the relevant property; it did not decide every temporal denominator. Penn Central, Keystone and Andrus preserve attention to the whole interest rather than a selected burdened strand. First English requires compensation for the period of an already established taking; it does not establish that every temporary development prohibition is one. Physical occupation and appropriation of a leasehold are different governmental acts, not answers to the regulatory denominator question.

This rule creates no immunity for temporary restraints. Duration, impact, actual investment-backed expectations and the action's character can make a restriction compensable under the proper contextual inquiry; a nominal temporary label cannot convert indefinite destruction of the owner's interest into an ordinary planning delay. There is no fixed thirty-two-month safe harbor, automatic environmental exception or universal duration threshold. Here the owners lost their Penn Central theory below, did not pursue it and disavowed it. The Court therefore affirms the categorical judgment without remanding that abandoned claim.

**Rule supported by two.** A regulation that deprives an owner of every economically beneficial use for a defined, substantial period can take the use of property for that period even though ownership and possible future use remain afterward. The operative complete deprivation, not permanent extinction of title, supplies the Lucas trigger; subsequent restoration limits compensation rather than cancelling the loss. First English's compensation rule and Lucas's actual-taking-period remedy disfavor making planned expiration a categorical escape. This position does not impose compensation for every permit-processing interval: the challenged enacted prohibitions, the asserted complete loss and the thirty-two-month period distinguish routine administration. No invented minimum duration or broad per se delay rule is adopted.

**Two-path test.** The six-Justice path must answer the real economic value of present use and the risk of serial temporary labels. It does so by preserving contextual review and refusing to treat a label as conclusive, while distinguishing a taking's existence from its compensation period. The two-Justice path must answer why the claimant may define the relevant property by the restriction's duration when spatial subdivision cannot always produce a total loss. It treats the government's complete suspension of use as a taking of the distinct present-use period, not a selection of an otherwise useful physical fraction. Its remand still requires a sustained complete-deprivation finding and genuine antecedent property or nuisance limitations. Neither side treats beneficial environmental purpose as a substitute for the taking inquiry.

### Governing statutory structure and qualifications

Section 110(a)(2)(D) requires adequate plan provisions, consistent with Title I, prohibiting sources or other emissions activity from emitting a pollutant in amounts that significantly contribute to another State's nonattainment or interfere with its maintenance of the applicable national primary or secondary standard. The adjacent deterioration/visibility branch and the distinct requirements to assure compliance with §§115 and 126 remain operative in their own settings; their presence is not a decision of a new claim under them.

Section 110(a)(2)(A) requires enforceable emission limits and other control measures, means or techniques, including economic incentives such as fees, marketable permits and auctions of emissions rights, with necessary or appropriate schedules and timetables. It confirms the implementation setting and the range of control methods. It is not, standing alone, permission to redefine a statutory air-quality objective. Under §110(k)(3), EPA must approve an entire submission if it meets all applicable requirements. EPA may approve a compliant portion and disapprove another portion, but the revision is not treated as meeting the Act's requirements until the entire revision is approved. That mandatory approval structure protects state choice within the national requirements.

Section 110(k)(5) operates after EPA finds substantial inadequacy to attain or maintain the relevant national standard, adequately mitigate the identified interstate transport, or otherwise comply with the Act. EPA must require the revision necessary to correct the inadequacy, must notify the State and make its findings and notice public, and may set a reasonable submission deadline no later than eighteen months after notice. To the extent EPA considers appropriate, the finding subjects the State to the requirements applicable when it developed and submitted the deficient plan; EPA may adjust applicable dates, but may not adjust a Part D attainment date unless that date has elapsed. None of those qualifications is replaced by a judicial deadline in this case.

Section 110(c)(1) requires a federal implementation plan within two years after EPA finds a missing required submission or failure to satisfy the minimum completeness criteria, or disapproves a submission wholly or partly, unless the State corrects the deficiency and obtains approval before the federal plan is promulgated. The federal plan is an actual statutory alternative to state administration; this case makes no new finding that a particular trigger has occurred. Section 301(a)(1) permits regulations necessary to carry out EPA's statutory functions. That power and the specific interstate and plan-review assignments supply an actual rulemaking delegation, but no authority to add a different environmental objective.

### Issue I — May control cost inform the reduction required from a contributing State?

#### Six-Justice sufficient rule

After EPA establishes a State's relevant interstate contribution through air-quality analysis, §110(a)(2)(D)(i)(I) permits it to consider the availability of highly cost-effective controls in determining the emissions reduction required to eliminate the contribution it treats as significant under this implementation provision. The statute does not require cost consideration, benefits exceeding costs, or a general cost-benefit balance. Cost alone cannot establish the interstate contribution or authorize regulation of unrelated emissions; the national air-quality objective and the demonstrated interstate relation remain necessary boundaries.

The text requires judgment about significance within an implementation program; it supplies neither a numerical physical-contribution cutoff nor a single required method for selecting the reducible amount. Congress assigned EPA to judge plan adequacy and correct interstate deficiencies, while expressly contemplating diverse control techniques. Read together, those provisions leave room for a reasonable implementation judgment incorporating control availability and expense after the relevant pollution connection is established. Mead's actual-delegation threshold is satisfied by the specific plan-review and interstate duties together with §301 rulemaking exercised through this binding rule. Chevron then permits the agency's reasonable resolution of the remaining implementation ambiguity. Formal procedure alone would not create that power, and the conclusion does not rest solely on the absence of an express cost prohibition.

EPA's two-stage method matters: it first used magnitude, frequency and relative contribution of modeled interstate effects to identify contributing States, then used highly cost-effective controls to identify the required reduction. The cost inquiry therefore supplements an established air-quality connection rather than creating one. The six would sustain that legal role for cost; they would not independently find every modeling result correct, make $2,000 per ton a statutory or constitutional safe harbor, or decide an unpresented challenge to the numerical selection's explanation.

Whitman governs §109's health- and welfare-based national standards and expressly preserves provision-specific implementation questions. It neither bars all cost consideration under §110 nor itself authorizes this particular use. Union Electric's refusal to make economic infeasibility a ground for rejecting a qualifying state plan does not decide the different federal interstate-adequacy judgment. Train preserves initial state selection of source controls within the statutory result. Arkansas v. Oklahoma confirms that a demonstrated interstate problem can receive authorized federal implementation, while MCI prevents administrative policy from replacing an actual statutory command. The rule here must remain within the enacted interstate condition; none of those cases supports a general presumption of federal power over anything environmentally useful.

#### Two-Justice sufficient contrary rule

Scalia and Thomas read the phrase describing amounts that contribute significantly to nonattainment or interfere with maintenance as a pollution criterion. Cost measures the expense of eliminating emissions, not the amount's contribution to another State's failure to meet a standard. Congress's express recognition of economic methods gives States ways of meeting a required result; it does not authorize EPA to redefine that result by how cheaply controls can be installed. Once the statutory question is resolved at that level, Chevron supplies no authority to substitute the agency's preferred cost criterion.

They would require EPA to identify the prohibited contribution under the enacted air-quality criterion and reconsider the budget determination that substituted highly cost-effective removability for that criterion. They do not require elimination of every molecule of interstate pollution or a unique judicially chosen significance number. Neither would forbid every consideration of expense in choosing means under an independently authorized implementation provision. They refuse to preserve this same budget by simply renaming the stage at which its cost formula operates.

#### Adversarial test and join boundaries

The sustaining path's strongest objection is Sentelle's distinction between physical contribution and control expense: calling the measure implementation does not permit it to alter an emissions-defined duty. The six answer with the provision's delegated judgment concerning which contribution is significant in a multi-state implementation setting, the prior physical connection and the retained statutory air-quality objective. They must therefore reject a rationale resting only on an assumption that cost is always permitted unless forbidden. The contrary path's strongest objection is that significance itself calls for evaluative choice and Congress supplied an implementation framework expressly accommodating economic techniques. The two dissenters answer that evaluative choice must concern the enacted pollution relationship, not a different measure of significance selected from policy preferences. Their remedy is agency reconsideration, not judicial replacement.

No Justice adopts a rule that Whitman's national-standard cost exclusion automatically decides §110. No Justice treats cost-effectiveness and benefit-cost balancing as synonyms. The exact benchmark's independent reasonableness is outside this supplied review, not a factual gap filled by the Court.

### Issue II — Do statewide NOx budgets unlawfully displace the States' choice of measures?

All eight would reject the categorical challenge to a lawful statewide emissions budget, with Scalia and Thomas expressly withholding approval of the cost-derived amount for the reasons above. Train gives States initial responsibility to select source-by-source limitations and other measures that satisfy the Act; EPA may enforce the applicable result but may not displace that choice merely because it prefers a particular source program. A statewide budget does not itself cross that line when the State retains real options concerning which sources and measures will supply the necessary reduction.

The record supplies actual options: stationary or mobile-source measures, participation in EPA's optional trading program, a State's own trading program, or no trading program. The model program's particular source requirements attach to the State's election to use that option; they do not govern every State independently of its choice. The prohibition on replacing required NOx reductions with VOC controls does not show a forbidden federal means choice on this record: EPA and OTAG concluded VOC reductions would not adequately address the long-distance transport problem for which the budget was required. States retain authority to add VOC controls for associated local disbenefits. The Court does not make every pollutant-specific demand lawful by definition or sustain nominal freedom consisting only of permission to spend more.

The strongest contrary path is that a tightly set budget and the inability to choose VOC reductions effectively determine state control policy, regardless of optional trading. Its answer is the actual range of retained source and program choices and the supported pollutant-specific distinction. No supplied fact shows that only one source plan can meet the budget. The Court may rely on those attributed findings; it may not invent technical impossibility or assume every future budget preserves meaningful alternatives.

The constitutional structure reinforces, but does not replace, this statutory analysis. New York v. United States and Hodel distinguish a lawful federal regulatory alternative from a command that an unwilling State legislate or assume private liabilities. Section 110(c)(1)'s federal plan mechanism supplies the relevant alternative when its statutory triggers occur. FERC v. Mississippi retains its cooperative-regulation scope. Neither that alternative nor the absence of commandeering would authorize EPA to remove the greater choice Train and the statute preserve. The eight reject the present categorical state-choice objection on the actual statutory options; no freestanding expansion of constitutional federal power is necessary.

### Issue III — Does Congress supply sufficient guidance for the authorized cost-informed implementation?

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would sustain the delegation under the governing intelligible-principle doctrine. The principle comes from Congress: identified air pollutants governed by national standards, a demonstrable interstate emissions relationship, nonattainment and maintenance endpoints, significant contribution, adequate plan provisions consistent with Title I, and defined plan-review and correction functions. These connected limits identify the subject, purpose and boundaries of the delegated judgment. The six expressly reject the lower court's suggestion that a relatively confined exercise of power to date can cure missing legislative guidance. The present rule is sustained constitutionally because the statute provides sufficient limits, independently of those choices. A particular number can still be unexplained or unlawful under ordinary review even when the underlying delegation is constitutional; this ruling adjudicates no unreviewed numerical-method challenge.

The strongest contrary constitutional path argues that permitting expense to define significant contribution lets the agency choose its own regulatory ambition without a congressional stopping point. The six answer that cost does not become an independent statutory objective or jurisdictional predicate: it operates inside the enacted interstate air-quality task, after the contribution is shown and subject to the mandatory adequacy and consistency requirements. That conclusion applies the received doctrine, not a claim that any public-interest instruction would suffice. No court-created price cap is used as constitutional repair.

Scalia and Thomas do **not reach** this constitutional question after their statutory-reversal conclusion. ### Shared statutory and constitutional conclusions

All eight Associates would affirm the Second Circuit's judgment sustaining the permanent injunction at its actual posting and knowing-purposeful-linking scope. The conclusions concern the functioning DeCSS tool and the proved dissemination, not every description of cryptography, scientific publication or hyperlink. The agreement below is direct at the stated propositions; it is not a general rejection of constitutional fair-use or public-domain claims.

**Statutory coverage.** Section 1201(a)(2) independently prohibits trafficking in technology primarily designed or produced to circumvent effective access control, technology with only limited commercially significant other purpose or use, or technology knowingly marketed by the person or a concerted actor for that circumvention. These are alternative predicates, not cumulative conditions. Effective control requires authorized information, process or treatment in the measure's ordinary operation; circumvention includes decryption or bypass without the copyright owner's authority. The demonstrated DeCSS posting supplies the designed-circumvention application. Section 1201(c)(1) preserves rights and defenses to infringement, including §107 fair use; it does not insert a general fair-use trafficking defense into (a)(2). Subsection (c)(2) likewise leaves contributory and vicarious infringement law separate. This judgment does not equate every circumvention with infringement.

**Exceptions and precise limits considered.** A class exemption under (a)(1)'s periodic rulemaking excuses the individual access prohibition at the specified scope; (a)(1)(E) prohibits using that exemption or rulemaking determination as a defense to other provisions, including trafficking. The nonprofit library/archive/educational acquisition exemption permits good-faith evaluation for acquisition only when an identical copy is not reasonably available in another form; retention must last no longer than necessary and the accessed copy cannot be used for another purpose. For a library or archives, its collections must be open to the public or available both to affiliated researchers and other persons researching a specialized field. It expressly supplies no (a)(2) or (b) trafficking defense. Reverse engineering under (f) concerns a lawfully obtained right to use a program and previously unavailable elements needed for interoperability of an independently created program; identification and analysis must be for that sole purpose and noninfringing. Necessary tools may be developed and employed for that analysis or interoperability, and information or means may be shared solely to enable that interoperability, without infringement or other illegality. Good-faith encryption research under (g) requires lawful acquisition of a published work, necessity, a good-faith attempt to obtain authorization and no infringement or other illegality. Tool development is for the researcher's own qualifying research; provision is to a collaborator for qualifying research or verification. The direction of dissemination, relevant expertise and notice of findings to the owner inform qualification. Security testing under (j) requires the owner or operator's authorization and the sole purpose of good-faith testing, investigation or correction of a system flaw; the act must not infringe or violate other applicable law. Qualification factors include whether information is used solely to promote the owner's or operator's security or shared directly with the developer, and whether its use or maintenance avoids facilitating infringement or other illegality, including privacy or security violations. Those are statutory factors, not silently converted categorical prerequisites. Developing, producing, distributing or employing tools is permitted solely for qualifying testing, provided the means do not otherwise violate (a)(2). No supplied finding establishes that these defendants meet one of those distinct permissions. Their existence is not a factual finding that every legitimate scientific use is accommodated, nor a license to decide unpresented constitutional applications.

**Code and posting.** Executable code can convey ideas to human readers and perform an operation. Its expressive character brings the First Amendment into the inquiry; its functional operation is not erased by that character. The particular application targets unauthorized circumvention of access protection, without preferring an idea about encryption or a message in the films. Turner and O'Brien therefore supply intermediate review of the statutory burden: a substantial interest independent of suppressing expression and no substantially excessive speech burden. Protection of digital access controls against the distributed working decryption tool materially advances the statutory interest. A rule confined to completed infringement would not address the distinct unauthorized-access injury Congress regulated. This judgment does not adopt neutrality merely from a benign purpose or declare that software is always conduct.

**Injunctive fit and linking.** Madsen supplies the additional requirement that a neutral speech-burdening injunction burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the proved violation. The posting restraint concerns the working tool, not abstract discussion. Linking receives a separate analysis because a link can communicate a source or assist access. This restraint is sustained on clear and convincing findings that the responsible person knows the prohibited material is on the linked site, knows it is technology that may not lawfully be offered, and creates or maintains the link for the purpose of disseminating it. On those findings, limiting posting while allowing purposeful direct delivery through a link would leave the same demonstrated trafficking function available. A mere citation, reporting link or uninformed referral is outside these terms. The Court does not make the three requirements and this proof quantum universal constitutional minima or statutory elements for every linking case; it sustains this actual protected scope. The Government's reserved objection to the proof standard requires no enlargement of the decree.

**Fair-use and limited-times constitutional claims.** The defendants assert no identified personal fair use of a movie, and the record contains scant evidence of the effect on prospective fair users. The assertion that every lawful use entails a constitutional entitlement to a preferred digital-copying method does not defeat this injunction; neither does the judgment hold that technologically obstructed fair use can never present a constitutional problem. The limited-times claim was presented only in a footnote below and lacks an allegation or evidence of a plaintiff withholding a public-domain work or an injunction preventing copying of one. Both independent limits support declining that abstract challenge here.
