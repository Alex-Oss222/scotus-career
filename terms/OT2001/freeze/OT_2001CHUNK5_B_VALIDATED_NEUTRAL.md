## 69. United States v. Cotton

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Cotton
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 625; No. 01-687.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-20. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether omission from the operative federal indictment of drug quantity needed to authorize the enhanced maximum requires correction of the enhanced sentences on an unpreserved claim, in light of the charging and jury omissions, the asserted strength of the quantity proof, and the separate conditions of Rule 52(b). This is a neutral paraphrase; it does not assume that the proof is legally uncontroverted or that notice cures either omission.
* **Question Granted:** The petition’s automatic-vacatur question, including the jurisdictional premise and application of plain-error review; no separate challenge to the conspiracy convictions is added.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit vacated the enhanced sentences and remanded with instructions to impose terms not exceeding twenty years. It applied plain-error review, treated the indictment defect as jurisdictional, and concluded that the grand-jury deprivation satisfied the fourth condition without regard to evidentiary strength. The United States seeks reversal. The partial dissent would deny correction under the fourth condition on the quantity evidence and notice shown.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks restoration of the sentences; defendants seek sentencing relief for the missing quantity allegation and finding.
* **Material Record:** The original indictment alleged at least five kilograms of cocaine and fifty grams of cocaine base. The superseding indictment changed the conspiracy period, added defendants, and alleged only a detectable amount; it is the operative indictment. The jury was expressly told that quantity was not important and returned no quantity finding. Both sides report seizures totaling approximately 380 grams of cocaine base. The Government also relies on cooperating-witness testimony, stipulated quantities of physical exhibits, and notice of life exposure. The defendants challenged credibility and the larger quantities attributed to individuals as reasonably foreseeable for Guidelines purposes. The sentencing judge, using a preponderance standard, attributed at least 1.5 kilograms to six respondents and over 500 grams to Matilda Hall, then imposed five life terms and two thirty-year terms. Those findings and exhibit stipulations do not themselves supply a grand-jury allegation, a petit-jury quantity verdict, or an admission of the aggravated offense.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The indictment and jury-quantity objections were not raised at trial; the respondents did raise sentencing quantity and credibility objections. Forfeiture is distinct from intentional waiver. Section 3231 supplies jurisdiction over federal offenses, while the parties dispute whether the operative charging omission removes authority to impose the enhanced sentences. Keep that question separate from error, plainness, substantial-rights effect and fourth-condition discretion. No preserved constitutional claim or total absence of a charging instrument is presented.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument occurred April 15, 2002. The supplied merits event is May 20, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Harris and Ring concern which aggravating facts require element procedures; Angle concerns sentencing constraints and consecutive terms. Cotton presents an omitted indictment allegation with no timely objection, so jurisdiction and Rule 52(b) correction are separate from the underlying jury-allocation question.
* **Participation Matter:** At argument or the authorized full merits submission, and again at the May 20, 2002 decision, all nine seated Justices participate: Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, and Breyer. ### Issue 1: Indictment omission and judicial power

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Apprendi v. New Jersey requires the jury to find beyond a reasonable doubt, or the defendant to admit, a fact necessary to increase the authorized statutory maximum. It does not declare every omission jurisdictional or settle state grand-jury incorporation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish legal authority to hear the class of case from a defect in the accusation and from an unauthorized sentence. The label jurisdiction does not replace analysis of the governing jurisdictional enactment.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments; federal criminal jurisdiction; the charged drug statutes; Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 52(b).
* **Material Other Authority:** Ex parte Bain supplies the older jurisdictional language the parties contest. Johnson v. United States addresses forfeited failure to submit a legally necessary matter to the jury.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Quantity needed for a higher statutory maximum is a jury fact under the authenticated simulated rule. The sentencing judge’s finding cannot itself enlarge the jury-authorized ceiling.
* **Open Point:** Whether the charging omission removes subject-matter jurisdiction or instead remains a nonjurisdictional constitutional error.

### Issue 2: Forfeited error and correction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Olano requires error, plainness, an effect on substantial rights, and a separate discretionary decision concerning fairness, integrity, or public reputation. Johnson v. United States denies correction of its particular forfeited materiality omission on the clear materiality of the source-of-funds account and the intervening change from settled trial law; it establishes no universal overwhelming-evidence exception.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Preserve the distinction between forfeiture and waiver and between prejudice and the fourth-stage discretion. Neither an actual-innocence requirement nor an automatic-reversal rule follows from Johnson.
* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 52(b) governs the forfeited claim on direct review.
* **Material Other Authority:** Apprendi v. New Jersey establishes the substantive error; it does not itself supply the appellate remedy.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Unpreserved constitutional jury errors are not categorically exempt from the ordinary correction inquiry.
* **Open Point:** How the strong quantity proof and the specific indictment omission bear on the fourth requirement in this record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Fourth Circuit applied all four plain-error conditions. Its jurisdictional characterization and protection of the independent grand-jury function supplied its reason to exercise correction discretion; it treated evidentiary strength as irrelevant to this indictment defect. The partial dissent rejected that categorical application and relied on the threshold evidence, notice, and the independent fourth condition.

**Government’s position.** Congress gave the district court power over federal criminal prosecutions. An indictment error does not withdraw that power. The Government characterizes the proof that the conspiracy involved at least fifty grams of cocaine base as overwhelming and uncontested, distinguishing objections to larger individual Guidelines quantities. It argues both lack of substantial-rights prejudice and, independently even if such prejudice is assumed, no warranted correction under the fourth condition. Its prediction about what a properly instructed grand or petit jury would have done remains an argument about appellate relief, not an actual finding.

**Defendants' position.** The operative grand-jury indictment never charged, and the petit jury never found, the fact required for the greater penalty. A lawful trial is not just a forecast that a properly instructed jury probably would have convicted of an aggravated offense. They request correction of sentences exceeding the authorized verdict, not acquittal of the conspiracy.

**Limiting principles.** Strong evidence does not make the trial error disappear. The Government cannot carry this argument into preserved claims or genuinely disputed quantities without the applicable separate analysis.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can reject the jurisdictional characterization while remanding for proper plain-error review, or apply the fourth requirement to the established record itself. Correction would require lawful resentencing within the available authorization or other legally permissible proceedings, not automatic dismissal of the prosecution. Refusal to correct would leave the challenged sentences in place on this forfeited record.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A jurisdictional holding can establish that omission of an offense element does not withdraw federal criminal subject-matter jurisdiction. A remedial holding could deny correction if the Court independently concludes that the supported threshold evidence and circumstances satisfy the proper fourth-condition inquiry; it cannot assume that conclusion in the neutral facts or adopt a universal harmlessness rule for uncharged elements. A rule requiring correction whenever the maximum is exceeded would need to confront Olano and Johnson directly rather than silently displace them.

**Uncertainty and supported alternatives:** The parties agree that seized cocaine base substantially exceeded fifty grams, but the legal significance of that aggregate evidence, individual attribution objections, witness-credibility challenges and the absent jury determinations must be analyzed separately. The excerpts support the Government’s threshold-specific characterization as advocacy; they do not establish an admission by each respondent or a general absence of quantity disputes. The original indictment may bear on notice but cannot be substituted for the superseding indictment. A fourth-condition resolution may assume substantial-rights effect; a ruling that resolves ordinary prejudice, attributes a concession to an individual, or rests on a specific transcript fact must validate that narrower predicate from an authorized bounded record extract. ### Issue 1: Counsel and suspended imprisonment

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Nichols v. United States permits the particular later use of a valid uncounseled fine-only misdemeanor. The controlling coalition does not supply a broad rule allowing incarceration for an uncounseled conviction whenever imprisonment is postponed.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Alabama Supreme Court treated the suspended term as imprisonment imposed for the original offense. Because its execution could follow without a new constitutionally adequate trial of guilt, it invalidated that component.

**Alabama's position.** Scott uses actual imprisonment as the administrable boundary. Shelton was not incarcerated, and a suspended sentence can encourage compliance without expanding appointment costs to every misdemeanor. The State argues that later procedural protections can prevent unconstitutional incarceration if revocation becomes necessary.

**Shelton's position.** A suspended term is an adjudicated imprisonment sentence awaiting execution. Revocation adjudicates compliance with conditions, not guilt of the original offense. Counsel supplied only then cannot investigate, cross-examine, or contest the evidence at the original trial when it mattered.

**Necessary limits.** Shelton's rule does not require counsel for every petty charge or invalidate every uncounseled conviction. Alabama cannot rely on the possibility of later counsel without specifying a lawful proceeding that actually repairs the original defect. The Court need not decide independent probation or punishment for a separate later offense.

Reversal would allow entry of the suspended term, with any required protection deferred to activation. A conditional rule allowing later incarceration only after a new counseled adjudication would require a valid state procedural route and cannot be assumed from an ordinary revocation hearing.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court may require counsel or valid waiver whenever a conviction directly supports a jail sentence, including a suspended term. That rule reaches the imprisonment authorization rather than every misdemeanor adjudication. The alternative actual-custody rule could defer appointment until liberty is immediately threatened, but must address why counsel at that later stage can protect the trial rights already lost.

**Source and remedy limits:** The lower court expressly rejected valid waiver on the record described above. The State’s corresponding-vacatur account of probation is a litigation representation; the operative state judgment itself directs vacatur of suspended jail and affirms the remaining aspects. No separate finding of probation severability or separate probation judgment is supplied. A stand-alone probation regime is analytically distinct. Any proposed later-incarceration solution must identify a lawful proceeding that remedies the original counsel deprivation; an ordinary revocation hearing supplies no new assault trial. Exact restitution figures differ in the OCR of the State’s statement and lower opinion and do not decide the counsel question; this packet makes no amount-specific reconstruction.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Brown v. Gilmore +
* **Citation or Docket:** 258 F.3d 265 (4th Cir. 2001). No simulated Supreme Court docket or grant date is supplied.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-20. Supplied simulation placement of the lower-court addition.
* **Question Presented:** Whether Virginia’s requirement that public schools observe a daily minute of silence, during which students may meditate, pray, or engage in other silent activity, violates the Establishment Clause?
* **Question Granted:** The supplied lower-court addition authorizes merits review of the facial Establishment Clause question above. It supplies no historical Supreme Court grant wording, docket, grant date, or argument date; none is invented.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eastern District of Virginia sustained the statute against the facial challenge. The Fourth Circuit affirmed. The student and parent challengers seek invalidation and injunctive relief.
* **Relief Requested:** The challengers seek an injunction against the mandatory silence statute; Virginia seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Virginia had authorized a school minute of silence since 1976. The 2000 amendment made the observance mandatory. The law permits meditation, prayer, or other silent activity; it does not require a prayer or a profession of belief. Implementation instructions prohibit teachers from selecting or suggesting a religious activity and prohibit audible devotion. Legislative statements included religiously motivated support, while the Governor and other supporters also emphasized reflection, calm, and civility. The facial record therefore contains evidence relevant to purpose but no finding that every permitted observance is a devotional exercise. A child may remain silent without communicating belief. ### Issue 1: Neutral silence or official devotional exercise

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Chaudhuri distinguishes a genuine silent opportunity for private thought from official worship. Agostini's modified Lemon rule examines governmental exercise of religious authority or function, with purpose, effects and entanglement informing that inquiry. Good News Club reaffirms the distinction between administering private access and adopting devotional activity. * **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify whether the institution itself conducts, directs, or substantially pressures participation in religious exercise. Private use of a neutral opportunity differs from official worship. Formal voluntariness does not excuse actual coercion, while universal silence does not become worship solely because prayer is one permitted use.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Fourth Circuit treated the statute as a permissible accommodation that leaves each student’s silent activity private. It relied on the absence of required prayer and on secular purposes supported by the record.

**Challengers' position.** The mandatory amendment and statements of its proponents are offered as evidence of an effort to return prayer to public schools. The lower dissent also relies on the religious preamble, rejected proposal to remove the prayer reference, contemporaneous resolution seeking a constitutional amendment for school prayer, the limited accommodation of seated silent worship, and the distinction between compulsory classroom observance and Good News Club’s private after-hours access. Student affidavits describe religious practices involving movement or sound and fears of ridicule; these are evidence and allegations, not findings of future actual coercion. In a compulsory school setting, the State's designation of prayer can communicate an expectation of religious participation even without spoken words. They ask the Court to treat the enacted interval as an official religious practice.

**Virginia's position.** The State requires only silence, which is useful for reflection and settling a classroom. Students may pray, think about unrelated subjects, or do nothing religious. Excluding prayer from a neutral silent interval would itself burden private choice. The implementation directions expressly disallow teacher-directed religious conduct.

**Limits.** Virginia cannot defend an actual prayer instruction by invoking neutral statutory text. Conversely, the challengers cannot equate every religiously motivated vote with an institutional devotional act when the resulting legal duty and implementation are nondevotional.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would sustain the facial statute while preserving challenges to coercive implementation. Reversal could require enjoining a state-directed devotional interval if the evidence supports that characterization. The Court should not declare all silence statutes valid or all mandatory silence religious.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** One path applies Chaudhuri to a school minute requiring no religious act, disclosure, or observance and supported by neutral implementation. It would cover comparable private-choice intervals but not directed prayer. Another path finds that the statute and operation communicate or impose an institutional religious exercise; such a ruling must identify that exercise or substantial pressure rather than rest only on the existence of religious supporters.

**Source and framing limits:** This is a facial preapplication challenge decided on summary judgment; the judicial record contains no established classroom administration directing prayer under the amendment. The lower majority and dissent dispute what the statutory text, legislative context, implementation instructions and student affidavits show. Admissibility of press statements was disputed below and not decided by the majority, which included the statement for completeness. The instructions prohibit teacher direction and audible prayer and warn against student coercion; those are implementation restrictions, not additional statutory words. A secular objective cannot justify actual governmental worship, and no completed coercive event is necessary if the official program itself supplies a prohibited religious function. Conversely, possible future misconduct cannot be presumed. Apply the modified Lemon inquiry, including its specific-prohibition and permissible-relationship routes, rather than the lower court’s historical independent-prong analysis.

CHAPTER 1022 An Act to amend and reenact § 22.1-203 of the Code of Virginia, relating to one minute of silence in public schools. [S 209] Approved April 19, 2000 Be it enacted by the General Assembly of Virginia: 1. That § 22.1-203 of the Code of Virginia is amended and reenacted as follows: § 22.1-203. Daily observance of one minute of silence. In order that the right of every pupil to the free exercise of religion be guaranteed within the schools and that the freedom of each individual pupil be subject to the least possible pressure from the Commonwealth either to engage in, or to refrain from, religious observation on school grounds, the school board of each school division shall establish the daily observance of one minute of silence in each classroom of the division. During such one-minute period of silence, the teacher responsible for each classroom shall take care that all pupils remain seated and silent and make no distracting display to the end that each pupil may, in the exercise of his or her individual choice, meditate, pray, or engage in any other silent activity which does not interfere with, distract, or impede other pupils in the like exercise of individual choice. The Office of the Attorney General shall intervene and shall provide legal defense of this law.
