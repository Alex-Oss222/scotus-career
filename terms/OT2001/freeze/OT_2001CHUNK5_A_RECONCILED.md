### Pricing rule and grounds retained

Section 252(d)(1) permits the Commission to select a reasonable forward-looking economic measure of the cost of providing a network element. It does not confine cost to the incumbent's embedded investment or mandate reimbursement of every historical expenditure. The instruction to determine cost without reference to a rate-of-return or other rate-based proceeding defeats the asserted exclusive historical-cost reading without itself prohibiting every conceivable consideration of historical costs. Permission to include reasonable profit remains permission, not a guarantee of every sunk cost.

The seven sustaining Associates accept the actual TELRIC methodology as a reasonable exercise of the governing AT&T delegation. The benchmark uses efficient technology currently available and the lowest-cost configuration at existing wire-center locations. It includes total element long-run incremental cost and reasonable forward-looking common costs, capital cost and economic depreciation. The element-specific stand-alone-cost ceiling, the aggregate common-cost allocation requirement and exclusion of retail costs remain operative. Embedded and opportunity costs and revenues subsidizing other services do not become inputs merely because incumbents prefer their recovery. Incumbents must substantiate proposed rates through compliant cost studies; the supplied rules retain the notice, comment and reviewable factual-record requirements when the stated proxy limits are exceeded.

Hope supplies latitude in regulatory methodology rather than a right to a single accounting system. Duquesne confirms that a methodology's label does not guarantee recovery of each past expenditure. The statute delegates binding implementation; Mead and Barnhart require actual authority and its exercise rather than expertise alone. Current technology, retained wire centers, capital and depreciation allowances, common-cost recovery and the actual state-commission implementation process connect the benchmark to providing service. These limits answer the claim that the rule is costless access to an imaginary network. They do not prove that every future rate is lawful or that TELRIC is obligatory under every regulatory statute.

The incumbent objection is substantial: a rate tied to a more efficient network may encourage leasing and discourage facilities investment while the incumbent still operates its existing assets. The seven sustaining Associates find that concern insufficient to invalidate this permissible administrative choice. The choice allows access and entry while retaining compensation and implementation limits; a hypothesized injury does not establish that Congress forbade it. An alternative economic policy may be reasonable without making this one unlawful.

Breyer accepts forward-looking cost in principle and substantial administrative latitude but finds the agency's answer inadequate concerning the particular mandatory efficient-network benchmark's effects on replacement investment, independent facilities construction and deregulation. His State Farm concern is the connection between this chosen means and the statutory end, not judicial preference for embedded costs. His simulated AT&T explanation-and-investment position reinforces that concern. He would preserve the partial invalidation and seek agency reconsideration; he supplies neither a judicial replacement formula nor a requirement to repay all sunk investments. No source-supported changed premise requires abandoning his verified historical pricing dissent.

### Combination rule: necessary assistance, not unrestricted assembly

Section 251(c)(3), as already construed in simulated AT&T Part XI, does not unambiguously assign every physical combining task exclusively to the entrant. The refreshed order supplies the further justification: access to lawfully obtainable elements must be usable, and entrants may lack the facilities or network information needed to make the necessary connections. Paragraphs 293–294 describe incumbent assistance if necessary, permit an entrant to combine when reasonably able, and require incumbent performance when the entrant is unable. Paragraph 293 covers both combinations of incumbent elements and connections to entrant-held elements.

All eight read the request trigger and functions-necessary language of (c) and (d) with that contemporaneous assistance explanation. Compulsory incumbent performance reaches the necessary connecting functions when the entrant is not reasonably able to perform them in the actual network setting. This ability limit is a legal interpretation of the regulation and original explanation together; it is not text expressly printed as an exclusive condition in either regulatory paragraph. The broad contrary upon-request reading is tested and rejected because it would detach compulsory labor from the explained purpose of enabling usable access. The agency's original explanation supplies this connection; a court is not inventing it from policy alone or later advocacy.

The construction does not impose an independent command on an able entrant to perform the work. Permission remains permission. It identifies when incumbent assistance can be compelled, without forbidding voluntary lawful arrangements. It creates no own-facilities prerequisite, numerical cost threshold, categorical willing/unwilling test or new general proof burden concerning entrant ability. Practical access to facilities and information matters; theoretical capacity in a different setting does not establish reasonable ability here. No particular carrier's ability is adjudicated.

Compensation is separately required by lawful rates and terms; payment alone would not create statutory authority. Nor does the assistance duty identify which elements must be made available. The independent necessity and impairment requirements preserved by simulated AT&T remain conditions of element entitlement. The original order's broader element-list and feasibility passages do not revive the vacated list or reduce all access questions to technical feasibility.

### Distinct conditions, feasibility and proof

- **Paragraph (c):** The requested incumbent-element combination must be technically feasible **and** must not impair other carriers' unbundled-element access or interconnection. Both conditions govern even when the elements are not ordinarily combined or not ordinarily combined in that manner. Paragraph 296's explanatory sentence uses a disjunction, but the promulgated rule expressly contains the conjunction and separately enumerates both conditions. The rule controls. The source discrepancy is preserved; it is neither silently corrected nor treated as proof of a printing mistake. The surrounding explanation protects both reliability and other-carrier access. The discrepancy requires no new rulemaking or remand because the operative text already resolves the duty and the remaining explanation is intelligible.
- **Paragraph (d):** Connection to requester-held elements has its own technical-feasibility condition. Paragraph (c)(2)'s distinct explicit other-carrier condition is not inserted into (d). Statutory nondiscrimination and the actual reliability/security framework retain their proper separate operation.
- **Technical feasibility:** Engineering and operational concerns matter. Novelty, necessary modification, recoverable cost or adjustments to billing/accounting do not alone establish infeasibility. Ordinary practice is not an absolute ceiling on feasible connections. A site with no possible expansion may make access infeasible, technically or otherwise; possible expansion is different, with the requester bearing necessary expansion costs under the governing scheme. Physical and virtual collocation retain their distinct statutory rules, and §251(f)'s rural exceptions and modification provisions retain their actual terms.
- **Reliability and security:** Specific, significant and demonstrable network concerns remain relevant; carriers retain responsibility for network management, control and performance. Paragraph 203 requires clear and convincing evidence of specific and significant adverse impacts when the incumbent refuses the described requested-point access or interconnection on reliability/security grounds. Footnote 622 connects that technical-feasibility discussion to combinations. Applying the stated treatment to the same kind of combination objection is a contextual legal reading of the cross-reference, not a claim that the footnote repeats the evidentiary words. This requirement is not extended to every infeasibility issue, every other-carrier-impairment claim, entrant ability, or unrelated fact.
- **Paragraphs (e) and (f):** Under (e), the incumbent proves technical infeasibility to the state commission for a denial under (c)(1) or (d). Under (f), it separately proves the other-carrier impairment specified by (c)(2). The incumbent's possession of network information supports these allocations. Neither paragraph itself states a universal clear-and-convincing standard. Substantially similar successful connections are substantial evidence of feasibility, including the supported quality level, not a conclusive identity presumption for all facilities.
- **Identification and support:** The requester must identify sought elements before obtaining them. Paragraph 297 recognizes that missing network information can prevent complete specification even during negotiations or arbitration and requires incumbent cooperation. There is no automatic default, entitlement to unspecified elements or impossible pre-negotiation identification requirement. Different provisioning, element variants and necessary support systems remain subject to current lawful element entitlement.

These conditions answer the objections that the rules compel an unlimited assembly service, dispense with genuine network protection, or place unsupported proof demands on incumbents. The original order supplies its own statutory, practical and informational reasons. Christensen supports enforcing unambiguous promulgated terms rather than using explanatory language to amend them; Mead, Barnhart, Gulf Power and New York v. FERC require and permit the reasoned exercise of the actual grant. A second explanation remand is unnecessary on this facial record. Particular applications remain governed by these real conditions rather than by a new hearing or factual finding invented here.

## 67. Lapides v. Board of Regents of University System of Georgia

### Reconciled rule, disposition and remedial limits

All eight Associates support reversing the Eleventh Circuit's no-waiver determination. A State that authorizes voluntary removal of an action to federal court relinquishes its federal-forum immunity from the state-law claims for which it already permits adjudication in its own courts. The consequence follows from actual voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction. It does not require a separate state-law authorization for the Attorney General to declare an abstract immunity waiver. The State's express reservation of federal immunity in its tort statute is real; it is the subsequent authorized removal, not enactment of the state-court waiver alone, that produces the federal consequence.

The verified historical decision is unanimous and formally states “Reversed.” Assembly should use reversal as the disposition and explain the ensuing ordinary jurisdictional and claim proceedings; it must not turn this into an order mandating remand to state court. The available scope is confined to state-law claims for which state-court immunity has been waived. No rule disposing of every removed federal claim, unwaived state claim, or other possible immunity is necessary.

Will's independent rule remains: the State is not a person subject to the pleaded §1983 damages claim. Removal waiver neither creates a cause of action nor cures that statutory defect. Failure on that ground does not automatically negate original federal jurisdiction at the time of removal; the individual defendants' claims and actual jurisdictional anchors retain their separate treatment. If original jurisdiction is absent, the applicable mandatory remand rule governs.
