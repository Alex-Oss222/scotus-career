## Reconciled rules and application

The five functional-pendency Associates adopt the following sufficient rule: a properly filed state collateral application remains pending during timely continuation through the State's ordinary review process, including the interval before timely higher-court review. California's ordinary successive-level original-petition procedure performs that review function, so its terminology alone does not interrupt statutory pendency. Timely continuation under actual state law remains necessary; a completed process, an untimely next step or an unrelated new application receives no exclusion simply because it concerns collateral relief.

The statute protects an ordinary state-review opportunity while retaining the federal limitation. Making every ordinary inter-court interval count against that limitation would encourage federal filings before the State completes its review. Boerckel supplies the ordinary-round exhaustion background; Duncan retains the sovereign and filing conditions, rather than creating an equitable substitute for them. The strongest contrary argument is that no particular application remains before a court after denial of an original petition. The five answer that pending, in this statutory setting, includes timely completion of the ordinary review course. They do not deem every possible original writ in every State an appeal: extraordinary collateral options outside the ordinary course have different functions. The actual state procedure must be identified.

The three contrary Associates would reject exclusion of the gap between completed California original applications. An application already finally denied is distinct from a later document initiating a new proceeding, even if both seek relief from the same conviction. Functional similarity and exhaustion policy cannot make the later application already filed. Their position does not decide every interval before an actual statutory appeal; such an appeal can seek continued disposition of the original application. They accept that this distinction may make the choice of state procedure important, but regard that result as a consequence of the enacted application requirement, not authority to recast the whole collateral-review course as one filing.

All eight retain proper filing, pendency, state diligence and ultimate habeas entitlement as separate questions. Alternative merits treatment does not by itself erase a valid independent state untimeliness determination. Under the five's rule, a clear state determination that the relevant interval was unreasonable defeats interval exclusion even if the state court also considered the merits. The actual state order here requires interpretation rather than a conclusive inference from its merits language. The remand considers the order's actual state-law meaning, relevant timing circumstances and exact days. It prescribes neither a nationwide reasonable-time ceiling nor an automatic certification requirement. The approximate narrative interval is not a computed federal deadline.

The five do not decide that the petition was timely, award equitable tolling or infer an extraordinary impediment. Artuz's reservation about exceptions to filing deadlines remains intact. Duncan's independent lawful commencement or equitable grounds remain available only if actually preserved and supported. The four statutory starting points retain their own conditions. Nothing here adjudicates conviction validity or satisfies section 2254(d).

## Remedy and completion

The five vacate the Ninth Circuit's timeliness judgment and remand for the actual state-timing inquiry and calculation under functional pendency. The three would reverse its interval-exclusion rule and return for the lawful calculation without those California original-petition gaps, preserving only independently available grounds on their own requirements. Neither disposition grants a writ, orders release or resolves the constitutional claims. A denial required by a lawful untimeliness conclusion remains a limitations disposition.
