## Operative propositions and limits

The shared framework asks whether, considering all objective circumstances, a reasonable passenger would feel free to decline the officers' requests or otherwise terminate the encounter. The passenger's own decision to travel may restrict movement without police seizure. Absence of a warning does not itself establish detention, and a quiet request does not itself establish freedom to refuse. The inquiry addresses the whole encounter rather than counting isolated favorable facts.

The five apply that framework to find no seizure of either respondent before the searches. The officers displayed badges but concealed their weapons, made quiet individual requests and left the aisle passable. Hoover's position by the driver's seat and the officers' coordinated presence deserved consideration but did not communicate a command to remain or cooperate. The driver handled tickets; the police neither collected nor retained them. The five do not infer passenger knowledge of an officer's uncommunicated willingness to allow refusal. They do not rely on supposed security benefits of bus searches as a substitute for the objective inquiry. Brown's arrest was relevant to Drayton's circumstances but did not automatically detain him or everyone nearby.

The three assess the same supplied facts differently. Coordinated officers at the front and rear, close questioning of seated passengers and the stated interdiction purpose communicated expected participation even without blocked passage, raised voices or visible weapons. Brown's arrest heightened that setting for Drayton. Their conclusion rests on that combined message, not on absence of advice alone. With no independent justification asserted for the seizure presented for review and the searches immediately following in the same uninterrupted encounter, the evidence remained the product of the unlawful detention. They do not decide the voluntariness of otherwise untainted consent.

The supplied account of the driver's ticket handling is preserved. Souter's historical account discussed the driver's retention of tickets; that does not establish police ticket seizure and does not authorize altering the frozen record. No hypothetical refusal, obstruction, threat, or police control of the schedule becomes a finding. The competing inferences about conveyed control remain competing applications to the supplied encounter.

## Precedent and remedy

Bostick supplies the governing contextual inquiry and rejects automatic treatment of bus encounters as seizures. Robinette rejects a mandatory warning while preserving the totality inquiry. Bond distinguishes ordinary passenger expectations from an additional police intrusion; it does not classify every request as a seizure. Current Arvizu preserves the distinction between a stop issue and separate consent issues; Knights supplies no suspicion or advance authorization for this encounter. Neither after-discovered drugs nor Brown's arrest can retroactively justify an earlier seizure.

The five would reverse the seizure-based suppression judgment and return the matter for proceedings consistent with that resolution. No independent consent ruling, Terry justification, automatic reinstatement of both convictions, or new factual finding is included. Brown's conditional plea and Drayton's trial posture remain distinct procedural settings on remand. The three would leave suppression in place on seizure and taint alone. No Justice reaches an unpresented basis merely to broaden the decision.
