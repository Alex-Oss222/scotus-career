* **Question Granted:** The supplied merits scope is the question stated above, with the preserved limitations identified below. No separate simulated grant order or grant date is supplied.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit, holding the advertising and solicitation restrictions unconstitutional and the relevant exemption provision nonseverable.

* **Material Record:** The statutory exemption for qualifying drug compounding required unsolicited prescriptions and prohibited promotion of particular compounded drugs, while allowing general promotion of compounding services. Licensed pharmacies challenged those conditions. The Government appealed the constitutional ruling; neither side properly brought the circuit’s separate nonseverability ruling into review.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The question concerns truthful, nonmisleading commercial information about otherwise lawful compounding, not authority to prohibit unsafe drugs or false medical claims. ### Issue 1: Commercial speech and the compounding exemption

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Central Hudson and Fox require material advancement and reasonable fit for truthful lawful commercial speech. 44 Liquormart retains that framework. Lorillard applies it separately to advancement and the actual breadth of tobacco-advertising restrictions, rejecting insufficiently justified adult-speech burdens without adopting a universal least-restrictive-means requirement. Edenfield, Rubin and Greater New Orleans remain applicable to actual harms, scheme coherence and fit.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable rule protects truthful information about lawful activity and places justification on the Government. Evidence and reasonable prediction may suffice without a mandatory type of study, but speculation and unexplained mismatch do not. Concrete alternatives bear on fit without mechanically converting the inquiry into strict scrutiny.
* **Other Governing Law:** Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission of New York; Board of Trustees of State University of New York v. Fox; the enacted prescription, promotion, and exemption provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc. addresses a different content-based noncommercial setting and does not automatically replace the operative commercial-speech framework.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law already governs the advertising condition’s constitutional analysis. The unresolved application is whether this restriction reasonably fits the asserted need to preserve individualized compounding without allowing mass-market avoidance of drug approval.
* **Open Point:** Whether restricting drug-specific truthful promotion materially serves that distinction with an adequate fit, considering direct controls on compounding scale and practice and the useful speech swept in.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The circuit found inadequate advancement and tailoring. The Government seeks to preserve drug approval, allow necessary individualized compounding, and prevent the exception from becoming a route to industrial manufacture; it treats advertising as a proxy for the scale of production. The pharmacies argue that truthful information helps clinicians and patients locate appropriate preparations and that direct production and distribution limits address the actual concern. The Government need not prove an absolutely least restrictive rule, but must explain the speech proxy and its mismatch. The pharmacies gain no entitlement to misleading claims or exemption from otherwise valid safety rules.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could hold these particular conditions insufficiently fitted under existing commercial-speech doctrine while leaving substantive safety authority intact. Reversal could sustain the information restriction as a justified proxy within the statutory balance. A narrower ruling could address only supported lawful speech applications if the posture permits it, but cannot rewrite the omitted production safeguards itself. Because nonseverability is unreviewed, affirmance does not judicially create the exemption stripped of its speech conditions. The effect of the operative judgment must be distinguished from a policy preference for compounding access.

## 62. US Airways, Inc. v. Barnett

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** US Airways, Inc. v. Barnett
* **Citation or Docket:** No. 00-1250; 535 U.S. 391.
* **October Term:** 2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS. Review of the judgment identified below under the supplied merits question.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-04-29. Historical final opinion or disposition date, used as the simulated merits event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the ADA requires consideration of reassignment or retention as a reasonable accommodation when it conflicts with an employer’s seniority system, and how reasonableness and undue hardship allocate the inquiry. This is a formulation of the reviewed question.
* **Question Granted:** The supplied merits scope is the question stated above, with the preserved limitations identified below. No separate simulated grant order or grant date is supplied.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** En banc Ninth Circuit, reversing summary judgment for US Airways and requiring an individualized accommodation inquiry.
* **Relief Requested:** US Airways seeks judgment based on the seniority conflict. Barnett seeks further proceedings on his request to remain in the mailroom position.
* **Material Record:** After a back injury, Barnett moved from cargo work to a mailroom job. When senior employees planned to bid for it, he requested retention as an accommodation. The employer denied the request. The seniority policy was employer-created; the handbook disclaimed contractual status and reserved amendment. The district court emphasized longstanding reliance on the system.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The question does not establish every disability, qualification, vacancy, or damages predicate. An employer-created seniority policy differs from a collective-bargaining obligation or an incumbent’s actual job. The request’s effectiveness, ordinary reasonableness, and particular undue hardship must not be merged.
* **Relevant Dates:** The injury occurred in 1990 and the competing bids arose in 1992. The merits event is April 29, 2002. The applicable ADA is the original statutory regime, with the simulation’s existing interpretations; later amendments do not apply.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Toyota concerns the disability definition for manual tasks. Echazabal concerns a medically supported threat-to-self defense. Barnett instead concerns a qualified worker’s requested reassignment and the operation of an employer-created seniority policy; no medical-danger finding resolves that conflict.
* **Participation Matter:** No in-world recusal, vacancy, or nonparticipation is authenticated in the supplied materials.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** ADA §§12111(9), 12111(10), and 12112(b)(5)(A); summary judgment. The employee’s initial accommodation showing and the employer’s statutory undue-hardship showing have different functions.

### Issue 1: Accommodation and an employer-created seniority system

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Garrett preserves the ADA's separate coverage, qualification, accommodation and defense requirements. Reassignment to a vacant position is an accommodation example; §12112(b)(5)(A) assigns the employer the significant-difficulty-or-expense defense under its actual statutory factors. Sutton and Murphy distinguish coverage from qualification and accommodation. No completed holding creates a seniority-system presumption for the arrangement presented here.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable doctrine requires an individual statutory inquiry, identifies the essential functions, and preserves the distinct accommodation question. Olmstead v. L.C. applies reasonable accommodation within its public-service context and does not decide this employment seniority issue.
* **Other Governing Law:** The ADA identifies reassignment to a vacant position as a possible accommodation and permits an employer to demonstrate undue hardship. A preference may be necessary to provide the statutory accommodation; equal application of a rule does not alone answer the claim.
* **Material Other Authority:** Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Hardison addresses religious accommodation and a protected collectively bargained seniority system under a different statute. Its outcome cannot be transplanted without examining those differences.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The supplied law resolves disability-definition and distinct safety questions but does not establish either a categorical seniority defense or a presumption against every conflicting accommodation.
* **Open Point:** Whether the employer’s policy itself makes the request unreasonable, whether a rebuttable presumption is warranted, or whether demonstrated reliance and operational effects must be assessed through ordinary reasonableness and hardship.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The district court found the seniority disruption unduly burdensome; the circuit rejected automatic treatment and required case-specific analysis. US Airways emphasizes predictable advancement and reliance of many workers, arguing that accommodation cannot override a neutral system. Barnett emphasizes statutory reassignment, his existing performance in the position, and the employer’s retained power to depart from its own policy. He must show a reasonable accommodation rather than merely an effective preference. The employer must distinguish real disruption and others’ rights from the bare assertion that any exception is incompatible with seniority.

**Verified argument limitation:** Barnett’s counsel expressly disclaimed entitlement to summary judgment and accepted that the employer could present undue-hardship and other statutory defenses. That concession limits the requested relief to further adjudication; it does not establish that every seniority exception is reasonable. [Argument transcript, December 4, 2001, p. 30](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/argument_transcripts/2001/00-1250.pdf#page=30).

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could require an individualized inquiry without a special policy-based presumption. Reversal could make conflict categorically unreasonable, while an intermediate rule could presume unreasonableness subject to demonstrated exceptions or weak reliance. A vacatur could clarify burdens and allow renewed summary judgment. None of those paths automatically proves Barnett entitled to the job or permits displacement of an existing employee. The record supports a rule about this employer-created system; enforceable labor-contract conflicts require attention to their different legal predicates.
