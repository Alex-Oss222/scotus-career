## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Sixth Circuit treated the sentencing omissions as an effective abandonment of adversarial testing and presumed prejudice. The state court had instead assessed particular counsel decisions and prejudice under Strickland.

**Tennessee's position.** Counsel remained active, drew on evidence already heard, cross-examined, and used an available procedural choice to prevent damaging rebuttal. Those facts defeat total-abandonment treatment. The state court's rejection of the ordinary ineffective-assistance claim was at least within the statutory bounds of reasonableness.

**Cone's position.** The guilt-stage insanity evidence did not adequately explain the reasons for a life sentence after guilt was found. He contends that the opening, limited cross-examination and objections did not supply meaningful advocacy at the decisive sentencing stage, and that counsel's reasons rested in part on mistaken law. He seeks Cronic treatment of the asserted breakdown. Alternatively he requests remand for Strickland review, including the district court's added Fretwell inquiry and its unresolved default ruling on investigation-related mitigation. His account of no advocacy is a legal characterization; the documented opening, mercy plea, cross-examination and objections remain part of the record.

**Limits.** The State cannot justify every silence as strategy. Cone cannot obtain presumed prejudice merely by showing that better advocacy was possible. The postconviction findings and counsel's actual preparation matter to the ordinary claim.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Rejecting Cronic would eliminate that presumed-prejudice ground; it would not by itself decide ordinary Strickland relief or the other pretermitted sentencing claims. A remand may permit the Sixth Circuit to address the proper prejudice standard, review scope and distinct default questions in ordinary sequence. The Court could itself decide the presented ordinary claim only if that issue is within review and the relevant record and independent grounds are adequate. Even rejection of that claim would not authorize a blanket restoration of the district court's entire sentencing judgment without disposing of or preserving the remaining appellate claims. Affirmance of a valid sentence-only writ would permit a new lawful penalty proceeding; it would not acquit Cone or judicially impose a life term.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding can confine presumed prejudice to complete failure of the relevant adversarial function and treat the identified tactical omissions as ordinary ineffective-assistance claims. It need not immunize all sentencing silence. A contrary Cronic application would have to identify why counsel's documented participation did not amount to adversarial testing of the sentencing case, rather than equate an incomplete presentation with no representation.

**Uncertainty:** The additional investigation allegations, the adequacy and independence of the second-petition bar, asserted cause from unavailable postconviction investigative funding, the district court's actual treatment of Fretwell, and any surviving sentence grounds have not been decided by this validation. Cone's remand brief presents them; it does not establish their merits. Before a path finally rejects all sentencing relief, obtain the specific lower rulings and record needed for each unresolved ground. A remand preserving them avoids inventing their resolution.
