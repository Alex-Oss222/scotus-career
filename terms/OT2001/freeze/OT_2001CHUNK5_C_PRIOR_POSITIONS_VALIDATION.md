## Requested positions and their actual authority

### Dague

The requested O'Connor and Stevens positions are present. O'Connor's separate dissent permits a narrowly demonstrated class-wide market adjustment but would vacate and remand because the record does not substantiate the 25 percent amount. Stevens joins Blackmun's dissent, which would affirm the supported enhancement because contingent compensation differs economically from guaranteed hourly compensation and an adjustment need not compensate losing litigants. Stevens has no separate writing in this entry.

Neither position controls. Scalia, White, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas supply the five-Justice prohibition of nonpayment-risk enhancement under the two reviewed environmental fee statutes. The entry expressly preserves different compensation schemes, delay and correction of an unreasonable lodestar as outside its holding. O'Connor's own published summary must not be enlarged by assigning to her every separately stated condition in Stone-Zsela's dissent. The source supports the positions at the depth actually published; it does not make the two market-based dissents identical or mandate an enhancement.

### Baker

Kennedy's concurrence, joined by O'Connor and Thomas, is present at substantive depth. It rests on the reported Michigan rules concerning same parties, full opportunity, mutuality, actual nonparty-injunction applications and the burden on the party asserting preclusion. It finds no basis for compulsory exclusion on the supported nonparty record. It distinguishes Elwell's personal duty from a legal requirement that the separate forum exclude testimony, and does not make the Bakers' evidence request a motion to modify the Michigan decree. It reserves actual personal enforcement and broader cross-border restraint questions.

That Michigan-specific rationale has three Justices and is noncontrolling. Parts I and III, including the common recognition framework and bounded mandate, have all nine joins. Part II's federal recognition/evidence-operation ground has six direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The unanimous judgment therefore does not make all nine adopt Part II or turn the three-Justice concurrence into a competing controlling rule. The source's limits on the unavailable full Michigan opinions, complete historical rule and unprovided instruments remain intact; no exhaustive state-law exception list follows.

### Alden

Kennedy's dissent, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, is present. All four accept review of the separately filed state action, express FLSA authorization and continuing wage duties. Their disagreement concerns the State's retained constitutional protection from this private retrospective treasury action in its own courts, including the public fisc and political responsibility. They distinguish regulation from remedy, preserve Hilton's specialized statutory and reliance basis, distinguish Howlett's non-State defendant and acknowledge the actual limited status of Seminole and Union Gas. Their proposed affirmance preserves distinct consent, public enforcement, appropriate Section 5 and officer-remedy routes.

The dissent is noncontrolling. Part I has nine joins. Souter's Parts II–III have five: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The controlling constitutional rule concerns the expressly authorized state-court FLSA remedy; it is not itself a general federal-forum amenability holding or a finding that wages are owed.

### College Savings Bank

This companion entry supplies the requested context without merging its grounds. Part I's express authorization is unanimous. Part II's confined rejection of the own-product-advertising Section 5 theory has eight joins; Stevens dissents on that component. Part III's rejection of constructive consent through ordinary commercial participation has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

The five-Justice reversal has no common affirmative amenability rationale. Breyer, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies the four-Justice prospective commercial-condition theory; Stevens adds a separate Section 5 prevention theory; Stone-Zsela alone supplies Part IV's Article I and review-scope ground. None can be converted into a majority ground by combining judgment votes. O'Connor's dissent, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, accepts Parts I–III and would affirm within the actual questions. It leaves the distinct Article I route undecided in that review rather than asserting a controlling categorical Seminole prohibition. The public entry also preserves the rejection of commerce-based consent separately from actual submission and accepted-benefit questions.

### Florida Prepaid

Kennedy's opposition, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, is present. The four join Parts I and IV while disputing Section 5 fit and the Article I extension. Their Section 5 objection concerns substantive connection to unconstitutional deprivation, rather than a numerical evidence quota, required findings, universal exhaustion or a finding that Florida's untested remedies suffice. They distinguish federal regulation and clear legislative intent from the structural power to impose the private suit. Their remedy is limited vacatur and remand for preserved actual-submission contentions and other properly available grounds, not unconditional dismissal or an invented consent finding. Their stated willingness to overrule Union Gas if required is not an actual overruling; the shared remand leaves that further question undecided.

Parts I and IV are unanimous. Parts II and III each command the same five direct joins and independently sustain the action under Section 5 and Article I. Those affirmative holdings control without a combined-rationale construction. The four who had supported a commercial-condition theory in College Savings accept its contrary Part III as controlling in this companion, and all nine reject continued commerce as consent here. The opposition grounds do not displace either constitutional holding, decide litigation waiver or establish patent liability.

### Powell

Stevens's dissent, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, is present and supports both prospective enforcement channels. It reads the identified beneficiaries, express implementation authority and particular regulation as authorized elaboration, with Section 1983 independently sufficient once the statutory right is identified. It distinguishes right creation from foreclosure and rejects automatic foreclosure from general funding administration. Its proposed ordinary adjudication retains coverage, actual prohibited practice, causal injury and fitted relief; it finds no violation or funding entitlement. Stevens could leave the direct-action alternative unresolved if Section 1983 were sustained, but does not join its rejection.

The original five-Justice opinion separately rejects the direct action and asserted Section 1983 effects right. The later Sandoval entry in this supplement expressly displaces only the identified direct-action prohibition. Powell's separate Section 1983 holding and completed judgment remain. The earlier public entry's law-after-decision account is accurate as of its own date and must not be read as an unchanged May 2002 direct-action rule. The four-Justice Section 1983 dissent remains noncontrolling.

### Garrett

O'Connor's dissent, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, supplies the requested structural and Section 5 opposition. It accepts statutory clarity and substantive employment-regulation authority, would reconsider the Article I enforcement precedents to the necessary extent, and disputes the fit of this private damages regime to identified unconstitutional employment conduct. Its objection retains real preventive authority without an incident quota, universal violation requirement, sunset or constitutional tort in every preventive action.

Kennedy's supplementary dissent is joined by O'Connor only. It recognizes official indifference and stereotyping, rejects an assumption of compliance from state antidiscrimination laws or silence in reported litigation, and locates disagreement in the remedy's evidentiary and statutory fit. Scalia and Thomas are not listed as joining that supplementary writing. The Court's five-Justice opinion independently sustains the presented Title I enforcement under Article I and Section 5; neither dissent controls or decides Title II, qualification, discrimination, actual relief or every other immunity question.

### Sandoval

Thomas's dissent, joined throughout by O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy, supplies both requested grounds: Title VI-specific lack of authorization for the broader direct regulatory-effects action, and statutory stare decisis against overturning Powell's directly relevant prospective-relief rule. It preserves contextual implication, does not impose a universal express-action requirement, and acknowledges the distinct validity and administrative-enforcement questions. It alleges no proved recipient reliance and adopts no separate funding-notice ground. Its proposed reversal and ordinary remand create no new claim or guaranteed amendment.

The five-Justice opinion controls the bounded direct prospective Title VI action and express partial overruling. Valid, sufficiently definite implementing requirements, actual regulatory elements, coverage and fitted relief remain essential; statistical imbalance alone does not suffice. The decision preserves Powell's separate Section 1983 holding, Section 601's intent requirement, the accepted limits of the reviewed merits record and final judgments. The dissent supplies published prior positions, not authority to substitute its rejected rule for current controlling law.
