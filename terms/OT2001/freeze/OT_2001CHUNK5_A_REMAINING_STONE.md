## 65. Verizon Communications Inc. v. FCC

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the recognition of forward-looking pricing authority, reverse the invalidation of the challenged efficient-cost methodology and additional bounded combination rules, and remand for proceedings consistent with their statutory limits.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Restore the challenged regulations against these facial objections. Preserve challenges to particular rate results and disputes over feasibility, compensation, and whether an element must be made available. Award no carrier damages or guaranteed historical-cost recovery.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Efficient-cost pricing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach statutory methodology review; reject a facial confiscation conclusion unsupported by a complete rate record.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the delegated cost-setting authority permits an efficient forward-looking estimate tied to existing wire centers.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board’s allocation of FCC authority and propose a new holding on the previously open methodology.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The statutory cost language permits the FCC’s forward-looking efficient-technology benchmark, including reasonable common costs and profit, because cost need not mean reimbursement of the incumbent’s historical investment. The method remains reviewable for statutory fidelity and actual unconstitutional rate effects.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding validates this method’s legal availability, not every input, state commission calculation, or resulting charge.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** AT&T v. Iowa Utilities Board establishes the FCC's implementing authority but leaves the particular cost method for this case. Mead requires an actual delegated lawmaking function; the Act's cost-setting assignment and formal regulations provide that function here. Reading cost in the enacted competitive-access scheme does not compel reimbursement of historical monopoly investment. An efficient technology model anchored to existing wire centers, with common costs and a reasonable profit, is a permitted policy choice. Stone combines statutory interpretation with evaluation of the technical design: text and delegated scope are decisive on authority, and reasoned expert judgment receives substantial weight within that scope. A competing accounting policy does not prove illegality. The facial statutory challenge can be resolved without calculating individual rates or adjudicating a confiscation claim lacking a concrete financial record.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Actual confiscation requires a particular rate and financial record absent here.

### Issue 2: Assembly needed for usable access

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the additional combination rules as a separable regulatory question.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the statutory duty can include compensated technical work necessary to deliver usable authorized elements.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing access framework and propose an additional implementation holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The FCC may require technically feasible, compensated combination work under the challenged conditions when needed to make authorized access usable without impairing the incumbent’s operation or disadvantaging other competitors.
* **Reach Within the Question:** Existing-element eligibility and necessity-and-impairment limits remain independently operative.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 251(c)(3)'s duty to provide access in a manner allowing combination would be ineffective if an incumbent could furnish unusable fragments while withholding work only it can practicably perform. Stone extends the authorized-access principle beyond the existing-combination rule, subject to the challenged regulations' feasibility, requested-access, compensation and operational conditions. The statutory connection to usable access is decisive; a general desire to assist competitors is not a sufficient source. AT&T's independent necessity-and-impairment limits on which elements must be supplied remain fully operative. The ruling validates this implementation tool, while actual engineering and compensation disputes need transaction-specific findings and do not justify denying the legal power categorically.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Particular engineering disputes and element-eligibility determinations lack the necessary transaction-specific facts here.

## 68. Voting Integrity Project, Inc. v. Keisling +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment rejecting the election-day preemption claim.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave Oregon’s receipt-by-election-day mail-voting system operative against this challenge. Order no restructuring of state election machinery and grant no relief concerning ballot receipt after the deadline, a question not presented.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Final selection rather than each preliminary act

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the federal statutory question; no constitutional voting restriction is necessary to the judgment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether Congress’s uniform date prohibits ballots cast in advance for a selection completed on election day.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Foster v. Love’s operative distinction and propose its further application to Oregon’s system and the relevant federal offices.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Federal election-day statutes do not preempt advance casting of ballots where the State’s election remains open and the final legal selection is consummated on the prescribed day. Oregon’s system, requiring receipt by that day and not fixing an earlier winner, satisfies that requirement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers the challenged universal mail procedure as well as the materially equivalent advance-ballot feature. It does not permit early final selection or decide a different post-deadline receipt system.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Foster distinguishes the final selection of officials from preliminary electoral acts. Stone applies that enacted distinction to Oregon's advance ballots, with the statutory timing rule decisive. Voting remains open until the federally prescribed day; counting and final selection are not moved to an earlier binding election. Earlier ballot preparation or casting therefore does not establish the conflict that existed in Foster. The interpretation preserves both Congress's uniform date and state authority over the means of receiving votes. Practical access reinforces the reading but does not create an exception to federal law. This is a safe harbor for advance casting within an election completed on the designated day, not a decision about ballots first received afterward or about a genuinely earlier final selection.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Post-election ballot receipt, specific ballot disputes, and independent constitutional claims lie outside the question presented.
