## Page v. Bartels

### Components and confirmed record

Review concerns the final three-judge trial judgment denying injunctive relief. It does not review the earlier single-judge preliminary ruling. Statutory Count I asserts results dilution; statutory Count II expressly asserts an intentional Section 2 violation. Constitutional Count III presents the tried equal-protection dilution and participation theories; its undeveloped due-process label supplies no separate issue. Count IV presents the asserted Fifteenth Amendment abridgment. The final material district disputes concern Districts 27 and 34.

The numerical changes—52.8% to 27.5% Black voting-age population in District 27 and 3.9% to 35.3% in District 34—neither prove loss nor preserve opportunity by themselves. The trial court made express local findings of opportunity in each district even without Hispanic support, and separately rejected the alleged participation chilling. The expert analysis covered more than 150 elections, relevant preferred candidates, outside support, the affected electorates and the primary stage. Contrary projections were offered and evaluated, with stated concerns about sample size, special circumstances, incumbency and the fit of countywide contests to legislative elections. This is not a finding that contrary evidence was absent or that the credited projections guarantee every future victory.

The purpose finding is independently identifiable before the opinion's later predominance discussion: the account of the commission's work concludes that it did not intend to diminish minority voting rights, 144 F. Supp. 2d at 355; finding 6 separately addresses discriminatory design, id. at 362. The constitutional application and note 18 also identify failed purpose and the additional no-dilution ground, id. at 368–369. The reconciliation preserves those actual findings under the correct claim-specific purpose rule. It does not adopt predominance as a necessary element of intentional dilution or treat the presence of other considerations as conclusive proof against unlawful purpose.

### Complete operative grounds

**Statutory results count: six direct joins.** A claimant asserting this district-fragmentation injury must establish relevant opposing-bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat the protected group's preferred candidates, with special circumstances and the relevant nomination and election stages considered. Supported failure of that condition defeats this results claim without requiring retention of a numerical-majority district and without deciding the first-capacity route, cohesion or ultimate totality. The finding must concern real preferred candidates and the affected electorate, not officeholders' race or a statewide balance.

Here the district-specific findings address continuing ability to elect and the nomination stage, including opportunity without reliance on Hispanic aggregation. Contrary expert evidence was considered; its existence does not itself establish clear error. The population change cannot substitute for proof of the voting condition. The six do not adopt a 27.5% safe harbor, fixed crossover percentage, guaranteed victory, right to mere influence or permission to offset a local violation through gains elsewhere. Valdespino's two capacity routes remain available where the first condition must be reached; independent totality remains necessary where the preliminary conditions are met. The earlier numerical formulation recited in the lower opinion is not reinstated.

**Intentional statutory Count II: six direct joins, separately resolved.** The pleaded theory that the commission deliberately diminished minority voting rights fails on the supported contrary purpose finding, independently of the results count's third-Gingles disposition. The actual claimed diminution and participation impairment also failed on separately supported findings. An allegation of intent does not erase those findings, and awareness of racial consequences is not itself a purpose to deny equal opportunity. This is the application of the actual intentional theory, not a new comprehensive intent-only Section 2 test, a requirement that every Section 2 plaintiff prove purpose, or a ruling that every possible intentional statutory claim requires the same Gingles showing. Scalia and Thomas reject this district-allocation claim on their antecedent noncoverage ground instead.

**Fourteenth Amendment: eight direct joins at the stated scope.** The asserted intentional dilution and materially impaired participation require their actual injury, purpose or unequal-treatment predicates and causation. The supported no-dilution and no-chilling findings independently defeat the respective injuries here; the separate absence of a purpose to diminish minority voting rights supplies an additional sufficient ground for the purposeful theory. Participation was examined directly rather than treated as legally irrelevant whenever a preferred candidate can win. The lower court's later nonpredominance analysis is not the governing test, and no Justice joins an inference that other districting purposes necessarily exclude an unlawful one.

Brooks supplies the purpose sequence if prohibited purpose is established as a substantial or motivating factor, including the government's applicable same-decision burden. The threshold purpose finding is not established here, so no unproved same-decision defense is presumed and no burden is silently shifted to plaintiffs after that threshold. Arlington Heights and Feeney distinguish prohibited purpose from awareness, without conferring immunity on political objectives.

**Fifteenth Amendment: eight direct joins.** Assuming without deciding that the Amendment reaches the pleaded dilution theory, the supported absence of purposeful racial abridgment defeats the claim. The lower court's suggestion that legislative dilution categorically falls outside the Amendment is not adopted. Voinovich supplies the preserved coverage reservation and the possibility of disposition on failed purpose.

### Grounds, adverse paths and limits

**Nonparty preclusion: eight direct joins.** Shared political interests, a common desired injunction, notice, competent advocacy and a suit's public importance do not alone establish constitutionally sufficient authority to bind absent persons. Consent, actual control, genuine substantive privity, an authorized representative proceeding or another due-process-consistent arrangement can support binding effect on its actual predicates. They cannot be supplied by a virtual-representation label or a general obligation to intervene. The agreement admitting testimony is not consent to binding adjudication.

Richards's allowance for genuine representative public-right proceedings does not authorize preclusion whenever litigation affects many voters. The lower court identified shared interests and incentives, not the missing representative authority. Its reliance on tactical sequence cannot substitute for that authority, especially where the court disclaimed improper tactics. The conclusion rejects the stated ground without declaring that every possible factual connection is disproved or directing an exploratory hearing to construct another basis. Devlin concerns persons actually bound in a mandatory class and creates no class or representative relationship here.

**Merits: four direct joins for affirmance.** Under controlling Shaw, Miller and Easley, racial predominance in creating a shared territorial electorate does not by itself establish an independently cognizable deprivation. On Count I, favorable reasonable inferences about motive do not cure that legal defect. On Count II, the alleged favorable treatment consists of improving minority incumbents' expected reelection prospects through the same district boundaries and resulting electorate. Changing the beneficiary description does not create a separately allocated candidacy right, racial ballot qualification, civic status or material participation restriction on this record.

The strongest objection is that an explicit racial promise of incumbent protection itself constitutes unequal treatment. The four answer by identifying the particular governmental operation: these counts challenge territorial composition and the electoral prospects arising from it. They do not require a second governmental act, monetary loss or electoral defeat to make all unequal treatment actionable. Nor do they reason that a person without a vested right to reelection can never suffer discrimination. The actual mechanism matters. Anderson v. Martin's official racial ballot treatment remains distinct; Adarand supplies justification for a covered racial allocation and does not create the missing deprivation from design alone. No general protection for racial favoritism in officeholding is announced.

The four can affirm without adopting the lower court's nonpredominance inference or using Rule 52 deference appropriate to Page's different trial posture. For this legal ruling they assume the favorable reasonable motive inferences. The broader Fifteenth Amendment coverage question remains reserved; the actual territorial theory supplies no independently cognizable denial or abridgment under the governing boundary.

**Proposed rule: four direct joins for limited vacatur.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reconsider the controlling exclusion of predominant racial assignment itself. Under that proposed cause, Bartels's commitment not to adopt a plan substantially threatening minority incumbents is more than a population number or mere awareness. Considered with the actual line-drawing challenge and favorable reasonable inferences, it requires genuine examination of relative racial priority rather than treating regular shape or the existence of other criteria as conclusive.

The strongest adverse evidence is the detailed, unrebutted account of contiguity, compactness, boundaries, equality, political fairness and voter continuity. The four do not disregard it, recharacterize it as fictitious or infer that all ordinary criteria were abandoned. They conclude that those considerations do not necessarily answer whether race predominated under their proposed test. They would return both actual counts for ordinary proceedings addressing any material disputes and the necessary justification. No automatic trial, proven violation or new map follows. Their position is an express request to reconsider current law, not an assertion that current law already adopts their cause.
