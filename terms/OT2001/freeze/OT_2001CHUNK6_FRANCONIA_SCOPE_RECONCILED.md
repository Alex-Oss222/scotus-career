## Legal grounds retained at that scope

The contract rule remains the original F-C1. On the unrestricted-prepayment promise assumed for accrual review, the Government undertook to accept permitted prepayment upon exercise of the option. Repudiation allows an effective election to treat future nonperformance as present breach; it does not compel that election. Without an effective election, the cause accrues when the promised performance is due and wrongly refused or otherwise breached. The actual timing and any legally sufficient tender excuse require supported borrower-specific determinations. No universal tender, refusal, futility or election is supplied.

Roehm supplies the election rule. The actual and anticipatory components of Mobil distinguish repudiation from a missed performance date; Winstar preserves an actual monetary undertaking despite retained sovereign legislative power. Neither permits an assumed undertaking to become final proof of formation, authority, contractual scope or liability. Stevens's Mobil objection to total repudiation and restitution and Ginsburg's Winstar construction objection remain compatible with the accepted-assumption accrual answer. Bay Area Laundry supplies a completion analogy, not an ERISA timetable; TRW supplies no discovery extension and Young supplies no disabling stay here. The corrected trial-court dates change no legal premise necessary to that answer.

The reviewed Franconia takings claim retains its separate F-T1 ground and confined remedy. The historical Court's Part II-B rejects the dependent temporal dismissal without finding an independent taking or universal finality. That supports the present removal of the categorical ground. The original model's property, act, injury and finality distinctions describe the necessary lawful application on remand under Suitum, Palazzolo and the other entering authorities, rather than a newly decided constitutional entitlement. A contract-performance refusal does not automatically complete every taking, and a genuinely complete alleged appropriation is not automatically postponed by the contract rule. The actual pleaded interest and challenged act matter.

The prior statutory source extract and its complete operative qualifications remain in force. Paragraph (4)'s negotiation duty is distinct from paragraph (5)'s sale procedures and exceptions. The conditional permission to accept prepayment after 180 days without a bona fide offer when transfer funds are available is not an unconditional duty. Paragraph (5)(G)'s continued-use/subsequent-sale route is distinct from its minority-opportunity and nondisplacement-or-available-alternative-housing route; that latter alternative requires sufficient action to make the housing available to each displaced tenant, not adequate supply alone. Paragraph (5)(F)'s fiscal-year restriction, fifteen-month lack-of-budget exception and request-order allocation retain their own terms. No borrower is found eligible, no process is presumed futile, and an exception from paragraph (5) is not made to eliminate paragraph (4).
