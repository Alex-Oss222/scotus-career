# Rucker: 1997 grievance exception and eviction procedure

**Scope:** Neutral completion of the cross-referenced regulatory text only. The supplied event is March 26, 2002. These are the April 1, 1997 edition provisions already implicated by the earlier packet's lease and eviction rules. No later regulatory language, individual compliance finding, merits recommendation or vote is added. Earlier frozen files remain unchanged.

Section 966.51(a)(1) ordinarily applies the PHA grievance procedure to individual grievances between a tenant and the PHA. The exception in (a)(2)(i) requires a HUD due-process determination that the jurisdiction's law requires an opportunity for a court hearing containing the defined safeguards before eviction. With that determination, a PHA **may** exclude a grievance concerning termination or eviction involving either criminal activity threatening the health, safety or peaceful enjoyment of the premises of other residents or PHA employees, or drug-related criminal activity on or near the premises. This is permission, not a requirement to exclude, and the older geographical condition remains part of the text.

The determination concerns the identified judicial eviction procedure. Paragraph (a)(2)(iv) permits use of that procedure without the PHA administrative grievance hearing; it does not authorize removal without a court route satisfying the determination. HUD need not use notice-and-comment procedures to issue a determination, but must publish the listed procedures and make its supporting legal analysis available as stated in (ii)-(iii). Paragraph (b)'s separate exclusions concern tenant disputes not involving the PHA and class grievances; its statement about policy negotiations does not itself adjudicate the federal suit or an individual defense.

Section 966.53(c) requires adequate notice of termination and eviction grounds, the right to representation by counsel, an opportunity to refute the PHA's evidence including confrontation and cross-examination and presentation of any affirmative legal or equitable defense, and a merits decision. The exact complete definition follows.

The separate section 966.4 requirements still operate. Notice periods differ for nonpayment, threats to health or safety, and other cases. Notice must identify specific grounds, the tenant's reply right, document access and, when required, the right to request a grievance hearing. A required grievance opportunity delays termination until the request period expires and, upon a timely request, the grievance process concludes. When the PHA may exclude the grievance and actually chooses exclusion, the notice must state the lack of a grievance-hearing entitlement, identify the judicial eviction procedure and HUD's determination, and identify the applicable criminal-activity category.

Court eviction and administrative eviction remain distinct. Under section 966.4(l)(4), eviction without a court action requires jurisdictional authorization for an administrative action after a due-process administrative hearing, and an opportunity for a pre-eviction hearing under the PHA grievance procedure. The judicial-procedure exception is not permission to omit that administrative-route safeguard. Section 966.4(m)'s requested document access and its prohibition on proceeding when access is denied remain operative; section 966.4(n) incorporates the grievance rules with the express section 966.51(a)(2) exception.

### 24 C.F.R. 966.51, complete section

```text
§ 966.51 Applicablity.
(a)(1) The PHA grievance procedure shall be applicable (except as provided in paragraph (a)(2) of this section) to all individual grievances as defined in § 966.53 of this subpart between the tenant and the PHA.
(2)(i) The term due process determination means a determination by HUD that law of the jurisdiction requires that the tenant must be given the opportunity for a hearing in court which provides the basic elements of due process (as defined in § 966.53(c)) before eviction from the dwelling unit. If HUD has issued a due process determination, a PHA may exclude from the PHA administrative grievance procedure under this subpart any grievance concerning a termination of tenancy or eviction that involves:
(A) Any criminal activity that threatens the health, safety, or right to peaceful enjoyment of the premises of other residents or employees of the PHA, or
(B) Any drug-related criminal activity on or near such premises.
(ii) The issuance of a due process determination by HUD is not subject to 24 CFR part 10, and HUD is not required to use notice and comment rulemaking procedures in considering or issuing a due process determination.
(iii) For guidance of the public, HUD will publish in the FEDERAL REGISTER a notice listing the judicial eviction procedures for which HUD has issued a due process determination. HUD will make available for public inspection and copying a copy of the legal analysis on which the determinations are based.
(iv) If HUD has issued a due process determination, the PHA may evict the occupants of the dwelling unit through the judicial eviction procedures which are the subject of the determination. In this case, the PHA is not required to

provide the opportunity for a hearing under the PHA’s administrative grievance procedure.
(b) The PHA grievance procedure shall not be applicable to disputes between tenants not involving the PHA or to class grievances. The grievance procedure is not intended as a forum for initiating or negotiating policy changes between a group or groups of tenants and the PHA’s Board of Commissioners.
[40 FR 33406, Aug. 7, 1975. Redesignated at 49 FR 6714, Feb. 23, 1984, and amended at 56 FR 51579, Oct. 11, 1991; 61 FR 13273, Mar. 26, 1996]
```

### 24 C.F.R. 966.53(a), individual grievance

```text
(a) Grievance shall mean any dispute which a tenant may have with respect to PHA action or failure to act in accordance with the individual tenant’s lease or PHA regulations which adversely affect the individual tenant’s rights, duties, welfare or status.
```

### 24 C.F.R. 966.53(c), complete basic due-process definition

```text
(c) Elements of due process shall mean an eviction action or a termination of tenancy in a State or local court in which the following procedural safeguards are required:
(1) Adequate notice to the tenant of the grounds for terminating the tenancy and for eviction;
(2) Right of the tenant to be represented by counsel;
(3) Opportunity for the tenant to refute the evidence presented by the PHA including the right to confront and cross-examine witnesses and to present any affirmative legal or equitable defense which the tenant may have;
(4) A decision on the merits.
```

### 24 C.F.R. 966.4(m)-(n), documents and grievance procedures

```text
(m) Eviction: Right to examine PHA documents before hearing or trial. The PHA shall provide the tenant a reasonable opportunity to examine, at the tenant’s request, before a PHA grievance hearing or court trial concerning a termination of tenancy or eviction, any documents, including records and regulations, which are in the possession of the PHA, and which are directly relevant to the termination of tenancy or eviction. The tenant shall be allowed to copy any such document at the tenant’s expense. A notice of lease termination pursuant to § 966.4(l) (3) shall inform the tenant of the tenant’s right to examine PHA documents concerning the termination of tenancy or eviction. If the PHA does not make documents available for examination upon request by the tenant (in accordance with this § 966.4(m)), the PHA may not proceed with the eviction.
(n) Grievance procedures. The lease shall provide that all disputes concerning the obligations of the tenant or the PHA shall (except as provided in § 966.51(a)(2)) be resolved in accordance with the PHA grievance procedures. The grievance procedures shall comply with subpart B of this part.
```
