### Issue 1: Statutory element or mandatory sentencing factor

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Jones v. United States and Castillo v. United States identify aggravated statutory elements through text, structure, tradition, and consequence. Castillo v. United States concerns the former firearm-type language and does not mechanically control the amended brandishing provision.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Congress’s location and label are evidence of statutory meaning, not dispositive constitutional classifications. Avoidance requires an actually plausible competing reading rather than insertion of missing words.
* **Other Governing Law:** 18 U.S.C. §924(c)(1)(A), (4), and the applicable penalty provisions; Fifth Amendment charging protection in federal prosecutions.
* **Material Other Authority:** The current statute states a basic offense followed by graduated minimums for brandishing and discharge. The defined conduct and increased minimum support the competing element reading.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The supplied statutory cases preserve federal element protections where an element exists. They do not decide the constitutional validity of every sentencing factor.
* **Open Point:** Whether the amended structure makes brandishing a sentencing condition or an aggravated offense element.

### Issue 2: A mandatory minimum with an unchanged maximum

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Apprendi v. New Jersey expressly reserves unchanged-maximum mandatory minima and leaves McMillan v. Pennsylvania undisturbed. Its rule for higher authorized maxima therefore does not already decide this question.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Legal punishment consequences must be distinguished from ordinary judicial choice within an authorized range. A new extension requires candid treatment of the contrary received precedent.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment due process and grand-jury protection; Sixth Amendment jury guarantee; the lawful bench-trial waiver and ordinary error-review rules.
* **Material Other Authority:** In re Winship and United States v. Gaudin supply proof and factfinder protections once the fact’s constitutional element status is established.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The existing maximum rule protects a present-offense fact that enlarges the ceiling, while the minimum distinction remains an operative open boundary subject to McMillan v. Pennsylvania.
* **Open Point:** Whether to extend those protections to the fact that removes legally available lower punishment, and what that change means for this uncharged brandishing determination.

### Issue 3: Error review and the sentence’s actual basis

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Olano requires error, plainness, an effect on substantial rights ordinarily shown by the defendant, and an independent determination that discretionary correction is warranted. Johnson measures plainness at appellate consideration when settled contrary trial law changes during direct review; its refusal of relief on clear materiality is record-specific. Neder applies qualified Chapman review to its preserved element omissions and withdrawals, requiring the Government to establish noncontribution to the actual verdict beyond a reasonable doubt.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Merits error, preservation, prejudice and discretionary correction remain separate. Neither a bench-trial waiver nor overwhelming evidence automatically cures a missing element; neither constitutional importance nor a changed range automatically establishes reversible prejudice.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure 51 and 52; the ordinary application of a new constitutional rule to a case pending on direct review; Guidelines §2K2.4 and independently lawful departure requirements.
* **Material Other Authority:** The sentencing court used a lower proof standard on a contested aggravator and imposed the resulting minimum. Its favorable view of seven years and refusal to commit to an unaffected alternative must both be considered. The circuit’s clear-error review of brandishing does not establish a beyond-reasonable-doubt adjudication.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Johnson supplies the intervening-law plainness rule and separate correction inquiry; Neder does not already classify every sentencing error as harmless or structural. The proposed same-term dispositions in Cotton, Angle and Ring are not controlling judgments.
* **Open Point:** Whether the actual error satisfies the applicable review requirements, including whether relief is warranted even assuming the newly added constitutional theory was forfeited, and whether any independently established sentencing basis preserves the seven-year judgment.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Fourth Circuit treated brandishing as a sentencing factor and followed McMillan because the maximum did not change. **Harris:** A fact compelling additional imprisonment should receive element protections; the disputed finding was made under a lower standard, and the mandatory finding produced the guideline sentence. **United States:** Congress used sentencing language; seven years remained within the statutory exposure, and the sentencing judge considered it appropriate even while declining to commit to that result after reversal. **Remedial limits:** Availability of seven years is distinct from a judgment independently selecting that sentence under lawful procedures. The preserved statutory objection and later constitutional argument must not be conflated, and a new merits rule supplies neither automatic reversal nor a substitute aggravator finding.

## Controlling change supplied by Angle

Angle Part I-A holds that a present-offense fact legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or binding floor of punishment must receive element protections: a valid element admission or proof beyond a reasonable doubt to the constitutionally proper factfinder. Ordinary judicial assessment remains permissible within independently authorized punishment. Six Justices directly adopt that rule: Souter, Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg. McMillan is overruled insofar as ordinary judicial present-offense findings may raise a mandatory floor merely because the maximum is unchanged. The source packet's earlier McMillan-retention premise is superseded to that extent.

Angle Part I-B separately requires applicable federal grand-jury charging protection for a covered aggravating fact. Souter, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg directly adopt that holding. It does not depend on a join in a different rationale. A quantity-free charge, ordinary sentencing findings and notice of exposure do not supply the element; an admission or waiver must satisfy its own requirements.

Angle preserves all four Rule 52(b) conditions and the ordinary defendant prejudice burden. Its count-excess plainness application concerns settled trial law changed during direct review; it does not announce a timing rule for every unsettled question. A lawful inevitable aggregate may bear on prejudice, but hypothetical exposure or a disputed range calculation does not establish that aggregate. Cotton's particular fourth-condition refusal of relief remains intact; neither Cotton nor Angle transforms evidence into a verdict or makes correction automatic.

## Harris: the statutory and constitutional questions after Angle

Jones and Castillo retain their actual statutory holdings. The amended brandishing provision still requires its own text-and-structure analysis. A plausible element construction may resolve the statutory issue, but a sentencing-factor label does not avoid Angle's governing constitutional rule when the present-offense fact legally raises the binding minimum. Retaining McMillan's displaced minimum distinction would now require express reconsideration of Angle, rather than ordinary application of existing law. The supplied alternatives must be assessed on that corrected basis.

The lawful bench-trial waiver changes the factfinder but does not supply the omitted allegation or convert the contested preponderance finding into proof beyond a reasonable doubt. The sentencing statutory objection and the later constitutional argument remain distinct for preservation. If correction is assessed under Rule 52(b), each condition retains independent work; if the applicable preserved-error rule governs, its burden and limits remain separate. The judge's approval of seven years and refusal to commit to it without the brandishing ruling must both be considered. No independently imposed alternative sentence may be inferred. Any sentence relief preserves the basic conviction and supplies neither an automatic five-year sentence nor authority for a new aggravated trial.

The remaining lawful analytical alternatives include statutory-element construction with its error consequences; factor construction followed by application of Angle's constitutional floor rule; supported reconsideration of that new precedent; and a distinct conclusion under the applicable error-review conditions. A merits conclusion is not itself a remedy.

## Ring: capital eligibility remains a separately presented boundary

Angle establishes the binding-range principle but does not decide Walton's capital allocation or expressly overrule Walton. The opening Apprendi capital reservation therefore cannot be described as already resolved by Angle. Ring requires a direct decision whether the range principle extends to the additional aggravating fact necessary for death eligibility, and explicit treatment of Walton if its contrary allocation is displaced. The legal fact necessary to authorize death remains distinct from ultimate selection, mitigation, weighing and incorporation of federal grand-jury protection against the States.

The two principal paths remain retention of Walton with a reasoned capital distinction, and application of the element principle to capital eligibility with express overruling of the contrary allocation. Angle materially changes the available range-law premise but does not mechanically decide between those paths. Atkins's separate categorical limit for established intellectual disability does not allocate aggravator factfinding.

Arizona's actual-record harmlessness argument remains distinct from that constitutional issue. A robbery verdict does not become a pecuniary-gain finding by its label. Any applicable Neder inquiry must preserve its burden, actual-verdict or qualifying uncontested-evidence requirements, and prohibition on new appellate credibility findings. Strong aggravating evidence is not itself a verdict; omission of a jury finding does not erase the need to decide an applicable error-review argument. The remedy must preserve the underlying conviction and avoid directing an independently unauthorized new capital proceeding.
