SHOULD REMAND THIS CASE TO THE 
COURT OF APPEALS FOR CONSIDERA- 
TION OF GARY CONE’S SIXTH AMEND- 
MENT CLAIMS UNDER STRICKLAND. 

Because the Sixth Circuit resolved Cone’s ineffective 
assistance of counsel claims by relying upon Cronic, it was 
unnecessary for that court to consider how Cone’s ineffec- 
tive assistance of counsel claims should be resolved under 
the two-pronged test of Strickland v. Washington. This 
Court does not ordinarily pass upon questions that were 
not considered and decided by the lower courts. See, e.g. 
Gilbert v. Homar, 520 U.S. 924, 935-936 (1997); Wright v. 
West, 505 U.S. 277, 297 (1992). If therefore, this Court con- 
cludes that Strickland provides the Sixth Amendment 
rule applicable to Cone’s circumstances, it should remand 
this case to the Sixth Circuit to apply Strickland’s two 
pronged test to the record facts. 

Such a disposition would be required by Supreme 
Court Rule 14.1(a) which states “Only the questions set 
out in the petition [for certiorari], or fairly included 
therein, will be considered by the Court.” The certiorari 
petition here raised solely the questions (1) whether the 
Court of Appeals had improperly applied a de novo stan- 
dard of review in alleged contravention of 28 U.S.C. 
§2254(d), and (2) whether the Court of Appeals had 
improperly bypassed the rules of Strickland by deciding 
Cone’s Sixth Amendment claim under Cronic. No ques- 
tion of the proper application of Strickland itself to the 
facts of this record is subsumed in either of these ques-

review, and the Court of Appeals is the proper forum to 
conduct such review in the first instance. Yist v. 
Nunnemaker, 501 U.S. 797, 806 (1991).
