## Source conclusion

The ALJ's Findings and Conclusions section reports Castro's testimony in present tense about using the borrowed certificate and false identification for work in this country. This is actual published factfinding material, not merely a later party invention. It gives evidentiary support to a reading that the practice continued through the 1993 hearing. The underlying recital, however, identifies no particular postdischarge employer, date of presentation, or interim job at which a document was tendered. It contains no express finding that documents were tendered for each or any identified interim job. Those more specific propositions would be inferences from the general present-tense admission, not the terms of an identified finding.

The Board separately recounts the same present-tense admission and the explanation of why the certificate was borrowed. Thus a neutral account cannot state categorically that there is no evidence supporting continued use. It also cannot state as an undisputed, job-specific fact that Castro used fraudulent documents to obtain the listed interim jobs. The competing temporal readings require explicit attribution.

The ALJ characterizes the driver's license and social security card as apparently derived from the certificate. That characterization is an inference recorded by the ALJ; it is not a reproduced admission about how those documents were procured.

The Board's no-exception observation is expressly about receiving the evidence and the finding that Castro obtained employment with the fraudulent birth certificate. The observation does not separately say that a particular postdischarge tender was established or unchallenged.

The Board affirmatively found reasonable mitigation efforts, specifying work as a carpenter's helper, gardener, day laborer, and mechanic's assistant, plus unsuccessful factory applications. It cut off backpay on June 14, 1993, when Hoffman learned of the initial fraud and lack of authorization. These are distinct lower-adjudication determinations. They do not resolve whether a particular interim job entailed another document violation. If mitigation and cutoff are not challenged on review, they remain the remedial posture; a statutory challenge to awarding backpay can still raise the legal significance of continued unauthorized work or document fraud. It cannot silently replace the Board's factual findings with an assumption that every interim job involved a new false tender.

### ALJ factual recital, Pet. App. 92a

~~~text
The Respondent discovered at the backpay hearing that 
Castro used fraudulent identification in applying for work 
with the Respondent. Castro used a fraudulent birth certificate 
as identification for immigration purposes.’ Castro testified 
that he used a friend’s birth certificate and that he uses that 
certificate to work in the United States. Castro also had a 
California driver’s license and a social security card evidently 
obtained by use of the fraudulent birth certificate. He testified 
that he uses the false identification in order to work in this 
country. Castro is not authorized to work in this country. The 
Respondent contends that since it did not know that Castro 
was not authorized to work it cannot be now ordered to 
employ him in contradiction of the immigration laws.
~~~

### Board mitigation finding, Pet. App. 86a n.12

~~~text
We additionally have considered and rejected the Respondent's 
argument that Castro is not entitled to backpay because he cannot, 
according to the Respondent, make reasonable efforts to find interim work 
in light of his undocumented status. The record shows that Castro worked 
as a carpenter's helper, gardener, day laborer, and mechanic's assistant 
following his unlawful layoff, and in addition unsuccessfully applied for 
work at certain factories. Thus, we find that Castro satisfied his obligation 
to make reasonable efforts to find interim work following his unlawful 


layoff by the Respondent.
~~~
