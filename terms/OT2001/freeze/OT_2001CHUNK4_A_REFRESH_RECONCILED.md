### Supported components and remedies

**M-A — adverse effect remains necessary here.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas require a conflict adversely affecting counsel's performance before dispensing with ordinary Strickland outcome prejudice in this no-objection setting. Judicial failure to inquire does not itself establish that predicate. Holloway's automatic remedy concerns compelled conflicted joint representation after counsel's timely objection; the known risk here does not establish that distinct trigger. Wood's actual-conflict language does not create a separate route dispensing with adverse effect on these facts. On the granted legal question, these four affirm denial of habeas relief, leaving the existing adverse-effect determination operative. They neither find that no confidential information existed nor require proof of a different verdict once the applicable conflict exception is established. They reserve Sullivan's comprehensive application outside its established setting.

**M-S — Stevens's disclosure, choice and protective-duty ground.** Stevens would set aside the conviction and obtain a new trial because the State appointed the murder victim's recent lawyer in a capital case without disclosure and the defendant's informed acceptance, despite the appointing judge's knowledge. Counsel's disclosure duty, the accused's meaningful opportunity to reject this incompatible appointment and the court's protective duty are related constitutional protections. The combination presents the exceptional circumstances in which Cronic permits presumed impairment of meaningful assistance; Holloway, Sullivan and Wood support the judicial safeguard. Continuing confidentiality after death matters under Swidler, but no particular confidence or omitted defense is found. Stevens's rule is not that any remote former representation or every ethical breach invalidates a conviction. It is also not a request for a further hearing to discover whether the State-created, undisclosed victim-counsel relationship satisfies an additional factual test before relief becomes due.

**M-SO — Souter's prospective judicial-duty ground.** Souter would reverse the habeas denial and require a new trial because the judge had notice, before the representation unfolded, of the particular risk and did nothing to examine it, secure an intelligent waiver or arrange replacement when warranted. His rule requires a sufficiently identified risk triggering the judicial duty, not suspicion about any former client. Holloway's preventive protection should not depend exclusively on counsel objecting when the court already has adequate notice; a potentially conflicted lawyer's silence is an especially poor reason to eliminate the safeguard. Souter expressly distinguishes this prospective duty from later discovery of a conflict. He reads Wood's retrospective inquiry as requiring actual conflict and adverse effect; he does not support the model's characterization of Wood as an actual-conflict-only remand in this case. His discussion of possible adverse effects at 535 U.S. 208–209 n.13 expressly recognizes that factual issue was not accepted for review. It supplies no separate factual holding or new hearing directive here.

**M-BG — Breyer and Ginsburg's narrow structural ground.** Breyer and Ginsburg would reverse the habeas denial and obtain a new trial because the State itself supplied the recent criminal lawyer of the murder victim to represent the accused in a capital case. The immediate and egregious incompatibility, the capital proceeding's use of the victim's character and the State's creation of the arrangement make this a structural breakdown under Cronic and the trial-framework distinction in Fulminante. The risk and difficulty of reconstructing the effects support a categorical remedy at this specific conjunction of facts; they are not a finding that the completed federal hearing was procedurally defective. These two expressly decline to resolve the general relationship among Holloway, Sullivan and Wood or to join a general known-risk/noninquiry rule. Their position is therefore neither M2 nor an implicit join to Souter's broader prospective-duty theory. Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton supplies their concern for institutional fairness and integrity; it does not turn an appearance concern alone into the general Sixth Amendment rule.

The four relief Justices support the same ultimate constitutional remedy but not a single four-Justice rule governing when it is owed. Their votes cannot be counted as four direct joins to Stevens's framework, Souter's framework, Breyer and Ginsburg's framework, or the rejected M2 rule. No Marks calculation is appropriate merely because four positions favor relief. Their historical separate writings establish distinct grounds; historical authorship and joins do not preassign any in-world writing.

### Reconciled common propositions and strongest alternatives

**W-A — permissible functional duration.** Under the twelve-month branch of the adult definitions in sections 423(d)(1)(A) and 1382c(a)(3)(A), the agency may require the disabling inability produced by the impairment to last, or be expected to last, continuously for at least twelve months. The grammar does not unambiguously require impairment-only duration, and the integrated work-disability definition permits the agency's functional construction. The expected-death branch remains distinct. The five-month Title II waiting period does not compel an impairment-only reading and has no identical SSI counterpart. This sustains a lawful agency choice; it does not hold that Congress has excluded every different reasonable implementation.

**W-B — permissible use of actual predecision work.** The statute does not fix an earlier assessment date at which a reasonable expectation of twelve months becomes irrevocable before a favorable disability finding. The agency may use known actual substantial work within twelve months, occurring before the relevant notice finding disability, to reject an initial duration claim. The words concerning expected duration permit timely prospective decisions without requiring the agency to ignore later-known facts in the initial adjudication. Section 422(c)'s cessation protection presupposes qualifying Title II entitlement; it does not independently establish a missing initial disability predicate. The agency's choice is reasonable, not an inevitable single construction of the statute.

**W-C — application and remedy.** Walton's October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding was affirmed below and remains undisturbed. His later initial closed-period award was superseded on administrative review; no favorable disability-finding notice before the return is supplied. The circuit's impairment-only and accrued-expectation/trial-work grounds, including its independent unreasonableness conclusion, therefore do not sustain its reversal. All eight support reversing those legal rulings and remanding for implementation of the denial at the reviewed scope. No new earnings calculation, medical-recovery finding or classification of Walton's work as an unsuccessful attempt is made.

The strongest impairment-only argument is that the relative clause directly modifies impairment and that entitlement does not depend on prior payment or a completed award. The latter proposition is accepted; the former does not render the linked functional interpretation impermissible. The strongest early-entitlement argument is that an earlier reasonable expectation already accrued entitlement, making the return protected trial work. The statute does not identify that earlier point as controlling regardless of later facts. The agency's reasoned distinction between initial eligibility and cessation, and between a current prediction and retrospective reconstruction, permissibly answers it.

Adjudication timing can produce different outcomes for otherwise similar claimants. The agency expressly acknowledged and defended that consequence as an accommodation of prompt prospective awards and exclusion of demonstrated short-term disability. The Associates accept its reasonableness here without declaring administrative delay generally harmless or treating the work-incentive purpose as irrelevant. They do not elevate that purpose into an overriding command that erases the initial duration condition. These are answers to both circuit grounds, not a conclusion that denial of deference alone decides the case.

### Qualifications that remain operative

The exclusion in section 404.1592(d)(2)(iii) requires both substantial work within twelve months of onset of the disabling impairment and work before notice of a determination or decision finding disability. A favorable disability finding, its notice, an award, payment and statutory entitlement are distinct. A claimant returning more than twelve months after onset but before adjudication may obtain trial-work protection if duration and the other entitlement conditions are satisfied; passage of twelve months alone is insufficient if disabling inability ended earlier for another reason. A claimant returning within twelve months after a notice finding disability may qualify even when that notice precedes the benefit-award notice. Neither branch requires prior receipt of payment. The holding does not decide every reopening or reliance issue concerning an already favored claimant.

The waiting-period-work exclusion is separate. The pre-application exclusion concerns months before application, not permanent disqualification because the claimant once worked after medical onset. Statutory blindness and the separate child definition are not adjudicated. Title II insured status, application, age, the ordinary five-month waiting condition and the qualifying prior-entitlement route remain separate; the latter covers qualifying entitlement or disability cessation within the statutory sixty-month window. None is silently transferred to SSI.

Trial-work services are not identical to substantial gainful activity. The operative nine service months fall within sixty consecutive months and need not themselves be consecutive. New medical or other evidence independent of protected trial work may establish cessation under the governing rules before nine months. No repeated trial-work period is created within the same period of disability or entitlement. The separate unsuccessful-work-attempt rules retain their own predicates, which are not newly found for Walton. The present rule does not determine new earnings thresholds or invalidate unrelated portions of the final document.
