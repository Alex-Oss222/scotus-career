## Components and final operative positions

**C1-R — mixed cable–Internet coverage, reversal:** Sections 224(a)(4) and (b) cover a qualifying attachment by a cable television system even when it also carries Internet service. The specific formulas in (d) and (e) retain their mandatory operation when applicable but do not exhaust or narrow the antecedent attachment definition. Classification of Internet access and the lawfulness of a particular chosen rate are not necessary to reverse the categorical jurisdictional exclusion. Reverse the Eleventh Circuit on this component and remand consistently with coverage. Do not approve the cable formula for every commingled attachment, settle the classification, or infer a newly presented rate challenge.

**C1-V — mixed services, agency-explanation remand:** Vacate this portion of the appellate judgment and return to the FCC for a reasoned identification of the statutory basis governing its rate regulation: subsection (d), subsection (e), or residual subsection (b)(1). The agency must first classify the offered cable-modem access sufficiently to determine whether a mandatory formula applies. Likely ultimate jurisdiction and rejection of a categorical exclusion do not justify allowing the existing agency determination to stand without that explanation. This position does not hold that mixed attachments are excluded; it withholds approval of the agency order and the broader residual-authority conclusion on the present explanation.

**C2-R — wireless coverage, reversal:** An attachment by a provider of telecommunications to the public for a fee is covered without regard to the transmission facilities used, when the attachment and owner satisfy section 224. The wire-communications language defining the utility concerns the covered owner and facilities, not an exclusion of a wireless provider's attached equipment. Reverse the wireless exclusion and remand. Both wireline attachments by wireless providers and their qualifying wireless equipment are covered. The rule does not reach every independent wireless tower or property outside the statutory definitions.

The statutory utility exclusions, incumbent-local-carrier definition and qualifying state-regulation allocation remain. The specific formula controls within its terms; general coverage supplies no permission to evade it. The text supplies the sufficient coverage ground, and no new general deference holding is required. Attachments of wholly different kinds are outside these questions. No classification factual premise about the common-carrier status of particular cable companies is adopted.

## Selected separate-position substance

## Final operative paths

**A — serious difficulty, vacate and remand:** Due process does not require total or complete inability to control behavior. It does require proof that the present qualifying condition causes serious difficulty controlling dangerous conduct, sufficient in light of the condition's nature and severity to distinguish the eligible person from a dangerous but ordinary recidivist. Diagnosis and predicted danger alone do not supply that functional limit. The determination need not take mathematical form, use a single clinical label, establish involuntariness of every act, or follow a universally prescribed jury-instruction formula. The legal inquiry is functional; psychiatric classifications inform rather than decide it.

Vacate the Kansas Supreme Court's complete-incapacity construction and remand for application of the serious-difficulty rule under Kansas's prescribed beyond-a-reasonable-doubt burden. The trial commitment is not automatically reinstated. The nature of the diagnosed condition and evidence of actual control impairment must be assessed together; no rule makes antisocial personality disorder automatically sufficient or insufficient. Labels distinguishing emotional, cognitive and volitional problems do not substitute for this functional inquiry. No general holding on confinement justified solely by an emotional abnormality without the functional showing is supplied by this record. Any independently preserved punitive-operation objection remains separate, with its own claim and proof; this grant supplies no new punishment adjudication.

**B — no additional independent control element, reverse and remand:** Reverse the complete-incapacity requirement and return for disposition under the statute's genuine present abnormality-or-disorder and causally related dangerousness predicates. The Constitution does not demand a freestanding additional control finding beyond those substantively limiting predicates. The separate issue of proved punitive operation remains governed by Hendricks and Seling. This position is not a diagnosis-only or dangerousness-only authorization and does not treat the high proof burden as creating a missing substantive predicate. It regards the actual qualifying disorder causing the danger as the substantive distinction, without adding a separately measured degree of volitional incapacity.

Both paths reject complete incapacity. That shared rejection does not make B a join in A's additional substantive requirement or in its application remand. An affirmance importing Hendricks's punishment facts is unavailable. A total-incapacity rule lacks controlling support and would exclude serious disorders merely because some volition remains.

# Lee v. Kemna — frozen historical reconciliation

**Event and cutoff:** No. 00-6933; January 22, 2002, after October 29, 2001 argument. All nine participate. Only the eight Associate commitments are fixed here; no Chief position or author is supplied. This case and Crane use their common pre-January-22 baseline.

## Final rules and remedies

**A — exceptional inadequacy:** Vacate the Eighth Circuit judgment treating the continuance-rule omissions as an adequate state ground and remand for consideration of the due-process claim under the remaining applicable habeas law. Ordinarily enforceable state procedures can be inadequate when applied exorbitantly in exceptional circumstances. Here the combined sudden absence of subpoenaed witnesses previously present at trial, the substantial information already supplied about the alibi and anticipated return, the brief requested delay, and the judge's stated scheduling impediments make rigid insistence on additional written and repeated showings inadequate to bar federal consideration. The conclusion respects Missouri's construction of its rules and decides the federal adequacy question. It does not create a general emergency exception, require a prior case on identical facts, impose an obligation to announce every procedural defect, or infer opponent consent from silence.

Rule 24.09 requires a written motion and affidavit unless the adverse party consents to an oral application. Rule 24.10 independently requires materiality and diligence; the absent witness's identity and residence if known, or diligence to learn them, and reasonable grounds to expect procurement of attendance or testimony within a reasonable time; the expected particular facts and absence of another available person who could prove them as fully; and absence not procured, consented to or contrived by the applicant, with good faith rather than vexation or delay. If the court considers the affidavit insufficient, the rule requires it to permit amendment. The oral exception to 24.09 does not erase those substantive purposes. The A position rests on this record's substantial presentation, not a finding that the oral exception was exercised or every state form was literally satisfied.

Osborne supplies the distinction between useful presentation and purposeless repetition after the matter has been adequately placed before the trial judge. Coleman retains genuinely independent and adequate grounds; Ford preserves fair notice and regularity without making those features alone conclusive. Trest's nonjurisdictional rule does not itself decide adequacy. The legitimate information and trial-management purposes of the Missouri rules are recognized. The answer to the dissent is that the immediate record gave the essential practical basis for the short request and that the judge's stated inability to continue was not curable by a perfect written motion. This is a combined exceptional application, not a general judicial power to excuse forms whenever the requested delay seems reasonable.

**B — adequate default:** Affirm the federal default judgment. Rule 24.10 supplies a legitimate, established and applicable substantive information requirement even if one assumes the writing form alone would be inadequate in this emergency. Prior descriptions of alibi testimony and earlier attendance do not necessarily establish the witnesses' current expected testimony and availability, diligence, or all other facts material when the continuance is requested. The State has an interest in contemporaneous focused presentation and a reviewable record. An uncited procedural basis may support appellate affirmance; the judge's scheduling reason does not eliminate an otherwise useful rule. Osborne's futile repeated legal objection is distinguishable. This position does not hold that every formally regular state rule is adequate regardless of its actual operation.

For A, removal of this default bar neither resolves the constitutional merits nor converts the relatives' account of an officer directing their departure into a finding. The asserted cause and innocence routes need not be decided to remove the inadequate bar. The separate lower-court gateway rulings are not affirmatively reversed by this disposition, and no new hearing entitlement is created. For B, affirmance likewise rests on adequacy and the preserved lower judgment, without new factfinding about abandonment, fabrication or innocence. The state conviction remains in force pending lawful further proceedings under A, or stands under B.

The platform-integration contention nevertheless requires an adequate inquiry into category fit and actual competitive mechanism. No efficiency finding follows from calling the software integrated.

**T2-R — rule of reason for this combination:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the rule-of-reason direction for the tying arrangement presented, including the contractual and removal restrictions. The holding is confined to this combination of a principal platform for third-party applications and complementary software functionality; it is neither software immunity nor a general rule that mentioning an efficiency defeats an established category. The actual explanation must be tested against each actual restriction. Distributing application interfaces may justify something different from forcing the full browser or prohibiting its subsequent removal. The rule permits a suitably abbreviated inquiry when actual experience, direct effects and the mechanism suffice; it does not invariably demand a comprehensive market-definition or industry study.

**T2-D — differentiated categorical route:** Stevens agrees that genuine functional integration requires fuller competitive inquiry but opposes a blanket direction covering every restriction. Where a separable contractual or removal restraint fits the ordinary forced-tie category and its remaining elements, that established category remains available. The lower court must distinguish that conduct from genuinely integrated functionality; technological accompaniment alone is no exception. He would modify the remand accordingly, without reinstating the whole trial judgment or deciding ultimate liability.

**T3 — preserved tying-remand limits:** Under T2-R, plaintiffs pursuing the claim must prove net anticompetitive effect in browser competition, considering the benefits actually supported for each restriction. Operating-system injury supporting section 2 does not itself establish the section 1 browser-market injury. The restrictions on OEM removal and the omitted removal utility remain possible section 1 theories; any additional remedy must be justified. The default-browser override's rejected section 2 ground does not foreclose rebuttal of its justification on the distinct browser-market question. Price bundling requires resolution of the positive browser-attributable price increment on this actual record, followed, if proved, by the effects/benefits inquiry. Conflicting evidence about competitors' discounts remains unresolved. Commingled code is not silently added as an independent trial tying basis.

The existing attempted-monopolization reversal is not reopened. Its case-specific remand preclusion remains: plaintiffs may not pursue tying injury dependent on a precise browser-market definition or independent entry barriers they failed to establish for the attempted-monopolization claim, apart from what is implicit in the tying arrangement. This is preservation of this lower mandate, not a universal antitrust rule. Stevens's differentiated route preserves the same unpresented-claim boundary and the ordinary requirements of the category actually used. No Justice grants final tying liability, a finding of reasonableness or automatic additional relief.

**D1 — violation and reassignment:** All eight affirm the established section 455(a) violation and assignment of further proceedings to a different judge. The cumulative undisclosed press relationship on pending merits and remedy questions creates a reasonable appearance of partiality. The violation does not depend on deciding actual bias. Even the former Liteky concurrence's extra judicial-source concern does not shield these outside dealings. The holding applies the governing objective standard; it does not adopt an independent constitutional structural-error rule or a ban on every judicial public statement.

**D2 — existing remedy:** All eight preserve vacatur of the entire June 7, 2000 remedial decree, both structural and conduct provisions, and require lawful remedial proceedings before another judge. The lower court's independent grounds—unresolved remedy-specific factual disputes without an appropriate hearing, inadequate remedial explanation and materially altered liability foundations—remain sufficient. Relief must be tied to the liability lawfully established and a proper remedial record. Neither mandatory divestiture nor categorical immunity from it is ordered; competing predictions about division remain proffers requiring adjudication at their proper stage.

**D3-F — further tailored liability inquiry:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it conclusively rejects additional section 455 relief from retained liability findings and dependent conclusions. Remand through the appellate court for the new judge to apply Liljeberg's risks of injustice to the parties, injustice in other cases and damage to public confidence, and determine which material factual foundations require fresh determination. The inquiry must address the pre-findings timing and merits subjects of the concealed contacts. Ordinary clear-error review cannot itself replace neutral evaluation of contested credibility or permissible competing inferences. Undisputed matters and independently sustainable legal conclusions may remain at their established scope; prior legal rulings remain pertinent to facts lawfully determined. Reuse of an appropriate record is permitted. No all-issues retrial, automatic invalidation of every finding, or requirement to prove actual bias in a particular finding is imposed.

**D3-P — preserve the appellate allocation:** O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer affirm retention of the liability findings and conclusions surviving ordinary merits review, while preserving reassignment and full decree vacatur. Their sufficient reason is the combined Liljeberg assessment: innocent plaintiffs' burden, the actual differentiation of sustained and rejected liability bases, limited additional deterrence and the public-confidence protection furnished by replacing the judge and entire remedy. They expressly account for contacts predating findings and do not say that all misconduct occurred after liability. They accept the lower allocation as sufficient in this case; they do not make actual bias necessary for broader relief or declare clear-error review an automatic cure. No new appellate finding or general harmlessness rule is adopted.

All eight accept the common Liljeberg and Rule 52 propositions. D3-F and D3-P disagree over the sufficiency of this particular allocation, not over whether actual bias is required or whether appellate review retries facts. Those shared rules cannot be used to erase the genuine remedial division.

## Selected separate-position substance for assembly

Stevens's tying position preserves the ordinary per se category for separable coercive restrictions that satisfy its elements. Functional integration and independent demand must both be considered; a possible benefit from common interfaces does not make compulsory retention of a complete browser an innovative design. His alternative is a differentiated legal classification and remand, not automatic liability. Jefferson Parish, actual Kodak and the concrete-restraint approach of his California Dental dissent join support the distinction. The seven T2-R Justices answer that the relationship between product definition, interface distribution and actual restriction should be resolved through the bounded effects inquiry here; that process can reject a justification that does not explain a given restriction.

The D3-F position requires more than remedy-stage replacement because the undisclosed conduct began before factfinding and concerned the findings' very subjects. Liljeberg's public-confidence and future-injustice concerns do not demand actual-bias proof; clear-error review does not independently choose among permissible credibility or expert assessments. A new judge should determine what may remain binding and decide anew the material foundations requiring reconsideration, while retaining independently supportable matters and reusing an appropriate record. This is tailored relief, not presumptive universal taint.

The D3-P position gives independent weight to innocent plaintiffs, the cost and limited additional deterrent value of repeating adjudication, the appellate court's differentiated merits corrections, and public confidence restored by reassignment and replacement of the entire remedy. The misconduct's most pronounced remedial expression helps justify the chosen line but does not erase its earlier beginning.
