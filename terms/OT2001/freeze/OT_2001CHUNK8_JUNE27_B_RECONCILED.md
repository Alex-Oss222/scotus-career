## 117. Zelman v. Simmons-Harris; Hanna Perkins School v. Simmons-Harris; Taylor v. Simmons-Harris

### Historical comparison and legal fork

Historical result: reversal in all consolidated dockets, 5-4. Rehnquist's opinion was joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; O'Connor and Thomas also concurred. Stevens dissented alone; Souter dissented with Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer; Breyer dissented with Stevens and Souter. Ginsburg did not join Breyer's separate dissent. These historical writing relationships confer no assignment or simulated join.

The historical majority's continuous Mueller-Witters-Zobrest private-choice line is not the law entering this case. Zobrest supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding here. Agostini retains Lemon through a unified governmental-authority-and-function inquiry. Jackson and Strout directly address integrated tuition and reject parental routing as a sufficient cure; Mitchell independently requires actual-use proof and confined correction while preserving genuinely private benefits and separated secular work. Brown's private silent interval purchases no instruction. Historical Zelman cannot silently displace those rules.

Components are (A) constitutional attribution of the supported integrated tuition purchases; (B) proof and the limits of affiliation, statistics, neutrality and choice; and (C) the judgment, provider/payment relief and Ohio severability, identically across Nos. 00-1751, 00-1777 and 00-1779. The competing lawful paths are application of the binding tuition rule with confined relief, or express reconsideration at the asserted genuine-individual-benefit relationship. Simply calling capped tuition different from operating-cost payments fails under Strout. Conversely, rejecting routing immunity does not establish every provider's actual use or authorize categorical exclusion.

## 120. Republican Party of Minnesota v. White

### Historical comparison and legal fork

Historical result: reversed and remanded, 5-4. The four sitting Associates who supported reversal were O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer dissented. Scalia's Court opinion had complete joins; O'Connor and Kennedy separately concurred. Both Stevens's and Ginsburg's dissents were joined by all four dissenters. Those writing relationships are not simulated assignments or final joins.

The actual announce clause, its state-adopted constructions and the meaning of nonpromissory campaign announcements remain central. The historical majority and Ginsburg differed about the clause's authoritative meaning and the weight of counsel's oral-argument answers. That is a legal construction dispute in the published sources, not a new factual stipulation. The frozen sustaining commitments do not rely on inventing a promises-only construction: they defend the actual restriction against functional campaign commitments even where a candidate uses no literal promissory formula. The independent campaign-context ground appears in Stevens's dissent and in Ginsburg's explanation of why nonpromissory announcements may exert the same electoral pressure. Historical support therefore survives without pretending that all sources agree on the precise construction.

Components: (A) the actual content restriction and constitutional fit; (B) impartiality, its appearance and the difference between legal views, party bias, closed-minded commitment and promised results; (C) relief and reserved ethics questions. Suster and Kruse forbid a general judicial-office exception from demanding speech review but leave distinct ethics interests intact. Liteky's federal recusal rule does not itself decide a campaign-speech prohibition. Watchtower, BE&K and Trans Union do not replace the applicable strict scrutiny with a universal rule about exceptions or institutional speech.

## 122. People Who Care v. Rockford Board of Education, School District No. 205

### Historical comparison and legal fork

The historical anchor is the Seventh Circuit's 246 F.3d 1073 decision, not a Supreme Court merits vote. The complete original opinion, [saved court text](../sources/chunk8-comparator/people-who-care-ca7.txt), confirms reversal and remand with instructions to grant the school board's requested dissolution effective June 30, 2002. The court expressly declined to give earlier relief than requested. Its conclusion relied on assignment integration, expenditure, its appraisal of plaintiffs' failure to separate causal sources of residual disparities, and the distinction between actual compliance and enthusiasm for a decree. The reported inability of counsel to identify a decree violation is not independently certified from a transcript here.

The entering Freeman and Jenkins rules require the district's complete release showing and its showing that imbalance for which it disclaims responsibility is not traceable to its violation. The historical circuit opinion's demand that plaintiffs partition residual causes cannot replace that allocation. The prior unappealed 1994 intentional-discrimination judgment stands. Satisfaction of a student-assignment measure, spending and the absence of newly proved unlawful acts do not automatically establish completion in every retained domain. Conversely, discrepancy, fear of backsliding, criticism of the decree and an arbitrary waiting period do not justify indefinite control.

Components are (A) full/satisfactory compliance and practical elimination; (B) the defendant's causal showing and review of findings; (C) demonstrated whole-decree good faith; (D) retained-control necessity/practicability, connections and nonobstruction; and (E) the June 30 termination direction. Termination or partial withdrawal can be lawful on the complete showing. Remand here does not presume that all existing measures remain justified; it requires the correct legal inquiry and preserves settled findings under clear-error review.

### Frozen result and mandate limits

All eight Associates vacate the Seventh Circuit's direction to terminate the existing decree effective June 30, 2002 and remand for the complete Freeman/Jenkins inquiry. The displaced direction supplies no dissolution. The lower courts may order lawful full or partial release on the required showing, must define released and retained obligations and supervision, and must assess whether retained control is necessary or practicable for compliance elsewhere and whether release would obstruct unfinished relief. Partial withdrawal is permitted, not compelled. Existing findings receive ordinary clear-error review; no general retrial or appellate substitute factfinding follows.

No new liability claim, national test-score parity, fixed monitoring period, compulsory program, plaintiff consent, neutral reviewer or supervisory institution is imposed. Ruiz's PLRA rule is distinct. Balderas, Page and Robertson concern different territorial electoral acts and do not reopen settled school liability or transfer this release burden to the students.
