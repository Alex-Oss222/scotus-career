## 66. Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it treats the community-standards feature as sufficient by itself, and remand without dissolving the preliminary injunction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Preserve interim protection while the lower courts assess the complete offense, its adult-speech burden, and the unresolved grounds under the proper standard. Do not authorize prosecution merely because one facial argument fails.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The whole statutory restriction must be assessed

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the reviewed community-standards question, but not the unadjudicated ultimate validity of the entire enactment.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether geographic variation alone proves that this statute unconstitutionally suppresses protected expression.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing protection of adult Internet expression and propose a specific holding rejecting a categorical medium-based inference.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Incorporating community standards into this multi-element commercial-Web offense does not, standing alone, establish facial invalidity. Constitutional review must assess the offense’s actual reach, objective limiting elements, defenses, and demonstrated burdens together. That conclusion supplies no presumption that the complete enactment survives.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns the claimed independent defect in COPA. It does not approve criminal punishment for protected adult speech or imply that an Internet speaker can practicably segregate every community.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Reno protects adult Internet speech against a broad criminal restriction, but it does not establish that every reference to community standards independently invalidates a different multi-element law. Stone distinguishes that proposed categorical inference. Miller's objective serious-value limitation and COPA's actual statutory elements and defenses must be considered with the geographic burden; their existence is not a finding that COPA ultimately survives strict scrutiny. The legal function of each element is decisive on the limited question before the Court. A combined assessment is required because one phrase cannot be assessed as though it were the whole offense. The remaining tailoring and alternative-means grounds were not resolved by the appellate rationale, so the preliminary injunction remains while those grounds are decided. No new speech exception or presumption for commercial Web restrictions is announced.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Full statutory validity, the factual adequacy of age verification, and the effectiveness of alternative protections were not finally adjudicated in the reviewed appellate rationale.


## 67. Lapides v. Board of Regents of University System of Georgia

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Eleventh Circuit’s immunity ruling for the state-law claims Georgia permits in its own courts, and remand.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The Board may not defeat those claims by asserting immunity from the federal forum it selected. The district court must independently resolve federal cause-of-action defects, supplemental jurisdiction, and the merits. No damages, reinstatement, or correction of personnel records is ordered by the waiver holding.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Actual submission to the selected forum

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach waiver for the state-law claims, keeping statutory defendant status and the individual defendants' defenses distinct.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the State’s voluntary selection of a federal forum is compatible with an immunity defense based solely on that forum.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinction between actual litigation submission and constructive commercial consent; propose the open removal-waiver holding.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A State that voluntarily removes claims it allows to be litigated against it in its own courts relinquishes its immunity objection to their adjudication in the federal forum it chose. Removal does not create substantive liability, repair an invalid cause of action, or surrender unrelated defenses.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the state tort claims and the State’s own authorized removal. It does not infer consent from receiving regulatory benefits or conducting business.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Clark and Gunter recognize waiver through actual submission to adjudication; College Savings Bank rejects a different fiction of consent from ordinary regulated activity. Stone extends the former rule to authorized removal and distinguishes the latter. Georgia deliberately selected the federal tribunal for claims it permits against itself in state court, making its procedural choice decisive. Letting it then invoke forum immunity would use removal to extinguish adjudication rather than relocate it. This is a categorical litigation consequence within a defined class, justified by coherent administration and actual consent rather than a balance of governmental dignity against the plaintiff's injury. Will's separate statutory defendant rule remains, and supplemental jurisdiction must still be exercised lawfully. No broader rule for claims immune in the State's own courts is necessary to resolve these tort claims.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Removal of claims for which the State retains substantive immunity in its own courts is outside the necessary question. Individual liability and the tort merits require separate adjudication.
