## Components, competing rules and their remedies

### M1: require an actual conflict adversely affecting representation

Under M1 the court's failure to perform an assumed duty to inquire into this particular potential conflict does not by itself remove the requirement that divided loyalty adversely affect counsel's performance. That adverse-effect requirement is distinct from Strickland's ordinary reasonable probability of a different lawful outcome. Once its proper predicate is shown, the conflict exception does not additionally demand ordinary outcome prejudice. Within the reviewed legal question, the existing adverse-effect determination supports affirmance.

Holloway, 435 U.S. 475, supplies the special protection where counsel timely identifies a conflict in joint representation and the court compels continuation without the required inquiry or protection. Sullivan, 446 U.S. 335, supplies the actual-conflict/adverse-effect route where that trigger is absent. Wood, 450 U.S. 261, preserves a duty to inquire into a sufficiently apparent particular conflict and requires an actual-conflict determination; M1 reads its reference to actual conflict within the substantive protection against impaired representation. Noninquiry is therefore not itself a complete denial of counsel under Cronic. The most favorable assumption for Mickens is that the conflict route used below is available here; deciding its application does not require a comprehensive extension to every successive representation.

The strongest contrary argument is that the defendant could not make Holloway's timely objection to a relationship neither counsel nor the appointing judge disclosed. M1 accepts the seriousness of that circumstance and the assumed inquiry duty. It distinguishes the court's preventive obligation from the additional constitutional predicate for collateral relief: notice of a particular risk does not itself establish impaired assistance. Counsel's present refusal to concede adverse effect does not erase the adjudicated finding or expand the granted legal question. Nor does counsel's narrower oral request alone establish that M1 is correct; M1 must still answer the actual-conflict-only Wood argument on its merits.

### M2: actual unwaived divided loyalty after neglected known-conflict inquiry

Under M2, when the court knew or reasonably should have known of the particular conflict requiring inquiry and failed to perform that duty, actual unwaived divided loyalty suffices for the conflict remedy without a further showing that the conflict changed an identifiable performance choice. This is the narrower path maintained by petitioner at argument and already accepted by four Associates in the original handoff.

Actual divided loyalty under this path requires supported, presently incompatible representational obligations or interests in the defense, rather than the bare existence of the former relationship or an abstract professional risk. For this successive representation, an inquiry would have to identify how a continuing duty arising from representation of the victim materially conflicted with counsel's obligations to Mickens. That inquiry is distinct from demanding proof that counsel actually abandoned a particular defense, question or strategy because of the division. Swidler, 524 U.S. 399, supplies survival of confidentiality after death; it supplies neither the contents of a confidence nor the actual incompatibility here. The former client's death does not eliminate the issue, but prior representation does not establish it automatically.

M2 reads Wood's actual-conflict and waiver remand as the specific protection applicable to a court that neglected a known-conflict inquiry. It gives legal significance to Wood's distinction from an uninformed court without dispensing with actual conflict. Holloway explains why reconstructing the effects of divided loyalty can inadequately protect the right; it does not eliminate Wood's actual-conflict condition on this presentation. The strongest contrary argument is that Sullivan's language defines the constitutional conflict through adverse effect and that a federal hearing already tested that subject. M2 answers that Wood's neglected-duty predicate changes the operative inquiry, and an adverse-effect finding cannot alone answer whether actual unwaived divided loyalty existed under that distinct rule. It does not answer by inventing a deficient hearing.

The formal position is vacatur of the adverse-effect-based denial and a bounded remand. The unresolved legal task is whether the supported existing findings establish or negate actual unwaived divided loyalty under M2's distinction, rather than whether adverse effect has again been proved. The lower court must use findings that answer that question; it must not discard them merely because petitioner dislikes their result. A properly independent finding that no actual divided loyalty existed under this rule would remain sufficient to deny relief. No new evidentiary hearing is automatically required. Additional development remains subject to ordinary lawful predicates. This Court does not itself find actual conflict, absence of waiver, or entitlement to a new trial on the supplied material.

### M3: potential-conflict-only reversal is not adopted on this presentation

The earlier Stevens row additionally favored protection based on the undisclosed court-created victim-representation risk without a separate actual-conflict finding. The express oral limitation is material to whether this case should decide that broader rule. The refreshed commitment withdraws M3 as a ground for deciding this case. Stevens continues to regard the protective rationale as substantial, but M2 supplies his sufficient adjudicative ground within the maintained request and Wood's actual-conflict condition. He does not transform a rejected litigation theory into an additional holding merely because it could produce the same remand.

This is a reasoned scope choice, not a holding that the Court invariably lacks power to consider a legal proposition counsel disclaims. The broader rule would require an independent justification for reaching it, its constitutional predicate and its collateral operation. Neither the necessary scope of this disposition nor the fresh source provides that justification. No other Associate supported M3 in the original handoff, and none adopts it now.

## Proposition support, collateral limits and precise remand

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas directly support M1's rule and affirmance. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly support M2's actual-conflict-only rule and vacatur/remand. The latter four do not merely share a judgment while disagreeing on the necessary predicate: all four require actual unwaived divided loyalty and accept the bounded remand described above. No Associate now supports M3 as an adjudicative ground in this matter. No fifth vote is inferred and no Chief Justice position is supplied.

All eight keep actual conflict, adverse effect and ordinary outcome prejudice distinct. Their disagreement is whether the neglected known-conflict duty makes actual unwaived divided loyalty sufficient without a separate effect on performance. An opinion cannot define actual conflict in M1's effect-dependent sense and count the M2 Justices as joining it, or use M2's independent-duty sense and count the M1 Justices as joining it. Agreement that the court should have inquired does not erase that disagreement.

The claim did not receive a state merits adjudication on the supplied record. Section 2254(d) therefore does not bar the inquiry merely because the federal application was filed after enactment. The federal evidentiary hearing does not supply that missing state-merits predicate. The circuit's concealed-fact cause determination remains distinct from default prejudice, constitutional entitlement and relief. M1 preserves denial on the reviewed constitutional ground without needing a new collateral defense. M2 removes the legal adverse-effect prerequisite but does not automatically adjudicate any independent remaining default or nonretroactivity defense.

The M2 Justices view Wood as the preexisting source for this known-conflict protection, not as permission to announce a universal new rule for all successive representation. The transcript supplies no newly established finality date or preservation and content of a Teague defense. The entering Lambrix and Trest principles keep nonretroactivity and procedural default separate and nonjurisdictional; they do not permit assuming a disputed preserved defense away. The bounded remand does not order a writ before an outcome-determinative independent defense is properly resolved. If assembly proposes an unconditional new-trial command or a new-rule disposition, a scoped neutral account of the actual collateral predicates is necessary first.

On M2's remand, existing findings retain their ordinary force insofar as they answer whether material incompatible obligations actually existed and whether any valid waiver occurred. The Court changes the legal requirement; it does not find a previously unverified flaw in evidence-taking or authorize a general second chance to prove adverse effect. A finding that independently negates actual divided loyalty under M2 would still defeat relief. If the existing findings leave that distinct legal component unanswered, the lower court resolves it on the existing record, with further development only when independently lawful and necessary. The present handoff does not establish the substantive conflict or authorize immediate release, acquittal, a new sentence alone or a specified new evidentiary hearing.
