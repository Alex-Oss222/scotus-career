## Historical evidence and the actual changed premise

The supplied comparator reports a summary affirmance on June 17, 2002, at 536 U.S. 919. It reports no individual tally and no explanatory merits opinion. That disposition is strong evidence for leaving the judgment in place, but it supplies neither eight individual affirmance commitments nor an articulated reason for rejecting the statutory challenge. No individual historical vote, dissent, joinder or rationale is invented here. The recovered papers establish the historical litigation positions; they do not reveal the Justices' reasons for the summary order.

The relevant changed legal premise is the effective June 17, 1999 Valdespino holding: a single protected group may satisfy the first Gingles condition through a lawful, geographically compact district with rigorously demonstrated dependable crossover support sufficient to elect its preferred candidates, even without the retained numerical-majority route. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined that holding and its bounded remand. The historical Fifth Circuit numerical-exclusivity rule cited by the congressional court cannot displace it. This is a specific prior adjudication, not a general ideological reason to depart from history.

That change does not mechanically require vacatur here. Appellants asserted seven Latino registered-voter-majority districts; this is not simply another established sub-fifty-percent citizen-voting-age case. Their inference from registration to citizen voting-age population was disputed. They also presented electoral-performance evidence and challenged the compactness and adequacy of the findings. The motions to affirm identify concrete marginality, turnout, possible loss of existing electoral opportunity and geographic deficiencies. Valdespino leaves all those issues, the other voting conditions, causation, independent totality and the claimant's burden intact.

The review question is therefore whether the lower court's compactness, practical-effectiveness and totality determinations furnish a discernible independent lawful basis under the current capacity rule. Four Justices find the explanation inadequate to resolve the actual disputed evidence under both recognized routes; two find independent findings sufficient when read with the trial; two reject the requested statutory district-allocation entitlement at an antecedent level. The new law matters to the first four because population-based rejection is no longer sufficient and their prior joinders require review of supported functional capacity. The source evidence makes that application concrete without establishing that any proposed district succeeds.

## Frozen compatibility and remedial boundaries

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support the same limited vacatur and bounded remand. O'Connor and Kennedy support affirmance on the independent grounds stated above, with Kennedy's narrower explanatory join. Scalia and Thomas support affirmance on statutory noncoverage and supply no join in an application of the dilution framework. The non-controlled tally is four for each disposition. It is not a final Court judgment and does not authorize aggregation of incompatible reasons.

Any limited vacatur reaches only the congressional rejection of the appealed additional Latino-opportunity arrangement. The lower court must apply the governing capacity inquiry and identify any sufficient independent compactness, voting-proof or totality basis; it need not reopen every finding. The claimant still must establish every necessary condition and unequal opportunity. No universal demographic denominator, crossover formula, compactness formula, influence-only entitlement or distinct-group coalition entitlement is adopted. Ordinary procedural law governs any permissible supplementation. The obsolete thirty-seat plan does not revive. Existing election arrangements remain subject to lawful orders; no special election or election timetable is prescribed.

The congressional-only review boundary is a supplied event premise. The historical papers also challenge the Senate map; their presence does not expand this event into separate House or Senate judgments. Affirmance similarly leaves the congressional judgment in place only with whatever rationale obtains actual support at assembly. No capacity holding may be inferred by combining noncoverage votes with votes applying Section 2.

**Historical departure:** A limited-vacatur Court disposition, if supported at assembly, would differ from the historical summary affirmance because the effective Valdespino decision supplies a functional-capacity route absent from the lower court's cited exclusive numerical rule. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer each joined that prior rule and its limited-remand application; on this disputed appellate record each requires a sufficiently identified independent basis before accepting the judgment. This describes their present commitments and the changed institutional result, not undisclosed individual historical votes.
