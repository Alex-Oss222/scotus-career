## 74. Bell v. Cone

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Bell v. Cone
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 685; No. 01-400.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-28. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether counsel’s failure to present additional mitigating evidence and waiver of closing argument at capital sentencing amount to a complete failure of adversarial testing warranting presumed prejudice, and whether the state court’s rejection of the ineffective-assistance claim permits federal habeas relief under AEDPA.
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Sixth Circuit granted relief from the capital sentence under a presumed-prejudice approach. Tennessee seeks reversal of that judgment and restoration of the district court’s denial of habeas relief.
* **Relief Requested:** Tennessee seeks reversal of the habeas grant; Cone seeks a new lawful sentencing proceeding.
* **Material Record:** Cone was convicted of two murders committed during a violent course of conduct. At the guilt phase, the defense presented mental-health and drug-use evidence and his mother’s testimony in support of its theory. At sentencing, counsel referred to mitigating circumstances, cross-examined prosecution witnesses, presented no new defense witnesses, and waived closing argument. The waiver prevented the prosecutor from giving a further rebuttal argument under the applicable sequence. The sentencing jury found aggravating circumstances and imposed death. In state postconviction proceedings, counsel explained the decisions, and the state court rejected the ineffective-assistance claim under the ordinary performance-and-prejudice framework. The challenged acts occurred within a represented proceeding, not one in which counsel was absent for the entire sentencing stage.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The petition is governed by AEDPA. Federal relief requires more than an independent disagreement with the state court’s application of established federal law. The legal categorization under United States v. Cronic is distinct from whether counsel’s particular choices were deficient under Strickland v. Washington. Calling the consequence severe cannot eliminate the need to identify the actual adversarial failure. A strategic label also does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument occurred March 25, 2002. The supplied merits event is May 28, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Horn concerns a preserved Teague retroactivity defense before granting habeas relief; Bell concerns Strickland, Cronic and §2254(d) review of the state merits adjudication. Henderson’s application predates AEDPA. Carey concerns the limitations clock rather than the governing ineffective-assistance standard.
* **Participation Matter:** None independently established for the simulation.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Mickens v. Taylor precedes this event and could materially define presumed prejudice, but no completed simulated decision is supplied. Alabama v. Shelton concerns lack of counsel and cannot be assumed to resolve this distinct performance claim.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Presumed prejudice or particular attorney error

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Roe v. Flores-Ortega distinguishes a complete deprivation of the relevant proceeding from particular errors within represented litigation. Williams v. Taylor requires assessment of informed investigation and the full mitigation case under the ordinary ineffective-assistance framework.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the function allegedly denied and whether counsel failed to perform it altogether. Particular omissions ordinarily require performance and prejudice analysis; strategic characterization must be grounded in reasonable preparation and actual choices.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Sixth Amendment; AEDPA's restriction on relief from state adjudications; the state sentencing record.
* **Material Other Authority:** Strickland v. Washington governs deficient performance and a reasonable probability of a different result. United States v. Cronic describes the narrow circumstances for presumed prejudice from a complete failure of adversarial testing.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** An actual total denial cannot be treated as ordinary harmless attorney error, but a challenge to particular sentencing decisions does not automatically establish that denial.
* **Open Point:** Whether the combination of no new mitigation witnesses and no closing argument, within the described active representation, crosses Cronic's threshold.

### Issue 2: Federal collateral review of the state adjudication

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Williams v. Taylor separates an objectively unreasonable application from a merely incorrect one and rejects the proposition that relief is barred whenever any reasonable jurist could defend the state result.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Apply the correct established rule to the actual state-court adjudication. Deference does not convert speculation about counsel's preparation into a finding; independent disagreement alone does not authorize relief.
* **Other Governing Law:** The then-applicable federal habeas statute and its legal and factual review provisions.
* **Material Other Authority:** Strickland and Cronic supply the underlying federal rules; they are not interchangeable grounds for relief.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The state court's performance and prejudice analysis receives the statutory review, but use of a genuinely wrong legal rule can independently matter.
* **Open Point:** Whether the state adjudication was objectively unreasonable on this sentencing and postconviction record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Sixth Circuit treated the sentencing omissions as an effective abandonment of adversarial testing and presumed prejudice. The state court had instead assessed particular counsel decisions and prejudice under Strickland.

**Tennessee's position.** Counsel remained active, drew on evidence already heard, cross-examined, and used an available procedural choice to prevent damaging rebuttal. Those facts defeat total-abandonment treatment. The state court's rejection of the ordinary ineffective-assistance claim was at least within the statutory bounds of reasonableness.

**Cone's position.** Capital sentencing was the decisive opportunity to explain why he should live. Failing to develop a distinct mitigation presentation and then making no closing argument left aggravation effectively unanswered. Prior guilt-phase evidence did not necessarily perform the individualized sentencing function. He argues that the omissions must be assessed together rather than isolated into innocuous fragments.

**Limits.** The State cannot justify every silence as strategy. Cone cannot obtain presumed prejudice merely by showing that better advocacy was possible. The postconviction findings and counsel's actual preparation matter to the ordinary claim.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal of the Cronic rationale would require evaluation under Strickland and AEDPA, either by the Court on the adequate record or on remand. If the state adjudication satisfies that framework, the writ should be denied. If it was objectively unreasonable and the constitutional deficiency prejudicial, relief would concern sentencing, not automatic acquittal or a judicially imposed life term.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A holding can confine presumed prejudice to complete failure of the relevant adversarial function and treat the identified tactical omissions as ordinary ineffective-assistance claims. It need not immunize all sentencing silence. A contrary Cronic application would have to identify why counsel's documented participation did not amount to adversarial testing of the sentencing case, rather than equate an incomplete presentation with no representation.

**Uncertainty:** An uninformed failure to investigate would differ from a reasonably prepared strategic decision. The supplied record does not authorize inventing additional mitigation witnesses or their testimony.

## 75. Festo Corp. v. Shoketsu Kinzoku Kogyo Kabushiki Co.

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Festo Corp. v. Shoketsu Kinzoku Kogyo Kabushiki Co.
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 722; No. 00-1543.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-28. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a claim amendment required to satisfy patentability, including disclosure requirements, creates prosecution-history estoppel, and whether that estoppel categorically bars all equivalents for the amended limitation or permits a more particular inquiry into surrendered subject matter?
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Federal Circuit, sitting en banc, applied a complete bar to equivalents for claim limitations narrowed to obtain patentability. Its judgment displaced Festo’s infringement recovery. Festo seeks rejection of that categorical rule and reconsideration of the infringement claims.
* **Relief Requested:** Festo seeks vacatur of the Federal Circuit’s complete-bar judgment and renewed consideration of infringement; SMC seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** The patents concern industrial devices using magnetically coupled pistons. During prosecution, claim language was narrowed, including limitations concerning sealing rings and a magnetizable sleeve. SMC’s competing devices used a two-way sealing ring and a sleeve made from a nonmagnetizable material. The asserted infringement depends on equivalence, rather than literal satisfaction of the amended limitations. The reasons for each amendment, the relationship between the amendment and the accused variation, and the technological alternatives reasonably available when the amendment was made matter separately. The record does not permit treating every amendment as directed specifically at SMC’s later design.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The case concerns a legal rule applied to preserved patent claims. There is no identified jurisdictional defect. Whether an amendment triggers estoppel is distinct from what it surrenders. A decision rejecting the complete bar would not itself establish equivalence, rebut an applicable presumption, or reinstate damages without application to the particular limitations. The existing prosecution record must supply the relevant explanation; the Court should not invent examiner demands or undisclosed inventor knowledge.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Federal Circuit’s en banc decision preceded the petition. Argument occurred January 8, 2002. May 28, 2002 is the supplied event date. The time of the amendment, rather than the time of litigation alone, is material to foreseeability and what could reasonably have been claimed.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate listed brief.
* **Participation Matter:** None independently established for the simulation.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** J. E. M. Ag Supply precedes this event but supplies no completed simulated decision and does not itself settle prosecution-history estoppel. No earlier supplied OT2001 decision resolves this question.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Amendment, surrender, and residual equivalents

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Warner-Jenkinson requires assessment of equivalents limitation by limitation, preserves prosecution-history estoppel for surrendered scope, and places on the patentee the burden of explaining an amendment required during prosecution. An unexplained narrowing amendment carries a presumption tied to patentability and the affected limitation. It did not adopt an absolute bar to every equivalent of every amended limitation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Claim language, each material limitation, the prosecution explanation, and the scope actually surrendered must remain distinct. Equivalence cannot erase a limitation, while estoppel cannot be assumed to resolve a subject the prior holding reserved.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Patent Act’s patentability and disclosure requirements define the examination setting. The public receives notice through the issued claims and the prosecution record; the equitable scope of infringement remains bounded by those legal requirements.
* **Material Other Authority:** The Federal Circuit’s en banc reasoning is the position under review. Earlier patent decisions discussed in Warner-Jenkinson illuminate surrender and the importance of a meaningful claim, without supplying a new simulated complete-bar holding.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Required narrowing and unexplained prosecution changes already require an estoppel inquiry. Particular infringement requires comparison with the asserted limitation. The existing rule does not let a patentee recapture what examination required it to relinquish.
* **Open Point:** Whether disclosure-related narrowing is within the trigger and what showing permits residual equivalents after a triggered surrender presumption.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Federal Circuit sought a determinate public boundary by treating a narrowing amendment related to patentability as surrendering the entire space between the original and amended limitations. Its concern was that a flexible estoppel inquiry left competitors unable to rely on the prosecution record.

**Festo’s position.** A complete bar treats a change addressing one defect as surrender of unrelated, unforeseeable substitutes. The doctrine of equivalents has always accommodated the limits of language, and the prior simulated decision preserved it while requiring actual attention to prosecution. Festo requests an inquiry tied to what the amendment reasonably relinquished.

**SMC’s position.** The applicant controlled its claim language and obtained the patent in exchange for narrowing it. Allowing a later equivalents claim makes the concession unreliable and burdens competitors with an unpredictable reconstruction of motives. SMC also argues that any patentability requirement, including disclosure, can justify estoppel, because examination concerns more than novelty alone.

**Limits and alternatives.** Festo cannot rely merely on the desirability of protecting inventors; a surrendered limitation must have consequences. SMC’s notice argument does not establish that an amendment necessarily addresses every later-developed substitute. A rebuttable presumption and an actual-surrender inquiry are materially different possible responses to those competing concerns.

**United States as amicus:** The government proposes a presumption that a narrowing patentability amendment preserves no equivalents for the amended limitation, rebuttable through a concrete showing tied to the prosecution record and the specific accused equivalent. It rejects both the complete bar and an unrestricted flexible inquiry. It requests vacatur and remand to determine whether the actual amendments narrowed relevant subject matter and whether the patentee can establish an exception, including later-developed technology. This intermediate rule must be considered separately from either party’s broader formulation.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance of the complete bar would end the equivalents claim for the affected limitations without resolving technological equivalence itself. Rejection of that bar would require remand to identify the amendments, their patentability relation, and any permitted showing that the asserted equivalent was outside the surrender. The Court could also distinguish whether an amendment triggers estoppel from whether its effect is rebuttable.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** One supported rule would attach presumptive surrender to required narrowing for any Patent Act requirement, while allowing the patentee to demonstrate that an asserted equivalent was unforeseeable, only tangential to the reason for narrowing, or otherwise not reasonably describable at the time. Another would make the prosecution record’s demonstrated purpose the principal boundary without a categorical presumption over all intervening scope. A complete-bar rule would favor maximal administrative certainty but change the narrower existing simulated treatment. None of these paths establishes that SMC’s device actually infringes.

**Uncertainty:** The amendment-specific explanation and the availability of each accused substitute may require further findings. A later technological substitution cannot be labelled foreseeable or tangential from its name alone.

* **Question Granted:** The supplied question governs the simulation. The actual Third Circuit judgment is the judgment under review; no historical Supreme Court grant is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The District of New Jersey enforced the nationwide Prudential settlement by restricting the Lowes’ use of evidence in their Florida litigation. The Third Circuit affirmed. The Lowes seek reversal of the restriction insofar as it prevents proof concerning policies excluded from the settlement.
* **Relief Requested:** The Lowes seek relief from the federal restriction; Prudential seeks affirmance of the settlement-enforcement order.
* **Material Record:** The nationwide settlement addressed allegedly deceptive insurance sales practices, including churning and representations about premium obligations. They pursued Florida claims involving excluded policies and sought evidence of a broader pattern, including transactions involving released policies. * **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Judgment protection and retained claims

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Kokkonen distinguishes jurisdiction to enforce an incorporated or retained settlement from an ordinary independent contract dispute. Matsushita recognizes that a valid representative release can reach claims sharing the settled factual predicate, while preserving constitutional limits on binding representation and the position of valid opt-outs. Baker v. General Motors distinguishes recognition of a judgment from control of another forum’s evidence and proceedings.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the binding judgment, parties and claims actually resolved, the source of enforcement authority, and the particular act enjoined. Notice or failure to opt out is not unlimited individual consent, and recognition of a release is not necessarily a transfer of evidentiary control over distinct claims.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Anti-Injunction Act permits a federal injunction against state proceedings only within its specified exceptions, including protection or effectuation of federal judgments. Rule 23 requires valid representation and a legally sufficient settlement procedure. The settlement’s operative release and exclusions define what the Lowes surrendered.
* **Material Other Authority:** Amchem and Ortiz reject the assumption that settlement benefits cure defects in representative structure. * **Existing Rule’s Reach:** A federal court may enforce a properly retained settlement and protect claims actually disposed of when an applicable statutory exception is satisfied. Released claims do not become live merely because a party changes the requested remedy.
* **Open Point:** Whether protecting the released claims permits a categorical prohibition on evidence used solely to prove liability on policies the settlement expressly left outside the release.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Third Circuit accepted that the settlement’s finality required protection against indirect litigation of released transactions. It treated the proposed use of those transactions as inconsistent with the release and affirmed the federal restriction, while recognizing the difficulty that policy-specific exclusions could create for litigants.

**The Lowes’ position.** A claim and the evidence used to prove it are different. The settlement preserved litigation about specified policies; a categorical evidence bar can make that preservation ineffective. They ask the Court to confine judgment-protection injunctions to claims and issues actually resolved, leaving relevance and permissible use in the retained case to the state tribunal.

**Prudential’s position.** The release protects against litigation based on the settled sales practices, not merely a second damages line for each released policy. Discovery into those matters and their presentation as a fraudulent course of conduct can recreate the settled controversy and undermine the settlement’s value. Prudential requests enforcement of the release according to its breadth, including measures against indirect circumvention.

**Limits.** The Lowes may not use the excluded-policy suit to recover for released policies or obtain a contradictory adjudication of a settled claim. Prudential must identify the judgment being protected; a general desire to avoid damaging evidence does not itself establish that the proposed state adjudication relitigates it.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would preserve the evidence restriction and require the Lowes to litigate retained policies within that limit. Reversal could dissolve the categorical restriction while preserving an injunction against actual prosecution of released claims. Remand may be appropriate to separate proposed proof of retained-policy liability from disguised demands for compensation on released transactions.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow judgment-protection rule would allow injunctions against a released claim or adjudication inconsistent with an issue conclusively resolved, while rejecting a blanket prohibition merely because evidence concerns a released transaction. A broader release-enforcement rule could sustain a specifically supported restriction where the retained suit necessarily seeks to relitigate an adjudicated matter. Neither route requires a general rule that settlement releases never affect evidence, or that all prior-transaction evidence is admissible. The injunction must fit both the settlement and the statutory exception.

**Uncertainty:** Exact proffered evidence, the state claims’ elements, and the release’s language control application. The existing record does not warrant a general finding that the Lowes’ entire state action is a sham or that every reference to released policies is indispensable.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A rule permitting adjudication can rest on the Commission’s exercise of federal regulatory authority over maritime operations, even when a private party initiates the proceeding and requests reparations. It need not decide immunity in every agency or a later independent judicial collection action. A rule extending immunity could focus on compulsory adjudication of private monetary demands but would leave valid federal abrogation and United States enforcement available. Neither route turns commercial participation into consent.

**Uncertainty:** The record establishes state-arm status but no merits liability or final remedy. Statutory applicability to particular alleged conduct remains distinct from the constitutional objection to the tribunal.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The courts used reasonable hours multiplied by a reasonable rate, with the agreement given a secondary role. That method aimed to produce administrable awards without allowing accumulated benefits to yield compensation disproportionate to the legal work.

**Claimants and counsel.** The statute regulates an established contingent-fee relationship and caps the amount; it does not substitute a government-designed hourly contract. The risk of no recovery and delayed payment must remain meaningful. They request presumptive respect for the agreed percentage, followed by review for unreasonable results.

**Commissioner’s supported concern.** Judicial review must protect benefits and prevent windfalls. A contract signed before a disabled claimant knows the size of the eventual award cannot conclusively establish reasonableness. An agreement-centered approach therefore needs practical checks for poor performance, delay, and compensation disproportionate to the representation, even if an exclusively hourly calculation is rejected.

**Limits.** A fee below twenty-five percent is not automatically reasonable. Hours remain informative, but the mere fact that a successful contingent case yields a high effective hourly rate does not establish an abusive fee. The statutory ceiling and the separate EAJA refund rule must both be enforced.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would retain the existing lodestar-based awards. Reversal would require new reasonableness determinations beginning with the agreements, not automatic payment of twenty-five percent. The district courts could reduce a fee for deficient representation, counsel-caused delay increasing accrued benefits, or a disproportionate windfall demonstrated by the case’s circumstances.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A contract-centered rule would treat a lawful contingent agreement as the initial measure, subject to a statutory ceiling and independent review, with hours and rates used as a cross-check. A lodestar rule would give priority to hourly valuation and account for contingency only through permitted adjustments. The text supports judicial supervision on either account; the dispute concerns whether that supervision replaces or evaluates the parties’ bargain. Neither path resolves administrative fees or guarantees an award on a future unsuccessful claim.

**Uncertainty:** Separate calculations are required for each representation. The record does not authorize a single revised dollar amount or a finding of delay or poor performance for counsel who have not been shown to have caused it.
