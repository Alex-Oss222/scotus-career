## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Eighth Circuit distinguished a serious distribution offense from the vastly larger drug quantity in Harmelin and distinguished genuine parole eligibility from possible clemency. **State’s position.** Drug dealing is a serious public wrong even in small units; legislatures need not calibrate punishment in direct proportion to weight. The State requests a highly deferential threshold that leaves this sentence intact. It also argues that Teague’s restriction on new law requires federal acceptance of reasonable state interpretations of federal law even in this pre-AEDPA proceeding. That review-standard argument should not be recast as a separately specified new-rule defense that the published record does not describe.

**Henderson’s position.** Permanent imprisonment for this first conviction is qualitatively different from a severe finite sentence. The State cannot justify it by attributing all harms of the drug trade to this offender. His rule permits exceptional relief without judicially fixing an ordinary sentencing schedule.

**Limits and alternatives.** The challenge does not deny the State power to imprison drug sellers. A sentence-specific constitutional conclusion need not invalidate the underlying penalty range for all defendants or reopen the conviction.
