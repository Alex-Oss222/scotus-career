## 9. United States v. Knights — December 10, 2001

**Reach and proposed disposition:** Reach the Fourth Amendment question; reverse the Ninth Circuit's purpose-based suppression judgment and remand. Eight Associates support this disposition and the bounded condition-plus-suspicion rule. No final conviction or blanket admissibility ruling follows.

**Entering law:** Ornelas supplies independent review of ultimate reasonable suspicion with ordinary protection of historical findings. Whren's unanimous rule rejects an additional investigatory-motive barrier when the actual intrusion is independently justified; it does not create home-search authority. Griffin recognizes the lawful supervisory relationship and reduced privacy without eliminating constitutional protection. Edmond and Ferguson preserve purpose inquiry for their asserted suspicionless or special-needs justifications; Ferguson expressly distinguishes ordinary patients from lawful probation supervision. Scott's Souter opinion requires exclusion in its defined revocation setting after an unconstitutional search; search validity was assumed there and lawful conditions were preserved.

**Proposition available for full joinder:** A residence search supported by individualized reasonable suspicion of criminal activity is reasonable in this case when the probationer is subject to the known express condition permitting searches by probation and law-enforcement officers. The condition reduces the legitimate expectation of privacy, while individualized suspicion and the actual supervisory status supply a substantial justification; the detective's ordinary investigative purpose does not independently invalidate this combination. The decision rests on constitutional reasonableness, not a finding of voluntary abandonment of every Fourth Amendment right.

**Two-path comparison:** The best argument for the purpose limitation is that Griffin's special-needs exception cannot authorize a general criminal investigation and residential privacy ordinarily demands a warrant and probable cause. The answer is limited: Griffin did not make its particular justification exclusive; this actual probation condition and particularized suspicion can be assessed under reasonableness without applying a suspicionless program exception. Conversely, the best objection to the government's rule is that a written condition could become a general investigative license. The adopted combination and the preserved requirement of lawful scope prevent that result; writing a condition alone cannot validate every search. No result concerning a search without individualized suspicion is necessary.

## 11. Schrader v. Blackwell — December 10, 2001

**Reach:** All eight would reach the limited ballot-cue issue. The continuing party plaintiff, periodic recognition requirement, prior loss and later reacquisition of status, and election-length litigation establish a recurring dispute capable of evading full review. Recognition for a cycle and completion of the 1998 election do not secure permanent recognition or end the challenged rule. No new candidacy or election outcome is assumed. The completed election will not be reopened. The preserved issue is the requested Libertarian cue for the independently qualified nominee; the earlier filing-deadline objection is not part of review.

**Source clarification:** The full lower opinion, 241 F.3d 783, confirms actual Libertarian convention nomination, petitions identifying that nomination, sufficient district signatures and party participation as plaintiff. These facts answer the packet's concern that affiliation must not be inferred from unilateral self-labeling. The district court's broader declaration and the Sixth Circuit's reversal identify the judgment under review. The current Court remains responsible for applying the simulated Timmons rule rather than treating historical descriptions in the lower opinion as law.

**Entering law:** Burdick requires weighing the actual burden against precise interests and fit, not automatic strict scrutiny or automatic deference. Timmons's Thomas merits opinion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Breyer, permits the particular nonsevere restriction within a complete nomination system. Its holding recognizes association and allows preventive administration without proof of a past disruption, but supplies no categorical approval of every label denial. Stevens's dissent, joined by Ginsburg and in relevant parts Souter, gives independent weight to the actual party choice; Souter's separate position preserves a demonstrated stability rationale while requiring fit. Anderson, Storer, Jenness, Tashjian, Eu and Norman retain their stated scopes.

**Competing paths:** The recognition-linked path treats the official party designation as part of a coherent nomination system. Candidate access and campaigning remain; statewide party qualification conveys a different status from district nomination signatures. Its strongest objection is that this candidate's actual affiliation is verified and a truthful cue need not claim recognized-party status. The affiliation-distinction path finds the categorical rule insufficiently fitted to that narrower information interest. Its strongest objection is that permitting a new designation can weaken the state's defined nomination routes and require courts to redesign ballots. The disagreement is over the fit of this restriction and the institutional significance of the requested cue, not whether association exists or whether every ballot word is protected expression.

## 13. Toyota Motor Manufacturing, Kentucky, Inc. v. Williams — January 8, 2002

**Proposed disposition:** All eight vacate the appellate partial summary judgment declaring actual disability and remand for individualized assessment of the claimed major life activity of performing manual tasks. None would decide on this record that Williams necessarily lacks a disability or is necessarily entitled to employment relief.

**Entering law:** The Standards and Tests rule titled Mitigating measures in actual ADA disability operates from Sutton and Murphy, June 22, 1999. Beneficial mitigation does not eliminate the underlying impairment; individual substantial limitation remains required. Their regarded-as-working holdings address occupational breadth and do not turn a class-of-jobs formula into the definition of every major life activity. Bragdon rejects diagnosis-only coverage, demands an individual application and does not restrict major activities to economic or daily activities. Kirkingburg keeps actual safety qualifications distinct. The Kennedy Sutton and O'Connor Murphy separate opinions, joined by the other three actual-mitigation dissenters, distinguish impairment from individual limitation but do not establish a permanence requirement or a near-total-incapacity threshold.

**Common operative rule:** Substantial limitation of manual tasks must be assessed by the individual's limitation of important manual functions, considering their practical breadth, severity and duration in the person's life. Evidence from work and outside work is relevant; inability to perform the repetitive or specialized tasks of a particular production assignment does not establish the statutory major-life-activity limitation merely by grouping those tasks as a class of jobs. Conversely, performance of some ordinary household tasks does not conclusively defeat a substantial limitation of other important manual functions. The underlying impairment is assessed under Sutton and Murphy; coverage, qualification, accommodation and safety remain distinct.

The reference to practical breadth, severity and duration describes application of substantial limitation; it creates no mandatory checklist, numerical threshold or fixed duration minimum. The Court need not announce that nearly all manual functions must be impossible, that every qualifying impairment must be permanent, or that work evidence is excluded from the activity inquiry. No later amendment supplies the rule.

**Alternative-path comparison:** Toyota's strongest point is that the appellate shortcut confuses inability to perform a category of occupational tasks with substantial limitation of manual activity. Williams's strongest answer is that sustained gripping and repetitive or overhead movement can be important manual capacities even when she can do some basic self-care. Both points survive. Automatic coverage from the job-class finding fails the individualized inquiry; a new outside-work-only or nearly-total-loss rule would add a restriction not established by the statutory text and entering holdings. A remand under the ordinary activity-specific inquiry resolves the legal error without selecting disputed functional facts.

## 14. Great-West Life & Annuity Insurance Co. v. Knudson — January 8, 2002

**Reach:** All eight reach §502(a)(3)'s remedial classification. Four affirm this judgment on the particular demand; four would vacate and remand for recognition of the asserted equitable restitution/trust-beneficiary route. The latter position does not award reimbursement or resolve the alternative plan-construction ground.

**Entering law:** Mertens's Scalia opinion, joined by Kennedy, Souter and Thomas among the current Associates, limits appropriate equitable relief to traditionally equitable categories; equity's former power to award compensation is insufficient. The White dissent joined by Stevens and O'Connor favors traditional trust restoration but does not control. Varity's Souter opinion, with Stevens, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer on the remedial portion, preserves appropriate individual equity within the statutory limitation and its normal adequate-remedy constraint. Harris Trust's Thomas opinion, unanimously joined, permits traditional equitable redress against a knowing nonfiduciary recipient, while retaining property, transaction, notice and purchaser-defense predicates. It supplies no universal tracing rule or ordinary compensation action.

**Competing paths and strongest objections:** The personal-debt path asks what relief these plaintiffs seek from these defendants: repayment of a plan obligation without the requisite identified settlement property in the defendants' possession/control. A prayer for an injunction or restitution cannot change an ordinary obligation to pay into a traditionally equitable remedy. Its strongest counterargument is that restitution measured by the beneficiary's gain and enforcement of an agreement to restore an advance to a trust were themselves familiar equity, not compensation for the creditor's loss. The equitable-restitution path accepts that category argument and rejects a categorical refusal to consider it merely because the initial benefits were lawfully paid. Its strongest counterargument is Mertens: a broad trust relationship cannot erase the statutory category limitation, and relief cannot be imposed against nonparty property. Neither side may equate all monetary relief with damages or all gain-based repayment with equity.

## 15. Searles v. Van Bebber — January 8, 2002

**Reach and disposition:** All eight affirm the Tenth Circuit's remedial disposition at the reviewed scope: compensatory award vacated; one dollar nominal relief required; punitive amount returned for redetermination while established constitutional liability, no-physical-injury and punitive-culpability findings remain intact. The prior judgments clearing the other officials remain undisturbed. The fee consequence follows the revised damages judgment; no independent fee doctrine is decided.

**Entering law:** Prisoner mental-injury suits and litigation procedure operates from the original PLRA and Harris v. Garner, May 29, 2001. Confinement when the action is brought fixes §1997e(e)'s application, and later release does not cure that action. Harris expressly left nominal and punitive relief open; it creates no all-remedies bar. Carey distinguishes injury compensation from nominal recognition; Stachura rejects damages based merely on a right's abstract value. Smith v. Wade permits punitive damages for evil motive or intent, or reckless or callous indifference to federal rights; it does not require punitive fault always to exceed the substantive liability standard. Kolstad preserves the distinct statutory setting and culpability of punitive relief rather than making compensation a universal prerequisite.

**Operative rules:** Section 1997e(e) bars compensation for mental or emotional injury suffered in custody without the required prior physical-injury showing, including such injury arising from a religious-rights violation. Renaming distress as the value of the constitutional right does not preserve this compensatory award. Nominal damages recognizing an established violation and punitive damages punishing sufficiently culpable conduct do not compensate that injury category and are not categorically barred by this provision. Punitive damages retain Smith's distinct culpability requirement and cannot reproduce barred compensation under a different name.

**Alternative paths:** Searles's strongest compensation argument treats denial of religious exercise as an independent constitutional injury. That establishes a real violation, but not entitlement to the specifically emotional-injury compensation the jury awarded; nominal recognition and lawful punishment preserve the distinction. The broad all-damages reading relies on the statute's reference to an action rather than one remedy, but overlooks what the action is for: an established constitutional violation can support relief that does not compensate mental or emotional injury. The harmless-error alternative would preserve a demonstrably independent punitive amount; the actual instruction tied that amount to the invalid compensation, so independence cannot be assumed. The culpability finding is separately supported and does not share the amount's defect.

Any preserved constitutional challenge to barring this compensation is rejected at this application because the right remains judicially enforceable through the remedies distinguished here; no categorical declaration about every possible PLRA application is adopted.
