## 2. TRW — statute and remaining accrual question

The original neutral facts and posture are sufficient for the granted legal question. The authenticated 2000 Code text of §1681p reproduces the operative provision without a post-1970 amendment: two years from when liability arises; a separate two-year period after discovery of a material and willful misrepresentation of information required to be disclosed to an individual, where that information is material to establishing the defendant's liability to that individual. This is not a general knowledge-of-violation extension. No later amendment governs these disclosures.

Rotella, February 23, 2000, is unanimous but expressly leaves the general injury-occurrence/injury-discovery choice open. Klehr, June 19, 1997, preserves genuinely new injuries, previously unprovable damages and diligent concealment under their distinct predicates. Neither supplies a universal discovery rule. Rejection of general discovery therefore does not answer when a complete claim with legally required injury arose. The neutral record supplies no factfinding sufficient to adjudicate every injury date or to award an exception.

## 4. Brown — preserved component and exact lower-court limits

Read the relevant amended appellate opinion, 221 F.3d 329. The victim supplied a Black male, youth and hand-injury description, and a dog trail pointed toward the campus before ending. Police obtained a list of Black male students, then questioned more than two hundred people during the wider sweep. One woman's alleged encounter bears on departure from the other descriptive traits. These are reported litigation facts, not a fresh finding that every encounter was coercive or every defendant participated.

The lower court treated description-based selection as facially neutral unless additional discriminatory animus was shown. Its express-classification rule elsewhere did not require a comparator group; its dismissal here rested on exclusion of the investigation from that category. The limited granted question challenges that exclusion. The independent Fourth Amendment dispositions, discontinued claims, unchallenged nonparticipation affidavits and other grounds retain their actual procedural force. A classification remand does not revive everything in the original complaint.

Peña's common substantial-justification standard is the operative law for competitive racial classifications; extending its justification principle to these investigative burdens is a new contextual application. Its important lawful objective, evidence-supported substantial relationship, coverage, actual burdens and concrete practicable alternatives must remain intact. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas did not join that standard in Peña; their strict-scrutiny positions remain separate published positions. The question's historical strict-scrutiny wording cannot silently replace controlling law.

Lesage, November 29, 1999, separates unequal-process injury from proved consequential loss, appropriate defendants, immunity and prospective exposure. It does not itself establish this police classification or a damages entitlement. Conduct-date immunity is assessed as of September 1992, not by treating later simulated decisions as contemporary notice.

It expressly preserves existing individual actions and does not presume an adequate substitute remedy. ## 8. Behar — statement-specific source correction and surviving issues

Read the full lower appellate judicial text, 238 F.3d 168, including its notes. Its subsidiary-meaning ground covers all three Vancouver statements, numbered 5, 6 and 7 in the opinion, not merely statement 5. Statement 5 identifies the exchange as a source of church funds; 6 and 7 concern individual takeover/stock/property accusations. The court expressly did not resolve the of-and-concerning question for these statements; Chief Judge Walker's footnote view is not the panel's alternative holding. Discovery had concentrated on actual malice and deferred truth/falsity. The panel separately sustained actual-malice dismissals for other groups of statements. Statements 11 and 12 were not appealed.
