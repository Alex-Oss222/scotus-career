## 23. National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co.

### Components and sufficient grounds

G1: Reverse the exclusion of qualifying cable-system attachments merely because they carry Internet service together with cable television. Sections 224(a)(4) and (b)(1) supply coverage; the category-specific formulas regulate the subsets they describe without silently deleting other covered attachments. Where a formula's terms apply it remains mandatory. The question does not authorize a rate award or classification of Internet service.

G2: Reverse the categorical exclusion of wireless telecommunications attachments. Telecommunications service is defined without a facilities limitation. The wire-use language identifies a qualifying utility and facility rather than requiring every attachment to transmit only by wire. Coverage remains conditional on an actual attachment by the defined provider to a qualifying utility structure; the statutory exclusions and state-regulation allocation remain intact.

G3: The remedy is reversal of the challenged jurisdictional portions and remand for proceedings consistent with those coverage holdings. The rate calculation noted separately in the government's brief is not an additional question decided. No automatic rate refund, definitive service classification, access order for an identified pole or Takings Clause judgment follows. The statutory text supplies a common sufficient ground without requiring all Justices to share a fallback deference theory.

The principal competing path reads subsections (d) and (e) as exhaustive regulatory categories and requires classification before coverage. It protects the precise formula limits, but does so by giving them coverage language they do not contain. The coverage path's principal objection—that general subsection (b) authority could evade the formulas—is answered only by retaining those formulas whenever triggered. The wireless exclusion has a separate weakness: it transfers the wire-use condition from the utility definition to a service definition deliberately written without that restriction. Neither response converts agency expertise into permission to disregard enacted limits.

## 24. Lee v. Kemna

### Components and adversarial paths

L1 is the federal adequacy of the state continuance default as applied. L2 is the consequential disposition of the federal judgment. L3 is preservation of the distinct due-process merits and remaining habeas questions; no Justice commits to deciding them on this review.

Path A: The combined use of Rules 24.09 and 24.10 is inadequate in these exceptional circumstances. The sudden disappearance of subpoenaed witnesses after actual attendance, substantial presentation of the grounds and expected alibi, short requested postponement and trial judge's independent scheduling explanation make the later insistence on additional formalities an insufficient ground to exclude the federal claim. This is an as-applied conclusion, not an exemption for every emergency or a rule that a judge must remind counsel of known procedural requirements. Vacate the federal default judgment and remand for the due-process claim to be considered subject to other applicable rules. No cause or innocence gateway is needed to pass a ground found inadequate.

Path B: The state ground is adequate. The rules existed, governed these requests and served independent purposes; an oral assertion that witnesses may still be in town does not necessarily supply the sworn, specific showing of probable timely attendance, diligence and non-procurement. A state appellate court may apply those rules even if the trial judge gave another reason. Lack of a reported identical midtrial episode does not establish an unforeseeable rule. Affirm the default judgment; the rejected cause and innocence theories do not produce relief on the presented record.

The strongest challenge to A is that federal adequacy review becomes individualized assessment of whether obedience would have seemed useful, replacing Coleman's limited gateways. The strongest challenge to B is that it elevates a formal deficiency into a federal bar even when the court had substantially the pertinent facts and its ruling would not have changed upon receiving paper. Osborne and Ford require that objection to be addressed, rather than treating every state-law violation as adequate. Neither path assumes that the witnesses were truthful, that a security officer directed them away, or that the continuance itself was constitutionally required.

## 25. Kansas v. Crane

### Components and adversarial paths

C1: Due process does not demand complete inability to control conduct. Neither Foucha nor the controlling Hendricks/Seling holdings impose that categorical condition. Rejecting total incapacity does not make dangerousness or a diagnostic label alone sufficient.

C2-A: A genuinely civil commitment of this kind requires proof of serious difficulty controlling dangerous conduct caused by the qualifying condition. The condition's nature and severity and the functional impairment must sufficiently distinguish the person from an ordinary dangerous recidivist. No numerical measurement, fixed medical vocabulary, total loss of choice or predetermined instruction wording is prescribed. Kansas's reasonable-doubt statutory burden remains operative.

C2-B: Reject a newly prescribed independent constitutional control element. A present qualifying abnormality or disorder causally linked to the dangerous conduct, established under the Act's substantive requirements and safeguards, supplies the relevant civil distinction; the Constitution does not require an additional autonomous finding of serious volitional impairment. The State still cannot commit on past wrongdoing plus dangerousness alone, and the statute must genuinely be satisfied.

C3-A: Vacate and remand to apply the serious-impairment rule under the State's proof burden; no automatic restoration of the initial commitment. C3-B: Reverse the complete-incapacity construction and return under the existing statutory predicates without directing a new independent control finding. Neither remedy decides a separately preserved punitive-operation objection or imports Hendricks's treatment record. All eight accept Hendricks and Seling as governing law at their actual scope.

The strongest complete-incapacity case is that only total involuntariness separates treatment from punishment for propensity; it fails because Foucha demands lawful substantive limits but does not impose that absolute, while Addington recognizes practical psychiatric uncertainty. The serious-impairment rule's strongest objection is indeterminacy and duplication of the statutory condition; its response is a functional constitutional boundary without a demand for mathematical precision or special terminology. The no-new-element path's strongest objection is that causally linked diagnosis and predicted crime can still sweep in ordinary recidivists. Its supporters require a real current disorder and genuine statutory causal showing, but do not adopt C2-A as a separate constitutional element. These distinct grounds cannot be combined by mere counting.
