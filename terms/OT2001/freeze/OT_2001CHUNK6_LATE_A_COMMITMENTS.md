## 1. Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Echazabal

**Provisional disposition:** All eight Associates would reverse the Ninth Circuit's categorical rejection of the own-health qualification defense and remand for application of the valid regulation to the unresolved medical-evidence question. They do not direct automatic reinstatement of Chevron's summary judgment.

### Governing propositions and live alternatives

**E1 — Statutory authority.** Original ADA section 12113(a) permits a properly proved, job-related qualification standard consistent with business necessity, with its accommodation condition. Subsection (b)'s statement that qualification standards “may include” the absence of a threat to others does not make that example exhaustive. Section 12111(3) continues to define the statutory term directed to others; the EEOC's own-health rule is sustainable as an implementation of subsection (a)'s broader defense, not as a revision of that definition. Section 12116 supplies actual Title I rulemaking authority, exercised in the formal implementing regulation. Under Haggar, Mead and Walton, the bounded rule is a permissible exercise of that authority, rather than the sole implementation Congress compels.

**E2 — Actual limits of the permitted defense.** The rule permits exclusion for a significant risk of substantial harm to the individual's own health arising from performance of the job when the risk cannot be eliminated or reduced by reasonable accommodation. It requires an individual assessment of present ability to perform essential functions safely, founded on reasonable medical judgment using current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence. Duration, nature and severity, likelihood, and imminence must receive their prescribed consideration. Diagnosis, generalized assumptions about a disability, the employee's willingness to assume risk, or a physician's sincere belief alone does not decide the defense. The employer must establish the defense; describing safety as an essential function cannot circumvent its substantive and evidentiary conditions.

Bragdon's unanimous objective-assessment holding supplies the pertinent method, although its Title III threat-to-others rule did not decide Title I authorization. Its distinct founded weight for public-health judgments does not transform Chevron's private physicians into public-health authorities. Sutton, Murphy and Toyota govern disability coverage separately. The motion assumed disability; that assumption is not a final coverage adjudication. Barnett's distinction between reasonable accommodation and undue hardship is retained without importing a seniority presumption into medical risk.

**E3 — Application and remedy.** The Ninth Circuit must consider the unresolved challenge to the foundation and treatment of the medical evidence under ordinary summary-judgment principles and the regulation's objective rule. The district court acknowledged a factual dispute and discounted the opposing declarations because they were prepared later. The proper inquiry distinguishes later medical discoveries from a later declaration addressing knowledge and evidence available when the decision was made. The Supreme Court need not itself settle that disputed evidentiary question to decide the regulation's authorization.

The defense retains its reasonable-accommodation condition. Echazabal did not argue accommodation in opposition to Chevron's motion, although he did against Irwin. This remand creates no preserved Chevron accommodation claim, commands no new accommodation investigation, and does not reopen Irwin's distinct judgment. The lower court must respect the actual motion assumptions, preservation and scope of review.

**Alternative A — Exclude own-health risks.** The strongest argument is Congress's repeated choice of danger to “others,” reinforced by the statute's rejection of exclusion based on paternalism. An agency cannot convert a deliberately limited defense into an unrestricted health veto. The adopted answer is that subsection (a) independently supplies a general defense while subsection (b) supplies a permissive example. The narrow objective regulation, with its significant-risk and accommodation conditions, addresses supported occupational danger rather than stereotypes. Reading every reference to others as an exclusive negative command would suppress subsection (a)'s independent operation.

**Alternative B — Resolve the case solely through essential-function qualification.** Section 12111(8) concerns ability to perform the job's essential functions with or without accommodation. It cannot by itself dispose of a contested medical safety defense without the objective showing and preservation inquiry. No established inability or undisputed significant risk supports that alternative on this record. It is not an independent ground for reinstating Chevron's judgment.

## 2. Devlin v. Scardelletti

**Provisional disposition:** Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the appellate-status barrier and remand for consideration of preserved settlement objections. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would affirm the status dismissal because intervention remains necessary. All distinguish that issue from the already affirmed denial of intervention and from the settlement's merits.

### Governing choices and remedial scope

**D1 — Limited party status in this mandatory own-right class.** A member of a mandatory Rule 23 class whose own pension rights are disposed of by a binding settlement, and who timely presents objections to approval, may appeal the approval order without first obtaining formal intervention. His objection supplies a concrete adversarial role respecting the very disposition binding him. This is party status for that appeal, not authority to assume control of the class or appeal every management order. Membership, financial interest and binding effect remain distinct from Article III injury and from the procedural step of objecting.

Felzen remains controlling for an otherwise nonparty shareholder seeking to continue litigation of the corporation's derivative claim. Its express limits leave the present own-right mandatory-class question open. Marino's objectors were outside the class; its ordinary intervention rule does not decide whether an actual class member is already a party for the limited approval appeal. Richards and Matsushita identify valid representative binding and representation limits; they do not independently confer appeal rights or prove deficient representation. Amchem and Ortiz preserve certification requirements without supplying a new certification issue here.

**D2 — Formal intervention remains necessary.** The contrary ground treats represented membership and the right to object as insufficient for independent appellate party status. The member's representative litigates and may appeal; Rule 24 establishes when a member gains his own litigating role. The direct interest and absence of opt-out do not themselves eliminate that procedural prerequisite. Felzen's distinction between assisting settlement supervision and continuing litigation, reinforced by Marino and the retained route to challenge a real intervention denial, supports this position. Section 1291's final-decision requirement does not expressly eliminate the party rule, and Rule 23's binding operation does not expressly grant every member appellate capacity.

**Strongest objection to D1:** The proposed contextual status may bypass a properly denied intervention motion and burden collective resolution with numerous independent appeals. D1 answers that review is confined to a member's timely objection to the approval disposing of his own non-excludable claim, not a reopening of intervention or permission to represent the class. Ordinary preservation, appeal and appellate-management rules remain.

**Strongest objection to D2:** The court conclusively determines the objector's own rights, leaves no opt-out, and has already heard his objection; a separate entry motion adds formality while withholding meaningful direct review. D2 answers that representative adjudication necessarily distinguishes membership from personal control, and intervention supplies the established route to challenge representation and acquire appellate status. The fact of binding effect cannot alone decide the status question. Neither side claims that adequacy of representation is proved simply by the class label.

**Remedy:** D1 reverses only the status barrier and returns the attempted approval appeal for ordinary consideration of preserved objections. D2 leaves the dismissal in effect. Both leave the affirmed intervention ruling in its existing posture and adjudicate no pension or ERISA merits. United Airlines v. McDonald permits timely postjudgment intervention in its setting but does not make this motion timely; the suggested limited-intervention solution cannot be adopted by inventing an intervention entitlement. Prudential's May 28 release-specific rule supplies no appellate status and no finding that Devlin's rights were excluded from this settlement.

## 3. Franconia Associates v. United States, including the Grass Valley petitioners

**Provisional disposition:** All eight Associates would reverse the contract dismissals insofar as they rest on automatic accrual at ELIHPA's enactment or implementation, vacate the takings limitations disposition insofar as it rests on the same undifferentiated early date, and remand both components under their respective rules. The judgment is a limitations disposition on assumed undertakings, not a finding that every petitioner's suit is timely or that the Government owes compensation.

### Posture and preserved statutory conditions

The petitioners here entered their loans before December 21, 1979. Their cases were filed May 30, 1997 and September 16, 1998. The Court of Federal Claims used May 23, 1988, the regulations' effective date, as the latest accrual point; the Federal Circuit used February 5, 1988 enactment. The two rationales are distinct even though both would make these filings late. The United States assumes, for this accrual question, the alleged unrestricted prepayment promise. Actual authorization, contract scope, ultimate breach and damages remain governed by Hercules and Winstar on their own predicates. That assumption must neither become ultimate liability nor be withdrawn to avoid deciding the presented legal issue.

Section 2501 bars a claim filed more than six years after first accrual. The claim-specific inquiry does not create a discovery extension or suspend a period that has actually begun. Its river-and-harbor rule, legal-disability/beyond-the-seas provision and officer-fee-account provision do not supply an established exception for these borrowers. TRW and Young preserve the distinction between identifying accrual, adding an unprovided discovery rule and excluding a period during which another statute legally disables enforcement; no such disabling stay is asserted here.

ELIHPA does not absolutely forbid every prepayment. Before acceptance, paragraph (4) requires reasonable efforts to negotiate at least twenty years of continued low-income use from the agreement's execution. Assistance depends on appropriations and determinations of necessity, fair return and the least costly consistent alternative. The borrower need not accept incentives. If agreement will not be reached after a reasonable period, paragraph (5) generally requires a fair-market offer to a qualified nonprofit or public purchaser; the two appraisers and, if needed, binding third appraisal, purchaser capability, and useful-life affordability obligations retain their statutory work. A qualified national or regional organization is considered if no local purchaser qualifies. After 180 days without a bona fide purchase offer, acceptance of prepayment or a refinancing request is permissive, not mandatory, and this clause applies only when transfer funds are available.

Paragraph (5)(G)(i) concerns an undertaking to continue the designated use for the Secretary-determined period, no shorter than the referenced twenty-year period measured from the loan, followed by the prescribed offer to a qualified purchaser. Paragraph (5)(G)(ii) separately requires no material effect on minority housing opportunities together with either protection against displacement from changed use or increased charges, or adequate safe, decent, affordable rental supply and sufficient action to make it available to every displaced tenant. Supply alone is insufficient. Neither route establishes any petitioner's eligibility. Paragraph (5)(F)'s fiscal-year unit/budget limit, subject to (G), has its own exception after fifteen months without available budget authority following the request or enactment, whichever is later, and its request-order allocation rule. Relief from paragraph (5) does not by its terms eliminate paragraph (4). These conditions matter to actual government performance and a final regulatory burden; they cannot be compressed into an absolute ban or presumed successful relief.

### Contract accrual: rule and alternatives

**F-C1 — Performance when the option is exercised.** On the undertaking assumed for these dismissals, the promised performance is acceptance of permitted prepayment when the borrower chooses to exercise the option. A legislative announcement that the Government will not honor that future performance constitutes repudiation; it does not by itself make the promised future performance presently overdue. The injured party may elect to treat a legally sufficient repudiation as breach and sue, but need not exercise that election merely because the legislation is known. Absent such election, accrual awaits the time performance is due and is refused or otherwise breached. A valid offer of prepayment and refusal can supply that event; any legally sufficient excuse for tender, earlier effective election or different actually pleaded obligation must be assessed on its own supported facts.

Roehm supplies the ordinary election principle. Mobil's fifth-lease holding establishes that legislation may anticipatorily repudiate a future federal contractual process even before its application trigger occurs. Its actual submitted-plan breaches and restitution election are distinct. Winstar preserves monetary contractual responsibility without preventing Congress from legislating; an announcement changing legal permission does not thereby extinguish the assumed contractual undertaking. Bay Area Laundry reinforces the distinction between an existing obligation and a completed enforcement claim, although its ERISA demand timetable and period do not govern these contracts.

**Contrary present-option path:** The bargain can be characterized as continuously available freedom to escape restrictions; legislation withdrawing that option then inflicts a complete present breach. Section 2501's first-accrual wording and sovereign fiscal repose oppose allowing a claimant to delay indefinitely after an actionable injury. The answer here is that the assumed promise concerns performance when the borrower exercises a reserved choice, not a separate warranty that Congress will enact no inconsistent law. Ordinary repudiation makes an early suit available by election without making that election compulsory. The repose period still runs once this claimant's cause is complete; it does not run merely from notice that future performance is threatened. An actually different present-performance promise could have a different accrual time, but cannot be invented to sustain these dismissals.

**Narrower construction-first path:** A remand solely to decide whether any promise existed would avoid the legal accrual question that the Government accepted on the allegations. That is not the selected path. The Court answers the assumed-promise question and then returns genuinely remaining contract and timing matters. The Government may contest ultimate formation, authority and scope in their proper posture; neither the assumption nor reversal settles those issues.

**Contract remedy:** Reject automatic 1988 accrual and remand for each claim's actual performance obligation, effective election or refusal, filing interval, and genuinely remaining defenses. The November 1993/March 1994 Perri account remains an attributed affidavit, with its reconsideration-stage admissibility and consequences unresolved. No universal request, tender, refusal, futility or election date is supplied. The 1992 extension to later loans creates no new accrual event for these already covered pre-1979 borrowers merely by its existence.

### Taking accrual: independent rule and alternatives

**F-T1 — A completed constitutional claim requires its own property and burden inquiry.** The as-applied taking theory is not automatically complete when ELIHPA is enacted merely because the enactment repudiates an assumed contract. A claim concerning imposed use restrictions must identify the protected interest, the governmental act fixing the challenged burden and the actual injury, including a sufficiently definitive agency determination where meaningful application choices remain. Available statutory release conditions and genuine agency choice matter; a process incapable of changing the challenged restriction is not compulsory exhaustion. If a pleaded theory instead asserts an already complete appropriation or destruction of a particular contract right, its actual completeness and timing require their own analysis, rather than automatic importation of contract-performance accrual.

Suitum requires a final use determination without demanding acts that cannot change it. Palazzolo permits omission of materially equivalent applications after a definitive restriction while preserving genuinely different designs or available exceptions. Neither establishes the actual finality of every petitioner's housing restriction. Tahoe-Sierra preserves the contextual inquiry for a properly presented regulatory claim and does not turn every statutory delay into a categorical taking. Lujan v. G & G's available-contract-action holding concerns procedural due process; it does not alone decide this takings theory.

**Contrary same-date path:** The lost interest is only the contractual exit option; if legislation destroyed it, constitutional and contractual harm occurred together in 1988. This is strongest if the complaint truly alleges a completed destruction on enactment rather than an unsettled as-applied real-property burden. But the assumed federal promise remains legally cognizable, and the statute preserves meaningful release routes. Neither the pleaded interest nor a complete permanent burden for every borrower may be assumed from the label. The automatic common-date rationale cannot sustain the present dismissal of the distinct as-applied component.

**Alternative lack-of-finality path:** If an effective exception or process can still materially change the burden, a regulatory taking claim may be premature, even though it is not already time-barred. Petitioners' futility assertions do not establish that every further step is useless. Conversely, the Government's statement that the offer-for-sale process was not completed does not establish that every step can change each claimant's answer. Actual agency conduct and the statutory routes must be assessed below. The incomplete borrower-specific record does not support an aggregate new finality dismissal in this Court.

**Taking remedy:** Vacate the undifferentiated early-accrual disposition and remand for each preserved taking claim's identified property interest, challenged act, complete injury, actual finality and filing interval, with remaining lawful grounds addressed separately. This is neither a holding of constitutional taking nor a ruling that contract and taking accrual must always coincide or must always differ. Contract relief, its adequacy and any independent merits defense require their own legal basis and supported record.

## 4. McKune v. Lile

**Provisional disposition:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the injunction against the specified automatic consequences for declining unprotected incriminating disclosures. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse that Fifth Amendment injunction and remand because the established consequences do not constitute compulsion. The two groups share the existence of a real incriminating risk, but disagree about compulsion and its application. The four-to-four Associate division supplies no final Court judgment.

### Common premises and the independent compulsion question

Lile's disclosures create genuine exposure concerning uncharged offenses and possibly perjury. His conviction, asserted innocence and past testimony do not extinguish every such risk. Mitchell's unanimous retained-privilege/limited-waiver rule and Reiner's no-innocence-forfeiture rule govern those distinct issues. Neither qualified confidentiality nor the reported absence of prior prosecution supplies use and derivative-use immunity. Kastigar identifies the protection needed if immunity is used to replace the privilege; no immunity is found or conferred here.

The consequences are an automatic Level III-to-Level I loss of privileges, including materially reduced property, activities, visitation, earnings and expenditures, and transfer to a cell in the maximum-security area with more restricted movement. The $140-to-$20 canteen allowance illustrates the record's actual magnitude. Housing, custody classification and privilege level are not identical. The officials' conflicting danger evidence does not establish inevitable assault or a specific added risk for Lile. There is no sentence extension, removal of earned good time, elimination of the opportunity to earn it, or established automatic loss of parole. Each side must assess this actual combination rather than substitute a more severe or entirely discretionary regime.

**Current Sandin law:** “State-created liberty in prison discipline,” effective June 19, 1995, retains genuine substantive constraints plus meaningful deprivation as a sufficient, nonexclusive due-process entitlement rule. It rejects mechanical mandatory-word analysis but does not adopt an exclusive atypical-and-significant-hardship rule. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas advocated that replacement in dissent. Neither their proposal nor the controlling due-process rule mechanically supplies the answer to a separately established Fifth Amendment penalty. A prisoner's lack of a freestanding entitlement to a privilege is not itself dispositive of unconstitutional compulsion.

### Competing sufficient grounds

**M-A — Substantial automatic penalty for protected silence.** The combination of automatic deprivation of existing earned privileges and compelled transfer with more restricted movement imposes substantial pressure specifically because the prisoner declines incriminating admissions without protection. Murphy's penalty principle and Garrity, Spevack, Gardner and Turley forbid government attaching substantial penalties to surrender of the privilege. The prison setting affects the practical assessment but does not eliminate this protection. Treatment may properly require candor for clinical participation; that premise does not independently justify substantial automatic pressure to expose oneself to prosecution.

The strongest objection is that prisoners have no right to the preferred housing or the most favorable treatment incentives and that rehabilitation depends on candid acknowledgment. The response distinguishes ordinary program benefits and supported administrative classification from the combined automatic sanctions attached to this refusal. Sandin's nonexclusive treatment of constitutional liberty reinforces the need to test the independent right, without itself establishing the Fifth Amendment violation. No finding that every lost privilege is sufficiently coercive is necessary.

Woodard involved an optional clemency invitation without a supported automatic penalty, and its eight-Associate ordinary-civil-inference ground expressly preserved actual compulsion. Baxter permits a supported civil inference rather than automatic punishment sufficient by itself. Mitchell bars use of silence to prove offense facts at sentencing, but M-A does not depend on finding a new sentencing proceeding or sentence increase here. Turner supplies legitimate administrative context; a rational rehabilitative purpose does not alone answer the independent penalty prohibition.

**M-R1 — Actual consequences fall below substantial compulsion.** The Fifth Amendment does not prohibit every adverse consequence or incentive that makes cooperation attractive. The specified reductions concern the allocation of prison privileges and housing within a valid sentence. Their established scope, including unchanged sentence and credits and the absence of a proved added physical danger, does not impose pressure severe enough to compel testimony. Garrity and the employment/licensing/contracting cases concern consequences of a different magnitude and practical character. The automatic feature establishes the connection to silence but does not eliminate the need for substantial compulsion.

The strongest objection is that these are existing privileges taken away for invoking a genuine right, not merely an unaccepted offer. M-R1 answers that the actual severity and setting still matter; calling the consequence a penalty cannot make every modest program incentive constitutional compulsion. Woodard and Baxter do not directly authorize the automatic regime, but preserve the necessary distinction between difficult choice and compelled testimony. This ground leaves serious privilege penalties, compelled waivers, sentence extensions and criminal use of compelled answers outside the present permission.

**M-R2 — Additional prison-context ground supported by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** Lawful imprisonment supplies the relevant baseline for assessing ordinary restrictions and rehabilitative incentives; consequences must be evaluated within that setting rather than by equating every prison benefit with outside employment or livelihood. Changes among ordinary sentence-authorized conditions may encourage participation without becoming a Fifth Amendment command. Their Sandin dissent supports the importance of prison context, but remains noncontrolling and is not cited as a governing Fifth Amendment test. The three can join M-R1's sufficient actual-consequences conclusion; they additionally favor this prison-management explanation. They do not need a finding that the program is the only effective treatment or that no automatic prison sanction can compel testimony.

O'Connor accepts context and the actual-severity holding, but refuses to make an atypical-and-significant-hardship requirement, a due-process entitlement test, or absence of sentence effects a universal necessary condition of Fifth Amendment compulsion. The common reversal ground therefore must not depend on such a categorical conversion. A broader rule that no ordinary confinement change can ever be a Fifth Amendment penalty lacks her support.

### Remedies and retained procedural limits

M-A preserves the injunction against the challenged consequences conditioned on these unprotected admissions. Kansas may offer voluntary treatment, provide constitutionally sufficient immunity, or make classifications on independently supported grounds; the Court need not order an immunity grant, devise treatment or establish an entitlement to every preferred condition.

M-R1 reverses the Fifth Amendment injunction at the established regime's scope and returns the case for conforming proceedings. The distinct penile-testing challenge was rejected in the district court and its appellate treatment followed the Fifth Amendment disposition. Its proper preserved procedural consequence belongs to remand; a compulsion reversal does not adjudicate the testing procedure valid on every constitutional ground. Neither disposition changes the criminal judgment, establishes new parole facts or authorizes criminal use of genuinely compelled statements.
