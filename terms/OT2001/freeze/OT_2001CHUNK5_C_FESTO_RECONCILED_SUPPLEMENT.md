## 1. Corrected common commitment

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer each retain vacatur of the en banc complete-bar judgment. Each now joins the following amendment-specific determination: the Stoll ring restriction, Stoll magnetizable-sleeve restriction and Carroll ring restriction are qualifying patentability-related narrowings. The R3 presumption of surrender therefore applies at each limitation, subject to an otherwise available defense and ordinary preservation rules. Festo bears the burden to establish that the particular accused equivalent falls outside the surrendered scope. No asserted equivalent is now held surrendered or preserved conclusively.

This conclusion is a legal application to the accepted claims and prosecution, rather than a finding that Festo conceded every predicate. It does not require the accused infringer to prove the particular equivalent surrendered before the presumption arises. Actual narrowing, the reason for narrowing and the extent of equivalent surrender remain separate inquiries.

## 2. Amendment-specific determinations

### Stoll rings

The original independent claim described sealing means at each end. The replacement independent claim incorporated the more specific pair-of-rings restriction previously found in a dependent claim. The original independent claim's general sealing requirement and the narrower ring structure are not interchangeable merely because they perform related functions. Incorporating the dependent limitation constrains the formerly broader independent claim. Its presence elsewhere in the application does not give the old independent claim that same restriction. This is the bounded comparison of the accepted sealing-function language with the chosen pair-of-rings structure; it adopts no general means-plus-function construction or rule about whether the accused single ring is equivalent. These accepted changes establish actual narrowing of the pertinent claim; the Court does not attribute counsel's unidentified oral transfer example to a particular limitation as an admission.

The ring restriction was adopted in the replacement claim submitted to obtain allowance after the section 112 rejection. The response sought reconsideration under that provision and explained the claimed impurity-prevention structure when addressing the German references. Even accepting Festo's account that the earlier claim already distinguished those references, the applicant chose a more specific claim to meet the statutory disclosure objection. Narrowing for a better description of the invention is a patentability reason; the examiner need not have dictated the precise ring language. Festo's clarification account therefore does not establish a nonpatentability reason. The Court need not resolve SMC's additional contention that the rings were independently necessary to avoid the art.

The explanation that the old sealing means already furnished the pertinent distinction may nevertheless bear on the relationship between the amendment and the single-ring substitute. That is a possible scope argument under R3, not a categorical denial of the independent claim's literal narrowing or the qualifying statutory purpose. It does not itself rebut the scope presumption.

**Source anchors:** Concession supplement §§1–4 and E1, E3–E5; Petitioner 7, Reply 6, Argument 6–7 and 47–48; validated original and replacement claims and response, JA II-24–25, II-30–31 and II-36–40. These references identify the already validated material and add no raw-source finding.

### Stoll magnetizable sleeve

The original independent claim did not restrict the sleeve to magnetizable material; the replacement incorporated that restriction from an earlier dependent claim. The material requirement narrows that independent claim. Neither an allegedly unnecessary addition nor the inventor's wish for the broader protection can remove an issued material limitation. This determination concerns the sleeve, not the distinct cylinder tube or Carroll rings.

The operative public act was submission of this materially narrower independent claim in the response seeking allowance after the section 112 rejection. The applicant chose to incorporate the sleeve restriction while recasting the rejected claim. In this documented setting, the restriction is part of the narrowing offered to secure the patent. A separate examiner demand, proof that magnetizability was independently necessary, or proof that the attorney selected the best means of answering the objection is not a condition of that legal effect.

The earlier attorney-addition account receives fair consideration. It asserts that the inventor neither intended nor authorized the restriction, that it was unnecessary, and that the claim should be read as though the words were omitted. Even taking the asserted absence of inventor intent as true, it does not establish a nonpatentability purpose for the prosecuting attorney's actual submission or negate the objectively chosen narrowing in the patentability response. The alleged error cannot be resolved by hypothetical deletion of the claim term. Nor does the district court's earlier conclusion that independent necessity was unexplained refute the actual response and claim history. The Court does not decide that the inventor testified falsely or that the attorney acted deliberately against his wishes.

A genuinely supported nonpatentability explanation for the attorney's actual choice can matter under R2; an asserted mistake is not categorically excluded merely because an attorney made it. Here the proffer identifies inventor nonintention, lack of necessity and a requested legal consequence, rather than a supported nonpatentability reason for selecting this restriction.

The trigger is thus sustained on the objective submission and the legal insufficiency of the proffered contrary grounds to displace its patentability character. It is not inferred from an unreviewed source, SMC's supposed concession, the district judge's word mystery, or silence treated as misconduct. It does not establish a rule that every voluntary textual change is presumed patentability-related. R2's distinct rule for otherwise unexplained required narrowing remains; this application does not enlarge that presumption to every voluntary edit. The supported explanation has been assessed here, without demanding contemporaneous formulae or promising another evidentiary opportunity.

Whether the nonmagnetizable accused sleeve lies outside the scope actually surrendered remains subject to Festo's separate R3 burden. Rejecting deletion of a literal limitation does not conclusively extinguish all its equivalents.

**Source anchors:** Concession supplement §§2–4 and E1, E6–E8; JA II-24–25 and II-36–40; earlier objections JA I-54–56 and district order JA I-75. The correction assesses the legal force of those validated contents, rather than finding a new historical attorney motive.

### Carroll rings

Original claim 1 did not recite the ring restriction; replacement claim 9 expressly required the pair of rings in the amended combination. Disclosure of rings in the specification did not itself impose that limitation on the earlier claim. The addition therefore narrows the relevant literal claim. The Court does not treat some other component's narrowing as a substitute for identifying this ring restriction.

The amended combination was submitted in reexamination to distinguish the German reference and preserve patentability. The applicant chose to include the ring restriction in that replacement. An added feature need not be independently novel, indispensable to allowance or singled out by the examiner before its inclusion in the chosen patentability narrowing has legal effect. The verified emphasis on spaced magnets, end members and cushions, and the asserted presence of two rings in the reference, do not exempt the ring limitation from the qualifying amendment.

Those same particulars can matter to the extent of surrender: the reason distinguishing the combination may bear only tangentially on the single-ring substitute. Festo's acknowledgment of some unrelated Carroll abandonment is likewise principally a position about relevant surrender. Neither that acknowledgment nor the denial proves that this accused equivalent was surrendered, and neither conclusively rebuts R3. The Court makes no finding that every limitation of every amended combination must share one purpose; it applies the rule to this accepted replacement, its ring addition and the documented patentability response.

**Source anchors:** Concession supplement §§1–4 and E2, E4; Petitioner 8; Argument 6–7; validated reexamination response and allowance, JA II-180–189. Stoll's section 112 rejection and sleeve explanation are not attributed to Carroll.

## 3. Corrected remand and complete-resolution check

The court of appeals receives the case to apply the rebuttable scope rule at the three established triggers. The complete-bar rule prevented the distinct inquiry whether Festo can carry its burden concerning the particular single-ring and nonmagnetizable-sleeve equivalents. That changed legal rule requires ordinary application and, only if necessary under ordinary procedure, subsidiary factual or technical work. It does not require returning the already resolved literal narrowing and patentability-purpose determinations. No fact gap is invented to justify that broader return.

R3 continues to permit a concrete, objectively supported, prosecution-anchored explanation that the particular variation falls outside the surrender. Reliable explanatory or technical material is not universally excluded merely because it is outside the contemporaneous file; it cannot contradict clear public surrender, erase a claim term or substitute private wishes for the issued instrument. The patentee retains the burden. There is no closed exception list, automatic protection for an assertedly unforeseeable substitute, guaranteed admission, mandatory hearing, automatic reopening or unconditional second chance. The lower courts must provide the legally appropriate application of the corrected rule under ordinary procedure, including the actual older-record qualification. This does not prejudge technological availability or the sufficiency of either rebuttal.

R5 remains a distinct condition. The Stoll trial exchanges establish neither general abandonment nor a Carroll-specific preservation determination. The government's report concerning Carroll summary judgment remains attributed. The legal triggers are not a ruling that SMC preserved every defense or that any forfeiture is excused. A properly presented preservation objection must be resolved in ordinary sequence on the pertinent procedural act and governing rule before an unavailable defense can supply relief. This remand creates no new preservation exception and does not convert an unreviewed filing into default. It leaves application of existing procedural law to the actual procedural history, not a new legal standard undecided.

If an asserted equivalent remains legally available, the existing element-specific rule governs the remaining infringement analysis. Overall similarity may not erase a material limitation; bad intent is not required. The en banc court's unaddressed all-elements objection and any genuinely disputed technical equivalence remain ordinary application and infringement work after legal surrender boundaries are determined. The Court does not announce a universal appellate review standard, allocate every possible equivalents issue, invalidate a patent or restore a verdict or damages automatically.

Under Engine §8, the reached trigger law and these three trigger applications are resolved. What returns is application of the new scope rule, associated necessary facts, and surviving ordinary procedural and infringement work under supplied standards. Rejection of the complete bar alone did not authorize returning every trigger; that unsupported portion of the earlier mandate is withdrawn. The first return is to the court of appeals, with district-court proceedings only as legally necessary.
