## Legislative facts

In 1989, Georgia and Maryland prohibited execution on the relevant disability ground, and fourteen States had no capital punishment. By the present event, eighteen death-penalty States had adopted disability-specific legislation: Georgia, Maryland, Kentucky, Tennessee, New Mexico, Arkansas, Colorado, Washington, Indiana, Kansas, New York, Nebraska, South Dakota, Arizona, Connecticut, Florida, Missouri and North Carolina. Twelve States then lacked capital punishment; thirty-eight retained it. These are distinct denominators and neither determines a constitutional conclusion by arithmetic.

Kentucky and Tennessee acted in 1990; New Mexico in 1991; Arkansas, Colorado, Washington, Indiana and Kansas in 1993–1994; New York in 1995; Nebraska in 1998; and the final six listed States in 2000–2001. No identified enactment reinstated the authority after adopting an exemption. Several enactments were recent and their operation had limited experience.

The protections were not uniform. Eleven of the eighteen enactments used prospective effective-date restrictions tied to offenses, convictions or sentencing; seven applied without those particular temporal restrictions. New York retained a correctional-custody murder exception.
