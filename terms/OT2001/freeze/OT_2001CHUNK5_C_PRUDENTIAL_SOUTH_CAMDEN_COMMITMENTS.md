## 76. In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation

### Question, components and operative commitments

The review concerns the Third Circuit judgment affirming the March 29, 2000 federal restriction on the Lowes' retained Florida litigation. The Lowes remained in the settlement for two eligible policies and excluded two others through the policy-specific election expressly offered in the notice. The federal order reaches motion practice, discovery, presentation of evidence and other action involving the facts of released transactions. The Florida action is stayed and inactive subject to ordinary reopening; its retained claims have not been dismissed.

The following propositions define the scope to which the individual rows refer:

- **P1 — Enforcement jurisdiction.** Incorporation of the release and express retention of jurisdiction permit the district court to enforce the settlement within Kokkonen's rule. This answers the postdismissal-jurisdiction objection; it does not satisfy section 2283 or determine what was released.
- **P2 — Relitigation exception and the actual release.** The incorporated release extinguishes claims and relief within its lawful scope, including a genuinely indirect effort to obtain relief on a released transaction. It does not, consistently with the express policy-specific exclusions, turn every fact common to a released policy and an excluded policy into a released claim or a conclusively determined issue. Section 2283 therefore does not permit this blanket evidentiary restraint solely because proof overlaps. A supported injunction against an actual released claim or inconsistent determination remains available; the subject and use of the proposed proof must be connected to that protected adjudication, rather than identified by association alone.
- **P3 — Aid of jurisdiction.** Ongoing administration of a complex settlement is relevant but does not establish that the retained state claims or their overlapping proof seriously impair the federal court's ability to administer or dispose of its case. No such necessity sufficient for this order's breadth is established by the supplied reasons. Retained jurisdiction, settlement size and a general concern about future claims do not replace the statutory showing. The All-Writs Act does not remove section 2283's independent restriction.
- **P4 — Disposition and remedy.** Reverse the Third Circuit insofar as it sustains the categorical restriction and remand to remove that restriction. The settlement and release remain operative; any further request for protection must identify the actual released claim or issue, its lawful preclusive scope, the conduct requiring restraint and the applicable section 2283 exception. The Florida court applies its own reopening and evidentiary rules. The remand grants neither automatic admission of a particular item nor automatic recovery on any retained count.

The proposed rule is a statutory and release-specific answer to the order under review, not a categorical rule that settlement obligations can never affect evidence. The express exclusions and the distinction between adjudicated claims and evidence perform the necessary legal work. The Lowes' independent constitutional notice and vagueness objections need not be resolved to remove the present restraint on these sufficient statutory grounds. The Court does not use those objections to reopen certification or the settlement's completed approval.

## 79. South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection

### Question, components and distinct paths

The Third Circuit reversed the preliminary injunction's section 1983 basis. The residents originally pleaded both intentional discrimination under section 601 and a direct effects theory under section 602; the later amendment added section 1983. The original theories were not shown to have been abandoned. The injunction was suspended on June 15, 2001. The present question includes the asserted section 1983 right, the separate prospective Title VI route and the status of the original section 601 intentional claim.

- **S1 — Section 1983, current-law path.** Powell's separate holding rejects the asserted broader individual effects right under section 1983. Section 601's intentional-discrimination right and section 602's agency instructions do not themselves create that additional individual right; regulatory elaboration cannot supply one that the statute lacks. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas adopt this ground and would retain the rejection of the section 1983 basis.
- **S1-A — Section 1983, supported reconsideration path.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain their published Powell position: the identified beneficiaries, statutory nondiscrimination protection, express implementation authority and a valid, sufficiently definite implementing requirement can together define the individual entitlement, with section 1983 supplying the enacted remedy. They would reject the categorical section 1983 bar and reconsider Powell to that extent. They do not treat every regulation or program-administration command as a right. This is their proposed legal change, not entering law or a controlling proposition of this handoff.
- **S2 — Direct Title VI effects route, current-law path.** Powell's direct-action holding, which Sandoval applied to a different agency's regulation and a different state program, recognizes a private action for declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient's ongoing violation of a valid, duly issued section 602 regulation prohibiting discriminatory effects, within Title VI's particular statutory context; the action rests on that holding, not on a regulation's own force. Sections 7.35(b) and (c) are section 602 effects regulations of that kind, and a state permitting program receiving EPA assistance is within the action if coverage is established. The retained direct effects theory may therefore proceed if the residents establish an applicable regulation validly issued under the authority Congress conferred, a defendant covered through the required federal-assistance relationship, a concrete exclusion, denial of benefits or other material discriminatory effect within the regulation's actual terms attributable to an identified permitting policy or method of administration, an actual or imminent continuing effect that prospective relief can meaningfully correct, and an appropriate defendant and fitted remedy. Regulatory validity is not presumed where genuinely contested; a numerical disparity alone establishes no violation; relief is declaratory and prospective only; and section 602's notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing and funding-termination procedures continue to govern the agency's own powers, with agency petition and complaint processes remaining. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas apply this holding as governing statutory precedent, as Justice O'Connor's Sandoval opinion concurring in the judgment did; they keep their published Powell objection to its account of congressional authorization as their own view but do not ask the Court to reconsider Powell or Sandoval, and they would vacate the rejection of the direct route.
- **S2-A — Direct Title VI route, integrated-statute path.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer apply Powell's direct-action holding, which they joined, and Sandoval's Court opinion, which Souter wrote and the other three joined: section 601's beneficiaries and section 602's express implementation direction support a bounded prospective action to enforce valid, duly issued section 602 effects requirements against the proper covered defendant. They would vacate the rejection of the retained direct claim and return it for its actual requirements. Section 7.35(b)'s criteria-or-methods rule requires the actual prohibited effect, including its alternative concerning defeat or substantial impairment of program objectives for protected persons. Section 7.35(c)'s site-selection provision has its own purpose-or-effect terms and is not automatically a pleaded, proved claim merely because it is in the copied regulation. Coverage, the particular requirement's validity and definiteness, causation and fitted relief remain actual adjudicative questions. This applies entering law; it proposes no legal change.
- **S3 — Jurisdiction, defendant and the distinct intentional claim.** The asserted substantial federal controversy is distinct from eventual entitlement. Under Verizon Maryland and Young, an actually connected officer may face prospective relief for an alleged continuing federal violation within the enacted remedy's limits; Commissioner Shinn is named, but his required connection and the relief's proper scope must be applied to this record. The remand does not resolve NJDEP's direct amenability by assuming consent. The original section 601 intentional-discrimination claim retains its separate intent requirement and actual procedural footing.
- **S4 — Disposition and remedy.** Vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it rejects the retained direct Title VI effects theory and insofar as it forecloses further proceedings on the original section 601 intentional-discrimination claim, both of which the February 13, 2001 complaint pleaded and the amendment did not abandon, and remand for those claims and the ordinary defendant and equitable inquiries under S3. On the direct claim the remand determines regulatory validity and coverage insofar as genuinely contested, the challenged permitting or administrative method, the supported effect and its causal connection, the proper defendants and, only if a violation is proved, violation-matched prospective relief. The eight Associates support this vacatur and remand, S2 and S2-A supplying distinct grounds for the same direct-route vacatur, but divide on S1 versus S1-A as specified below. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would otherwise affirm, insofar as the Third Circuit rejected the section 1983 basis; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would vacate the whole judgment and also return the section 1983 theory. Vacatur itself does not revive the previously suspended preliminary injunction; operative preliminary relief must rest on a valid route and the requisite findings. The existing findings retain their actual status for ordinary review; the Court requires no new hearing or wholesale reopening merely because the source excerpts do not reproduce every finding.

The S1 division is real. The common direct-route and intentional-claim remand does not turn the four proposed section 1983 supporters into adopters of the contrary section 1983 rule. Nor does agreement on the direct route turn the four who apply Powell's direct-action holding as governing precedent into adopters of its account of congressional authorization or of the regulatory-right theory under section 1983.
