# Compaq — opinion form fixed before assignment

Event: Compaq Computer Corp. v. Commissioner, merits, May 28, 2002.

The legal work warrants one signed Opinion of the Court after full merits submission. The dispute requires a reasoned application of the income and substance principles to a completed market transaction and a distinction between historical findings and legal characterization. It is not an unexplained or summary disposition. No related case is combined with it.

The opinion has three substantive parts, chosen without examining assignment counts or considering an author:

1. The governing review and computation: preserve supported subsidiary historical findings; independently review tax characterization; include the taxpayer's gross dividend in pretax income, and account for the legally available credit when computing worldwide after-tax profit.
2. The substance and purpose of the actual transaction: retain meaningful substantive-sham scrutiny while recognizing this established market purchase, dividend entitlement and resale, with an actual return and residual risks; reject tax motivation alone, an ownership-only or nominal-profit safe harbor, a newly imposed risk threshold, and a universal formulation unnecessary to this record.
3. The consequences within the reviewed dispute: affirm recognition and removal of the sham-dependent deficiency and negligence penalty, leave only computation under the applicable provisions within the Commissioner's stipulation, and treat the contingent expense-deduction request as inoperative after recognition. The penalty ground is the absence of an underpayment from the rejected adjustment, not a finding that limited investigation always establishes reasonable care.

The eight frozen Associate commitments support all three parts. Stone's approved position supports Parts I and II and Part III's recognition, deficiency, penalty and computation treatment. His supplement is silent on the separate contingent request to deduct expenses if the transaction is disregarded. The standing fallback supplies his agreement with the majority's disposition of that request only, without an added reason. The concluding sentence addressing that contingent request therefore has eight opinion joins and Stone's judgment-only agreement; no separate writing is needed to supply a ground he has not approved.

This limited join does not require dividing authorship: the eight Associates can each carry the entire opinion. It is recorded to preserve the boundary of Stone's authorized position, not to change assignment eligibility or counts. The eligibility assessment and independent recount follow this form decision.
