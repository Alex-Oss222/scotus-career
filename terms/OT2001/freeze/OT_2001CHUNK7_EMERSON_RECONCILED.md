## Record and doctrinal sufficiency

The pertinent order followed the September 4 evidentiary hearing and was entered September 14. The August ex parte order is not the charged predicate. The testimony described a threat to kill the wife's friend, communicated to her; it expressly did not describe a threat to kill her. The wife's testimony, Emerson's opportunity to respond and the order are distinct from a concession or separately adjudicated criminal act. The order contains no express danger finding. Nothing here supplies missing words or converts the threat's target into the wife.

The six affirming Associates accept the legal significance of the hearing and resulting order under the protective-necessity standards described in the lower opinion, PDF pages 103–108. Those standards are the lower court's construction of Texas law; they are neither findings recited in the order nor requirements Congress stated in section 922(g)(8). The full sequence supports the six's conclusion that an actual protective adjudication occurred. The strongest contrary inference is that routine divorce restraints could have issued without an individual danger determination. That objection is answered on the supplied combination of specific evidence, process, substantive legal requirements and targeted bodily-injury restrictions, rather than by declaring every properly signed form adequate.

Napier's precise facts included violence and possible recurrence. Its six-Justice rule permits a civil danger predicate but does not itself decide whether this threat-only order suffices. The present six therefore adopt a confined extension: a force-prohibiting order entered after actual notice and meaningful opportunity to contest particular violent-threat evidence may supply the predicate when applicable law requires real protective necessity, the order addresses that injury, and the firearm disability depends on the operative order. Completed injury and statutory (C)(i) terminology are unnecessary. This is the model's disclosed legal choice on the existing record, not an added fact discovered during reconciliation.

Preventing armed domestic injury is important. The possession disability substantially addresses armed escalation associated with the adjudicated protective necessity: a command against injury does not remove firearm access, and a public-carry restriction does not address private domestic encounters. Neither inevitable violence nor earlier gun use must be shown. Yet the exclusion's individual scope still needs justification; others' continued access to arms supplies none. The actual predicate and order-dependent operation do the limiting work. Time alone, a safety label or generic civil status does not make a full prohibition merely incidental or adequate under intermediate scrutiny. Wright's strict-scrutiny category and prohibition on general abolition of meaningful ordinary defensive home possession remain intact.

Modification by the issuing court and appellate mandamus were available. Direct interlocutory appeal was not. The order lasted until divorce decree or further order; any provision incorporated into the final decree would have its own review posture. No short maximum duration, guaranteed successful restoration or actual use of review is assumed. A general conviction requirement would contradict Napier, while a demand to assess the full federal restriction remains an available constitutional objection. The two dissenting commitments choose the latter route.

The statute's disjunction remains independent of constitutional sufficiency. Ordinary collateral relitigation of the divorce evidence is also distinct from reviewing the additional federal disability under the personal-right framework. Lewis's conviction-disability rule does not render every later civil-order disability immune from constitutional examination. Neither side authorizes routine retrial of every state factual or procedural question.

The Fifth Amendment disposition is independently sufficient to reject that particular dismissal ground, but does not itself resolve the arms objection. The complete lower account at PDF pages 12–16 and n.7 identifies the signed federal warning before the injunction, later knowledge of the order and its contents, and possession alleged more than sixty days later. The form is a concrete circumstance prompting inquiry, not an irrebuttable finding of later recollection. Lambert's absence-of-warning setting is therefore not this record. The current Staples holding concerns known regulated weapon characteristics and disclaims a general knowledge-of-illegality requirement. Current Bryan requires knowing unlawful dealing under its distinct willfulness provision; it does not enlarge every possession statute's knowledge requirement. No guilt determination or waiver of trial proof follows.

The federal response's transfer, impossibility and property theories are attributed arguments outside the supplied review, not new preservation findings. Good-faith dispossession was not the factual situation recited below, PDF page 14 n.6 and page 109 n.65. No universal ruling on those defenses, third-party arrangements or the property interest is needed. The supplied review and independent dismissal ground are resolved without a new Commerce Clause or Tenth Amendment holding.
