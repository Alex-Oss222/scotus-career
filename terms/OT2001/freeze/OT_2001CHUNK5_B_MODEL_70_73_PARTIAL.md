## 70. Verizon Maryland / United States v. Maryland Public Service Commission

### Scope, components and operative commitments

The two Maryland dockets, Nos. 00-1531 and 00-1711, receive the same proposed disposition: reverse the judgment sustaining the categorical dismissal and remand for adjudication under the following confined rules. All eight Associates support this disposition and the common grounds below. The limited Question 2 grant is the starting constraint: assume without deciding that section 252(e)(6)'s determination-review sentence does not itself authorize this enforcement suit, and decide the independent section 1331 question. Consolidation with one another and argument alongside Mathias do not broaden that grant.

**M1 — Independent federal-question jurisdiction.** Section 1331 supplies subject-matter jurisdiction over this substantial claim that the commission's later enforcement order violates the Telecommunications Act and the applicable federal rules. The claim's failure on its merits, if established, would not retrospectively make the asserted federal controversy insubstantial. Interpreting an interconnection agreement as part of that controversy does not itself turn an asserted federal violation into a purely state-law dispute. Conversely, no rule is adopted that every disagreement about an approved agreement arises under federal law.

Section 252(e)(6) does not displace that otherwise available jurisdiction in this action. Its first sentence expressly supplies exclusive remedies for the particular failure to act described in paragraph (5): FCC substitution and judicial review of the FCC's action. Its second sentence permits federal district-court review of commission determinations for compliance with sections 251 and 252 and does not repeat that exclusivity. Paragraph (e)(4)'s state-court restriction concerns approval or rejection of agreements; it does not say that section 1331 disappears for a substantial federal challenge to the later action at issue. This ruling neither narrows the first sentence's express failure-to-act exclusivity nor decides the complete reach of the second sentence. State-law requirements preserved by (e)(3), the different negotiated/arbitrated approval standards and state contract claims retain their distinct work.

Bell v. Hood, as applied in the actual Steel Co. holding, supplies the substantial-claim/merits distinction. Schacht and Lapides preserve claim-specific immunity and cause-of-action questions rather than treating them as destruction of all federal jurisdiction. AT&T's federal implementation/state application allocation supplies context; neither it nor Verizon Communications decides this review question in advance.

**M2 — The challenged action is administrative.** This suit targets the later commission determination following the FCC's intervening action, not appellate reversal of the Maryland court's earlier judgment. The restriction on lower federal appellate review of state-court judgments does not become a categorical restriction on review of state administrative orders. That conclusion decides no properly presented claim- or issue-preclusion defense arising from the actual earlier litigation and no question about a disguised attack on a state judgment in some different case.

**M3 — A properly considered officer-relief alternative cannot sustain the categorical dismissal.** Officer immunity and the asserted indispensability of the commission were independent grounds sustaining dismissal below. They are considered only to determine whether those grounds require affirmance despite the answer to the granted question. Florida Prepaid's actual respondent-alternative rule permits that inquiry without enlarging the relief sought; it does not grant all the originally petitioned questions.

The complaint alleges that the commissioners responsible for the order will continue enforcing duties contrary to federal law. It seeks, among other relief, an injunction against that enforcement. Young permits adjudication of that prospective federal claim against the connected officers. Whether federal law actually invalidates the order is a merits question; accepting the allegation for this threshold inquiry does not decide it. The state commission's role in an administrative scheme does not itself remove the officer route, and this statute's review provisions do not provide the sort of exclusive limited remedial sequence that would be circumvented by permitting this prospective claim. The actual Seminole Tribe rule itself rejects automatic displacement merely because a statute is detailed, while retaining its own remedial limits.

The requested declaration's relationship to future enforcement, accrued private payment duties and the request that private carriers refund compensation remain separate. A requested private-carrier refund is not simply a demand for payment from the State's treasury; that distinction does not establish the refund's availability. No entitlement to repayment, retrospective state compensation, declaration solely about a completed violation or command enforcing state contract law is approved. Edelman and Pennhurst remain operative. On remand the courts must determine the actual claim and relief that may proceed, rather than deem every requested remedy prospective because an injunction is also requested.

The commission's absence cannot by itself make these connected-officer claims impossible: officers may supply effective prospective restraint of the enforcement they conduct. The district court must address any genuinely remaining party and remedy question at that claim's actual scope. This is not a universal ruling that a state commission can never be a required party, and it does not establish that every private refund claim can proceed without it.

**M4 — Questions not decided.** No ruling is made on the full coverage of section 252(e)(6), direct commission amenability, consent from undertaking a regulatory role, an independently sufficient congressional authorization of direct-State suit, or the compensation merits. Florida Prepaid's Article I permission remains actual law; it is neither rejected nor automatically applied without an unmistakable authorization inquiry. College Savings Bank's commercial-consent rule does not automatically classify a regulatory role, and Lapides's voluntary-removal predicates are absent from this original federal action. Section 1331 is a jurisdictional grant, not an implied damages cause or a determination that all pleaded relief is legally available. The equitable officer claim, statutory claim sufficiency, defendants, merits and remedy retain their own requirements.

## 73. Mathias v. WorldCom Technologies, Inc.

### Threshold commitment and limits

**All eight Associates would dismiss the writ as improvidently granted, without deciding the substantive telecommunications or immunity questions.** This is an exercise of review discretion on the actual favorable-judgment posture, not a holding that Article III categorically forbids every appeal by a prevailing official or that every denied motion is merely reasoning. No new merits or general appellate-aggrievement rule is established by the writ dismissal. The Seventh Circuit's judgment remains undisturbed; its jurisdiction and waiver reasoning receives no Supreme Court endorsement.

The commission order was sustained. The commissioners separately sought dismissal from the federal proceeding, their motions were postponed and denied, and the lower court treated the favorable merits determination as binding after that denial despite the lack of a formal judgment against them. Those are distinct facts and the denied dismissal request is not ignored. The postponed decision also deprived them of an opportunity to pursue an ordinary interlocutory immunity appeal before completion; the procedural objection is serious. It does not itself restore that lost opportunity after the merits litigation has concluded or supply reimbursement for its past burdens.

The separate motion denial is adverse to the commissioners' legal position, but the requested review now principally seeks reversal of the jurisdictional and immunity bases on which the completed favorable adjudication occurred. Its binding merits disposition validates the commission order; it neither requires the commissioners to change that order nor adjudges a substantive right against them. A favorable merits determination's binding character is not by itself an adverse collateral command. The certiorari vehicle accordingly does not require resolution of the asserted sovereign right through a judgment changing the merits or awarding a presently identified distinct remedy.

Electrical Fittings is the strongest alternative. It permits elimination of an adverse adjudication actually included in a favorable decree without requiring certain preclusion or an already pending later lawsuit. Roper's explanation includes the concern about adverse precedent or preclusion connected to that decree, not just monetary injury. Here no adverse compensation or regulatory-right adjudication is embedded in a decree against the commissioners. Removing a denial of dismissal would require deciding the contested immunity/jurisdiction issue or vacating a favorable merits adjudication merely to replace its legal foundation; it is not the narrow elimination of an immaterial adverse substantive decree component that Electrical Fittings ordered. This difference supports declining discretionary review here without announcing that precedential effects can never matter.

Forney permits review of an actual unmet claim for greater relief and expressly reserves wholly favorable relief and efforts to obtain another explanation. The commissioners' requested dismissal makes their analogy nonfrivolous, but an unsuccessful defensive request is not automatically identical to the claimant's still-unawarded benefits. Roper permits review of its collateral certification decision because of the named parties' continuing interest; it does not compel review of every preserved collateral issue. An independent continuing adverse adjudication could warrant a different posture-specific result. No such general possibility is eliminated by the proposed writ dismissal.

This conclusion does not infer from an unavailable paper that the commissioners failed to plead or prove something. The operative lower opinions and orders sufficiently establish the present posture. Nor does it require inventing a future case, preclusion judgment, waiver, damages demand or enforcement burden. The absence of certain future preclusion is not the decisive premise. The ground is the actual relation among the favorable merits, the denied defensive motions and the confined corrective relief available in the review now sought.
