## Components and governing limits

**T1 — Functional platform integration and the undifferentiated tying judgment.** The District Court's existing per se tying judgment cannot stand without accounting for whether the asserted integration materially changes the category's competitive premise. Separate demand remains evidence of distinct products under Jefferson Parish and Kodak. It does not by itself settle whether this novel platform combination warrants automatic condemnation. Conversely, describing software as integrated does not prove efficiency or eliminate separate products.

**T2 — Contractual and removal restrictions within the tying claim.** The separate question is whether the OEM restrictions, omitted removal utility and related restrictions all receive rule-of-reason treatment as part of the presented combination, or whether ordinary coercive restrictions remain eligible for the established per se category when no material functional integration connects them to the asserted benefit. A benefit from distributing application interfaces does not automatically justify forcing the entire browser application or preventing subsequent removal. Commingling is not added as a District Court tying ground: the lower opinion identifies it as a distinct monopoly-maintenance basis.

**T3 — Scope of a tying remand.** Every Justice rejects a final finding of reasonableness or liability on the unresolved competitive issues. Under the rule-of-reason path, actual harm to browser competition must be shown and the claimed benefits tested against each restraint. Operating-system harm supporting section 2 is not a substitute. The price-bundling theory retains the unresolved positive-increment issue; the competing-vendor discount conflict cannot be resolved by assertion. The default-browser override's section 2 treatment does not foreclose its different browser-market inquiry. An additional section 1 remedy must be justified. No Justice independently reopens the attempted-monopolization reversal or gives plaintiffs another opportunity to prove the precise market and independent entry barriers foreclosed by that judgment; what is implicit in the tying arrangement retains its stated scope. These case-specific preservation limits are not adopted as universal antitrust requirements.

**D1 — Appearance violation and reassignment.** The cumulative secret merits interviews, beginning before the findings and continuing through remedy, meet the informed-observer standard. Ordinary judicial evaluation does not explain the additional choice to discuss pending credibility, integration and remedy questions privately with reporters while withholding notice from the parties. The violation does not depend on proving the judge's actual mental state. Reassignment is required. A finding that every interview account is accurate, an independent constitutional-bias holding, and a general ban on every judicial public statement are unnecessary.

**D2 — Existing remedial decree.** Preserve vacatur of the entire June 7, 2000 decree, including structural and conduct relief, and require lawful remedial proceedings before another judge. The independent hearing, explanation and altered-liability defects remain sufficient lower-court grounds. The disqualification issue provides an additional reason. Neither divestiture nor its categorical exclusion is ordered; relief must rest on the liability that lawfully remains and a proper remedial record.

**D3 — Retention of liability findings and dependent conclusions.** Liljeberg requires a separate civil-remedy assessment of injustice to these parties, effects on other cases and public confidence. Its finality and Rule 60(b)(6) prerequisites are not transplanted to this direct appeal. Actual bias is not an indispensable condition of retrospective relief. Nor does an appearance violation automatically nullify every earlier act. Rule 52(a) remains ordinary clear-error review for findings properly retained. Documentary findings receive that standard too; describing review as painstaking creates no intermediate standard and no fresh appellate factfinding. A needed new factual determination belongs in a trial court.

The competing D3 dispositions are definite, rather than hypothetical alternative judgments:

* **Further tailored inquiry:** Vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it finally denies further section 455 relief from retained liability findings and dependent conclusions, and remand for a new judge to determine what can properly be retained under Liljeberg and to reconsider the material factual foundations requiring fresh determination. Do not order an automatic retrial of every issue. Undisputed matters and independently sustainable legal conclusions may remain at their established scope; any material disputed inference or credibility determination that requires reconsideration must be decided anew rather than deemed cured because it survived clear-error review. The previously announced legal rulings on antitrust elements remain relevant to the facts lawfully found. This disposition does not itself declare every retained finding tainted.
* **Preserve the appellate allocation:** Affirm the refusal to vacate the retained liability findings and conclusions beyond the ordinary merits corrections, while preserving reassignment and a wholly new remedy. The reasons must rest on the combined Liljeberg interests and the actual division of the proceedings, not an actual-bias prerequisite or a fiction that the appellate court retried the case. This is a case-specific remedial judgment, not a rule that normal appellate review invariably cures an appearance violation.

## Independent issue-specific commitments

### Stevens

**Tying commitment.** Vacate the undifferentiated tying disposition and require a differentiated remand. His Jefferson Parish majority opinion, 466 U.S. 2, 9–18 and 21–25, and his full Kodak majority join preserve the established rule for separate-demand products forced upon buyers through tying power. His join in Breyer's California Dental separate opinion gives operative weight to the actual restriction rather than a benign description of its purpose. Browser demand and contractual suppression of removal cannot be erased by the word integration.

**Competing paths and decisive distinction.** Rule-of-reason treatment of every restriction is materially plausible because the platform may make additional functionality useful to third-party applications. Its strongest objection is that this explains distribution of useful functionality more readily than compelled retention of the whole application. Retaining the ordinary tying category for every aspect is also too broad: history of separate demand may inadequately describe a genuinely new functional combination. Stevens therefore accepts fuller competitive inquiry for that integration but refuses an automatic exception for separable coercive restrictions. Courts should determine the character of each restraint and apply the existing per se requirements where the conduct is an ordinary forced tie, rather than require plaintiffs to disprove every suggested innovation benefit under a newly universal effects test.

**Remedy and join barrier.** He does not reinstate the full District Court liability judgment: factual dependencies, the precise restraint classified and remaining tying elements still require lawful resolution, and D3 requires further inquiry. He can join T1, the rejection of software immunity and the restraint-specific factual directions. He cannot join a direction that all contractual or removal restraints escape the existing category solely because they accompany this platform combination. He proposes no overruling of Kodak or revival of attempted monopolization.

**Disqualification commitment.** Support D1 and D2; choose further tailored inquiry under D3. His Liljeberg opinion, 486 U.S. 847, 862–870 and notes 11 and 14, treats nondisclosure, the opportunity to seek timely relief and the limits of deferential appellate review as relevant to retrospective relief. His full join in Kennedy's Liteky opinion rejects an added actual-bias threshold. Here the disclosed beginning of the contacts precedes the findings and concerns their subjects. A line drawn only at the remedial decree does not adequately answer that temporal and substantive connection.

**Adversarial remedy test.** Preserving the appellate allocation protects innocent plaintiffs and avoids waste; Liljeberg itself accords due consideration to appellate remedial judgment. That is the strongest alternative. It does not, for Stevens, justify insulating contested findings merely because the appellate court found no actual bias and no clear error. Conversely, wholesale vacatur would discard undisputed matters without showing that it serves the statutory interests. His chosen remand gives the new judge the needed assessment while preserving usable evidence and legal rulings. A join cannot require proof that a particular finding was actually biased, and cannot describe every prior finding as necessarily invalid.

### O'Connor

**Tying commitment.** Affirm the rule-of-reason remand for this combination, including its contractual and removal aspects. Her Jefferson Parish concurrence, 466 U.S. 32–42, argues that tying analysis should admit actual efficiencies rather than incur the costs of economic inquiry and then exclude its benefits. Her Kodak dissent join and her California Dental controlling-opinion join reinforce the need to test the competitive mechanism. Those positions support this bounded result without requiring her broader Jefferson Parish proposal to become the holding.

**Competing paths and decisive distinction.** Keeping the existing category for separable coercion has substantial force: operating-system power is retained below, unlike the alleged derivative power she rejected in Kodak. Nevertheless, the unresolved platform benefit and product boundary make rule-of-reason analysis a sound way to separate beneficial distribution from unjustified obstruction. Its strongest objection is that a seller could invoke innovation to delay condemnation of ordinary coercion. Her answer is a real, restriction-specific inquiry, not immunity or acceptance of Microsoft's explanations as facts. Conventional tying law remains outside the decided setting.

**Remedy and join barrier.** She joins the remand's actual-effects inquiry, unresolved price increment, different browser-market question and limited remedial scope. No new browser monopoly finding is necessary merely to pursue the preserved section 1 theory. She will not join an assumption that every restriction is beneficial or an instruction reviving foreclosed attempted-monopolization proof. Her broader preference for rule-of-reason tying law is not demanded as a condition of this join.

**Disqualification commitment.** Support D1 and D2; preserve the appellate allocation under D3. Her Liteky concurrence join permits disqualification for improper outside circumstances even though its treatment of judicially formed attitudes differs from the controlling opinion. The secrecy and audience make this an outside circumstance on either approach. Her separate Liljeberg dissent, 486 U.S. 874, left the independent remedial assessment to the lower courts even while disagreeing over constructive knowledge. These contacts present no constructive-knowledge problem, and the court below has already addressed the relevant remedial interests.

**Adversarial remedy test.** Further inquiry is plausible because the interviews began before the findings and touched credibility. Clear-error review is no independent trial. Nevertheless, she gives weight to the appellate court's differentiated merits review and its express consideration of plaintiffs' innocence, repetition, deterrence and public confidence. Reassignment and full replacement of the remedy address the stage with the most pronounced remedial misconduct. The combination supports retention here without making actual bias indispensable. She cannot join a general clear-error cure, a premise that all misconduct occurred after liability, or a categorical refusal to reopen liability in another appearance case. Her vote affirms the remedial allocation, not every expression in the lower opinion.

### Scalia

**Tying commitment.** Affirm rule-of-reason treatment of the presented combination. His Kodak dissent and authorship of the actual Khan and NYNEX opinions supply distinct reasons to demand fit between the practice and the category. Kodak does not permit this Court to reject an established operating-system-power determination by assuming equipment rivalry; the current reason for rejecting automatic tying condemnation is the competitive character of this platform integration. His California Dental majority join supports fuller inquiry where the offered explanation cannot fairly be disposed of by labels.

**Competing paths and decisive distinction.** Existing tying law could cover separate browser demand and express restrictions without modification. The objection to a new category limit is especially strong for contractual nonremoval. But treating design, distribution and access restrictions under the rule of reason allows those distinctions to do their actual work without making the initial market convention conclusive. This is not a finding of benign conduct. Conversely, a broad software exception would exceed the record and turn an unproved explanation into immunity. He accepts the bounded, effects-based remand, not that broader route.

**Remedy and join barrier.** No final judgment for Microsoft on tying; plaintiffs retain the preserved opportunity to prove browser-market harm outweighing supported benefits. He will not join automatic additional relief for conduct already addressed under section 2 or a new opportunity to supply the failed attempted-monopolization predicates. His position needs no general abolition of per se tying and no new section 2 merits holding.

**Disqualification commitment.** Support D1 and D2; preserve the appellate allocation under D3. His Liteky concurrence protects ordinary judicially formed judgments, but the secret press relationship is an additional outside circumstance, not simply an adverse ruling. His Liljeberg dissent join, 486 U.S. 870–874, cautions against disregarding the particular justification for disturbing prior adjudication. Its constructive-knowledge and closed-judgment objections do not transfer to these knowing interviews during a direct appeal; the relevant continuity is that violation and remedy remain separate.

**Adversarial remedy test.** A further inquiry can be justified by the pre-findings contacts and the inability of clear-error review to remake credibility choices. Against it stands the actual appellate analysis under all three Liljeberg interests, not merely the absence of actual bias. The extensive innocent-party burden, preservation of identified liability after legal correction and new remedy before a new judge support the lower allocation. He would not presume every adjudicative act invalid from the statutory violation. He also rejects a new intermediate appellate factual-review standard. He can join case-specific retention, but not an actual-bias requirement, an appellate-trial fiction or a claim that this disposition establishes harmlessness for all appearance violations.

### Kennedy

**Tying commitment.** Affirm rule-of-reason treatment of this platform combination, with a concrete explanation tied to each restriction. His Kodak majority join protects evidence of independent demand and rejects categorical economic assumptions; his California Dental dissent join insists that a possible benefit cannot endlessly postpone evaluation of demonstrated competitive restraints. His Khan and NYNEX joins also recognize limits on automatic categories. Together those positions support testing this new integration without accepting its asserted benefits as true.

**Competing paths and decisive distinction.** Stevens's differentiated categorical route is plausible for contractual barriers to removing a separately demanded browser. The objection to a common effects inquiry is that API availability may say little about forced retention of the browser application. Kennedy resolves that objection within the inquiry: a substantial platform explanation must be examined, but it supplies no justification for a restriction it does not explain. The present record and per se analysis do not establish the net browser-market effect of every challenged practice. He does not treat his California Dental sufficiency vote as a finding that this different record is sufficient.

**Remedy and join barrier.** He joins further proceedings without a compulsory comprehensive market study in every case; depth follows what the actual competitive explanation requires. He rejects an industry exemption, an affirmative efficiency finding and equating operating-system injury with tying injury. The price and default-browser questions retain their neutral-record limits. The accepted remand does not reopen all antitrust merits.

**Disqualification commitment.** Support D1 and D2; choose further tailored inquiry under D3. His own controlling Liteky opinion makes reasonable appearance an independent statutory protection; his Liljeberg majority join accepts retrospective relief without actual bias when public confidence and fairness require it. The operative concern includes secret views on integration and credibility before findings, not simply the later breakup analogy.

**Adversarial remedy test.** The lower court's innocence, litigation-burden and deterrence reasons are substantial, and a decree-specific remedy may be sufficient in some cases. But allowing retained contested findings to stand merely because no actual bias was discerned gives too little effect to the established appearance violation at the factfinding stage. Ordinary clear-error review cannot decide whether a different neutral factfinder would choose the same permissible inference. A new judge should identify what is independently retainable and which material findings require fresh determination under all three Liljeberg interests. No general duty to retry every witness follows. He cannot join either automatic universal vacatur or automatic preservation after appellate review.

### Souter

**Tying commitment.** Affirm the bounded rule-of-reason remand. His California Dental controlling opinion calibrates inquiry to a restraint's actual mechanism and material explanation; his Kodak majority join preserves separate demand and the evidentiary inquiry into power. Functional connection does not merge products as a matter of law, but historically distinct demand does not alone establish that every new platform combination fits an automatic category.

**Competing paths and decisive distinction.** Keeping ordinary categorical treatment for readily separable contractual coercion is a serious alternative. It prevents an undefined claim of innovation from swallowing Kodak. The difficulty is identifying the actual product and restraint without considering the asserted shared functionality and platform use. Souter chooses rule-of-reason analysis for this combination, requiring the actual explanation to match the actual restriction. Its principal counterargument is excess litigation over plainly harmful terms; California Dental answers by permitting abbreviated inquiry when experience and the established mechanism genuinely suffice, not by making exhaustive analysis obligatory.

**Remedy and join barrier.** He will join no universal software exception, no requirement of a complete market-definition trial for every theory, and no assumption that the claimed interface benefit excuses prevention of browser removal. His chosen remand leaves disputed price facts and ultimate competitive balance for lawful proceedings. It preserves the unchallenged attempted-monopolization disposition and no more than the case-specific remand limits it entails.

**Disqualification commitment.** Support D1 and D2; choose further tailored inquiry under D3. His full join in Kennedy's Liteky opinion makes cumulative reasonable appearance sufficient. His own California Dental remedial discussion, joined by O'Connor, Scalia and Thomas, permits appellate assessment of an existing record but does not authorize appellate reweighing of supported facts. That institutional distinction matters here: the court of appeals expressly used clear-error review, not independent resolution of the credibility and expert conflicts.

**Adversarial remedy test.** Liljeberg allows preservation and gives the reviewing court's remedy due consideration; the innocent-party burden strongly opposes an all-issues retrial. Still, the lower opinion's reliance on absence of actual bias does not adequately explain retaining all pre-remedy findings after secret comments on their subjects. The new judge should perform the necessary differentiated inquiry, retaining what is independently supportable and reconsidering what cannot properly remain binding. Souter does not require a new evidentiary hearing for every fact, and does not treat documentary evidence as exempt from Rule 52(a). A common opinion must preserve both limits.

### Thomas

**Tying commitment.** Affirm rule-of-reason treatment of this combination. His Kodak dissent join and California Dental controlling-opinion join support caution before automatic condemnation of a commercially integrated arrangement. His Khan and NYNEX joins preserve category-specific treatment and actual competitive injury. The existing finding of operating-system power is not negated by the derivative-power objection he joined in Kodak.

**Competing paths and decisive distinction.** Applying the settled category to contractual restraints is plausible because Microsoft does not dispute the identified binding acts and distinct browser demand is supported. Yet the claimed platform function makes the competitive relation different enough to warrant testing actual harms and benefits. Its strongest objection is the weak fit between interface diffusion and prohibiting removal. That weakness may defeat the particular justification; it does not establish that the entire combination should receive automatic condemnation. He rejects both a general software privilege and a final judgment that the integration is efficient.

**Remedy and join barrier.** Rule-of-reason proceedings must remain within the preserved claim, address browser-market competition and justify any added remedy. No reopening of all section 2 issues or invention of market and price findings is permissible. The broader intellectual-property position he joined in CSU does not supply an adjudicated defense within these granted questions; no copyright or patent immunity is introduced.

**Disqualification commitment.** Support D1 and D2; preserve the appellate allocation under D3. His Liteky concurrence join does not protect genuinely outside dealings with the press. It supplies no prior holding that the remedy for an established violation is automatic: that remedy was expressly left open. His present remedial conclusion applies controlling Liljeberg rather than attributing an unexpressed remedial vote to his Liteky join.

**Adversarial remedy test.** The rival path correctly emphasizes pre-findings interviews and the deference inherent in ordinary review. Thomas nevertheless accepts the lower court's combined assessment of litigation burdens, the preserved and rejected liability bases, the comparatively concentrated remedial misconduct and public confidence restored by reassignment and new remedial proceedings. Absence of demonstrated actual bias is a contextual consideration, not a threshold barring relief. He will not join a rule that every statutory appearance defect invalidates an otherwise retainable factual record; he also will not join altered Rule 52(a) review or a statement that the court of appeals made new facts. No broader remedial position is imputed to him from the sources.

### Ginsburg

**Tying commitment.** Affirm the bounded rule-of-reason remand, with inquiry that can be abbreviated where the actual competitive mechanism permits it. Her California Dental dissent join requires attention to actual enforcement and refuses to let a merely conceivable benefit defeat supported effects. Her CSU majority join preserves testing of asserted exclusion and justification without a universal econometric prerequisite. These positions support a real evidentiary inquiry, not an insulation of product design.

**Competing paths and decisive distinction.** The conventional category is plausible for forced purchase and nonremoval unrelated to functionality. Its strongest claim is that separate browser demand and coercion are familiar, not technically novel. However, the breadth and operation of this platform combination supply a distinct competitive question inadequately resolved by the existing per se judgment. Ginsburg chooses an inquiry that can reject an unsupported justification promptly and consider direct effects without demanding every possible market study. Her California Dental vote found a different administrative analysis sufficient; it does not establish Microsoft's browser-market balance.

**Remedy and join barrier.** She will not join presumed efficiency, a categorical software exemption, or a compulsory new proof apparatus that current law does not require. She joins the remand's restriction-specific assessment, price-conflict reservation, separate browser-market requirement and justification for additional relief. Her CSU join creates no compulsory licensing or new monopoly-maintenance holding here.

**Disqualification commitment.** Support D1 and D2; choose further tailored inquiry under D3. Her Liteky concurrence join concerned the treatment of judicially formed attitudes; that writing expressly recognized outside circumstances as independently material. Secret communications on pending merits remove this case from ordinary public trial administration. The concurrence did not decide retrospective relief after a violation, so her present remedy is an application of Liljeberg, not an invented prior join in it.

**Adversarial remedy test.** Innocent plaintiffs and preservation of a large usable record weigh heavily against indiscriminate retrial. The strongest retention argument is that the court of appeals already considered those interests and reviewed the findings carefully. But clear-error affirmance still leaves the disputed assessment with the judge whose appearance was compromised; it is not proof that each material finding is independently retainable. The timing and subject matter of the contacts warrant returning the denial of further liability relief for a tailored new-judge assessment. That inquiry may preserve undisputed or independently supportable matters, and may reuse an appropriate existing record. Her join requires neither actual-bias proof nor a universal presumption of taint.

### Breyer

**Tying commitment.** Affirm rule-of-reason treatment of the presented combination. His California Dental separate opinion, joined by Stevens, Kennedy and Ginsburg, endorses a flexible method and tests the actual justification against practical competitive effects. His Khan and NYNEX joins support refusing to extend a categorical label beyond its premise. The potentially distinct value of a common software platform warrants examination, though distribution of useful interfaces does not itself justify every restriction.

**Competing paths and decisive distinction.** Retaining the conventional category for separable contractual coercion could avoid unnecessary cost and prevent an invocation of innovation from obscuring exclusion. Its weakness here is that deciding which restrictions do useful integrating work itself requires a competitive examination. Breyer therefore chooses rule-of-reason treatment with depth fitted to the issue. The strongest objection is that this rewards Microsoft with a second, unnecessarily elaborate inquiry after adverse conduct findings. His answer is not a compulsory new industry study: genuine direct proof may suffice, and unsupported benefits do not require endless refutation. The original per se judgment nevertheless did not resolve the required browser-market comparison for each practice.

**Remedy and join barrier.** He joins the actual-effect and benefit assessment, the distinct price-increment inquiry and the limits on added relief. He rejects any premise that antitrust is incapable of examining software design, that every plausible efficiency defeats liability, or that the attempted-monopolization claim is revived. No lower-court market conflict is resolved by economic supposition.

**Disqualification commitment.** Support D1 and D2; preserve the appellate allocation under D3. In re Allied-Signal Inc., 891 F.2d 967, 970–973 (1st Cir. 1989), is his own pre-divergence application of an objective informed-observer standard and, independently, Liljeberg's remedial interests outside Rule 60(b). It gives concrete weight to disruption, the nature of the apparent conflict and fairness to affected parties. He was not seated for Liteky or Kodak and receives no attributed join in either.

**Adversarial remedy test.** Allied-Signal involved relatively remote clerk-family relationships, broad opportunities to know of them and potential disruption of interrelated settlements. Those facts are absent here; it cannot excuse the secret interviews or establish an objection waiver. The direct, concealed misconduct and pre-findings timing make a further inquiry a serious alternative. Nevertheless, the court of appeals has supplied a differentiated liability judgment, considered the three remedial interests and required replacement of the whole remedy before another judge. Breyer accepts that combined allocation as sufficient on this record, giving substantial weight to the unnecessary repetition imposed on innocent parties and declining to presume all completed factfinding unreliable. He does not claim settled reliance or settlement risks in Microsoft that the neutral packet does not supply. Ordinary review is corroborating procedural context, not a second trial; lack of actual bias is not a condition for relief. His position leaves broader relief available when the nature of the preserved findings and appearance problem require it.

## Reconciliation and assembly constraints

The provisional common tying rule has seven direct supporters: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. They support rule-of-reason treatment of the combination presented, actual examination of each challenged restriction and its explanation, and retention of established categories beyond this holding. Their differing prior opinions do not prevent a join at that stated level of generality. None supports a general efficiency defense to all per se rules. Stevens's agreement on further inquiry into genuine functional integration does not make him an eighth supporter of the all-restrictions direction.

All eight support the appearance holding and prospective reassignment, and the removal of the existing remedial decree. The former Liteky concurrence's additional judicial-source formulation need not be reproduced as controlling law: these interviews supply materially outside circumstances under that formulation too. A proposed opinion that simply applies the governing objective rule to those circumstances presents no source-based join barrier.

The D3 division concerns the sufficiency of the lower court's particular allocation, not whether Liljeberg governs, whether actual bias must be proved, whether clear-error review is fresh factfinding or whether universal vacatur is mandatory. Those four propositions must not be used to manufacture agreement or disagreement. The four supporting further inquiry have not found every retained factual proposition actually tainted. The four supporting retention have not found every appellate-correctable violation harmless. Each group can join the common rule while disagreeing over its application and remedy.

Assembly must test any proposed final text against these actual reservations. No agreement is supplied to a new substantive antitrust question, an automatic liability retrial, a categorical validation of all liability, an invented efficiency or press-account finding, or a new opinion-assignment choice. If a proposed formulation removes a recorded objection, its exact scope must be tested rather than presumed from agreement on judgment.
