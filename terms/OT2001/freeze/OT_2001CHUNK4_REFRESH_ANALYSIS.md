## 50. Barnhart v. Walton — duration, entitlement and trial work

### Record and reviewed judgment

The complete December 18, 2000 Fourth Circuit opinion, 235 F.3d 184, supports a more precise sequence than the intake's compressed account. Walton applied for disability insurance benefits and supplemental security income on April 12, 1995, alleging a schizophrenic disorder with depression. The amended onset date was October 31, 1994. He began part-time work in May 1995 and full-time work on December 10, 1995. After the first hearing in July 1996, the ALJ initially awarded a closed period ending with full-time work. On administrative remand, the ALJ found substantial gainful activity in October 1995 and denied benefits because inability to perform that activity did not persist for twelve months. The circuit affirmed the substantial-gainful-activity finding, but reversed the duration and trial-work rulings and remanded.

The Commissioner did not dispute that the impairment itself lasted at least twelve months. The circuit reasoned that the duration clause modifies impairment alone; entitlement could begin after the five-month waiting period without a prior award; trial-work protection then prevented use of the October 1995 activity to show cessation. Independently, it regarded the agency construction as unreasonable even if the text were ambiguous. Both grounds require attention. The circuit's conclusion about entitlement is its disputed statutory holding, not a fact that neutral preflight may assume. The October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding is the affirmed factual component; the present questions do not authorize recalculating it from later earnings thresholds.

### Complete statutory and regulatory setting

The exact source excerpts supply §423(a)(1), the waiting-period provision in §423(c)(2), the disability definition in §423(d)(1), and §422(c)'s trial-work provisions. Entitlement, payment after the waiting period, medical impairment, functional inability, duration, adjudication and trial-work protection remain separate concepts. Section 422(c)(2) addresses whether disability has ceased during a trial-work period; it does not expressly state that every initial eligibility condition is satisfied by applying for benefits. Conversely, §422(c)(3) ties the beginning of trial work to entitlement, so entitlement cannot simply be replaced with the date the agency mails a favorable award without construing the statute.

The SSI adult definition in §1382c(a)(3)(A) is separately supplied with its child-definition exception and neighboring severity provisions. Walton is an adult; the child exception is not his eligibility rule. Trial work under §422(c) is a Title II provision. The shared duration question does not silently make the Title II waiting period or trial-work regime an SSI entitlement rule.

Section 405(a) expressly authorizes necessary or appropriate rules and procedures consistent with Title II and reasonable rules concerning proof of entitlement. Section 1383(d)(1) applies that subsection to the SSI part. These grants address the actual authority question missing from an expertise-only account; they do not authorize inconsistency with the benefit provisions. The current CFR copies include §§404.1505, 404.1509 and 404.1592. The latter excludes trial work when substantial gainful activity is demonstrated within twelve months of onset and before a favorable notice or decision, as well as work during a required waiting period; its other eligibility and termination conditions remain distinct.

Timing matters. The lower opinion identifies SSR 82-52 and the March 6, 1995 proposed rule as the agency interpretation supporting the challenged adjudication. It states that the proposed rule became effective August 10, 2000 and was not retroactive to Walton's case. The 2001 CFR is therefore evidence of the later formalized rule, not an instruction to backdate it. Section 402.35(b)(1) establishes that Social Security Rulings bind the agency's components and represent adopted precedent opinions, orders, policy statements and interpretations. That internal binding effect is not automatically the delegated force of law required by Mead. The model must identify what statutory or regulatory construction the adjudication actually adopted and the legal status of that instrument.

### Entering law and live alternatives

The exact Mead, Christensen, Auer, Good Samaritan and Smiley entries and relevant current public decisions are copied in the reading slice. Mead requires both delegated force-of-law authority and an interpretation exercising it; neither informal form nor expertise alone answers that inquiry. Christensen preserves persuasive consideration when controlling weight is unavailable. Smiley permits adequately explained interpretive change and, under its stated no-clear-contrary-guidance condition, antecedent interpretive use without backdating a regulation. Auer governs a fair interpretation of an actual agency regulation within its text; it cannot turn an optional or contrary regulatory term into a new statutory requirement. Blumer supplies current independent statutory construction with persuasive agency support; it does not make all administering-agency interpretations controlling. Edelman and Ragsdale preserve the boundary between implementing statutory requirements and adding consequences the statute does not permit.

One supported route reads the duration requirement as encompassing disabling inability caused by the impairment and permits the agency to use actual predecision substantial work to resolve a prediction of twelve-month inability. The contrary objection is grammatical and structural: the duration clause refers to impairment, and the entitlement/trial-work provisions contain no prior-award requirement. A second route adopts the circuit's impairment-only construction and accrued entitlement, requiring an explanation of why trial-work protection attaches before adjudication and how the initial disability condition operates. Its contrary objection is that a short inability accompanying a lasting medical condition would create entitlement even after actual recovery of work capacity and would use cessation protection to establish initial qualification. A third distinct route may accept a functional-duration requirement yet hold that a reasonable expected duration already established entitlement before the actual return; that route must confront the statutory present/past language and whether a prediction remains dispositive when actual events are known before adjudication.

For each route, an independent persuasive construction remains possible if Mead's threshold is not met. Denial of controlling deference alone neither awards benefits nor sustains the circuit's construction. A current formal regulation does not alone decide its antecedent use. Reversal must answer the circuit's independent statutory and reasonableness grounds, and any divided resolution must identify the benefit component and remand consequence. The supplied sequence supports deciding the legal question without finding medical recovery, creating an unsuccessful-work-attempt exception or deciding the rights of a claimant already awarded benefits before returning to work.

## 58. Michigan v. Environmental Protection Agency — interstate NOx budgets

### Record, scope and lower reasoning

The complete March 3, 2000 D.C. Circuit opinion, 213 F.3d 663, including Judge Sentelle's dissent and footnotes, establishes that the judgment under review addresses the one-hour ozone standard. The eight-hour findings had been stayed by EPA and the circuit stayed that part of review. The current Whitman decision does not automatically reactivate the stayed portion or enlarge these three supplied questions.

EPA first identified contributing States through the magnitude, frequency and relative amount of modeled interstate effects. It then required elimination of the amount removable by highly cost-effective controls, using a $2,000-per-ton benchmark; the remaining budget was projected from emissions and required controls. The inquiry concerns the legal permissibility of that role for cost. No party in the lower judgment claimed that benefits must exceed costs, that EPA was authorized to use that balance here, or that the word significant requires cost consideration. A permissive-cost conclusion would not establish any of those propositions or independently validate every numerical assumption.

The majority relied on text and the circuit's presumption favoring consideration of cost absent clear exclusion. Sentelle rejected that presumption in this setting: the enacted phrase concerns amounts of pollution contributing to another State's nonattainment, while control expense measures something different. He would invalidate the rule on statutory grounds and did not reach the subsidiary issues. This is a material alternative legal ground, not a disagreement with EPA's mathematical models.

The majority's state-choice ruling rests on Train v. NRDC, 421 U.S. 60 (1975), Union Electric and Virginia v. EPA. EPA's rule set the required result, while States retained actual choices among stationary and mobile sources and other control measures. They could opt into EPA's model trading program, use their own trading program, or use none; the model's source requirements govern participation in that option. The rule did not let a State substitute VOC controls for its NOx budget, because EPA and OTAG concluded VOC controls would not adequately address the long-distance transport problem. States could add VOC controls for associated local disbenefits. The budget's pollutant specificity and the retained means choices must both be considered; statutory discretion is not proved solely by saying that States may spend more.

The lower nondelegation account materially differs from controlling Whitman. It acknowledged that EPA did not explain a statutory stopping point for the particular dollar figure and relied in part on the provision's relatively confined practical exercise to date. Whitman forbids curing absent congressional guidance by agency self-limitation. A present judgment sustaining the delegation therefore requires sufficient limits in Congress's statute, independently of this agency's modesty, geographical reach or chosen price. The source's description of the exact price's rationale does not itself decide a different, unreviewed arbitrary-and-capricious challenge to that figure.

The circuit vacated the rule as to Wisconsin, Missouri and Georgia and remanded; it separately remanded inadequate notice concerning the electric-generating-unit definition and the control level for large stationary internal-combustion engines. The supplied review does not reopen those separate corrections. The circuit denied the remaining petitions. A disposition on the three categorical questions must preserve that component structure rather than announce wholesale affirmance of an untouched rule.

### Governing text and law

The exact §110(a)(2)(D) excerpt includes the command of adequate provisions, consistency with Title I, significant interstate nonattainment contribution, maintenance interference, and the separate deterioration/visibility and §§115/126 requirements. The latter branches do not become additional holdings merely because the complete text is supplied. Section 110(a)(2)(A) expressly identifies enforceable limitations, means and techniques including specified economic incentives. Section 110(k)(3) commands approval of a submission satisfying all applicable requirements. Section 110(k)(5) requires revision after a finding of substantial inadequacy and specifies public findings and notice, the maximum submission deadline and the distinct date-adjustment qualification. Section 110(c)(1) provides federal implementation after the specified submission or disapproval triggers, unless the State corrects the deficiency and obtains approval before promulgation. Section 301(a)(1) gives EPA general rulemaking authority to carry out its statutory functions. None is a roving authority to add a different environmental objective.

Whitman keeps national-standard cost setting distinct from implementation; it does not itself approve this use of cost. Mead requires an actual delegated rulemaking assignment, while MCI protects mandatory statutory functions. Arkansas v. Oklahoma preserves authorized interstate administration and supported technical findings at its own scope. Train supplies the directly relevant pre-divergence allocation of initial source-by-source control choices; the State-choice question cannot be reduced to the constitutional floor alone. New York v. United States distinguishes federal regulation and a lawful alternative to state administration from a compulsory command to regulate. The later New York v. FERC public law applies provision-specific allocation and preserved state functions, without a general presumption either for or against federal power.

### Live paths and consequences

Sustaining the challenged cost method requires reading the whole interstate provision to permit control expense in determining the required reduction after a demonstrated interstate pollution connection. It must answer Sentelle's physical-contribution objection and may not rest solely on absence of a prohibition. Rejecting cost at that stage requires explaining how the enacted pollution criterion excludes it and the resulting agency reconsideration; it need not require eradication of all interstate emissions or constitutional adjudication. A construction allowing costs at a different statutory stage must identify that stage and its authority, rather than keep the same budget merely by relabeling it.

On state choice, sustaining the budget requires applying Train to the actual options and scientific NOx/VOC distinction. A defect requires identifying what choice the statute reserves and how the program removes it, with relief fitted to that defect. Mere existence of a federal plan alternative does not answer the separate statutory means-allocation question. If constitutional nondelegation is reached, the alternatives concern sufficient enacted guidance versus an impermissibly unbounded assignment; neither historical agency restraint nor a court-created dollar cap supplies Congress's missing choice. Every route must distinguish the broad three questions from the unreviewed geographic and notice rulings.

## 61. Thompson v. Western States Medical Center — compounding and truthful promotion

### Complete provision and record

Section 503A, enacted in FDAMA §127 on November 21, 1997 and effective after one year, exempts qualifying compounded drugs from §501(a)(2)(B)'s manufacturing-practice requirement, §502(f)(1)'s adequate-directions requirement and §505's new-drug approval requirement. It is not an exemption from every federal or state drug rule. The complete enacted text is copied in the source appendix.

The intake's shorthand about unsolicited prescriptions must be read with §503A(a)'s actual alternative: the statute also specifies a notation on the prescription order, approved by the prescribing practitioner, that a compounded product is necessary for the identified patient. It separately permits limited advance compounding based on the prescribed history within the specified established relationships. Section 503A(c) restricts promotion of a particular drug, class of drug, or type of drug, while permitting promotion of the compounding service. All those categories matter; the provision does not merely regulate mass-media advertising or only named brand products.

The remaining enacted controls govern permitted substances and their sources and certificates, monographs, withdrawn unsafe or ineffective products, and regular or inordinate compounding of essentially commercial copies. A patient-specific change producing a significant difference, as determined by the prescribing practitioner, is excluded from that copy category. Another condition addresses drugs presenting demonstrable compounding difficulties. The interstate condition is either a State memorandum of understanding addressing inordinate interstate quantities and complaint investigation, or, without that State agreement, out-of-state distribution not exceeding five percent of total prescription orders dispensed or distributed by the pharmacy or physician. The statute does not prescribe a twenty-percent MOU ceiling; that figure in the lower opinion must not become statutory text. The agreement is with a State, not an individual pharmacy. The five-percent denominator is total prescription orders, not only interstate sales or compounded products. PET compounded drugs and radiopharmaceuticals are excluded from §503A, and manufacturer-directed mixing under approved labeling lies outside its compounding definition.

The complete February 6, 2001 Ninth Circuit opinion supports the summary-judgment posture, prepared promotional materials mailed or distributed at medical conferences, and the Government's noncontention there that the speech was unlawful or misleading. The circuit accepted health and approval-integrity interests as substantial but rejected the separately described interest in balancing availability against widespread compounding on the offered showing. It found advancement and fit insufficient and reversed the district court's severability ruling, leaving §353a invalid in its entirety. The supplied review excludes that separate severability issue. Neither the appellate court's assessment nor its paraphrase of the statute is an independent factual finding that compounding at any scale is safe.

### Governing law and supported alternatives

The controlling Central Hudson/Fox framework remains real harm, material advancement and reasonable fit for truthful, nonmisleading promotion of lawful activity. The exact Edenfield, Rubin, Greater New Orleans, 44 Liquormart and Lorillard entries and published separate positions are copied. No controlling rule requires the least restrictive imaginable alternative, a fixed empirical study, or universal strict scrutiny of commercial speech. A condition selecting commercial speech for loss of a benefit still requires the applicable speech analysis; Lanphere's existing access-condition rule supports that distinction at its actual scope. Playboy's strict scrutiny and Turner's content-neutral prediction review remain different settings.

A supported affirmance can assume the asserted health, approval-integrity and individualized-access interests are substantial, yet find insufficient fit between a complete drug/class/type promotion restriction and the manufacturing boundary, given useful clinician/patient information and concrete conduct controls. It must explain why the Government's proposed speech proxy fails even allowing reasonable prediction and why the identified alternatives address the same legitimate aim. A different affirmance could find a specific failure of material advancement on this record; it must identify that failure separately rather than treat an incomplete empirical record as automatically fatal.

A supported reversal can recognize the combined objective of preserving individualized access while keeping commercial manufacturing subject to approval, and treat solicitation and product promotion as incentives and indicia of production scale. That path must confront professional audiences, individualized prescriptions, the allowed general advertising, existing statutory safeguards and the burden on useful truthful speech. Merely calling the exemption voluntary, or assuming advertising necessarily proves industrial production, does not answer those objections. The Government's interest in preserving a safety-review regime is analytically different from suppressing information because patients might make disfavored lawful choices; each Justice must state which asserted objective the supported rule addresses.

Possible direct quantity, distribution, prescription and commercial-copy controls are fit comparisons, not new judicial requirements. Existing controls must be distinguished from additional restrictions Congress or an authorized agency might lawfully adopt. Neither a disclaimer nor full approval automatically preserves individualized access; their adequacy must be assessed against the actual combined interest. No decision here may simply announce a speech-free §503A exemption after affirmance, because the unreviewed nonseverability judgment has its own consequence. Reversal of the constitutional predicate likewise requires an explicit conforming disposition, without independently adjudicating severability or certifying any particular drug's safety.

## 62. US Airways, Inc. v. Barnett — reassignment and the employer's seniority system

### Record, lower grounds and scope

The complete October 4, 2000 en banc Ninth Circuit opinion, including Gould's concurrence and both dissents, supports separate components. The court reversed summary judgment on the accommodation claims and remanded, but affirmed summary judgment on retaliation. It discussed both the requested mailroom retention and proposed cargo accommodations, including lifting equipment and changed duties. It also adopted an interactive-process ground. The granted seniority question does not itself adjudicate every cargo proposal, retaliation, or the entire interactive-process doctrine. A judgment resolving the seniority ground must state its effect on the reviewed judgment without silently disposing of independent unreviewed claims.

Barnett had transferred to the mailroom using seniority after his injury. Two more-senior employees planned bids. The employer allowed him to remain for five months while considering the accommodation request, then put him on job-injury leave. The lower majority distinguished retaining him in the position from ejecting a person already occupying it. His injury preceded the ADA's effective date, but the requested accommodation and denial occurred after July 26, 1992. The claim is not a retroactive challenge to the 1990 injury.

The original intake supplies the handbook's employer-created, noncontractual character and reserved amendment power. The lower opinion confirms the absence of a collective-bargaining agreement. Trott's dissent describes a longstanding system applying to approximately fourteen thousand customer-service agents and the reliance objection; the majority reports the employer's domino-effect assertion and rejects its sufficiency on that summary-judgment record. These are competing legal and evidentiary assessments. The majority's footnote notes a catastrophic-illness exception, but supplies neither the complete policy terms nor its frequency of use. No inference that the entire system is illusory, freely disregarded in practice, or routinely modified follows from the reservation or that footnote alone.

The majority treated seniority as a factor in individualized undue hardship and found a triable dispute. Gould, joined by circuit Judge Thomas, expressly equated reasonableness with an accommodation's effectiveness for the employee and placed employer difficulty entirely in hardship. That separate explanation must not be mistaken for an already controlling Supreme Court rule. Trott's dissent rejected preference over neutral seniority; O'Scannlain's dissent instead challenged disability coverage and the majority's waiver ruling. The majority said the employer had not properly raised coverage on appeal. This is a disputed appellate-preservation account, not permission for this Court to find Barnett nondisabled or to revive a ground outside the supplied review. The authenticated argument limitation permits further adjudication, not summary judgment awarding Barnett the position.

### Statutory distinctions and entering law

Section 12111(8) concerns essential functions of the position held or desired. Section 12111(9) identifies reassignment to a vacant position, adjustments to policies and other possible accommodations; it says may include and does not make every requested transfer reasonable. Section 12112(b)(5)(A) addresses known limitations of an otherwise qualified applicant or employee, with an employer showing of undue hardship. Section 12111(10) defines that hardship as significant difficulty or expense and lists nature/cost, facility resources and staffing and operational impact, overall entity resources/size/facilities, and workforce and organizational relationships. The exact text preserves those factors and separates the defense from the employee's reasonable-accommodation showing.

The relevant original ADA provisions predate divergence and became effective before the claimed denial. The 1991 Code amendments concern foreign employment and do not supply this domestic seniority rule. Later ADA amendments are unavailable. Current Toyota, Sutton and Murphy distinguish actual-disability coverage from qualification and accommodation; they do not establish a presumption about seniority or decide the contested preservation issue. Waffle House concerns the Commission's independent authority and remedies, not an individual entitlement to this position. Olmstead's public-service adjustment and resource inquiry does not transplant its Title II program into workplace reassignment. The separate Title VII accommodation law preserves Hardison's own statutory and seniority setting and lower hardship threshold. Neither a direct transplant of that threshold nor disregard of actual seniority reliance follows.

### Live alternatives and remedial consequences

One route requires an ordinary individualized determination of reasonableness and, where it is shown, demonstrated statutory hardship, treating seniority and genuine reliance within those inquiries. It must explain the independent work of reasonable, answer why seniority does not ordinarily settle that showing, and avoid treating effectiveness as conclusive. Another route recognizes that established seniority expectations ordinarily make a conflicting reassignment unreasonable but permits a claimant to demonstrate case-specific circumstances undermining that reason. It must ground that generalization in statutory reasonableness, distinguish it from the employer's hardship burden, identify what evidence can answer it and explain why this employer's reserved authority does or does not suffice. No list of mandatory special facts is supplied in advance by preflight.

A categorical route treats a bona fide seniority conflict as beyond reasonable reassignment; it must confront statutory reassignment and policy adjustment, the absence of a parallel express ADA seniority exemption, the employer-created policy and the lack of an incumbent displaced from this job. The opposite categorical claim that a neutral policy can never defeat an effective request must confront reasonable as a statutory limitation and real expectations of other workers. Equal application of a rule alone does not resolve an accommodation duty; the possibility of preferential adjustment alone does not prove every requested preference reasonable.

An individualized affirmance supports further proceedings under the correct rule, not liability or a guaranteed mailroom appointment. A clarified presumption could require vacatur and remand to allow the proper showing. A categorical reversal still needs to specify the disposition of the reviewed seniority component and cannot silently enter judgment on unreviewed alternative accommodations. No current source establishes recurring policy violations or a numerical operational burden. If a proposed result depends on precise handbook exception terms or actual amendment practice, obtain those Joint Appendix passages; do not turn the brief record limit into a failure of proof by a litigant.
