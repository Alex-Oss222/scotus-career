### Issue 1: Fabrication, liberty and the official’s function

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Buckley excludes ordinary investigative fabrication from absolute prosecutorial immunity and preserves genuine advocacy. Albright’s judgment supplies no majority rule disposing of all fabrication claims. Fletcher v. Kalina separates evidentiary misconduct, constitutional injury, causation and defenses.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the particular deprivation, the officer’s own conduct and the causal chain. Later judicial action is not categorically independent when procured through material deception.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment due process; the personal federal-officer damages action litigated under Bivens; ordinary causation requirements.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Investigation is not immunized by title. The absence of absolute immunity alone does not establish either a constitutional wrong or damages.
* **Open Point:** Whether the pleaded fabrication-caused detention is actionable and sufficiently clearly unlawful at the time.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Coffey separates fabrication from its later protected courtroom use and argues that neither the initial act alone nor an intervening prosecutor’s presentation creates liability. Zahrey alleges that the fabricated material was created precisely to procure his confinement, making the detention its foreseeable consequence. The causal question cannot be answered simply by changing the official’s hat between investigation and advocacy.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirming the appellate judgment permits adjudication of the supported personal claim, without a damages award. Reversing on qualified immunity requires identifying what material legal boundary was unclear in 1996. An absolute-immunity ruling on a disputed function not resolved in this appeal would exceed the conceded posture.

## 45. Milstein v. Cooley +

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Milstein v. Cooley
* **Citation or Docket:** 257 F.3d 1004 (9th Cir. 2001); lower-court No. 99-56682.
* **October Term:** 2001
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS; simulation-added review of the identified lower-court judgment.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-03-19 (proposed simulation placement, not a historical Supreme Court event).
* **Question Presented:** Whether absolute prosecutorial immunity bars the pleaded acts of investigative fabrication, procuring false evidence, making a crime report, and using the resulting false accusations in advocacy and public statements, including the separate ordinary advocacy acts alleged in the complaint.
* **Question Granted:** For this proposed simulated review, the stated question defines the scope. No historical grant or verbatim certiorari-petition wording is represented.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** Ninth Circuit; affirmed dismissal in part and reversed in part under a functional immunity analysis.
* **Relief Requested:** Milstein seeks adjudication of his §1983 claims; the prosecutors seek immunity from personal damages for their challenged conduct.
* **Material Record:** A defense lawyer alleges that prosecutors procured a witness’s false accusation, acted as complaining witnesses, pursued charges and made public statements. His complaint was dismissed on absolute-immunity grounds. The allegations remain unproved.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Review the individual acts alleged, not a single label for the prosecution. The Ninth Circuit expressly reserved qualified immunity on the remanded claims; this review addresses absolute immunity, not that unadjudicated defense. Denying absolute immunity does not establish constitutional injury, causation, municipal attribution or damages.
* **Relevant Dates:** Ninth Circuit decision: July 20, 2001.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Zahrey concerns qualified immunity and fabrication-caused detention on an assumed investigative function.
* **Participation Matter:** No case-specific nonparticipation established.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** Completed decisions through June 28, 2001; no later simulated merits decision is assumed.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** No completed OT2001 decision supplied for use as precedent.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** None indispensable on the identified opening law. A prepared position in another case does not establish a holding.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Review dismissal independently under the pleading standards then applicable; the official seeking absolute immunity must establish that the function falls within it.

### Issue 1: Function and knowing falsification

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Buckley excludes investigative fabrication and press statements from absolute prosecutorial immunity. Fletcher v. Kalina excludes personal factual certification but preserves genuine advocacy even when dishonesty is alleged; the Court did not adopt Stone’s broader dishonesty exception. Crawford-El v. Britton requires ordinary proof and permits lawful case management without a categorical heightened-proof barrier to necessary discovery; it retained Harlow and Anderson’s separate qualified-immunity defense.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Classify each act by function. Advocacy, investigation, testimony and personal certification are not interchangeable; an improper motive alone does not change a protected function.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983; due process; the substantive elements of the particular constitutional deprivation.
* **Material Other Authority:** Imbler v. Pachtman; Burns v. Reed; Forrester v. White; Briscoe v. LaHue where actual witness testimony is challenged.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The ordinary functional rules already reach some alleged acts. A dishonesty exception for otherwise protected advocacy would change existing law.
* **Open Point:** Whether that defined exception should be recognized for knowing material fabrication causing an independently actionable deprivation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Milstein seeks adjudication of alleged investigative fabrication and deliberate false accusations. The prosecutors defend immunity from the litigation itself: an intent exception can force honest advocates into discovery whenever a disappointed defendant alleges knowing falsehood, and criminal or professional sanctions can address misconduct without that exposure. Existing absolute immunity deliberately tolerates some meritorious claims going uncompensated to protect independent advocacy. A narrower exception must answer that tradeoff, not merely observe that falsification is unlawful.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Applying existing law would separate unprotected investigation and publicity from protected advocacy. A defined falsification exception could also permit otherwise protected acts to be examined on proof of knowing material falsity and constitutional causation. Neither approach makes all malicious prosecution actionable or removes defenses from honest advocacy.

## 46. Department of Housing and Urban Development v. Rucker / Oakland Housing Authority v. Rucker

* **Citation or Docket:** Nos. 00-1770 and 00-1781; 535 U.S. 125.

### Issue 1: Lease authority and knowledge

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The prior decisions do not contain a decision construing this public-housing clause. MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co. requires an agency to remain within its statutory authority; Christensen v. Harris County does not permit agency interpretation to displace enacted language.

* **Material Other Authority:** Goldberg v. Kelly and Mathews v. Eldridge concern procedures for protected interests, not a categorical substantive entitlement to retain a tenancy after every permissible condition is breached. Constitutional avoidance requires a serious constitutional question and a genuinely available construction.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law governs statutory interpretation and procedural protection. It does not decide that a public tenancy can never be subject to conduct-based conditions beyond the tenant’s personal fault, nor that authorization eliminates all individualized review.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statute includes an unstated tenant-knowledge condition and, if not, whether its permissive termination rule is facially incompatible with due process.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit read the statute to protect innocent tenants. The tenants argue that control, the housing program’s purpose, and constitutional concerns favor a knowledge condition. HUD and Oakland argue that the clause separately identifies covered persons and makes their activity a permissible ground for termination without that qualification. Their interpretation must acknowledge local discretion and lawful process; the tenants’ argument cannot treat every civil lease consequence as punishment for another person’s crime. An individualized objection to an actual eviction differs from the categorical statutory theory supporting the injunction.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Reversal could reject the implied knowledge requirement and remand the injunction while preserving factual and procedural defenses in each eviction. Affirmance could adopt a knowledge-based construction or sustain a properly presented constitutional limitation. A statutory ruling would not itself establish the alleged drug activity, terminate the leases, or order removal. The supported holding concerns listed relationships and qualifying activity, not misconduct by anyone with a remote connection to the tenant or an unrestricted power to evict arbitrarily.

## 47. S.S. ex rel. Jervis v. McMullen +

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** S.S. ex rel. Jervis v. McMullen +
* **Citation or Docket:** 225 F.3d 960, Eighth Circuit en banc. No Supreme Court docket or grant date is supplied.

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. A proposed brief does not establish an effective same-term decision.

Sorema N.A. ruling could bear on the proper pleading method, but cannot supply findings or an adopted danger rule. The future Hope v. Pelzer cannot establish contemporaneous notice.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** 42 U.S.C. §1983 and pleading-stage review. Constitutional deprivation, personal causation, culpability, available defenses, and compensable injury are separate elements. A State’s family-reunification responsibilities do not themselves decide the federal question.

### Issue 1: Affirmative custodial transfer and danger

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence recognizes a bounded claim where an officer actually restrains a person and knowingly directs an involuntary transfer into appreciated serious danger, with authority and opportunity for a reasonable response. It expressly leaves general noncustodial danger liability open. Pinder v. Johnson distinguishes an affirmative deprivation from an unfulfilled protection promise; Uhlrig v. Harder resolves conduct-date immunity without adopting a general danger test.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The registers require the defendant’s own restraint or affirmative causal conduct and preserve the difference between providing no public protection and causing a constitutional deprivation. Custody of an assailant or protective assurances alone are insufficient.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fourteenth Amendment constrains state deprivations of liberty. DeShaney v. Winnebago County Department of Social Services rejects a general protective duty and discusses return from temporary custody to an existing private danger.
* **Material Other Authority:** Youngberg v. Romeo concerns protection arising from actual civil custody. Its institutional professional-judgment context is relevant but does not mechanically decide the culpability of every later placement decision.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The direct-restraint rule already protects against the specified coercive transfer. The supplied law has not adopted a general rule for returning a child from protective custody to a parent who permits a specific private danger.
* **Open Point:** Whether the relevant comparison for this affirmative release is the protected custodial position immediately before it or an imagined situation in which the State never intervened, and how DeShaney’s return language limits that inquiry.

### Issue 2: Culpability after an opportunity to deliberate

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** County of Sacramento v. Lewis requires an unjustified purpose to harm in a genuinely rapid pursuit, while preserving the significance of actual opportunity to deliberate. City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence permits its bounded knowing-restraint claim without importing that rapid-pursuit requirement. Farmer v. Brennan informs actual knowledge and reasonable response, not an automatic transplant of prison doctrine.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Negligence does not become a constitutional deprivation because the eventual private harm is severe. Actual appreciation, genuine opportunity, responsibility, and unreasonable disregard must be tied to each official.
* **Other Governing Law:** Substantive due process prohibits the qualifying abuse of state power; §1983 does not federalize ordinary malpractice or every erroneous placement judgment.
* **Material Other Authority:** Daniels v. Williams excludes negligence. The contextual reasoning of Lewis requires examination of the official act, available choices, and competing obligations.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The supplied rules reject a universal malice requirement and a universal deliberate-indifference test. The actual circumstances determine whether deliberate disregard can constitute the relevant abuse.
* **Open Point:** Whether the pleaded specific knowledge and considered return, rather than an emergency judgment or negligent oversight, satisfy the constitutional culpability threshold.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The en banc court reasoned that return to the original home did not worsen the child’s position compared with no state intervention, and alternatively that the allegations did not reach the required culpability. S.S. argues that knowingly withdrawing actual custodial protection and choosing the dangerous placement is an affirmative act, and that ample deliberation makes the alleged disregard actionable. Officials invoke DeShaney, family reunification, and the need to distinguish constitutional abuse from professional error. S.S. must establish more than predictable private harm; officials cannot equate a legitimate general goal with a finding that the particular alleged choice was reasonable.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could preserve the original-situation comparison or hold that the pleaded culpability is insufficient. Reversal could recognize this custodial-transfer claim and permit proof of knowledge, causation, and unjustified disregard. A narrower vacatur could require the lower court to apply the supplied direct-restraint precedent without deciding every noncustodial danger theory. Any successful pleading path leaves conduct-date immunity and supported defenses for adjudication; neither the offender’s guilt nor the gravity of harm establishes personal damages liability. A broad duty to guarantee safe family placement would exceed the asserted affirmative-deprivation theory.

## 48. Hoffman Plastic Compounds, Inc. v. NLRB

### Issue 1: Reconciling labor remedies and employment authorization

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB rejects an automatic forfeiture of statutory labor relief from misconduct in the hearing and preserves the Board’s reasoned remedial discretion. McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co. separates an unlawful employment decision from subsequently discovered lawful grounds for discharge, ordinarily limiting future relief after a sufficient employer showing. Neither case concerns this precise immigration restriction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The registers preserve statutory boundaries on remedies, reject unsupported all-or-nothing forfeitures, and require attention to the actual relation between misconduct, liability, and relief. Agency choice cannot command conduct prohibited by another enactment.
* **Other Governing Law:** The NLRA authorizes remedial backpay for unfair labor practices. IRCA prohibits specified knowing employment of unauthorized workers and fraudulent work-authorization documentation; the statutory schemes must be reconciled without inventing a repeal.
* **Material Other Authority:** Sure-Tan, Inc. v. NLRB addresses workers who departed the country and their legal availability for reinstatement and backpay. Southern Steamship Co. v. NLRB illustrates the limit against a labor remedy conflicting with another federal prohibition.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Existing law denies the Board authority to compel illegal employment and requires lawful remedial fit. It has not resolved whether this pre-discovery monetary award itself constitutes the forbidden employment or is an independently authorized consequence of discrimination.
* **Open Point:** Whether IRCA categorically displaces backpay for unperformed work even when the employer violated the NLRA and the award terminates when the disqualification becomes known.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The circuit accepted the Board’s accommodation of the two statutes. Hoffman argues that paying for work Castro could not legally perform undermines verification and rewards document fraud. The Board argues that a limited award enforces an independent labor duty without ordering unlawful employment, while eliminating it would reduce the consequences of targeting unauthorized workers for antiunion discharge. The Board must answer the distinction between compensation for unperformed work and earned wages; Hoffman must identify the statutory conflict rather than treat immigration status as a complete defense to labor law.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance could permit the limited pre-discovery award while denying reinstatement and postdiscovery accrual. Reversal could categorically disallow this backpay category while preserving the unfair-labor-practice finding and other lawful relief. A narrower remand could require reconsideration of the calculation or mitigation only if a preserved defect requires it. None of those paths authorizes future unlawful employment or decides all wage protections for unauthorized workers. The holding must identify whether it rests on statutory displacement, remedial discretion, or the employee’s fraud.
