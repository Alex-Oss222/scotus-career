## Refreshed result and sufficient grounds

All eight Associates support reversing the challenged invalidation of Rule 51.315(c)–(f), construed and applied with the agency's actual assistance explanation and the separate limitations below. They can join the same complete combination rationale at this level. Thomas changes from a corrected-standard remand to restoration because the newly supplied original explanation answers his identified objection to approving affirmative duties without the agency's own account of their statutory connection and limits. The other seven retain their judgment positions but now join an expressly supported assistance construction rather than a duty whose entrant-ability limit was previously unverified.

### 1. Necessary assistance to obtain usable statutory access

Section 251(c)(3) permits rules requiring an incumbent to perform necessary functions connecting lawfully obtainable unbundled elements, including connection to requester-held elements, when the requesting carrier is not reasonably able to perform the combining itself. The agency's paragraphs 293–294 explain the affirmative duties in that assistance setting: the entrant may combine when reasonably able; incumbent performance is required when the entrant is unable. Practical absence of access to necessary facilities or network information can establish the need for help. A hypothetical capacity to connect in some other setting is not a substitute for reasonable ability in the actual incumbent network.

The commitments adopt this scope by reading the regulation and its contemporaneous explanation together; they do not claim that (c) or (d) expressly recites an exclusive inability condition. The request language identifies how the duty is invoked, while the functions-necessary language operates within the order's account of when incumbent performance is needed to enable access. Paragraphs 293–294 therefore do more than predict that some entrants will need help: they explain the division of performance the Commission understood its rules to implement. That is a fair limit on compulsory incumbent assistance, rather than deletion of the request trigger. The strongest contrary reading would treat every feasible request as compelling incumbent labor even where the requester is reasonably able; the agency's assistance explanation and the enabling language of §251(c)(3) support rejecting that broader construction here.

This construction applies to the common affirmative-assistance premise of both (c) and (d). It does not turn the regulation's request trigger into an entitlement to compel unnecessary assembly simply because an entrant prefers the incumbent to do work the entrant is reasonably able to perform. Conversely, it supplies no freestanding own-facilities prerequisite or requirement that an entrant duplicate infrastructure before seeking access. The relevant question is ability to accomplish the necessary connection, not ownership as an independent qualification. No numerical cost threshold or general burden of proof concerning entrant ability is added.

Permission for an able entrant to combine remains permission: this construction imposes no independent command that the entrant perform the work. It determines when incumbent assistance can be compelled, while leaving voluntary arrangements subject to lawful terms. No entitlement or denial follows solely from describing an entrant as willing or unwilling; reasonable ability and the necessary functions must be assessed in the actual network setting.

The source now supplies this limiting explanation directly. Paragraph 293 discusses both incumbent-network combinations and combinations with entrant-held elements; paragraph 294 explains when the respective carrier performs the work. Reading the operative duty in that declared setting gives effect to both the regulation and the contemporaneous implementation account. This is not an interpretation fashioned solely by later advocates or a replacement for the words adopted by the Commission.

AT&T Part XI already rejects an unambiguous exclusive assignment of physical combining to entrants. Paragraphs 292–294 establish the further statutory connection that Part XI left for review: access must be usable for the authorized telecommunications service, and the incumbent may hold the facilities and information without which the entrant cannot perform the connection. Compensation alone would not create this duty; the duty rests on the enacted access obligation and its implementation. Section 252 separately supplies lawful costing and terms.

### 2. Separate paragraph conditions and operative rule text

For paragraph (c), the requested combination must be technically feasible **and** must not impair other carriers' ability to obtain unbundled-element access or interconnect with the incumbent network. Both conditions govern even where elements are not ordinarily combined, or not ordinarily combined in the requested manner. Ordinary incumbent practice is evidence relevant to connection, not the outer boundary of all lawful work.

Paragraph (d) independently governs connection to elements the requester possesses and requires technical feasibility. It does not contain (c)(2)'s distinct express other-carrier condition. The statute's nondiscrimination requirements and the source's actual reliability/security treatment continue to apply within their own scope; they are not a textual amendment transferring every condition of (c) into (d).

Paragraph 296 uses a disjunctive word in its explanatory description, while the operative paragraph (c) uses a conjunction and separately enumerates both requirements. The adopted regulation controls the legal duty. Its unambiguous conjunction cannot be displaced by that explanatory sentence. The rest of paragraph 296 identifies reliability and other-carrier access as concerns and permits refusal on either established ground; that surrounding account supports preserving both protections rather than converting either one into an optional condition. No source quotation is silently corrected, and the record does not characterize the wording difference as a verified printing mistake.

The discrepancy does not require a further rulemaking or judicial remand: the governing regulatory text already gives the answer, and the actual assistance, reliability and information explanations remain intelligible without enforcing the disjunctive description as a competing rule. Christensen's distinction between interpreting a regulation and amending its unambiguous permission supports this treatment. The Court would enforce the promulgated conjunction, not rewrite the explanation or give the agency power to dispense with either requirement in (c).

### 3. Feasibility and reliability retain their actual qualifications

Technical feasibility concerns engineering and operational constraints. A connection does not become technically infeasible merely because it is novel, requires a necessary modification, entails recoverable costs, or requires adjustment of accounting or billing arrangements. The source distinguishes those matters from genuine technical or operational obstacles and explains why a network designed without third-party access may need adaptation to fulfill the enacted duty. The separate rural-relief provisions retain their own terms.

Space is not declared irrelevant in every case. A site without any possibility of expansion may make access or interconnection at that point infeasible, technically or otherwise. Where expansion is possible, its cost does not itself make the connection technically infeasible; the requesting party bears the necessary expansion cost within the governing statutory scheme. The distinct physical-collocation and virtual-collocation provisions are not replaced by this combination holding.

Specific, significant and demonstrable threats to network reliability or security remain relevant to feasibility. Each carrier retains responsibility for management, control and performance of its network. Where a refusal rests on alleged reliability/security impacts of the requested interconnection or access at a point, paragraph 203 requires the incumbent to establish specific and significant adverse impacts to the state commission by clear and convincing evidence. Footnote 622 brings the technical-feasibility treatment of reliability and security into the combination discussion. Accordingly, that stated standard follows a combination objection insofar as it is the described reliability/security objection to the proposed access or interconnection. It is not made a universal evidentiary standard for every dispute under (c)–(f).

The last application is a contextual reading of the source's cross-reference, not a claim that footnote 622 repeats the evidentiary words. The footnote directs the reader to the technical-feasibility treatment of the same network concern; applying the identified treatment to that same objection preserves its stated safeguards without extending it to distinct denial grounds.

These requirements are a reasonable implementation of the technical objection in the supported setting. An incumbent can identify concrete engineering risks while a requester ordinarily lacks the pertinent network information; the demand for a substantiated, significant impact prevents an unsupported assertion from defeating the statutory duty. It does not require the incumbent to surrender responsibility for its network or prove that every imaginable catastrophe will occur. The rule preserves an actual feasibility defense rather than making technical possibility an automatic command to provide every element.

### 4. Distinct proof allocations

Paragraph (e) requires the incumbent to prove technical infeasibility to the state commission when denying under (c)(1) or (d). Paragraph (f) separately requires proof that a requested combination would impair other carriers' access or interconnection when denying under (c)(2). Paragraphs 205 and 296 supply the agency's own reason for locating that proof with the incumbent: it has the information necessary to substantiate the asserted network objection. The duty to provide usable access and the incumbent's control of that information support these allocations.

A successful connection using substantially similar facilities is substantial evidence of feasibility, including the supported quality level; interface or protocol similarity can support the comparison. This is evidence, not a conclusive presumption that every site or facility is identical. The reliability/security standard described above retains its own trigger. Neither (e) nor (f) creates a universal clear-and-convincing standard, and no such standard is transferred to a distinct other-carrier-impairment ground solely because that ground concerns the same requested combination. No separate proof burden concerning entrant ability is invented.

### 5. Independent access, identification and compensation limits

The duties apply to elements the requester is lawfully entitled to obtain. AT&T's necessity and impairment holdings remain independent constraints; the original order's broader discussion in paragraphs 206 and 295 cannot revive the vacated element list or turn technical feasibility into the only condition of access. Eligibility as a network element, entitlement to obtain it, and feasibility of a particular connection do different legal work.

Paragraph 297's explanation of different provisioning, distinct element variants and necessary operational/support systems reinforces the connection between usable access and assistance. It does not independently adjudicate the availability of a particular variant or system under current law. A requester must identify the elements it seeks before obtaining them. The source recognizes that lack of information can prevent complete identification before negotiations or arbitration begin or end and requires incumbent cooperation in identifying needed elements. That explanation creates no automatic default, blanket entitlement to an unspecified element, or requirement to complete impossible identification before negotiations can occur.

Just, reasonable and nondiscriminatory rates and terms, together with the applicable §252 cost rules, govern the compensated work. The agency's account that requesting carriers bear relevant connection costs does not convert §252(d)(1)'s permission to include reasonable profit into an independent statutory command. The TELRIC issue remains a separate component; a Justice's agreement with compensated combining does not establish agreement with the challenged pricing methodology.

## Supported competing paths and strongest objections

**Restore the rules as contemporaneously explained.** The agency has supplied a direct statutory relation, a practical assistance setting, network protections, an information-based account of proof allocation and a compensation framework. The operative text resolves the explanatory conjunction difference. The strongest objection is that the broad upon-request wording of (c) and (d) does not itself recite a reasonable-ability limitation, and the older element-selection rationale rested in part on propositions AT&T later rejected. The answer is that the original assistance explanation addresses both duties, the preserved regulatory conditions still govern, and the independent access limits can be enforced without importing the displaced list. The duties do not depend solely on paragraph 295's aggregation example or paragraph 206's overly broad feasibility account.

**Vacate the lower rationale and require additional rule-specific review.** This would accept Part XI, but treat the relation between the upon-request commands and paragraphs 293–294, the explanatory disjunction, or the heightened reliability proof as an insufficiently explained agency choice. It would leave final validity for further proceedings rather than affirm the lower court's exclusive-task premise. Its strongest support is the requirement, reflected in Gulf Power and New York v. FERC, that courts not substitute plausible policy for the agency's actual statutory explanation. The refreshed source answers that concern: the relevant explanation is now present, addresses the same assistance functions and supplies the reason for the disputed proof allocations. An additional remand would seek an explanation of points the original order already answers. No independent conflicting operative regulation or carrier-specific unresolved fact requires that route in this facial review.

**Invalidate the actual duties or burdens notwithstanding Part XI.** This path could reject the required assistance as an unreasonable extension of access or find a particular burden unsupported, without reviving exclusive task allocation. Its strongest argument is that facilitating entry and paying for work cannot themselves license every requested assembly or require an incumbent to disprove speculative risk. The adopted construction and limits answer it: actual need for assistance, lawful element entitlement, distinct feasibility and access protections, specific supported reliability impacts, and information-based proof govern. Compensation is a separate requirement rather than the source of authority. No Associate finds an independently unlawful component on this record after those limitations are preserved.

## Remedy, unchanged components and handoff

The combination disposition reverses the Eighth Circuit's invalidation of Rule 51.315(c)–(f) and returns the consolidated cases for implementation consistent with the construction above. The challenged rules are restored at that scope. A particular request is governed by lawful element entitlement, reasonable ability and necessary assistance, each paragraph's actual conditions, the applicable proof requirements and lawful compensation. Existing Rule (b) protection remains separately effective. No new agency rulemaking, carrier-specific finding or evidentiary proceeding is required to repair this facial judgment.

**Preserved commitments:** The original Verizon pricing and facial-confiscation commitments remain unchanged. Seven Associates support restoring the challenged pricing provision; Breyer retains the partial pricing-vacatur position on his stated explanation/investment ground. All eight reject facial confiscation-based invalidation on the supplied methodology record. The new source concerns connection assistance and technical justification; it neither supplies a new TELRIC reason nor establishes an actual confiscatory rate. Original matters 66–68 remain unchanged because this source supplies no premise for those speech, immunity or election issues.

**Specific change requiring reconciliation:** Thomas's combination disposition and join capacity change for the Justice-specific reasons in his row. The other seven retain restoration but now expressly adopt the contemporaneously supported assistance scope. The old statement that entrant inability lacked an actual agency source no longer describes the refreshed record. The original freeze remains preserved as the prior independent handoff; this refresh supplies its prospective replacement only for the affected component.

No historical comparison, assignment or Chief Justice merits choice was made. No new source-critical blocker remains for the bounded conclusions stated here. The incorporated source blocks remain in the validated supplement; they are not retyped in this handoff.
