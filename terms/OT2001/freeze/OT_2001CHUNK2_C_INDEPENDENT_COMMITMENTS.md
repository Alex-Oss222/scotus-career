## 27. Owasso Independent School District No. I-011 v. Falvo

### Components, rule and supported alternatives

**O1 — Statutory coverage.** Reverse the Tenth Circuit's treatment of papers temporarily exchanged and marked by classmates during the described instructional process as institutionally maintained education records. Section 1232g(a)(4)(A) requires both a direct relation to a student and maintenance by the institution or a person acting for it. Short instructional handling while marking an answer does not itself perform that recordkeeping function. Teacher direction and identifiable performance establish neither maintenance nor institutional custodianship by themselves.

The strongest contrary path regards a teacher-directed student grader as the institution's agent maintaining the paper while producing a grade. That path takes the privacy interest seriously but merges the independent requirements: a document may be educational and a pupil may follow instructions without the pupil maintaining a school record. The conclusion concerns the actual temporary process, not the proposition that students can never serve a genuine institutional recordkeeping role. The momentary announcement necessary to report a peer-assigned mark in this process does not transform that preceding paper into a maintained record.

No centralized-file, permanent-storage or single-location requirement is adopted. Teacher gradebooks, actually retained papers, and disclosures from an existing maintained record remain distinct questions. The sole-possession exclusion for instructional personnel is an exclusion from coverage, not a reason to erase the affirmative maintenance element or a rule that all teacher papers are exempt. FERPA's access-log and custody provisions may illustrate recordkeeping but add no extra statutory element.

**O2 — Enforcement route.** Assume without deciding that the asserted action is available solely to reject the pleaded practice on statutory coverage. Blessing's provision-specific individual-right and foreclosure inquiries remain unchanged; this is not hypothetical subject-matter jurisdiction. No merits ruling on a covered FERPA right, private cause, proper defendant, immunity, damages or later Gonzaga question follows. The later scheduled case supplies no entering law. Actual Sandoval supports its particular Title VI action and supplies no general prohibition on private enforcement.

**O3 — Judgment and remedy.** Reverse the reviewed coverage ruling and remand for proceedings consistent with that limited construction. No order approves the educational policy, finds absence of embarrassment, decides retained records, or resolves independent claims beyond the review. The enforcement question cannot enlarge the statute's substantive coverage.

## 28. Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co.

### Statutory components and competing paths

**S1 — Liability categories.** The signatory operator in section 9701(c)(1) is the person that is or was a signatory to a coal wage agreement. Section 9701(c)(2)(A) separately identifies: (i) a member of the section 52(a) controlled corporate group including the signatory; (ii) a trade or business under section 52(b) common control with it; and (iii) a person with a partnership or joint-venture interest with it in a coal-industry business employing eligible beneficiaries, except a person whose only interest is as a limited partner. The final sentence includes successors in interest of persons described in those three clauses. Under (c)(2)(B), the relationships are determined on July 20, 1992, or immediately before the signatory ceased business if it had already ceased by that date.

Section 9706(a) assigns eligible retirees to a signatory that, directly or through a related person, remains in business: first the most recent qualifying post-1977-agreement signatory employing the retiree for at least two years; next the most recent such signatory employer; finally the signatory with the longest covered employment before the 1978 agreement's effective date. Section 9706(b)(1)'s related-person employment aggregation and disregards retain their terms. Section 9706(b)(2) permits transfer of an assignment when a person becomes an assigned operator's successor AFTER enactment; the transferor and its related persons remain guarantors, and the assigned operator must notify the Fund's trustees.

The supported facts establish a 1973 asset purchase, assumption of an agreement and disputed assignments of 86 beneficiaries in 1993–1997. They do not establish that the purchase itself placed Jericol or Sigmon within a specified related-person class or made them the original signatory. A contractual undertaking and a statutory assignment are distinct.

**Enumerated-category path — affirm.** Congress included successors of the specified related persons and a particular post-enactment reassignment mechanism but supplied no general direct-signatory asset-successor class for this pre-enactment transaction. The differing treatment may seem anomalous, but it remains legally intelligible: Congress can delimit burdens and preserve existing categories differently. The Commissioner cannot add a liable class solely to improve financing. Purchasers independently satisfying the statute, a genuine sham transaction, or separately enforceable assumed contractual obligations remain outside this categorical asset-successor holding.

**Integrated-continuity path — reverse.** Read the related-person succession and post-enactment transfer provisions with the assignment scheme as preserving liability through business succession, including the direct continuation of the signatory's coal operation and assumed labor undertaking. The strongest support is the incongruity of reaching successors of secondary related persons while excluding the direct purchaser of the operation carrying the benefit undertaking. The strongest objection is exact: neither the express related-person list nor the post-enactment transfer provision names this pre-enactment direct-signatory purchaser. This path therefore must rest on a contextual construction of the integrated statutory class, not a general judicial power to assign solvent companies or a finding that purchase always equals common control.

**S2 — Administrative and constitutional limits.** The assignment theory cannot receive controlling force merely from administrative responsibility. Mead and Christensen require the actual delegation and mechanism; no final legislative rule establishing this theory is supplied. Independent construction resolves the dispute. Eastern Enterprises invalidates its own assignments and supplies no controlling affirmative constitutional rationale that decides this statutory question. No vote below depends on treating the Coal Act as generally invalid, invoking the takings ground rejected by five Justices there, or finding these obligations constitutional.

**S3 — Remedy.** Affirmance preserves relief against the assignments at issue. Reversal removes the categorical asset-successor bar and remands for application of the integrated rule to the supported transaction and remaining preserved objections; it neither fixes premiums nor automatically rejects distinct constitutional or contractual questions. No factual remand is justified solely to search for a missing common-control relationship.

## 29. Romeu v. Cohen

### Distinct questions and supported alternative paths

The recurring prospective eligibility dispute remains reviewable. No 2000 ballot, count or electoral allocation is reopened. Romeu seeks participation in New York's existing presidential electorate through its former-resident absentee rules, not electors for Puerto Rico. The same-date Igartua proposal supplies no entering law.

**R1 — Federal classification.** UOCAVA mandates absentee voting for its covered overseas class; it does not itself forbid New York to extend a state-established nonresident franchise more broadly. The constitutional issue nevertheless includes the federal classification among otherwise qualified former residents: a mandatory protection can distribute electoral opportunity unequally even though it is a floor. That issue must not be disposed of by falsely describing federal law as a command that New York exclude every territorial resident.

**R2 — State exclusion.** New York's rule conditions the overseas route on residence outside the United States and disavowal of voting or registration in any State or United States territory. The latter concerns any domestic elections, not merely casting a second presidential ballot. It independently excludes this former resident. The existing overseas franchise is the relevant comparator; mere authority to insist on ordinary state residence does not alone answer equality within an exception the law actually supplies.

**Membership/accommodation path — affirm R1 and R2.** Bona fide residence may delimit a State's political membership. Overseas preservation is a justified, incremental accommodation to former residents outside the domestic electoral system; a citizen settling in Puerto Rico becomes part of a different domestic polity and retains its civic participation. Territorial residence is not a suspect classification. The distinction is rationally related to that accommodated class and equal treatment of territorial residents, rather than punishment for movement or an assessment of deserving citizens. This path treats the requested former-state membership, not the practical severity of losing a presidential vote by itself, as the threshold distinction from burdens on an existing resident electorate. It does not claim that a local election is another presidential ballot or that every geographic distinction is valid.

**Existing-franchise equality path — reverse R1 and R2.** Once the government retains otherwise qualified former residents as presidential voters, complete exclusion of the comparable former resident who moves to Puerto Rico requires electoral burden-and-fit scrutiny. The injury is exclusion from choosing the same national officers, not merely inconvenience in obtaining an absentee ballot. This complete exclusion is a severe burden within the preserved franchise and requires a narrowly drawn rule advancing a compelling interest. The State and federal classification must respond to that particular participation interest. Local or territorial voting does not supply the missing presidential vote; the state declaration reaches more than duplicate voting. The offered incremental-accommodation and equal-territorial-treatment interests do not satisfy that demanding justification for total exclusion from this existing former-resident presidential franchise. No permanent constitutional membership in every former State is recognized: the defect is unequal treatment within the preserved nonresident mechanism. Article II's allocation to existing state electors remains intact.

The strongest objection to the equality path is that it imports a full voting right before establishing continuing membership and thereby risks compelling any emigrant exception to become universal. The answer is bounded to comparable prior eligibility and the actual former-resident presidential franchise; neutral verification, no duplicate presidential voting, existing qualification rules and lawful prospective organization remain available. The strongest objection to accommodation is the absence of any presidential substitute for either group; its supporters answer that domestic political membership supplies the relevant distinction without pretending that it yields a presidential vote.

**R3 — Alternative constitutional theories.** No Justice finds an independent permanent right under Article IV to carry a former State's ballot, or reads Saenz's equal treatment of new state citizens as a requirement that the former State preserve membership. The right to travel does not itself guarantee retention of every former-state electoral incident. For the equality-path Justices these propositions do not answer the distinct selective-franchise claim; they reject the asserted independent carryover/travel theory while sustaining equality. Due process supplies no separate general entitlement on the supported facts beyond the equality issue adjudicated.

**R4 — Remedies.** Affirmance sustains the reviewed dismissal and preserves the distinction's present operation without deciding power to legislate every imaginable broader franchise. Reversal would set aside dismissal of the federal and state equality claims and remand for prospective equal treatment within the existing former-resident presidential system, with defendants, severability and implementation addressed under their separate authority. Federal minimum obligations remain operative unless and until lawfully conformed to the judgment; the State may not cure its unequal rule simply by denying the federally required overseas vote. No immediate unconditional ballot, new territorial electors, pro rata electoral plan or retrospective count follows. Independent alternative-theory rejection need not be reopened.

## 30. Igartua De La Rosa v. United States

### Components and alternative paths

**I1 — Reviewability and scope.** Reach the concrete constitutional question rather than dismissing every territorial electoral dispute as a political question. The district court ordered Puerto Rico to arrange voting and appoint electors for 2000 and subsequent elections. The prospective command remains a live issue. The rejected ICCPR and UOCAVA theories were not appealed by the plaintiffs or Commonwealth and do not return through this review. Congressional representation and Puerto Rico Law 403's independent local-law validity are outside it.

**I2 — Allocation.** Affirm the First Circuit's vacatur and directions to dismiss the claim for the particular territorial-elector order. Article II assigns appointment to States; the Twelfth Amendment operates within that allocation; the Twenty-Third Amendment separately supplies electors to the District of Columbia. Equal national citizenship does not itself specify another electoral unit, its number of electors, its appointment authority or the duty to count them. Puerto Rico is not a State and has no corresponding amendment. The requested federal judicial order therefore lacks the asserted constitutional foundation.

The strongest contrary path relies on equal national citizenship and the importance of selecting officials exercising authority over territorial residents. It identifies a real political exclusion but supplies no constitutional number or method for electors outside the express units. Thornton's fixed qualifications and Rice's invalidation of a racial qualification concern participation within constitutionally established electoral structures; neither creates a new structural unit. Saenz likewise protects equality on becoming a State's citizen, not a new electoral allocation. Bush's ballot-equality holdings cannot answer this antecedent allocation issue.

**I3 — Remedy and limits.** Leave the district court's territorial appointment and count command vacated and affirm the specified dismissal. Do not vacate Law 403 as a separate act, revive the unappealed claims, rule on a former-State absentee franchise or offer a pro rata voting system. No constitutional conclusion declares that territorial citizens lack ordinary rights, decides Puerto Rico's political status, or bars political efforts toward lawful broader participation. Romeu's same-date proposed result is neither authority nor a premise here.

## 31. Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer

### Exact statutory relationships and components

**B1 — Resource-hearing methodology.** Section 1396r-5(a)(1) gives this section priority over inconsistent Medicaid provisions; (a)(3) preserves ordinary income/resource determinations and methodology except where the section specifically provides otherwise. The section protects a resource amount and income support through distinct provisions.

Section (b)(1) prevents the COMMUNITY spouse's income from being deemed available to the INSTITUTIONALIZED spouse, except as (b)(2) provides. It is not a reciprocal general prohibition written in the opposite direction. Section (b)(2)'s title/instrument rules expressly govern income attribution for the POSTELIGIBILITY determination under (d), with their trust, joint-payment, instrument and rebutted-ownership qualifications. Section (c)(2) governs resources at initial eligibility, excluding the amount computed under (f)(2)(A); (c)(4) later separates community-spouse resources. These dates and directions cannot be collapsed.

Section (d)(1)(B) permits the community-spouse income allowance to be deducted from the institutionalized spouse's income for care only to the extent that income is actually made available to or for the community spouse. Section (d)(2) measures that allowance by the maintenance minimum less income otherwise available, with the court-order rule of (d)(5) remaining distinct. Section (e)(2)(C) requires a substituted resource allowance when the spouse establishes that the existing allowance, in relation to the income it generates, is inadequate to raise the community spouse's income to the maintenance minimum. The exceptional-circumstances/significant-financial-duress adjustment to the maintenance minimum under (e)(2)(B) is a different provision and remains separate.

**Permissible-income-first path — reverse.** The resource hearing forecasts the community spouse's income after eligibility; it may consider the anticipated community-spouse income allowance authorized by (d) before enlarging protected resources under (e). The posteligibility title-attribution rules do not expressly define all projected income for this separate hearing. Section (a)(3)'s preserved methodology room and the combined income-and-resource protection permit the State's choice. This does not require the institutionalized spouse to transfer income, deem a proposed transfer accomplished, or authorize a (d)(1)(B) payment deduction for money not actually made available. A transfer's optional character does not itself entitle the couple to a larger resource shelter while leaving the authorized income support unused. The claim is to the statute's maintenance protection, not maximal preservation of assets.

**Resources-first path — affirm.** Section (e)(2)(C) speaks to the community spouse's income and the income generated by the resource allowance in determining eligibility. An optional later transfer from the other spouse is not presently that income. The specific resource-protection command qualifies general methodological flexibility. Although (b)(2)'s attribution rules are expressly posteligibility, their careful separation of ownership and transfer reinforces the scheme; it is not necessary to misstate (b)(1) as a reciprocal prohibition. The State cannot defeat the mandatory resource increase by assuming that an optional posteligibility payment has supplied an existing income deficiency.

Each path must answer the other: income-first must preserve actual payment and attribution limits; resources-first must explain why a necessarily prospective resource-income forecast cannot include the very support allowance the same scheme authorizes. Neither may infer Irene's later transfers or invent a consent, escrow, evidentiary-burden or guaranteed-payment condition as a compromise.

**B2 — Agency weight.** The November 1993 memorandum described income-first as required; the March 3, 1994 clarification permitted income-first or another reasonable interpretation pending final regulations. The September 7, 2001 publication is a proposed rule, not an effective final rule. Good Samaritan does not disqualify a considered view just because it changed. Mead and Christensen prevent automatic controlling weight from advisory guidance or a proposal. The policy reasons receive their earned persuasive weight. The final statutory commitments below do not depend on pretending that these documents bind a court or State as enacted law.

**B3 — Remedy.** Reversal rejects the categorical federal prohibition on Wisconsin's method and remands for ordinary application and remaining eligibility calculations; it does not determine a transfer, amount, initial eligibility date or benefit award. Affirmance preserves the resources-first legal constraint and requires recalculation under it, without granting all requested resources or declaring immediate eligibility. Actual payment calculations remain subject to (d)(1)(B), and no unclaimed exceptional-duress or court-order finding is supplied.

## 32. Winn-Dixie Stores, Inc. v. Commissioner

### Components and alternative paths

**W1 — Effect of the statutory exception.** For the 1993 tax year, compliance with section 264(c)(1)'s four-of-seven exception removes the prohibition in section 264(a)(3) to the extent that exception provides; it does not independently grant the section 163 deduction or immunize the borrowing from ordinary substantive-sham principles. Compliance with the exception is an accepted premise of this appeal. No dispute about its premium-counting conditions, period calculation or another tax year is decided; the question is the legal effect of established compliance, not a new construction of its eligibility conditions.

Knetsch rejects the inference that absence of a particular statutory disallowance affirmatively grants a deduction for a transaction lacking the substantive indebtedness the deduction contemplates. Gregory likewise requires statutory substance rather than formal steps alone. Real policies, loans and payments may exist while the particular tax characterization still fails. The issue is not whether any cash moved.

**W2 — Application to accepted findings.** Affirm because the challenged broad employee-insurance borrowing had no nontax business purpose and no possible pretax profit on the accepted findings: interest and administrative costs exceeded pretax benefits. The taxpayer disclaimed factual challenge in the appeal. The conclusion therefore does not rest on tax motive alone or on an inference that employer-owned insurance can never serve a business purpose. It does not establish whether absence of either purpose or profit alone always suffices, whether every negative pretax return is a sham, or whether Congress cannot confer deliberate tax incentives.

**Formal-authorization alternative — reverse.** The strongest contrary construction treats the later detailed four-of-seven limitation as an objective congressional permission for exactly the borrowing meeting its terms, making another judicial qualification a nullification of the exception. Knetsch's dissent supplies the classic legislative-line objection; Gitlitz confirms that courts may not deny an express tax benefit just because the result seems generous. But the exception's enacted work is to remove its own prohibition, not to declare that every compliant arrangement has the substance required by section 163. The exception still operates for genuine indebtedness. No source establishes displacement of Knetsch's antecedent statutory inquiry.

**Correction/remand alternative.** A remand would be warranted if the lower court equated tax motivation alone with sham or ignored a genuine independent economic effect. The accepted findings establish both the absence of a nontax business purpose and any possibility of pretax profit in this arrangement. The bounded affirmance needs neither a universal conjunctive/disjunctive test nor a new trial of those findings.

**W3 — Remedy and scope.** Affirm the deficiency judgment at the reviewed deduction scope. No new penalty, tax-year liability, generalized corporate-insurance prohibition, determination of another transaction, or later economic-substance codification enters. Compaq is a different later matter, with different asserted economics, and supplies no law or required outcome. Gitlitz's express pass-through and basis rules, and its preservation of independent deduction prerequisites, remain intact.
