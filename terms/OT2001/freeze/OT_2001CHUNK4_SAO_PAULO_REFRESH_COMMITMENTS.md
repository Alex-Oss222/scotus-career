## Competing applications of the objective standard

**P1 — reverse.** An informed, detached observer would consider the actual executive-committee role together with the mistaken presidential attribution, lack of preparation or approval, and vague awareness. A genuine leadership affiliation with the association is stronger than a wholly false association, but on these facts it does not reasonably identify the judge with this brief's particular advocacy in the way the erroneous motion suggested. The corrected factual account supplies more than a personal assurance of fairness: it identifies what the judge did and did not do. The statutory standard requires a reasonable appearance concern, without demanding actual bias; it does not treat every position expressed by an organization as the personal litigation position of every leader who did not participate in that expression.

**P2 — affirm.** Even without personal authorship or approval, contemporaneous leadership of an organization taking a position in similar litigation can reasonably identify a judge with that institutional position. This is the strongest contrary path because section 455(a) protects appearances and requires no subjective hostility. The correction of the title alone therefore cannot be dispositive, and absence of actual participation is not a categorical defense to recusal.

The eight Associates reject P2 on the full supported facts. Membership in the executive committee gives the affiliation genuine weight, but the established nonparticipation and vague awareness substantially limit what an informed observer could reasonably attribute to the judge in this particular matter. The record supplies an association expressing an institutional position and a leader who neither prepared nor approved it, with his name mistakenly attached under an expired office. It supplies no further supported relationship connecting him to the advocacy at issue. That combination is insufficient here without treating membership itself as decisive. P1 neither requires proof of actual bias nor creates immunity for organizational leadership; different supported responsibilities, adoption, participation or other circumstances could change the appearance assessment.

Liteky, 510 U.S. 540, supplies the single cumulative objective standard. Microsoft's January 22, 2002 Part II applies that rule to supported outside conduct without an actual-bias requirement. The latter's undisclosed discussions concerning live merits, credibility and remedy are a materially different appearance predicate. The distinction is substantive conduct and its relation to the case, not a rule that only press contacts or intentional misconduct can require recusal. Liljeberg, 486 U.S. 847, keeps remedial consequences separate once a violation is established. Its three interests cannot themselves create a violation absent the statutory appearance predicate.
