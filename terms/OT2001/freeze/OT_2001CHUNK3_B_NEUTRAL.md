### Issue 1: Unpreserved plea error and the relevant record

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Olano distinguishes forfeiture from waiver, ordinarily places the substantial-rights burden on the defendant under Rule 52(b), and preserves a separate discretionary fourth requirement. Johnson v. * **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable rule requires error, plainness, substantial-rights effect, and a distinct justification for correction. * **Other Governing Law:** Rule 11 requires specified advice before acceptance of a plea; Rule 11(h) addresses variances not affecting substantial rights. Rule 52(b) addresses unpreserved error. Those enacted rules should be read together.
* **Material Other Authority:** McCarthy v. United States addresses the earlier Rule 11 regime and must be read with the subsequent harmless-error provision. Boykin v. Alabama concerns a constitutionally valid plea; it does not eliminate the need to identify the actual claim and record.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Olano governs ordinary forfeited errors, but does not expressly decide whether Rule 11(h) displaces that framework or whether review is confined to the plea colloquy. A judge’s omission remains an error even if another part of the record bears on its consequences.
* **Open Point:** Whether Rule 11 creates a special exception to ordinary forfeiture review, and whether prior record advisements may inform prejudice without substituting for the judge’s required procedure.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The Ninth Circuit placed the harmlessness burden on the Government and excluded earlier advisements from its inquiry. Vonn argues that the special plea rule assigns the judge a personal duty and that Rule 11(h)’s express harmlessness language controls. The Government argues that Rule 52(b) applies to an unobjected-to omission and that substantial rights cannot be evaluated through an artificially restricted record. Its position must preserve the Rule 11 error itself; Vonn’s position must justify an implied exception to the general rule rather than rely only on the importance of informed pleas.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Vacatur and remand could require ordinary plain-error review using relevant record material, leaving application to the court of appeals. Affirmance could sustain the special harmlessness rule or find the convictions unsound even under the ordinary rule if that alternative is properly decided on the record. A colloquy-only rule would limit the evidence of actual knowledge; a whole-record rule would permit earlier advisements without making them conclusive. Neither scope permits appellate courts to invent conversations outside the record or to treat every signed form as proof that every omission was immaterial.

### Issue 1: Conditions of confinement

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Farmer v. Brennan requires a sufficiently serious risk and the official’s actual knowledge and unreasonable disregard. Obviousness can support an inference of knowledge but does not compel it. The rule distinguishes ordinary conditions decisions from rapid uses of force.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The reusable standards require individual culpability and causation, permit circumstantial proof, and treat an objectively reasonable response as inconsistent with deliberate indifference even if harm occurs.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Eighth Amendment applies to punishment after conviction. Section 1983 supplies the remedial channel; seriousness, culpability, and attributable injury remain distinct.
* **Material Other Authority:** Wilson v. Seiter addresses conditions and culpability; Hudson v. McMillian does not impose its force-specific inquiry on every restriction of exercise. Davenport v. DeRobertis supplies relevant circuit notice about prolonged denial of exercise, not a new nationwide numerical minimum.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Farmer already governs knowing disregard of a serious health risk. The application to a prolonged, nearly total deprivation requires the actual conditions and justification; a finding of harm alone does not prove deliberate indifference.
* **Open Point:** Whether the assumed combination of duration, confinement, complaints, and lack of a sufficient response meets that established rule.

### Issue 2: Clearly established law and personal immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Saucier v. Katz leaves Harlow and Anderson’s objective defense in force without a new controlling merits-first rule. Elder requires all relevant preconduct law; Wilson asks whether the right was sufficiently clear; Crawford-El bars a heightened proof standard merely because immunity and intent coexist.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The prior decisions preserve immunity as a distinct question and require conduct-specific notice without making a factually identical Supreme Court case indispensable. A later ruling cannot retroactively establish clarity.
* **Other Governing Law:** Qualified immunity concerns individual damages liability, not the substantive validity of a prison practice or all prospective relief.
* **Material Other Authority:** Anderson v. Creighton requires appropriate particularity. The circuit’s exercise precedents may inform notice within that jurisdiction, subject to their actual facts and effective dates.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Officials retain protection for reasonable mistakes about the legal limits under the circumstances. A generalized reference to security does not automatically defeat a specifically established constraint.
* **Open Point:** Whether the law effective in 1996 gave fair notice that six months of near-total exercise deprivation with known serious effects and no adequate justification crossed the constitutional line.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The circuit treated the favorable facts as sufficient under the Eighth Amendment and existing exercise precedents. Officials invoke security needs, inadequate proof of serious injury, lack of notice, and differences among their roles. Delaney argues that prolonged total deprivation and repeated complaints made the danger apparent and required a response. He must prove each defendant’s culpability rather than attribute the warden’s knowledge to every guard. The officials must accept the proper assumed facts for this appeal or confine their arguments to nonappealable factual disputes.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope, Judgment Consequences, and Uncertainty:** Affirmance would preserve a trial on materially disputed facts, not enter a damages judgment. Reversal on constitutional grounds could hold that the assumed conditions or culpability fail Farmer; reversal on immunity alone could shield these officials while leaving the constitutional question unresolved or separately addressed. If an argument merely disputes which facts are supported, the appropriate consequence is dismissal of that appellate component rather than a new factual finding. A defensible holding addresses this duration and severity with known risk and available response; neither an exact universal exercise quota nor a blanket lockdown exemption is necessary.
