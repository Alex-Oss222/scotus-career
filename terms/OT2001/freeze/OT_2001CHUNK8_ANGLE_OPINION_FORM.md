# Angle: opinion form and governing propositions

Determined from the approved position and frozen reconciled commitments before the assignment recount. One signed lead merits opinion resolves the consolidated appeals: mandatory-range constitutional classification and express partial overruling of McMillan; the existing Angle resentencing directions; and Phifer count error, plainness, the defective stacking premise and the remaining Rule 52(b) remand. These related components require one explanation of the same quantity/range relationship. The substantial constitutional question and competing remedial positions warrant a signed opinion.

The tentative disposition coalition is Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg for constitutional directions and Phifer appellate vacatur. All nine preserve Angle's independent sentence vacatur and the convictions. O'Connor, Kennedy and Breyer reject the new range rule and would affirm Phifer on their lawful mandatory-total premise.

Scalia joins the binding-range rule, actual count error, rejection of unsupported stacking and the remaining Rule 52(b) remand. His separate partial concurrence assumes plainness and preserves the timing reservation in his frozen commitment. The other five directly adopt the count-excess plainness determination by application of existing Johnson and Cotton; no general new timing rule is adopted. The five Associates supporting the range rule also adopt the applicable federal grand-jury charging consequence. Stone's supplement supplies the factfinder/proof rule but no separate federal charging ground; the standing fallback supplies only his disposition agreement on that silent component. Stone therefore does not join that distinct charging proposition. One author can carry all controlling parts; division among authors is unnecessary. No opinion form or proposition was selected to affect counts.
