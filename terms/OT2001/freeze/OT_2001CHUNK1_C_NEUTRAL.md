### Issue 1: Pending grievances and lawful consideration of earlier discipline

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling holding resolves the Board’s use of pending disciplinary actions. The prior decisions do not turn contractual grievance pendency into either conclusive validity or conclusive invalidity.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Separate the existence of authority to consider information from the evidentiary and procedural safeguards governing its use. Review the actual administrative decision, including prejudice to the final penalty.

* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** An agency may explain a progressive penalty through the employee’s established disciplinary history, but a later invalidation can affect the premise of that explanation. Neither proposition decides how extensive collateral review must be.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Federal Circuit treated the unresolved grievance process as making reliance on earlier discipline impermissible. Its rule avoided the risk that a removal would rest on penalties later overturned, without deciding the independent sufficiency of each penalty. **Postal Service:** Neither the civil-service framework nor ordinary administration requires the employer to suspend progressive discipline until every contractual challenge ends. Meaningful Board review can protect the employee while allowing the agency to manage continuing misconduct. Its proposed general rule permits consideration, subject to review and correction. **Gregory:** A nominal opportunity to contest an old penalty is insufficient if the Board then treats it as presumptively reliable and applies only narrow collateral review. Grievance rights could become practically useless if removal occurs first. The overturned warning supplies a concrete problem beyond theoretical delay. **Alternative grounds and limits:** Rejecting the categorical rule does not establish that the Board’s particular standard satisfies every statutory or constitutional requirement. Conversely, the reversal of the warning does not itself prove that no lawful removal penalty remains. The court must identify the penalty’s dependence on the invalid action.

### Issue 1: Overlapping statutory protection for plants

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Asgrow Seed Co. v. Winterboer construes the former plant-variety saved-seed exception and preserves its version-specific limits. The later amendment eliminates specified farmer-sales protection subject to transition rules. Neither establishes that plant-variety certificates are the exclusive possible federal protection.

* **Other Governing Law:** The utility-patent statute, Plant Patent Act, and applicable version of the Plant Variety Protection Act provide the governing enacted law.
* **Material Other Authority:** Diamond v. Chakrabarty supplies historical context for the breadth of patent-eligible subject matter. Statutory protections may overlap while imposing different proof requirements and consequences.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Asgrow Seed Co. v. Winterboer governs its certificate-based seed exception. It does not import that exception into utility-patent law or resolve all restrictions associated with patented seed.
* **Open Point:** Whether Congress made the plant-specific regimes exclusive or left utility patents available when their independent statutory requirements are met.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The courts rejected the theory that the later plant statutes displaced utility-patent protection for plants. **Pioneer:** The general patent text is broad, and neither plant statute expressly removes plants from it. Pioneer’s eligibility argument likewise does not prove each patent valid or each contractual limitation enforceable. The Court must distinguish a patent’s statutory exclusionary right from rights asserted solely through a sales agreement.

**Overlapping-protection path:** Affirm that qualifying plants may receive utility patents. **Plausible Holding Scope:** Eligibility remains subject to all ordinary utility-patent requirements; no categorical displacement of certificate-specific exceptions within their own regime. **Exclusive-special-statute path:** Reverse and hold that the specialized plant statutes displace utility patents for the covered plants. **Plausible Holding Scope:** The ruling would require identifying the exact class excluded and its statutory basis. **Patent-specific path:** Reject categorical exclusion while leaving other validity or infringement defenses for ordinary proceedings. **Plausible Holding Scope:** The present judgment decides the statutory category, not every patent or seed transaction.

### Issue 1: Reasonably calculated notice within government custody

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** City of West Covina v. Perkins requires notice sufficient to identify the deprivation and responsible custodian while distinguishing publicly available legal procedures from facts uniquely known to the government. It does not decide this certified-mail delivery process.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Evaluate notice prospectively by the practical means reasonably available and the recipient’s known situation. Actual receipt and a reasonably calculated delivery procedure are distinct; neither a bare mailing ritual nor guaranteed success is the universal constitutional measure.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Due Process Clause and the administrative forfeiture procedures then in force govern. The institution’s actual mail-routing evidence bears on the constitutional application.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank & Trust Co. supplies the historical reasonably-calculated formulation. The government’s special knowledge of the prisoner’s location and control over delivery are central factual circumstances.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** West Covina’s distinction between notice and legal instruction does not relieve the government of getting notice through a reasonably reliable route. It also does not impose actual receipt as an absolute condition of every valid deprivation.
* **Open Point:** Whether this established certified-mail and internal-distribution procedure was sufficient without proof of actual receipt.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The court found the certified mailing and described prison procedures sufficient, without requiring proof that the prisoner personally received the item. **United States:** Due process requires a reasonable attempt, not proof of successful communication. Certified mailing to the known institution, supported by a routine internal delivery system, is a practical and ordinarily effective method. **Dusenbery:** The government controlled both ends of the route. A mailroom signature does not establish a reliable connection to the inmate, and weak evidence about internal distribution could reduce notice to a formal dispatch. His strongest rule demands a demonstrated process reasonably directed to the particular confined person, rather than an inflexible actual-receipt guarantee. **Alternative grounds:** The additional attempted addresses do not independently cure a defective institutional route merely because they increase the number of mailings. Conversely, the absence of a retained inmate signature does not prove that the process was constitutionally unreasonable. The adequacy of the evidence must be assessed without inventing either actual receipt or known delivery failure.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Reasonable-procedure path:** Affirm if the evidence establishes a regular and reasonably reliable route from certified receipt to inmate distribution. **Plausible Holding Scope:** Actual receipt is not indispensable; a prison mailroom signature alone is not universally sufficient. **Control-sensitive path:** Require an additional available verification step where the government’s own custodial arrangements otherwise leave delivery unreliable, and remand. **Plausible Holding Scope:** The requirement would depend on demonstrated weaknesses and feasible measures, not an absolute receipt rule. **Failure-notice path:** If the government knew the notice had failed, assess the need for further available steps on that actual record. **Plausible Holding Scope:** No known-failure finding is assumed here. A notice violation would reopen the appropriate forfeiture process, not automatically resolve ownership or forfeiture merits.
