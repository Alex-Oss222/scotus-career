## What each statutory permission requires

**Consumer-report coverage, section 603(d), 15 U.S.C. 1681a(d).** Subject to the enumerated exclusions copied in the source annex, the definition reaches a communication by a consumer reporting agency bearing on the listed credit or personal characteristics when used, expected to be used, or collected in whole or part as an eligibility factor for personal/family/household credit or insurance, employment, or another section 604 purpose. The collection-purpose language remains distinct from the particular buyer's later marketing use. This supplies statutory context; it does not reopen an unpreserved classification claim or find any new characteristic in Trans Union's lists. The exclusions are preserved in the exact text, but no unrelated affiliate, card-approval or employment communication is assumed to be a comparable marketing permission.

**Written instructions, section 604(a)(2), 15 U.S.C. 1681b(a)(2).** Subject to subsection (c), an agency may furnish a report in accordance with the written instructions of the consumer to whom it relates. The actual instructions define that permission; silence or mere failure to opt out is not written instructions. This is distinct from subsection (c)(1)(A), which authorizes an otherwise covered unsolicited credit or insurance report when the consumer authorizes the agency to provide that report to that person. The latter provision does not itself use the word written; the two clauses must not be merged or given identical terms by paraphrase.

**Underlying credit/insurance purpose, section 604(a)(3)(A), (C).** The agency must have reason to believe the recipient intends to use the information for a credit transaction involving that consumer and extension of credit to, or review or collection of an account of, that consumer, or for underwriting insurance involving that consumer. Subsection (a)(3)(F)'s separate legitimate-business-need route is tied to a business transaction initiated by the consumer or review of an account to determine continued compliance with its terms; it is not an unqualified permission for any profitable marketing use. Section 603(m) excludes review of an existing account or insurance policy, or collection of an existing account, by the person holding that relationship from the defined unsolicited-transaction category. These are different statutory predicates, not factual findings that the challenged lists satisfy them.

**Unsolicited firm-offer route, section 604(c)(1)(B).** For a credit or insurance transaction not initiated by the consumer, the alternative to the consumer-authorization route requires all three event-date conditions: a firm offer of credit or insurance; agency compliance with subsection (e); and no effective consumer election under subsection (e) excluding the consumer's name and address from the agency's lists. Failure to opt out is therefore one condition of this particular route, not a general marketing authorization. The reproduced provision is the event-date text.

**Meaning of firm offer, section 603(l), 15 U.S.C. 1681a(l).** The offer must be honored if the consumer meets the specific consumer-report criteria used to select the consumer. The statute permits further conditioning on: application information showing satisfaction of specific creditworthiness or insurability criteria established before selection and for deciding whether to extend the offered credit or insurance; verification that the consumer continues to meet the selection criteria using a report, application or other relevant creditworthiness/insurability information, or verification of application information to determine satisfaction of those criteria; and furnishing collateral whose requirement was established before selection and disclosed in the offer. Thus the offer is neither an unconditional guarantee nor a merely discretionary invitation to market unrelated products. No additional judicial minimum-value rule is supplied.

**Information limits, section 604(c)(2)-(3).** A recipient under the firm-offer route may receive only the consumer's name and address; an identifier not unique to that consumer used solely to verify identity; and other information that does not identify the consumer's relationship or experience with a particular creditor or other entity. Separately, an agency may not furnish a record of unsolicited credit/insurance inquiries except through section 609(a)(5)'s disclosure to the consumer, on request and subject to the proper-identification requirement, of inquiries identifying that consumer in the preceding year. The statute therefore does not permit an unrestricted credit file merely because a firm offer will be made.

**Opt-out safeguards, section 604(e).** A consumer may notify the agency through its notification system or submit the agency's signed election form. An election becomes effective five business days after notice and covers the agency's affiliates. System-only notice lasts two years beginning five business days after notice, subject to earlier cancellation; the signed-form election continues until the consumer cancels through the system. After system notice, the agency must explain the two-year limit if no signed form is submitted and, when the form is requested during that notice, provide it within five business days. The agency must maintain a system including a toll-free telephone number, permit notice with appropriate identification, and publish at least annually in a general-circulation publication for its service area the possible use of consumer-file information and the address and toll-free number for exclusion. A system and publication maintained for affiliates counts for them; nationwide reporting agencies must maintain the notification system jointly. The statute's original publication deadline was 365 days after September 30, 1996. These are statutory obligations, not findings that any particular agency fulfilled them.

**Separate user obligations, sections 604(f) and 615(d).** Use or acquisition requires an authorized purpose and the prospective user's general or specific purpose certification under section 607. For each written solicitation based on a report furnished through the firm-offer route, section 615(d) separately requires a clear and conspicuous statement that report information was used, the consumer met selection criteria, any applicable criteria or collateral condition may prevent extension, the consumer can prohibit unsolicited-transaction use, and the consumer can exercise that right through the notification system; the statement must include its address and toll-free number. Selection, extension and collateral criteria must be retained for three years from the offer. Federal and state unfair/deceptive-practice authority is preserved. Section 615(c)'s separate liability defense requires proof by a preponderance that reasonable compliance procedures were maintained at the time of the alleged violation; it does not delete the duties or become an exception to section 604's permissible purposes. This supplement finds no violation of these duties and adds no claim for their enforcement.

## Consequences for the neutral alternatives

The government may argue that consumer-directed disclosure and limited, enforceable credit/insurance offers under these safeguards differ from unrelated marketing of credit-derived classifications. The challenger may contest whether those distinctions adequately protect the same asserted privacy interest and whether materially equivalent disclosures remain permitted. Neither position is decided by calling an offer firm or by calling an exception consent. The actual communication, purpose, information limits and statutory safeguards must be compared; their existence alone establishes neither material advancement nor reasonable fit. A statutory command is not evidence of real-world compliance, and this supplement supplies no additional administrative finding, consumer instruction or particular offer.
