## Verified distinction for the existing competing paths

Electrical Fittings distinguishes objections to unnecessary findings from an adverse adjudication actually included in a favorable decree. It permitted an appeal to eliminate the decree's patent-validity adjudication, without deciding patent validity on appeal. The lower appellate court's view that the adjudication would not bind the defendants in a later suit did not foreclose that relief.

Roper, pages 333–337 and footnote 7, expressly discusses that distinction. Its ordinary favorable-judgment rule is a rule of appellate practice, while a continuing personal stake remains constitutionally necessary. Roper itself identifies the named plaintiffs' economic interest in sharing litigation costs and confines the permitted appeal to class certification. Its Electrical Fittings account does not demand proof that another suit has already begun or that preclusion is certain. It does not establish that every rejected motion or unfavorable reason creates a continuing stake.

Stevens's separate Roper concurrence treats absent members of a properly maintainable class as sufficiently present for the limited controversy question. That distinct class-party explanation does not itself identify a present injury to the Mathias commissioners.

The clean Mathias packet already preserves the separate denial of the commissioners' motions, the absence of a formal adverse judgment, the favorable merits and the lower court's statement about binding effect. This verification adds no preclusion finding, enforcement obligation, later lawsuit or claim for reimbursement. Whether the existing rulings create an appealable continuing adverse effect remains for independent modeling.
