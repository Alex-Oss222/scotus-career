## Historical rule and precise comparison

The final official opinion, 535 U.S. 722–742, is unanimous and has no separate writing. Kennedy's historical authorship is source evidence only. It vacates the complete-bar judgment and remands. All eight current Associates independently support that disposition and the central distinction between a qualifying narrowing, the reason for amendment and the extent of surrender.

Historically, actual narrowing to comply with any Patent Act requirement, including section 112, may produce estoppel; a truly cosmetic amendment does not. A patentee cannot avoid actual surrender merely by describing the examiner's concern as formal. The Court rejects total extinction of equivalents and puts the burden of showing that the particular equivalent was not surrendered on the patentee. Its examples include an equivalent unforeseeable at the relevant prosecution stage, an amendment rationale only tangentially related to that equivalent, and another reason why the substitute could not reasonably have been described. Its concluding formulation asks what a person skilled in the art could reasonably have been expected to claim at amendment. Those explanations constrain actual surrender; they are not a finding that either accused substitute satisfies an exception.

The report uses application in the example concerning unforeseeability and amendment in the concluding formulation. This reconciliation does not silently replace those words with a later doctrinal elaboration. Nor does it turn the examples and open other-reason category into a closed mechanical list, an automatic safe harbor from merely asserting unforeseeability, or a general evidentiary prohibition not announced in this opinion.

R1–R3 are compatible with the controlling historical core. They preserve all material claim limitations, the qualifying patentability trigger, the patentee's purpose-explanation burden and the separate burden to rebut presumptive surrender of the asserted equivalent. Their concrete, objective, prosecution-anchored rebuttal can give the historical examples their actual explanatory force; a later substitute's mere resemblance or a private intention to keep broad protection does not establish rebuttal. The final historical opinion does not require a universal file-only exclusion of reliable technical explanation. The actual Markman holding expressly permits useful extrinsic technical evidence in judicial construction, and actual Warner-Jenkinson expressly requires fair treatment of supported explanations and older prosecution records. R3 preserves those rules without promising admission, reopening or a hearing.

The material difference lies in R4 and R6's application remand. Historical Part IV treats estoppel applicability as established and returns the scope-of-surrender questions. It expressly characterizes the ring and sleeve additions as conceded responses to section 112 or prior-art objections. The refreshed handoff does not make that same final trigger determination. It returns the actual scope, purpose and burden applications on the documented record, in addition to surrender extent, without requiring factual rediscovery or retrial.

## Why the source gate does not itself decide the trigger

The historical recitation is strong evidence and has not been erased. The clean primary-source gate, however, permits a more precise attribution than an unqualified concession of every trigger: Festo accepts the written additions; Stoll's section 112 rejection and response are established; Carroll's replacement combination was offered against prior art; and counsel acknowledges some Carroll abandonment while denying its relevance. The same sources preserve dispute over actual narrowing and each limitation's purpose. The sleeve's alleged attorney error, SMC's effective-concession characterization and the district's mystery description are different statements by different actors. They are not a single judicial finding or stipulation.

That absence of a blanket admission is not a conclusion that estoppel fails. Moving a restriction from a dependent claim into an independent one can narrow the latter. A feature need not be separately novel or demanded word-for-word by the examiner to be part of a voluntary patentability narrowing. Prior art's possession of two rings therefore does not establish that the amended ring limitation surrendered nothing. The existing explanation burden can apply even when Festo labels the addition unintended. The issued sleeve restriction cannot be deleted by testimony about the inventor's wishes. The stronger SMC path remains legally available.

Nor is disagreement alone a reason to remand. The legal reason is that the en banc judgment applies the rejected conclusive complete bar, while actual Warner-Jenkinson requires claim-specific legal surrender and fair consideration of an explanation before its existing purpose presumption does its work. The new R3 scope presumption also requires a distinct application. The source correction removes the proposed shortcut of treating all applications as undisputed; it does not compel the Court to leave them open. The eight independently choose to return those applications under the corrected framework rather than decide a disputed construction and purpose package through that shortcut. Their actual prior joins support that limited choice. The court of appeals may decide a trigger on the existing record where sufficient; no automatic new factual proceeding follows.

The narrower historical return, preserving an established trigger and considering only scope, was a serious alternative. The verified writings could support particular trigger conclusions through legal analysis, independently of any admission. The present handoff does not declare that alternative impossible or that the complete record lacks the answer. It declines to make a blanket current determination across the three limitations without the amendment-specific application required by its operative rules. This is a material difference in what the Court decides now, not an opposite merits finding that estoppel is inapplicable.

### R1. Actual narrowing is a legal inquiry into the particular claim

The parties' accepted written history is fixed for this handoff: the replacement Stoll independent claim incorporates the ring and sleeve restrictions previously appearing in dependent claims, and the replacement Carroll claim includes the ring limitation. The earlier Stoll independent claim already contained sealing means at each end. No one must rediscover those written events merely because their legal effect is disputed.

Actual narrowing is determined by comparing the pertinent claim's legally operative scope before and after amendment, in the patent and prosecution context. A limitation's earlier appearance in a dependent claim does not categorically prevent its addition to an independent claim from narrowing that independent claim. Conversely, added or rearranged words alone do not dispense with construction of the actual before-and-after scope. The Court does not use the oral rebuttal's unidentified transferred element as a stipulation concerning a particular Stoll limitation.

The supplement supports these comparison rules and the absence of an unqualified concession. It does not justify replacing construction with a blanket finding that each disputed addition was substantively narrowing. The original commitment's refusal to decide all applications by the same assumed reason is retained; the remand now expressly proceeds from the documented claim history rather than treating it as missing.

### R2. Patentability purpose and the separate explanation rule

The original trigger remains: actual narrowing to obtain the patent by satisfying a patentability requirement can produce estoppel. The category is not confined to novelty or prior-art avoidance; genuine narrowing to satisfy a disclosure requirement can qualify. A voluntary amendment receives the same substantive inquiry. A formal objection is neither categorical immunity from estoppel nor proof that each ensuing limitation was required for patentability.

Stoll's section 112 rejection, request for reconsideration and accompanying prior-art remarks are verified source events. They do not establish that the examiner required the ring or sleeve limitation or that both had the same purpose. Carroll's replacement combination was offered against the German reference, but that overall purpose does not eliminate the need to assess the particular ring amendment. The feature need not have been separately novel or indispensable to patentability before a voluntary narrowing can have surrender consequences. The presence of two rings in the reference is therefore not an automatic answer for Festo, just as the reference's submission is not an automatic answer for SMC.

Where required narrowing has no disclosed legally sufficient reason, Warner-Jenkinson places the explanatory burden on the patentee seeking the equivalent. After fair consideration, inadequate explanation permits the rebuttable presumption of a patentability-related reason and estoppel at the limitation. A supported nonpatentability explanation must be considered; historical silence is not misconduct, and nonexistent contemporaneous formulations cannot be demanded. No broader automatic purpose presumption for every voluntary textual change is added.

SMC's statement that Festo effectively concedes an unexplained sleeve purpose is SMC's inference. Festo's earlier attorney-error account is an actual party position, not a finding that the explanation is true or legally sufficient. The district court's description of the sleeve reason as a mystery is an earlier record assessment, not a concession or this Court's final application of the present burden. No source absence is used to find that the explanation has failed.

### R3. Surrender scope and the now-explicit evidence limit

The eight Associates retain the proposed intermediate rule: after a qualifying patentability-related narrowing is established, the patentee presumptively surrendered the disputed equivalent at the amended limitation. The patentee may rebut by establishing a concrete, objectively supported basis, anchored in the prosecution and considered in the infringement setting, that the relinquished scope did not encompass that variation. A successful rebuttal removes that estoppel barrier only; it proves neither technical equivalence nor infringement. A failed rebuttal forecloses the asserted equivalent at that limitation.

This scope presumption does not require the accused infringer first to prove that the particular equivalent was actually surrendered. That would collapse the distinction between trigger and scope. It also does not replace the separate purpose presumption in R2. Festo's qualified Carroll acknowledgment admits some abandoned matter while denying its relevance to this suit; it neither supplies every trigger nor conclusively rebuts a triggered scope presumption.

The new supplement expressly presents a dispute about confinement to the prosecution file. The refreshed commitment resolves that issue narrowly. Objective grounding in the prosecution is required, but the rule is not a universal exclusion of reliable explanatory or technical evidence merely because it is offered outside the contemporaneous file. Such material may illuminate the actual claim, prosecution and reason for amendment, particularly in an older record. It may not contradict a clear public concession, erase a claim term or substitute the inventor's uncommunicated wish for the issued instrument. This clarification follows the older-record qualification in Warner-Jenkinson and Markman's distinction between useful technical explanation and the legal meaning of a public claim.

The original phrase requiring a basis in the prosecution record must therefore be carried forward with this express limit. No closed-file evidentiary rule, automatic admission of every proffer, mandated hearing or guaranteed reopening is adopted. The lower courts apply ordinary procedure to the particular explanation. Alleged attorney error does not authorize hypothetical deletion of the sleeve limitation; a reliably supported nonpatentability reason still receives the consideration the governing rule requires. Mere assertion of mistake is not conclusive.

No closed exception list, automatic unforeseeability protection or finding about the availability of either accused substitute is supplied. The government proposal is advocacy supporting the intermediate path, not preexisting law. No current-case historical rationale supplies this commitment.

### R4. Amendment-specific application retained for ordinary review

**Stoll rings.** The written transfer and earlier sealing means are established. Festo's clarification account and asserted unchanged distinction from the references must be considered against SMC's claimed prior-art purpose. The disposition neither finds that the two-ring limitation was required by the examiner nor treats Festo's account as an established nonpatentability reason. Claim construction, actual narrowing and purpose remain distinct legal applications on that documented record.

**Stoll sleeve.** The incorporated magnetizable-material restriction is distinct from the cylinder tube and from the ring limitations. The inventor's alleged lack of intention cannot remove that claim term. Whether a sufficient nonpatentability explanation exists, or the applicable explanation burden and presumption establish a patentability reason, must be addressed at that limitation. Neither an unexplained-purpose characterization nor an overall section 112 response establishes complete surrender of all sleeve equivalents.

**Carroll rings.** The replacement combination, its explanatory remarks and the allowance's identified magnet/end-member/cushion relationship are established source contents. Festo's acknowledgment of some unrelated abandonment does not concede the single-ring equivalent. Its assertion that the reference already used two rings also does not alone decide the legal effect of placing a two-ring requirement in the replacement claim. The legal analysis must distinguish the amended combination's patentability purpose, the effect of the particular limitation and any rebuttal of the scope presumption.

These applications can be resolved on the existing record where sufficient. Disagreement between the parties does not itself require factfinding, and the Court imposes no assumption that all trigger issues must be retried. The court retains Markman's construction role and Warner-Jenkinson's legal-surrender sequence; remaining legally available disputed infringement facts retain ordinary factfinder treatment. No new appellate standard or universal allocation rule is announced.

### R5. Preservation and independent infringement grounds

The new trial excerpts do not establish a general abandonment of estoppel: SMC immediately disputed the judge's characterization and distinguished its arguments. They arose in the Stoll trial setting. They therefore do not establish a Carroll-specific waiver, preserve every Carroll issue, or displace the government's attributed report that Carroll estoppel was not argued at summary judgment.

Any dispositive preservation ruling must identify the exact defense, procedural act and governing preservation rule for the pertinent patent and stage. The court of appeals addresses a properly presented objection in ordinary sequence. No inference of omission from an unavailable source supplies a waiver. The separate all-elements objection, identified as unaddressed by the en banc court, is not decided by rejection of the complete bar. The no-bad-intent rule and each material claim limitation remain operative.

### R6. Retained judgment and the two material alternatives

All eight Associates retain vacatur of the en banc judgment and remand for application of R1–R5. The return is first to the court of appeals; district-court proceedings follow only as necessary under the corrected rule and ordinary procedure. The written amendment history is not open for factual rediscovery merely because the Court rejects an overbroad concession premise. Neither new evidence nor an additional opportunity to satisfy a previously applicable burden is guaranteed.

**Affirmance alternative:** SMC has a substantial argument from the replacement combinations, the sleeve's disputed explanation and the public's reliance on issued limits. That can establish estoppel under the correct inquiry. It does not justify the conclusive complete bar or turn counsel's qualified statements into a stipulation disposing of every predicate and asserted equivalent. The documented disputes do not supply a common independent ground to affirm the entire judgment without applying the corrected framework. All eight reject that disposition on this review.

**Reinstatement alternative:** Festo's record distinctions and objections to the complete bar support renewed legal review. They do not establish that no qualifying narrowing occurred, that every purpose explanation succeeds, that any scope presumption is rebutted, or that every preservation and all-elements objection fails. Its requested immediate reinstatement therefore goes beyond what the corrected legal rule and established source facts decide. All eight reject automatic restoration of either infringement judgment or damages.

The intermediate remand remains sufficient because the judgment rested on the rejected complete-bar rule and application of the corrected burden and scope rules belongs in the returned appellate proceeding. This is a chosen legal disposition, not an assertion that the complete historical record is unavailable or a demand for additional facts simply because the parties disagree. No patent is invalidated and no new liability, hearing or evidentiary right is created.
