## 69. United States v. Cotton — No. 01-687

**Reconciliation result:** Correct all eight provisional C3 commitments. Each Associate would reverse the Fourth Circuit's sentence-vacatur judgment and remand for proceedings consistent with rejection of this forfeited claim under Rule 52(b)'s fourth condition. The enhanced sentences receive no relief on this claim; the remand does not invite a fresh choice whether to correct the same omissions on the same record. The convictions remain intact. C1 and the distinct Rule 52(b) framework remain; seven affirmatively apply Johnson's intervening-law plainness rule and Scalia assumes plainness. All eight assume substantial-rights effect without resolving ordinary prejudice or structural classification.

### Governing components and historical test

**C1 — Power and charging protection.** Section 3231 gives the district court jurisdiction over this charged federal conspiracy. An omitted aggravating quantity allegation is an indictment defect, not withdrawal of subject-matter jurisdiction. Bain is overruled only to the extent it treats that defect as jurisdictional. The rule against substantive amendment without grand-jury resubmission, with its exception for changes of form, remains; neither the original indictment nor the judge's findings replace the superseding indictment. Total absence of a charging instrument is not presented.

**C2 — Error, forfeiture and separate conditions.** The operative indictment omitted the quantity necessary for the enhanced maximum, the jury was told quantity was immaterial, and it returned no quantity finding. Apprendi supplies genuine constitutional error; notice, exhibit stipulations and a preponderance finding do not supply an element admission or verdict. The unpreserved claim is forfeited rather than intentionally waived. Error, plainness, substantial-rights effect and the fourth condition remain separate. The present result assumes the third condition and does not resolve whether the grand-jury omission is structural.

**C3 — Particular fourth-condition decision.** On the supported record, the evidence that this conspiracy involved at least fifty grams of cocaine base is overwhelming and essentially uncontroverted at that threshold. Both sides report physical seizures totaling approximately 380 grams. The credibility and defendant-specific objections described in the packet concern larger Guidelines quantities and do not make that basic conspiracy threshold genuinely contestable on this record. The jury's conspiracy verdict, the threshold-specific physical evidence, and the distinction between the fifty-gram statutory threshold and larger sentencing quantities support refusing correction despite the actual missing jury determinations. The conclusion is an appellate judgment about forfeited relief, not a substituted conviction of an aggravated offense or an inferred personal admission by each respondent.

Neither the grand jury's independent role nor the verdict-based maximum is diminished: Johnson already distinguishes genuine deprivation of an element determination from entitlement to appellate correction of an unpreserved error. The institutional fairness inquiry here does not require the lower sentencing ceiling despite decisive proof of the threshold and the intervening change from settled trial law. This is not a universal rule that overwhelming proof defeats every constitutional claim. Preserved claims, materially disputed threshold evidence, or different trial-strategy circumstances require their own applicable analysis. No general defendant-specific quantity-attribution rule is adopted, and no prospective guilt determination, new hearing or new prosecution is directed.

**Why the provisional remand does not survive.** The historical Court, including every Associate now participating, addressed the same operative-indictment omission, absent jury quantity determination, sentencing objections, threshold evidence and forfeiture. Its final report expressly distinguished the 1.5-kilogram Guidelines dispute from the fifty-gram conspiracy threshold, 535 U.S. 625, 632–634 and note 3. That historical judicial assessment is not imported as an admission or as a neutral fact. The reconciled legal assessment rests on the already validated record and its expressly supported immediate fourth-condition path.

The simulated Johnson rule is narrower than a universal overwhelming-evidence exception, but the historical Cotton reasoning is also a particular fourth-condition application. The former therefore does not displace the latter's necessary premise. Simulated Apprendi protects an actual verdict or element admission, but it reserves appellate-remedy questions and involved a preserved claim; nothing in it makes forfeited relief automatic. Vonn concerns a distinct Rule 11 omission and an existing-record inquiry assigned to the court of appeals. Its choice to remand that case creates neither an appellate-first requirement nor a Justice-specific change in the treatment of this fully supported threshold. Generic caution about record assessment, or preference for giving the court of appeals another opportunity, supplies no material changed premise for any of the eight. Further review of the complete Cotton report, the final neutral record, and the exact Olano/Johnson/Apprendi/Vonn public law confirms correction rather than an unsupported departure.

## 71. Alabama v. Shelton — No. 00-1214

**Reconciliation result:** Preserve the independent five-to-three Associate division. Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm the invalidation of the suspended thirty-day jail term. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse that present invalidation and reserve the constitutional sufficiency of any later activation procedure. All preserve the conviction and the independent monetary punishment affirmed below. No valid waiver is found.

### Components and mandatory limits

**S1 — Present suspended imprisonment.** The five would require counsel, absent a knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver, at the trial establishing guilt when the resulting suspended prison sentence can be activated for that underlying offense. They distinguish actual selection of a jail term from mere statutory authorization of imprisonment. Scott's fine-only option remains. The three instead treat actual deprivation of liberty as the constitutional attachment point and would not invalidate entry of the conditional sentence solely because it might later lead to imprisonment.

**S2 — Waiver and later proceedings.** Capacity, familiarity with paid counsel and competent self-representation are not an informed waiver of appointed counsel. Godinez does not collapse those questions. Ordinary Alabama revocation does not retry guilt; the five hold that its actual safeguards cannot cure the lost trial representation. The three do not approve ordinary revocation as a cure; they reserve whether sufficient safeguards would accompany execution. Gagnon's separate due-process inquiry at revocation does not itself provide the original trial right.

**S3 — Relief and probation.** The five affirm the suspended-jail vacatur; the three would reverse only that present constitutional invalidation. The State's representation that the linked probation falls with the suspended term is preserved as its state-law account, not an independent express command attributed to the judgment below. The legality of freestanding probation is not decided. The late proposed state-law alternative of probation enforceable through counseled contempt remains for initial state-court consideration rather than a federal holding on the undeveloped theory.

**S4 — The complete-retrial qualification.** Neither position forbids placing an uncounseled defendant on probation with incarceration conditioned on a guilty verdict after a complete counseled trial de novo. The five recognize that arrangement as the functional equivalent of permissible pretrial probation; no such procedure is found or ordered in Shelton's case. Whether some safeguards short of complete retrial could satisfy the Sixth Amendment remains open; the five express the concern that later representation cannot ordinarily reconstruct the lost critical guilt stage, but decide only that Alabama's actual minimal procedures are insufficient. The three rely on the possibility of constitutionally sufficient future process to reserve the activation question; they do not find such a procedure already available. A complete-retrial exception, an unresolved lesser-safeguards question and an undecided probation-alone question are three different limits.

S4 makes explicit the material qualification in the complete historical majority and dissent, 535 U.S. 654, 668 note 5, 674–681. It does not change the selected result or add a record fact. The frozen language disavowing an invented retrial route is retained; it must not be expanded into a prohibition on every possible later counseled proceeding. The Court's refusal to entertain the late request for counsel whenever imprisonment is authorized remains a separate preservation limit.

## 72. Brown v. Gilmore — reviewed judgment 258 F.3d 265

**Reconciliation result:** Preserve eight Associate commitments to affirm rejection of the facial challenge, with direct joins in B1–B3 below. There is no historical Supreme Court merits alignment. The Fourth Circuit's two-to-one result cannot become a Supreme Court poll, and its historical three-prong Lemon method does not supply the governing law.

### Governing components and lower-court comparison

**B1 — Actual legal inquiry.** Apply Lemon as modified by simulated Agostini: the applicable specific prohibition or distinct doctrine; an established permissible relationship where appropriate; otherwise governmental attribution and function. Purpose, effect and institutional relationships remain evidence within that inquiry rather than three independently dispositive prongs. Chaudhuri's unanimous genuine-silence rule supplies a relevant permitted relationship; the compulsory school setting and younger audience still require examination under the official-worship and independent-pressure rules of Lee and Santa Fe. Good News Club's after-school private-use setting is not a substitute for that examination.

**B2 — Statute and supported operation.** The statute requires a daily interval of seated silence without distracting displays, allowing each pupil to meditate, pray or pursue another silent activity compatible with others' choice. It neither supplies devotional content nor requires a pupil to choose, disclose or profess a belief. Administrative instructions against teacher recommendations to pray, audible devotion and student religious coercion are evidence of operation, not added statutory words. The official conduct on this record is provision and maintenance of silence, with private mental choice.

The prayer-related preamble, retained prayer reference, mandatory statewide amendment, legislative statements, contemporaneous resolution and Attorney General defense provision are relevant context. They do not establish a governmental devotional function merely because supporters sought to encourage religion. A secular objective could not save an actual official exercise of worship; the supported text and operation here establish no such exercise. The Attorney General's mandatory duty to intervene and defend operates on its enacted terms but directs litigation rather than religious profession. The disputed admissibility of press quotations need not be resolved.

**B3 — Pressure and limit of affirmance.** The compelled silence requires neither participation nor apparent participation in an identified religious observance. Evidence that some religions use movement or audible worship does not establish a command to adopt seated silent prayer or a denominational classification; no child is required to pray. The record does not establish inherent substantial governmental religious pressure in this private-thought interval. Completed coercion is not a prerequisite when the official program itself prescribes worship; this program has not been shown to do so. Teacher-led devotion, religious direction or coercive implementation remains reviewable. No distinct free-exercise claim or general constitutional immunity for silence statutes is decided.

King's full dissent presents the strongest counterargument: mandatory observance, young pupils and prayer-oriented history may reveal religious solicitation beneath neutral wording; accommodation may selectively benefit seated silent worship; compulsory school observance differs from private after-school access. The eight have considered that mechanism and context, rather than merely requiring an incident of completed prayer. Under actual Agostini, an independent-purpose conclusion alone does not invalidate the statute, and the permitted unrestricted mental choice, absence of an official religious text or speaker, and safeguards against direction answer the asserted devotional function on this facial record. The lower majority's historical purpose/effect/entanglement rationale is therefore replaced by the actual governing framework, even though the disposition is the same.
