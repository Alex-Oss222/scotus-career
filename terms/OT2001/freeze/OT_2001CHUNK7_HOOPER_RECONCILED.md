## Operative statutory rule

Under §853(n)(6)(A), a third-party claimant must establish by a preponderance of the evidence a legal right, title or interest that invalidates forfeiture in whole or part because, at the time of the acts giving rise to forfeiture, the interest was vested in that claimant rather than the defendant or was superior to the defendant's right, title or interest. The temporal requirement applies to both the vested-interest and superior-interest alternatives. Assuming the spouses' community interests exist under California law, interests arising only through receipt of the proceeds produced by the offense satisfy neither alternative on this record.

Section 853(n)(6)(B) separately protects a bona fide purchaser for value of the relevant right, title or interest who, at purchase, was reasonably without cause to believe the property subject to forfeiture. That is a separate route, not a free-standing innocence exception and not merely the timing test of subsection (A). No purchaser-for-value claim is asserted here. When a claimant proves either statutory route, amendment of the forfeiture order to reflect that determination is mandatory, not discretionary.

Section 853(a)(1) expressly includes proceeds obtained directly or indirectly from the offense and property derived from those proceeds. Section 853(c)'s relation-back operation supports priority after forfeiture is lawfully adjudicated, subject to the actual third-party protections. It does not itself adjudicate the spouses' claims or excuse the ancillary inquiry. The state-law interest may be assumed without deciding the district court's disputed state-law interpretation because the federal statutory requirement independently defeats these claims.

The spouses' strongest contrary argument is that immediate community vesting and innocent ownership should protect their half interests. But the asserted interest arises with the very proceeds produced by the crime. It is not an independently existing lawful asset exposed to another person's later wrongdoing. Marriage and lack of culpability do not supply the missing temporal or purchaser predicate. Conversely, the husbands' crimes do not automatically expose all property belonging to their wives to forfeiture. The protected legitimate-property interests below remain undisturbed.

## Precedent treatment

Buena Vista remains governing at its actual simulated scope: the former civil statute permits a donee to invoke its owner defense, subject to proving the defense, and relation back does not eliminate that opportunity before lawful forfeiture. The decision expressly distinguishes criminal forfeiture. Hooper neither withdraws that civil eligibility nor imports it into §853's different third-party categories. The difference in statutes answers the asserted inconsistency; no ruling about retroactive application of a later civil amendment is needed.

Bennis remains controlling for a separately held interest in an ordinarily lawful automobile used by another when the owner establishes nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention. The disputed proceeds do not fall within that protected category. The Justices who joined that rule—Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer—therefore retain it while joining this statutory affirmance. The other four apply its binding scope without enlarging it. No new comprehensive rule governing innocent owners, instrumentalities or every constitutional forfeiture claim is announced.

Lester's lower-court treatment of untainted substitute property is distinguished from proceeds. Current Bollin independently requires statutory authority for the particular property and order: potential substitution did not authorize the continuing §853(e) restraint there, whereas §853(a)(1) expressly covers these proved proceeds. Federal supremacy implements a valid statutory command; it cannot supply missing authority. No substitute-property order is adjudicated here.

Current Farmer requires meaningful focused review of a supported claim that assets may be legitimate and needed for counsel, without authorizing use of proved forfeitable proceeds. The present spouses used an ancillary hearing and present a statutory priority claim. No unraised process or counsel claim is decided. Scalia's distinct Farmer explanation does not become an eight-Justice balancing rationale here. Craft preserves the distinction between actual state-law rights and their federal consequence; neither its majority nor its dissent creates a general marital exemption.
