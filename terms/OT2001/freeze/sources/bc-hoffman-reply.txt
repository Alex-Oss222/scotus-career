INTRODUCTION 


Jose Castro committed at least four criminal acts during his 
extended illegal stay in the United States. First, he entered 
this country illegally in violation of the INA. 8 U.S.C. § 1325. 
Second, he obtained employment at Hoffman by presenting 
fraudulent immigration documents in violation of the INA 
and IRCA. 18 U.S.C. § 1546; 8 US.C. § 1324c. Third, he 
committed perjury in violation of 18 U.S.C. § 1621 by lying 
under oath about his identity during the NLRB’s compliance 
proceeding. See, ABF Freight Sys., Inc. v. NLRB, 510 US. 317, 
328-29 (1994). Fourth, he committed a continuing violation 
of the INA by remaining in this country illegally during his 

at Hoffman and for at least four years 
thereafter. Montero v. INS, 124 F.3d 381, 385 (2d Cir. 1997). 
And, finally, the record indicates that Castro compounded 
his prior illegal conduct by continuing to use fraudulent 
documents to obtain employment during the backpay 
period. See infra pp. 14-15. Against this backdrop of 
repeated criminal misconduct, this Court must consider 
whether Hoffman's violation of the NLRA, a civil statute not 
punitive in nature, justifies an award of backpay to an 
undocumented alien who never was lawfully present or 
lawfully entitled to work in this country during the backpay 
period. Under Sure-Tan v. NLRB, 467 U.S. 883 (1984), and 
this country’s immigration laws and policies, backpay must 
be denied and the decision of the D.C. Circuit must be 
reversed. 


The Board and amici adopt a series of false premises to 
convince this Court to affirm the D.C. Circuit's erroneous 
decision. They contend that Sure-Jan denies backpay only

The INA controls immigration by making unauthorized 
entry into this country illegal and by authorizing entry for 

of employment only in specific circumstances. 
8US.C. §1182(a)(5) (formerly 8 US.C. § 1182(a)(14)); 
8 U.S.C. § 1101(a)(15); 8 U.S.C. § 1325. For instance, a tourist 
may legally enter this country on a tourist visa for pleasure 
for a temporary period of time. But, the tourist visa does not 
permit the visa holder to engage in employmer* during the 
holder's stay. 8 U.S.C. § 1182(a)(5); 8 US.C. 1. '1(a)(15)(B); 
see also, 8 C.F.R. § 274a.12 (list of classes of aliens authorized 
to work). Rather, to legally enter this country and perform 
work, the INA has long required an alien to hold an 
immigrant or nonimmigrant visa that specifically authorizes
