## 69. United States v. Cotton

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit judgment: the omission is nonjurisdictional, and this forfeited error does not satisfy the fourth requirement for plain-error correction.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand for further proceedings consistent with rejection of the stated vacatur grounds. Leave the enhanced sentences undisturbed on this claim; do not treat the decision as a new jury finding, conviction, or validation of a preserved-error procedure.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Nonjurisdictional error and bounded appellate consequence

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach jurisdiction first, then the forfeited constitutional sentencing claim. Treat the omission as error and assume the substantial-rights requirement without turning that assumption into a finding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the charging defect destroys judicial power and, if not, whether this record requires discretionary correction.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Apprendi, Olano, and Johnson. Propose clarification that contrary jurisdictional language in Ex parte Bain does not survive for an element omission in a federal prosecution otherwise within statutory jurisdiction.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Omission of the aggravating quantity does not eliminate subject-matter jurisdiction. Although the enhanced sentences lack the necessary jury authorization, an unpreserved error supported by overwhelming and uncontroverted threshold-quantity evidence need not be corrected under Rule 52(b)'s fourth requirement on this record. The error remains constitutional error.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule concerns a forfeited element omission and this evidentiary setting. It does not authorize sentencing judges to disregard Apprendi or reduce every constitutional defect to a prediction of guilt.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Subject-matter jurisdiction comes from the statutory criminal grant, not flawless pleading; Stone would overrule Ex parte Bain only to the extent it makes this element omission jurisdictional. That correction preserves the grand jury's constitutional role while putting remedial review under Rule 52(b), where settled doctrine locates forfeited errors. Johnson's 1997 judgment is a close analogy, not a universal overwhelming-evidence exception. Here the record of the conspiracy, the threshold quantities, notice of the enhanced exposure and absence of a material quantity contest support the conclusion that this unpreserved error does not seriously impair the fairness, integrity or public reputation of the proceeding. Those actual circumstances are decisive under the fourth condition; no substitute jury finding is made and substantial-rights impact remains assumed. Preserved errors retain Neder's distinct actual-verdict inquiry. Neither a changed legal classification nor this remedy legitimizes future omission of a required element.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Preserved error, contested quantity, intentional waiver, and an entirely missing indictment are outside this record.

## 70. Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission of Maryland / United States v. Public Service Commission of Maryland

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand because ordinary federal-question jurisdiction and the prospective officer-relief route permit the challenge to proceed.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Reinstate adjudication of the federal-law claim against the responsible commissioners. Determine the merits and shape any injunction only after adjudication. Do not order payment between carriers, damages from Maryland, or a blanket waiver by the commission.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A forum for the alleged ongoing federal violation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Decide jurisdiction and the available officer-relief route. Do not adjudicate the underlying compensation question or direct commission waiver where unnecessary.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether this substantial federal claim can be heard and prospectively remedied despite the State’s immunity objection.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinction among jurisdiction, statutory authority, consent, and remedy; propose the specific telecommunications review holding left open by prior holdings.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The Telecommunications Act does not exclude general federal-question jurisdiction over this claim. A suit to prevent commissioners' continuing enforcement of an order alleged to violate federal law may proceed against those officers for genuinely prospective relief. The intercarrier financial consequences do not make it a damages action against the State.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule applies to a substantial federal challenge and responsible officers enforcing an ongoing order. It does not federalize every contract dispute or supply a cause against every state agency.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 1331 supplies jurisdiction over the substantial federal claim unless Congress displaces it; §252's review provision does not do so. Ex parte Young permits relief against officers enforcing an allegedly unlawful ongoing order, and Idaho v. Coeur d'Alene's distinct sovereign-title setting does not convert intercarrier payments into state-treasury damages. Statutory jurisdiction and the prospective character of relief are decisive, with each source doing its separate work. Stone thus answers that the complaint may proceed without relying on constructive consent from participation or adjudicating the compensation merits. Because those sufficient grounds dispose of the barrier below, a broader construction of §252(e)(6) and direct commission waiver would add no necessary relief. The lower court must decide federal compliance and tailor any injunction, not presume Verizon wins the underlying dispute.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Direct commission waiver is unnecessary to the disposition. The correct treatment of Internet-bound traffic and the exact injunction depend on merits issues not resolved at this threshold stage.

## 73. Mathias v. WorldCom Technologies, Inc.

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Dismiss the writ of certiorari as improvidently granted because petitioners seek review only of nonbinding reasoning in a judgment favorable to them.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the Seventh Circuit judgment undisturbed without approving its unnecessary findings. Order no new injunction, damages, agency proceeding, or remand to manufacture adversity.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: No advisory correction of nonbinding reasoning

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Resolve appellate aggrievement first. Do not reach the statutory or immunity merits because the petitioners prevailed and the disputed findings have no established binding consequence.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a favorable judgment can be reviewed solely because its unnecessary reasoning is unwelcome.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the favorable-judgment rule of New York Telephone Co. v. Maltbie; do not reach the telecommunications questions or announce that all prevailing-party appeals are constitutionally barred.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A party that received a favorable judgment and identifies only unnecessary, nonbinding adverse findings is not entitled to appellate review for the purpose of revising those findings. The importance of the underlying issue does not supply an adverse judgment.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers this request to edit reasoning. It does not foreclose a prevailing party's challenge to an actual adverse judgment component or independently binding collateral effect.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** New York Telephone's favorable-judgment rule resolves the petition. These commissioners obtained the operative judgment they sought and identify only unnecessary findings lacking binding effect in later litigation. Stone applies that threshold rule and dismisses the writ as improvidently granted, rather than pronouncing an unnecessarily broad Article III prohibition on every appeal by a prevailing party. The judgment's legal consequences are decisive; disagreeable reasoning alone supplies no relief for this Court to grant. Deciding the three telecommunications questions here would add an advisory resolution to a case that does not require one. A genuinely adverse component or binding collateral consequence would present a materially different appellate question. The lower judgment remains undisturbed, and its unnecessary reasoning receives no endorsement.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** All three substantive questions are unnecessary once this threshold defect is resolved. No opinion on their merits is proposed here.
