## 50. Barnhart v. Walton — duration, entitlement and trial work

### Record and reviewed judgment

The complete December 18, 2000 Fourth Circuit opinion, 235 F.3d 184, supports a more precise sequence than the intake's compressed account. Walton applied for disability insurance benefits and supplemental security income on April 12, 1995, alleging a schizophrenic disorder with depression. The amended onset date was October 31, 1994. He began part-time work in May 1995 and full-time work on December 10, 1995. After the first hearing in July 1996, the ALJ initially awarded a closed period ending with full-time work. On administrative remand, the ALJ found substantial gainful activity in October 1995 and denied benefits because inability to perform that activity did not persist for twelve months. The circuit affirmed the substantial-gainful-activity finding, but reversed the duration and trial-work rulings and remanded.

The Commissioner did not dispute that the impairment itself lasted at least twelve months. The circuit reasoned that the duration clause modifies impairment alone; entitlement could begin after the five-month waiting period without a prior award; trial-work protection then prevented use of the October 1995 activity to show cessation. Independently, it regarded the agency construction as unreasonable even if the text were ambiguous. Both grounds require attention. The circuit's conclusion about entitlement is its disputed statutory holding, not a fact that neutral preflight may assume. The October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding is the affirmed factual component; the present questions do not authorize recalculating it from later earnings thresholds.

### Complete statutory and regulatory setting

The exact source excerpts supply §423(a)(1), the waiting-period provision in §423(c)(2), the disability definition in §423(d)(1), and §422(c)'s trial-work provisions. Entitlement, payment after the waiting period, medical impairment, functional inability, duration, adjudication and trial-work protection remain separate concepts. Section 422(c)(2) addresses whether disability has ceased during a trial-work period; it does not expressly state that every initial eligibility condition is satisfied by applying for benefits. Conversely, §422(c)(3) ties the beginning of trial work to entitlement, so entitlement cannot simply be replaced with the date the agency mails a favorable award without construing the statute.

The SSI adult definition in §1382c(a)(3)(A) is separately supplied with its child-definition exception and neighboring severity provisions. Walton is an adult; the child exception is not his eligibility rule. Trial work under §422(c) is a Title II provision. ### Issue 1: An informed observer and mistakenly attributed advocacy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Liteky v. United States supplies a single objective appearance standard: an informed, detached observer considers relevant conduct and circumstances together. Judicial origin is neither a categorical bar nor an added exceptional-intensity requirement; outside origin is neither necessary nor sufficient. Actual bias need not be proved.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The operative standard asks whether the full supported circumstances reasonably call impartiality into question. It protects ordinary adjudicative conduct without shielding actual partiality and preserves §455(a)’s independence from §455(b).
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 455(a) requires disqualification when impartiality might reasonably be questioned. Section 455(b)’s separate grounds are not substitutes for the appearance inquiry.
* **Material Other Authority:** Liljeberg v. Health Services Acquisition Corp. supports an objectively informed inquiry and preserves separate remedial considerations for an established violation. It does not require observers to disregard facts correcting an apparent association.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing rule already decides the proper observer and information set. Application to a mistaken, unauthorized organizational attribution requires no change to the standard.
* **Open Point:** Whether the full circumstances, including continuing executive-committee membership alongside mistaken presidential attribution and no preparation or approval, provide an objectively reasonable basis to doubt impartiality under the settled rule.

## C. Decision-material positions and corrected information set

The circuit treated its related-case recusal ruling as controlling. Defendants rely on an association with advocacy concerning similar defendants and issues; Sao Paulo relies on the corrected attribution and absence of actual participation in preparing or approving the brief. The correction does not eliminate all organizational connection: executive-committee membership is an established contemporaneous role that an informed observer must know. It is equally impermissible to convert that role into a finding that the judge wrote, approved, authorized, supervised or knowingly adopted this submission when the supplied factual extract expressly records nonparticipation and vague awareness.

The source thus defeats two categorical shortcuts. The judge had not ceased every organizational role by the time of filing, and membership in the executive committee does not itself establish his personal preparation or approval of the brief. Defendants need not prove subjective hostility to present an appearance claim. Sao Paulo need not treat a mistaken name on a motion as genuine authorship. The observer considers all supported circumstances together under Liteky and Microsoft, rather than either the mistaken filing alone or the judge's personal assurance of fairness alone.

## D. Lawful paths and precise limits

Reversal could conclude that the erroneous presidential attribution, continuing committee membership, lack of preparation or approval, and vague awareness, evaluated together, do not reasonably call impartiality into question. It must address the real continuing role rather than rest on a nonexistent total separation from the association. Affirmance could conclude that the contemporaneous leadership association, evaluated with those same limitations, nevertheless creates an objectively reasonable appearance concern. It must explain why that concern survives the actual nonparticipation and correction rather than impute unsupported advocacy.

A factual remand requires a genuine unresolved material fact. The existence of executive-committee membership is now supplied; it is not an invented hypothetical or a reason to presume undisclosed work on the brief. The record does not establish the committee's responsibilities for amicus filings, a personal tobacco-litigation practice, an attorney-client relationship in the earlier case, a financial interest, or an independently triggered §455(b) ground. Those are not findings available from this source. Their absence from the supplied extract is also not affirmative proof of their opposite. No such additional fact is needed merely to apply the objective rule to the established circumstances.

Violation and remedy remain distinct. The three Liljeberg interests concern injustice to the parties in this case, injustice in other cases, and undermining public confidence in the judicial process; Microsoft supplies the current copied application. No merits determination concerning tobacco liability or foreign-government damages follows from this recusal question. Neither result requires a categorical rule for every former officer, every current association member, or every prior client.
