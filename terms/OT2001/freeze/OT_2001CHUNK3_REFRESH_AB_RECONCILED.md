## 35. Raygor v. Regents of University of Minnesota — February 27, 2002

### Components, supported facts and entering law

Section 1257 reaches Minnesota's final limitations judgment. The federal dismissal of July 11, 1997 remains final after withdrawal of the appeal. Kimel does not reopen it. The employees asserted related state discrimination claims with ADEA claims in 1996; the Regents expressly asserted immunity in their answers. Discovery, mediation and agreement on a schedule do not negate that express reservation. State refiling was approximately three weeks after dismissal. These dates support deciding statutory coverage, not a final day-by-day timeliness calculation.

The controlling question is whether section 1367(d)'s protection for a claim asserted under subsection (a) includes a supplemental state-law claim against a nonconsenting State dismissed on immunity grounds. An independently authorized federal ADEA claim, immunity from federal adjudication of a state-law claim, and a limitations rule for a competent state court are distinct.

The operative register provisions are **Express federal ADEA actions against state employers**, effective January 11, 2000 (Kimel; general Article I operation June 23, 1999), and **Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim**, effective June 22, 1998 (Schacht). Kimel supplies the authorized federal claim; Schacht prevents immunity on one claim from erasing the entire action's independent federal jurisdiction. Florida Prepaid requires power and unmistakable authorization separately. College Savings Bank supplies no constructive consent from ordinary activity. Pennhurst preserves the nonconsenting State's state-law federal-forum protection. Vermont Agency preserves a bounded statutory-authorization inquiry even when Article I power exists. Alden distinguishes review of a new state judgment from reopening a terminated federal proceeding.

Subsection (a) requires an action within original jurisdiction and a same-case-or-controversy relationship. Subsection (b)'s diversity-only limitations do not govern this ADEA action. Subsection (c) permits declining jurisdiction for a novel or complex state-law issue, substantial predominance, dismissal of all original-jurisdiction claims, or exceptional circumstances with other compelling reasons; immunity is not converted into a discretionary subsection (c) ground. Subsection (d) protects the relevant claim while pending and for thirty days after dismissal unless state law provides longer tolling; its separate category of other voluntarily dismissed same-action claims is not the basis of these involuntary dismissals.

### Two lawful paths and strongest objections

**R1 — exclusion and affirmance.** Read subsection (d)'s cross-reference as preserving claims eligible for subsection (a)'s supplemental adjudicative grant, rather than independently extending a State's exposure through claims that grant does not unmistakably authorize against it. Generic all-other-claims language does not overcome the sovereign-defendant restriction. The strongest objection is that asserted describes filing, not adjudicability, and state-forum tolling neither orders federal adjudication nor creates a new cause. The R1 answer is limited: Congress linked this protection to subsection (a), and the state-law claim's immunity remains intact despite the federal claim's valid jurisdiction. Neither a general Article I prohibition nor the false proposition that the whole action lacked federal jurisdiction is necessary.

**R2 — coverage and reversal/remand.** Read asserted under subsection (a) to encompass the related state claim actually brought alongside a competent federal claim, even though immunity ultimately prevents its adjudication. The enacted protection preserves a competent state forum; it does not abrogate federal-forum immunity. The strongest objection is that a claimant cannot obtain a statutory consequence merely by asserting an unauthorized State-defendant claim, and state limitations can define the State's consent. The R2 answer distinguishes newly authorized adjudication or damages from a procedural consequence attached to a related claim in an independently proper federal case. Congress expressly specified pending-period and postdismissal protection without an immunity-dismissal exclusion. No State consent to federal adjudication is inferred.

**Bounded correction path.** Vacatur solely to correct the mistaken whole-action ADEA premise is lawful if that classification is indispensable to the state judgment. It is not necessary for a Justice who resolves the preserved state-claim coverage question independently under R1 or R2. No modeled Justice remands merely to reconsider a proposition the present statutory ruling resolves.

## 37. New York v. FERC / Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC — March 4, 2002

### Separate components, statutory limits and agency grounds

New York challenges Order 888's coverage of independently provided interstate transmission used for retail energy sales. Enron seeks extension to the transmission component of bundled retail service. Review is of the D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining the order. The Court does not prescribe a tariff, classify a particular facility or decide a particular retail rate.

The two section 201(b)(1) grants concern interstate transmission and wholesale interstate sales. The wholesale qualification attaches to sales, and the exclusion of other sales does not erase the separate transmission grant. The facilities exclusions for generation, local distribution, intrastate-only transmission and wholly self-consumed energy retain their stated specific-authority qualifications. Section 201(a)'s policy text must be read with those operative grants, not made an independent repeal of them. Section 201(b)(2) does not turn compliance with a section 210 or 211 order into general public-utility jurisdiction; section 201(f)'s governmental exclusions also remain.

Sections 205(a)–(c) require lawful rates, nondiscrimination and filed schedules for jurisdictional services. Under section 206(a), a requisite hearing and finding that a jurisdictional rate or affecting practice is unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or preferential requires a lawful prospective determination and order. Section 206(b) puts the unlawfulness burden on the Commission or complainant. A remedy choice is not permission to leave proved discrimination uncorrected.

Section 212(e) preserves other authority subject to its actual exceptions. Section 212(g) prohibits an order inconsistent with state retail-marketing-area law. Section 212(h) prohibits conditioning an order on, or requiring, direct transmission to an ultimate consumer. Its distinct resale-entity rule requires both membership in the specified public, rural-financed or independently public-service-obligated classes (or their qualifying wholly owned entities) and service to that customer on October 24, 1992 or use of owned/controlled transmission or distribution facilities to deliver all the energy. Contract alone does not create the listed public-service obligation. The final sentence preserves state and local authority. No unidentified arrangement is found to meet those exceptions.

The actual discernible grounds are separate. Order 888 found discriminatory wholesale access and prescribed functional unbundling: equal tariff terms for the utility's own wholesale transactions, separate generation/transmission/ancillary-service rates, and equal use of electronic transmission information. It retained state local-distribution, siting, reliability and retail-service functions, with its indicator procedure and attention to state recommendations. For bundled retail service, the order characterized the delivered transaction as a state-regulated retail sale and also found compulsory retail unbundling unnecessary to remedy the established discrimination. The federal brief's assertion that Enron supplied inadequate support is advocacy, not a separate finding. No fictional discretionary rationale replaces the order's own reasons.

Relevant register provisions are **Limited modification authority and mandatory tariff filing** (MCI, June 17, 1994), **Medicare corrective adjustments and permissible interpretive change** (Good Samaritan, June 7, 1993; Smiley's qualified interpretive operation June 3, 1996), and **Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review** (Haggar, April 21, 1999; Mead, June 18, 2001). Iowa Utilities Board distinguishes enacted federal assignments from independently operative restrictions. Gulf Power, January 16, 2002, separates coverage from mandatory rate formulas and preserves actual classifications. Sigmon Coal, February 19, and Blumer, February 20, require statutory fit and actual agency reasons; neither invents a universal narrow-delegation rule. These are method and boundary authorities, not prior FPA adjudications.

**E1 — affirm the limited remedy on the independent agency finding.** The order's reported finding that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary to eliminate the discrimination it found is an actual agency ground, not counsel's invention. **E2 — vacate the bundled component and remand for a lawful agency explanation.** The unqualified interstate-transmission grant cannot be avoided merely by selling energy and transmission for one charge. This is not a judicial finding that every bundled practice is discriminatory, that Enron satisfied every burden, or that compulsory unbundling must issue. A remand for corrected analysis is narrower than an order dictating Enron's tariff.

§ 1367. Supplemental jurisdiction
(a) Except as provided in subsections (b) and (c) or as expressly provided otherwise by Federal statute, in any civil action of which the district courts have original jurisdiction, the district courts shall have supplemental jurisdiction over all other claims that are so related to claims in the action within such original jurisdiction that they form part of the same case or controversy under Article III of the United States Constitution. Such supplemental jurisdiction shall include claims that involve the joinder or intervention of additional parties.
(b) In any civil action of which the district courts have original jurisdiction founded solely on section 1332 of this title, the district courts shall not have supplemental jurisdiction under subsection (a) over claims by plaintiffs against persons made parties under Rule 14, 19, 20, or 24 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, or over claims by persons proposed to be joined as plaintiffs under Rule 19 of such rules, or seeking to intervene as plaintiffs under Rule 24 of such rules, when exercising supplemental jurisdiction over such claims would be inconsistent with the jurisdictional requirements of section 1332.
(c) The district courts may decline to exercise supplemental jurisdiction over a claim under subsection (a) if—
(1) the claim raises a novel or complex issue of State law,
(2) the claim substantially predominates over the claim or claims over which the district court has original jurisdiction,
(3) the district court has dismissed all claims over which it has original jurisdiction, or
(4) in exceptional circumstances, there are other compelling reasons for declining jurisdiction.
(d) The period of limitations for any claim asserted under subsection (a), and for any other claim in the same action that is voluntarily dismissed at the same time as or after the dismissal of the claim under subsection (a), shall be tolled while the claim is pending and for a period of 30 days after it is dismissed unless State law provides for a longer tolling period.
(e) As used in this section, the term ‘‘State’’ includes the District of Columbia, the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico, and any territory or possession of the United States.


## 16 U.S.C. 824(a)-(f) (2000); FPA 201

§ 824. Declaration of policy; application of subchapter
(a) Federal regulation of transmission and sale of electric energy
It is declared that the business of transmitting and selling electric energy for ultimate distribution to the public is affected with a public interest, and that Federal regulation of matters relating to generation to the extent provided in this subchapter and subchapter III of this chapter and of that part of such business which consists of the transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce and the sale of such energy at wholesale in interstate commerce is necessary in the public interest, such Federal regulation, however, to extend only to those matters which are not subject to regulation by the States.
(b) Use or sale of electric energy in interstate commerce
(1) The provisions of this subchapter shall apply to the transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce and to the sale of electric energy at wholesale in interstate commerce, but except as provided in paragraph (2) shall not apply to any other sale of electric energy or deprive a State or State commission of its lawful authority now exercised over the exportation of hydroelectric energy which is transmitted across a State line. The Commission shall have jurisdiction over all facilities for such transmission or sale of electric energy, but shall not have jurisdiction, except as specifically provided in this subchapter and subchapter III of this chapter, over facilities used for the generation of electric energy or over facilities used in local distribution or only for the transmission of electric energy in intrastate commerce, or over facilities for the transmission of electric energy consumed wholly by the transmitter.
(2) The provisions of sections 824i, 824j, and 824k of this title shall apply to the entities de-

## 16 U.S.C. 824d(a)-(c) (2000); FPA 205

§ 824d. Rates and charges; schedules; suspension of new rates; automatic adjustment clauses
(a) Just and reasonable rates
All rates and charges made, demanded, or received by any public utility for or in connection with the transmission or sale of electric energy subject to the jurisdiction of the Commission, and all rules and regulations affecting or pertaining to such rates or charges shall be just and reasonable, and any such rate or charge that is not just and reasonable is hereby declared to be unlawful.
(b) Preference or advantage unlawful
No public utility shall, with respect to any transmission or sale subject to the jurisdiction of the Commission, (1) make or grant any undue preference or advantage to any person or subject any person to any undue prejudice or disadvantage, or (2) maintain any unreasonable difference in rates, charges, service, facilities, or in any other respect, either as between localities or as between classes of service.
(c) Schedules
Under such rules and regulations as the Commission may prescribe, every public utility shall file with the Commission, within such time and in such form as the Commission may designate, and shall keep open in convenient form and place for public inspection schedules showing all rates and charges for any transmission or sale subject to the jurisdiction of the Commission, and the classifications, practices, and regulations affecting such rates and charges, together with all contracts which in any manner affect or relate to such rates, charges, classifications, and services.


## 16 U.S.C. 824e(a)-(b) (2000); FPA 206

§ 824e. Power of Commission to fix rates and charges; determination of cost of production or transmission
(a) Unjust or preferential rates, etc.; statement of reasons for changes; hearing; specification of issues
Whenever the Commission, after a hearing had upon its own motion or upon complaint, shall find that any rate, charge, or classification, demanded, observed, charged, or collected by any public utility for any transmission or sale subject to the jurisdiction of the Commission, or that any rule, regulation, practice, or contract affected such rate, charge, or classification is unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or preferential, the Commission shall determine the just and reasonable rate, charge, classification, rule, regulation, practice, or contract to be thereafter observed and in force, and shall fix the same by order. Any complaint or motion of the Commission to initiate a proceeding under this section shall state the change or changes to be made in the rate, charge, classification, rule, regulation, practice, or contract then in force, and the reasons for any proposed change or changes therein. If, after review of any motion or complaint and answer, the Commission shall decide to hold a hearing, it shall fix by order the time and place of such hearing and shall specify the issues to be adjudicated.
(b) Refund effective date; preferential proceedings; statement of reasons for delay; burden of proof; scope of refund order; refund orders in cases of dilatory behavior; interest
Whenever the Commission institutes a proceeding under this section, the Commission shall establish a refund effective date. In the case of a proceeding instituted on complaint, the refund effective date shall not be earlier than the date 60 days after the filing of such complaint nor later than 5 months after the expiration of such 60-day period. In the case of a proceeding instituted by the Commission on its own motion, the refund effective date shall not be earlier than the date 60 days after the publication by the Commission of notice of its intention to initiate such proceeding nor later than 5 months after the expiration of such 60-day period. Upon institution of a proceeding under this section, the Commission shall give to the decision of such proceeding the same preference as provided under section 824d of this title and otherwise act as speedily as possible. If no final decision is rendered by the refund effective date or by the conclusion of the 180-day period commencing upon initiation of a proceeding pursuant to this section, whichever is earlier, the Commission shall state the reasons why it has failed to do so and shall state its best estimate as to when it reasonably expects to make such decision. In any proceeding under this section, the burden of proof to show that any rate, charge, classification, rule, regulation, practice, or contract is unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory, or preferential shall be upon the

Commission or the complainant. At the conclusion of any proceeding under this section, the Commission may order the public utility to make refunds of any amounts paid, for the period subsequent to the refund effective date through a date fifteen months after such refund effective date, in excess of those which would have been paid under the just and reasonable rate, charge, classification, rule, regulation, practice, or contract which the Commission orders to be thereafter observed and in force: Provided, That if the proceeding is not concluded within fifteen months after the refund effective date and if the Commission determines at the conclusion of the proceeding that the proceeding was not resolved within the fifteen-month period primarily because of dilatory behavior by the public utility, the Commission may order refunds of any or all amounts paid for the period subsequent to the refund effective date and prior to the conclusion of the proceeding. The refunds shall be made, with interest, to those persons who have paid those rates or charges which are the subject of the proceeding.
