# Ruiz — form and compatibility before assignment

The argued merits review will issue one signed Opinion of the Court. Its connected but distinct questions are I, section 3742(a)(1)'s jurisdiction-determining inquiry; II, the asserted universal advance impeachment-disclosure duty; III, a substantive exculpatory disclosure floor and the affirmative-defense term in the presented agreement with the continuing factual-innocence promise; IV, protective measures and the bar on an irrevocable waiver of the floor; V, conditions on otherwise available bargaining benefits; and VI, application to the withdrawn recommendation, affirmance of the sentence vacatur and the remand inquiry. The form and proposition boundaries are fixed before the count or author selection. No same-day opinion supplies entering law.

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete confined rationale and affirmance. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy join Parts I and II and would reverse. Thomas would reverse on the separate trial-function ground and objects to drawing a constitutional line by the predicted degree of informational assistance; he joins no part. His agreement that the statutory basis may be examined, that no universal impeachment duty exists and that no accepted promise exists does not count him in the stage-specific and practical rationale. The Court will distinguish information's practical importance from a universal timing requirement, preserve actual informed choice and separate the affirmative-defense category from impeachment and factual innocence.

Stone-Zsela's approved position requires affirmance of the vacatur and limited sentencing remand because the blanket waiver is invalid to the extent it authorizes withholding known favorable information materially useful for investigation, counsel's advice or the plea decision. His prospective duty includes significant impeachment and affirmative-defense information, is confined to information known after reasonable case-team inquiry, and preserves genuine privilege and safety protections through arrangements compatible with meaningful choice. His retrospective relief requires its own causal and materiality showing. He supports section 3742 examination through the connected legal-error theory, not general review of discretionary leniency. His remedy requires consideration of the lost recommendation on a lawful basis, without an automatic reduction, compelled bargain or disturbance of the conviction. He distinguishes Mezzanatto's defined use of the defendant's own statements and rejects neither every limited informed waiver nor bargaining pressure categorically. He joins the floor opinion as the constitutional minimum, and his broader duty belongs in his published concurrence. No fallback is used.

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer hold the condition invalid to the extent it demands surrender of the floor; O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would sustain it at the presented scope. That produces a five-to-four affirmance while all nine accept the section 3742 inquiry and reject a universal impeachment duty. A court may determine whether the constitutional claim establishes a sentence imposed in violation of law; because this claim succeeds to the stated extent and is connected to the withdrawn recommendation, section 3742(a)(1) supports review. This is not assumed jurisdiction or general review of the understood discretionary refusal.

Only the admitted continuing factual-innocence disclosure promise is an operative contractual premise. The historical text's additional assertion of earlier production is not needed, adopted or treated as compliance. No particular withheld item, actual informed waiver of the rejected term, accepted bargain or established witness danger is inferred. The absence of a particular item limits the remedy and the questions decided; it is not a new innocence prerequisite or an alternative reason an unconstitutional condition could never injure.

The Chief is in the affirmance coalition and assigns. Eligible authors of the full opinion are Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy join only Parts I and II, and Thomas joins no part; none is eligible for the whole opinion. The actual all-Justice count, pool information and case-specific author selection await release after Harris and Ring and the intervening Trans Union Record. No assignment or Record is made here.
