# Ruiz — form and compatibility before assignment

The argued merits review will issue one signed lead opinion. Its connected but distinct questions are I, section 3742(a)(1)'s jurisdiction-determining inquiry; II, the asserted advance impeachment-disclosure duty; III, the affirmative-defense term in the presented agreement with the continuing factual-innocence promise; and IV, reversal of the unconstitutional-condition sentence vacatur. The form and proposition boundaries are fixed before a fresh count or author selection. No same-day opinion supplies entering law.

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the complete confined rationale and reversal. Thomas concurs in the judgment on the separate trial-function ground and objects to drawing a constitutional line by the predicted degree of informational assistance; he is not a full lead-opinion joiner. His agreement that the statutory basis may be examined and that no accepted promise exists does not count him in the seven-Justice stage-specific and practical rationale. The Court will distinguish information's practical importance from the constitutional timing requirement, preserve actual informed choice and separate the affirmative-defense category from impeachment and factual innocence.

Stone's approved position requires a dissent from the reversal. He would affirm the vacatur and limited sentencing remand because the blanket waiver is invalid to the extent it authorizes withholding known favorable information materially useful for investigation, counsel's advice or the plea decision. His prospective duty includes significant impeachment and affirmative-defense information, is confined to information known after reasonable case-team inquiry, and preserves genuine privilege and safety protections through arrangements compatible with meaningful choice. His retrospective relief requires its own causal and materiality showing. He supports section 3742 examination through the connected legal-error theory, not general review of discretionary leniency. His remedy requires consideration of the lost recommendation on a corrected basis, without an automatic reduction, compelled bargain or disturbance of the conviction. He distinguishes Mezzanatto's defined use of the defendant's own statements and rejects neither every limited informed waiver nor bargaining pressure categorically. All those qualifications belong in his published dissent summary. No fallback is used.

The majority and Thomas reject the asserted condition at the decided scope; Stone accepts its invalidity and the requested limited remand. That produces an eight-to-one reversal while preserving the seven-Justice complete rationale. A court may determine whether the constitutional claim establishes a sentence imposed in violation of law; after this claim fails, section 3742(a)(1) provides no review of the understood discretionary refusal on this theory. This is not assumed jurisdiction or a refusal to evaluate the legal allegation.

Only the admitted continuing factual-innocence disclosure promise is an operative contractual premise. The historical text's additional assertion of earlier production is not needed, adopted or treated as compliance. No particular withheld item, actual informed waiver of the rejected term, accepted bargain or established witness danger is inferred. The absence of a particular item limits the questions decided; it is not a new innocence prerequisite or an alternative reason an unconstitutional condition could never injure.

The Chief is outside the reversal coalition, so Stevens, its most senior participating Associate, assigns. Eligible authors of the full lead opinion are Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Thomas is not eligible for that complete rationale; Stone's dissent is not eligible. The actual all-Justice count, pool information and case-specific author selection await release after Harris and Ring and the intervening Trans Union Record. No assignment or Record is made here.
