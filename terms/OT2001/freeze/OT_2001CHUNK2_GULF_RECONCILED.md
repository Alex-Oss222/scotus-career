## Components and final operative positions

**C1-R — mixed cable–Internet coverage, reversal:** Sections 224(a)(4) and (b) cover a qualifying attachment by a cable television system even when it also carries Internet service. The specific formulas in (d) and (e) retain their mandatory operation when applicable but do not exhaust or narrow the antecedent attachment definition. Classification of Internet access and the lawfulness of a particular chosen rate are not necessary to reverse the categorical jurisdictional exclusion. Reverse the Eleventh Circuit on this component and remand consistently with coverage. Do not approve the cable formula for every commingled attachment, settle the classification, or infer a newly presented rate challenge.

**C1-V — mixed services, agency-explanation remand:** Vacate this portion of the appellate judgment and return to the FCC for a reasoned identification of the statutory basis governing its rate regulation: subsection (d), subsection (e), or residual subsection (b)(1). The agency must first classify the offered cable-modem access sufficiently to determine whether a mandatory formula applies. Likely ultimate jurisdiction and rejection of a categorical exclusion do not justify allowing the existing agency determination to stand without that explanation. This position does not hold that mixed attachments are excluded; it withholds approval of the agency order and the broader residual-authority conclusion on the present explanation.

**C2-R — wireless coverage, reversal:** An attachment by a provider of telecommunications to the public for a fee is covered without regard to the transmission facilities used, when the attachment and owner satisfy section 224. The wire-communications language defining the utility concerns the covered owner and facilities, not an exclusion of a wireless provider's attached equipment. Reverse the wireless exclusion and remand. Both wireline attachments by wireless providers and their qualifying wireless equipment are covered. The rule does not reach every independent wireless tower or property outside the statutory definitions.

The statutory utility exclusions, incumbent-local-carrier definition and qualifying state-regulation allocation remain. The specific formula controls within its terms; general coverage supplies no permission to evade it. The text supplies the sufficient coverage ground, and no new general deference holding is required. Attachments of wholly different kinds are outside these questions. No classification factual premise about the common-carrier status of particular cable companies is adopted.

## Selected separate-position substance

Souter and Thomas support the wireless holding but oppose the mixed-service reversal. Their ground is reasoned agency decisionmaking, not categorical noncoverage. If cable-modem access is cable service, subsection (d)'s methodology controls; if it is telecommunications service, subsection (e)'s methodology controls; only after excluding those commands can residual rate authority be assessed. An unclassified transmission service and a general decision to use the cable rate do not identify the required statutory path. They distinguish the Internet service supplied by an Internet service provider from the transmission connecting a customer to that provider; the FCC's treatment of the former does not necessarily classify the latter. The Commission's own continued lack of classification prevents sustaining the order on a judicial assumption that the question is already answered. They would require classification and explanation on remand, leaving the agency the first application and the reviewing court a concrete decision to examine. Permian Basin's reasoned-method requirement supports the objection; policy favoring broadband deployment supports likely eventual coverage but cannot cure the missing explanation.

The six C1-R Justices answer that the question is jurisdiction over the attachment: either possible classification leaves the statutory attachment definition satisfied, and a rate challenge is not before the Court. They preserve every mandatory formula and adopt no present conclusion on a chosen rate. That answer is sufficient for their common rule but does not secure Souter's or Thomas's join.
