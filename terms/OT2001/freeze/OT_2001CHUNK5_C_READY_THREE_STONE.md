## 76. In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical evidence restriction and remand to confine any injunction to actual prosecution or relitigation of matters disposed of by the federal judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the Florida action concerning expressly excluded policies to proceed subject to ordinary state evidentiary rules and the enforceable release of other claims. The district court may identify and prohibit an actual attempt to recover on released claims within the statutory exception. No award on the retained claims, admission of a particular document, or reopening of the settlement follows.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Finality without extinguishing the preserved action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the Anti-Injunction Act and the scope of the operative order. Assume only the settlement’s established validity as to the released policies, not universal federal control of related proof.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the judgment-protection exception authorizes the categorical exclusion of released-policy evidence from claims expressly preserved by the settlement.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinctions between release, enforcement jurisdiction, and evidentiary control; propose the unresolved application of the judgment-protection exception.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A settlement-enforcement court may prevent prosecution of released claims within the Anti-Injunction Act’s exceptions, but may not bar evidence in a separately retained claim merely because the evidence concerns a released transaction. It must identify the claim or conclusively resolved issue whose relitigation the injunction prevents and tailor the order to that adjudicative conflict.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers policy-specific exclusions and evidence offered for those preserved claims. It does not permit another recovery on a released policy or invalidate a demonstrated contractual restriction through a holding unrelated to its terms.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Anti-Injunction Act's exceptions are the controlling statutory boundary; settlement-management convenience cannot enlarge them. Baker distinguishes respect for a judgment from control of another tribunal's evidence, and Matsushita protects an actually binding release without erasing claims expressly retained. Stone applies those principles to the policy-specific exclusions. Evidence of a released transaction may help prove a different retained claim without seeking a second recovery or reopening a decided issue. The federal court must therefore identify the particular claim or issue being relitigated, including any genuine threat to jurisdiction, rather than prohibit proof merely because its subject touches the settlement. Finality and federalism support that textual limit but do not replace it. A narrower injunction may prevent an actual end run around the release; admissibility for the preserved Florida claims remains with that court under its ordinary rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Admissibility of particular evidence and the merits of the excluded-policy claims require proffers and state-law determinations outside this record. No reopening of the settled claims is authorized.
## 78. Gisbrecht v. Barnhart

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for separate reasonableness determinations beginning with the lawful contingent-fee agreements.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Enforce the twenty-five-percent ceiling. Permit supported reductions for substandard representation, attorney-caused delay, and a disproportionate windfall; use time records as evidence rather than an obligatory replacement formula. Apply the required refund of the smaller overlapping EAJA award. Do not fix the final fees on this record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Review the bargain under the statute

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the methodology for court-representation fees, keeping EAJA and administrative fees separate.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether statutory review of a reasonable claimant-funded fee begins with the contingent agreement and how that review protects benefits.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Propose the unresolved statutory interpretation; do not claim that Schaefer and Forney already adopt it.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The court should begin with a lawful contingent-fee agreement, enforce the statutory cap, and independently determine whether the resulting fee is reasonable in the particular representation. It may reduce the result for deficient service, attorney-caused delay, or a demonstrated windfall; hours and rates inform that inquiry without making lodestar the mandatory substitute for the agreement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers fees for successful court representation paid from past-due benefits. It preserves separate EAJA conditions and the prohibition on overlapping retention. It does not govern administrative compensation through an unexamined analogy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 406(b) regulates a claimant-funded contingent arrangement by imposing a ceiling and judicial reasonableness review. Its enacted design is decisive. Stone distinguishes government-paid fee shifting, for which lodestar doctrine serves a different purpose, and preserves Schaefer's separate rules for EAJA entitlement and timing. Beginning with the agreement gives effect to contingent risk and the representation actually obtained; treating it as conclusive would erase the statutory protection of benefits. The resulting standard permits reasoned reductions for deficient service, lawyer-caused delay and a disproportionate windfall, using hours as evidence rather than an automatic formula. It is a context-sensitive reasonableness inquiry under a firm statutory cap. Individual awards require the services and recovery in each matter, so the legal method is fixed now and the amounts remain for application.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The final amount in each case depends on services, delay, benefits, and overlap findings not conclusively determined here.
## 79. South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the rejection of the §1983 effects basis for the injunction and of any directly implied Title VI action to enforce the §602 effects regulations; vacate the disposition only insofar as it forecloses further proceedings on the genuinely preserved §601 intentional-discrimination claim; remand solely for that claim and ordinary proper-defendant issues.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** Neither impact-only route can sustain or reinstate the injunction, and the suspended preliminary injunction does not revive. On remand the district court adjudicates the original §601 intentional-discrimination theory, pleaded on February 13, 2001 and not abandoned by the amendment, on its own proof and procedural footing, together with ordinary proper-defendant questions. Prospective relief may issue against a proper state officer only upon an established §601 violation and that officer’s connection to it; NJDEP’s own amenability to suit is not assumed. The permit is neither invalidated nor sustained on its regulatory merits, no order directs EPA to begin enforcement proceedings, and the residents retain the agency petition and complaint processes existing law provides.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The effects regulations do not create a §1983 right

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The enforcement route is a legal antecedent to the reviewed injunction; no finding of intentional discrimination is supplied.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can the asserted effects regulation itself supply the individual statutory right required by §1983?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply and reaffirm Powell’s §1983 holding, which Sandoval left undisturbed; decline the request to reconsider it.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** No. Section 601’s intent protection and §602’s agency-directed implementation provisions do not create the broader individual effects right asserted under §1983. A regulation may elaborate an individual right that Congress has conferred, but it cannot create a missing one; EPA’s disparate-impact provisions, 40 C.F.R. §7.35(b) and (c), therefore supply no right for §1983 to enforce. The rejection rests on the absence of a right, not on congressional foreclosure of an established one.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion concerns the asserted regulation-created effects right; it does not foreclose enforcement of an independently conferred individual statutory right or a preserved intentional-discrimination claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Text, statutory structure and precedent are decisive. Powell decided this question: section 601 confers a personal right against intentional discrimination, and section 602 authorizes federal agencies to effectuate that guarantee through regulations and administrative enforcement; neither provision confers an individual right against effects that section 601 itself does not prohibit. Sandoval followed Powell’s companion direct-action holding and left this holding in place. Treating any valid regulation as a new §1983 right would remove Congress’s role in defining the entitlement. Blessing, Wright and Wilder remain governing at their scope, but each rested on a statute that itself conferred the entitlement a regulation elaborated. The residents identify no new statutory text, contrary intervening decision or concrete reliance that would justify reopening so recent a statutory precedent, and the seriousness of the environmental burden does not supply a right Congress withheld.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No new damages remedy for disparate impact is presented.

### Issue 2: No direct private action enforces the effects regulations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The original complaint pleaded a direct §602 effects theory, and the residents ask that their request for prospective protection proceed on that route if §1983 fails. The question is a legal antecedent to any relief on the effects regulations and requires no further finding.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Once §1983 fails, may the requested prospective protection rest on a directly implied Title VI action to enforce the §602 effects regulations?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Sandoval and Powell; decline the request to recognize a permit-specific direct route notwithstanding them.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** No. Title VI supplies no private action to enforce the additional disparate-impact prohibitions of EPA’s §602 regulations where the challenged conduct does not violate §601, whatever the form of relief sought. Assuming without deciding that 40 C.F.R. §7.35(b) and (c) validly implement §602, the residents cannot enforce them in a private suit, and a different pleading label, a request confined to prospective relief or the particular permit at issue does not change that answer. Valid regulations remain enforceable through the means Congress authorized: agency petition and complaint processes and §602’s notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing and funding-termination procedures.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule governs private enforcement of the §602 effects regulations in this permitting dispute as Sandoval and Powell govern it generally; it does not decide the regulations’ validity, limit agency enforcement, impose an unlegislated environmental equality code or affect the separate §601 intentional-discrimination claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Sandoval, following Powell, holds that Congress has not authorized private enforcement of these additional effects prohibitions and that a regulation cannot supply the missing authorization by its own force. The residents’ direct theory is that claim: it would enforce §7.35’s effects terms on a showing of effects, without proof that NJDEP violated §601. Neither a request for an injunction, a different state program nor a finding rather than an allegation of disparate effects distinguishes it, as Sandoval held of Powell, and the permit-specific setting does no more. The residents offer no new statutory text, contrary intervening decision or concrete reliance that would warrant reopening two recent statutory precedents. The burden on the neighborhood is serious, and Congress gave the funding agency the means to address a recipient’s noncompliance with its regulations; a court may not add the private remedy Congress withheld.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Regulatory invalidity or an independent permit-law claim not preserved in this appeal is not decided.

### Issue 3: The preserved §601 intentional-discrimination claim proceeds on its own footing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The February 13, 2001 complaint alleged that NJDEP and Commissioner Shinn intentionally discriminated in violation of §601, and the record does not show that the amendment adding §1983 abandoned that theory. Insofar as the appellate disposition forecloses further proceedings on it, its status is properly before the Court; no finding of discriminatory purpose is supplied.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does reversal of the §1983 injunction foreclose further proceedings on the original §601 intentional-discrimination claim, and what governs those proceedings?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply current law: §601’s private action for intentional discrimination, which Powell and Sandoval preserve; Verizon Maryland on jurisdiction; Ex parte Young on prospective relief against a state officer.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** No. A genuinely preserved §601 intentional-discrimination claim remains independently cognizable after the impact-only routes fail, and it proceeds on its own proof and procedural footing. The residents must prove discriminatory purpose; disparate effects may be weighed with other evidence, but intent may not be inferred from unequal environmental conditions alone. The district court has jurisdiction because the claim presents a substantial federal question, a threshold distinct from the claim’s merits. Prospective relief may issue against a proper state officer only upon an established §601 violation and the officer’s actual connection to the challenged conduct; naming an officer does not by itself satisfy that condition, and NJDEP’s own amenability to suit is not assumed.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the residents’ original §601 intentional-discrimination theory and ordinary proper-defendant issues. It does not revive the suspended injunction, decide the permit’s regulatory merits, convert the effects allegations into a new §601 claim or impose a general duty to equalize cumulative pollution burdens.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Powell and Sandoval each preserve §601’s private action for intentional discrimination; they deny only the additional effects routes. The complaint pleaded that claim, the amendment added §1983 without withdrawing it, and the district court itself identified the intentional-discrimination charge as a remaining claim when it called for supplemental briefing. The appellate disposition rests on the failure of the §1983 theory; insofar as it forecloses the §601 claim, it reaches a claim it did not decide. Verizon Maryland separates jurisdiction from entitlement, so the district court may hear the claim without any assumption that it will succeed. Ex parte Young permits prospective relief only against an officer connected to a continuing violation, and only after that violation is established; whether Commissioner Shinn has that connection, and whether NJDEP itself may be sued, are for the district court on the record. The limited vacatur protects the claim the residents actually preserved; it neither awards relief nor presumes intent.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether discriminatory purpose can be proved, and whether any relief is warranted, require findings the record lacks and are not decided.
