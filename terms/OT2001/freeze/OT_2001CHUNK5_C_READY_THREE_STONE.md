## 76. In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical evidence restriction and remand to confine any injunction to actual prosecution or relitigation of matters disposed of by the federal judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the Florida action concerning expressly excluded policies to proceed subject to ordinary state evidentiary rules and the enforceable release of other claims. The district court may identify and prohibit an actual attempt to recover on released claims within the statutory exception. No award on the retained claims, admission of a particular document, or reopening of the settlement follows.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Finality without extinguishing the preserved action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the Anti-Injunction Act and the scope of the operative order. Assume only the settlement’s established validity as to the released policies, not universal federal control of related proof.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the judgment-protection exception authorizes the categorical exclusion of released-policy evidence from claims expressly preserved by the settlement.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinctions between release, enforcement jurisdiction, and evidentiary control; propose the unresolved application of the judgment-protection exception.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A settlement-enforcement court may prevent prosecution of released claims within the Anti-Injunction Act’s exceptions, but may not bar evidence in a separately retained claim merely because the evidence concerns a released transaction. It must identify the claim or conclusively resolved issue whose relitigation the injunction prevents and tailor the order to that adjudicative conflict.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers policy-specific exclusions and evidence offered for those preserved claims. It does not permit another recovery on a released policy or invalidate a demonstrated contractual restriction through a holding unrelated to its terms.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Anti-Injunction Act's exceptions are the controlling statutory boundary; settlement-management convenience cannot enlarge them. Baker distinguishes respect for a judgment from control of another tribunal's evidence, and Matsushita protects an actually binding release without erasing claims expressly retained. Stone applies those principles to the policy-specific exclusions. Evidence of a released transaction may help prove a different retained claim without seeking a second recovery or reopening a decided issue. The federal court must therefore identify the particular claim or issue being relitigated, including any genuine threat to jurisdiction, rather than prohibit proof merely because its subject touches the settlement. Finality and federalism support that textual limit but do not replace it. A narrower injunction may prevent an actual end run around the release; admissibility for the preserved Florida claims remains with that court under its ordinary rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Admissibility of particular evidence and the merits of the excluded-policy claims require proffers and state-law determinations outside this record. No reopening of the settled claims is authorized.
## 78. Gisbrecht v. Barnhart

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for separate reasonableness determinations beginning with the lawful contingent-fee agreements.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Enforce the twenty-five-percent ceiling. Permit supported reductions for substandard representation, attorney-caused delay, and a disproportionate windfall; use time records as evidence rather than an obligatory replacement formula. Apply the required refund of the smaller overlapping EAJA award. Do not fix the final fees on this record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Review the bargain under the statute

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the methodology for court-representation fees, keeping EAJA and administrative fees separate.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether statutory review of a reasonable claimant-funded fee begins with the contingent agreement and how that review protects benefits.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Propose the unresolved statutory interpretation; do not claim that Schaefer and Forney already adopt it.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The court should begin with a lawful contingent-fee agreement, enforce the statutory cap, and independently determine whether the resulting fee is reasonable in the particular representation. It may reduce the result for deficient service, attorney-caused delay, or a demonstrated windfall; hours and rates inform that inquiry without making lodestar the mandatory substitute for the agreement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers fees for successful court representation paid from past-due benefits. It preserves separate EAJA conditions and the prohibition on overlapping retention. It does not govern administrative compensation through an unexamined analogy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 406(b) regulates a claimant-funded contingent arrangement by imposing a ceiling and judicial reasonableness review. Its enacted design is decisive. Stone distinguishes government-paid fee shifting, for which lodestar doctrine serves a different purpose, and preserves Schaefer's separate rules for EAJA entitlement and timing. Beginning with the agreement gives effect to contingent risk and the representation actually obtained; treating it as conclusive would erase the statutory protection of benefits. The resulting standard permits reasoned reductions for deficient service, lawyer-caused delay and a disproportionate windfall, using hours as evidence rather than an automatic formula. It is a context-sensitive reasonableness inquiry under a firm statutory cap. Individual awards require the services and recovery in each matter, so the legal method is fixed now and the amounts remain for application.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The final amount in each case depends on services, delay, benefits, and overlap findings not conclusively determined here.
## 79. South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the regulation-created §1983 right, but vacate the disposition insofar as it forecloses the pending prospective Title VI theory; remand for that route and appropriate interim relief.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The §1983 rationale cannot sustain the injunction. Require prompt adjudication of the original direct statutory theory, proper recipient and prospective defendant, actual regulatory elements and preliminary-relief requirements. Preserve or issue protection only through a supported lawful order under that theory; neither automatically restore the original injunction nor declare the permit valid.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: A direct statutory action does not create a different §1983 right

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The enforcement route is a legal antecedent to the reviewed injunction; no finding of intentional discrimination is supplied.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can the asserted effects regulation itself supply the individual statutory right required by §1983?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Reaffirm Powell’s surviving §1983 holding and reconcile it with Sandoval’s express partial overruling.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** No. Section 601’s intent protection and §602’s agency-directed implementation provisions do not create the broader individual effects right asserted under §1983. Sandoval authorizes a bounded direct prospective action from the integrated Title VI statute and leaves this separate right-creation rule intact.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion concerns the asserted regulation-created effects right; it does not foreclose enforcement of an independently conferred individual statutory right or a preserved intentional-discrimination claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Text, statutory structure and the precise treatment of precedent are decisive. The source of an action and the source of a right under another remedial statute are distinct legal questions. Treating any valid regulation as a new §1983 right would remove Congress’s role in defining the entitlement; treating Powell’s original direct-action bar as unchanged would disregard Sandoval’s actual holding. Neither shortcut is justified by the seriousness of the environmental burden. The existing cases can be reconciled without further overruling.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No new damages remedy for disparate impact is presented.

### Issue 2: The prospective claim must receive its own adjudication

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The action began with the direct Title VI theory; the intervening legal premise used to abandon that route is incompatible with the governing law. Its pending procedural footing must be applied without inventing a new unrelated claim.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** What follows for the requested prospective protection once §1983 fails?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Sandoval to the original prospective enforcement theory while requiring proof of its actual limits.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A statutory beneficiary may seek prospective compliance by a covered recipient with a valid and sufficiently definite EPA §602 requirement. The court must determine coverage, the applicable regulatory duty, prohibited effect and causal relationship, and fit relief to the established violation. An effects disparity is evidence, not an automatic violation.
* **Reach Within the Question:** This permits adjudication of the identified permitting claim; it does not impose an unlegislated environmental equality code, presume intentional discrimination or compel a particular permit outcome.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The decisive rule is the authorized statutory channel. The agency’s administration of a funded program must be measured against the actual regulation, including legally relevant cumulative effects if that duty reaches them. The private action protects those the statute benefits without giving the court general permitting authority. Remand is necessary for unresolved coverage, regulatory and equitable determinations; it does not reserve whether this category of prospective action exists. Stone decides that legal question now and requires the relief inquiry to address both the residents’ threatened injury and the lawful limits of the order.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Regulatory invalidity or an independent permit-law claim not preserved in this appeal is not decided.
