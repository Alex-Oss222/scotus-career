## 76. In re Prudential Insurance Co. of America Sales Practice Litigation +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the categorical evidence restriction and remand to confine any injunction to actual prosecution or relitigation of matters disposed of by the federal judgment.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Permit the Florida action concerning expressly excluded policies to proceed subject to ordinary state evidentiary rules and the enforceable release of other claims. The district court may identify and prohibit an actual attempt to recover on released claims within the statutory exception. No award on the retained claims, admission of a particular document, or reopening of the settlement follows.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Finality without extinguishing the preserved action

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the Anti-Injunction Act and the scope of the operative order. Assume only the settlement’s established validity as to the released policies, not universal federal control of related proof.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether the judgment-protection exception authorizes the categorical exclusion of released-policy evidence from claims expressly preserved by the settlement.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinctions between release, enforcement jurisdiction, and evidentiary control; propose the unresolved application of the judgment-protection exception.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** A settlement-enforcement court may prevent prosecution of released claims within the Anti-Injunction Act’s exceptions, but may not bar evidence in a separately retained claim merely because the evidence concerns a released transaction. It must identify the claim or conclusively resolved issue whose relitigation the injunction prevents and tailor the order to that adjudicative conflict.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers policy-specific exclusions and evidence offered for those preserved claims. It does not permit another recovery on a released policy or invalidate a demonstrated contractual restriction through a holding unrelated to its terms.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Anti-Injunction Act's exceptions are the controlling statutory boundary; settlement-management convenience cannot enlarge them. Baker distinguishes respect for a judgment from control of another tribunal's evidence, and Matsushita protects an actually binding release without erasing claims expressly retained. Stone applies those principles to the policy-specific exclusions. Evidence of a released transaction may help prove a different retained claim without seeking a second recovery or reopening a decided issue. The federal court must therefore identify the particular claim or issue being relitigated, including any genuine threat to jurisdiction, rather than prohibit proof merely because its subject touches the settlement. Finality and federalism support that textual limit but do not replace it. A narrower injunction may prevent an actual end run around the release; admissibility for the preserved Florida claims remains with that court under its ordinary rules.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Admissibility of particular evidence and the merits of the excluded-policy claims require proffers and state-law determinations outside this record. No reopening of the settled claims is authorized.
## 78. Gisbrecht v. Barnhart

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse and remand for separate reasonableness determinations beginning with the lawful contingent-fee agreements.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Enforce the twenty-five-percent ceiling. Permit supported reductions for substandard representation, attorney-caused delay, and a disproportionate windfall; use time records as evidence rather than an obligatory replacement formula. Apply the required refund of the smaller overlapping EAJA award. Do not fix the final fees on this record.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Review the bargain under the statute

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the methodology for court-representation fees, keeping EAJA and administrative fees separate.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether statutory review of a reasonable claimant-funded fee begins with the contingent agreement and how that review protects benefits.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Propose the unresolved statutory interpretation; do not claim that Schaefer and Forney already adopt it.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** The court should begin with a lawful contingent-fee agreement, enforce the statutory cap, and independently determine whether the resulting fee is reasonable in the particular representation. It may reduce the result for deficient service, attorney-caused delay, or a demonstrated windfall; hours and rates inform that inquiry without making lodestar the mandatory substitute for the agreement.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers fees for successful court representation paid from past-due benefits. It preserves separate EAJA conditions and the prohibition on overlapping retention. It does not govern administrative compensation through an unexamined analogy.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Section 406(b) regulates a claimant-funded contingent arrangement by imposing a ceiling and judicial reasonableness review. Its enacted design is decisive. Stone distinguishes government-paid fee shifting, for which lodestar doctrine serves a different purpose, and preserves Schaefer's separate rules for EAJA entitlement and timing. Beginning with the agreement gives effect to contingent risk and the representation actually obtained; treating it as conclusive would erase the statutory protection of benefits. The resulting standard permits reasoned reductions for deficient service, lawyer-caused delay and a disproportionate windfall, using hours as evidence rather than an automatic formula. It is a context-sensitive reasonableness inquiry under a firm statutory cap. Individual awards require the services and recovery in each matter, so the legal method is fixed now and the amounts remain for application.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** The final amount in each case depends on services, delay, benefits, and overlap findings not conclusively determined here.
## 79. South Camden Citizens in Action v. New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Law effective through June 28, 2001, subject to any intervening decision actually rendered before this matter.
* **Conditions:** The stated record and review scope govern; allegations remain allegations and proposed holdings are not existing Court law.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone’s Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the rejection of the §1983 effects theory; vacate the appellate rejection of the directly implied Title VI prospective claim; vacate the disposition insofar as it forecloses the preserved §601 intentional-discrimination claim, and preserve and remand that claim; remand the direct effects claim for the remaining merits and remedy questions.
* **Stone’s Remedy and Remand Position:** The §1983 effects theory cannot sustain or reinstate the injunction. On remand the district court adjudicates the direct effects claim under Powell’s prospective action: regulatory validity and coverage insofar as genuinely contested; the challenged permitting or administrative method; supported effect and causal connection; proper defendants; and, only if a violation is proved, violation-matched prospective relief. It separately adjudicates the original §601 intentional-discrimination theory, pleaded on February 13, 2001 and not abandoned by the amendment, on its own proof and procedural footing. A proper connected state officer may be subject to prospective federal relief on an established violation; neither NJDEP’s amenability nor a particular permit violation is presumed. The suspended preliminary injunction does not automatically revive; any renewed interim relief must be justified on the existing record and lawful current circumstances. Existing findings receive ordinary review, and no new hearing is directed. No new permit denial, agency funding cutoff or damages award is ordered; no judicial order equalizes environmental conditions, and unequal exposure is not presumed to violate Title VI.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: The effects regulations do not create a §1983 right

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The enforcement route is a legal antecedent to the reviewed injunction; no finding of intentional discrimination is supplied.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Can the asserted effects regulation itself supply the individual statutory right required by §1983?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply and reaffirm Powell’s §1983 holding, Part III of the opinion Stone wrote for the Court, which Sandoval left undisturbed; decline the residents’ request to reconsider it.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** No. The EPA regulations do not create an individual statutory effects right absent from Title VI. Section 601’s intent protection and §602’s agency-directed implementation provisions do not create the broader individual effects right asserted under §1983. A regulation may elaborate an individual right that Congress has conferred, but it cannot create a missing one; EPA’s disparate-impact provisions, 40 C.F.R. §7.35(b) and (c), therefore supply no right for §1983 to enforce. The rejection rests on the absence of a right, not on congressional foreclosure of an established one, and it does not foreclose §1983 enforcement when a different statute actually confers the individual entitlement a regulation implements.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The conclusion concerns the asserted regulation-created effects right; it does not foreclose enforcement of an independently conferred individual statutory right, the direct prospective action Powell recognizes or a preserved intentional-discrimination claim.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Text, statutory structure and precedent are decisive. Powell decided this question in a holding separate from its direct-action holding: section 601 confers a personal right against intentional discrimination, and section 602 authorizes federal agencies to implement Title VI through regulations; a valid regulation does not independently create the broader individual statutory right §1983 requires where the statute does not confer it. Sandoval applied Powell’s direct-action holding and left this holding in place. The source of a direct statutory action and the source of a right under a different remedial statute are distinct questions; the availability of Powell’s bounded prospective action does not convert the regulation into the individual right §1983 enforces. Blessing requires a particular congressional entitlement, and Wright and Wilder rested on statutes that themselves conferred the entitlement a regulation elaborated. Treating any valid regulation as a new §1983 right would remove Congress’s role in defining the entitlement. The residents identify no new statutory text, contrary intervening decision or concrete reliance that would justify reopening so recent a statutory precedent.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** No new damages remedy for disparate impact is presented.

### Issue 2: Powell’s direct prospective action enforces EPA’s §602 effects regulations

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The February 13, 2001 complaint pleaded a direct §602 effects theory, and the amendment adding §1983 did not withdraw it. Insofar as the appellate disposition rejects that theory, its availability is a legal antecedent properly before the Court; whether a violation occurred requires findings the record does not yet supply.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Once §1983 fails, may the residents’ request for prospective protection proceed through Powell’s direct prospective action to enforce EPA’s §602 disparate-impact regulations, and what governs that claim on remand?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply Powell’s direct-action holding, Part II of the opinion Stone wrote for the Court, and Sandoval, which applied that holding to a different agency’s regulation.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** Yes. Plaintiffs concretely affected by a covered recipient’s methods may bring the Powell prospective action to enforce valid EPA §602 disparate-impact regulations, including 40 C.F.R. §7.35(b) and (c), if the actual regulations apply and the violation is proved. The residents must establish an applicable regulation validly issued under the authority Congress conferred; a defendant subject to it through the required federal-assistance relationship; a concrete exclusion, denial of benefits or other material discriminatory effect within the regulation’s actual terms, attributable to an identified permitting policy or method of administration; an actual or imminent continuing effect that prospective relief can meaningfully correct; and an appropriate defendant and a remedy compatible with immunity, statutory and equitable limits. Section 7.35(b) reaches criteria or methods of administration with the prohibited effect, including defeat or substantial impairment of the program’s objectives for protected persons; §7.35(c) governs site or location selection under its own purpose-or-effect terms and is pleaded and proved on those terms. Disproportionate statistics may support proof, but a numerical racial difference alone does not conclusively establish a violation; the residents must connect any disparity to the challenged method and the regulation’s operative prohibition, and the recipient may contest comparison, causation, materiality and coverage and invoke any qualification or defense the valid regulation supplies. No prior agency finding is required. Relief is a declaration of an established violation and violation-matched prospective relief requiring the recipient to end or correct the prohibited method.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the residents’ direct claim under EPA’s §602 effects regulations as applied to NJDEP’s permitting and administration, and it applies Powell to every valid Title VI effects regulation within its bounded prospective scope. It does not establish a regulation’s validity where genuinely contested, a recipient’s coverage or a violation; it supplies no damages for unintended disparate impact, punitive damages, retrospective compensation, private funding termination or authority to redesign an entire program; and it orders no equalization of environmental conditions.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Powell holds that a person suffering a concrete injury within a federally assisted program may obtain declaratory and appropriately confined prospective equitable relief against a covered recipient’s ongoing violation of a valid, duly issued §602 regulation prohibiting discriminatory effects, within Title VI’s particular statutory context. Sandoval applied that holding to a different agency’s regulation; the action is not confined to one program. EPA’s §7.35(b) and (c) are §602 effects regulations of the same kind, and a state permitting program receiving EPA assistance is within the action if coverage is established. The appellate disposition rests on the premise that no private action enforces §602 effects regulations; that premise is contrary to Powell and Sandoval, so its rejection of the direct claim is vacated. The objection that §602 commits enforcement of effects regulations to the funding agency, and that a regulation cannot create a private action, is the one Powell considered and rejected and Sandoval rejected again; statutory precedent carries special force, and no new statutory text, intervening decision or changed circumstance warrants reconsidering two recent statutory precedents. Structural reasoning about agency responsibilities supplies limits but does not make the funding-termination procedure the exclusive route to prospective compliance; §602’s notice, voluntary-compliance, hearing, congressional-reporting and program-specific termination restrictions continue to govern the agency’s own powers. The distinction between equitable compliance and retrospective compensation confines the remedy. The residents’ showing of a heavily burdened minority neighborhood is evidence; it must be tied to the challenged permitting method and the regulation’s terms, and the burden on the neighborhood does not itself establish a violation.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** An independent permit-law claim not preserved in this appeal is not decided (ground (b)).

### Issue 3: The preserved §601 intentional-discrimination claim proceeds on its own footing

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** The February 13, 2001 complaint alleged that NJDEP and Commissioner Shinn intentionally discriminated in violation of §601, and the record does not show that the amendment adding §1983 abandoned that theory. Insofar as the appellate disposition forecloses further proceedings on it, its status is properly before the Court; no finding of discriminatory purpose is supplied.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Does reversal of the §1983 injunction foreclose further proceedings on the original §601 intentional-discrimination claim, and what governs those proceedings?
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply current law: §601’s private action for intentional discrimination, which Powell and Sandoval preserve; Verizon Maryland on jurisdiction; Ex parte Young on prospective relief against a state officer.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** No. The preserved §601 intentional-discrimination claim remains independently cognizable and proceeds on its own proof and procedural footing. The residents must prove discriminatory purpose; disparate effects may be evidence relevant to purpose but do not alone establish intentional discrimination. The district court has jurisdiction because the claim presents a substantial federal question, a threshold distinct from the claim’s merits. A proper connected state officer may be subject to prospective federal relief on an established violation; naming an officer does not by itself establish that connection, and neither NJDEP’s amenability nor a particular permit violation is presumed.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the residents’ original §601 intentional-discrimination theory and ordinary proper-defendant issues, and its officer and jurisdiction rules apply equally to the direct effects claim. It does not revive the suspended injunction, decide the permit’s regulatory merits, convert the effects allegations into a §601 claim or impose a general duty to equalize cumulative pollution burdens.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Powell and Sandoval each preserve §601’s private action for intentional discrimination, distinct from the bounded effects action and from §1983. The complaint pleaded that claim, the amendment added §1983 without withdrawing it, and the district court itself identified the intentional-discrimination charge as a remaining claim when it called for supplemental briefing. The appellate disposition rests on the failure of the §1983 theory; insofar as it forecloses the §601 claim, it reaches a claim it did not decide. Verizon Maryland separates jurisdiction from entitlement, so the district court may hear the claim without any assumption that it will succeed. Ex parte Young permits prospective relief only against an officer connected to a continuing violation, and only after that violation is established; whether Commissioner Shinn has that connection, and whether NJDEP itself may be sued, are for the district court on the record. The suspended injunction rested on the §1983 effects theory and does not automatically revive; renewed interim relief must rest on an available claim and the requisite findings.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Whether discriminatory purpose can be proved, and whether any relief is warranted, require findings the record lacks and are not decided.
