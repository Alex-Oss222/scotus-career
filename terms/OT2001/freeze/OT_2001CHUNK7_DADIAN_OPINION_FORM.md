# Dadian v. Village of Wilmette — opinion form

Event: 2002-06-20.

Form: Signed opinion of the Court applying the housing and public-entity accommodation statutes to a tried municipal record, with separate analysis of defense allocation and actual instructional harmlessness. All nine share these grounds; no independent intent-based holding is necessary..

### Necessary reasonable access includes a supported exception to a driveway rule

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the FHAA and ADA Title II, a municipality must make a reasonable modification of its driveway restriction when the plaintiffs establish that it is necessary to afford a qualified resident equal housing access, subject to an established applicable defense. Actual usability for the particular resident governs necessity; a preferred or more valuable design alone is insufficient, and a genuinely equally effective alternative can defeat the asserted need.

**Authority:** @AUTHOR@'s opinion is joined by @JOINS@; all nine adopt the complete accommodation rule and application.

**Controlling explanation:** The statutes require an individual examination of the obstacle caused by disability. Mrs. Dadian's longstanding walking limitations and the evidence comparing the longer rear route with shorter front access permitted a rational jury to find disability, necessity and reasonableness. The existence of some driveway did not establish equal usable access, and the rear design was not conclusively equivalent as a matter of law. Martin confirms that uniform rules and institutional preference do not replace the statutory individual inquiry. Toyota's attention to substantial limitation and daily activities does not turn this tried walking evidence into a pleading defect; Barnett's employment seniority conflict is absent. The Village's appraisal and other evidentiary objections do not justify a new trial: the challenged material bore on the comparison or impeachment, and any error was harmless against the principal medical, personal and meeting evidence. Because accommodation independently supports the same relief, the Court need not decide whether intentional discrimination was independently proved. The property-specific injunction follows from this record rather than a general entitlement to front driveways.

**Precedent treatment:** PGA TOUR, Inc. v. Martin supplies individual statutory assessment, while its Title III fundamental-alteration defense remains distinct. Toyota and Barnett retain their separate definitions and employment context.

### The Village bears its objective safety defense; this instructional error was harmless

**Holding and operative rule:** A municipality asserting that the accommodation presents a direct threat must prove, through objective individualized evidence, a significant risk that reasonable accommodation cannot eliminate. Safety applies to accommodation as well as intentional discrimination; confining the instruction to the latter was error, but this jury's actual rejection of the same asserted threat makes that placement error harmless.

**Authority:** All nine join the same Court opinion on defense allocation, the error and its record-specific harmlessness.

**Controlling explanation:** Bragdon requires an objective assessment rather than stereotypes or unsupported fear. The Village may protect children and pedestrians, but must connect its asserted turning and traffic danger to the actual driveway, driver and possible mitigation. Impaired walking does not itself prove unsafe driving. Assigning the affirmative safety defense to the Village leaves the Dadians responsible for disability, necessity and reasonable accommodation. The instruction should have applied to accommodation, yet the jury necessarily rejected the asserted danger under the theory to which it was given. Repeating that same determination under the accommodation label would not correct a consequential error here. This is not a rule that every favorable verdict cures an omitted instruction: harmlessness depends on this verdict's resolution of this defense.

**Precedent treatment:** Bragdon v. Abbott supplies objective individual assessment and the defense's evidentiary basis; Echazabal's workplace self-threat rule is not imported into municipal housing law.
