## Barnes v. Gorman

### Statutory remedy and competing sufficient grounds

The original question remains the availability of punitive damages through Title II's incorporation of §794a and §504's adoption of Title VI remedies in §794a(a)(2). The separate federal-employment route in §794a(a)(1), and the 1991 Act's specified employment remedies, do not become the controlling statutory channel. No preceding decision changes that chain, displaces Franklin or Pennhurst, or decides whether this incorporated remedy includes punitive damages.

**Chevron:** The actual holding concerns Title I's general job-related-and-business-necessity qualification defense, its nonexclusive threat-to-others example, and an agency regulation adopted under the actual §12116 delegation. It permits an own-health defense only with the prescribed individualized, objective medical assessment and reasonable-accommodation limits. It does not establish punitive remedies under Title II or the Rehabilitation Act. The Court's refusal to turn one statutory example into an exclusive catalogue cannot itself decide Barnes: the original punitive-unavailability ground did not infer an express Title II punitive prohibition from the employment provisions. It relied on the incorporated remedy and distinct notice for exceptional punishment. Conversely, the availability ground relied on Franklin's appropriate-relief presumption within an acknowledged action, not an agency's power to create remedies. Neither ground depends on Chevron's rejected categorical exclusion of a qualification defense. Chevron's reservation of medical-evidence and ordinary remand issues does not reopen Barnes's settled compensation or liability.

**Franconia:** A government's assumed contractual promise may be repudiated before performance is due, and a promisee may ordinarily await the specified performance date rather than immediately elect breach. The Court does not decide every question about the promise's formation or authority, nor use a contract-accrual rule to settle the separate taking claim. Those conclusions about an actual government contract neither require treating a civil-rights funding statute as a literal private contract for every remedial purpose nor prohibit a remedy-specific contractual expectation argument. The original five-Associate ground used the latter and rejected the former. The original three-Associate ground disputed the proposed remedial limitation without disputing ordinary contract accrual. No original Justice's ground assumes that Franconia's contract promise includes punitive damages or that it supplies Title VI's remedial rules.

**Bollin:** Section 853(e) preserves property described in subsection (a); potential substitute property under subsection (p) does not acquire that status merely because the government may eventually forfeit it. Subsection (g) requires an actual qualifying forfeiture order. This is a decision about the boundaries of an express asset-preservation power and the necessary predicates for a distinct authority. It does not abolish Franklin's appropriate-remedies rule in an already recognized action or establish that every unenumerated remedy is unavailable. The original punitive-unavailability path still must explain punishment's distinct notice problem in the incorporated scheme; the punitive-availability path still must establish why that scheme permits aggravated-wrong punishment. Neither can substitute Bollin's different statutory cross-reference for that analysis.

**Mackby, reconsidered in the complete copy:** The FCA's expressly authorized monetary sanctions can be partly punitive and therefore subject to the Excessive Fines Clause. Their punitive function and excessiveness inquiry do not supply an additional remedial authorization in Title VI. Nor does a constitutional limit on an authorized sanction establish a categorical statutory bar to punishment elsewhere. The original distinction among availability, aggravated culpability and permissible amount survives on both paths.

**Compaq, Holmes Group, Zandford and Faasse:** Compaq's actual transaction, economic-substance and tax treatment do not create a universal rule governing statutory civil-rights remedies. Holmes Group's patent-counterclaim and appellate-jurisdiction holdings do not alter the jurisdiction or remedial incorporation here. Zandford's contemporaneously integrated securities-fraud scheme distinguishes later independent theft; it supplies no punitive-damages rule for this action. Faasse applies the already governing Mussari/Schroeder rule to the interstate support obligation and its statutory predicates. It neither enlarges nor narrows Barnes's remedies.

### Constitutional protection, procedure and mandate

**McKune:** The controlling judgment forbids the specified automatic prison restrictions imposed for refusal to supply unprotected incriminating admissions. Its government-penalty analysis concerns compulsion under the Fifth Amendment, including actual criminal exposure and the linked material restrictions; it does not decide whether a statutory damages action authorizes punitive relief. The prospective injunction neither grants immunity nor creates damages. The dissenters' assessment that the particular restrictions do not exert sufficient compulsion is likewise not a holding about civil-rights punitive damages, funding notice or municipal liability. No original Barnes ground rested on the proposition that every governmental punishment requires statutory punitive compensation, or that the constitutional meaning of a penalty itself settles remedial authorization.

**Farmer:** A substantial and specific showing that continuing civil seizure may prevent use of legitimate assets for trial counsel requires a prompt focused adversary hearing. The threshold for obtaining process remains distinct from Farmer's preponderance burden for the confined release claim, and successful proof requires access to the needed legitimate funds unless a separately established lawful basis prevents release. The Mathews explanation and Scalia's distinct post-seizure explanation both resolve that supported procedural claim. They do not decide the availability of punishment for a completed statutory discrimination wrong. Barnes's original alternatives already separate entitlement to a remedy from the factual and amount predicates for granting it: the exclusion path removes a legally unavailable punitive component; the availability path retains independent review rather than automatically restoring the jury's sum. Farmer requires no alteration of either distinction and supplies no new evidentiary hearing entitlement in Barnes.

**Devlin:** A bound, unnamed member of a mandatory class who timely objects may appeal approval without successfully intervening. The limited party status is tied to that member's own affected rights and the class posture; it does not erase preservation rules, reverse the denial of untimely intervention or adjudicate the settlement's merits. It does not require the Court to reach Barnes's newly advanced Newport municipal ground. Bollin likewise leaves its unpreserved intermediary ground outside the decision and creates no entitlement to reopen it. These decisions introduce no new general preservation rule that changes the original Barnes analysis. This conclusion concerns a changed dated-law premise only; it does not adjudicate whether a separately reconciled Justice has a sufficient case-specific basis to reach that alternative ground.

**Morgan:** The distinction between timely discrete claims, one connected hostile environment, historical evidence and liability is particular to the statutory charge period and the claim actually proved. The disposition preserves actual equity and record questions rather than declaring that every old event returns to trial. It does not establish punitive availability or a new exception permitting revival of an unpreserved municipal defense. Its separation of legal scope, proof and remedy is consistent with both original Barnes alternatives, which preserve the compensatory judgment and require a distinct legal basis for punitive relief.

**Traffic Stream:** Alienage jurisdiction for the BVI corporation follows its relation to the United Kingdom at commencement. No jurisdictional premise of Barnes depends on a contrary rule; the decision supplies no merits or remedial holding here.

## Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of New York, Inc. v. Village of Stratton

### Speech burden, fit and the limited grant

The original grounds address the actual prior personal registration, publicly available identifying information and permit requirement for the reviewed noncommercial religious and political advocacy. They do not depend on a personal-name field on the permit, a fee, an actual denial, a current 5 p.m. cutoff, an empty fraud record or a general right to enter over an owner's objection. The grant remains the identification, anonymity and advance-permission question; none of the new decisions supplies an independent free-exercise, hybrid-rights or vagueness question for decision.

**McKune:** The controlling opinion confirms that a legitimate governmental purpose does not by itself answer an independent constitutional protection. Its assessment turns on real self-incrimination exposure and the specific linked package of prison restrictions, not on whether a protected liberty or benefit was separately guaranteed. That holding does not establish a First Amendment licensing test, and the original Watchtower conclusion did not require one. Advance identification and interruption of spontaneous advocacy are expressive burdens evaluated under the prior speech authorities, even when issuance is routine and free. The new decision therefore changes no factual burden premise or applicable anonymity precedent.

The four McKune dissenters recognize the criminal exposure but find the specified restrictions insufficiently coercive in the custody setting. O'Connor does not adopt Sandin as an exclusive test or require an extended sentence; Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas add prison-context grounds. None of those positions supplies a reason to abandon the same Justice's date-eligible writings and joins about speech, identification and administration outside prison. O'Connor's and Scalia's original acceptance of a narrow failure-of-fit ground, Kennedy's original anonymity analysis and Thomas's strict-scrutiny ground therefore remain independently supported. The five Justices supporting the McKune injunction do not gain a universal licensing prohibition from that case; the original Watchtower ground still requires the actual speech burden and the demonstrated mismatch of this ordinance.

**Farmer:** Its prompt post-seizure review protects a supported, time-sensitive claim to use potentially legitimate assets for trial counsel. Neither the Mathews explanation nor Scalia's separate ground decides whether a town may require advance permission for ordinary advocacy. The original Watchtower remedy does not claim that a facial warrant, a permit form or one procedural safeguard settles all constitutional questions; it examines the speech condition itself. Farmer does not make an adversary hearing the necessary or sufficient cure for that condition, and it does not require a speaker to apply for a permit or suffer a criminal prosecution before presenting the already supported pre-enforcement challenge. Its asset-specific release burden cannot be transferred to the speaker's First Amendment claim. Scalia's refusal to join Mathews leaves his original speech-fit rationale intact; Thomas's join in Farmer does not surrender his distinct First Amendment scrutiny method.

**Chevron:** An expressly delegated employment regulation may permit an individualized defense with objective medical evidence and reasonable-accommodation limits. It supplies no new authority for a general local speech license. Its treatment of a statutory example as nonexclusive neither makes the Village's interests illegitimate nor establishes that their actual evidentiary support is sufficient for every covered speaker. The original Watchtower ground already credits the commercial-scam evidence, vulnerable residents, limited police resources and investigation benefits. The disputed point remains fit to ordinary noncommercial advocacy, not the existence of a valid governmental concern.

**Zandford:** The securities holding concerns concealed appropriation integral to the securities transactions themselves. It neither establishes fraud by the advocates here nor requires advance identification of every person whose conduct might facilitate investigation. The original judgment leaves actual fraud enforcement available; no new fraud premise must be imported from a different record.

**Franconia:** The separate contract-accrual and takings analysis depends on the promise's due performance, the challenged governmental act, protected property, completed injury and the appropriate finality inquiry. It supplies no general requirement that a speaker seek and receive a licensing denial before challenging an already threatened prohibition. The Watchtower record's pre-enforcement posture, the operative misdemeanor commands and the limited question remain unchanged.

### Statutory, procedural and remedial limits

**Bollin:** The government must stop the unsupported substitute-property restraint unless a distinct legally authorized order supplies the necessary predicates. This does not decide a First Amendment question, but it preserves the distinction the original Watchtower remedy already draws: removal of the invalid prerequisite does not exempt conduct from separately valid laws. The original invalidation remains confined to the reviewed noncommercial advocacy under §§116.01 and 116.03 and dependent carry/display enforcement. Section 116.07, actual owner exclusion, and ordinary fraud and trespass enforcement are preserved. Bollin provides no basis for invalidating every commercial, fundraising or disclosure application.

**Devlin and Morgan:** Devlin's limited status for a mandatory-class objector does not alter the parties, the grant, or preservation in Watchtower. Morgan's charge-period and continuing-environment rules do not create an additional First Amendment claim or permit supplementation with unproved events. Their confined appellate and remand treatment supplies no reason to broaden the reviewed question or enter independent religious-exercise and vagueness holdings.

**Compaq, Holmes Group, Mackby, Traffic Stream and Faasse:** Their operative rules concern the actual tax transaction, patent appellate jurisdiction, constitutionally limited FCA monetary sanctions, alienage jurisdiction and the federal interstate-support offense, respectively. None changes the applicable speech precedents, the ordinance, the enforcement posture or the scope of relief. Mackby's treatment of a financial sanction as punitive does not transform the ordinance's threatened criminal penalties into a damages claim or require an excessive-fines holding not presented here. Faasse's recognition of a valid federal interstate obligation does not supply a constitutional exception for ordinary local advocacy.

### Justice-specific continuity and result

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer retain their original sufficient failure-of-fit ground, including the assumption that the ordinance is neutral and that the Village's less demanding standard could apply. Their own McIntyre, Buckley and Thomas v. Chicago Park District writings and joins continue to distinguish supported election or shared-space administration from a general prerequisite to household advocacy. The new decisions neither erase those distinctions nor supply the missing logistical or election-administration predicate. O'Connor's and Breyer's prior acceptance of some registration and reporting requirements remains an actual limit, not a commitment to approve all advance identification.

Thomas retains the original direct anonymous-speech protection and strict-scrutiny ground resting on his McIntyre and Buckley writings. Neither his McKune dissent nor his joins in the intervening statutory and procedural cases changes that method. His separate methodological barrier to adopting lesser scrutiny as his own sufficient ground remains precise; a shared conclusion that the ordinance also fails a less demanding fit test does not erase it.

No original disposition, sufficient explanation, scrutiny barrier, grant limit, factual qualification or remedial boundary requires an independent change because of intervening public law. This is not an assessment of any unseen reconciliation of Watchtower.
