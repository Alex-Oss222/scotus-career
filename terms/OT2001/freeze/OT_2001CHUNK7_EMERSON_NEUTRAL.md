## Complete material lower-court record

1. Sacha Emerson filed for divorce on August 28, 1998 and requested restraints addressing twenty-nine specified acts. An ex parte temporary restraining order issued that day. That ex parte order is not the charged predicate and does not satisfy the federal post-hearing requirement.
2. The state court conducted an evidentiary hearing on September 4. Sacha had counsel; Emerson appeared without counsel, announced ready and could participate. The published record contains no indication that he could not retain counsel or wanted counsel. Those limits establish no waiver of an independent constitutional right that the applicable law might require.
3. Most testimony concerned financial matters. Asked whether Emerson had threatened to kill her, Sacha denied that proposition and stated that he had threatened to kill a friend of hers and had telephoned her about it. The district opinion, quoted in the appellate footnote, identifies that friend as the man with whom she had an affair. Emerson declined cross-examination and presented no evidence refuting or explaining this testimony. In another connection he described anxiety and a poor mental state. Neither an unrebutted allegation nor his decision not to cross-examine is itself a separately found assault, a concession of dangerousness or a constitutional waiver.
4. On September 14, the court entered a temporary injunction enumerating twenty-two prohibited acts. Pertinent provisions prohibited threatening Sacha, in person, by telephone or in writing, with unlawful action against any person; intentionally, knowingly or recklessly causing bodily injury to her or a child of either party; and threatening her or a child of either party with imminent bodily injury. Its duration ran until the final divorce decree or further court order. It contained no express finding that Emerson posed a future danger to his wife or daughter. The published record identifies no attempt by Emerson to modify or challenge it.
5. The indictment originally had five counts. On the government's motion, the district court dismissed counts two through five. The sole count at issue alleged possession on November 16, 1998 of a Beretta pistol in and affecting commerce while the September 14 order remained operative. The present posture is pretrial dismissal, not a guilty verdict or conditional plea. The continued operation of the September order through the charged possession was undisputed. The firearm had been purchased from a licensed dealer on October 10, 1997; its prior interstate or foreign travel was assumed for the pretrial commerce challenge, not adjudicated at a criminal trial.
6. At that purchase, Emerson completed and signed ATF Form 4473. A question asked about domestic-relations restraints, and the signed certificate explained that an affirmative answer to the relevant questions prohibited purchase or possession. The form's reverse stated the hearing, qualifying-restraint and alternative-danger/force criteria. His negative response was correct when made. The purchase occurred before the injunction, but the form supplies a concrete warning source rather than a presumption that every citizen knows every criminal provision. The appellate account states there was no question that he knew of his possession, the order and its actual contents. The charged possession occurred more than sixty days after the order; the record does not present a good-faith dispossession effort.
7. The district court dismissed on Second Amendment and Fifth Amendment grounds after a pretrial hearing. It rejected the Commerce Clause and Tenth Amendment objections. The Fifth Circuit reversed the dismissal and returned the prosecution. It rejected a statutory construction requiring both alternatives in subsection (C), rejected the ignorance-of-law ground in light of the knowledge and written-warning record, and sustained the arms application. Its constitutional application depended on the substantive requirements of Texas injunctive law and the legal significance of the entered order; it did not report an express danger finding that the order lacked.
8. The appellate concurrence and federal response described later alleged gun-pointing and other threatening conduct. The appellate majority expressly distinguished those accounts from indictment allegations or facts found by a trier and reported that it had been informed of a state acquittal on related charges. None of that alleged later behavior supplies a proved dangerousness predicate, a federal conviction, a finding at the September hearing or retrospective justification for the order. The reported acquittal likewise does not erase the different civil proceeding or establish that every underlying act did not occur.

## Statutory commands and state-law characterization

Section 922(g)(8) requires all of (A), (B) and one of (C)(i) or (C)(ii): actual hearing notice and an opportunity to participate; an order restraining harassment, stalking or threats against the specified intimate partner or child, or other conduct placing the partner in reasonable fear of bodily injury to the partner or child; and either a finding of a credible threat to the specified person's physical safety or an express prohibition of using, attempting or threatening physical force reasonably expected to cause bodily injury. The final prohibition separately covers interstate or foreign shipment or transportation; possession in or affecting commerce; and receipt of firearms or ammunition previously shipped or transported in interstate or foreign commerce. Section 924(a)(2)'s knowledge requirement remains distinct from the statutory order criteria and from the constitutional defense. The exact text is mechanically preserved in the source appendix, PDF page 6; the warning form and its certificate appear at pages 14–15.

The absence of a (C)(i) finding does not defeat (C)(ii) as a matter of statutory text. Neither constitutional avoidance nor lenity permits changing that disjunction to a conjunction. That conclusion does not establish the constitutionality of every application meeting the text.

The Fifth Circuit construed Texas Family Code §6.502 to permit the pertinent injunction after notice and hearing only upon the necessary protective showing. It relied on Texas decisions requiring threatened, actual rather than merely conjectural injury and on the predecessor statute's reasonable-necessity and probable-injury requirements. This is a lower court's legal construction of the state regime, not an express factual finding in Emerson's order and not language Congress placed in §922(g)(8). The state text and cited decisions are quoted in the saved opinion at pages 104–108.

Under §6.507, an interlocutory direct appeal was unavailable. Modification by the issuing court and appellate mandamus for abuse of discretion were available; the order ended at the divorce decree unless replaced or incorporated, with final-decree provisions then reviewable on direct appeal. Availability of a procedure does not establish that Emerson used it, would have prevailed or was promised restoration at a fixed date. No statute-imposed maximum duration is inferred.

- Love v. Pepersack, December 11, 1995: the guarantee belongs to individuals, does not depend on organized-militia enrollment, applies to States, and reaches acquisition necessary to protected keeping. Coverage and permissible restriction remain separate. Its treatment of Lewis leaves the extant-felony disability undisturbed and does not make that disability a complete answer to a different nonfelon claim.
- Hale, February 12, 1996: personal invocation does not confer categorical immunity from firearm regulation. The actual weapon, conduct and restriction matter.
- Wright, October 5, 1998: coverage is followed by review corresponding to actual burden. A core deprivation requires a compelling interest, narrow tailoring and no comparably effective materially less restrictive alternative. A substantial regulation requires an important objective, substantial relationship and no unnecessary or effective elimination of protected activity. Incidental regulation requires reasonableness and cannot operate arbitrarily, discriminatorily or cumulatively to destroy the right. General abolition of meaningful ordinary defensive home possession is independently forbidden. The latter rule does not exempt every person, weapon or use. Mere safety terminology cannot reduce the scrutiny that the practical operation requires.
- Fraternal Order of Police, October 7, 1998: a conviction-based domestic-violence disability satisfies intermediate scrutiny on the defined official-use claim. It neither decides every private-possession application nor supplies criminal safeguards to a civil order by analogy.
- Napier, June 1, 2001: a civil order supported by adjudicated violence and recurrence risk, after actual hearing notice and opportunity to participate, may supply the danger predicate without a criminal conviction. Six Justices—Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer—adopt the substantial-regulation classification and intermediate-scrutiny application. No allegation-only, ex parte, unrelated-order or duration-only justification is approved. It establishes no general civil proof standard, maximum duration or restoration machinery. Its civil findings were not relabeled statutory (C)(i) findings. Scalia's dissent, joined by Thomas, accepted coverage and governing Wright law but required actual restriction-specific justification and would remand; it required no conviction and inferred no defective process from unavailable transcripts. ## Neutral alternatives and exact decisional fork

**Application sustaining the prosecution.** The law need not wait for a completed assault or require particular verbal findings. A hearing addressing actual threatening conduct, a force-prohibiting order entered under state law requiring a real protective necessity, continuing order dependence and available judicial review may supply a sufficient adjudicative basis for this application. The strongest objection is that a general presumption of regularity and boilerplate prohibitions may not establish that the actual judge determined a dangerousness predicate; an individual's entire possession right cannot be burdened on an accusation merely because the statute's words are satisfied.

**Application requiring a constitutional return.** Reject the collective account, a criminal-conviction requirement and a mandatory (C)(i) formula; vacate the arms application for review of the actual order's adjudicated protective basis and the operation of the full disability under Wright and Napier. The strongest objection is that the existing hearing, unrefuted specific threat, state-law requirements and targeted injunction already permit that determination; returning the case merely because findings were implicit would make a verbal formula constitutionally compulsory by another route. A return must identify a genuine restriction-specific inquiry, not demand unavailable files or retry every civil evidentiary ruling.

**Application dismissing the count.** A proven lack of any adequate adjudicated protective predicate could defeat this particular prosecution notwithstanding formal statutory satisfaction. The strongest objection on this record is that no such absence has been established: the hearing occurred, threat testimony was received, the protective order issued, and the record supplies no adjudicated conclusion that the state judge acted solely on unrelated boilerplate. The published record does not justify treating either insufficient proof of a crime or missing operator materials as proof of constitutional invalidity.

**Facial invalidation.** Napier supplies a valid civil-order application of this statute. An argument that civil process or absence of (C)(i) wording always makes the statute invalid cannot survive its holding. Any narrower facial theory would need its own sufficient legal ground; none follows from the order's generic wording alone.

**Collateral review.** Statutory elements, ordinary collateral relitigation of the state order and constitutional review of the resulting federal disability are different inquiries. A rule leaving the order in force until modified need not exempt the new federal burden from constitutional review. Conversely, recognizing that review does not automatically allow a federal criminal trial to retry every factual or state-law issue settled by the divorce court.

**Fair warning.** The specific signed federal warning and knowledge of the order distinguish the asserted claim from Lambert's unprompted passive omission. The contrary point is that the warning preceded the injunction and is no finding of later recollection; a decision can reject mandatory knowledge of illegality without treating the signature as conclusive proof of every element at trial.
