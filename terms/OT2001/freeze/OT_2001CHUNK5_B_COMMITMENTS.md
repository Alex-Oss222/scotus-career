## 69. United States v. Cotton — No. 01-687

### Components and alternative paths

**C1 — Judicial power.** All eight Associates would hold that §3231 supplies subject-matter jurisdiction over the charged federal conspiracy despite omission of a quantity allegation necessary for the enhanced maximum. They would overrule Bain only insofar as its jurisdictional formulation requires the contrary conclusion. This does not remove the grand-jury protection, cure the charging defect, authorize an otherwise unlawful sentence or decide a total absence of a charging instrument.

**C2 — Error and review.** The superseding indictment controls; its predecessor cannot substitute for the missing allegation. The jury was told quantity was unimportant and supplied no quantity finding. Apprendi's actual present-offense rule makes the missing element determination constitutional error; the judge's preponderance findings and notice of life exposure are neither a verdict nor an admission. The forfeited claim uses Olano's distinct Rule 52(b) requirements. Seven Associates accept plainness under Johnson's intervening-settled-law rule; Scalia assumes plainness without enlarging the rationale he did not previously join. All eight assume substantial-rights effect solely to review the fourth-condition method; none decides third-condition prejudice or structural classification.

**C3 — Judgment and remedy.** All eight would vacate the Fourth Circuit's reviewed sentence-vacatur ruling and remand for case-specific plain-error application on the existing record. The jurisdictional premise and categorical exclusion of evidentiary strength cannot sustain that ruling. The remand neither conclusively restores the original sentences nor requires twenty-year terms. The convictions remain intact. No new hearing, jury proceeding, grand-jury presentation or prosecution is directed; any resulting sentencing remedy needs its own lawful basis.

The remand creates **no individualized quantity-attribution requirement**. It decides neither whether aggregate conspiracy quantity nor whether defendant-specific attribution defines the statutory penalty fact. Even assuming the aggregate is the relevant fact, the lower court excluded proof strength altogether and did not exercise correction discretion under the proper legal rule. The court of appeals can perform that task on the existing record. No new findings are mandated. The choice to remand is not a holding that this Court can never apply the fourth condition itself.

**Path A — Vacate and remand.** Johnson permits consideration of evidentiary strength but establishes no universal overwhelming-evidence exception. Vonn is a recent, limited analogue for returning record-specific application after settling the governing review framework; its Rule 11 setting does not govern this distinct error. The strongest objection is that the 380 grams seized, conspiracy verdict, other testimony and notice make remand unnecessary. Those considerations support a serious no-correction argument. They do not require this Court to adopt the Government's disputed description of the whole record when correcting the appellate court's categorical error is sufficient to dispose of its judgment.

**Path B — Deny correction here.** The physical quantity greatly exceeds fifty grams, and the Government distinguishes challenges to larger Guidelines quantities from the basic threshold. The strongest objection is that aggregate seizures and notice are not actual element determinations, and objections to witnesses and attribution cannot simply be erased by describing the entire case as uncontested. Under Johnson, those concerns belong in a case-specific correction inquiry; neither strong evidence nor the absence of an element finding automatically decides it. The selected remand leaves this supported no-correction argument available.

**Path C — Require correction automatically.** The independent grand jury, missing petit-jury finding and sentences above the verdict-supported maximum supply substantial fairness concerns. But Olano and Johnson do not permit a jurisdictional label or a categorical exclusion of evidence to replace the separate fourth condition. Correction can follow its proper application, not merely the existence of a genuine forfeited element error.

## 70. Verizon Maryland / United States v. Maryland Public Service Commission

### Scope, components and operative commitments

The two Maryland dockets, Nos. 00-1531 and 00-1711, receive the same proposed disposition: reverse the judgment sustaining the categorical dismissal and remand for adjudication under the following confined rules. All eight Associates support this disposition and the common grounds below. The limited Question 2 grant is the starting constraint: assume without deciding that section 252(e)(6)'s determination-review sentence does not itself authorize this enforcement suit, and decide the independent section 1331 question. Consolidation with one another and argument alongside Mathias do not broaden that grant.

**M1 — Independent federal-question jurisdiction.** Section 1331 supplies subject-matter jurisdiction over this substantial claim that the commission's later enforcement order violates the Telecommunications Act and the applicable federal rules. The claim's failure on its merits, if established, would not retrospectively make the asserted federal controversy insubstantial. Interpreting an interconnection agreement as part of that controversy does not itself turn an asserted federal violation into a purely state-law dispute. Conversely, no rule is adopted that every disagreement about an approved agreement arises under federal law.

Section 252(e)(6) does not displace that otherwise available jurisdiction in this action. Its first sentence expressly supplies exclusive remedies for the particular failure to act described in paragraph (5): FCC substitution and judicial review of the FCC's action. Its second sentence permits federal district-court review of commission determinations for compliance with sections 251 and 252 and does not repeat that exclusivity. Paragraph (e)(4)'s state-court restriction concerns approval or rejection of agreements; it does not say that section 1331 disappears for a substantial federal challenge to the later action at issue. This ruling neither narrows the first sentence's express failure-to-act exclusivity nor decides the complete reach of the second sentence. State-law requirements preserved by (e)(3), the different negotiated/arbitrated approval standards and state contract claims retain their distinct work.

Bell v. Hood, as applied in the actual Steel Co. holding, supplies the substantial-claim/merits distinction. Schacht and Lapides preserve claim-specific immunity and cause-of-action questions rather than treating them as destruction of all federal jurisdiction. AT&T's federal implementation/state application allocation supplies context; neither it nor Verizon Communications decides this review question in advance.

**M2 — The challenged action is administrative.** This suit targets the later commission determination following the FCC's intervening action, not appellate reversal of the Maryland court's earlier judgment. The restriction on lower federal appellate review of state-court judgments does not become a categorical restriction on review of state administrative orders. That conclusion decides no properly presented claim- or issue-preclusion defense arising from the actual earlier litigation and no question about a disguised attack on a state judgment in some different case.

**M3 — A properly considered officer-relief alternative cannot sustain the categorical dismissal.** Officer immunity and the asserted indispensability of the commission were independent grounds sustaining dismissal below. They are considered only to determine whether those grounds require affirmance despite the answer to the granted question. Florida Prepaid's actual respondent-alternative rule permits that inquiry without enlarging the relief sought; it does not grant all the originally petitioned questions.

The complaint alleges that the commissioners responsible for the order will continue enforcing duties contrary to federal law. It seeks, among other relief, an injunction against that enforcement. Young permits adjudication of that prospective federal claim against the connected officers. Whether federal law actually invalidates the order is a merits question; accepting the allegation for this threshold inquiry does not decide it. The state commission's role in an administrative scheme does not itself remove the officer route, and this statute's review provisions do not provide the sort of exclusive limited remedial sequence that would be circumvented by permitting this prospective claim. The actual Seminole Tribe rule itself rejects automatic displacement merely because a statute is detailed, while retaining its own remedial limits.

The requested declaration's relationship to future enforcement, accrued private payment duties and the request that private carriers refund compensation remain separate. A requested private-carrier refund is not simply a demand for payment from the State's treasury; that distinction does not establish the refund's availability. No entitlement to repayment, retrospective state compensation, declaration solely about a completed violation or command enforcing state contract law is approved. Edelman and Pennhurst remain operative. On remand the courts must determine the actual claim and relief that may proceed, rather than deem every requested remedy prospective because an injunction is also requested.

The commission's absence cannot by itself make these connected-officer claims impossible: officers may supply effective prospective restraint of the enforcement they conduct. The district court must address any genuinely remaining party and remedy question at that claim's actual scope. This is not a universal ruling that a state commission can never be a required party, and it does not establish that every private refund claim can proceed without it.

**M4 — Questions not decided.** No ruling is made on the full coverage of section 252(e)(6), direct commission amenability, consent from undertaking a regulatory role, an independently sufficient congressional authorization of direct-State suit, or the compensation merits. Florida Prepaid's Article I permission remains actual law; it is neither rejected nor automatically applied without an unmistakable authorization inquiry. College Savings Bank's commercial-consent rule does not automatically classify a regulatory role, and Lapides's voluntary-removal predicates are absent from this original federal action. Section 1331 is a jurisdictional grant, not an implied damages cause or a determination that all pleaded relief is legally available. The equitable officer claim, statutory claim sufficiency, defendants, merits and remedy retain their own requirements.

## 71. Alabama v. Shelton — No. 00-1214

### Components and alternative paths

**S1 — Imprisonment authorization.** Five Associates would require trial counsel or a knowing, intelligent and voluntary waiver when the resulting conviction directly supports a jail term, including one whose execution is suspended. The rule concerns the judgment authorizing confinement for the original offense; it neither requires counsel for every misdemeanor carrying possible jail nor invalidates a valid fine-only conviction.

**S2 — Waiver and later process.** The Alabama Supreme Court found no established informed waiver. Understanding paid counsel, performing competently and receiving cautions did not establish knowledge of appointed counsel if indigent. Godinez separates capacity from actual waiver. Ordinary revocation adjudicates compliance with conditions, not guilt of the assault. Counsel then does not retrospectively provide trial assistance. No new counseled trial, reopening mechanism or automatic revocation-counsel entitlement is supplied.

**S3 — Remedy.** Five Associates would affirm vacatur of the thirty-day suspended jail term and preserve the conviction and other portions affirmed by the operative state judgment. Alabama's separate account that linked probation correspondingly falls remains its representation about linkage, not an express independent command of that judgment or a constitutional rule against freestanding probation. Three Associates would reverse the present constitutional invalidation of entering the suspended term because no imprisonment has occurred, while reserving its execution. Neither side treats ordinary revocation counsel as a cure authorizing future jail on the uncounseled adjudication.

**Path A — Vacate the conditional custodial term now.** Argersinger and Scott protect against incarceration based on an uncounseled trial. Here the court has selected an actual thirty-day term for the original offense, not merely left statutory jail exposure possible. Its execution would rely on the already completed guilty adjudication. Nichols permits a distinct, later counseled Guidelines use of a valid fine-only conviction, and its narrower majority-supported permission supplies no general execution rule. The strongest objection is that Shelton remains free and Scott deliberately uses an actual-imprisonment boundary. The five would apply that protection to the existing authorization of confinement: because ordinary revocation cannot repair the underlying trial, the invalid custodial component should be removed now rather than retained as an enforceable threat. The State remains free to choose a valid noncustodial disposition.

**Path B — Permit entry but reserve execution.** Scott's practical custody boundary permits an uncounseled misdemeanor adjudication while no confinement occurs. The strongest difficulty is that this suspended term supplies the direct legal basis of any later jail and might never lawfully be executable on the existing conviction. The three accept that execution limit but do not treat it as a present appointment violation. They do not identify or invent a state procedure that would make later confinement valid. This position preserves a conditional disposition without deciding the legality of a future deprivation; its practical usefulness alone cannot authorize unconstitutional execution.

Electrical Fittings is the strongest alternative.
