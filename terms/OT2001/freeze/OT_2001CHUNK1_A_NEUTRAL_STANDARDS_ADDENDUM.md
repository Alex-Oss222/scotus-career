**Common substantial-justification standard for governmental racial classifications.** Present operation is Eisenberg, May 30, 2000, for the supported K–12 educational objective; Peña's underlying standard remains effective June 12, 1995. Later applications matter to Justice-specific modeling: all eight Associates applied the common standard in Engineering Contractors, September 21, 1998; Kennedy's standards portion in City of Dallas, June 18, 1999, was unanimous. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retained their preference for strict scrutiny but applied governing precedent. In Eisenberg, O'Connor and Kennedy joined the actual-restriction common-standard portion and its remand; Scalia and Thomas acknowledged the rule's binding force while opposing the particular application and remedy. Brown's modeling must account for these later commitments and cannot generate a strict-scrutiny coalition merely from the earlier Peña concurrence.

The current common standard requires an important objective within lawful authority, a substantial evidence-supported connection, justified coverage and actual burdens, and a supported response to concrete practicable alternatives that comparably advance the objective with materially less racial disadvantage. No universal least-restrictive-means rule, empirical study, numerical threshold, consultation procedure or permanent monitoring requirement follows. Applying that standard to an intentional investigative classification remains a new contextual question, rather than a claim that the earlier competitive-opportunity cases already adjudicated police investigations.

This prohibits the specified state tax without clear congressional authorization and determines incidence from the enacted payment/remittance/credit structure. It supplies no exemption from Congress's federal wagering taxes and no refund amount.
