# Holmes Group — form fixed before assignment

One signed Opinion of the Court resolves the jurisdictional question after argument and gives the definite statutory transfer mandate. Part I explains why a patent counterclaim alone does not supply the incorporated arising-under source when the plaintiff's complaint is nonpatent; it preserves actual or constructive plaintiff amendments and does not assert that appellate jurisdiction is invariably fixed at the original filing. Part II applies section 1631's three predicates to direct Federal Circuit transfer to the Tenth Circuit, with original-date treatment and the continuing limits of the district court's conditional order. The legal distinction between the allocation rule and its supported remedy determines those two parts before counts or an author are considered.

Stone, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer support the complete formulation. Stevens's recorded amendment/timing objection is answered by the reconciled common formulation, without adopting his further policy arguments or inventing a circulation history. His full join is within the expressly reconciled permissible scope. Ginsburg and O'Connor withhold Part I's categorical counterclaim rule but join Part II's independent transfer application; a concurrence in part and in the judgment explains their adjudicated-compulsory-counterclaim alternative. That ground is not a logical subset of Part I and supplies no additional controlling rationale. No separate Stevens writing is necessary to preserve a qualification the common opinion already states.

The seven complete joiners can each carry the entire lead opinion; no split authorship is necessary. Stone's approved remand to the proper regional route under applicable transfer authority supports the definite section 1631 remedy on the now-established predicates; it does not require leaving satisfied conditions for another inquiry. No fallback supplies a Stone ground. Ginsburg and O'Connor cannot author the complete opinion because their agreement with Part I's disposition does not adopt its rationale.
