## 49. Mickens v. Taylor — March 27, 2002

### Reach, components and source limits

The question is the legal consequence of a neglected conflict inquiry, assuming the judge knew or reasonably should have known of this particular potential conflict. The supplied adverse-effect finding and limited question are respected. No defect in the hearing's evidence-taking, consideration of an identified strategy, credibility findings or definition of a particular alternative is invented. Section 2254(d) supplies no gate without a state merits adjudication of this claim. The distinction between constitutional entitlement, default prejudice and any applicable nonretroactivity defense survives.

There are two material constitutional paths. **M1** requires an actual conflict adversely affecting representation despite the inquiry failure; the existing no-adverse-effect determination then sustains denial within the question reviewed. **M2** treats a court's neglected duty concerning a particular known conflict differently from Sullivan's uninformed court: actual, unwaived divided loyalty warrants relief without a further adverse-effect showing. M2 requires reconsideration under that legal distinction, not a finding that every prior representation creates an actual conflict. **M3**, a more protective position within M2's reversal coalition, treats this court-created, undisclosed victim-representation risk as sufficient for the Holloway protection even without a separate actual-conflict finding.

Holloway's specific trigger is compelled joint representation after timely conflict representations, and its remedy does not require reconstruction of lost advocacy. Sullivan requires actual adverse effect where that trigger is absent and the court has no duty to inquire on its facts. Wood's text and remand create the dispute: its footnote 18 links inquiry failure to reversal, while its operative disposition still requires a determination of actual conflict and absence of waiver. M1 reads that actual conflict in light of interference with representation; M2 gives the known-conflict inquiry obligation independent remedial significance. Wood did not award a new hearing merely because a judge might have asked a question.

The strongest objection to M1 is that a defendant kept unaware of the same risk the appointing judge should recognize cannot provide Holloway's timely objection, and unexplored divided loyalty can escape later reconstruction. Its answer is that the protected assistance, rather than compliance with every preventive procedure, remains the constitutional interest: known risk does not establish impaired representation, and a hearing has already tested effect. The strongest objection to M2/M3 is that they loosen Sullivan's constitutional predicate, especially for successive representation, and risk a new collateral rule. M2 answers through Wood's actual-conflict and waiver conditions; M3 confines its proposed protection to the judicial appointment of the victim's recent lawyer without disclosure. Neither may infer the contents of privileged information from Swidler.

## 52. Adams v. Florida Power Corp. — April 1, 2002

### Components and two paths

**A1** rejects the categorical bar: section 623(a)(2) permits an employee to challenge a specifically identified neutral classification or practice causing the prohibited adverse effect because of age, subject to section 623(f)(1)'s protection for differentiation based on reasonable factors other than age. Identifying the practice and demonstrating its causal adverse effect are the employee's tasks. Reasonableness is an actual statutory limitation; correlation alone establishes neither liability nor exemption. This is distinct from (a)(1)'s intentional-treatment rule.

**A2** sustains the bar: read the age-causation language across the Act as prohibiting age-based decisions, while (a)(2) additionally covers discriminatory limiting, segregating and classifying before completed discharge or lost employment. The reasonable-factor provision confirms the permissible use of nonage criteria and addresses disguised age classifications without creating a separate effects cause of action.

A1's strongest objection is that Congress omitted the 1991 Title VII impact framework from the ADEA and that many legitimate factors correlate with age. Its response is that the existing effects-oriented text supplies the route while the ADEA's own reasonable-factor protection gives broader latitude than an imported business-necessity requirement. A2's strongest objection is that it makes the reasonable-factor qualification difficult to explain and underreads the same classification language construed in Griggs. Its response is that the ADEA's context and express nonage-factor permission materially differ from Title VII; not every textual overlap imports that Act's cause of action. Hazen reserved this question and cannot decide either path by implication.

The certified categorical ruling is a real controlling legal ruling. Barkett's competing sufficiency assessment was not adopted by the district court or circuit majority as an independent finding. Neither an affirmance on an invented pleading failure nor a dismissal of the writ on that assumed fact is supported. Swierkiewicz does not resolve impact availability or excuse substantive causation; its ordinary procedural rule does forbid turning an evidentiary checklist into an unannounced heightened pleading code.

## 54. Venetian Casino Resort, L.L.C. v. Local Joint Executive Board of Las Vegas — April 1, 2002

### Actual agreement, components and opposing paths

The complete mechanically copied agreement controls the model's description. Its sidewalk construction and maintenance requirement provides unobstructed passage connecting the public sidewalks; it binds successors, must be recorded, permits State supervision concerning hazards and improvements, and requires a dedicated replacement if this sidewalk is removed, altered or abandoned. Its private-property reservation, denial of a current taking, restricted state-action disclaimer and future-dedication clause must also be given effect. The County's executive position is neutral as to forum classification; its refusal to arrest is not an appellate endorsement of every union theory.

**V1** attributes this particular street-passage function to government and affirms traditional-public-forum treatment of the replacement route. Government permitted the former public sidewalk's removal while securing a continuing connected substitute and enforceable public-access obligations. The constitutional question concerns that route, not whether the casino as an institution is governmental. Formal title and a disclaimer do not erase the actual public function and control retained through the agreement. The future-dedication clause protects continuous replacement if the location changes; it does not make the existing mandatory through-route a freely closable commercial invitation.

**V2** reverses the attribution and public-forum judgment. The agreement requires pedestrian access but expressly preserves private ownership and contemplates a later dedication if the existing private sidewalk changes. Maintaining a passage open to the public is not itself an exclusively governmental function. Public safety regulation and construction approval do not establish governmental control over the disputed speech exclusion. It is insufficient that a former public route was nearby or that officials desired public-forum treatment.

The strongest objection to V1 is that it converts public-access obligations into a conveyance the parties expressly refused. V1 answers through constitutional attribution of the specific replacement thoroughfare, without adjudicating a new state-law easement or taking. The strongest objection to V2 is that it permits government and the casino to eliminate a public speech location by moving an indispensable continuing route a short distance while preserving its function and imposing successor-bound access obligations. V2 answers that actual governmental responsibility for speech access must still be established; location, use and public benefit cannot replace that predicate.

Neither side may rely on a controlling airport classification from Lee: the copied companion decisions contain no such majority rationale. Grace supplies the character of an ordinary public sidewalk, Marsh the limited public-function principle, and Lloyd/Hudgens preserve ordinary private commercial control. Brentwood and Lebron reject conclusive private labels only where the government's actual responsibility is supported. Forum classification follows attribution rather than supplying it circularly.

### Coalition and remedy limits

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support V1's attribution and traditional-forum rationale directly. Scalia and Thomas support V2 and do not reach forum classification as a freestanding merits question. The six V1 commitments do not rest on either airport plurality or an arithmetic synthesis of incompatible standards.

Affirmance preserves the judgment concerning this replacement sidewalk and rejects exclusion solely on the asserted private-title ground. Traditional-forum protection remains subject to the applicable rules for content-based restrictions and lawful content-neutral time, place and manner controls; obstruction is not immunized. No present record adjudicates every feature of the demonstration, casino-interior access, a distinct permit rule, a taking, damages or police-arrest entitlement. V2's reversal would require further proceedings consistent with its attribution ruling rather than this Court's award of an unestablished damages amount or coercive enforcement order.
