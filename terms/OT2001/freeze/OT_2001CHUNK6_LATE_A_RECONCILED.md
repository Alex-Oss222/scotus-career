## 1. Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Echazabal

**Retained result:** All eight Associates reverse the categorical invalidation of the own-health regulation and remand. The historical opinion confirms the independent model's distinction between the statutory example and the broader qualification defense. Section 12113(a) permits job-related standards consistent with business necessity, subject to accommodation; subsection (b)'s permissive example does not exhaust that defense. The regulation implements the broader provision without changing section 12111(3)'s definition concerning others. Express section 12116 delegation and its formal exercise satisfy the controlling Haggar/Mead threshold. Walton's formal delegated-regulation ground, which Scalia joined separately from its additional contextual ground, supports the same route. No new deference principle is needed.

The rule remains a significant risk of substantial harm that cannot be eliminated or reduced by reasonable accommodation, assessed individually as to present safe performance using reasonable medical judgment based on current medical knowledge or the best available objective evidence. Duration, nature and severity, likelihood, and imminence retain their prescribed work. Bragdon's unanimous objective method makes professional sincerity insufficient; its distinct public-health weighting does not confer special status on private employer physicians. Neither the historical judgment nor the present regulation establishes actual risk on this disputed record.

The historical opinion's footnotes confirm limits already supported by the neutral packet: disability was assumed for Chevron's motion, accommodation was not argued against that motion, and the essential-functions alternative was not independently decided by this Court. The model's rejection of that alternative as an immediate summary-judgment shortcut does not permanently foreclose the properly preserved alternative if it becomes relevant below. Reversal directs consideration under the objective regulation; it does not compel a new hearing or new accommodation inquiry. Footnote 3 likewise gives no basis to decide whether every safety-related qualification standard must satisfy this regulation. The approved rule concerns the challenged own-health defense.

The historical opinion uses OSHA's workplace-safety policy to support reasonableness while expressly leaving actual OSHA liability open. The model's bounded employment-safety explanation is consistent with that distinction. Neither an independent OSHA exemption nor an employee's willingness to assume risk supplies a categorical outcome. The regulation's individual objective conditions answer the principal paternalism argument; they cannot be replaced by a general employer-welfare judgment.

The complete historical Court opinion confirms the substantive assumed-promise posture and ordinary repudiation/election rule. An undertaking to accept an optional prepayment is performable when the option is exercised. An announcement of nonperformance can be acted upon as an anticipatory breach, but the promisee need not elect acceleration. Without an effective election, the cause ordinarily accrues when the performance becomes due and is wrongly refused. Section 2501's first-accrual language does not compel the earliest possible anticipatory suit; no discovery rule or tolling doctrine is needed. Congress can retract its announcement, and legislation does not make ordinary repudiation principles inapplicable merely because an administering officer must obey it.

The common current-law basis is Roehm and the actual, continuing-promise principles of Winstar and Mobil. Mobil's fifth-lease ground is expressly anticipatory in this timeline. Stevens's disagreement there concerns actual total-repudiation/materiality and restitution; it does not require him to treat this different assumed future-performance promise as presently breached on enactment. Ginsburg's Winstar disagreement requires actual contract-specific construction before ultimate liability; it does not defeat a limitations answer on the expressly accepted assumed undertaking. The model correctly preserves both objections instead of inventing unconditional merits joins.

## 4. McKune v. Lile

### Reconciliation of changed law and independent grounds

**Retained result:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the restraint on the challenged automatic consequences under M-A. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas reverse under M-R1, with the latter three additionally supporting M-R2's prison-context explanation. The full historical plurality does more than cite Sandin: it uses the atypical-and-significant-hardship measure to formulate a sufficient no-compulsion rule for a legitimate clinical prison program. That governing-law premise is displaced. The simulated Sandin Court retained substantive state constraints and meaningful deprivation as a sufficient, nonexclusive prison-liberty source, while the replacement threshold received four dissenting votes. Neither version of a due-process entitlement rule mechanically decides this Fifth Amendment claim. A historical alignment preserved through treating the rejected threshold as law would be unsound.

The historical plurality also states independent grounds based on the modest actual consequences, ordinary prison administration, unchanged sentence and credits, and the distinction between difficult choices and compelled testimony. Those grounds can be assessed independently of its Sandin rule. The frozen Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas positions rest on their own Woodard joins, their distinct Mitchell positions, and the prison-context reasoning of their Sandin dissent. The automatic link requires scrutiny and differs from Woodard's invitation and Baxter's evidentiary inference. It does not eliminate the inquiry into actual coercive severity. The three may join M-R1's specific severity conclusion without claiming that every ordinary confinement change is categorically incapable of compelling testimony.

O'Connor's historical concurrence expressly agrees with the dissent that Fifth Amendment compulsion is broader than the due-process hardship threshold. She regards these actual restrictions as insufficiently coercive while acknowledging that they are burdens, rather than merely the denial of an offered benefit. She does not endorse the plurality's claim that any pressure less than possible execution or longer imprisonment must be permissible; her discussion distinguishes consequences imposed through the criminal process from extraprocess efforts to compel testimony. The frozen M-R1 formulation preserves this distinction. Her own Woodard opinion and Mitchell partial join support attention to actual compulsion; her Sandin dissent does not convert a due-process proposal into a universal Fifth Amendment condition.

The historical dissent finds both the direct automatic condition and the substantial combined loss constitutionally important. It distinguishes Woodard's optional interview, Baxter's nonautomatic inference and Murphy's absence of a supported threatened revocation. Its reasoning reinforces the frozen four affirming positions. The supported package of restrictions suffices for their M-A conclusion without adopting every disputed historical characterization of danger or every additional detail mentioned in that writing. No specific increased assault risk, permanent inability to regain a level, scarce-bed finding or program-effectiveness finding is newly imported here. Valid rehabilitation is not by itself a complete answer to an established privilege penalty.

Reversal concerns this regime and leaves the distinct testing claim to its actual preserved procedural posture.
