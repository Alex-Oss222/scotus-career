### Common statutory rationale and exact deference

The seven reject the Federal Circuit's rule because neither the adverse-action scheme nor the reviewing statute makes completion of contractual grievances a condition of considering disciplinary history. Under sections 7512–7513, Board review of the removal necessarily can examine the discipline relevant to assessing the penalty. The absence of a direct Board appeal from an earlier minor action does not eliminate that incidental review. Parallel grievance and Board proceedings can reach different conclusions without making the Board's independent review categorically unauthorized.

Section 7703(c) preserves review for arbitrary or capricious action, abuse of discretion, legal error, failure to follow required procedures, and lack of substantial evidence. Overton Park supplies the narrow character of arbitrary-and-capricious review; State Farm forbids a court from substituting its preferred administrative judgment while requiring lawful reasoned action. Those principles permit the Board to choose independent review rather than waiting for every grievance or ignoring every contested prior action. Darby's prohibition on adding a procedural prerequisite absent from the enacted scheme supports the same conclusion by analogy, not by applying APA section 704 directly to this distinct statute.

The common opinion does not collapse court-to-Board review into the agency's preponderance burden under section 7701(c)(1)(B), or into the Board's own collateral-review standard. Greenwich Collieries preserves that distinction. Nor does the administrative review standard displace independent statutory interpretation under the actual limits preserved by Brown v. Gardner, Christensen and Mead. The Board's administrative discretion remains bounded by the statute. Chemical Foundation's presumption of regularity and appropriate respect for disciplinary administration do not prove that every prior action is correct or that Bolling satisfies an obligation the common opinion expressly reserves.

The strongest contrary argument concerns the risk that uncompleted review allows unreliable prior discipline to magnify the removal penalty. That risk does not establish that every pending grievance disables any lawful consideration. Independent review can provide a route for meeting the agency's legal obligations; the particular mechanism must still be adequate. The common opinion's conditional premise is essential: it does not pronounce a written notice, an existing record and an opportunity to challenge sufficient for every statutory or constitutional purpose. The completed reversal of the warning presents a different issue from pendency and receives the remand treatment stated below.

### Thomas's separate statutory-sufficiency position

Thomas joins the limited statutory opinion as far as it goes, while concluding that the Postal Service's own reliance on Bolling makes its statutory sufficiency a proper issue to address. The fact that Gregory did not independently seek a different mechanism does not, in his view, disable her response to the petitioner's asserted ground. This is a disagreement about which legal grounds the presented dispute permits the Court to resolve; it is not a new grant, a newly preserved freestanding constitutional claim, or a change to the reported litigation facts.

He would uphold the statutory framework. Full fresh review applies if written notice, a recorded action, or the opportunity to challenge above the original imposing authority was absent. If all three exist, the employee may show clear error on the existing earlier record. Section 7503 provides procedural protections for minor discipline without granting a direct Board appeal. In his view, the CSRA does not require the agency to retry the facts underlying every previous action merely because it uses that action in assessing the later penalty: the agency must establish the earlier disciplinary action, while the appealed charge and penalty remain subject to their own governing obligations. The contractual grievance route and Board route protect different rights and the statute does not make one route's conclusion an automatic prerequisite for the other.

This more affirmative statutory view is Thomas's separate ground. It is not counted as the common opinion's approval of Bolling or as a ruling that the agency can shift its entire burden on the removal to the employee. His objection to a general statutory linkage between the two review structures also differs from Ginsburg's view of their practical relationship. He accepts the remand addressing the overturned warning without commanding a reopening or deciding the penalty consequence himself. No comprehensive constitutional ruling on every application of restricted review is supplied.

### Ginsburg's judgment-only grounds

Ginsburg agrees to vacate and remand but does not rely on the asserted adequacy of independent Bolling review. Restricted clear-error review may fail to satisfy the statutory preponderance requirement in an application where the earlier merits remain unadjudicated. An opportunity to contest a recorded action does not itself establish that its merits have received an adequate decision. She therefore withholds assent to treating the mechanism as the sufficient safeguard on which the common statutory explanation depends, while neither holding Bolling invalid nor requiring fresh review of every final prior action.

Her remand rests on the Board's verified power to reconsider, Gregory's election of Board review of the removal from the available review channels, the express reservation of Bolling's adequacy, and rejection of a categorical premise that the employing agency itself cannot consider pending discipline. The reopening provision supplies a real route through which Gregory may ask the Board to take account of a revised disciplinary record. It does not guarantee that the Board will reopen or that she will obtain relief. A refusal to address a material invalidated predicate could raise an arbitrary-and-capricious question; that possible legal consequence is not a holding that every reversal mandates reopening.

The removal election is not an abandonment of the separate earlier grievances, proof that Gregory could personally compel union arbitration, or a substitute for adequate Board process. Ginsburg's disagreement with Thomas also preserves the difference between an action no longer open to a timely challenge and one actually being challenged: withholding a categorical command to retry all final discipline does not settle what review a still-contested action requires. The present record supplies no final adjudication of that question. Her position remains a concurrence in the judgment, rather than a nominal full join stripped of its substantive reservation.

## 10. J. E. M. Ag Supply v. Pioneer Hi-Bred - reconciled non-Stone commitments

**Disposition.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg affirm rejection of the categorical statutory-ineligibility defense. Stevens and Breyer reverse and remand because the plant-specific enactments supply the exclusive statutory regime for the plant varieties they cover. **Affirmance explanation.** Chakrabarty's broad reading of manufacture and composition of matter rejects a living-subject exclusion and treats human invention, rather than life alone, as material. The PPA accommodated obstacles to plant protection through a limited right and a relaxed description requirement. It does not say that an invention meeting the general statute's stricter conditions loses that protection. The PVPA likewise confers a distinct right under distinct eligibility and disclosure requirements. Its own saved-seed and research limits remain effective within that regime; they do not purport to negate every separate federal patent right. The strongest contrary argument is that overlap reduces the practical value of those limits. The six answer that different proof requirements and different exclusionary rights permit both enactments to operate, and overlap does not itself establish exclusivity. This reasoning does not depend on treating administrative practice or congressional silence as binding statutory interpretation.

**Dissent explanation.** Stevens and Breyer accept that Chakrabarty permits a human-made microorganism outside the specialized plant categories. They reject extending that answer to the very subject Congress addressed separately. The 1930 PPA expressly amended the general patent provision, imposed the asexual-reproduction condition, and restricted the right to that form of reproduction; the PVPA later supplied protection for seed reproduction with saved-seed and research exceptions. On their reading, the general utility route would bypass those particular decisions. Recodification did not undo them. The different eligibility thresholds do not explain why Congress would protect saved seed and research only for varieties lacking the utility statute's additional qualifications. Their conclusion concerns the statutory plant categories and does not exclude all living inventions or independently qualifying processes.

## 16. Chao v. Mallard Bay Drilling - reconciled non-Stone commitments

**Disposition.** All eight Associates reverse the Fifth Circuit and remand for proceedings consistent with rejection of both the displacement and workplace objections. All eight join the same grounds at the same level of generality. The historical participating Associates reached the same result; Scalia's added simulated participation rests on the authorized baseline and his own sources.

### Complete propositions, application and limits

1. Under section 4(b)(1), another federal agency displaces OSHA only through actual exercise of its statutory power to prescribe or enforce occupational-safety or health standards for the relevant working conditions. Mere possession of jurisdiction is insufficient. The exercise may consist of pertinent specific regulations or actual comprehensive regulation of the relevant category; word-for-word duplication of an OSHA rule is unnecessary. Minimal regulation of some unrelated vessel matters does not establish whole-vessel or industry-wide displacement.

2. The Coast Guard had not actually regulated the cited evacuation and emergency-response working conditions of this uninspected inland drilling barge through pertinent rules or a comprehensive regime. General maritime responsibility and the casualty investigation do not supply that missing exercise. The statute therefore leaves OSHA authority over these conditions. The result does not require choosing between the hazard-based and area-based descriptions of working conditions: no comprehensive or pertinent actual regulation has been established under either description. This is a bounded resolution on the supplied regulatory setting, not a holding that all uninspected-vessel conditions belong to OSHA.

3. The employment aboard the barge within Louisiana occurred in a workplace in a State under section 4(a). Anchoring in navigable waters does not remove it from the statute's geographic coverage; no general maritime exception appears in that text. The geographic question is independent of the separate other-agency displacement provision.

The principal contrary argument seeks coherent maritime control from the Coast Guard's broad responsibilities. It cannot replace Congress's actual-exercise requirement or convert unrelated regulation into comprehensive occupational-safety coverage. Recognizing genuine comprehensive regulation preserves coordination without erasing the statutory condition. The receiving court implements the rejection of both objections in the actual administrative posture. The citation merits were not challenged; the remand supplies no reopening of those merits or newly prescribed hearing.
