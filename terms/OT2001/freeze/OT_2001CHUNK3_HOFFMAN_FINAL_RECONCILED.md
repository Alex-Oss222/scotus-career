## Record and statutory premises shared by all eight

The en banc D.C. Circuit enforced the Board's limited backpay order. Review concerns backpay for unperformed work following the established antiunion discharge in 1989. It does not reopen the unfair-labor-practice finding or decide wages for work already performed. Castro obtained the Hoffman job in 1988 using another person's birth certificate and lacked work authorization throughout the relevant period. The Board denied reinstatement and ended backpay at Hoffman's actual discovery on June 14, 1993. Its order states the amount through the last quarter of 1992 and identifies January 1 through June 14, 1993 as the remaining period; this handoff invents no total or new calculation.

The ALJ and Board recite Castro's present-tense testimony about using the borrowed certificate and false identification to work in the United States. That is evidence supporting an inference of continued use through the hearing, not merely counsel's invention. The recitals do not identify a postdischarge employer, a dated new document tender or a particular interim job secured by such a tender. Petitioner advances the continuing-use inference; respondent disputes its job-specific reach. Respondent's suggestions of a verification failure or casual work outside verification duties remain possible explanations, not findings that any identified job was lawfully procured. Petitioner's suggestion that the backpay prospect or Board communications induced repeated fraud is likewise not an established motivation or communication. The ALJ's description of the driver's license and social security card as apparently obtained through the certificate is the ALJ's inference, not a separately reproduced admission of that acquisition sequence.

The Board's no-exception statement addresses receipt of the evidence and the original fraudulent acquisition of employment. It establishes no separately unchallenged tender at a named interim employer. The Board found reasonable interim-work efforts, describing work as a carpenter's helper, gardener, day laborer and mechanic's assistant, plus unsuccessful factory applications. That finding concerns efforts; it does not prove how each job was secured or that every job was legally available. Conversely, a statutory challenge does not erase the finding or turn all interim jobs into proved document violations.

The employer initially did not dispute the calculation method in specified answers, then disputed calculations and periods at the March 4, 1993 hearing; the Board found its method valid and reasonable. The Board also rejected the contention that undocumented status prevented reasonable mitigation. The continuing legal challenge to backpay and the administrative calculation contest therefore remain distinct. No blanket waiver or forfeiture is inferred. A distinct factual-calculation issue must be shown to remain properly presented if a proposed disposition depends on it; neither group below decides an unidentified issue by assuming either preservation or forfeiture.

Section 10(c) authorizes affirmative action, including backpay, to effectuate NLRA policy, subject to its statutory limits and review. Its separate actual-discharge-for-cause bar is not satisfied merely by finding a reason discovered after an antiunion discharge. ABF establishes that deliberate hearing misconduct does not automatically forfeit relief, while preserving review of misconduct that defeats a remedial predicate and of arbitrary or inadequately explained relief. McKennon requires an actual-would-discharge showing before its ordinary after-acquired-evidence limits, treats pre-discovery backpay as a starting measure subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances, and does not itself construe IRCA. Neither precedent mandates every remedial award or authorizes violation of another enactment.

Section 1324a distinguishes knowing hiring or fee-based recruitment/referral of an unauthorized worker, required employment verification, and knowing continued employment. Its employment-specific definition turns on permanent-resident status or authorization under the statute or by the Attorney General at the relevant time. The good-faith verification defense concerns the knowing-hire prohibition; it supplies no authorization to continue employment after actual discovery of lack of authorization. No finding of Hoffman's earlier immigration-law liability is made here. The original 1986 restrictions and verification framework govern the 1988 hiring and 1989 discharge. The 1996 acceptable-document changes, including deletion of the birth-certificate category, do not retroactively explain why use of someone else's certificate was wrongful earlier.

Section 1546(b), in force before the hiring, prohibits the specified use of identification known or reasonably understood not to have been lawfully issued for the possessor, false identification, or false attestation for section 1324a(b) verification. Its authorized governmental activity exceptions remain intact and no such exception is factually implicated here. The later five-year penalty is not the original-hiring penalty. No new criminal conviction or sentencing finding follows. Section 1324c applies only to violations on or after November 29, 1990; it cannot be the prohibition governing the 1988 original tender. Its later benefit/application amendments are not applied to earlier events. A post-enactment violation would require its own time, act, knowledge and statutory-purpose predicates. The present testimony may support a temporal inference, but no finding of a named later tender or a violation under a particular amended subsection is made.

These provisions do not generally criminalize the worker's mere performance of work. All eight distinguish employment restrictions, verification duties, specified fraudulent acts, labor liability and the legality of the monetary remedy. No vote rests on a literal claim that writing the backpay check itself hires Castro or on a general rule stripping unauthorized workers of NLRA coverage. No remedy permits future unauthorized employment or grants immunity from independently applicable sanctions.

## Competing lawful paths and exact scope

**H1: bar this backpay category.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse enforcement insofar as it awards backpay for unperformed work during established lack of authorization in the employment obtained by fraudulent verification documents. The Board's make-whole calculation rests on the continuation of an employment opportunity whose lawful availability the independent federal verification scheme excludes. Southern Steamship requires accommodation of the separate federal prohibition; Sure-Tan's legal-availability limitation matters even though its departure facts differ. The conflict concerns this remedy's premise, not repeal of NLRA coverage or literal criminalization of the payment.

The strongest opposing path says that IRCA specifies its own consequences, the money directs no employment, and the discovery cutoff obeys the knowing-continuation prohibition. These four answer that a public remedial objective cannot restore wages from the fraudulently secured, federally unauthorized position merely because no future hiring is commanded. Actual lack of authorization and original procurement fraud suffice for this bounded rule. It does not depend on proving that every interim job required false documents, a post-1990 section 1324c offense or Board inducement. The four do not resolve every case involving undocumented status, different immigration authorization or hiring facts, earned wages, or every other statutory remedy.

**H2: sustain this limited accommodation.** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm enforcement of the limited award. Section 10(c) supplies a retrospective labor remedy for an actual independent employer violation; IRCA's verification, knowing-employment and fraud consequences do not categorically withdraw that monetary authority. Denial of reinstatement and a cutoff at actual discovery preserve the prohibition against knowing continued employment. ABF supports the Board's public remedial function and reviewable discretion; McKennon supports separating the actual unlawful decision from later-discovered disqualification without treating its particular ADEA rule as dispositive. Southern Steamship does not require rejection where the order directs no forbidden employment, and Sure-Tan's actual departure/unavailability setting does not establish a universal bar on this retained remedy.

The strongest opposing path identifies the unlawful employment counterfactual and incentives for further document fraud. These four accept that continuing-use evidence is real and that neither an interim job nor a mitigation instruction authorizes false documentation. They nonetheless find the Board's known-fraud, limited monetary order a permissible accommodation rather than an override of IRCA. Independent sanctions remain available, and denial of all backpay would reduce the labor-law consequences of selecting this workforce for antiunion discharge. That consequence supports the statutory accommodation; it does not independently authorize a remedy outside the statutory grant. They do not find all interim procurement lawful, declare continued fraud irrelevant to every particular remedy objection, or approve automatic backpay for every unauthorized worker.

**H3: no unsupported explanation or calculation remand.** All eight considered a narrower return instead of deciding the statutory conflict. ABF preserves actual remedial review; FERC likewise distinguishes an independent sufficient agency ground from a materially misunderstood authority. Here the Board recognized the original fraud and lack of authorization, denied reinstatement, set the discovery cutoff and resolved the stated mitigation objection. Its limited accommodation is the very legal choice under review. The supplied facts establish no separate preserved explanatory or calculation defect requiring a different disposition. The missing full transcript cannot become an instruction to find a new interim violation, and the existence of a disputed inference is not itself a failure to consider the established misconduct. The four affirming Justices require no additional balancing formula; the four reversing Justices find the categorical bar sufficient without new findings. A demonstrated, properly presented independent defect would require its own analysis, not an invented waiver or mandatory new hearing.

### Historical statutory-conflict rationale and all six notes

The historical Court treats the Board's broad section 10(c) discretion as bounded by other federal law. Fansteel and Southern Steamship illustrate remedies that cannot disregard serious unlawful conduct and distinct congressional prohibitions; Bildisco and the other cross-statute cases reject automatic deference to the Board outside its assigned field. Sure-Tan preserves NLRA coverage while requiring accommodation of immigration law in remedies. The Court expressly declines to decide whether Sure-Tan's quoted work-availability language alone conclusively resolves this case; its sufficient analysis instead emphasizes the intervening IRCA scheme. ABF is distinguished because hearing misconduct did not itself make the underlying employment federally unavailable.

The historical Court identifies the actual combination of unauthorized work, original procurement through false verification documents and replacement wages for unperformed work. It rejects the claim that a discovery cutoff and the absence of an express backpay prohibition settle the statutory conflict. Its additional concerns include immigration incentives, evasion and mitigation through further unlawful employment or document use. It preserves the established labor violation, cease-and-desist and notice obligations, and lawful enforcement consequences. It does not order immigration prosecution or adjudicate earned wages.

### Historical dissent and its substantive position

The historical dissent accepts the labor violation and the seriousness of the immigration restrictions but finds no statutory withdrawal of the limited monetary remedy. Section 10(c) protects effective enforcement as well as compensation. It argues that removing backpay can reduce the cost of antiunion discrimination against unauthorized workers and thereby undermine the immigration scheme's effort to remove the employment incentive. The competing assertion that possible backpay induces unlawful entry is, in its view, too speculative. The House Report and relevant agencies' positions support its accommodation argument, without changing the actual prohibitions on verification fraud or knowing employment.

The dissent applies ABF's rejection of automatic remedial forfeiture for serious employee misconduct and questions why administration of the other statute by a different agency requires a contrary result when the responsible immigration authorities support the order. It distinguishes Fansteel and Southern Steamship by the employee wrongdoing that supplied the actual good cause for discharge there; here the antiunion discharge itself is the violation, and later discovery does not rewrite its motive. It reads Sure-Tan in the departure/reentry context and notes that the Court does not treat that decision's sentence as independently conclusive. The present money order requires no unlawful reentry, reinstatement or new fraud. It also emphasizes the Board's ability to tailor the award, the actual discovery cutoff, and deference to a reasoned lawful agency accommodation.

The final H2 position retains that substantive framework, with actual authorization, statutory limits and the source qualifications now explicit. It does not turn the historical dissent's deference argument into a license to override an established federal prohibition, make deterrence alone a source of remedial authority, or announce that no interim document-use evidence exists. An order requiring no future fraud and evidence supporting earlier continuing use are compatible propositions.

## Corrected primary-record and temporal reconciliation

**Continuing use is supported, but its precise reach remains disputed.** The historical Court recites postdischarge fraudulent procurement as an admission and describes acquisition of the driver's license and Social Security card as admitted. The predecision ALJ and Board texts are more precise: they report present-tense use of the borrowed certificate and false identification for work, while the ALJ describes acquisition of the additional documents as apparently resulting from the certificate. The first supports a continued-use inference through the hearing; the second is the ALJ's inference about procurement. Neither supplied recital identifies a named interim employer, a dated later tender or a job-by-job finding. The petitioner argues continued use; the respondent disputes a job-specific tender and suggests possible alternative routes. Neither counsel's account alone settles those propositions. The final handoff preserves the actual evidence and inference without asserting its absence or inventing a particular transaction.

**The corrected account is not a merits reversal of an administrative finding.** It identifies the terms and limits of the source that the model may use. No group finds that later fraud did not occur. H1 requires only established lack of authorization and original procurement fraud, not a proved later tender, Board-induced fraud or every-job violation. H2 considers the continued-use evidence real but finds the limited monetary accommodation lawful without treating a specific unestablished event as a separate defective award premise. A genuinely material, properly presented particular remedy challenge would require its own actual record and analysis; the absence of the complete recovered transcript supplies no default, concession or new hearing mandate.

**The award and cutoff remain separate.** The primary Board order states $66,951 plus the specified interest, less required federal and state withholding. Its note 12 explains that the stated amount computes backpay through the last quarter of 1992. January 1 through June 14, 1993 is identified separately as the remaining period. June 14 is the discovery cutoff, not proof that the stated figure is a final total through that date. No new total, interest calculation, finding of additional entitlement or enlargement of accrual is supplied by this reconciliation. H2 enforces the actual limited Board order with its own terms; H1 disallows the disputed backpay category and returns only for implementation of that conclusion.

**No blanket waiver resolves the calculation or mitigation issue.** The Board's no-exception statement concerns admission of evidence and the original fraudulent acquisition of the Hoffman job. Separately, specified initial answers did not dispute the calculation method; the employer then contested calculations and periods at the March 4, 1993 hearing, and the Board found its method valid and reasonable. It also rejected the legal argument that lack of authorization prevented reasonable mitigation, finding actual efforts at interim work. Those distinct matters cannot be collapsed into an unchallenged finding about every later tender or a universal forfeiture of objections. The continuing statutory challenge neither erases the effort finding nor proves legal procurement of each job. The final model identifies no separate preserved defect requiring a calculation or explanation remand, rather than assuming all such defects waived.

**The statutes retain their actual dates.** Section 1324a's knowing-hire, verification and knowing-continuation framework predates the 1988 hiring. Section 1546(b)'s specified false-identification and attestation prohibition likewise supplies the relevant preexisting document-use restriction; its later five-year maximum is not the original-hiring penalty. The historical Court's combined reference to these provisions cannot establish an earlier section 1324c violation.
