## Martin v. Hadix: actual positions

She joins Parts I-II protecting services completed before the PLRA's April 26, 1996 enactment and finding no clear textual command to reprice them. She would also preserve the earlier compensation treatment for later services in the existing institutional representations because the undertaking carries continuing professional responsibilities and is not a series of freely chosen new engagements. The differing statutory transition language supports but does not independently compel her reading. ## Buckhannon: actual positions

The complete May 29, 2001 entry has Ginsburg and Breyer joining Souter's controlling opinion in full, with Stone-Zsela and Stevens. Under the two reviewed FHAA/ADA fee provisions, a legally substantial action that materially causes an enduring concrete benefit may establish prevailing status without a judgment or consent decree. The claimant must prove legal substance, benefit and material causation; timing, expenditure, favorable words or voluntary change alone do not suffice. The holding is grounded in those statutes and is not a freestanding equitable fee power or an automatic rule for different language.

Ginsburg and Breyer also join the independent reasonable-amount holding: after eligibility, the court separately assesses actual success, excludes unrelated unsuccessful work and explains the award under Farrar and Hensley. A positive amount is not guaranteed; no mechanical damages ratio or automatic payment for all work suffices. Neither has a separate writing in this entry. O'Connor's contrary judicial-sanction threshold, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas, is noncontrolling; it does not displace the five-Justice rule.

This entry establishes their willingness to read an enacted fee mechanism through its text and proved practical success while keeping eligibility separate from reasonable amount. It does not decide contingent-fee reasonableness, risk enhancement or a section 406(b) starting point. Its catalyst eligibility cannot erase section 406(b)'s express favorable-judgment condition. The remand fixes no award and leaves the merits mootness judgment intact.
