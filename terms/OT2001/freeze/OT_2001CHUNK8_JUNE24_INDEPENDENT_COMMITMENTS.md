## 111. BE&K Construction Co. v. NLRB — June 24, 2002

### Reach, issue components and proposed judgment

All eight Associates would reverse the Sixth Circuit's enforcement judgment insofar as it sustains litigation-based liability under the Board's unsuccessful-suit-plus-retaliatory-purpose rule, and remand through that court for consistent Board proceedings. Legal review does not require deciding that every underlying claim was objectively reasonable. The dependent litigation-expense remedy cannot stand merely on the rejected standard. The underlying lawsuit's adverse results remain undisturbed.

The components are B1, validity of that Board standard; B2, the reach of the replacement statutory rule and any broader petitioning protection; and B3, the scope of remand and remedies. A shared B1 disposition is not a shared endorsement of every proposed B2 rule.

### Competing paths and their strongest objections

**B1-A — reject the Board's rule by statutory construction.** The Board may protect organizing against abusive litigation, but a reasonably based effort to obtain judicial relief does not lose all petitioning value when it fails. Reading section 8(a)(1) to impose liability whenever litigation loses and a retaliatory purpose exists reaches genuine attempts to obtain judicial redress and presents a serious First Amendment problem. Bill Johnson's supplies the labor-law accommodation; Professional Real Estate Investors supplies the later distinction between a tenable petition and misuse of process. DeBartolo requires a judicial answer to the statutory and constitutional boundary before agency deference can do work. The proposed common rule rejects the actual Board formulation without constitutional invalidation of section 8(a)(1).

The strongest objection is that Bill Johnson's expressly discussed later Board action after a loss or withdrawal, when interference with an ongoing judicial forum has ended; labor retaliation may operate through expenses rather than an antitrust effect. The answer is that the Court must confront that language and limit it, rather than describe it as nonexistent. Bill Johnson's decided an injunction against pending litigation and did not make the constitutional treatment of every reasonable completed suit the issue adjudicated there. Exposure to substantial retrospective liability can also deter a genuine attempt to obtain relief. The unsuccessful result is not a finding that the claim had no reasonable basis. This justification supports rejection of the Board's present rule while preserving the separate question of a differently confined process-abuse rule.

**B1-B — sustain the Board on the concluded-litigation distinction.** The Act protects employees against retaliation and the older opinion's concluded-suit discussion supports retrospective relief after a full judicial opportunity. Its strongest obstacle is the standard's failure to distinguish genuine pursuit of a tenable legal remedy from abuse: hostility to the underlying union activity does not answer whether the employer legitimately sought a judicial result. None of the eight accepts that obstacle as overcome on this packet. Labor expertise cannot itself settle the independent petitioning boundary.

**B2-A — transfer the full single-suit objective-baselessness gate.** Applying Professional Real Estate Investors to this form of labor liability would require objective baselessness before motive can defeat protection for an ordinary single suit seeking a lawful result. Its strongest objection is that the antitrust holding preserved other categories and did not decide all NLRA remedies. Scalia and Thomas are prepared to adopt that extension, with the same category limits. Kennedy regards its objective protection as persuasive but does not require that larger rule to decide this case. Souter adheres to the existing antitrust gate but does not regard his earlier join as a commitment to importing an entire test into labor law.

**B2-B — reject only the actual rule and leave genuinely different abuse theories open.** This avoids treating a judicially tenable allegation as immunity for fraud, an unlawful litigation objective, a pattern of proceedings, or independently actionable conduct. Its strongest objection is uncertainty about what proceedings remain permissible. The answer is a definite prohibition on reinstating the same loss-plus-retaliation rule, coupled with an express refusal to authorize liability under any unstated substitute. Any new ground must be legally valid, actually available and supported on this record. The remand is permission for lawful reconsideration, not a finding that another violation exists.

## 114. Trans Union Corp. v. FTC — June 24, 2002

### Reach, components and proposed judgment

All eight Associates would affirm the D.C. Circuit's denial of relief on the supplied First Amendment question, sustaining the FTC restriction as applied to the identified credit-derived marketing lists. This is review of the combined principal and rehearing rulings in one matter, not two judgments or a certiorari disposition. No numerical Supreme Court docket is invented. Statutory classifications and arbitrary-and-capricious claims outside the granted review are not newly adjudicated.

T1 concerns speech coverage and the governing standard; T2 concerns substantial confidentiality and material advancement; T3 concerns reasonable fit, actual permissions and alternatives; T4 concerns the confined relief and matters left open.

### Competing paths and strongest objections

**T1-A/T2-A/T3-A — protected commercial use, independently reviewed and valid here.** The sale of these lists communicates information: selection by a credit-file characteristic can reveal that characteristic even when the transmitted fields are only names and addresses. Their proposed use is commercial targeting, and the Court can apply ordinary commercial-speech review to this application without deciding that every information sale is commercial. U.S. West requires that review despite lawful possession, regulatory status and other marketing channels. It also recognizes a substantial interest in preventing unknowing repurposing of confidential service-derived information.

The actual external disclosure supplies the relevant harm here. Preventing that disclosure without authorization directly protects the financial confidentiality that the source records serve. This is different from suppressing useful product information to prevent consumers from responding to it. Thompson's reasonable-fit invalidation therefore requires attention but does not decide the case. Lorillard keeps advancement and proportionality distinct; neither the privacy interest nor a merely possible benefit automatically validates the means.

The strongest objection concerns the FCRA's credit-purpose and firm-offer permissions and the burdens of obtaining approval. Discovery Network and Greater New Orleans prevent the government from invoking privacy while allowing materially equivalent disclosures for unrelated marketing. On the packet, however, the permitted credit uses retain their credit-function connection, the prescreening category involves a firm credit offer rather than unrelated solicitation, and consumer written authorization reflects the consumer's own choice. Those distinctions are relevant to confidentiality rather than arbitrary distinctions among disfavored messages. This finding neither validates every statutory exception nor removes the actual conditions of any permission. The Court does not infer unconditional prescreening authority or a permission to disclose a full credit file.

Reasonable fit is established by restricting the particular repurposing that discloses the selected financial trait while leaving authorization and other marketing sources available. Other marketing mitigates burden but is not an exemption from scrutiny. Notice with an opportunity to object is a lawful alternative to consider; U.S. West and Fox do not make that alternative constitutionally mandatory merely because it is cheaper. Once data has been sold to an unrelated recipient, its disclosure has occurred; prevention of unknowing release is a direct confidentiality measure. This conclusion uses the packet's actual external-sale premise, not a finding that every consumer would refuse, that silence is a privacy choice, or that the agency proved a particular consumer's monetary injury.

**T2-B/T3-B — invalidate or remand for an inadequately explained selective restriction.** Stevens and Kennedy's U.S. West dissent gives this alternative particular force: confidential data alone does not establish why one marketing use is harmful and a comparable use harmless. The path would be compelling if the record concerned only internal use, if list membership revealed no protected financial trait, or if unrelated materially equivalent releases were permitted. The present packet establishes the different external-sale and characteristic-disclosure premises. Its credit and consent permissions do not establish that contradiction. No Justice selects an unsupported factual reclassification or imports U.S. West's internal-use objection unchanged.

**T1-B — no speech review because this is only information commerce.** This would avoid the commercial-speech inquiries, but conflicts with the unanimous U.S. West coverage rule and the communicated financial characteristic. Reno v. Condon's information-market setting does not decide this speech question. None of the eight adopts this route. Nor does anyone adopt strict scrutiny for every credit-list sale on this record.

## 115. United States v. Ruiz — June 24, 2002

### Reach, components and proposed judgment

All eight Associates would reverse the Ninth Circuit's judgment vacating the sentence on the theory that the identified disclosure condition is constitutionally invalid. The constitutional theory supplies no basis to alter the district judge's discretionary refusal of the two-level reduction. No accepted bargain is enforced, no automatic discount is awarded, and Ruiz's later guilty plea is not vacated.

R1 is appellate authority under section 3742(a); R2 is advance impeachment disclosure; R3 is the affirmative-defense term and the innocence promise; R4 is waiver, causal injury and fitted sentencing relief. The distinction between R2 and R3 must remain visible. The proposed common rationale is not a declaration that impeachment can never influence a sensible plea decision, or that every prospective surrender of unknown information is valid.

### Competing paths and strongest objections

**R1-A — determine the legal claim necessary to appellate authority.** A mere discretionary refusal to depart, when the judge understands the discretion, does not become appealable because the defendant wanted a better sentence. Ruiz nevertheless identifies an asserted constitutional condition connected to the denied recommendation and resulting sentence. A court can determine whether that assertion establishes a sentence imposed in violation of law; authority to determine its own jurisdiction does not create unrestricted sentence review. Resolution against the constitutional theory defeats this route to sentence relief. Mine Workers supplies that limited jurisdiction-determining power; the packet's Steel Co. requirement prevents an assumption of jurisdiction to deliver unrelated merits advice.

**R1-B — decline all consideration because the judge knew departure discretion existed.** Its strongest support is the undisputed discretionary ruling. Its defect is failure to distinguish disagreement over discretion from the claimed constitutional ground. All eight permit examination of that ground to determine whether the statutory basis exists; none recognizes a general appellate power to reconsider the discretionary choice itself.

**R2-A — no constitutional requirement of advance impeachment disclosure as a condition of this early plea arrangement.** Brady, Bagley and Kyles protect fair adjudication through usable favorable evidence; they do not by themselves establish a right to receive the government's full impeachment preparation before deciding whether to bargain. Brady v. United States recognizes informed voluntary choice without complete certainty about the prosecution's strength. Godinez separates capacity from actual waiver; Bousley requires real knowledge of the offense. Those requirements remain, but they are not an entitlement to know every fact that might change the expected value of trial. The defense remains able to decline the bargain, seek trial and invoke the trial-disclosure rules.

The strongest counterargument is that credibility evidence can be decisive to competent advice, and disclosure after a guilty plea may have no useful trial-stage function. Kyles's attention to preparation makes that argument substantial. The selected answer is about constitutional timing and the scope of informed choice, not about evidence being unimportant. A knowing relinquishment of trial can be made while accepting uncertainty about witness credibility; the present legal challenge establishes no misinformation about the charge, actual false representation, proved concealed innocence material or deficient legal assistance. Legitimate early-resolution and witness-confidentiality interests also counsel against requiring full trial preparation as a constitutional prerequisite to this bargain. They are general legal considerations, not invented findings of danger to a particular witness here.

**R2-B — impose a qualified duty for information materially important to plea advice.** It would protect important credibility information without mandating an open file, and permit judicial safeguards for demonstrated safety or privilege concerns. Its strongest difficulty is the proposed constitutional trigger: material importance to a bargaining assessment is wider than the received fair-trial rule, and the packet supplies neither a particular item nor an actual advice defect requiring that extension. No Justice in this provisional assessment adopts that new general duty for this offer. None concludes that counsel's presence or a signature automatically defeats a supported plea-invalidity claim.

**R3-A — reject categorical invalidity of the affirmative-defense clause in the presented agreement.** The innocence-disclosure promise remains operative and distinct; it is not redescribed as encompassing every favorable use or every affirmative defense. On the presented early-bargaining terms, the Constitution does not require the Government to identify and disclose every item bearing on a potential affirmative defense before making this offer. The same informed-choice principles permit acceptance of evidentiary uncertainty. This does not decide the treatment of a proved concealed item establishing factual innocence, an affirmative misrepresentation about a known defense, or a claim that an actual plea lacked knowledge of the offense or effective assistance. No particular withheld defense is found on this record.

**R3-B — deem all affirmative-defense information essential to voluntary choice.** The strongest argument is that a complete legal defense may matter even when factual conduct is admitted and thus may fall outside the innocence promise. That is why the two categories cannot be merged. But the categorical timing duty does not follow merely from that possibility: knowledge of the rights and charge and assessment of uncertain proof remain distinct. The narrower present resolution does not pronounce every conceivable suppression of a known dispositive defense harmless or waiveable.

**R4-A — reverse the invalid-condition basis without enforcing a hypothetical contract.** Mezzanatto permits an actual informed limited waiver but does not establish a universal power to demand any surrender. Here Ruiz never accepted the agreement. The Court therefore determines whether the offered condition supplied the claimed constitutional injury; it does not enforce an agreement she made. Because the challenged requirement is not constitutionally invalid at the scope decided, neither rejection of it nor the lost favorable recommendation makes this discretionary sentence unlawful on the presented theory.

**R4-B — preserve a remand solely because a benefit was lost after refusal.** An unconstitutional condition can cause a cognizable loss even without an entitlement to the underlying benefit; absence of a discovered hidden item is not a universal answer to such a claim. But this path first needs an unlawful condition, which the selected R2/R3 grounds reject. If that predicate were established in another case, causal injury and authorized relief would still need adjudication; a court could not simply award the value of an unaccepted offer. No separate harmless-error test or innocence prerequisite is added to the definition of a completed constitutional violation.

### Reach, components and proposed judgment

K1 is whether probable cause alone permits the entry; K2 is whether the destruction concern supplies an exception on this record; K3 is the consequence for particular evidence, any later independent warrant and the conviction.

The common rule is that probable cause to arrest or to believe evidence is inside does not itself authorize a warrantless, nonconsensual home entry. A valid exception such as exigent circumstances must independently justify crossing the threshold. The lower court expressly omitted that inquiry; the Court does not supply an affirmative exigency finding from the officers' asserted concern.

### Alternative paths and strongest objections

**K1-A/K2-A — correct the rule and remand the unresolved application.** Payton supplies the home-entry rule. McArthur's limited preservation measure required a real destruction risk and other constraints in combination; it did not authorize entry on probable cause. Knights depended on a lawful known probation condition plus suspicion, neither established here. Kyllo separates an earlier intrusion from a later warrant. Arvizu preserves the difference between authorization for one action and an independent basis for a later search. The present probable-cause-only rationale therefore fails.

The strongest objection is that observation of suspected dealing, the departing buyer's arrest and a risk of destruction may already support exigency. The packet does not establish the necessary factual and legal determination. Those facts permit an argument, not an automatic exception. The appropriate receiving court must assess the preserved claim and supported facts under the governing law, without an invented finding that officers' concern was objectively sufficient or an invented factual inference about what occupants knew.

**K3-A — remand also preserves actual exclusion questions.** An unlawful entry and admissibility of every later-discovered item are distinct. Murray requires genuine independence for a later warrant; issuance after entry is not itself independence and cannot retroactively validate the entry. Preserved evidence-specific causal and independent-source issues can matter to the conviction. Their actual predicates must be found rather than assumed.

**K3-B — order immediate universal suppression or invalidate the conviction conclusively.** Its strongest justification is protection of the home and the direct discovery of drugs on Kirk after entry. A blanket command would decide both an unadjudicated exception and the causal status of distinct evidence. None of the eight adopts it.
