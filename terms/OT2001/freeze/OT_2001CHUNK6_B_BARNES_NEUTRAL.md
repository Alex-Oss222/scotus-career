# Barnes neutral preflight — 2002-06-17

Research cutoff: immediately before June 17, 2002. The issue is punitive-remedy availability under ADA Title II and Rehabilitation Act section 504. Liability, compensatory loss and punitive relief remain separate. No commitment is made.

## Verified scope and procedural record

The petitioner’s question refers to punitive damages against a municipal government under these provisions. Respondent contests categorical exclusion and availability of a new municipal-immunity ground. Commissioners are sued in official capacities. The lower court held the Police Board was not an arm of the State; the reviewed question does not silently reopen sovereign immunity, disability qualification, transport liability or compensatory quantum.

The Eighth Circuit opinion in the petition appendix supplies the operative record: Gorman’s disability and wheelchair needs; 1992 arrest and transport in a van without wheelchair securing equipment; contested testimony described in the verdict winner’s favor; $1,034,817.33 actual damages and $1,200,000 punitive damages. The district court set aside punitive damages as categorically unavailable. The court of appeals reversed that legal exclusion but expressly required an independent district-court review of whether the evidence supports punitive culpability and an appropriate amount. Respondent’s counsel acknowledged that review had not occurred. Affirming legal availability therefore preserves the remand; it does not automatically reinstate the dollar award.

The Eighth Circuit proposed evil motive or intent, or reckless/callous indifference to federal rights, plus nonexcessiveness. This is a lower-court remedial instruction, not an assumed Supreme Court holding governing these statutes. The compensatory verdict remains independently supported in the present posture. A categorical punitive bar disposes of that component while leaving the compensatory judgment intact.

Newport’s municipal-punishment immunity was neither raised nor decided below according to respondent’s specific preservation argument, adopted in the supplied neutral packet. The defendants previously argued state-arm immunity, a different question. Reaching the new municipal ground requires express justification; governmental status appearing in the current question does not itself establish preservation.

## Statutory chain and present law

ADA section 203, 42 U.S.C. section 12133, adopts the remedies, procedures and rights of 29 U.S.C. section 794a for discrimination under section 12132. Section 794a(a)(2), governing section 504, adopts Title VI remedies. Section 794a(a)(1), federal employment, instead references Title VII; it is not the route here. Exact official 2000 Code provisions are copied in B_PRIMARY_SELECTIONS.md; full sections 12132 and 794 are also saved. Section 504’s funding relation and Title II’s broader public-entity coverage are distinct even if common remedial incorporation answers both questions. A funding-contract analogy needs an explanation of its application through that chain to Title II; not all Title II defendants accepted the same funding contract.

The 1991 Act separately authorized limited punitive relief for designated employment claims and excluded governments in that provision. It does not directly enumerate these Title II/section 504 claims. The contrast supports competing inferences, but is not itself an express punitive prohibition in the provisions reviewed here. ## Fair alternatives and remedies

Defendants and the United States argue that fair notice of funding obligations, traditional compensatory contract remedies and selective congressional punitive authorizations exclude this additional punishment. Gorman argues that incorporated established actions and Franklin include traditional punitive relief unless Congress limits it, and that Title II prohibits civil-rights violations independently of accepted funding. A literal contract model must account for that structure and Franklin; an all-remedies inference must account for punishment’s distinct predicate and the asserted notice limitation.

One merits path excludes punitive damages under the incorporated scheme while preserving compensation and ordinary prospective remedies. Another permits punitive consideration for sufficiently culpable violations and affirms the need for evidence and amount review below. A narrower municipal ground requires resolving preservation and explaining why it disposes of this official-capacity award without deciding every private-recipient case. Notice of a discrimination duty and a compensable injury do not conclusively establish notice or entitlement to punishment. Full-remedies reasoning must identify the enacted limitations that defeat it; a notice rule must distinguish the duty, attribution and remedy.

### Issue 1: Punitive relief under incorporated civil-rights remedies

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools permits appropriate compensation in an established statutory action and expressly leaves punitive relief open. Gebser v. Lago Vista Independent School District and Davis v. Monroe County Board of Education preserve recipient responsibility as a separate question.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** An existing cause of action, an actionable violation, recipient attribution, causation, and the particular remedy remain distinct. A funding-notice objection does not categorically defeat compensation for intentional discrimination.
* **Other Governing Law:** 42 U.S.C. §§12132, 12133; 29 U.S.C. §§794, 794a(a)(2); Title VI’s incorporated remedial scheme.
* **Material Other Authority:** Pennhurst State School and Hospital v. Halderman supplies fair notice of funding conditions. Newport v. Fact Concerts supplies a distinct municipal punitive-damages issue, subject to preservation.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Compensation is available when the substantive and attribution requirements are met. None of the supplied decisions makes punitive damages an automatic incident of every implied civil-rights action.
* **Open Point:** Whether the incorporated remedies include punishment beyond compensation, and whether funding conditions or public-defendant principles supply the relevant limitation.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The Eighth Circuit read Franklin v. Gwinnett County Public Schools to permit punitive relief absent an express exclusion. **Defendants’ position:** The incorporated scheme does not give recipients notice of punitive liability; contractual expectations and the distinction between making good a loss and punishment support exclusion. **Gorman’s position:** Intentional disability discrimination is a civil-rights wrong, and ordinary appropriate remedies include punishment for the requisite culpability; a literal contract analogy should not displace that remedial tradition. **Limits and alternatives:** Neither side may reopen compensatory attribution through a remedies-only issue. The Solicitor General supports defendants on punitive relief. Governmental immunity would be a narrower ground only if properly reached.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

**Plausible Holding Scope and Judgment Consequences:**

**Merits path permitting punitive relief:** Treat ordinary punitive relief as available upon the independently required aggravated culpability. Reverse the district court’s legal exclusion and reinstate or review the punitive component under ordinary limits; this would govern the incorporated remedies, not every spending statute. **Merits path excluding punitive relief:** Treat compensation and equitable correction as the authorized remedial scope. Reverse the Eighth Circuit on that component while preserving compensation. **Threshold or narrower path:** An adequately preserved governmental-defendant limitation could dispose of this award without deciding private-recipient punishment; lack of preservation prevents silently using it. No path establishes that every successful disability claimant receives punitive damages.
