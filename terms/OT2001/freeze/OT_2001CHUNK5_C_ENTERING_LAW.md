## Standards and Tests selection: State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

### State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254

**Governing question:** Amended §2254(d)–(e) restrictions on state-custody habeas relief, factual presumptions and federal hearings.

**Current rule:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state proceedings, §2254(d) forbids granting relief unless the adjudication resulted in a decision contrary to, or involving unreasonable application of, clearly established federal law as determined by the Supreme Court, or rested on an unreasonable factual determination in light of the evidence presented in the state proceeding. Under §2254(e)(1), a state-court factual determination is presumed correct, with clear-and-convincing rebuttal borne by the applicant. If the applicant failed to develop a claim’s factual basis in state proceedings, §2254(e)(2) forbids a federal evidentiary hearing unless the claim relies either on a previously unavailable new constitutional rule made retroactive on collateral review by the Supreme Court or on a factual predicate not previously discoverable through due diligence, and the underlying facts would establish clearly and convincingly that but for constitutional error no reasonable factfinder would have found guilt of the underlying offense. The two §2254(d)(1) clauses do independent work. A conflict with the Supreme Court’s governing legal rule can satisfy the contrary-to clause without identical facts; an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Clearly established federal law comprises Supreme Court holdings at the relevant state merits decision, including general governing rules; later holdings, dicta and circuit law are not that source. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a poll of judges, and mere disagreement within a reasonable range is insufficient. Material truncation of supported mitigation or objectively indefensible weighing can satisfy that condition. The legal inquiry receives no imported clear-and-convincing burden.

The failure triggering §2254(e)(2) means deficient diligence or greater fault attributable to the applicant or counsel. Reasonable effort is measured against the information and procedures then available; identified material leads must be pursued, but investigative success is not required and every public document need not be found without an informative lead. A generic request is neither necessarily enough nor necessarily deficient. State-prevented diligent development does not trigger the restriction. When it is triggered, its alternative new-rule or undiscoverable-fact condition and its clear-and-convincing innocence condition are cumulative; no free-standing miscarriage exception replaces them. Avoiding that bar does not guarantee a hearing or a writ, and independently applicable state-default, factual-presumption, merits and ordinary hearing requirements remain.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent statutory clauses, Supreme Court holdings and temporal limit, and objective legal inquiry: Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams) — I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part I joined by the seven other Associates.
- Materially truncated or objectively indefensible mitigation assessment: Terry Williams — III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part II with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.
- An additional Fretwell unfairness demand in an ordinary lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with the governing rule, with actual independent grounds still requiring examination: Terry Williams — VI. The forbidden additional Fretwell requirement also supplies a contrary-rule ground, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part III with Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.
- Applicant-attributed deficient diligence and cumulative hearing exceptions: Williams v. Taylor (Michael Wayne Williams) — I. The hearing restriction requires applicant-attributed failure, April 18, 2000; Ginsburg’s Part I, unanimous. Reasonable available efforts and unknown concealed relationships: the same decision, III. Authority: Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg's six-Justice opinion, 1997-06-23.

**Unresolved interaction:** Neither exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses commands a majority in Terry Williams; no Marks synthesis supplies one. No universal unreasonable-extension or refusal-to-extend rule is adopted. Section 2254(d)(2), §2254(e)(1), and the hearing conditions retain distinct work. Michael Wayne Williams does not replace the former-law Keeney rule outside the amendment’s temporal domain. Satisfaction of a statutory gateway does not itself establish the underlying constitutional violation or authorize relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

### Temporal reach of ordinary AEDPA habeas amendments

**Governing question:** Which merits and factual-finding law governs a federal habeas application pending when AEDPA took effect.

**Current rule:** AEDPA's chapter 153 amendments at issue, including amended §2254(d), do not govern a noncapital federal habeas application filed before April 24, 1996. The governing pre-AEDPA law remains applicable to that application. This construction preserves separately directed provisions, chapter 154's express pending-case reach and Felker's treatment of the successive-petition provisions; it is not a blanket exemption of all pending proceedings from every AEDPA amendment.

The same application-date construction excludes amended §2254(d) and §2254(e)(1)’s replacement factual presumption from a capital application already pending at enactment when chapter 154 qualification has not been established. Former §2254(d), including its paragraph (8) whole-state-record qualification, governs that factual inquiry. Capital status alone establishes no chapter 154 qualification.

**Authority by component:**
- Application-date construction and preserved exceptions: Lindh, Lindh v. Murphy — The chapter 153 amendments at issue do not govern this pre-enactment noncapital application, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Breyer (six), 1997-06-23.

- Pending-capital extension for the identified merits and factual-presumption provisions: Carriger v. Stewart — Holding 1. Former-law treatment of the pending capital application, 1998-04-16.

**Present operation effective:** Carriger v. Stewart, 1998-04-16, for the specified capital-application extension; Lindh’s noncapital rule remains effective from 1997-06-23.

**Exceptions and limits:** Carriger does not decide §2254(e)(2)’s temporal application or require an evidentiary hearing.  The rule creates no constitutional entitlement to preserve every filing-date judicial doctrine and decides no capital State qualification, exhaustion, merits or ultimate entitlement to relief.

## Standards and Tests selection: Counsel consultation and loss of a first appeal

### Counsel consultation and loss of a first appeal

**Governing question:** Counsel's duties concerning the defendant's decision whether to take an available first appeal and the showing for restoration after deficient consultation.

**Current rule:** Counsel must follow an express instruction to appeal and respect an express, informed and competent direction not to appeal; choosing appellate issues is distinct from the defendant's choice whether to appeal. Without a governing clear instruction, counsel must consult when there is reason to think a rational defendant would want an appeal, including potential nonfrivolous grounds, or this defendant reasonably indicated interest. Consultation requires explaining advantages and disadvantages and a reasonable effort to ascertain the client's wishes. Counsel then follows the resulting instruction; silence alone requires no protective notice. Exceptionally clear and informative judicial advice may make repetition unnecessary only when the defendant's actual understanding is established sufficiently for counsel reasonably to treat it as a substitute. A bare announcement of the appeal period and available counsel does not establish that condition. After deficient nonconsultation, the defendant must show a reasonable probability that adequate consultation would have produced a timely appeal; after that causal loss is shown, restoration does not require probable success on appeal.

**Authority by component:**

- Defendant's appeal choice and causal-loss rule: Roe v. Flores-Ortega, Public Projection / Holdings / I. The appeal decision belongs to the defendant; III. Prejudice means a causally lost appeal, not probable appellate success, February 23, 2000; O'Connor Parts I and III, all nine.
- Contextual consultation duty: same Record, II-A. Consultation depends on the defendant's circumstances; O'Connor with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer (six).
- Judicial-advice qualification: same Record, II-B. Exceptionally complete judicial advice can make repetition unnecessary; O'Connor with Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer (five).

**Present operation effective:** Roe v. Flores-Ortega, February 23, 2000.

**Burdens and proof:** Nonfrivolous issues and prompt indications of interest may support probable choice, but neither is indispensable. Other substantial reasons may suffice; an uninformed defendant need not identify arguments the missing assistance would reveal. Interest sufficient to require consultation does not invariably prove that the defendant would have appealed, and deficient performance alone does not establish causal loss.

**Exceptions and limits:** The inquiry uses what counsel knew or should have known, including the proved plea, retained rights, benefits and sentence terms. A guilty plea alone establishes neither complete waiver nor an agreed sentence or lack of appealable issues. The rule creates no universal signed waiver, colloquy, judicial record-making duty or protective notice.

**Related current rules:** Appeal-warning omissions and causal loss on §2255 review distinguishes informed nonappeal from causally lost review. Advocate-assisted screening of an allegedly frivolous first appeal protects a different function within an existing appeal and does not presume causal loss from nonconsent.

## Standards and Tests selection: Mitigation investigation and prejudice in capital sentencing

### Mitigation investigation and prejudice in capital sentencing

**Governing question:** Counsel’s investigative obligation and the prejudice inquiry for omitted lawful capital mitigation.

**Current rule:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make an informed, reasonable decision that further inquiry is unnecessary. A later claim of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to pursue identified records; competent investigation does not require presenting every item it uncovers. Prejudice asks whether the complete supported aggravating and mitigating record creates a reasonable probability of a different lawful result sufficient to undermine confidence, rather than a more-likely-than-not outcome. Ordinary lawful-mitigation claims require no additional fundamental-unfairness showing under Fretwell. Where death requires a unanimous Virginia penalty jury and disagreement requires life, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death is enough; a merely possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy is not.

**Authority by component:**

- Reasonable-probability prejudice and Virginia’s lawful outcome: Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams) — II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia’s unanimity rule, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part I joined by all seven other Associates.
- Informed investigation requirement: Terry Williams — IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation, April 18, 2000; Breyer’s Part II joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O’Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg.

**Present operation effective:** Terry Williams, April 18, 2000.

**Related current rules:** Loss of a displaced substantive rule and counsel prejudice states Fretwell’s separate unavailable-benefit limit. State-court merits decisions and factual development under amended §2254 supplies the independent federal habeas restrictions; a constitutional error finding does not dispense with them.

## Standards and Tests selection: Element-specific patent equivalents and prosecution-history surrender

### Element-specific patent equivalents and prosecution-history surrender

**Governing question:** Nonliteral infringement and the effect of a required narrowing amendment.

**Current rule:** The doctrine of equivalents survives the 1952 Act, but equivalence must be shown for every material claim element in its patent and technical context. Overall similarity cannot erase a limitation. Function, way and result may inform substantial difference without an exclusive formula; bad intent is not an element. Prosecution-history estoppel prevents recapture of subject matter surrendered through narrowing to meet a patentability objection. If a required narrowing amendment lacks an established legally sufficient reason, the patentee seeking the disputed equivalent must explain it. An inadequate explanation after fair consideration supports a rebuttable presumption of a patentability-related reason and estoppel at the affected limitation.

**Authority by component:**

- Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co. — Equivalence survives but cannot erase a claim element, 1997-03-03; Ginsburg for all nine on element-specific equivalence; all eight Associates, but not Stone-Zsela, for no bad-intent element
- Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co. — The patentee must explain an otherwise unexplained narrowing amendment, 1997-03-03; Ginsburg for all nine supplies the explanatory burden and rebuttable presumption
- Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co. — Legal claim boundaries precede unresolved infringement facts, 1997-03-03; Ginsburg for all nine supplies judicial resolution of claim and surrender boundaries before unresolved factual infringement

**Present operation effective:** Warner-Jenkinson, 1997-03-03.

**Sequence or decisionmaker:** The court determines claim meaning and the legal effect of prosecution surrender before an asserted equivalent can establish infringement. If legally available, genuine disputed factual equivalence remains for the jury under ordinary procedures.

**Exceptions and limits:** A supported nonpatentability explanation and its legal effect must be considered; every amendment does not conclusively bar all equivalents. Older prosecution records must be evaluated in context, without demanding nonexistent contemporaneous words or treating silence as misconduct. This preserves the patentee's burden and creates no automatic reopening, hearing or repeated opportunity. No categorical bar covers every foreseeable unclaimed substitute without a narrowing amendment. Complete judge-jury allocation and appellate review for every equivalents question remain open.

**Holdings navigation:** Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co. (1997-03-03) — Business and Commercial Law / Patent equivalents and prosecution surrender; Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co. (1997-03-03) — Who must explain an otherwise unexplained required narrowing amendment.

## Standards and Tests selection: Jurisdiction to enforce a settlement after dismissal

### Jurisdiction to enforce a settlement after dismissal

**Governing question:** When a federal court may enforce a settlement after dismissing the original action.

**Current rule:** Enforcement of the settlement is a separate contract controversy requiring an independent jurisdictional basis unless the dismissal order lawfully incorporates the settlement obligation or expressly retains jurisdiction to enforce it. Mere awareness, approval or party consent does not confer jurisdiction. Under Rule 41(a)(2), the court may make compliance or retention a condition of its discretionary dismissal. For a stipulated Rule 41(a)(1)(ii) dismissal, the parties must agree to the retained enforcement authority and the court must timely embody it in its order; a private agreement alone is insufficient.

**Authority by component:**

- Independent basis or operative dismissal order: Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America — Postdismissal contract enforcement requires a jurisdictional basis; A lawful dismissal order may make settlement compliance enforceable, 1994-05-16. Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt the rule, application, and reversal with remand. Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Blackmun, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, and Ginsburg; all nine adopt this qualification defining the boundary of the jurisdictional holding.

**Present operation effective:** Kokkonen v. Guardian Life Insurance Co. of America, 1994-05-16.

**Open question:** Possible reopening under Rule 60(b), as distinct from exercising jurisdiction over a separate contract-enforcement action, is not decided.

**Holdings navigation:** Postdismissal contract enforcement requires a jurisdictional basis; A lawful dismissal order may make settlement compliance enforceable.

**Related current rules:** Congressional reopening of completed private federal judgments concerns a distinct statutory source for reinstatement under §27A(b). Morgan Stanley and Plaut separately sustain its constitutionality; the existence of a statutory source alone does not validate every reopening command.

## Standards and Tests selection: Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments

### Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments

**Governing question:** A state class-settlement judgment releasing claims within exclusive federal adjudicatory jurisdiction.

**Current rule:** Section 1738 first requires the effect the rendering State gives its judgment; class form, consent disposition and exclusive federal jurisdiction alone create no categorical exception. Approval of an authorized representative release differs from adjudicating the released federal liability. Exchange Act §27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 for this judgment category. An incorporated, affirmed Delaware release has its ordinary state-law effect subject to Delaware's identical-factual-predicate and representative limits; alleged ordinary state-law error does not itself reopen that judgment. Recognition remains subject to constitutional authority to bind absent persons and does not confer state jurisdiction over federal securities liability or authorize coercive interference with federal litigation.

**Authority by component:**

- State-law-first framework: Matsushita — Section 1738 governs this kind of judgment, February 27, 1996; unanimous Part I.
- Ordinary Delaware recognition: the same Record, “This judgment receives ordinary Delaware preclusive effect,” Part II, seven Justices (all except Stevens and Ginsburg).
- Section 27 nonrepeal: the same Record, “Section 27 does not impliedly partially repeal §1738 here,” Part III, eight Justices (all except Ginsburg).

**Present operation effective:** Matsushita, February 27, 1996.

**Open question:** Actual constitutional adequacy, lawful binding and the complete permissible scope of collateral review, including effect of a full and fair prior adequacy determination, remain unresolved. No universal same-transaction release rule or fiction of individually negotiated consent arises from failure to opt out; valid opt-outs remain outside the release.

**Related current rules:** Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants supplies the independent constitutional minimum.

**Holdings navigation:** Matsushita Electric Industrial Co. v. Epstein, 516 U.S. 367.

## Standards and Tests selection: Recognition of injunctions and another forum’s evidence process

### Recognition of injunctions and another forum’s evidence process

**Governing question:** Whether §1738 compels another forum to execute a personal testimony restriction as its own evidentiary rule.

**Current rule:** A state consent judgment receives its lawful rendering-state adjudicative effect without a general local-public-policy exception; consent or mere modifiability does not eliminate recognition. But recognition of a personal injunction does not by itself require a separate forum to enforce a broad testimony restriction as its evidence rule in strangers’ distinct action, where their claims or entitlement to ordinary process were not adjudicated. Recognition, preclusion, personal enforcement and the requested evidentiary act must be distinguished.

**Authority by component:**

- Lawful recognition without a public-policy exception; bounded recognition/execution distinction: Baker v. General Motors Corp., 1998-01-13; Stevens Part I for all nine; Part II with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-01-13.

**Exceptions and limits:** Injunctions are not categorically excluded from §1738; nonparty status alone supplies no universal exception, and sufficient binding relationships remain possible. No authority to modify the rendering court’s decree, general witness-access right or immunity from lawful personal enforcement follows. Privilege, work product, trade secrets, admissibility and legally supported preclusion retain their own predicates.

**Related current rules:** Recognition of state representative-settlement judgments supplies rendering-state effect; Constitutional authority to bind absent litigants supplies its independent limits.

**Holdings navigation:** Baker v. General Motors Corp. — Recognition of state-court judgments.

## Standards and Tests selection: Certification of settlement-only classes

### Certification of settlement-only classes

**Governing question:** Rule 23 prerequisites and the order of decision for a proposed settlement-only class.

**Current rule:** A settlement-only class must satisfy Rule 23(a) and the applicable Rule 23(b) requirements, including typicality, adequate representation and (b)(3) predominance. With no trial proposed, trial-management difficulties need not be examined. The bargain and settlement's actual operation inform the remaining requirements but do not relax them; Rule 23(e) fairness and settlement desirability do not substitute. Notice and opt-out rights cannot cure materially conflicting bargaining interests inadequately protected by common representatives and counsel. Common exposure or a common payment mechanism does not eliminate material governing-law, injury, causation, defense and claim differences for predominance. A nonmerits certification defect logically antecedent to class-dependent standing questions may be resolved first, without assuming jurisdiction to adjudicate anyone's substantive claim; any individual action that proceeds must independently satisfy Article III.

**Authority by component:**
- Antecedent certification and settlement requirements: Amchem, Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — I. Certification may be resolved before the particular class-dependent standing questions and Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — II. Settlement does not replace Rule 23's requirements, Thomas with all seven other participants (eight; O'Connor absent), 1997-06-25.
- Representation and predominance boundaries: Amchem, Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — III. The undivided representation is inadequate on this record and Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor — IV. Individual claims and governing-law differences defeat predominance, Thomas with Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg (six), 1997-06-25.

**Present operation effective:** Amchem, 1997-06-25.

**Exceptions and limits:** No universal subclass architecture or categorical bar on nationwide, mass-tort, issue, future-claim or settlement classes is adopted. Constitutional notice and reserved collateral-review questions remain open.

**Related current rules:** Certification of multistate damages classes supplies the common Rule 23(b)(3) showing and trial-jury constraints; this entry supplies the distinct settlement-only application. Article III power before disputed statutory merits forbids assumed jurisdiction for substantive merits, without prohibiting the logically antecedent nonmerits certification resolution here.

## Standards and Tests selection: Mandatory limited-fund settlement classes

### Mandatory limited-fund settlement classes

**Governing question:** What supports contested Rule 23(b)(1)(B) certification on a limited-fund rationale.

**Current rule:** The asserted limit must exist independently of the parties’ negotiated ceiling. Proponents must demonstrate an ascertained maximum insufficient for aggregate claims. The traditional characteristics—a definitely ascertained inadequate fund, devotion of the whole qualifying fund to those claims and equitable treatment of claimants sharing the relevant theory of recovery—are presumptively necessary; proponents must justify a departure. Settlement desirability and predicted eventual depletion do not establish their own maximum or replace applicable Rule 23(a) and (b)(1)(B) requirements.

**Authority by component:**

- Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “2. An independent maximum and the qualified traditional limited-fund framework”, 1999-06-23; Thomas Part II-A with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Seven votes supply independent limit and aggregate insufficiency; Part II-B with O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg supplies the six-vote presumptive characteristics and justified-departure burden. Stone-Zsela’s stronger whole-fund condition is not counted.

**Present operation effective:** Ortiz, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Rule 23(b)(3) predominance is not imported. No previously segregated asset, certain insolvency, mechanical equality overriding valid priorities or exclusive bankruptcy route is invariably required. Materially conflicting present/future or insurance allocation interests retain the independent-representation requirement; fairness review does not replace protection in negotiation.

**Open question:** The full range of justified departures and whether unliquidated mass-tort claims can ever satisfy the category remain unresolved. No universal exhaustion probability, opt-out right, whole-company retention prohibition or replacement agreement is adopted.

**Related current rules:** Certification of settlement-only classes retains Amchem’s applicable-prerequisite and independent-representation rules. Ortiz applies their bounded logically antecedent nonmerits sequence without assuming jurisdiction to decide individual substantive claims.

**Holdings navigation:** [Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp. — 2. An independent maximum and the qualified traditional limited-fund framework](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#2-an-independent-maximum-and-the-qualified-traditional-limited-fund-framework).

## Standards and Tests selection: IGRA State-action authorization and unresolved amenability

### IGRA State-action authorization and unresolved amenability

**Governing question:** What Congress authorized, as distinct from its constitutional power to subject a State to suit.

**Current rule:** IGRA unmistakably authorizes an action naming the State through its State-party action, evidentiary burden, order to State and Tribe and successive State-directed stages. General federal-question jurisdiction, §1362 or scattered State references alone would not suffice. That clear statutory authorization does not establish constitutional amenability: no controlling OT1995 holding either sustains or rejects the direct-State action, imposes an abrogation-before-officer-relief sequence or overrules Union Gas.

**Authority by component:**

- Directly adopted statutory intent, distinct from the divided constitutional question: Seminole Tribe — Holding IV, March 27, 1996; six actual adopters across writings: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. The three conditional alternative positions supply no additional actual constitutional joins.

**Present operation effective:** Seminole Tribe, March 27, 1996.

**Unresolved interaction:** The five votes removing the State-dismissal direction combine two affirmative-amenability grounds with three avoidance grounds; neither is a logical subset yielding a controlling constitutional rationale. Union Gas's actual damages judgment, statutory holding and five-vote constitutional conclusion survive at their decided scope; Hans and Fitzpatrick remain unaffected. Seminole itself yields no general Article I abrogation permission or prohibition, waiver from negotiation, automatic reciprocal consent or delegated United States exemption. Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999, resolves the Article I question left open here; see “Article I enforcement against States in federal court”. It does not decide IGRA's direct-State action.

**Holdings navigation:** Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, 517 U.S. 44.

## Standards and Tests selection: Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers

### Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers

**Governing question:** Whether a State’s lack of consent defeats the expressly authorized FLSA action in a competent state court.

**Current rule:** Sections 203 and 216(b) expressly authorize an employee’s action against a public agency for covered FLSA violations in a competent state court. Congress acting within valid commerce authority may displace the State’s asserted nontextual immunity from that expressly enacted remedy, and Article VI requires the competent state court to adjudicate it despite lack of consent. This is an independent state-forum constitutional holding, separate from substantive wage regulation, statutory clarity and proof of liability.

**Authority by component:**

- Alden v. Maine — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Congress expressly authorized this state-court FLSA action”, 1999-06-23; Souter Part I, all nine Justices. Supplies unmistakable statutory coverage.
- Alden v. Maine — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Maine’s immunity cannot defeat the expressly enacted state-court remedy”, 1999-06-23; Souter Part II with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. Supplies the constitutional displacement and Article VI enforcement obligation.

**Present operation effective:** Alden, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule does not rest on constructive consent and establishes no general federal-court amenability rule or result under every statute. Coverage, exemptions, violations, limitation and remedial conditions remain. Hilton’s specialized FELA state-court rule survives; Hans, retained Union Gas and the divided Seminole direct-State issue retain their own scopes.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit

### Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit

**Governing question:** Whether explicit prospective regulation and continued ordinary commercial activity establish State consent to private federal enforcement.

**Current rule:** A State’s continued operation of an otherwise lawful commercial undertaking is not unequivocal submission to federal jurisdiction merely because Congress clearly and prospectively subjects that activity to private federal enforcement, even if the undertaking is realistically optional. Unmistakable congressional intent remains distinct from the State’s voluntary act of consent; an opportunity to leave ordinary commerce does not itself constitute acceptance of a specifically offered federal benefit or voluntary invocation of judicial authority. A State’s continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes federal patent suit likewise is not consent. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available.

**Authority by component:**

- College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit”, 1999-06-23; Part III with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part IV, all nine Justices. Applies College Savings Bank Part III to continued operation of the prepaid-tuition program after the express patent remedy took effect; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept that holding as controlling.

**Present operation effective:** College Savings Bank and Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Actual voluntary waiver, properly conditioned federal benefits and interstate compacts remain distinct. Those decisions do not resolve particular funding or coercion questions, litigation-waiver acts or governmental-function classifications. Bradley separately supplies IDEA-specific consent from actual voluntary acceptance under its express funding condition; other benefits and coercion questions remain distinct. Hilton’s retained FELA coverage and specialized state-court enforcement remain intact.

**Former-rule note:** The residual Parden inference of federal-forum consent from ordinary commercial participation fails even under the new explicit prospective enactment. This does not wholesale overrule retained substantive FELA coverage or Hilton’s state-court rule.

**Open question:** Whether a State’s litigation conduct—appearance, a §1404(a) transfer request or requests for declarations of invalidity, noninfringement and unenforceability—waives immunity remains undecided. Florida Prepaid need not decide it because its Section 5 and Article I holdings sustain the judgment, and it makes no waiver finding.

**Holdings navigation:** [College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board — Continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#continued-ordinary-commerce-is-not-unequivocal-consent-to-federal-suit); [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#continued-commercial-participation-does-not-supply-consent-to-this-suit).

**Related current rules:** IDEA funding acceptance and program-specific federal-suit consent supplies Bradley’s bounded accepted-benefit holding of June 12, 2000, without changing the commercial-participation rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Express State coverage under federal intellectual-property remedies

### Express State coverage under federal intellectual-property remedies

**Governing question:** What the Lanham and patent statutes expressly authorize independently of constitutional amenability.

**Current rule:** Lanham Act §§1122(a)–(b) and 1125(a)(1)(B), (a)(2) unmistakably subject States and instrumentalities to federal suit and otherwise available legal and equitable remedies for covered violations on or after October 27, 1992. Patent Act §§271(h) and 296 independently expressly authorize private federal patent actions against States and instrumentalities for covered violations on or after October 28, 1992, with otherwise available patent remedies on their own terms. Each clarity conclusion is distinct from constitutional power, State consent, the underlying violation and entitlement to a particular award.

**Authority by component:**

## Standards and Tests selection: Article I enforcement against States in federal court

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

#### Williams v. Taylor (Terry Williams), 529 U.S. 362 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-8384.
**Decided:** April 18, 2000.

##### I. Section 2254(d)(1) has independent clauses and objective legal limits

**Holding:** For a claim adjudicated on its merits in state court, the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses perform independent work: conflict with this Court's governing legal rule can satisfy the first without factual identity, and an opposite result on materially indistinguishable facts also qualifies. Unreasonable application is an objective legal inquiry, not a count of judges; clearly established federal law consists of this Court's holdings at the relevant state merits decision, and a governing rule such as Strickland is not excluded merely because it is general.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies eight direct votes for these common propositions. It does not resolve the competing exclusive general allocations between the clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither four-Justice exclusive general allocation between the contrary-to and unreasonable-application clauses controls, and the Court adopts no Marks synthesis or universal extension/refusal-to-extend rule. The relevant state merits date is June 6, 1997. The legal inquiry acquires no clear-and-convincing burden from factual-review provisions; sections 2254(d)(2), (e)(1) and (e)(2) keep their independent conditions. No facial Article III or Suspension Clause question is decided.

##### II. Lawful mitigation receives ordinary prejudice review under Virginia's unanimity rule

**Holding:** In an ordinary sentencing ineffective-assistance claim, Strickland requires a reasonable probability of a different lawful result, sufficient to undermine confidence, assessed on the full supported aggravating and mitigating record; Fretwell adds no separate unfairness requirement. Because Virginia requires unanimity for death and directs life if the penalty jury cannot agree, a reasonable probability that one conscientious juror would decline death suffices to change the legally available outcome; a bare possible holdout or idiosyncratic mercy does not.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I, with the same eight-Associate coalition, directly adopts these rules. The seven-Justice Part II applies them in finding the actual constitutional violation.

##### III. The state prejudice application falls outside the range permitted by governing law

**Holding:** A state court unreasonably applies Strickland when its prejudice inquiry materially truncates the supported mitigation or weighs it in an objectively indefensible way; mere disagreement among reasonable jurists does not satisfy section 2254(d)(1). The actual treatment of Williams's documentary deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence, considered with the complete sentencing record, is objectively unreasonable and independently satisfies the statutory condition for review of his constitutional entitlement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies seven direct votes. The four Justices favoring broader governing-rule review independently join this actual objective-unreasonableness conclusion; their broader construction is unnecessary to it.

**Material application:** The actual prejudice analysis materially understated the documentary childhood deprivation, functional limitations and structured-setting evidence. The full assessment includes severe aggravation, adverse juvenile history, later family improvement, custodial misconduct and possible parole. The holding relies on neither a new diagnosis nor presumed credit or admissibility for every proffer.

##### IV. The preserved adequate-performance defense fails on the actual investigation

**Holding:** Counsel must reasonably investigate available mitigation or make a reasonable, informed decision that further investigation is unnecessary; a later assertion of strategy does not excuse an uninformed failure to investigate identified records. Williams's counsel performed deficiently by failing to obtain the available youth records and develop the supported mitigation, independently of tactical decisions about particular cumulative witnesses.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's same seven-Justice coalition adopts the performance rule and actual deficiency finding. Scalia and Thomas assume deficiency without deciding it; their votes do not support a performance holding.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Reasonable investigation enables an informed presentation choice; it does not compel use of every record or cumulative witness. The Court resolves the preserved adequate-performance defense on the available investigation record, not on assumptions below, illness allegations or a freestanding expert-access claim.

##### V. The complete sentencing picture establishes actual prejudice and sentence-only relief

**Holding:** When the supported evidence competent mitigation investigation would have produced is considered with the actual aggravation and defense evidence, there is a reasonable probability that at least one conscientious Virginia juror would have declined death. Williams therefore establishes both Strickland requirements and is entitled to a conditional writ requiring constitutionally adequate resentencing within a reasonable period or a lawful noncapital sentence, while his convictions and independent ungranted claims remain undisturbed.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II's seven-Justice coalition adopts actual prejudice and the stated remedy, independently of any broader general construction of section 2254(d)(1).

**Operative remedy or transition:** The lower federal court must implement a conditional sentence writ, allowing Virginia a reasonable period that court fixes for constitutionally adequate resentencing or replacement of death with a lawful noncapital sentence. Convictions and independent ungranted claims remain. No life-without-parole sentence, new evidentiary hearing, acquittal, immediate release or execution/stay action is ordered.

**Independent alternative holding:** A state merits rejection that requires additional fundamental unfairness beyond ordinary reasonable-probability prejudice for this lawful-mitigation claim conflicts with Strickland and Fretwell's express limitation and is contrary to clearly established Supreme Court law. **Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** Part III of Breyer's opinion, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for this alternative gateway ground. It does not establish either group's exclusive general allocation of the statutory clauses.

**Limits and questions not reached:** This six-Justice alternative supplies statutory eligibility in addition to the seven-Justice unreasonable-application ground. It does not replace the separate seven-Justice findings of actual deficient performance and prejudice needed for the writ, or establish either competing exclusive general clause allocation.

## Holdings selection: Roe v. Flores-Ortega, 528 U.S. 470

#### Roe v. Flores-Ortega, 528 U.S. 470

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1441.

**Decided:** February 23, 2000.

##### I. The appeal decision belongs to the defendant

**Holding:** Counsel must carry out a defendant's express instruction to take an available first appeal and must respect an express, informed and competent direction not to appeal. Counsel's authority to choose issues for an appeal does not transfer the defendant's decision whether to appeal to counsel.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices; nine directly adopt this proposition.

**Material application:** The hearing found no proved promise, no consent to nonfiling, little or no appeal understanding and an actual conversation. None establishes either an express appeal instruction or informed competent declination.

##### II-A. Consultation depends on the defendant's circumstances

**Holding:** When no clear instruction governs, counsel must consult when the circumstances give counsel reason to think that a rational defendant would want an appeal, including because of potentially nonfrivolous grounds, or that this defendant reasonably indicated interest in appealing. Consultation means explaining the advantages and disadvantages of appeal and making a reasonable effort to discover the client's wishes; after adequate consultation counsel must respect the resulting instructions, while silence alone requires no protective notice.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-A of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer, making six votes. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg do not join this duty formulation. The judicial-advice qualification in Part II-B has its separately stated coalition.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The inquiry considers all information counsel knew or should have known. Plea terms, retained rights, benefits and conformity of sentence to a bargain matter only as proved; a guilty plea alone establishes neither complete waiver nor frivolousness. Failure to make an informed demand is no categorical safe harbor.

##### II-B. Exceptionally complete judicial advice can make repetition unnecessary

**Holding:** When judicial instructions about this defendant's appeal rights are so clear and informative, and the defendant's actual understanding so established, that counsel can reasonably regard them as a substitute for consultation, counsel need not repeat the advice. A judge's bare announcement of the appeal period and availability of counsel does not itself establish that condition.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II-B of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; those five directly adopt this limited qualification. Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg do not join it.

**Material application:** The finding of little or no understanding prevents treating the judge's warning alone as sufficient. The conversation's unknown contents establish neither adequate consultation nor informed nonappeal.

##### III. Prejudice means a causally lost appeal, not probable appellate success

**Holding:** When deficient nonconsultation is established, the defendant must show a reasonable probability that adequate consultation would have led him to take a timely appeal. **Proposition-level authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices; nine directly adopt the entire causal-loss rule and its proof qualifications.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Nonfrivolous grounds and prompt interest may support causal loss, but neither is indispensable. An uninformed defendant need not identify arguments missing assistance would have revealed; other substantial reasons may show the probable choice. Interest triggering consultation does not necessarily prove an eventual appeal, and deficiency alone does not invariably show loss. The late notice has evidentiary weight without being conclusive.

**Holding:** The absence of consent to nonfiling alone does not establish both deficient assistance and causal loss under the governing consultation standard. Because the limited promise hearing did not resolve those inquiries, the judgment directing conditional restoration must be vacated and the individual Strickland claim returned for the necessary findings and application.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part IV of O'Connor's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer; six directly adopt vacatur and this remand. Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg would retain conditional restoration on the existing circumstances.

The lower court's Teague ruling on the categorical no-consent rule does not dispose of the individual Strickland claim. No categorical-rule retroactivity holding, Teague exception, universal signed waiver, colloquy, judicial record-making obligation or mandatory protective notice is created; the conviction's merits remain outside review.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The conditional-restoration judgment is vacated, 6-3, and remanded for consultation, deficiency and timely-appeal causation findings. Further factfinding follows only if ordinary procedure and unresolved material issues require it. Conditional restoration remains possible on the required showing; no immediate appeal, release, new trial or mandatory new hearing is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Strickland v. Washington: applied to actual consultation performance and causal loss; probable appellate success is not required after loss is established.
- Jones v. Barnes: applied to distinguish the client's appeal decision from counsel's choice of appellate issues.
- Rodriquez v. United States: applied to reject an appellate-merits prerequisite after causal deprivation of the appeal.
- United States v. Cronic: its distinction between missing assistance and defective performance remains; this decision does not presume a missing function from nonconsent alone.

##### Congress unmistakably authorized this federal patent action against States

**Holding:** Sections 271(h) and 296 expressly subject States and their instrumentalities to private patent actions in federal court for covered violations on or after October 28, 1992. **Holding:** A respondent may defend an unchanged favorable judgment on an appropriately supported alternative that neither enlarges its rights nor reduces the adversary's rights under that judgment. CSB's posture permits consideration of its preserved defenses of the immunity denial, but preservation and briefing alone do not expand the §5 question or establish an alternative's merits.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I has all nine Justices' joins at this precise level. Their actual decisions on the alternative grounds remain distinct.

##### Section 5 independently sustains the authorized patent action

**Holding:** Section 5 permits Congress to prevent unconstitutional property deprivations through congruent and proportional enforcement legislation reaching some conduct that is not independently unconstitutional. Sections 271(h) and 296 validly authorize this patent action on that basis; the plaintiff need not establish a separate constitutional violation to proceed under the Act.

**Proposition-level authority:** Stevens's Part II, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer, states an independently sufficient holding of the Court. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas disagree.

##### Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action

**Holding:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State's exposure to suit. ## Holdings selection: Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett, 531 U.S. 356

Source: [state/holdings/federal-courts-immunities-and-remedies.md](../../../state/holdings/federal-courts-immunities-and-remedies.md). Verbatim substantive reading copy.

#### Board of Trustees of the University of Alabama v. Garrett, 531 U.S. 356

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1240.
**Decided:** February 21, 2001.

##### Whether Congress validly authorizes private Title I employment actions against state employers

**Holding:** Congress may subject a State to the expressly authorized private Title I federal action when the covered employment obligation and its enforcement measure fall within the Commerce and Necessary and Proper Clauses. Those conditions are satisfied here, so the State defendants have no categorical immunity from these actions; authorization and amenability establish neither liability nor entitlement to a particular award.

**Proposition-level authority:** Opinion of the Court (Stone-Zsela), joined by Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; the same five Justices adopt both independent grounds.

**Material application:** Section 12202 unmistakably authorizes the actions and section 12117 supplies the enforcement channels. Covered employment is an economic relationship in actual labor markets; the ordinary private action enforces that valid duty rather than compelling state administration of a federal program. Consent and proof of a separate constitutional tort are unnecessary to maintain these actions.

**Independent alternative holding:** Title I's protection of qualified workers against disability discrimination, including reasonable accommodation subject to its enacted qualifications and defenses, is congruent and proportional preventive and remedial enforcement of equal protection against state employers. Section 5 therefore independently sustains the expressly authorized actions without requiring each employee to prove a separate constitutional violation.

**Proposition-level authority for alternative holding:** The same five Justices adopt Section 5 as an independently sufficient ground.

**Material application:** Congress may use bounded prevention against irrational disability prejudice without turning every statutory violation into a constitutional violation. Differentiated evidence of public-employment exclusion supports that inference; no quota of adjudicated violations, formal-findings requirement, geographic restriction or sunset is imposed.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Covered-employer status requires an industry affecting commerce and ordinarily at least 15 employees for each working day in at least 20 calendar weeks of the current or preceding year; the original two-year transition used 25 employees. Agents are included; the United States, wholly owned federal corporations, Indian tribes and bona fide private membership clubs other than labor organizations that are exempt from taxation under 26 U.S.C. section 501(c) are excluded from this employer definition. Actual coverage remains to be proved.

Disability retains impairment substantially limiting a major life activity, record-of-impairment and regarded-as branches. A qualified worker must perform essential functions with or without reasonable accommodation. Employer judgment receives consideration; a job description prepared before advertising or interviews is evidence, not a conclusive veto. Accommodation examples are permissive and include reassignment to a vacant position. They presume neither a valid independent ground nor a completed arbitration, and ordinary preservation limits remain. Their position does not compel arbitration of the agency or affirm the existing restriction on a displaced FAA premise.

These published positions are noncontrolling at the stated scope. [Public opinion account](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK2_PUBLIC_LAW.md#eeoc-v-waffle-house-inc-merits-2002-01-15).

### Raygor v. Regents of University of Minnesota — 2002-02-27

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Breyer, dissenting.** Section 1367(d) protects a related claim actually asserted alongside an authorized federal controversy even when immunity prevents a federal merits judgment on that claim. The filing-related word asserted does work distinct from maintainable. The pending-period and thirty-day protection, subject to longer state tolling, preserve an otherwise available state forum rather than create a new cause, impose liability or waive immunity. The subsection (a) reference supplies original-jurisdiction and relatedness predicates; it need not make eventual adjudicability a further condition absent from the dismissal protection. Pennhurst therefore survives this reading. Kimel supplies the valid federal ADEA anchor, while Schacht keeps the supplemental defense claim-specific. Vermont Agency's exclusion from a sovereign-defendant action does not answer this different procedural effect. The dissent would reverse the categorical exclusion, leaving actual filing arithmetic and remaining independent defenses for the state court and leaving the federal judgment closed.

**Stone-Zsela, separately dissenting.** Congress's authority to implement Article III, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, permits this reasonable protection against forfeiture while a federal tribunal determines its authority over related claims. The clear statement needed to authorize a treasury judgment cannot be transferred without analysis to a rule preserving time in a state forum the State otherwise permits. Text and procedural structure support coverage: immunity dismissal does not erase the fact that the claim was asserted with a valid federal controversy. The Chief Justice would apply the enacted pending-period and thirty-day protection, subject to a longer state period, while preserving the State's distinct forum immunity, substantive defenses and any independent finality defense. His position supplies no new state cause of action, no authority to reopen the federal dismissal and no automatic finding that these state filings were timely. His proposed construction and remand do not command a majority.

### Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc. — 2002-03-19

**O'Connor, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** The FMLA does not give an employer an unconditional right to count all qualifying company leave retroactively against the protected allotment. Section 2654 permits the Secretary to require timely designation as a condition on that counting. The rule identifies which leave consumes the statutory right, rather than creating a monetary award for every omission. Greater-benefit provisions and the need for usable notice support keeping undesignated company leave distinct from the statutory allotment.

The rule's safeguards matter. Designation follows qualifying information; the ordinary two-business-day period permits extenuating circumstances. Later knowledge and provisional designation pending certification can permit the specified retrospective operation. The dissent does not treat every first-day failure as irreversible or require all company benefits to run sequentially. Nor does it assume that Ragsdale was eligible when she first stopped work in February; the supported eligibility date is the March extension.

The majority's lack-of-prejudice objection, in the dissent's view, confuses the entitlement's lawful definition with the requirements for an actual award. A predictable designation rule makes leave usable without forcing an employee to reconstruct every hypothetical choice. Mourning supports appropriate prophylactic implementation, and Christensen's distinction between an advisory position and a valid regulation leaves this delegated rule open for independent evaluation. MCI's explicit filing duty and Sigmon Coal's missing liable-person category differ from the absence of an unconditional concurrent-counting command here.

The four would reverse and remand to apply the undiminished entitlement and then determine the actual violation and appropriate relief under section 2617. They do not infer earlier work capacity, a medically available reduced schedule, actual monetary loss or automatic reinstatement from the omitted designation. Ragsdale's postdischarge request and December release retain their actual dates. Ordinary employer and employee notice obligations remain distinct, and no paid-leave finding is supplied.

### Mickens v. Taylor — 2002-03-27

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens would reverse the habeas denial and secure a new trial because the State appointed the murder victim's recent lawyer in a capital case without disclosure or the accused's informed acceptance, despite the judge's knowledge. The right to a meaningful lawyer-client relationship, counsel's disclosure obligation and the court's protective duty operate together. This exceptional State-created arrangement warrants presumed impairment under Cronic; Holloway, Sullivan and Wood supply the conflict safeguards, while Swidler preserves continuing confidentiality without proving any particular confidence. His rule concerns this undisclosed capital victim-counsel appointment, not every former-client relationship or ethical breach. He does not require another actual-conflict hearing before recognizing the constitutional entitlement.

**Souter, dissenting.** Souter would reverse and require a new trial under the court's prospective duty to address a sufficiently identified risk before representation unfolds. When the judge already has adequate notice, Holloway's preventive protection should not depend solely on an objection from a potentially conflicted lawyer. The judge must inquire, secure an intelligent waiver or arrange replacement when warranted. Souter distinguishes that prospective duty from later discovery: he reads Wood's retrospective inquiry as requiring actual conflict and adverse effect, rather than supporting an actual-conflict-only remand here. His discussion of possible adverse effects supplies no decision of the factual issue outside the grant and no direction for another hearing.

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by Ginsburg.** These Justices would reverse and secure a new trial on a narrower structural ground. The State supplied the recent criminal lawyer of the murder victim to defend the accused in a capital case; the immediate incompatibility, the capital proceeding's use of the victim's character and the State's creation of the arrangement produce a breakdown in the trial framework. Cronic and Fulminante support relief without reconstructing individual performance effects at that conjunction of facts. Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton reinforces the concern for institutional fairness. They expressly leave the general relationship among Holloway, Sullivan and Wood unresolved and do not join Souter's general known-risk rule or an actual-conflict-only remand.

Each dissent seeks the new-trial remedy through ordinary conditional habeas relief, permitting the State to retry with constitutionally adequate representation. None orders immediate unconditional release, acquittal or a new sentence alone. Their constitutional positions do not adjudicate an independently presented collateral defense on predicates absent from the question decided. Counsel maintained the narrower actual-conflict theory; the dissenters give their own legal answers to the granted conflict question and the requested new-trial remedy.

### Henderson v. Norris — 2002-05-13

**Kennedy, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Parts II–III, joined by O'Connor throughout the separate writing and by Thomas in the assumed-framework application only.** The dissent would restore denial of the sentencing claim. The tiny quantity, first conviction and lack of ordinary parole make the sentence severe, but the completed distribution offense and supported record do not meet the extraordinarily demanding threshold under Harmelin. Legislative judgments can account for distribution's preventive and social harms without measuring sentence length arithmetically by weight. The comparison evidence does not convert the exceptional severity into a constitutional prohibition. The dissent acknowledges the real difference from Harmelin's quantity and treats clemency as discretionary grace; it assumes neither a large enterprise nor prior convictions. Thomas's agreement assumes narrow proportionality for this disposition and does not adopt its constitutional foundation. Merits denial makes a separately unestablished new-rule bar unnecessary to these positions.

**Scalia, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Parts II–III.** Harmelin's distinct textual and historical account rejects prison-duration proportionality as an independent requirement of the Cruel and Unusual Punishments Clause. The actual sentence and comparisons may present a sentencing-policy objection, but they do not establish the asserted constitutional right on his ground. His acceptance of the Excessive Fines inquiry in Bajakajian supplies no prison-duration rule. He accepts the controlling temporal law and does not rely on amended section 2254(d) or a fictitious criminal history. His rationale is distinct from the other dissenters' conclusion that this sentence passes a narrow proportionality test.
