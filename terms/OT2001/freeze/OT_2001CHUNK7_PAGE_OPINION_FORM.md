# Page v. Bartels — opinion form

Event: 2002-06-20.

Form: one signed reasoned summary Opinion of the Court in four parts, with a statutory concurrence in part and in the judgment.

The differing legal joins require a signed explanatory opinion: five support the territorial principle, seven the separate statutory results and intentional applications, and nine the confined constitutional applications. One member of the five can carry every part. The summary direct-review posture supplies no plenary argument or review and therefore adds no counted lead. Thomas, joined by Scalia, states the statutory noncoverage ground. The cases remain separate because Page concerns tried voting-opportunity claims and Robertson concerns different plaintiffs, preclusion and summary-judgment theories.

### The constitutional inquiry concerns a protected interest and the operation impairing it

**Holding and operative rule:** Racial consideration or predominance in a common territorial electorate does not itself establish a constitutional deprivation. The constitutional claims presented here must identify the governmental operation impairing a protected political interest and satisfy that claim's own elements; voters of every race receive the same protection, and Section 2 claims proceed independently on their statutory elements.

**Authority:** Part I of the signed opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: five direct votes. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas do not join this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court reaffirms the boundary drawn by Shaw, Miller and Easley. A common territorial electorate places residents in the same geographical constituency without different legal eligibility because of individual race. Racial motivation explains why boundaries were chosen; the relevant operation identifies what those boundaries did to the protected interest. Composition, shape and predominance may supply evidence, but cannot replace proof of an actual exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation or other independently recognized wrong. Conversely, a shared electorate does not excuse a proved denial of equal access. Intentional dilution requires purpose, material impairment of protected electoral opportunity and causation. A participation claim requires an identified protected activity and concrete material impairment attributable to the challenged racial treatment or prohibited purpose; it does not require an already lost election. Those distinctions require separate attention to the actual dilution and chilling claims rather than allowing the lower court's nonpredominance reasoning to answer them. This case applies the existing boundary to those claims; it adopts no comprehensive new test for unpresented partisan or regional-separation theories.

**Precedent treatment:** Shaw v. Reno, Johnson v. Miller and Easley v. Cromartie: reaffirmed at their established territorial-design boundary and protection of independent injuries. The actual statutory inquiry remains governed by Section 2 and its own precedents.

### The statutory dilution claim fails on the relevant opposing-bloc evidence

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 2 does not require retention of a numerical-majority district where, on supported findings concerning the affected electorate and relevant nomination and election stages, challengers fail to establish opposing-bloc voting ordinarily sufficient to defeat the protected group's preferred candidates. Failure of that necessary condition disposes of this claim without deciding the first capacity condition or the remaining totality inquiry.

**Authority:** Part II of the signed opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct votes. Scalia and Thomas agree only in the statutory judgment on their noncoverage ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that actual electoral opportunity, rather than a fixed racial percentage, governs the asserted loss. Gingles supplies the necessary voting conditions, and Voinovich permits disposition when the relevant opposing-bloc showing fails. Valdespino preserves both the numerical-majority route and rigorous proof of dependable crossover capacity, but neither route eliminates this separate requirement. The trial court credited an analysis of more than 150 elections, distinguished relevant contests and candidate preferences, and examined projected performance in each affected district. It also considered the Democratic nomination stage and evidence of African-American primary participation. Those findings support opportunity in both Districts 27 and 34 even without Hispanic support. The contrary expert evidence was received and rejected for identified methodological and contextual reasons; clear-error review does not authorize a new appellate credibility trial. The reduction from 52.8% to 27.5% Black voting-age population in District 27 therefore does not itself prove loss of opportunity. Possible gains elsewhere or the race of successful candidates would not answer that question, but the judgment rests on local preference, crossover and nomination evidence. De Grandy rejects both maximization and conclusive proportionality; it supplies no statewide offset for a local deprivation. No error in the independently sufficient findings requires remand. Distinct-group aggregation and influence-only entitlement remain undecided because neither is necessary after this condition fails.

**Precedent treatment:** Gingles and Voinovich: applied to the independently necessary opposing-bloc condition. Valdespino: both capacity routes remain intact without a new capacity determination. De Grandy: preserved against maximization, numerical shortcuts and statewide-offset reasoning. Balderas: its separate record and fractured application supply no substitute for these identified findings.

### The intentional statutory claim also fails on this record

**Holding and operative rule:** The separately pleaded intentional Section 2 claim fails because supported findings establish neither purposeful diminution of minority voting rights nor the actual impairment asserted. This ground is separate from the results claim and does not make intent necessary for a Section 2 results violation.

**Authority:** Part II of the signed opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer: seven direct votes. Scalia and Thomas agree only in the statutory judgment on their noncoverage ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court addresses Count II on its own terms. The trial court rejected the asserted purpose to diminish minority electoral opportunity and separately rejected the alleged loss and participation impairment. Those findings remain supported under clear-error review. Racial awareness and changing population shares do not themselves establish the pleaded purposeful diminution. The Court therefore affirms this statutory count without treating the results count as an intent claim or using lack of racial predominance as a required element.

**Precedent treatment:** The existing Section 2 results framework remains independent. Voinovich preserves claim-specific proof and does not make all statutory protection depend on purpose.

### The actual dilution and participation theories fail on supported findings

**Holding and operative rule:** Intentional dilution and materially unequal political participation require proof of the unlawful purpose or treatment, material impairment and causal connection belonging to the actual claim. The supported findings rejecting both loss of electoral opportunity and the separately alleged chilling of participation defeat these claims; the absence of established discriminatory purpose provides an additional ground.

**Authority:** The constitutional application is joined by all nine Justices: Part III addresses the Fourteenth Amendment and Part IV the Fifteenth Amendment. This complete agreement on the confined applications does not enlarge the five-Justice coalition for Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that neither demographic redistribution nor racial attention alone establishes the constitutional wrong alleged here. Easley preserves independently asserted intentional dilution and unequal-participation claims under their own elements. The trial addressed these independent theories. Supported local electoral findings defeat the dilution allegation, and the court separately rejected the predicted chilling of African-American and Hispanic participation. That second finding matters: a preferred candidate's ability to win does not universally answer an obstruction of political access. The court also found no purpose to diminish minority voting rights. Arlington Heights and Feeney distinguish awareness of racial consequences from a prohibited purpose; Brooks requires the government's same-decision showing once such a purpose is established as a substantial or motivating factor, a predicate not established here. The lower court's additional reliance on lack of racial predominance does not supply the governing test, but its independent findings sustain this judgment. No developed separate due-process theory is presented for decision.

**Precedent treatment:** Easley: its protection of independently asserted dilution and participation claims is applied; no reconsideration of the distinct territorial-design rule is necessary. Arlington Heights, Feeney and Brooks: their purpose and burden rules remain intact; no established prohibited-purpose predicate triggers the latter burden here.

### The Fifteenth Amendment claim fails without deciding dilution coverage

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming the Fifteenth Amendment reaches the asserted dilution theory, the supported absence of purposeful racial abridgment defeats this claim. The Court leaves the broader coverage question open and affirms the denial of relief on this component.

**Authority:** The constitutional application is joined by all nine Justices: Part III addresses the Fourteenth Amendment and Part IV the Fifteenth Amendment. This complete agreement on the confined applications does not enlarge the five-Justice coalition for Part I.

**Controlling explanation:** Voinovich permits rejection of an assumed dilution claim for failed proof of prohibited purpose while reserving the Amendment's coverage. The completed trial supplies that independent ground here. Awareness of racial composition and a change in numerical concentration do not displace the supported finding that the plan was not designed to diminish minority voting rights. The Court therefore does not adopt the lower court's categorical suggestion that legislative dilution falls outside the Fifteenth Amendment. This constitutional purpose inquiry does not convert Section 2's separate enacted results standard into an intent requirement.

**Precedent treatment:** Voinovich: applied for the assumed-coverage, failed-purpose route; the Fifteenth Amendment coverage reservation remains effective.

Form and governing propositions were fixed on their legal grounds before examining the assignment count or choosing an author.
