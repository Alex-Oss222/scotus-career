# Bass form and final compatibility before assignment

One per curiam merits opinion applies Armstrong in three distinct parts: I, the aggregate charging and completed-plea figures do not support the discovery order the District Court entered and the Sixth Circuit sustained; II, the refusal-based exclusion of the death notice falls with that order; III, under Armstrong's staged framework the proffer's limits do not defeat a confined first-stage threshold inquiry, which the District Court conducts on remand. Parts I and II state what all nine Justices hold; Part III states the five-Justice remand. No Part is added or divided to simplify joins. The short record application of settled rules supports an unsigned institutional opinion even though four Justices dissent from its last Part. Historical anonymity and workload do not choose the form.

Parts I and II have nine direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. They rest on the proffer's missing comparisons at the challenged selection stages and on the principle that a sanction enforces an existing valid order; they decide no source question. The [approved scoped supplement](../runtime/OT_2001CHUNK8_JUNE28_STONE.md) adopts both Parts and the remand. No source join or contrary source theory is inferred for the four Justices who join only Parts I and II.

Part III has five direct joins: Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. The [frozen reconciliation](OT_2001CHUNK8_JUNE28_RECONCILED.md) preserves four Associate commitments to the first-stage remand and four to ordinary pretrial proceedings without a directed inquiry. Stone-Zsela expressly adopts the comparison defect, the dependent-sanction consequence and the first-stage remand. The judgment sustaining the order is reversed, 9–0, and the case is remanded for the first-stage threshold inquiry, 5–4. The original order is not reinstated, and this Court orders no production.

O'Connor publishes an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas. They join Parts I and II and dissent from Part III: adhering to her Armstrong opinion, she would return the prosecution to ordinary proceedings and would not direct a court-run first stage on a proffer that has not shown the missing comparisons to be reasonably unavailable. Its author follows her own current grounds, not a historical writing inventory. No other separate writing is filed: Stevens and Breyer join the per curiam in full, with their Armstrong grounds stated in its Part III, and Souter and Ginsburg also join it in full. There is no change in a frozen Associate commitment, no fallback and no newly supplied Stone-Zsela reason.

The opinion must preserve the below-merits threshold, grounded statistical and circumstantial routes, specifically justified alternatives to unavailable names, stage-specific comparison, national relevance without automatic sufficiency, and the offered-versus-accepted plea distinction. Failure of this proffer to support the order is not proof of neutral administration or nonexistent comparators. Purpose is not independently rejected. The first stage is a court-run threshold determination, not production: any later focused order must rest on Rule 16(a)(1)(C) as Armstrong construes it, reach only existing, material and nonprotected records, explain materiality, the inadequacy of less intrusive evidence and confidentiality, and compel no creation of statistics. Rule 16's separate exclusions and protections are stated in their own terms wherever relied on; no universal file immunity or disclosure right is created.

No signed lead is assigned, no named Justice authors the per curiam opinion, and neither it nor the separate writing receives a signed-lead credit. No assignment count is required. The final form precedes Record writing and is independent of workload. Stewart must be written and validated first; it supplies no same-day substantive law to Bass.
