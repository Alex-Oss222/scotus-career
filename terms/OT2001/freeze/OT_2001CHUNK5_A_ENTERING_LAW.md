## Standards and Tests selection: FCC implementation of local-competition duties

### FCC implementation of local-competition duties

**Governing question:** Allocation of national rulemaking and state implementation under Communications Act §§201(b), 251 and 252.

**Current rule:** Section 201(b) authorizes FCC rules implementing §§251–252, including national standards for the statutory tasks while state commissions retain assigned negotiations, arbitrations, individual rates and other determinations. The implementing authority includes §251(b)(3)’s toll-dialing-parity duty subject to actual statutory limits. Authority to prescribe a methodology does not establish that a particular methodology, rate or implementation duty is lawful. The at-least-equal-quality requirement is a floor; absence of an express superior-quality command does not alone foreclose every implementing requirement for more. Likewise, the direction to enable requesting carriers to combine elements does not unambiguously allocate every physical combining task exclusively to the requester. Particular superior-quality and affirmative new-combination duties require examination of their actual statutory relation, conditions and agency reasons.

## Standards and Tests selection: Telecommunications network elements and bounded access

### Telecommunications network elements and bounded access

**Governing question:** Which telecommunications elements may require access and which incumbent combinations and approved-agreement arrangements remain available.

**Current rule:** Section 153(29)’s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment as well as physical facilities. Eligibility does not itself require unbundling. Under §251(d)(2), proprietary necessity and general impairment must independently constrain element selection, including consideration of realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent network. Any additional cost or quality decline, with outside alternatives categorically ignored, is insufficient. Technical feasibility may matter but cannot replace those statutory constraints. No irreplaceable-monopoly or numerical threshold is imposed.

Section 251(c)(3) supplies no freestanding requirement that a requesting carrier own local facilities. Particular access remains subject to lawful criteria and conditions. Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prevents dismantling already-combined requested elements unless the requester seeks separation; separately available or valued elements need not be physically destroyed. Under §252(i), Rule 51.809 permits access to an individual arrangement in an approved agreement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions, subject to proof of greater provision cost or technical infeasibility and availability only for a reasonable period after public filing. A favorable price cannot be detached from its relevant obligations.

## Standards and Tests selection: Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

### Delegated Customs interpretation and independent tariff review

**Governing question:** Delegated interpretive force of Customs regulations and classification letters in tariff litigation.

**Current rule:** A formally promulgated Customs regulation implementing actual congressional delegation receives Chevron review in tariff litigation. Courts first enforce unambiguous statutory requirements and delegation boundaries, then assess a reasonable agency choice within genuine ambiguity. The trade court’s duty to decide on its own record does not remove this legal framework. Agency labels and notice and comment alone establish neither authority nor validity; statutory construction, regulatory validity and application to found facts remain distinct.

Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations with the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. A transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter issued through the Mead mechanism does not satisfy the requirement. Administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves supply the missing legal force. Formal rulemaking or adjudication may evidence delegation, but neither notice and comment nor a document label is an exclusive prerequisite.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation”, 1999-04-21; Kennedy Parts I–III, all nine Justices.

- Delegated-force threshold and classification-letter exclusion: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / I. This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Haggar, April 21, 1999 for promulgated regulations and own-record factfinding; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the delegated-force threshold and the specified classification-letter mechanism.

**Open question:** Mead resolves the informal-letter reservation for the specified mechanism, not every informal interpretation. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations retain their separate inquiries. The particular Haggar regulation's ultimate reasonableness and importer's exemption are not established by its delegation holding; Mead likewise adopts no ultimate tariff classification. Reviewability of guidance does not itself establish controlling interpretive force.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Haggar Apparel Co. — Trade-court factfinding does not eliminate lawful delegated interpretation](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#trade-court-factfinding-does-not-eliminate-lawful-delegated-interpretation-1). [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).


**Related current rules:** Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation governs independent judicial consideration when controlling force is unavailable.

## Standards and Tests selection: Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

### Persuasive weight of a nonbinding agency advisory interpretation

**Governing question:** The judicial weight of an advisory opinion letter that does not exercise delegated authority with the force of law.

**Current rule:** An advisory interpretation without the force of law does not receive controlling statutory weight under Chevron merely because it comes from the agency administering the statute. It remains entitled to Skidmore consideration according to its reasoning, experience, consistency and persuasive force. Independent tariff construction must consider a noncontrolling classification letter's actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force. No fixed weight or result follows. A change in agency position may weaken consistency but does not exclude every offered reason; expertise and institutional endorsement likewise do not make a view automatically persuasive. Courts must identify the interpretation’s actual authoritative status; the agency label and an implementing grant do not make every advisory response binding.

**Authority by component:**

- Supported nonbinding advisory status and persuasive review: Christensen v. Harris County — II. This advisory letter receives persuasive consideration, not controlling statutory weight, May 1, 2000; Kennedy joined by Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Souter and Thomas.

- Required persuasive consideration of tariff-letter reasons: Canonical Decision Record `United_States_v_Mead_Corp_merits_2001-06-18.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / II. Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration, 2001-06-18; Kennedy with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Present operation effective:** Christensen, May 1, 2000; Mead, June 18, 2001 for the Customs-letter application and specified persuasive inquiry.

**Exceptions and limits:** The holding does not classify every informal agency interpretation or invalidate a future lawful regulation. Interpretation of the agency’s own regulation remains a separate inquiry under the text and Auer’s existing limits.

**Holdings navigation:** [United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218 — Customs classification letters and judicial interpretive authority](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#united-states-v-mead-corp-533-us-218).

## Standards and Tests selection: Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

### Adult access and content-based criminal network-speech restrictions

**Governing question:** Facial review of indecency restrictions on a general-purpose communications network.

**Current rule:** A content-based criminal restriction fails the First Amendment when uncertain, substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the government does not establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. Knowledge that a minor is present, other available channels and scienter do not by themselves cure uncertain content categories or adult-access burdens. Age-verification defenses must afford workable protection on the actual record; cost, loss of anonymity and lawful adult exclusion remain material. A severability clause permits removal of a discrete unlawful textual branch but does not authorize judicial creation of a minor-only offense or an absent value exception.

**Authority by component:**
- Overbreadth, uncertainty and narrow fit: Reno v. ACLU, Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — I. The challenged content restrictions violate the First Amendment; textual severance boundary: Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union — II. Textual severance preserves obscenity law; judicial rewriting cannot save the remaining restrictions, Ginsburg with Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer (eight), 1997-06-26.

**Present operation effective:** Reno v. ACLU, 1997-06-26.

**Open question:** Precisely drawn future controls with effective nonexclusionary age assurance, parental-approval questions, every Internet service's classification and extraterritorial scope remain open. The holding creates no independent Fifth Amendment rule and leaves obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation and direct harmful contact to their own predicates.

**Holdings navigation:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union (1997-06-26) — First Amendment / Internet content restrictions.

## Standards and Tests selection: Content-selected restrictions on protected adult cable programming

### Content-selected restrictions on protected adult cable programming

**Governing question:** The justification required for government restrictions directed at the sexual content of protected adult cable speech.

**Current rule:** Strict scrutiny requires a narrowly tailored restriction serving a compelling interest; the Government bears the ultimate justification burden. Once a concrete, plausibly effective arrangement imposing less restraint on speech is presented, the Government must show why its comparative inadequacy warrants the broader adult burden. Child protection and parental assistance do not by themselves establish necessity. Assessment concerns the actual medium, speech burden and supported implementation problems, with serious consideration but not conclusive deference to legislative predictions.

**Authority by component:**

- United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc.; No. 98-1682. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, I. Congress's content-selected cable restriction requires strict scrutiny, 2000-05-22; Ginsburg with all eight other Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-05-22; the component authority identified above.

**Exceptions and limits:** Neither scientific certainty nor a perfect alternative is required. A paper alternative alone does not establish practical effectiveness. The rule creates no statutory publicity duty, automatic restoration of an invalid restriction, universal blocking-device rule or prohibition of supported narrower regulation; obscenity remains distinct.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

### Commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions

**Governing question:** What instructions govern commercial-speech advancement and fit in selective restrictions?

**Current rule:** For truthful, nonmisleading commercial speech about lawful activity, the government must establish real harms, material advancement and a reasonable fit between its chosen restriction and its interests. Where commercial and newspaper racks cause the same physical harms, the commercial category requires justification tied to those harms; benign objectives alone do not make content-selected regulation content neutral. Incremental regulation is not invalid merely because its reduction is small, but alternatives bear on fit without a least-restrictive-means or all-sources-at-once requirement. In professional solicitation the government must demonstrate advancement in the relevant professional setting, without an invariable empirical-study requirement. Deceptive or coercive solicitation remains regulable. Central Hudson and Fox remain the framework; no general equality of commercial and noncommercial speech or categorical vice exception follows. When the State otherwise makes records available, a condition denying access and prohibiting use because the requester intends truthful, lawful commercial solicitation receives this same review. Absence of a general right to every government record does not remove a speech-selected condition from First Amendment scrutiny; neutral access, retrieval-fee and privacy-redaction rules retain their lawful scope.

## Standards and Tests selection: Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim

### Removal of federal claims alongside an immune state claim

**Governing question:** Whether Eleventh Amendment immunity affecting some claims defeats removal of an otherwise federal-question action.

**Current rule:** An action containing substantial §1331 federal claims is removable under §1441(a) even when Eleventh Amendment immunity bars particular State or official-capacity claims. Proper assertion of immunity prevents adjudication of the affected claim; it does not eliminate jurisdiction over the other federal claims or require remand of the entire action under §1447(c).

**Authority by component:**

- Immunity affecting one claim does not eliminate removal jurisdiction over remaining federal claims: Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht — controlling Holdings, 1998-06-22.

**Present operation effective:** 1998-06-22.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule decides neither whether removal itself waives immunity nor every unasserted-immunity issue, and does not supply jurisdiction over a claim lacking its own necessary foundation.

**Holdings navigation:** Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht, 524 U.S. 381 — Removal jurisdiction and claim-specific state immunity.

## Standards and Tests selection: Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit

### Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit

**Governing question:** Whether explicit prospective regulation and continued ordinary commercial activity establish State consent to private federal enforcement.

**Current rule:** A State’s continued operation of an otherwise lawful commercial undertaking is not unequivocal submission to federal jurisdiction merely because Congress clearly and prospectively subjects that activity to private federal enforcement, even if the undertaking is realistically optional. Unmistakable congressional intent remains distinct from the State’s voluntary act of consent; an opportunity to leave ordinary commerce does not itself constitute acceptance of a specifically offered federal benefit or voluntary invocation of judicial authority. A State’s continued commercial participation after Congress authorizes federal patent suit likewise is not consent. Actual consent through a distinct State choice, such as an express waiver or voluntary invocation of federal jurisdiction, remains available.

**Authority by component:**

- College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit”, 1999-06-23; Part III with Stone-Zsela, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.
- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Continued commercial participation does not supply consent to this suit”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part IV, all nine Justices. Applies College Savings Bank Part III to continued operation of the prepaid-tuition program after the express patent remedy took effect; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer accept that holding as controlling.

**Present operation effective:** College Savings Bank and Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** Actual voluntary waiver, properly conditioned federal benefits and interstate compacts remain distinct. Those decisions do not resolve particular funding or coercion questions, litigation-waiver acts or governmental-function classifications. Bradley separately supplies IDEA-specific consent from actual voluntary acceptance under its express funding condition; other benefits and coercion questions remain distinct. Hilton’s retained FELA coverage and specialized state-court enforcement remain intact.

**Former-rule note:** The residual Parden inference of federal-forum consent from ordinary commercial participation fails even under the new explicit prospective enactment. This does not wholesale overrule retained substantive FELA coverage or Hilton’s state-court rule.

**Open question:** Whether a State’s litigation conduct—appearance, a §1404(a) transfer request or requests for declarations of invalidity, noninfringement and unenforceability—waives immunity remains undecided. Florida Prepaid need not decide it because its Section 5 and Article I holdings sustain the judgment, and it makes no waiver finding.

**Holdings navigation:** [College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board — Continued ordinary commerce is not unequivocal consent to federal suit](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#continued-ordinary-commerce-is-not-unequivocal-consent-to-federal-suit); [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. **Related current rules:** IDEA funding acceptance and program-specific federal-suit consent supplies Bradley’s bounded accepted-benefit holding of June 12, 2000, without changing the commercial-participation rule.

## Standards and Tests selection: Article I enforcement against States in federal court

### Article I enforcement against States in federal court

**Governing question:** Whether Congress, acting within Article I, may subject a nonconsenting State to private federal-court enforcement of a valid federal obligation.

**Current rule:** Congress may authorize private enforcement against States when the underlying obligation and enforcement measure fall within its Article I authority and Congress unmistakably authorizes the State’s exposure to suit. The Patent Clause, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, supports the express federal patent remedy of §§271(h) and 296. The Eleventh Amendment limits the citizen-State diversity grants, not jurisdiction over a federal question; an out-of-State plaintiff’s citizenship does not change the claim’s federal source, and Hans does not establish an immunity Congress cannot displace in this setting. The rule is claim-specific: it removes immunity from the authorized action without finding a violation or an entitlement to relief.

**Authority by component:**

- Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action”, 1999-06-23; Stevens Part III with Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Independently sufficient alongside the Section 5 holding.

**Present operation effective:** Florida Prepaid, June 23, 1999.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule requires a valid enumerated-power obligation, an enforcement measure within that power and unmistakable congressional authorization; enacted clarity alone does not supply power. It does not rest on commercial-participation consent, which College Savings Bank rejects, and decides no actual litigation waiver. Validity, infringement, culpability and each remedy’s statutory and properly presented constitutional limits remain for ordinary adjudication; no compensation, injunction, fee or enhanced award is preapproved. The action is not converted into an officer suit; Young, Coeur d’Alene, Edelman and Pennhurst retain their boundaries.

**Former-rule note:** Union Gas’s retained five-vote constitutional conclusion supplies the foundation; the rule extends that conclusion without treating every proposition in its plurality rationale as binding. Seminole Tribe separated unmistakable statutory authorization from constitutional amenability and set aside the State-dismissal direction without a controlling constitutional rationale; it established no categorical Article I prohibition, overruling of Union Gas or abrogation-before-officer sequence. Florida Prepaid resolves the constitutional question Seminole left open.

**Related current rules:** “Congruent and proportional Fourteenth Amendment enforcement” supplies the independent Section 5 ground for the same patent remedy. “Express state-court FLSA remedies against public employers” separately governs the state-court forum. “Commercial participation and State consent to federal suit” remains intact.

**Holdings navigation:** [Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank — Article I independently sustains the expressly authorized action](../../../state/HOLDINGS.md#article-i-independently-sustains-the-expressly-authorized-action).

## Standards and Tests selection: Article III power before disputed statutory merits

### Article III power before disputed statutory merits

**Governing question:** Whether a court may assume disputed constitutional jurisdiction to resolve contested federal statutory liability.

**Current rule:** A federal court may not assume disputed Article III power merely to decide a contested statutory merits question. Statutory enforcement-and-penalty jurisdictional wording does not automatically make every liability element jurisdictional; substantial federal claims remain distinct from success on their merits. A court may dismiss for lack of personal jurisdiction before resolving subject-matter jurisdiction when personal jurisdiction is straightforward and presents no complex state-law question while subject-matter jurisdiction is difficult. Ordinarily an easily resolved subject-matter question should come first. The choice must respect statutory remand commands and review bars, comity and relevant forum interests, and may decide no contested merits; an established absence of subject-matter jurisdiction retains its statutory consequence.

**Authority by component:**

- Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. — 1. Constitutional power before this disputed statutory merits question, 1997-06-02; Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five, with O'Connor and Kennedy reserving other legitimate exceptional sequences

- Ruhrgas AG v. Marathon Oil Co. — Canonical Decision Record, Holdings, “A court may choose a straightforward nonmerits jurisdictional ground”, 1999-05-17; Scalia, all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** Ruhrgas, May 17, 1999, for bounded nonmerits sequencing in removed and originally filed cases; Steel Co.’s merits prohibition retains its June 2, 1997 date.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule prescribes no exhaustive hierarchy among thresholds and authorizes no assumed jurisdiction over disputed merits. Norton and Avrech retain their own controlled-merits and jurisdictional settings. A particular statutory priority, such as Price-Anderson coverage before tribal exhaustion, remains operative.

**Holdings navigation:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co. (1997-06-02) — Constitutional jurisdiction before a disputed EPCRA merits question. [Ruhrgas AG v. ## Standards and Tests selection: Final congressional selection on the federal election day

### Final congressional selection on the federal election day

**Governing question:** When a state congressional-election procedure conflicts with then-operative 2 U.S.C. §§1 and 7.

**Current rule:** An election is complete when the legal selection is consummated. A State cannot make an October congressional majority result the final legal choice, leaving no act of selection for the federal November election day, merely by calling the October contest a primary. Compatible preliminary proceedings may precede the federal day. Section 8’s vacancy and failure-to-elect provisions do not treat a successful final October majority as a failure to elect.

**Authority by component:**

- Final October selection and the federal election day: Foster v. Love, 1997-12-02; Ginsburg for all nine Justices.

**Present operation effective:** 1997-12-02.

**Exceptions and limits:** The rule supplies no comprehensive code for preliminary steps or later runoffs and decides no presidential timing question. Relief must displace the conflicting final-selection feature while preserving compatible state administration and ballot choices.

**Holdings navigation:** Foster v. Love — Federal election timing.

## Standards and Tests selection: Presidential elector appointment, ordinary state interpretation and federal conclusive treatment

### Presidential elector appointment, ordinary state interpretation and federal conclusive treatment

**Governing question:** Judicial construction of a legislatively enacted presidential-election code and then-operative 3 U.S.C. section 5.

**Current rule:** Article II preserves the legislature's choice of the manner of appointing presidential electors while permitting ordinary judicial interpretation and enforcement of its enacted code. A judicial remedy may not substitute materially new substantive rules for that scheme. State characterization is not conclusive, but ordinary interpretation is not itself legislative displacement.

Under then-operative 3 U.S.C. section 5, a final determination of an elector-appointment controversy receives conclusive treatment in the federal count when made pursuant to judicial or other procedures established by state law before election day and completed at least six days before the electors meet. That conditional benefit is not a federal prohibition of all later counting. Independently binding state limits remain operative, and section 7's elector meeting date is unchanged.

**Authority by component:**

- Section 5 benefit distinguished from a universal stopping command: Canonical Decision Record `Bush_v_Palm_Beach_County_Canvassing_Board_merits_2000-12-04.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / Federal timing and fundamental fairness remain distinct from the state-law clarification, 2000-12-04; nine joins to the timing proposition.
- Ordinary statutory construction versus material legislative displacement: Canonical Decision Record `Bush_v_Gore_merits_2000-12-12.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / III. The presented Article II objections do not independently invalidate the challenged directions, 2000-12-12; six direct joins.
- Complete conclusive-treatment conditions and unchanged meeting date: Canonical Decision Record `Bush_v_Gore_merits_2000-12-12.md`, Public Projection / Holdings / V-A. Section 5 supplies a conditional federal benefit, not a universal counting prohibition, 2000-12-12; all nine.

**Present operation effective:** 2000-12-12.

**Exceptions and limits:** This rule supplies no automatic qualification or forfeiture, universal competing-submission rule, entitlement to count until the meeting date, or federal extension beyond a binding state limit. A material unresolved state-law premise requires authoritative resolution; the specific December 2000 timing application does not establish a deadline for another controversy.

## Complete opening holding: AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board

Source: [administrative-law](../../../state/holdings/administrative-law.md).

#### AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board

**Docket or dockets:** Nos. 97-826, 97-829, 97-830, 97-831, 97-1075, 97-1087, 97-1099 and 97-1141; including California respondents in the AT&T, MCI and FCC petitions.
**Decided:** 1999-01-25.

##### I. National implementing rules coexist with state determinations

**Holding:** Section 201(b) authorizes FCC rules implementing the local-competition obligations added to the Communications Act by §§251–252. That authority includes national standards governing the statutory tasks while state commissions retain the negotiations, arbitrations, individual rates and other determinations Congress assigned to them.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Parts I–II are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. Six Justices control the general-authority and associated remand propositions; O'Connor, Thomas and Breyer dissent on their distinct allocation grounds.

##### II. The rural-exemption and agreement rules require review within the correct allocation

**Holding:** The categorical absence-of-federal-authority grounds for vacating Rules 51.405 and 51.303 and displacing otherwise lawful federal rules from state implementation cannot stand. Unresolved substantive objections return for review with the actual statutory rural-exemption, agreement-approval and state-law preservation limits intact.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same six-Justice coalition controls Part II. The remand does not adopt a new rule resolving the returned substantive objections.

##### III. A network element need not be a detachable physical object

**Holding:** Section 153(29)'s network-element definition includes features, functions and capabilities supplied through facilities or equipment, as well as the physical facilities or equipment themselves. Eligibility under that definition does not establish that §251(d)(2) requires unbundled access to every eligible function.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part III is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Those eight supply a controlling majority; Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment on the independent definition component.

##### IV. Necessity and impairment must perform independent limiting work

**Holding:** In selecting required network elements under §251(d)(2), the FCC must give independent limiting effect to necessity for proprietary elements and impairment for network elements generally, considering realistically available alternatives outside as well as inside the incumbent's network. Rule 51.319 is set aside because the adopted comparison and impairment standard did not perform that task.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IV is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, giving the complete rule seven votes. Stone-Zsela additionally joins the invalidity holding that the rule must make element-specific judgments giving necessity and impairment independent constraining force, and joins vacatur and agency remand; he does not supply a separate join in the more specific outside-alternatives requirement. Souter dissents from invalidation.

##### V. The Act imposes no freestanding own-facilities prerequisite

**Holding:** A requesting telecommunications carrier need not own some local facilities merely to qualify to seek unbundled access under §251(c)(3). Its entitlement to particular elements remains governed by §251(d)(2) and the lawful conditions of access and combination.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part V is joined by all seven other Associate Justices. Stone-Zsela agrees only in rejection of the independent own-facilities prerequisite, without a ground.

##### VI. Existing combinations need not be dismantled

**Holding:** Rule 51.315(b) lawfully prohibits an incumbent from separating requested network elements already combined in its network unless the requesting carrier asks for separation. Unbundled availability and valuation do not require physical destruction of an existing combination.

**Proposition-level authority:** All nine Justices join Scalia's Parts VI–VII; those parts control the existing-combination and individual-arrangement rules with their stated limits.

##### VII. An entrant may select an individual agreement arrangement with its pertinent conditions

**Holding:** Section 252(i) permits Rule 51.809's access to an individual interconnection, service or network-element arrangement in an approved agreement on the same pertinent rates, terms and conditions. The rule preserves proof of greater provision costs or technical infeasibility and limits availability to a reasonable period after public filing.

**Proposition-level authority:** The same nine-Justice coalition joins Part VII and controls this rule and its qualifications.

##### VIII. Dialing-parity authority and numbering-cost ripeness remain distinct

**Holding:** The categorical absence-of-FCC-authority ground for vacating intraLATA toll-dialing-parity rules is rejected, with remaining objections returned for review. The separate Rule 52.17 numbering-cost challenge remains unripe because its asserted disadvantage depends on particular cost-recovery rate decisions not before the Court.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part VIII parity-authority ground is joined by Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg. O'Connor agrees on the narrower §251(b)(3)/§251(d)(1) implementation ground stated in her separate opinion. Stone-Zsela agrees in the parity disposition without an additional ground; the five-Justice lead rationale independently controls. All eight Associates join Part VIII's separate numbering-cost ripeness holding; Stone-Zsela agrees only in that disposition.

**Holding:** The partial vacatur of Rule 51.317's technical-feasibility presumption and Order paragraphs 278 and 281 is affirmed because the presumption and its actual exceptions fail to preserve the required necessity and impairment limits. The separately sustained definition of technical feasibility and other unaffected portions of the rule remain outside that invalidation.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part IX is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those seven control the substantive presumption holding. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the partial invalidation; Souter would remand this component without a final validity ruling.

##### X. Superior-quality duties return for review under the correct framework

**Holding:** The invalidation of Rules 51.305(a)(4) and 51.311(c) is vacated and remanded for reconsideration of the actual conditions and agency justification under the correct implementing authority. The Court does not decide their ultimate validity or order unconditional reinstatement.

**Proposition-level authority:** All eight Associates join Part X's corrected-standard remand and its confined review directions. Thomas and Breyer do so under the Court's governing authority framework while retaining their dissent from Part I. Stone-Zsela agrees in the remand without adding a ground.

##### XI. Affirmative new-combination duties require separate reconsideration

Section 251(c)(3)'s direction to enable requesting carriers to combine elements does not unambiguously assign every physical combination task exclusively to the requester; the particular affirmative duties and burdens remain undecided.

**Proposition-level authority:** Scalia's Part XI is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Seven Justices control this remand; Stone-Zsela agrees only in its disposition, and Thomas dissents on his independent task-allocation ground.

## Complete opening holding: United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218

Source: [administrative-law](../../../state/holdings/administrative-law.md).

#### United States v. Mead Corp., 533 U.S. 218

**Docket or dockets:** No. 99-1434.
**Decided:** June 18, 2001.

##### This ruling mechanism does not exercise authority to bind the reviewing court

**Holding:** Controlling Chevron treatment requires congressional conferral, expressly or through the statutory scheme, of authority to make interpretations carrying the force of law and an interpretation issued in exercise of that authority. This transaction-specific, revisable Customs classification letter does not satisfy that requirement; general administrative responsibility, expertise and official institutional endorsement do not themselves confer controlling interpretive force on the mechanism.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** The revisable, transaction-specific letters issue from many offices and do not govern unrelated importers as regulations do. Their place in protest and judicial review shows administration rather than delegated interpretive lawmaking. The Solicitor General's representation, cosigned by Treasury's General Counsel, establishes Customs' official position but does not establish congressional delegation to this mechanism.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Formal rulemaking and adjudication are evidence of delegation, not exclusive prerequisites. A label or independent judicial factfinding does not alone defeat lawful deference. Later ruling-change procedures do not retroactively govern the 1993 action. Other statutory mechanisms and interpretation of an agency's own regulations remain outside the grant.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.: retained for reasonable choices within actual congressionally delegated interpretive authority; its threshold is applied to this Customs mechanism.
- United States v. Haggar Apparel Co.: its actual-delegation and statutory-boundary requirements are applied; its reserved informal-letter issue is resolved for this mechanism, while its regulation and independent-factfinding holdings remain intact.
- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Appalachian Power Co. v. EPA: its operational-legal-consequence reviewability holding and prohibition on interpretive amendment remain distinct; reviewability does not itself establish authority to bind a court's statutory interpretation.

##### Lack of controlling force does not eliminate persuasive consideration

**Holding:** A court independently construing the tariff must consider this ruling letter's reasons according to their actual thoroughness, expertise, logic, consistency, commercial understanding and other persuasive force under Skidmore. No fixed weight or result follows; the Federal Circuit erred by excluding that consideration merely because the letter lacked controlling force.

**Proposition-level authority:** Parts I and II of Kennedy's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, supply eight direct votes. Scalia does not join; Stone-Zsela's disagreement with the open remand does not qualify his join to these interpretive rules.

**Material application:** Customs' dictionary, commercial-usage and ring-binding reasons must be evaluated. Changed treatment may weaken consistency without eliminating all persuasive force; expertise and official endorsement likewise establish neither persuasion nor the answer.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Skidmore consideration is independent judgment, not a lesser mandatory deference or fixed weight. No choice between bound diaries and the residual duty-free category is made.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- Christensen v. Harris County: its distinction between controlling authority and persuasive reasoning is extended to this classification letter without excluding every informal agency interpretation.
- Skidmore v. Swift & Co.: applied as an independent persuasive inquiry, with no predetermined weight or mandatory classification outcome.

##### The judgment returns classification under the corrected rules

**Holding:** The Federal Circuit judgment is vacated and the case remanded for independent classification of the imported planners with consideration of the ruling letter's reasons according to their actual persuasive force under Skidmore. The remand establishes neither the bound-diary classification nor the residual duty-free classification, and it directs no particular assessment, refund amount, new agency proceeding or rulemaking.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III has seven direct supporters: Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, including Kennedy. Stone-Zsela and Scalia oppose the open classification remand and would sustain the challenged classification on different grounds.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Neither definitive classification commands a majority. The return rests on exclusion of permissible agency reasoning, not a missing product fact, a general right to first lower-court interpretation or a need for agency action. The distinct noncontrolling textual and deference grounds for sustaining the category do not combine into a Court construction.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Federal Circuit must independently classify the planners while considering the actual persuasive force of Customs' reasons. Any assessment or refund depends on that classification and entry calculations; no amount, new hearing, signature search or future rulemaking is required.

## Complete opening holding: Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union

Source: [first-amendment](../../../state/holdings/first-amendment.md).

#### Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union

**Docket or dockets:** No. 96-511.

**Decided:** June 26, 1997.

##### Whether the CDA's indecency and patently-offensive restrictions satisfy the First Amendment

**Holding:** A content-based criminal restriction on expression through a general-purpose communications network is facially invalid when uncertain and substantially overbroad categories suppress substantial protected adult speech and the Government fails to establish narrow tailoring in light of practical, materially less restrictive means of limiting minors' access. On this Internet record, §223(a)(1)(B)'s indecency prohibition and §223(d)(1)'s patently-offensive transmission and display prohibitions, together with their corresponding facility-permitting offenses, fail that requirement.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct votes. O'Connor joins parts of the judgment only.

**Material application:** Uncertain criminal categories reach substantial lawful adult discussion; scienter does not supply a clear content boundary. The record's age-verification costs, anonymity loss and exclusion of lawful adults leave the statutory defenses inadequate, while user filtering and precise treatment of unprotected material provide materially less restrictive approaches.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Vagueness contributes to the First Amendment holding; no separate Fifth Amendment ruling is made. The decision establishes no uniform classification of all Internet services and does not invalidate every age screen or every future control.

**Treatment of earlier authority:** Sable, Butler and Bolger preserve adult speech despite child-protection interests. Ginsberg's confined commercial rule for material obscene for minors is distinguished. Pacifica retains its broadcast setting; ACT/Pacifica preserves independent protection of adult access. Turner's content-neutral carriage setting is distinguished. Denver Area's compulsory-segregation holding supplies an adult-access analogy, without generalizing its fractured permission judgments. Broadrick supplies substantial overbreadth.

##### What textual severance and clarified preliminary relief remain permissible

**Holding:** The discrete words imposing indecency liability in §223(a)(1)(B) are severable from its obscenity prohibition. The Court affirms the clarified preliminary injunction against that indecency branch and its corresponding §223(a)(2) liability, and against §223(d)(1)–(2); the general severability provision does not authorize creating a new minor-only offense or inserting a value exception absent from the enacted text.

**Proposition-level authority:** Ginsburg's opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer; eight direct votes. O'Connor joins parts of the judgment only.

**Limits and questions not reached:** No minor-only offense or serious-value exception is judicially added. The Court reserves a blanket prohibition for seventeen-year-olds, parental approval, extraterritorial reach and future effective nonexclusionary age assurance. Independently lawful obscenity, child sexual-abuse material, threats, fraud, unlawful solicitation and direct harmful-contact laws retain their own predicates.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The clarified preliminary injunction is affirmed against indecency under §223(a)(1)(B) and corresponding §223(a)(2) liability, and against §223(d)(1)–(2). Section (a)(2) remains enforceable for (a)(1)(A), (C), (D), (E), and obscenity or child pornography under (a)(1)(B). Section (d) has no separate textual obscenity branch and receives no such carve-out. The relief remains preliminary; no permanent decree or new monetary award is entered.

**Material judgment:** Transmission and corresponding facility relief: 8–1, O'Connor opposing only the minor-only transmission applications. Display relief and preservation of severable independent law: unanimous.

## Complete opening holding: United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc., 529 U.S. 803 (2000)

Source: [first-amendment](../../../state/holdings/first-amendment.md).

#### United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc., 529 U.S. 803 (2000)

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-1682.
**Decided:** May 22, 2000.

##### I. Congress's content-selected cable restriction requires strict scrutiny

**Holding:** A governmental restriction selecting protected adult cable programming by its sexual content must satisfy strict scrutiny: it must be narrowly tailored to a compelling interest, with the Government bearing the ultimate burden of justifying its means. When a concrete, plausibly effective and less speech-restrictive arrangement is presented, the Government must establish why its comparative inadequacy warrants the broader adult burden.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part I of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices; nine direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Legislative predictions receive serious consideration, but neither scientific certainty, harm to every child nor a perfect alternative is required. A merely theoretical alternative is insufficient; actual adult burdens and supported implementation concerns matter.

##### II. The Government has not justified this adult burden against the noticed household alternative

**Holding:** Section 505 cannot impose its complete-blocking or sixteen-hour time-channeling burden on the protected programming at issue because the Government has not established that household-request blocking with adequate notice is comparatively inadequate enough to justify that burden. This is a developed-record failure of necessity, not a finding that adequate notice already occurred, that household blocking is flawless, or that every narrower child-protection measure is invalid.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct joins. Scalia and Breyer dissent from this application.

**Material application:** The alternative is household-request blocking with adequate notice identifying the affected channels, understandable audio/video bleed, possible child exposure without parental knowledge, free blocking and the telephone request method. It includes repeated notice at reasonable intervals and special mailed notice to prior blocking recipients after the adult channel changes. Billing inserts, preview channels and advertisements elsewhere are examples, not cumulative compulsory media. No precise interval, installation deadline, repair protocol or automatic replacement rule is established.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The child-risk finding remains; low distributed-device figures during unproved notice establish neither informed refusal nor failed adequately publicized blocking. Delay, malfunction, channel changes and operator costs matter without becoming universal failure findings. No completed notice experiment, guaranteed technology, cost-free installation or digital conversion is assumed.

##### III. Affirmance preserves the injunction without creating a judicial notice program

**Holding:** The declaration and injunction against §505 are affirmed at the adjudicated scope, while §504's distinct requested-blocking command remains effective. The affirmance creates no Supreme Court notice order, statutory publicity command or new authority over nonparty operators, and leaves unreviewed collateral lower relief only at its actual scope and procedural status.

**Proposition-level authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas; seven direct joins.

**Limits and questions not reached:** Section 504, codified at §560, requires a cable operator, upon a subscriber's request and without charge to that subscriber, to fully scramble or block each channel's audio and video so that a nonsubscriber does not receive it. It has no independent indecency or sexually oriented channel trigger. Subsection (b) supplies the same scrambling definition. This command took effect February 8, 1996. Subscriber-selected blocking and §505's default/time restriction are distinct; compliance with one does not automatically satisfy the other. Free service to the subscriber does not mean zero operator equipment or installation cost. Section 504 itself contains no notice program.

**Limits and questions not reached:** The adjudication concerns protected nonobscene adult programming. Equal protection, vagueness, obscenity enforcement and evidence-supported narrower measures are not decided. The lower court’s announced contractual-notice direction is neither a proved implementation event nor a new Supreme Court notice-power holding; inadequate notice does not automatically restore §505.

**Operative remedy or transition:** Affirm the declaration and enforcement injunction at their adjudicated scope. Section 504 survives separately. No proof-repair remand, technical program, mandatory device, new hours, publicity order or nonparty obligation is directed.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Action for Children's Television v. FCC / Pacifica Foundation v. FCC:** Applies independent review of necessity and the practical burden of a broad adult exclusion. Its broadcast-specific holding does not establish that every medium or every schedule is identical.
- **FCC v. Pacifica Foundation:** Preserves its confined contextual broadcast permission. It does not automatically sustain this cable restriction or withdraw protection from nonobscene adult programming.
- **Denver Area Educational Telecommunications Consortium, Inc. v. FCC / Alliance for Community Media v. FCC:** Applies the distinction between private editorial permission and Congress's integral compulsory restriction. Its invalidation of that scheme neither condemns every household-request tool nor validates the present timing burden.
- **Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union:** Applies protection of adult access and medium-specific scrutiny. It does not decide cable technology or create a finding that every parent receives effective notice.
- **Turner Broadcasting System, Inc. v. FCC:** Preserves independent evidentiary review and the distinct intermediate standard for content-neutral carriage. Neither conclusive deference nor that intermediate test governs §505.
- **City of Erie v. Pap's A.M.:** Applies the distinction between communicative selection and genuinely general conduct regulation. The present content-directed command cannot receive incidental-burden treatment merely because it concerns distribution technology.

## Complete opening holding: Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht, 524 U.S. 381

#### Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht, 524 U.S. 381

The Court need not decide the general jurisdictional classification of Eleventh Amendment immunity.

Removal authority must be established and immunity and any genuinely remaining remand questions treated at their proper claim-specific scope. The actual complaint contains federal claims; City of Chicago’s supplemental-claim discussion is an analogy, not an additional claim here. Wisconsin’s waiver by removing, the merits of the personal-capacity claims, any new relief against the State and compulsory retention of every hypothetical supplemental claim remain undecided. The unappealed dismissals stay outside the returned appeal.

**Operative remedy or transition:** The Seventh Circuit’s whole-action remand is vacated. The personal-capacity appeal returns for consideration under the correct jurisdictional framework and otherwise applicable law. No new merits relief, reinstatement, damages or claim against Wisconsin is ordered.

**Treatment of earlier authority:**

- **Hafer v. Melo:** Its personal/official-capacity distinction is applied; state immunity does not independently bar a personally payable judgment, and personal defenses and merits remain separate.
- **City of Chicago v. International College of Surgeons:** Its substantial-federal-claim removal anchor is applied. Its supplemental-jurisdiction and retention rules create no additional actual claim here.
- **Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida:** Its actual statutory and remedial holdings and unresolved direct-State rationale remain unchanged; this decision adopts no general abrogation theory.
- **Pennsylvania v. Union Gas Co.:** Its existing force is undisturbed; the Court neither overrules it nor decides direct-State amenability in this case.
- **Gutierrez de Martinez v. Lamagno:** Its distinct certification, substitution and removal scheme is not transplanted into ordinary federal-question removal.

## Complete opening holding: College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board

#### College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board

**Docket or dockets:** No. 98-149; October Term 1998.
**Decided:** 1999-06-23.

##### Congress expressly authorized federal suit and otherwise available remedies

**Holding:** Sections 1122(a)–(b) and 1125(a)(1)(B), (a)(2) unmistakably subject States and their instrumentalities to federal suit and otherwise available legal and equitable remedies for covered Lanham Act violations occurring on or after October 27, 1992. That enacted intent establishes neither constitutional power, State consent, a statutory violation nor entitlement to a particular award.

# National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co. — 2002-01-16

[Authoritative Record](../records/national-cable-telecommunications-assn-inc-v-gulf-power-co_merits_2002-01-16.md)

## Event

National Cable & Telecommunications Assn., Inc. v. Gulf Power Co.; Nos. 00-832 and 00-843. Merits decision, January 16, 2002; argued October 2, 2001. Review of the Eleventh Circuit's judgment setting aside the FCC's authority over mixed cable-Internet and wireless telecommunications pole attachments. The questions are whether section 224's category-specific rate formulas restrict its general coverage, and whether qualifying wireless equipment falls outside the statute.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participated at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The Court reverses the challenged categorical exclusions from the FCC's pole-attachment authority and remands.

## Judgment & Remedy

|---|---|---|---|
| Mixed cable-Internet attachments | Reverse the categorical jurisdictional exclusion and remand, 7-2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg, Breyer | Souter, Thomas; they would vacate for agency classification and explanation |

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Kennedy | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Ginsburg, Breyer throughout; Souter and Thomas as to Part II only | Supports both reversals | Seven join Part I, mixed-service coverage; all nine join Part II, wireless coverage |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Thomas | Souter | Concurs in wireless reversal; would vacate the mixed-service component for agency explanation | Mandatory formula classification and reasoned statutory basis |

## Holdings

### I. Adding Internet service does not remove a qualifying cable-system attachment from the statute

**Holding and operative rule.** An attachment by a cable television system that satisfies 47 U.S.C. §224(a)(4) remains within the FCC's authority under §224(b) when it carries Internet service together with cable television. Sections 224(d) and (e) prescribe mandatory rates within their respective terms; they do not exhaust the broader attachment definition or permit the agency to evade an applicable formula.

**Authority.** Part I of Kennedy's opinion has the full support of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer, supplying seven votes.

**Controlling explanation.** Congress defined the regulated attachment before prescribing methods for setting its rate. An attachment by a cable system does not cease to meet that definition when the system adds a service. The limitation to attachments used solely for cable service controls the cable formula's application; it does not rewrite the general definition. Section 224(b)'s direction to ensure just and reasonable rates, terms and conditions and its rulemaking grant therefore continue to govern covered attachments. Iowa Utilities Board supplies the distinction between an actual congressional assignment and the limits governing its exercise. The utilities' method objection deserves examination when a particular rate method is challenged, but does not establish a categorical absence of authority. Nor must the Commission resolve Internet classification to answer this coverage question: either possible classification leaves an attachment by a cable system within the general definition. Mandatory formulas remain mandatory when their conditions are met. Mead requires an actual delegation before an agency interpretation receives controlling force; the enacted text itself resolves this question, so no new deference rule is needed. The Eleventh Circuit's categorical exclusion cannot stand. Classification of Internet service and validity of a particular rate are outside the questions decided.

### II. Qualifying wireless equipment is a covered attachment

**Holding and operative rule.** Section 224 covers an attachment by a provider offering telecommunications to the public for a fee, without excluding the provider's equipment because it serves wireless transmission. Both the attachment and its owner must satisfy the statutory definitions; the utility's wire-use condition does not impose a separate wire-only restriction on the attached provider's service.

**Authority.** All nine Justices join Part II of Kennedy's opinion.

**Controlling explanation.** The telecommunications-service definition addresses service offered for a fee regardless of the facilities used. The reference to wire communications in the utility definition identifies the covered owner and structure; it does not alter the separately defined service or exclude physical wireless equipment attached to that structure. Reading the definitions together covers both wireline attachments used by wireless providers and qualifying wireless equipment. The contrary construction relocates an owner-related condition into a service limitation Congress did not enact. Iowa Utilities Board's distinction between statutory eligibility and the duties governing eligible facilities supports preserving those separate functions. The statute's exclusions for railroads, cooperatively organized persons and federal or state ownership remain, as do the incumbent-local-exchange-carrier limitation in §224(a)(5) and §224(c)'s allocation to qualifying state regulation. The holding reaches qualifying poles, ducts, conduits and rights-of-way; it does not extend authority to every independent wireless tower or property outside those definitions. The wireless categorical exclusion is reversed, with lawful rates, terms and conditions governed by the applicable statutory provisions.

## Precedent Treatment

- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board (January 25, 1999): applies its separation of congressionally conferred FCC authority from the independent limits on particular implementing rules; its local-competition holdings remain intact.
- United States v. Mead Corp. (June 18, 2001): preserves its actual-delegation requirement; the Court resolves section 224's coverage from its text without adding a deference holding.
- Permian Basin Area Rate Cases, 390 U.S. 747: the separate writing invokes reasoned ratemaking to require an agency explanation; that objection does not become a controlling ground for additional remand.

## Law After Decision

Section 224's general coverage encompasses qualifying mixed cable-Internet and wireless telecommunications attachments. A gap or dispute about an applicable rate formula does not itself create a jurisdictional exclusion. The statutory formulas, ownership and provider limits, and qualifying state-regulation allocation retain their force. These are new coverage holdings; no Internet-service classification or particular rate is approved.

## Separate Writings

Thomas, joined by Souter, concurs in the wireless holding but would vacate the mixed-service portion for a reasoned agency determination. If cable-modem access is cable service, subsection (d)'s formula controls; if it is telecommunications service, subsection (e)'s formula controls. Only after resolving those possibilities can residual authority under subsection (b) support the particular regulatory choice. The service supplied by an Internet service provider and the transmission carrying customers to that provider are distinct classification questions. The Commission's treatment of one does not necessarily resolve the other. Permian Basin's demand for a reasoned rate method supports returning that choice to the agency. Probable ultimate jurisdiction and policy favoring broadband deployment cannot supply the missing statutory explanation. This position rejects categorical noncoverage but withholds approval of the existing unexplained agency determination.

## Procedure After Action

The case returns through the Eleventh Circuit for proceedings consistent with both coverage holdings, preserving properly remaining classification and rate-method objections under the applicable statutory commands.

## Holdings

### Supplemental tolling does not cover this nonconsenting State's immune state-law claims

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1367(d) does not toll the limitations period for a state-law claim against a nonconsenting State that falls outside subsection (a)'s supplemental adjudicative grant because of sovereign immunity. A valid federal claim within original jurisdiction does not itself authorize the separate state-law claim or bring that immune claim within the linked tolling provision.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion is joined throughout by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Those five direct joins control the statutory rule and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory reference to subsection (a) limits which asserted claims receive subsection (d)'s protection. Pennhurst preserves a State's immunity from federal adjudication of its state-law duties without consent. Vermont Agency distinguishes constitutional power from affirmative statutory authorization to proceed against a sovereign. Subsection (a)'s general supplemental grant contains no unmistakable authorization for this nonconsenting State-defendant claim, and subsection (d) supplies no independent affirmative indication that Congress included it despite that exclusion.

The employees correctly distinguish tolling in a competent state forum from a federal merits judgment. But that difference does not eliminate the cross-reference Congress enacted. The word asserted does not make every related filing a claim selected by subsection (a). The ordinary protection lasts while a covered claim is pending and for thirty days after dismissal, unless state law provides longer tolling; those periods do not enlarge the class covered. The additional provision for other same-action claims voluntarily dismissed at or after dismissal of a covered claim does not govern these involuntary dismissals.

Kimel authorizes the federal ADEA action. Schacht accordingly prevents immunity on the supplemental claim from erasing the independent federal jurisdiction. Neither decision supplies consent to adjudicate the state claim. The Regents asserted immunity in their answers, and subsequent discovery, mediation and scheduling did not relinquish that express defense. College Savings Bank likewise rejects consent inferred merely from ordinary activity.

This statutory ground sustains the state limitations judgment without treating the federal ADEA claim as unauthorized or reopening its final dismissal. The Court need not decide Congress's constitutional power to enact a differently specified tolling rule: statutory coverage resolves the presented claim. The holding supplies no general rule excluding every dismissal outside subsection (c), and no general presumption against federal laws affecting States.

Alden v. Maine retains its distinction between review of a new state judgment and reopening a terminated federal case. Only the former is before the Court.

Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs concerns equitable tolling in its own statutory setting; it does not independently place these immune supplemental claims within section 1367(d).

## Law After Decision

Section 1367(d)'s linked tolling protection excludes the specified nonconsenting State's immune supplemental state-law claims. The independent federal ADEA channel remains valid, and immunity remains claim-specific. The decision rests on statutory coverage, leaving constitutional power to enact a differently specified tolling rule undecided because that issue is unnecessary to this judgment.

## Separate Writings

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Breyer, dissenting.** Section 1367(d) protects a related claim actually asserted alongside an authorized federal controversy even when immunity prevents a federal merits judgment on that claim. The filing-related word asserted does work distinct from maintainable. The pending-period and thirty-day protection, subject to longer state tolling, preserve an otherwise available state forum rather than create a new cause, impose liability or waive immunity. The subsection (a) reference supplies original-jurisdiction and relatedness predicates; it need not make eventual adjudicability a further condition absent from the dismissal protection. Pennhurst therefore survives this reading. Kimel supplies the valid federal ADEA anchor, while Schacht keeps the supplemental defense claim-specific. Vermont Agency's exclusion from a sovereign-defendant action does not answer this different procedural effect. The dissent would reverse the categorical exclusion, leaving actual filing arithmetic and remaining independent defenses for the state court and leaving the federal judgment closed.

**Stone-Zsela, separately dissenting.** Congress's authority to implement Article III, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, permits this reasonable protection against forfeiture while a federal tribunal determines its authority over related claims. The clear statement needed to authorize a treasury judgment cannot be transferred without analysis to a rule preserving time in a state forum the State otherwise permits. Text and procedural structure support coverage: immunity dismissal does not erase the fact that the claim was asserted with a valid federal controversy. The Chief Justice would apply the enacted pending-period and thirty-day protection, subject to a longer state period, while preserving the State's distinct forum immunity, substantive defenses and any independent finality defense. His position supplies no new state cause of action, no authority to reopen the federal dismissal and no automatic finding that these state filings were timely. His proposed construction and remand do not command a majority.

# New York v. FERC / Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC — 2002-03-04

[Authoritative Record](../records/new-york-v-ferc-enron-power-marketing-inc-v-ferc_merits_2002-03-04.md)

## Event

New York v. FERC, No. 00-568, and Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC, No. 00-809; 535 U.S. 1. Argued October 3, 2001; decided March 4, 2002; October Term 2001, chunk 3. On certiorari to the D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining Order 888. New York challenges FERC's authority over unbundled interstate transmission serving retail purchasers. Enron seeks compulsory extension of the open-access requirements to transmission in bundled retail service. ## Holdings

### I. The transmission grant includes unbundled interstate service for retail purchasers

**Holding and operative rule:** The Federal Power Act's separate grant over transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce includes independently supplied interstate transmission serving retail purchases. The wholesale limitation governs the separate sales grant; it does not narrow the transmission grant, and FERC may regulate the covered transmission's rates, terms and conditions without ordering a utility to begin retail wheeling.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court. That direct unanimous coalition controls the category and the following limits.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 201(b)(1) retains state retail-sale authority and, except as the Act specifically provides otherwise, excludes facilities used for generation, local distribution, intrastate-only transmission or transmission of energy consumed wholly by the transmitter. Its preservation of lawful state authority over hydroelectric exports remains. Section 201(b)(2) applies sections 210, 211 and 212 and their enforcement to the entities those provisions cover; compliance with a section 210 or 211 order does not otherwise subject an entity to general FPA jurisdiction. Section 201(f)'s exclusion of federal, state and local governments, their agencies, authorities and instrumentalities, corporations wholly owned directly or indirectly by one or more of them, and their officials acting in duty remains unless a provision specifically refers to them.

Section 212(g) prohibits orders inconsistent with state retail-marketing-area law. Section 212(h) prohibits an order conditioned on, or requiring, direct transmission to an ultimate consumer. Its separate rule for transmission to or for a resale entity permits the covered exception only when both conditions hold: the entity is a federal power marketing agency, TVA, a State or political subdivision or its agency, a corporation or association that has ever received a Rural Electrification Administration loan to provide electric service under the Rural Electrification Act of 1936, a person with a state- or local-law public electric-service obligation arising independently of contract alone, or a corporation or association wholly owned directly or indirectly by one or more of those listed entities; and it either served that consumer on October 24, 1992 or would deliver all the energy through transmission or distribution facilities it owns or controls. State and local authority over direct-to-consumer transmission remains preserved. Section 212(e) makes use of sections 210, 211, 213 or 212 unnecessary as a substitute for other lawful authority and, except as those sections provide, prevents them from limiting other Commission authority; it does not eliminate the retail-wheeling restrictions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a service does not become a retail sale merely because it transmits energy that the customer purchases at retail. The statute grants authority over interstate transmission separately from wholesale interstate sales. Reading the wholesale limitation into both clauses would insert a restriction Congress did not enact. Section 201(a)'s general policy statement must be read with those operative grants and specific exclusions, not made a repeal of them.

New York's strongest objection is that federal regulation of retail-associated transmission shifts an activity traditionally administered by States. The answer lies in the actual service regulated. The order reaches independent interstate transmission already provided voluntarily or under state direction; it does not require new retail wheeling or transfer retail-rate and local-distribution authority wholesale. State siting, local reliability and retail-service functions remain, and no particular facility classification is decided. The connected grid identifies the interstate facts; it creates no power apart from the text.

Iowa Utilities Board supplies the distinction between an enacted federal assignment and independent limits governing its exercise. Gulf Power likewise separates coverage from a particular regulatory method. Mead requires a real delegation before agency interpretation acquires controlling force; the enacted categories resolve this question independently, without a new deference rule. The statutory boundaries thus answer the allocation objection while sustaining the order's limited transmission coverage.

### II. This proceeding did not compel Enron's additional bundled-service remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** FERC could remedy the wholesale transmission-access discrimination established in this proceeding through the adopted open-access requirements while declining compulsory unbundling of bundled retail service on its independently sufficient determination that this additional remedy was unnecessary. Section 206 does require a lawful prospective determination and order after a hearing and finding that a jurisdictional rate, charge, classification or affecting rule, regulation, practice or contract is unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or preferential; the burden to prove that unlawfulness lies on the Commission or complainant, and the agency may not leave established discrimination uncorrected.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has the direct support of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those six votes control. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would remand this component for corrected agency analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that authority, the trigger for a duty to act and the necessary extent of correction are different questions. Sections 205 and 206 preserve lawful rates, nondiscrimination, filed schedules and mandatory correction when the required finding is made. They do not make every proposed structural extension obligatory merely because the Commission may possess authority to consider it.

The agency did characterize bundled delivered energy as a state-regulated retail sale. That ground cannot be erased by calling the order purely discretionary. But the agency also separately determined that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary to eliminate the discrimination it found. The Court sustains that actual, record-linked remedial determination without adopting a categorical jurisdictional exclusion. The functional-unbundling requirements address the established wholesale-access discrimination; Enron's demand does not show why the findings require the additional restructuring in this proceeding. Counsel's claim of inadequate record support is not substituted for the agency's own explanation.

The opposing Justices contend that the jurisdictional characterization undermines the agency's understanding of the corrective duty. That concern would require remand if the refusal depended on legal powerlessness. Here the independent unnecessary-remedy determination adequately supports the refusal of the particular additional remedy sought. Good Samaritan permits considered administration within a mandatory statutory structure but rejects unsupported litigation rationalizations. MCI forbids dispensing with an enacted obligation, and Blumer distinguishes a mandatory trigger from a permissible means; none requires the Court to prescribe Enron's preferred remedy. Gulf Power's demand to preserve applicable classifications and formulas does not remove the actual independent ground present here.

The holding establishes no permanent bundled-service exemption. A universal allocation of bundled-service jurisdiction beyond the authority needed for this order falls outside the particular remedial demand and is not decided. The demand for compulsory additional unbundling in this proceeding is fully resolved.

# Barnhart v. Walton — 2002-03-27

[Authoritative Record](../records/barnhart_v_walton_merits_2002-03-27.md)

## Event

Barnhart v. Walton, No. 00-1937. Argued January 16, 2002; decided March 27, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 235 F.3d 184. The questions are whether the agency may require twelve months of disabling inability as well as lasting impairment, and whether actual substantial work within that period before a favorable disability determination defeats projected-duration and trial-work theories. The circuit affirmed the October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding but reversed the denial on both legal grounds.

## Participation

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela and Justices Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate at argument and decision.

## Public Action

The challenged Fourth Circuit legal rulings are reversed, and the case is remanded.

## Judgment & Remedy

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower court must implement denial on the reviewed duration and trial-work eligibility grounds. Its affirmed October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding remains undisturbed.

## Opinion Topology

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and III | Souter | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Formal delegated-regulation ground; duration and timing rules, qualifications and disposition |

## Holdings

### I. The agency may require twelve months of disabling inability

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the twelve-month branch of the adult disability definitions in 42 U.S.C. §§423(d)(1)(A) and 1382c(a)(3)(A), the agency may require that the impairment cause inability to engage in substantial gainful activity lasting, or expected to last, continuously for at least twelve months. The expected-death branch remains distinct; the Court sustains a permissible delegated construction rather than declaring it the only statutory implementation Congress allows.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part I, is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the formal delegated-regulation ground, the functional-duration rule and its temporal qualification. Part II's additional contextual support is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Scalia does not join that additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the agency's construction fits the integrated definition. The relative clause concerning duration follows impairment, but the defined disability is inability to work because of that impairment. The wording does not unambiguously require benefits for a lasting diagnosis after the disabling inability ends. The agency may read the lasting condition as the condition that produces the described inability. The expected-death alternative concerns the impairment; it does not mean that inability or work itself causes death. Title II's ordinary five-month waiting condition does not compel an impairment-only interpretation, and that waiting rule is not imported into SSI.

Section 405(a), incorporated into SSI by §1383(d)(1), supplies rulemaking authority consistent with the benefit statutes. The agency exercised it in binding implementing regulations and the final trial-work provision, after a proposal and notice and comment. Mead requires actual delegation and its exercise, not expertise or form alone; those predicates are established here. Chevron permits the resulting reasonable choice. The July 2000 final rule became effective August 10, 2000. Smiley permits use of its authoritative interpretation of the preexisting statute for antecedent events without backdating a new legislative condition, where clear contrary prior official guidance is absent. The longstanding duration approach and 1995 proposal supply continuity here; no contrary assurance to Walton is established. The lower court's no-retroactivity statement therefore does not exclude the interpretive use at issue.

**Additional controlling explanation in Part II:** The sustained administrative interpretation and the agency's detailed attention to the connected eligibility provisions further support the reasonableness of its choice. These considerations operate within the established delegation and formal exercise of authority; they create neither power from longevity alone nor a holding that every earlier letter, manual or Social Security Ruling has controlling force. Scalia accepts the sufficient formal-regulation ground while withholding this additional contextual ground.

### II. Actual work before a favorable disability finding may defeat the initial duration claim

**Holding and operative rule:** When work demonstrating substantial gainful activity resumes within twelve months after onset of the disabling impairment and before notice of a determination or decision finding disability, the agency may use that known event to reject an initial claim based on an earlier expectation of twelve-month inability. Section 422(c)'s trial-work protection presupposes qualifying Title II entitlement and does not independently establish a missing initial duration condition.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the permissible timing construction, the qualifications below, and the disposition of Walton's two legal theories.

**Operative qualifications:** A claimant returning to work more than twelve months after onset but before adjudication may qualify for trial-work protection if actual duration and the other entitlement conditions are satisfied; passage of twelve months alone does not cure disabling inability that ended earlier. A claimant returning within twelve months after onset, but after notice finding disability, may qualify even when that notice precedes the benefit-award notice. A disability finding, its notice, an award, payment and statutory entitlement are distinct; neither qualification requires prior payment. The separate exclusion for work during a required waiting period remains separate, and the pre-application exclusion concerns months before application, not permanent disqualification for every earlier work episode.

Trial-work services are distinct from substantial gainful activity. The operative nine service months occur within sixty consecutive months and need not themselves be consecutive. New medical or other evidence independent of protected trial work may establish cessation under the governing rules before nine months; a repeated trial-work period is not created within the same period of disability or entitlement. Ordinary Title II insured status, application, age and the five-month waiting condition, including the qualifying prior-entitlement or cessation route within the statutory sixty-month window, remain separate conditions. The Title II trial-work scheme is not transferred to SSI. Statutory blindness, the separate child definition, genuinely unsuccessful work attempts and distinct reopening or reliance questions concerning a claimant with an existing favorable determination are outside the presented adult eligibility dispute.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute does not fix an earlier moment at which an expectation becomes irrevocable before the initial favorable determination. Expected duration permits prompt awards before twelve months have elapsed; it does not require the agency to disregard actual work already known when initial eligibility is decided. The agency may distinguish establishing disability from deciding whether an entitled person's disability has ceased. A protection against using trial work to establish cessation cannot alone create the initial entitlement it presupposes.

Walton objects that entitlement need not await an award and that adjudication timing can produce unequal results. The first point is correct and is preserved by the two qualifications above. The agency acknowledged the second and reasonably accommodated two statutory purposes: prompt prospective awards and exclusion of demonstrated short-term disability. That explanation does not declare every administrative delay harmless; it answers the claimed requirement to treat an earlier expectation as conclusive here. Good Samaritan preserves reasonable administrative choice within the assigned program, while Smiley preserves qualified interpretive use for antecedent events. The actual statutory grant and rulemaking satisfy Mead; no new generalized deference test or resort to Auer is needed.

Walton's October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding stands. His later closed-period award was superseded on administrative review, and no favorable finding notice preceded his return. The circuit's impairment-only interpretation, accrued-expectation theory and independent unreasonableness conclusion therefore fail. Its challenged legal rulings are reversed, with remand to implement the denial on these eligibility grounds.

- **Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A.:** Applied to qualified interpretive use for antecedent events without backdating a new legislative condition or disregarding clear contrary prior official guidance.

The agency may administer the adult duration definitions through a twelve-month requirement for disabling inability and use actual substantial work before the relevant favorable finding notice in deciding initial eligibility. **Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Scalia joins Parts I and III, including the formal delegated-regulation ground, permissible duration and timing constructions, qualifications and reversal. The Ninth Circuit judgment invalidating the challenged definitions is affirmed. Relief against enforcement through §§2256(8)(B) and (D) stands; the separate actual-child and obscenity prohibitions and the unchallenged identifiable-minor provision retain their independent operation. Substantial overbreadth supplies the ground without a separate Fifth Amendment vagueness judgment.

## Holdings

**I. Apparent minority does not replace exploitation or obscenity.** A criminal definition substantially reaching nonobscene depictions produced without exploiting an actual child cannot be sustained merely because the depicted person appears to be a minor. Section 2256(8)(B)'s restriction fails the narrow tailoring required for this content-based burden on protected expression, including its wholly virtual realistic and actual-adult applications.

**Authority:** Parts I and II of Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court are joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Breyer. Those six directly support both rules and the statutory qualifications below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ferber's exclusion rests on injuries to actual children in production and circulation; visual resemblance creates neither injury by itself. Miller's independent obscenity elements remain necessary to that category. Reno and Playboy require the Government to justify this protected-speech burden, while Broadrick requires substantial protected reach relative to legitimate applications. The asserted grooming, demand and proof difficulties are serious interests, but they do not establish the fit required for this prohibition. A lawful image's possible misuse cannot itself supply production abuse, and difficulty proving an actual child does not permit suppression of substantial protected substitutes. A realistic-image construction does not cure that defect or supply the absent serious-value protection. Actual exploitation, obscenity and independently unlawful solicitation retain their own predicates.

**II. Presentation does not transform a protected work into contraband.** Section 2256(8)(D) is independently substantially overbroad because promotional presentation can make a nonobscene, nonexploitative work prohibited material in connected distribution and possession offenses, including for persons unconnected with the presentation. A court may not save this definition by rewriting it as a separately defined intentional unlawful-offer offense.

**Authority:** The same six directly join Part II and this independent invalidity ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Ginzburg makes pandering evidence relevant within an obscenity inquiry; it does not automatically remove protection from the underlying nonobscene work. The enacted definition is broader than the Government's proposed knowing-promoter construction, and ordinary scienter does not confine all connected liability to the original promoter. Reno permits genuine severance but not invention of absent offense limits. A differently enacted unlawful-offer offense lies outside the question, as does the validity of the identifiable-actual-minor definition in (C).

**Operative statutory qualifications.** A minor is under eighteen. Sexually explicit conduct covers actual or simulated genital-genital, oral-genital, anal-genital or oral-anal intercourse, whether between persons of the same or opposite sex, bestiality, masturbation, sadistic or masochistic abuse and lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area; mere nudity does not itself establish the last category. Section 2252A(c) applies only to (a)(1)–(4), requires actual adult performers at production, and requires that the defendant did not advertise, promote, present, describe or distribute the work as depicting minors. It excludes wholly generated images and ordinary possession under (a)(5). Section 2252A(d), separately, concerns possession of fewer than three images: prompt, good-faith reasonable destruction steps or reporting the images to law enforcement and affording access to each image, without retaining any image or copy or permitting anyone other than law enforcement access to one. It requires reasonable destruction steps, not successful destruction, and provides no right to retain protected expression. The unchallenged (C) definition concerns a recognizable actual person who was a minor at the relevant creation, adaptation or modification or whose image as a minor was used; proof of the person's actual identity is unnecessary. These provisions do not cure the challenged protected reach.

## Precedent Treatment

- **New York v. Ferber, 458 U.S. 747, and Osborne v. Ohio, 495 U.S. 103:** Their actual-child injury predicates are preserved; they are not extended to an invented image or an adult performance solely because it resembles a child.
- **Miller v. California, 413 U.S. 15:** Obscenity remains an independent category with its own elements, not a substitute apparent-age rule.
- **Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union:** Its protected-adult-expression and substantial-overbreadth reasoning applies; its distinction between genuine severance and judicial rewriting defeats the proposed new offense limits.
- **United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc.:** Its content-based scrutiny and governmental justification burden govern the fit of the challenged criminal restrictions.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.:** Knowledge of depiction characteristics remains distinct from constitutional authority to suppress those characteristics; this case changes no surviving factual-knowledge requirement.
- **Broadrick v. Oklahoma, 413 U.S. 601:** Substantial protected reach, rather than a conceivable unusual application, supports facial relief here.
- **Ginzburg v. United States, 383 U.S. 463:** Pandering may inform an obscenity inquiry; it does not independently make a protected nonobscene work contraband for all possessors.

## Law After Decision

The two challenged definitions cannot support enforcement against the substantial protected expression they cover. Apparent minority without exploitation or obscenity does not suffice, and promotional characterization does not automatically transform a protected work into prohibited material. Independent actual-child, obscenity and unlawful-conduct rules retain their predicates. The identifiable-actual-minor category and a differently enacted unlawful-offer offense are outside this decision.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** Thomas agrees with both invalidations but does not join the Court's principal reasoning. Enforcement of actual-child prohibitions is the Government's strongest concern. The present predictive showing does not establish a technological proof obstacle sufficient for this breadth, but practical impossibility of distinguishing fabricated from actual-child images could present a different constitutional question. An appropriate affirmative defense or another narrowly drawn restriction might matter on that demonstrated premise. He neither requires a particular future defense nor approves shifting the actual-child element through a current presumption. His agreement in judgment does not endorse a categorical foreclosure of every enforcement-based restriction affecting protected expression.

**O'Connor, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part; Scalia joins Part II of her writing only.** In Part I she agrees with invalidation of the youthful-adult application: adult performers' protected expression remains exposed because the limited adult defense does not reach ordinary possession. She separately agrees that (D)'s presentation category lacks an adequately justified compelling fit, particularly when describing adult performers as minors removes the defense. In Part II she would preserve (B) for wholly virtual images virtually indistinguishable from actual-child images. The narrow construction excludes cartoons, sculptures and plainly unreal depictions. Congress may respond to supported enforcement predictions before actual proof failure becomes universal; in her view the child-protection interest and limited application satisfy the constitutional inquiry. Broadrick and application-severability principles permit preserving that application without extending the adult defense to possession. Her vagueness conclusion is confined to the realistic-image construction. Scalia joins only this virtual-image sustaining ground, not her adult or presentation invalidations.

**Scalia, dissenting.** Scalia would sustain both definitions under narrowing constructions. The statutory conduct requirement reaches actual or realistically simulated hardcore sexual acts, not merely a film's subject matter, and apparent minority means realistic images virtually indistinguishable from depictions of actual children. He adopts O'Connor's narrow virtual-image enforcement rationale but does not accept her adult-application invalidation. For (D), he reads offense scienter together with the definition to confine liability to knowing pandering by the responsible actor, excluding innocent downstream possession. Ginzburg supports the relevance of deliberate presentation to this confined transaction in his view. The Court regards that reading as alteration of a definition used in connected offenses and finds the remaining protected reach substantial. Scalia does not treat all nonobscene adult expression as unprotected or enlarge §2252A(c)'s defense.

## Procedure After Action

The judgment protecting the challengers against enforcement of §§2256(8)(B) and (D) remains in force. This merits review is concluded subject to the ordinary mandate; the surviving independent statutory categories remain enforceable on their own terms.

# Thompson v. Western States Medical Center — 2002-04-29

[Authoritative Record](../records/thompson_v_western_states_medical_center_merits_2002-04-29.md)

## Event

Thompson v. Western States Medical Center, No. 01-344. Argued February 26, 2002. Decided April 29, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing its February 6, 2001 judgment, 238 F.3d 1090, holding the challenged compounding speech conditions unconstitutional and section 353a nonseverable. The question is whether conditioning the federal compounded-drug exemptions on the prescription-solicitation and particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion restrictions violates the First Amendment. The separate nonseverability ruling was not brought within review.

Licensed pharmacies prepared promotional materials for distribution by mail and at medical conferences. The challenge concerns truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding. Section 503A(a) permits the identified patient's prescription through unsolicited receipt or a prescribing practitioner's approved notation that a compounded product is necessary for that patient. The ordinary route requires compounding by a licensed pharmacist in a State-licensed pharmacy or Federal facility, or by a licensed physician, on the identified patient's prescription from a licensed physician or another practitioner authorized by State law to prescribe. The separate advance-compounding route permits a licensed pharmacist or physician to prepare limited quantities before receiving the patient's valid prescription, based on a history of receiving valid prescriptions for that drug generated solely within an established relationship between the compounder and either that identified patient or the physician or other practitioner who will write the prescription. Section 503A(c) prohibits particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion while permitting promotion of compounding services; the necessity notation does not exempt a message from subsection (c).

Section 503A supplies only exemptions from section 501(a)(2)(B)'s manufacturing-practice requirement, section 502(f)(1)'s adequate-directions requirement and section 505's new-drug-approval requirement. Bulk drug substances must comply with an applicable United States Pharmacopoeia or National Formulary monograph and the United States Pharmacopoeia compounding chapter; if no monograph exists, they must be components of approved drugs or, if neither condition is met, appear on the Secretary's regulatory list. They must be manufactured by a registered establishment, including a foreign establishment registered under section 510(i), and accompanied by valid certificates of analysis. Nonbulk ingredients must comply with an applicable monograph, if one exists, and the compounding chapter.

The compounder may not use a drug product on the Secretary's published list of products withdrawn or removed because the product or a component was found unsafe or ineffective. Regular compounding, or compounding in inordinate amounts as defined by the Secretary, of essentially commercially available copies is barred; a change for an identified patient producing a significant difference determined by the prescribing practitioner is excluded from that copy category. The product must not be one identified by regulation as presenting demonstrable compounding difficulties reasonably demonstrating an adverse effect on safety or effectiveness.

The interstate alternatives are compounding in a State that has entered a memorandum with the Secretary addressing inordinate interstate distribution and providing for appropriate State-agency investigation of complaints about compounded products distributed outside that State, or, absent that memorandum, out-of-State distribution no greater than five percent of the pharmacy's or physician's total prescription orders dispensed or distributed. The statute contains no enacted twenty-percent memorandum ceiling. PET compounded drugs and radiopharmaceuticals are excluded. Mixing, reconstitution and other acts performed according to the manufacturer's approved labeling and other directions consistent with that labeling lie outside the statutory compounding definition. These distinct provisions are not interchangeable exceptions to the challenged promotion rule.

## Holdings

#### The challenged conditions burden truthful promotion more broadly than their justification supports

**Holding and operative rule.** The prescription-solicitation and particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion conditions of section 503A violate the First Amendment as applied to truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding because the Government has not established a reasonable fit between their informational burden and its asserted distinction between individualized compounding and manufacturing subject to approval. The holding applies the ordinary commercial-speech framework; it neither requires the least restrictive imaginable measure nor prevents restrictions on misleading claims, unlawful transactions, actual manufacturing conduct or narrower commercial-speech measures supported by an adequate justification.

**Authority.** Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas. These six Justices adopt the complete fit holding, statutory explanation and affirmance. Thomas accepts this sufficient application without endorsing the framework as the full measure of constitutional protection. This is a directly shared rationale, not an aggregation of distinct advancement findings.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that calling drug-specific silence a condition of an exemption does not answer the First Amendment question. Central Hudson and Fox require a proportionate relationship between a lawful commercial-speech burden and the substantial interest it serves. The Court assumes the Government's strongest combined account of drug safety, approval integrity, individualized access and material advancement. The defect is fit.

Product information can enable clinicians and patients to locate an individualized preparation without establishing manufacture for a broad market. Section 503A(a) allows the identified patient's prescription through unsolicited receipt or a prescribing practitioner's approved notation of necessity; its separate advance-compounding route permits a licensed pharmacist or physician to prepare limited quantities before receiving the patient's valid prescription, based on a history of receiving valid prescriptions for that drug generated solely within an established relationship with either that identified patient or the practitioner who will prescribe it. Section 503A(c), however, separately bars promotion of a particular drug, drug class or drug type while allowing service promotion. The necessity notation is not an exception to that separate speech prohibition.

Production, distribution, patient-prescription and commercial-copy controls bear directly on the conduct Congress seeks to distinguish. The Court recognizes the contrary concern: quantity limits can exclude medically needed preparations and may not address every demand-driven use of unapproved drugs. That does not explain why useful truthful product information must carry this broad burden. The Government has not adequately justified this proxy against the available conduct distinctions. The Court selects no production cap and finds no guarantee that every proposed alternative is equally effective. As 44 Liquormart and Lorillard require, the actual speech burden remains a distinct inquiry even when the regulatory objective and some preventive effect are accepted.

**Precedent treatment.** Central Hudson and Fox supply the governing substantial-interest, advancement and reasonable-fit framework; this decision applies their fit requirement without replacing it with strict scrutiny or an absolute alternatives rule. The separate precedent account below states the remaining treatments.

## Precedent Treatment

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission and Board of Trustees v. Fox: applied for ordinary commercial-speech scrutiny and proportional fit; no new tier or least-restrictive-means rule is adopted.
- 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island: applied for independent examination of the burden on truthful information; its broader separate theories are not converted into a controlling categorical ban.
- Lorillard Tobacco Co. v. Reilly: applied for separate advancement and fit inquiries and attention to the actual lawful-speech burden; this decision rests on fit and assumes the Government's advancement account.
- Edenfield v. Fane: applied for justification responsive to the actual professional setting; the physician audience is part of the covered speech burden.
- Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association v. United States: applied for practical examination of the fit between a selective speech restriction and the regulatory objective; no conclusion that all compounded products are safe follows.
- Lanphere & Urbaniak v. Colorado: its benefit-condition principle remains applicable at its actual scope; selecting speech as the condition does not eliminate ordinary constitutional scrutiny.

## Law After Decision

The reviewed compounding speech conditions fail the existing reasonable-fit requirement. The Court establishes no independent failure of material advancement, categorical commercial-speech immunity or replacement compounding program. The Ninth Circuit's unreviewed nonseverability determination remains part of the affirmed judgment. Substantive drug-safety and manufacturing authority retains its independent scope.

## Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring.** He joins the full fit holding because these restrictions fail even the lesser Central Hudson test. He separately regards suppression of accurate product information to prevent its effect on willing listeners as the mechanism his 44 Liquormart position rejects. Drug safety is legitimate, but that objective does not itself justify withholding accurate information; direct drug controls remain distinct. His concurrence is not judgment-only agreement and does not supply the Court with a broader categorical rule.

**Breyer, dissenting in part, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** The dissent would reverse the constitutional invalidation insofar as it rests on promotion to consumers. The statutory balance preserves medically needed preparations while preventing cumulative risks from a growing market of products that have not undergone approval. Product promotion can generate demand apart from a clinician's prior individualized assessment. Production caps, wholesale restrictions and controls on equipment or advance preparation may either exclude needed treatment or fail to address that demand. General service advertising and continuing physician involvement preserve meaningful channels. Under ordinary reasonable-fit review, the dissent therefore finds the consumer restriction proportionate despite its burden on truthful information. It reserves the distinct question of physician-directed promotion because that issue requires separate consideration; it neither sustains every communication restriction nor announces a benefit-condition exception to the First Amendment.

## Procedure After Action

The Ninth Circuit judgment is affirmed, subject to ordinary mandate administration. Its unreviewed nonseverability determination remains operative. Supreme Court review of the constitutional question is complete.
