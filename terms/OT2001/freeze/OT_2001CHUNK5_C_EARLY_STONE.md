## 74. Bell v. Cone

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Sixth Circuit’s grant of habeas relief because these particular omissions do not trigger presumed prejudice and the state adjudication does not meet the statutory standard for relief on this record.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Remand consistently with denial of relief on this ineffective-assistance ground. Preserve separate claims only if independently pending and legally available; do not invent such claims or disturb the convictions.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Particular omissions within active representation

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the Cronic categorization and the state adjudication under the proper collateral standard.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether these sentencing choices constitute total adversarial abandonment and, if not, whether they justify federal collateral relief under Strickland and AEDPA.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing distinction between complete denial and particular errors, together with Williams v. Taylor's unreasonable-application rule.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A represented capital sentencing proceeding does not become a complete absence of adversarial testing merely because counsel presents no additional witnesses and waives closing, where counsel relies on evidence already introduced, contests aggravation, and makes a supported tactical choice about rebuttal. Those decisions remain subject to Strickland, and this state adjudication is not objectively unreasonable on the established record.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding addresses this active representation and documented strategic account. It does not excuse abandonment, uninformed investigation, or a categorical refusal to present available mitigation.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Cronic supplies a narrow presumption for a complete loss of meaningful adversarial assistance; Strickland governs particular failures within representation. Cone's lawyer relied on mitigation already introduced, contested the aggravation evidence and waived closing to avoid a stronger prosecutorial rebuttal. Those concrete acts defeat a characterization of the entire sentencing phase as abandonment, while leaving the quality of each choice open to Strickland review. Under Terry Williams, objective unreasonableness remains distinct from mere disagreement and the full supported mitigation record must be examined. The state adjudication used a permissible standard and made a record-supported assessment of these decisions; the proposed Cronic shortcut does not overcome §2254(d). Statutory collateral limits are decisive after correct classification of the Sixth Amendment claim. Stone therefore denies relief on this ground without treating all tactical explanations as conclusive or importing later categorical restrictions on habeas fact development.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A claim based on additional uninvestigated evidence would require evidence not established on this record. No new mitigation facts are assumed.
## 77. Federal Maritime Commission v. South Carolina State Ports Authority

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit’s categorical immunity ruling and remand for the Commission to adjudicate the Shipping Act complaint.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The administrative complaint may proceed, with liability and authorized relief determined under the Shipping Act and ordinary administrative review. No berth, reparation, fee award, or injunction is ordered by this decision. A later independent private judicial enforcement action would require its own statutory and immunity analysis.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Federal adjudication of regulated port conduct

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Accept the uncontested state-arm status and reach whether it prevents this federal administrative proceeding. Do not adjudicate the underlying discrimination.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether a State may require dismissal of a Commission complaint solely because a private party initiated an adjudication within the federal maritime regulatory scheme.
* **Stone’s Legal Position:** Apply the rejection in Florida Prepaid, Alden and Garrett of categorical Article I incapacity and constructive commercial waiver, and propose the unresolved rule for this administrative forum.
* **Stone’s Proposed Holding:** State immunity from private judicial suit does not bar the Commission from administering the Shipping Act through its statutory complaint adjudication against a state-operated port. Private initiation and a request for reparations do not transform the Commission into an independent private court or remove the proceeding from federal regulatory administration.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding permits this Commission process, including adjudication of the remedies the Act authorizes within it. It does not establish a universal waiver, confer a new private judicial cause of action, or decide the immunity rules governing a later collection suit.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** The Shipping Act regulates port operations within Congress's maritime and commerce responsibility and assigns complaints, adjudication and authorized remedies to the Commission. Florida Prepaid, Alden and Garrett reject a categorical Article I immunity from properly authorized enforcement; College Savings Bank separately rejects fictional consent from doing business. Stone applies those structural principles and resolves the open administrative setting. The Commission's actual statutory role, not the private complainant's identity or courtroom-like safeguards, makes this federal administration of a valid regulatory duty. Procedural safeguards protect fairness rather than transform the agency into an independent private court. A State retains all coverage and merits defenses, but immunity supplies no categorical exemption from this Commission process. That is the decisive institutional distinction; the holding neither infers commercial waiver nor adjudicates a later independent collection action.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Liability, factual discrimination, and the amount or form of a lawful administrative remedy require findings absent here. A later private judicial enforcement action is outside the question presented.
