## 1. Validated conclusion

The parties accept that the new independent claims contain the disputed structural limitations. Festo itself describes adding the Stoll sealing-ring and magnetizable-sleeve limitations from earlier dependent claims and adding the Carroll sealing-ring limitation during reexamination. That accepted textual history must not be restated as an express, unqualified concession that each disputed addition was a substantively narrowing, patentability-driven surrender of the accused equivalent. Festo's own argument preserves the distinction: it accepts that a clarifying addition can narrow literal claim language while denying that such a change necessarily surrenders equivalents; it affirmatively contests surrender relevant to this suit. Its oral rebuttal also challenges treating movement of a Stoll limitation between claims as the relevant narrowing. Pet. Br. 7-8, 21-29; Reply Br. 5-6, 16-18; Tr. 6-7, 47-48; excerpts E1-E5 below.

One express, qualified oral acknowledgment appears at argument: counsel admits that Carroll involved abandonment of some subject matter, while immediately asserting that the abandoned matter was unrelated to the suit. In the same answer he asserts no Stoll abandonment. This cannot be converted into a concession that the Carroll ring addition surrendered the single-ring equivalent, or that the Stoll additions had the same purpose. Tr. 6:20-7:6; E4.

The sources also establish a genuine party disagreement over prior-art purpose. SMC contends both ring amendments were made to avoid prior art and treats the Stoll sleeve addition as unexplained, with prior-art avoidance offered as the alternative explanation. Festo rejects those descriptions as grounds for estoppel. The phrase that Festo effectively concedes an unexplained sleeve purpose is SMC's characterization of Festo's position, not Festo's own categorical admission. Resp. Br. 8-10, 20-23; Pet. Br. 24 & n.1, 28-32; Reply Br. 16-18; E6-E8.

These findings support an amendment-specific analysis on the existing source record. They neither establish that all trigger questions are open for factual retrial nor settle them by concession. Determining the legal effect of an accepted claim change remains different from establishing what counsel actually conceded.

## 3. Primary prosecution support and its limits

**Stoll.** The first office action, mailed July 2, 1981, rejects claims 1-12 under §112 because the manner of operation is unclear and asks whether the device is a motor or magnetic clutch; it separately objects to claims 4-12 as improperly multiply dependent. JA II-30-31, scan page 19. This is the examiner's stated rejection, not a finding that each later limitation was necessary to answer it.

The November 2, 1981 response cancels original claim 1 and substitutes a new independent claim, adds the ring structure and magnetizable sleeve, and restructures the dependent claims. Its remarks request reconsideration for compliance with §112 and submit two German references, distinguishing them by the structure preventing interference from impurities inside and outside the tube. JA II-36-40, scan pages 22-24. That combination of statements supports the parties' competing purpose arguments; the response does not itself decide which legal inference must follow for each disputed limitation. The original main claim already included sealing means at each end, while the dependent claims separately specified rings and magnetizable material. JA II-24-25, scan page 16. Thus comparison must concern the particular claims and features, not an assumption that all sealing functions first entered in the amendment.

**Carroll.** The July 21, 1988 response cancels claim 1 and proposes claim 9, which includes a pair of resilient rings near opposite axial ends of the central mounting member. JA II-180-181. The response says the replacement defines the invention more specifically against the art and describes the ring structure among its features. Its detailed comparison and summary emphasize spaced inner and outer magnets and resilient end cushions. JA II-182-186. The allowance identifies the combination of magnets, end members, and cushion members in their claimed relationship. JA II-188-189. These are positive contents of the recovered response and allowance, verified against scans 94-98. They support Festo's asserted distinction between the purpose of the overall replacement and the specific ring limitation, as well as SMC's reliance on the accepted combination; this supplement selects neither legal conclusion.

The response's identification of a combination does not itself establish that every feature was separately new. Conversely, showing that two rings existed in a reference would not by itself decide what was relinquished by the chosen amended claim. Those are distinct possible arguments about the trigger and the scope of surrender, not concessions supplied by this review.

## 4. Distinctions that remain live

1. **Claim language and actual narrowing.** Festo acknowledges the new limitations and the use of equivalents to reach the accused designs. Its general acknowledgment that clarification can narrow literal scope is not a claim-specific stipulation of a qualifying estoppel trigger. Its Stoll rebuttal affirmatively disputes the significance of moving an existing limitation into another claim. This leaves a legal construction question on documented claims, not uncertainty about whether the written changes occurred.
2. **Narrowing and patentability purpose.** Festo argues that statutory form and disclosure requirements should not be treated as the same estoppel-generating category as novelty, utility, and nonobviousness. SMC argues that substantive narrowing to satisfy statutory requirements, including §112, qualifies. The parties also disagree about the actual ring-amendment purposes; their general statutory dispute does not erase those particulars. Resp. Br. 23-27; Reply Br. 3-6; Tr. 5-7, 31-33, 44-47. The authorized entering-law rules control over either side's descriptions of precedent.
3. **Required or voluntary change.** Festo accepts the general proposition that an applicant can surrender subject matter while responding to a formal objection; the fact that the process started with form does not protect an actual surrender. Tr. 6:10-16. Its position is that relevant surrender did not occur here. This qualified acknowledgment is not a concession that the examiner required the ring or sleeve limitation, nor a finding that a voluntary change can never create estoppel.
4. **Trigger and extent.** Festo's opposition to a complete bar is independent of its threshold arguments. It maintains that surrendered matter cannot be recaptured but that other equivalents can remain. SMC seeks a complete bar for the affected limitations and invokes the existing unexplained-amendment rule for the sleeve. The government's intermediate proposal remains separately attributed in the authorized packet. None of these positions is a concession that SMC's particular material and ring substitutions fall within actual surrender.
5. **Burden and evidence.** Festo contests confinement of an explanatory showing to the prosecution file; SMC defends reliance on public materials. Pet. Br. 29-32; Reply Br. 6; Resp. Br. 21-23. SMC's quoted account of an earlier Festo en banc brief at Resp. Br. 22 n.18 is a secondary quotation of that filing and concerns a conditional consequence when no reason can be established. It must not become a stipulation that no reason is establishable here, or that the burden has already failed under the authorized entering law.

## 5. Preservation and remedy remain distinct

The authorized neutral packet reports, attributing the government, that SMC did not raise estoppel against Carroll at summary judgment. This source pass neither withdraws that limited report nor converts it into a final waiver ruling. The district court's reproduced order separately resolves Carroll ring equivalence by summary judgment and leaves Stoll ring equivalence for trial. JA I-78-80. That separation matters when using later trial exchanges.

The trial record shows a contested exchange, not an unqualified abandonment stipulation. At JA I-137-138 (trial day 4, pages 118-119), the judge states that ring estoppel had not been raised; SMC counsel responds that he understands estoppel also to concern the two seals and says counsel and court may be addressing different matters. At JA I-141 (day 6, pages 53-54), SMC counsel describes the case as not really an estoppel case while stressing the materiality of each claim element. When the judge interprets that as abandoning estoppel, counsel immediately rejects the characterization and reserves further noninfringement argument. E9-E10 preserve the surrounding exchanges.

Those exchanges occur in the Stoll trial setting, explicitly discuss the sleeve as well as rings, and do not supply a Carroll-specific appellate preservation ruling. Festo's Reply Br. 17 relies on part of the exchange and challenges the appellate treatment of prior-art purpose; that remains Festo's argument. A proposed disposition resting on Carroll waiver must identify the particular issue, the procedural act preserving or forfeiting it, and the applicable preservation rule, rather than extrapolating from a fragment of Stoll trial dialogue. This pass makes no claim that the relevant filings or evidence are absent from the complete record.

Rejecting a complete bar alone does not resolve the competing trigger accounts, the scope of any surrender, technical equivalence, the all-elements objection, or preservation. Festo requests reversal and argues for reinstating its infringement judgment (Pet. Br. 31-32, 45); SMC requests affirmance and identifies an all-elements objection left undecided by the en banc court (Resp. Br. 15 n.12, 48). These are requested remedies and attributed procedural positions, not an entered disposition. No automatic new evidentiary opportunity follows from this supplement.

In particular, describe Carroll's amended combination as offered to distinguish the German art, while expressly preserving Festo's denial that the sealing rings supplied that distinction. Describe Stoll's §112 rejection and response without stating that each new limitation was conceded necessary for §112. Treat the sleeve's asserted unexplained purpose separately from the rings.

There is no need to invent a blanket concession or to erase a documented dispute to make the matter ready.
