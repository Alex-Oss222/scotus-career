## Entering law and separable questions

### Termination, constitutional objections and estoppel

The actual pending motions invoke two distinct routes. Section 3626(b)(1)(A)(iii) makes pre-enactment relief terminable on a proper motion two years after April 26, 1996. Section 3626(b)(2) requires the defendant or intervener seeking immediate termination to establish the absence of substantive original findings of need, narrowness and least intrusiveness. Findings need no prescribed formula. Sufficient original findings can defeat the latter predicate without defeating an independently available elapsed-period motion under the former route. Both remain subject to amended subsection (b)(3).

Under the amendment effective November 26, 1997 and applicable to pending cases, retained relief requires written findings grounded in the record that it remains necessary to correct a current **and** ongoing federal-right violation, reaches no further than needed for correction, is narrowly drawn, and uses the least intrusive necessary means. The conjunction is not the earlier disjunction applied in Lewis and Plyler. No supported violation excluded solely by that textual change is established here; the reserved constitutional question about that particular operation need not be resolved.

Plyler (June 23, 1997), Rouse (January 15, 1998), Benjamin (May 7, 1998), Gavin (May 21, 1998) and Hadix (September 14, 1998) control the conditional prospective-relief framework. They reject permanent immunity for future decree enforcement while retaining judicial determination of facts and remedial necessity. Robertson (March 25, 1992) distinguishes changed governing law from a legislative result dictated under unchanged law. The relevant final-judgment distinctions in the supplied holdings preserve completed adjudication and accrued effects; the historical descriptions of Plaut in the Fifth Circuit opinion do not replace this timeline's governing law.

The presented vested-right theory fails because the negotiated decree supplies no perpetual entitlement to unchanged future judicial enforcement. The broader assertion that prospective relief can never implicate due process is not adopted. Actual statutory adjudication and a meaningful opportunity to offer necessary material proof remain required. Miller (June 19, 2000) separately preserves supported application objections while rejecting the categorical Article III attack on the automatic stay. Its different provision supplies no present stay or exemption from the termination inquiry.

The State's acceptance of the decree and the prisoners' compromise of their demands do not estop invocation of the enacted termination route. Those incidents of consent are already within a statute expressly covering consent decrees. They do not establish a separate promise never to invoke subsequently enacted law, an independent accrued claim, or a waiver of the relevant statutory right. The result does not establish that every imaginable affirmative undertaking would be ineffective. No such different undertaking is established or needed to decide the presented estoppel theory. Rouse, Benjamin and Gavin also reject automatic survival of judicial duties through a contract label; their assumed-contract analyses do not establish an actual independent agreement here.

### Findings supporting retention and material proof

The March court correctly required an actual explanation of why each retained duty meets subsection (b)(3). A lengthy finding of constitutional injury does not alone show that every term of an old decree remedies that injury, and repeating statutory conclusions does not perform the missing connection. Conversely, the statute demands substantive findings rather than ritual phrases, mandatory separate headings, or redundant repetition where one reason genuinely supports several obligations. A remedial measure may exceed a minimum conduct rule when the necessary remedial findings support that measure.

The March record supports the finding of a missing remedial connection in the 1999 retention decision. It does not support immediate wholesale termination as the automatic consequence of that omission: the lower court had identified constitutional injuries and could assess the fit of existing obligations. Gavin and Hadix require the inquiry that was missing; Benjamin requires a real opportunity for material current proof when a court has refused necessary proof; Rouse rejects a speculative demand for a fresh inquiry after an actual assessment. These different predicates remain distinct. The fifty-hour-per-side limitation reported for the 1999 hearing, without identification of necessary excluded evidence and its consequence, does not establish Benjamin's predicate.

June performed the remedial inquiry. That fact neither requires duplicating it nor establishes that its results satisfy the statute. An appellate affirmance of March's demand for findings leaves the correctness of June's separate implementation outside this supplied review. Any still-proper application of subsection (b)(3) must assess actual current and ongoing injury and the fit of each duty on the pertinent record; no finding of actual March 2002 prison conditions is made here.

### Medical and psychiatric cross-appeal

For conditions and medical-care liability, Estelle's negligence boundary and Farmer's actual-awareness, unreasonable-disregard and reasonable-response rules govern. Awareness can be proved circumstantially, including through an obvious, persistent danger; neither hiring outside medical institutions nor issuing written policies is categorical immunity. Serious inadequacy alone is also not a finding that the responsible officials possessed the required culpability. For prospective protection, current danger and knowing nonresponse matter, and later knowledge does not establish earlier damages liability.

The reported adverse medical finding and the serious deficiencies recounted in June remain lower-court determinations. The supplied March judgment does not separately decide whether the medical evidence compelled a contrary finding. Its statement that the constitutional findings permit remedial assessment is not an appellate affirmance of every contested finding. This review therefore supplies no new statewide medical-liability finding and does not convert lack of a systemwide finding into a finding of constitutionally adequate care everywhere. Any properly preserved medical issue still requiring disposition remains subject to ordinary review on its record, under the governing standard. Independent reversal of June or an independent resolution of the trial evidence would exceed the selected legal review and require the underlying 1999 medical reasoning, relevant appellate submissions and a source establishing the cross-appeal's procedural disposition.

New or modified relief under subsection (a) is distinct from retention under subsection (b). The June court's invitation for proposals, including a proposed future end date, establishes neither entry of those proposals nor satisfaction of subsection (a). The present legal answer does not close the class action simply because old relief may terminate.
