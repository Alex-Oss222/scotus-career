## EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc., No. 99-1823

### Final components

**W1 — Employment-contract exclusion.** All eight apply the actual Circuit City holding: this employee's employment arbitration agreement lies outside the FAA, including its asserted commerce reach. The FAA therefore supplies no enforcement command or federal arbitration policy capable of restricting this EEOC suit or its remedies. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas retain their published Circuit City disagreement, but do not substitute it for governing law. The exclusion does not invalidate the agreement under every source of law or resolve any independent state-law basis for enforcement against Baker.

**W2 — Independent enforcement and individual remedies.** Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer join the full ground that Baker's private agreement selecting an arbitral forum does not bar victim-specific relief in the Commission's own ADA enforcement action. Section 12117(a) supplies the enumerated Title VII powers, procedures and remedies; section 706(f)(1) authorizes the agency action after the statutory process; section 706(g) authorizes appropriate individual equitable relief; section 1981a expressly includes the Commission among plaintiffs authorized to seek the specified damages. The agency's cause is statutory, not an assignment of Baker's claim. He did not agree for the agency, and it did not agree to arbitrate. Even an independently enforceable state-law forum promise by Baker cannot, merely through its enforceability against him, make the Commission a contracting party or eliminate its federal remedy categories.

First Options supplies actual assent, not by itself the entire statutory-remedy conclusion. General Telephone supports the independent public action, including individual relief, without treating the Commission as a mere representative. Gilmer preserves an agency enforcement role alongside individual arbitration; its actual securities-registration setting does not overturn Circuit City's exclusion or limit agency relief to broad injunctions. Section 706(g)'s appropriateness language and section 1981a's permission to recover preserve the court's judgment about justified relief on proved facts; they do not enact a categorical exclusion whenever a beneficiary made a forum promise. A private forum agreement is distinct from an actual adjudication, settlement, release or satisfaction. The six do not need to decide any hypothetical FAA-covered contract to resolve this live statutory and independent-state-contract argument.

Scalia and Thomas do **not** join W2. They agree that the Commission may bring its independent public action and is not itself compelled to arbitrate. But when it seeks relief for Baker's individual injury, they would give effect to a valid commitment restricting his own judicial recovery, even if the Commission remains free to pursue broader public relief. For them nonparty assent answers direct compulsion, not the different question whether a court should award individual relief inconsistent with the beneficiary's enforceable promise. The absence of FAA coverage removes one source of enforceability; it does not itself resolve whether state law independently makes the promise binding. They would retain that issue on remand, applying their derivative-remedy limitation only if a lawful independently enforceable agreement and its relevant scope are established. No such finding is presumed here.

**W3 — Actual relief remains conditional.** Every Justice distinguishes permission to seek relief from an entitlement to receive it. The alleged disability, qualification, unlawful discrimination, injury, causation and remedy predicates remain for ordinary adjudication. Toyota's actual disability rules apply at their proper scope; a seizure is not itself a finding satisfying them. Great-West concerns a different ERISA remedy provision and cannot delete the ADA's express damages authorization. Scalia and Thomas additionally preserve W2's contractual-effect limit.

Section 706(g)(1) requires the prescribed intentional-unlawful-practice finding and permits an injunction and appropriate affirmative action, including hiring, reinstatement, back pay and other equitable relief. Back pay cannot accrue earlier than two years before the charge and must be reduced by interim earnings or amounts earnable with reasonable diligence. Section 706(g)(2)(A) separately limits specified employment and back-pay orders where the employment action rested on another reason; section 706(g)(2)(B)'s separate same-action limitation has its own section 703(m) trigger and supplies no new ADA mixed-motive or causation rule here.

Section 1981a applies to its specified intentional violations, not disparate-impact damages. Its accommodation-damages limitation applies when the employer establishes the specified good-faith consultation and efforts to identify and make an equally effective reasonable accommodation without undue hardship. Punitive damages require malice or reckless indifference to federally protected rights and exclude governmental respondents. Compensatory relief under this section excludes back pay, interest on it and other section 706(g) relief. The statutory combined caps cover future pecuniary and listed nonpecuniary compensatory damages plus punitive damages: $50,000 for more than 14 and fewer than 101 employees; $100,000 for more than 100 and fewer than 201; $200,000 for more than 200 and fewer than 501; and $300,000 for more than 500, subject to the twenty-calendar-week condition in the current or preceding calendar year. No employer-size finding is made.

Courts must prevent duplicate satisfaction of the same injury. No completed arbitration, settlement, release or prior satisfaction is established here. Their concrete consequences may be resolved on a proper record; the Court does not decide every possible preclusion or settlement situation or assume that arbitration already occurred.

**W4 — Judgment and remedy.** The six W2 Justices reverse the Fourth Circuit's categorical prohibition of victim-specific relief and remand for ordinary liability and remedy proceedings. The Commission may pursue the statutory menu without a preliminary adjudication of Baker's independent state-law forum obligation. The Court awards no damages, reinstatement or other relief and makes no discrimination or disability finding.

Scalia and Thomas would vacate the categorical FAA-based restriction and remand for consideration of any properly available independent state-law enforceability ground and its consequences under their W2 view. They therefore agree that the existing FAA rationale cannot stand, but dissent from finally rejecting the contract-based limitation on individual recovery. They do not vote to affirm a restriction whose necessary enforceability premise no longer follows from the FAA. The remand supplies neither a new party claim nor a finding that an independent enforcement ground was established; ordinary preservation and applicable-law limits remain. The permitted public enforcement action continues under either disposition.

### Reconciliation audit

Historical Waffle House reversed and remanded with Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joining the Court; Thomas dissented with Scalia and Rehnquist. That historical author/joinder fact supplies evidence of grounds, not a present assignment.

**Historical departure — Scalia.** Historically he accepted the FAA's coverage of Baker's employment contract and joined the demand to preserve its enforcement through a limitation on individual agency relief. Actual Circuit City excludes this agreement, eliminating the sufficient federal-enforceability premise and preventing affirmance on that basis. He therefore supports vacatur and a bounded remand instead of historical affirmance. His separate derivative-remedy objection survives because it does not depend on treating the agency as a contracting party; an independently enforceable state-law promise could present it. That unresolved independent premise warrants remand rather than an invented enforcement finding.

**Historical departure — Thomas.** His historical dissent expressly rests both on valid FAA enforcement and on courts' obligation to consider the employee's own binding commitments in awarding individual relief. Actual Circuit City removes the first premise and changes his formal remedy to vacatur and remand. It does not justify abandoning his second, separately stated ground. His own statutory distinction between suit authorization and entitlement to a particular remedy remains the Justice-specific basis for withholding W2 and preserving the state-law issue.

**Correction of provisional commitments.** The independent model gave Scalia and Thomas the full actual-case W2 ground while reserving only a hypothetical FAA-covered agreement. Full verification shows that this is too narrow a reservation: historical Part II-A, 534 U.S. 304–308, independently rejects treating the agency's nonassent as dispositive of individual relief. The provisional reasoning identifies no changed statute or prior simulated holding displacing that ground. Great-West concerns the proper remedy source; First Options concerns assent; neither says that an agency's individual relief must ignore an enforceable beneficiary commitment. The final commitments retain the changed FAA premise without discarding the intact separate statutory objection. This is not mechanical restoration of the historical vote: neither Justice affirms the existing FAA-based judgment.

For the other six, history confirms W2. O'Connor and Kennedy historically supported FAA employment coverage yet joined the independent-action holding; the actual broader employment exclusion strengthens their W1 route without changing W2. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer likewise retain their independent statutory ground alongside the actual exclusion. No majority supports automatic relief or a claim that the employee's conduct can never matter.

A separate writing by Scalia and Thomas must state the agreement on the governing employment exclusion and vacatur of the FAA rationale, their surviving derivative-remedy objection, why nonparty assent is not the answer to it, and the bounded state-law remand. Whether one writes with the other's join or both write is for assembly. No historical authorship is assigned.

No new fact is imported from the historical opinion. Its account of a valid FAA agreement cannot become a finding of independent state-law enforceability. The historical references to party interrogatories, agency practices and predicted future settlement consequences are not adopted as new neutral premises. The independent commitments already examined the derivative-remedy and FAA-only-remand paths, so this comparison changes their supported legal selection without adding a new question or factual premise.
