## JPMorgan Chase Bank v. Traffic Stream (BVI) Infrastructure Ltd.

**Reach and disposition:** Reverse the Second Circuit's jurisdictional dismissal and remand for its remaining appellate work. The BVI corporation is a subject of the United Kingdom within §1332(a)(2), through its incorporation under the authority of that recognized sovereign. The legal classification does not require a jurisdictional-fact remand on the established sovereign relationship. The debt judgment remains subject to ordinary appellate adjudication.

**Rule and limits:** A corporation organized under BVI law is a subject of the United Kingdom for this statutory alienage category because the territory's governmental and corporate authority rests under United Kingdom sovereignty. The corporation's statutory allegiance is not determined by the immigration, passport or modern nationality classification of an individual resident. The BVI need not itself be an independently recognized foreign state. The actual sovereign relationship at commencement supplies jurisdiction; neither the forum clause nor a later nationality enactment supplies a retroactive cure. Other §1332 requirements retain their independent operation.

**Alternative-path assessment:** The narrower incorporation/nationality theory correctly insists on an actual statutory category and rejects jurisdiction by convenience. It fails here because it treats organization under the dependent territory's law as organization outside the recognized sovereign's authority, and effectively omits the alternative statutory word “subjects.” The broader reading is bounded by the established BVI relationship, not a presumption that every overseas entity qualifies. Diplomatic and commercial purposes confirm the construction; they are not a substitute source of federal jurisdiction.

## National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan

**Components:** (D) independently completed discriminatory or retaliatory acts; (H) a connected hostile environment extending into the filing window; (E) distinct equity, evidence and proceedings after review. The agreed 300-day period begins May 3, 1994, for the February 27, 1995 charge. The March 8 amendment concerns the March termination; the verdict form's February description does not adjudicate a different termination date.

**D — common eight-Justice rule:** Each discrete act starts its own charge period when it occurs. A timely later act does not make an earlier completed discrimination or retaliation claim timely merely because the acts are related, share a decisionmaker or reflect a common unlawful purpose. The current consequences of an old act are not a new act. The statute's ordinary period is 180 days; where qualifying state/local proceedings were initially instituted it is 300 days or thirty days after notice that those proceedings terminated, whichever is earlier. The earlier thirty-day alternative is not disputed here. Section 706(e)(2)'s distinct intentionally discriminatory seniority-system adoption, subjection and injury triggers are preserved, not generalized into a related-act exception. Section 706(g)'s two-year backpay limit bounds relief on a timely practice and does not revive stale discrete practices.

**H — five Associates' connected-practice rule:** A hostile environment is the unlawful employment practice constituted by the connected discriminatory conditions. When an act contributing to that same environment occurs within the filing period, the charge may encompass the whole practice, including its earlier constituent acts. An individual incident need not independently satisfy the severe-or-pervasive threshold. Earlier actionability or the employee's recognition of the same environment does not by itself sever the continuing practice. A later unrelated act, a different environment, or an intervening change ending the earlier practice cannot revive an old environment. Relationship is an application inquiry under the actual record, informed by the kind and persistence of conduct, participants and any material interruption; these are contextual considerations, not an exclusive compulsory checklist. Harris and Oncale supply the substantive collective wrong and protected-status limits. This rule does not turn discrete personnel decisions into the same compensable practice merely because they provide evidence of hostility.

**H — O'Connor, Scalia and Kennedy's alternative:** An actionable, known hostile environment cannot indefinitely carry all earlier completed injury into a new compensation period solely through another related incident. A charge must be timely as to the violation for which relief is sought; later unlawful conditions remain actionable and older conduct may establish their character, severity or discriminatory cause, but the employee cannot recover for old completed violations merely by continuing to work in a similar environment. Harris's collective proof does not itself exempt completed claims from §706(e). These three would reverse the Ninth Circuit's broader limitations rule for environment recovery as well as discrete acts, while remanding independent evidentiary and instructional objections. They reject Amtrak's stronger suggestion that knowledge of earlier discrimination extinguishes a later new violation.

**E — common eight-Justice position:** Zipes preserves separately supported waiver, equitable tolling and estoppel; similarity of events or an internal grievance alone supplies none. Traditional prejudicial-delay relief requires proof of unreasonable delay and resulting prejudice, not a presumed bar from an old internal complaint. Any claim of such relief is assessed on its own facts and cannot import a general harassment prevention/nonuse defense that current law has not adopted. Old acts may be relevant evidence even when they cannot support independent recovery. Neither the existence of a timely charge nor admissible background evidence proves liability, continuity, attribution or loss.

## United States v. Bollin — Gormley's IRA restraint

**Disposition:** Reverse the Fourth Circuit's approval of §853(e) authority for continued restraint of this untainted IRA and remand the refusal to release funds for lawful further proceedings. Section 853(e) does not authorize restraint of potential substitute property merely because it might later be forfeited under §853(p). The government had obtained no order substituting and forfeiting the IRA; its continuing §853(e) restraint cannot be sustained by treating the existing money judgment as that missing order.

**Sufficient statutory ground:** Section 853(e) expressly preserves property described in subsection (a). Subsection (p) separately requires forfeiture of other defendant property, up to the qualifying value, when the defendant's act or omission makes subsection-(a) property unavailable by one of five conditions: inability to locate despite due diligence; transfer, sale or deposit with a third party; placement outside the court's jurisdiction; substantial reduction in value; or commingling not divisible without difficulty. The future mandatory substitution remedy does not convert the substitute asset into subsection-(a) property for interim restraint. Section (g) protects property actually ordered forfeited after such an order; the required IRA order is absent. Section (o)'s liberal construction permits faithful implementation of these grants, not omission of their limits.

**Alternative-path test:** The strongest affirmance theory combines mandatory substitute forfeiture, the risk of dissipation, §853(o), and Billman's RICO analogy. The statutory structure defeats that theory here: Congress supplied preservation of specified property in (e), final substitution in (p), and post-order protection in (g). The object Congress specified in (e) cannot be enlarged by a prediction that a different property category will eventually meet (p). The strongest objection to reversal is the possibility that a defendant can dissipate assets before the government obtains substitution. That consequence does not supply an omitted grant, and the Court leaves the government to powers actually conferred and predicates actually met.

**Other issues and remedy:** Georgia's IRA garnishment protection cannot defeat authorized federal forfeiture, but supremacy supplies no independent federal restraint power. The present result rests on §853(e), so the asserted appellate counsel entitlement need not be reached. The §982(b)(2) intermediary restriction was not raised below and requires facts if substitute forfeiture is later sought: merely handling without retaining the laundered property excludes substitution unless the offense involved three or more transactions totaling at least $100,000 in twelve months. That distinct restriction is not adjudicated on assumed facts. The remand removes the erroneous §853(e) basis and requires a lawful disposition of the release motion; any government request for substitution or independent postconviction protection must establish its own source and conditions. No constitutional exemption or automatic entitlement to exactly $80,000 is created. All eight can join this complete statutory ground and confined remand.

## United States v. Farmer

**Disposition:** Affirm the Fourth Circuit's vacatur of the summary hearing denial and remand for a prompt, focused adversary hearing. Its collateral-order review is proper for the asserted trial-counsel opportunity that would be lost if review awaited completion of trial. This is a post-seizure hearing decision, not a rule forbidding initial personal-property seizure or allowing expenditure of proved criminal proceeds.

**Common operative conclusion:** A criminal defendant who makes a substantial supported showing that seized funds may be untainted and that their restraint prevents retaining trial counsel is entitled, on this record of continuing civil seizure without an adjudicated forfeiture, to a prompt meaningful opportunity to contest taint and financial need. Farmer's sworn submissions, the magistrate's effective-indigency determination and the particular affidavit identifying legitimate merchandise meet the hearing threshold. Requiring final proof at the threshold would withhold the very procedure needed to test the claim. The government disputes remaining resources and taint; its concession permits a hearing upon sufficient need, rather than opposing all hearings.

**Hearing and relief:** Affirm the lower court's confined allocation for the requested release: Farmer bears the preponderance burden of establishing that the particular assets needed for counsel are untainted and seized without probable cause for continued forfeiture custody, and that he needs those funds to retain counsel. The government may contest other resources and present probable cause of taint. This is the lower court's adopted release standard, now affirmed for this counsel-access claim, not a preexisting Supreme Court test or a demand that the government prove the criminal charges at the hearing. The court must evaluate the actual disputed property and available resources; a seizure warrant is not conclusive at that adversarial stage. If the showing succeeds, the court must permit access to the legitimate funds required for counsel unless an independently established lawful basis prevents release; if it fails, this counsel claim supplies no release. The ruling does not shift the ultimate forfeiture burden, adjudicate all assets, or decide living-expense or interest relief outside the reviewed question.
