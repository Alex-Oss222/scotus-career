## The state proposition and remedy that the four reversing Justices join

The trigger is a citizen's otherwise comparable qualification for New York's retained former-resident presidential electorate, together with complete exclusion from presidential participation because the citizen moved to Puerto Rico. The trigger is neither territorial residence by itself nor a general inability to vote for President without prior membership in the state electorate. Within that preserved former-resident mechanism, complete exclusion severely burdens participation; the exclusion must be narrowly drawn to advance a compelling electoral interest. The stated domestic-membership and incremental-accommodation reasons do not adequately justify the complete denial in this application. Puerto Rico's local and resident-commissioner elections do not provide the missing presidential opportunity.

The equality inquiry does not make every emigrant permanently eligible in every former State. Comparable prior qualification, the State's existing mechanism and the absence of another presidential ballot do real limiting work. Burdick's complete-system inquiry remains controlling; the rule does not replace calibrated review with strict scrutiny of every residence or absentee requirement. The separate Article II allocation remains unchanged.

The four support reversal of dismissal of the state equality claim and remand for prospective relief against the responsible New York election official. The legal direction must prohibit using Puerto Rico residence, abode or domicile, registration, or voting in local elections as the reason to deny an otherwise qualified applicant access to this presidential absentee mechanism. It must also disapply, in this application, the departure-from-the-United-States formulation insofar as that formulation would independently exclude a citizen whose last relevant state domicile was New York before moving to Puerto Rico. A decree that removed only the outside-residence phrase and the registration/voting condition could leave the domestic-abode/domicile exclusion or departure-linked domicile requirement doing the same work. The complete lower opinion, paragraphs 3–6 and note 1, establishes those distinctions.

This is as-applied nonenforcement and equal access in the existing presidential program, not wholesale textual deletion from every federal-election provision. The lower court determines ordinary eligibility facts and implements the decree using the otherwise valid former-residence, age, registration and authentication requirements. It may enforce a restriction against a second presidential vote; it may not transform Puerto Rico's local vote into that second presidential vote. Federal minimum obligations remain operative. New York cannot equalize by withholding ballots that UOCAVA requires for the covered overseas voters. No unqualified ballot entitlement follows before the ordinary qualification findings.

This clarification implements, rather than changes, the reversing camp's existing equality-remedy commitment. It removes no federal condition by implication and decides no congressional-ballot entitlement outside the reviewed presidential claim.

## Federal reach and the judgment below

The lower opinion's opening description and paragraph 6 identify two forms of requested relief: access to the New York ballot and declarations that the federal and state statutes violate the asserted constitutional rights. Paragraphs 13–18 separately sustain the federal classification. The frozen packet and reconciliation accordingly treat R1 and R2 as distinct components. UOCAVA's floor status shows that a state remedy need not violate a federal prohibition. It does not show that the federal classification is constitutionally valid, that its challenge was abandoned, or that a declaration against that classification is already outside a live controversy.

The strongest argument for the proposed narrowing is that an enforceable state decree gives Romeu the same practical presidential voting opportunity as the protected overseas voter, leaving no additional ballot relief for a federal declaration to supply. That argument can matter to a properly supported determination of remaining relief or jurisdiction. On the present handoff, however, complete state relief is a proposed disposition with ordinary eligibility and decree implementation still ahead. The record does not establish a concession limiting all requested relief to an issued ballot, an independently adopted jurisdictional determination, or elimination of every concrete dispute with the federal defendants. Conversely, the separate prayer for a declaration does not by itself guarantee perpetual Article III jurisdiction after complete relief. Both automatic conclusions would exceed the record.

The supported present course is therefore to retain R1 as a component requiring an express final disposition. The existing Associate commitments remain: Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg would reverse its merits dismissal and remand for prospective equality relief; O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer would affirm its dismissal on the membership/accommodation ground. A statement that federal law is only a minimum is not either disposition.

## Independent theories and what remains on remand

R3 remains the narrow rejection already frozen: no freestanding permanent entitlement to a former State's franchise, no general Article IV entitlement to carry that franchise to a new residence, no independent travel-based carryover right, and no additional general due-process entitlement on these facts. Saenz's equal citizenship at a new state residence does not itself establish a retained New York electorate. The Fifth Amendment equality component remains R1 and must not be grouped with the rejected independent due-process theory.

All eight Associates maintain that R3 position. The proposed state remedy supports a distinct equality ground but supplies no changed premise on the existence or scope of those independent entitlements. This review therefore does not certify replacing their adjudication with a substantive reservation. Their rejection neither defeats the successful equality theory for the reversing Justices nor supplies the sustaining Justices with an answer to selective-franchise equality without their separate membership argument.

Assembly should distinguish theories from separable judgments. Rejection of an alternative theory does not require inventing a separately numbered claim or an additional partial affirmance if the pleading and judgment do not contain one. The opinion can expressly reject that proposed independent source of entitlement while the operative judgment identifies the actual federal and state dismissals it affirms or reverses. The approved handoff supplies no basis for saying an existing adverse federal merits judgment has vanished merely because another theory succeeds.

Ordinary application on remand is different from a reserved legal question. Prior qualification, authentication and compliance with lawful prospective procedures are factual or implementation work under the stated rule. They are not a reason to leave the constitutional status of the categorical territorial exclusion undecided. Broader legislation concerning a different electorate remains outside the relief reviewed; it is not an unresolved component of this case.
