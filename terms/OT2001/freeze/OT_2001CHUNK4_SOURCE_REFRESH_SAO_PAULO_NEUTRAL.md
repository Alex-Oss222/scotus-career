### Issue 1: An informed observer and mistakenly attributed advocacy

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Liteky v. United States supplies a single objective appearance standard: an informed, detached observer considers relevant conduct and circumstances together. Judicial origin is neither a categorical bar nor an added exceptional-intensity requirement; outside origin is neither necessary nor sufficient. Actual bias need not be proved.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** The operative standard asks whether the full supported circumstances reasonably call impartiality into question. It protects ordinary adjudicative conduct without shielding actual partiality and preserves §455(a)’s independence from §455(b).
* **Other Governing Law:** Section 455(a) requires disqualification when impartiality might reasonably be questioned. Section 455(b)’s separate grounds are not substitutes for the appearance inquiry.
* **Material Other Authority:** Liljeberg v. Health Services Acquisition Corp. supports an objectively informed inquiry and preserves separate remedial considerations for an established violation. It does not require observers to disregard facts correcting an apparent association.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The existing rule already decides the proper observer and information set. Application to a mistaken, unauthorized organizational attribution requires no change to the standard.
* **Open Point:** Whether the full circumstances, including continuing executive-committee membership alongside mistaken presidential attribution and no preparation or approval, provide an objectively reasonable basis to doubt impartiality under the settled rule.

## C. Decision-material positions and corrected information set

The circuit treated its related-case recusal ruling as controlling. Defendants rely on an association with advocacy concerning similar defendants and issues; Sao Paulo relies on the corrected attribution and absence of actual participation in preparing or approving the brief. The correction does not eliminate all organizational connection: executive-committee membership is an established contemporaneous role that an informed observer must know. It is equally impermissible to convert that role into a finding that the judge wrote, approved, authorized, supervised or knowingly adopted this submission when the supplied factual extract expressly records nonparticipation and vague awareness.

The source thus defeats two categorical shortcuts. The judge had not ceased every organizational role by the time of filing, and membership in the executive committee does not itself establish his personal preparation or approval of the brief. Defendants need not prove subjective hostility to present an appearance claim. Sao Paulo need not treat a mistaken name on a motion as genuine authorship. The observer considers all supported circumstances together under Liteky and Microsoft, rather than either the mistaken filing alone or the judge's personal assurance of fairness alone.

## D. Lawful paths and precise limits

Reversal could conclude that the erroneous presidential attribution, continuing committee membership, lack of preparation or approval, and vague awareness, evaluated together, do not reasonably call impartiality into question. It must address the real continuing role rather than rest on a nonexistent total separation from the association. Affirmance could conclude that the contemporaneous leadership association, evaluated with those same limitations, nevertheless creates an objectively reasonable appearance concern. It must explain why that concern survives the actual nonparticipation and correction rather than impute unsupported advocacy.
