# Earls: form and propositions before assignment

One signed lead opinion is warranted by this argued constitutional merits decision and its substantive application of Vernonia to the challenged nonathletic population. Part I states the search/no-waiver rule and contextual special-needs inquiry, rejecting categorical epidemic, safety-sensitive-duty and exhaustion prerequisites. Part II holds the nonathletic expansion unreasonable on this record even under the written safeguards. Part III treats supported implementation disputes correctly while affirming on the independently sufficient substantive ground and preserving ordinary prospective relief. The signed form and these propositions are fixed before author counts.

Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter and Ginsburg affirm on the complete common rationale. Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Breyer would vacate and remand: they approve the defined protective program on its described safeguards, reject categorical population prerequisites, and preserve supported material implementation disputes. They agree with Part I's common rules but not Parts II and III's substantive disposition. Their disagreement does not entail unconditional reinstatement of summary judgment. The no-waiver and contextual propositions are stated at their shared scope, without treating every reasonableness judgment as unanimous.

The partial joins and distinct formal dispositions require a topology table. The majority's adequate alternative ground means confidentiality disputes need not delay its judgment; no independent disclosure cause or automatic trial is created. Ginsburg's contextual application can be fully represented by the Court opinion; no redundant concurrence is required. Breyer's additional demand-reduction and counseling rationale warrants separate publication alongside his support for the vacatur position. No author is selected here.
