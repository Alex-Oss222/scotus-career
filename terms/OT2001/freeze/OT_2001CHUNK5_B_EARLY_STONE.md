## 71. Alabama v. Shelton

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm the judgment invalidating the suspended imprisonment while leaving the conviction and otherwise lawful noncustodial consequences intact.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** The uncounseled conviction may not be used to activate this jail term. Remand for any necessary state-law adjustment consistent with that limit. Do not order acquittal, damages, or invalidation of a distinct lawful probation disposition not adjudicated here.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Counsel at the adjudication that authorizes jail

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the suspended-imprisonment question on the established absence of valid waiver.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether trial counsel is required when the original conviction authorizes a jail term whose execution is deferred.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply the existing counsel-and-imprisonment principle and propose its specific suspended-sentence application; do not extend Nichols beyond its valid fine-only setting.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A State may not impose a suspended jail term for an offense tried without constitutionally adequate counsel or a valid waiver, because activation executes punishment for that original offense. Counsel at an ordinary revocation hearing cannot cure the absence of counsel when guilt and the imprisonment authorization were adjudicated.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The rule covers a sentence directly authorizing imprisonment, including deferred execution. It does not require appointment for every fine-only prosecution or decide a new charge tried with counsel.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Argersinger and Scott tie appointed counsel to imprisonment for the offense; Nichols preserves a valid fine-only conviction without answering whether the original court may impose jail subject to deferred execution. Stone extends the imprisonment rule to that direct authorization. The decisive point is the source of the eventual incarceration: activation executes the assault sentence, not a new sentence for independently tried misconduct. Counsel at revocation cannot reconstruct lost investigation, advice and trial choices concerning guilt. This categorical boundary follows the Sixth Amendment's allocation of assistance at the critical adjudication, rather than balancing each defendant's apparent guilt against administrative expense. The State remains free to select a lawful noncustodial sentence or provide counsel. Stand-alone probation and a genuinely new counseled prosecution are different arrangements not before the Court.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** Independent probation without a suspended term is not the sentencing arrangement presented. A genuine new counseled trial before imprisonment would require a different procedural record.

## 72. Brown v. Gilmore +

# SECTION II: STONE POSITION SUPPLEMENT

## A. STATUS

* **Approval Status:** Approved.
* **Current-Through Baseline:** Controlling law through June 28, 2001. Apply any subsequently completed decision effective before this matter is decided.
* **Conditions:** The stated jurisdictional, preservation and record limits apply. No alternative voting instruction is supplied.

## B. JUDGMENT AND REMEDY

* **Stone's Controlled Judgment or Disposition:** Affirm rejection of the facial Establishment Clause challenge under Agostini, Chaudhuri and the independent coercion rules.
* **Stone's Remedy and Remand Position:** Leave the neutral minute of silence in force. Do not authorize teacher-led prayer or substantial religious pressure. No remedial monitoring program is ordered absent a proved implementation violation.

## C. ISSUE POSITIONS

### Issue 1: Private activity within a nondevotional interval

* **Threshold and Merits Reach:** Reach the facial claim on the statute and implementation record. Do not decide a hypothetical classroom violation.
* **Question Stone Resolves:** Whether requiring silence while leaving the individual's activity private constitutes an official religious exercise.
* **Stone's Legal Position:** Apply Chaudhuri and the government-attribution and coercion distinctions in Agostini, Lee and Santa Fe.
* **Stone's Proposed Holding:** A daily public-school minute requiring silence but no religious act, expression, or disclosure, with genuine freedom to pray, reflect, or do neither, does not establish religion. This statute's permission of private prayer and some legislators' religious motives do not convert its neutral required conduct into official worship.
* **Reach Within the Question:** The holding covers the facial requirement and neutral implementation described here. It does not protect teacher-directed devotion, religious evaluation of students, or substantial pressure to pray.
* **Essential Reasoning and Record Application:** Agostini's government-function inquiry, Chaudhuri's treatment of silence and Good News Club's distinction between private expression and governmental adoption control the analysis. Stone applies them without adding an independent Lemon-purpose veto that current doctrine no longer supplies. The statute requires silence and genuine private choice, not prayer, religious disclosure or participation in an official exercise. Religious statements by some sponsors remain evidence of actual purpose and operation, but on this facial record they do not turn the required conduct into worship. Function and attribution carry decisive weight, with Lee and Santa Fe independently preserving the prohibition on official devotion and substantial school-created religious pressure. This limited holding protects silent nonreligious activity as fully as silent prayer; it authorizes no teacher direction of either belief or disbelief.
* **Boundary or Reserved Question:** A particular coercive classroom practice requires facts not established in this facial challenge.
