## Changed premise and retained result

All eight Associates retain the provisional judgment to affirm the denial of relief on the supplied First Amendment question, confined to the identified credit-derived lists. Their complete ground is now the actual combination of permissible purpose, consumer direction, a conditional but binding firm offer, information limits, agency opt-out duties and separate user safeguards. This is not an automatic result from the earlier votes.

Two earlier simplifications cannot perform the constitutional work. First, a permitted firm-offer list may disclose a selected financial characteristic to an outside recipient without affirmative consumer authorization. The privacy intrusion is not absent merely because the recipient makes a credit or insurance offer. Second, not every authorization route requires a writing: section 604(a)(2)'s written-instructions permission is distinct from section 604(c)(1)(A)'s authorization to provide the report to that person. The latter does not itself say written, but it is limited to the credit or insurance setting it governs. It is not a general oral-approval alternative for unrelated marketing.

The corrected comparison therefore does not rest on a claim that all lawful releases are affirmatively chosen, that all firm offers are unconditional, that only names and addresses can ever be provided, or that actual compliance is established. The source completion changes the supporting explanation and required scope of each join. It does not change any provisional judgment vote.

## Statutory premises that perform legal work

### Consumer direction and transaction purpose remain separate

Section 604(a), subject to subsection (c), permits furnishing in its enumerated circumstances and no others. Under subsection (a)(2), furnishing must conform to the particular consumer's written instructions. Silence and failure to opt out are not such instructions. The statute's actual words, rather than a newly invented per-use writing or duration rule, define the permission.

Under subsection (a)(3)(A), the reporting agency must have reason to believe that the recipient intends to use the information in a credit transaction involving the consumer and involving extension of credit to, or review or collection of an account of, that consumer. Subsection (a)(3)(C) separately concerns underwriting insurance involving the consumer. The business-need route in subsection (a)(3)(F) requires a transaction initiated by the consumer or account review to determine continued compliance with its terms; an unrelated marketer's commercial interest alone is not that permission. Section 603(m) separately excludes review of an existing account or insurance policy, or collection of an existing account, by its holder from the defined unsolicited-transaction category. That definition does not authorize every new marketing use by an existing business contact.

For otherwise covered credit or insurance transactions not initiated by the consumer, section 604(c)(1)(A) permits the consumer to authorize the agency to provide the report to that person. The report, recipient and credit/insurance context matter; this clause cannot be merged with the different written-instructions provision or generalized beyond its setting.

These routes support the consumer's ability to choose a release and the use of information within defined financial relationships. They do not establish a universal proprietary right in information, a constitutional rule requiring written approval for all information commerce, or a finding that Trans Union obtained an authorization.

### Firm-offer prescreening is an actual exception to affirmative authorization

Section 604(c)(1)(B) requires all three conditions: the transaction must consist of a firm offer of credit or insurance; the reporting agency must have complied with subsection (e); and no subsection (e) election excluding the consumer's name and address may be in effect. The absence of an election is one element of this particular route, not a general permission for consumer-report marketing.

Under section 603(l), an offer must be honored if the consumer meets the specific consumer-report criteria used to select that consumer. Further conditions are permitted, but bounded. Application information may establish satisfaction of specific creditworthiness or insurability criteria set before selection and for deciding whether to extend the offered credit or insurance. Verification may establish that the consumer continues to meet the selection criteria through a report, application or other relevant creditworthiness or insurability information, or may verify application information to determine satisfaction of the applicable criteria. A collateral condition must have been established before selection and disclosed in the offer.

This is neither unconditional entitlement to a loan nor a merely discretionary invitation to consider unrelated products. No Justice adds a minimum loan amount, minimum economic value, guaranteed favorable interest rate or other term absent from the supplied text. Conditional performance is still a statutory limit on the offeror; the Court must not replace it with a promise that every selected consumer actually receives credit or insurance.

The firm-offer route can convey the same inference about a selected financial trait that makes the challenged lists valuable. Its distinction is the accompanying financial transaction and protective limits, not an assertion that prescreening communicates nothing private.

### The information limits reduce disclosure without eliminating its meaning

Under section 604(c)(2), a recipient through the firm-offer route may receive the consumer's name and address, a nonunique identifier used solely to verify identity, and other information that does not identify the consumer's relationship or experience with a particular creditor or other entity. The third category is broader than names and addresses; it can include information bearing on financial characteristics without identifying a particular relationship. The comparison must acknowledge that residual disclosure rather than falsely describe a two-field-only system.

Section 604(c)(3) separately bars furnishing records of unsolicited credit or insurance inquiries, subject to section 609(a)(5)'s disclosure to the consumer. That exception concerns the preceding year's inquiries identifying the consumer, on request and subject to the proper-identification requirement. It is not a permission to give inquiry histories to unrelated marketers.

These provisions do not authorize unrestricted furnishing of a credit file under the firm-offer label. Their constitutional relevance is the boundary they set on lawful releases. Their existence is not proof that a particular seller or buyer obeyed them.

### Opt-out is effective under specified timing and duration rules

Section 604(e) permits a consumer to notify the agency through its notification system or submit its signed election form. The election becomes effective five business days after notice and applies to agency affiliates. A system-only election lasts two years beginning five business days after notice unless cancelled sooner; a signed-form election continues until the consumer cancels through the notification system. An election ceases to be effective after the consumer gives that cancellation notice.

After notification through the system, the agency must explain that the election is limited to two years without a signed form. If the consumer requests the form during that notification, the agency must supply it within five business days. The system must include a toll-free telephone number and permit notification with appropriate identification. At least annual publication in a general-circulation publication for the service area must explain possible consumer-file use and supply the address and toll-free exclusion number; the original deadline was 365 days after September 30, 1996. A compliant system and publication maintained for affiliates satisfies that obligation for them, and nationwide reporting agencies must maintain their notification system jointly.

The five-business-day interval permits release before an election takes effect, and a system-only election can expire. General publication does not establish that every consumer reads or understands it. None of these limits may be converted into perpetual exclusion after a telephone request, immediate blocking, affirmative authorization through silence, or a finding of effective public awareness. Nevertheless, the duties furnish a defined means to prevent future prescreened releases, including a continuing signed-form election, rather than unlimited discretion to disregard consumer choices.

### The agency's conditions and the user's duties are not interchangeable

Section 604(f) permits use or acquisition only for an authorized furnishing purpose, with a general or specific purpose certification by the prospective user under section 607. A permitted initial transfer does not create unrestricted authority to use the information for other purposes.

For every written solicitation based on the firm-offer route, section 615(d) requires a clear and conspicuous statement that report information was used; that the consumer met the selection criteria; that, if applicable, extension may be denied if the consumer does not meet the selection or applicable creditworthiness/insurability criteria or furnish required collateral; that the consumer can prohibit this unsolicited-transaction use; and that the consumer can do so through the notification system. The statement must give that system's address and toll-free telephone number. The offeror must retain selection criteria, extension criteria and collateral requirements for three years from the offer. Federal and state authority against unfair or deceptive practices remains.

The written-solicitation disclosure is a separate user duty, not a guarantee of individualized notice before the reporting agency's first disclosure. Its written-solicitation trigger is not enlarged to every oral communication. Criteria retention is a distinct requirement; it does not establish the offer's actual commercial value or compliance with its conditions. Section 615(c)'s defense requires the defendant to prove by a preponderance that reasonable compliance procedures were maintained at the time of the alleged violation. It limits liability under that section; it does not erase the duties or excuse an unauthorized purpose under section 604.

### Statutory classification remains at its actual procedural scope

Section 603(d) includes information bearing on the enumerated credit or personal characteristics when used, expected to be used, or collected in whole or part as an eligibility factor for the specified credit, insurance, employment or section 604 purposes. The collection-purpose trigger is distinct from the later buyer's marketing use. No statutory classification ruling is reopened here.

The copied definition also preserves exclusions for the reporting person's own transactions or experiences, communications of that information among affiliates, and other affiliate communications after clear and conspicuous disclosure and an opportunity before the first communication to prohibit it. It separately excludes the described credit-card authorization, a credit decision communicated at a third party's request when the consumer is given the decisionmaker's name and address and the required disclosures, and the communication defined in subsection (o). These exclusions do not establish that the identified Trans Union lists were public information or that unrelated external sale is equivalent to every affiliated or transaction-specific communication. The present holding does not adjudicate the constitutionality of those separate categories or infer missing facts about their operation.

## Adversarial comparison and selected rule

### T1 — coverage and governing scrutiny

**Selected path.** Apply independent ordinary commercial-speech scrutiny to the marketing use of these credit-derived lists. List membership communicates the selected financial characteristic even where the immediate fields are names and addresses. U.S. West supplies the review trigger despite lawful possession and alternative marketing opportunities. Profit by itself does not classify every communication as commercial. Central Hudson and Fox retain substantial interest, direct material advancement and reasonable proportional fit; there is no least-restrictive-means mandate.

**Competing path and strongest objection.** Treating the activity solely as information commerce would avoid speech review; applying full protection categorically to every sale would reject ordinary scrutiny. The first conflicts with U.S. West's unanimous coverage holding. The second exceeds the supplied commercial-targeting question and does not follow from the fact that the list conveys information. Public-concern publication and every sale of names remain outside the ruling. No Justice selects either categorical alternative.

### T2 — material advancement despite genuinely permitted private disclosure

**Selected path.** Protecting confidential financial information against repurposing outside defined consumer-directed or restricted financial uses is substantial. The challenged limitation materially advances that interest by preventing the identified external sale for unrelated targeting. Its causal connection does not depend on estimating how many recipients purchase an advertised product: the disclosure itself conveys the private selection information.

That account must be limited by what the law actually permits. Congress allows some unrequested external disclosure, including selection inferences and the residual information allowed under section 604(c)(2)(C), for the conditional firm-offer transaction. The statute therefore does not establish a policy of eliminating every unconsented disclosure. It draws a bounded financial-use permission accompanied by opt-out, information and user-use limits. Consumer direction and existing-account uses have different predicates. Preventing other repurposing still protects confidentiality in a material way; the exception does not authorize the same unrestricted unrelated use in another favored industry.

**Competing path and strongest objection.** The most substantial invalidity argument is that firm-offer lists and unrelated-marketing lists can disclose the same private trait. No advance individualized notice, unconditional extension of credit or proven meaningful offer value is required by the cited provisions. If the government's entire interest were simply to prevent disclosure of a trait to anyone, this exception would undermine that formulation. The selected conclusion therefore does not adopt that formulation. It protects confidentiality within a limited-purpose information system, while acknowledging and testing the specific accommodation for regulated financial offers. Discovery Network and Greater New Orleans require this comparison; they do not make every partial protection unconstitutional.

The provisions' existence alone does not prove actual compliance or measurable success. The packet's supported communication and repurposing premises supply the direct relationship for this challenge. No new empirical finding is made, and no claim about a particular unlawful prescreening operation is decided. Evidence that a formally confined exception actually permitted materially equivalent unrestricted releases could require a different application; the present record does not establish that premise.

### T3 — proportional fit and the proposed opt-out alternative

**Selected path.** The combined structure reasonably fits the confined interest. Written consumer instructions can authorize releases at their actual scope; the credit/insurance recipient-specific authorization route is separately available in its own field. Unsolicited firm offers without affirmative authorization are restricted by predetermined or verifiable criteria, disclosed preexisting collateral conditions, limited released information, authorized-purpose certification, an agency opt-out system with operative elections, written-solicitation notices and retained criteria. These are different constraints with different decisionmakers and triggers. They define the permissible comparison without being collapsed into a general consent exception.

The strongest less burdensome alternative would extend a comparable opt-out and information-limitation regime to unrelated marketing. The Court cannot dismiss this as mere absence of all-sources-at-once regulation, because the statute itself demonstrates that opt-out can protect some privacy interests. Nor can U.S. West's oral/written/electronic flexibility be transferred to section 604(a)(2): obtaining written instructions for this unrelated use is a real additional burden. The selected answer is that Fox requires a proportionate fit, not proof that no alternative could work. The lawful nonauthorizing route is tied to an actual credit or insurance offer that must be honored when its bounded criteria are met, a restricted financial purpose, and enforceable limits on information and subsequent use. Extending it to unrelated products would remove that transaction boundary and broaden the recipients and uses for which confidential credit-derived classifications could be sold without consumer direction. The original credit-file purpose and the actual external-sale premise make requiring the consumer's instructions for that expansion proportionate on the supplied record.

This does not depend on concluding that every permitted offer is beneficial, that opt-out generally fails, or that a writing is constitutionally necessary in every setting. The first prescreening release may precede individual solicitation notice; an election has a five-business-day lead time; a system-only election lasts two years; and permitted further information can reveal more than an address. Those are real qualifications, not defects omitted from the comparison. The package permits a confined financial accommodation while limiting further unwanted disclosure. Alternative marketing sources mitigate the burden but do not remove it or answer the constitutional question by themselves.

**Competing path and strongest objection.** Stevens and Kennedy's U.S. West dissent supports invalidation where privacy sensitivity merely substitutes for explaining a selected use distinction or a more demanding approval mechanism. Their objection would prevail here if the Court relied only on the word confidential, presumed consumer refusal, falsely equated all exceptions with affirmative consent, or treated all firm offers as unconditional grants. The completed statutory comparison avoids those premises. The distinction is between the authorized or specifically constrained financial routes and unrelated external repurposing, with the entire package doing work. A remand to invent additional findings is not selected because the supplied question and record permit this confined constitutional determination; missing evidence is not manufactured in the statute's name.

### T4 — relief and questions not reached

Affirm the denial of relief on the identified First Amendment claim and sustain the order at that application. No final statutory-compliance determination, damages, authorization, redesigned consent process or newly preserved claim follows. Statutory and arbitrary-and-capricious issues outside review retain their proper status. A different company's consent decree does not supply a new constitutional exemption or an adjudicated equal-treatment claim. No opinion declares every FCRA application valid or creates a general constitutional data-ownership rule.

## Provisional proposition-level agreement

All eight Associates directly support the following propositions at their stated level of generality:

1. The identified marketing use of credit-derived lists receives independent ordinary commercial-speech review; the corporate speaker, lawful possession and other marketing channels do not eliminate that review.
2. Confidentiality against repurposing outside the consumer-directed or specifically constrained financial uses is substantial, and preventing the challenged unrelated external sale directly and materially advances that interest. The rule does not prevent every private disclosure, and the firm-offer exception can itself communicate a financial trait.
3. On the supplied record, the actual package of purpose restrictions, chosen disclosure routes, conditional firm-offer obligations, information and inquiry-history limits, agency opt-out duties, authorized-use certification and separate written-solicitation/retention requirements reasonably fits the confined interest. An extension of opt-out to unrelated marketing is not constitutionally compelled merely because it is conceivable and less burdensome. No one holds opt-out generally invalid or written authorization universally necessary.
4. The First Amendment judgment should be affirmed at this as-applied scope, without adjudicating additional statutory categories, actual compliance or matters outside review.

Thomas's acceptance of this sufficient application remains distinct from a general endorsement of the ordinary tier as the complete constitutional account. Stevens and Kennedy's U.S. West dissent remains intact: their renewed joins depend on the complete different legal and disclosure setting here. Agreement on a judgment must not be used to delete any of these express grounds or qualifications.

## Precedent treatment, source limits and handoff

U.S. West remains the independent-review and supported-confidentiality precedent, with its flexible FCC approval mechanism and narrow new-category facts preserved. This decision does not copy that mechanism into the FCRA. Discovery Network and Greater New Orleans require an actual comparison of the permitted and restricted communications; the conclusion here distinguishes the supported financial-use package without weakening that requirement. Lorillard preserves independent advancement and fit questions. Thompson remains a reasonable-fit decision about product-promotion conditions; it assumed advancement and supplies neither strict scrutiny nor categorical invalidity of information restrictions. Fox continues to require proportionality rather than the least restrictive imaginable alternative. Condon supplies no independent answer to the speech question.

The exact statutory record and preparation receipt supply the legal conditions; they do not supply additional lower-court findings, consumer behavior, compliance, a particular offer's value or a completed transaction. The governing reason-to-believe and certification predicates are preserved rather than treated as actual findings in Trans Union's favor or against it. The separate source-text limitation concerning subsection (o) performs no independent substantive work and no unprovided content is inferred from its cross-reference.

**Legal blocker:** None for the confined provisional affirmance. Any broader opinion requiring a finding of actual notice, systematic compliance, a minimum offer value, an additional permissible category, or the constitutional validity of every FCRA exception would exceed the supplied record and require a refreshed neutral source premise. The current judgment does not require those findings.
