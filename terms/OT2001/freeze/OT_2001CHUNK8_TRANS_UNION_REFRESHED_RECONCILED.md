## Statutory comparison that the joins require

The following are the operative terms used in the constitutional comparison. They are obligations and permissions of law, not findings that a consumer, agency or recipient complied.

**Consumer direction and purpose.** Section 604(a)(2) permits furnishing in accordance with the consumer's actual **written instructions**. Failure to opt out is not such an instruction. Section 604(c)(1)(A) separately permits the consumer to authorize the agency to provide that report to that person in the credit or insurance setting; it does not itself say written and is not a general oral-approval permission for unrelated marketing. Under section 604(a)(3), the agency's reason to believe the recipient intends a permitted use remains necessary: credit extension, account review or collection involving the consumer; insurance underwriting involving the consumer; or the separate legitimate-business-need route tied to a consumer-initiated transaction or review for continued account compliance. An unrelated marketer's profitable use is not itself that business-need route. Section 603(m)'s exclusion for a relationship holder's review of an existing account or insurance policy, or collection of an existing account, does not authorize all new marketing by that holder.

**The nonauthorizing firm-offer route.** Section 604(c)(1)(B) requires a firm offer of credit or insurance, agency compliance with subsection (e), and no effective subsection (e) election excluding the consumer's name and address. Under section 603(l), the offer must be honored when the consumer meets the consumer-report criteria used for selection. Further conditions may involve application information showing satisfaction of specific creditworthiness or insurability criteria established before selection for the offered transaction; verification of continued satisfaction of selection criteria through a report, application or other relevant information, or verification of application information against the applicable criteria; and collateral whose requirement was fixed before selection and disclosed in the offer. The statutory commitment is conditional but real. No unconditional extension, minimum offer value, favorable rate or actual completed transaction is assumed.

**Information and subsequent-use limits.** Section 604(c)(2) allows the name and address, a nonunique identifier used solely for identity verification, and other information that does not identify the consumer's relationship or experience with a particular creditor or other entity. The last category is not limited to names and addresses; it and list membership can communicate financial information. Section 604(c)(3) separately prohibits furnishing unsolicited-transaction inquiry records except for section 609(a)(5)'s disclosure to the identified consumer, on request with proper identification, of the preceding year's inquiries identifying that consumer. Section 604(f) separately requires both an authorized furnishing purpose and the prospective user's general or specific purpose certification. A permitted transfer does not authorize unrestricted later use or a full credit file merely because a firm offer is contemplated.

**Agency opt-out duties and their actual limits.** Consumers may notify through the agency system or submit its signed election form. Exclusion begins five business days after notice and reaches agency affiliates. System-only exclusion lasts two years beginning five business days after notice, subject to earlier cancellation; signed-form exclusion continues until cancellation through the system. The agency must explain the two-year limit after system notice and provide the signed form within five business days when requested during that notice. The system must include a toll-free number and permit notice with appropriate identification. At least annual general-circulation publication for the service area must describe possible file use and provide the exclusion address and toll-free number; the original deadline was 365 days after September 30, 1996. A qualifying system and publication maintained for affiliates satisfies those obligations for them, and nationwide agencies must maintain the system jointly. These requirements provide a defined means to block future releases. They do not make a telephone election perpetual, an election immediately effective, or general publication proof of individual awareness.

**Separate user duties.** Section 615(d) requires each written solicitation based on the firm-offer route to conspicuously disclose use of report information; satisfaction of selection criteria; any applicable further criteria or collateral requirements that may prevent extension; the consumer's right to prohibit unsolicited-transaction use; and the notification method, address and toll-free number. Selection, extension and collateral criteria must be retained for three years from the offer. The written-solicitation duty does not guarantee individual notice before first disclosure and does not extend by implication to every oral communication. Federal and state unfair- or deceptive-practice authority remains. Section 615(c)'s separate liability defense requires proof by a preponderance of reasonable compliance procedures maintained at the time of the alleged violation. That defense does not delete the duties or excuse an unauthorized purpose under section 604. These user duties are relevant to the whole scheme but are not silently added as new express elements of section 604(c)(1)(B).

The consumer-report definition and its copied exclusions remain at their actual statutory scope. No unreviewed affiliate communication, card approval, third-party credit decision or subsection (o) communication is found equivalent to these lists or separately upheld. The definition's collection-purpose language is not replaced by the purchaser's later use. No additional classification claim is decided.
