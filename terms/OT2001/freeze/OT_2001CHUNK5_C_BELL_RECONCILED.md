## Authoritative reconciled components

### B1. Review framework and the Cronic application

All eight accept that the July 1, 1997 application is governed by amended section 2254 and that clearly established constitutional law is measured at the relevant state merits adjudication. Strickland and Cronic were available then; later Williams supplies the actual current construction of section 2254. The September 19, 1986 adjudication, November 4, 1987 intermediate appellate reasoning and later affirmance must retain their separate dates. Later Shelton, Cotton, Mickens and Flores-Ortega do not retroactively create a new April 1982 professional duty or new constitutional law at the state merits date.

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer reject Cronic's complete-failure application here. Counsel made an opening identifying mitigation and requesting mercy, directed the same jury to the already introduced expert evidence, obtained favorable military-service evidence on cross-examination and successfully opposed damaging prosecution evidence. Those are actual adversarial acts, not mere physical presence. Their possible inadequacy and the omitted witnesses or closing argument require ordinary performance and prejudice analysis rather than automatic presumed prejudice on this ground. Their rule does not abolish other recognized Cronic situations or approve every sentencing silence.

Stevens does not join that application. He regards the combined failure to provide an individualized case for life and respond to the prosecution's request for death, considered with counsel's disclosed approach to the penalty proceeding, as a failure of the relevant adversarial function. The brief opening and isolated protective acts do not in his assessment supply the meaningful capital-sentencing defense whose cumulative absence matters under Cronic. He does not deny that those acts occurred, use an act-counting rule, or announce that every failure to call a witness or give a closing argument is structural. His primary position is that the Sixth Circuit correctly recognized this cumulative breakdown and that requiring ordinary prejudice proof would fail to account for the missing adversarial presentation.

That is Stevens's legal assessment of the reviewed representation, not a new unanimous finding about every proposed witness or investigation allegation. No claim that later illness proves trial incapacity is adopted. The historical dissent relied additionally on that evidence and broader investigation accounts; this handoff does not turn those disputed or procedurally limited materials into new facts or decide their separate default treatment. His disagreement with the state characterization of the presentation as reasonable strategy remains explicit, rather than being erased by assuming that characterization resolves Cronic.

### B2. Ordinary tactical claim and the distinct development allegations

The seven who reject Cronic also reject the reviewed claim that the available mitigation and final-argument choices were constitutionally ineffective, because the state performance adjudication was not an objectively unreasonable Strickland application. This ordinary issue is fairly included in review of the state rejection and was addressed below. Their sufficient ground is performance, not the district court's added unfairness requirement or a new prejudice finding.

The supporting reasons are concrete and bounded: the credited reliance on experts whose testimony remained before the jury, the mother's ineffective appearance as counsel assessed it, interviews of other potential witnesses, the reviewed record's lack of a showing that the omitted testimony would have helped, and preventing the more effective prosecutor's unanswered rebuttal. Informed professional judgment remains reviewable. Neither the state label strategy nor the district's phrase unreviewable strategic decision-making controls the constitutional inquiry.

Cozzolino admits punishment evidence only when pertinent to a statutory aggravator or mitigation actually raised; rebuttal must explain or controvert a proposition actually advanced. Its troubled-childhood account was not rebutted by proof of present criminal proclivities. Later defense evidence can make premature admission harmless only by supplying actual rebuttal relevance. Thus generalized fear of all bad-acts questioning would be mistaken, while no rule immunizes every proposed character statement from relevant cross-examination. The seven make no new witness-specific admissibility finding and do not substitute counsel's fear for that legal rule.

Pritchett, available before trial, required resentencing when one of two aggravators failed and the court could not assume the jury's weighing of the remaining one. It supplied a limited basis for expecting a new hearing, not guaranteed reversal whenever any aggravator failed. Cone's later 1984 direct appeal distinguished the number and strength of surviving aggravators and found harmless error; that later decision cannot be attributed to counsel's April 1982 knowledge. The reproduced testimony also gives multiple reasons for waiving closing, including prior mitigation and avoiding the identified stronger rebuttal. Rejecting this confined claim does not endorse deliberately generating appellate error as invariably competent practice.

Stevens does not join final rejection of the ordinary claim. If his Cronic position does not prevail, he would permit the Court of Appeals to examine the asserted legal misunderstandings, the independence of the performance rationale and its relationship to the disputed record restrictions before ruling on Strickland. He accepts that the State has a serious independent-ground argument, not merely a strategy label, but does not find that argument sufficient to foreclose ordinary appellate review here. He makes no alternative present finding of actual Strickland deficiency or prejudice.

The seven's rejection concerns the reviewed choices on the limited record. It does not establish that every mitigation lead was adequately investigated or that every distinct development allegation is defaulted. The original state factual findings keep their actual scope; the broad language praising preparation is not silently enlarged into adjudication of every later allegation.

### B3. Fretwell and the independent-ground inquiry

All eight reject an additional fundamental-unfairness or unreliability requirement beyond Strickland's ordinary reasonable-probability standard for lawful mitigation. Actual Williams requires the full supported mitigation assessment and keeps statutory eligibility, constitutional error and remedy distinct. The district court's invocation and application of the added requirement is not approved.

The seven need not decide actual prejudice because their independent state-performance conclusion defeats this limited ordinary claim. This is not reliance on a contaminated prejudice analysis, nor a rule that any mention of strategy cures legal error. The source supplied the district's own distinct performance and closing discussion and the actual state rationale; the seven evaluate the limited performance ground under the correct law themselves. Stevens's alternative remand requires the Court of Appeals to address the disputed independence and scope rather than accepting it. The added Fretwell error does not itself establish a constitutional violation, require a writ on every claim or determine the separate default questions.

### B4. Disposition, remaining claims and remedial limits

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would reverse the Cronic-based sentence grant, reject the confined ordinary tactical claim on the sufficient performance ground, and remand the remaining sentencing matters. The Sixth Circuit pretermitted other sentence grounds; those remain for ordinary adjudication. The distinct investigation/development default issues, their adequacy and independence and asserted cause from unavailable investigative funding are not resolved here. Respondent's remand argument states those objections; it proves neither cause nor an inadequate state ground. Lee does not decide Tennessee's application.

Stevens would affirm the sentence-only conditional writ on Cronic. That preserves the existing 180-day structure for a new lawful penalty proceeding under the appellate judgment; it does not acquit Cone or directly impose a life term. If the Court rejects Cronic, his alternative is a remand leaving the ordinary tactical claim open as well as the separate investigation/default and other preserved sentence matters. He does not join the seven's exclusion of that ordinary claim from further merits consideration. His primary affirmance does not adjudicate unused alternative sentence grounds or enlarge the conditional relief.

The convictions are unaffected on every position. The seven's reversal supplies no replacement life sentence, execution, new guilt trial or immediate release. Ordinary further disposition of preserved claims determines any necessary district proceedings; no automatic evidentiary hearing, investigative funding entitlement, new testimony or medical finding is ordered. A source boundary establishes no litigant default or failure of proof. No blanket restoration of the district court's entire sentence judgment follows from the seven's disposition.
