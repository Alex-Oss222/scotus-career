**M1 — Section 1331 and the special review provision.** All eight join jurisdiction over the substantial claim that the later commission order conflicts with the Telecommunications Act or applicable federal rules. Bell v. Hood's complete qualification must be stated: the asserted federal claim does not establish jurisdiction if it clearly appears immaterial and made solely to obtain jurisdiction, or wholly insubstantial and frivolous. This complaint crosses that threshold; its eventual failure, or the absence of a valid rather than arguable statutory cause, is a separate merits question. No holding makes every dispute over an interconnection agreement federal or decides whether the Act creates the asserted private cause.

Assuming without deciding that the determination-review sentence of section 252(e)(6) does not cover this enforcement action, it does not withdraw otherwise available section 1331 jurisdiction here. The first sentence expressly makes FCC substitution and judicial review of FCC action exclusive for the failure to act described in paragraph (5); the second sentence addresses commission determinations and does not repeat that exclusivity. The order challenged here is commission action, not an established paragraph (5) failure. Paragraph (e)(4)'s express state-court restriction concerns approval or rejection of agreements. Neither this comparison nor the substantial federal claim decides the full coverage of paragraph (e)(6), abolishes its failure-to-act exclusivity, or eliminates preserved state-law requirements and contract questions.

**M2 — Administrative action and prior state litigation.** All eight join rejection of the categorical state-court-appellate-review objection. Actual claim or issue preclusion remains distinct and unadjudicated; no prior state judgment is vacated.

**M3 — Necessary alternative grounds only.** All eight join rejection of the categorical officer-relief and associated indispensability grounds insofar as those grounds would independently sustain dismissal despite the answer to Question 2. The Young threshold does not decide whether the federal claim ultimately succeeds. The actual Seminole Tribe holding permits its officer route within all enacted remedial limits; it is not the historical exclusionary holding.

Private financial consequences do not themselves make a prospective officer injunction retrospective State compensation. The actual prayers must remain separate: future enforcement restraint; a declaration affecting accrued private obligations; a direction concerning private-carrier refunds; and any demand against the State itself. No private refund is awarded or held available. No solely retrospective declaration, state-treasury award or state-law command is approved. Edelman and Pennhurst remain effective, and remaining party and remedy questions return at their actual scope.

## 73. Mathias v. WorldCom Technologies, Inc.

### Reconciled result and sufficient ground

**All eight Associates retain dismissal of the writ as improvidently granted.** This is a discretionary threshold disposition of this vehicle, not a merits affirmance, a determination of all appellate-aggrievement questions, or a categorical Article III prohibition on appeals by successful officials. No numerical historical merits vote is inferred from the per curiam disposition. The Seventh Circuit's judgment remains undisturbed and its jurisdiction, waiver and officer-relief reasoning is not adopted.

The favorable commission merits do not erase the separately denied dismissal motions. Those motions asserted a right to avoid federal adjudication, were postponed, and were denied after the merits; the lower court treated its favorable merits resolution as binding upon the commissioners despite no formal judgment against them. The opportunity for ordinary interlocutory immunity review had thereby passed. These are material procedural facts, not merely an unfavorable passage in an opinion.

Nevertheless, the requested present review principally challenges the jurisdiction and immunity bases of the completed favorable adjudication. Its binding merits sustain the commissioners' own order and impose no adverse substantive change to that order. The independent analysis identifies no distinct corrective component comparable to the unnecessary adverse patent-validity adjudication removed in Electrical Fittings. This conclusion is a legal characterization of the supplied instruments; it does not infer nonpleading, noninjury or default from an unavailable paper.

Electrical Fittings permits removal of an unnecessary adverse adjudication in a favorable decree without certain future preclusion or an already pending lawsuit. Roper recognizes a continuing stake in its confined collateral-review setting and does not limit such stakes universally to money. Forney permits review of a claimant's actual unawarded relief and reserves wholly favorable relief and requests merely for another explanation. The commissioners' denied dismissal request makes the competing analogy serious, but none of these authorities compels discretionary resolution of the broad statutory and immunity issues in this favorable-merits vehicle. The distinction is not that all unsuccessful motions are mere reasoning, nor that precedent can never matter. No general rule forecloses review of a distinct adverse collateral adjudication with an appropriate continuing stake and corrective remedy.

The historical dismissal strongly confirms this confined result. Its statement about nonbinding adverse findings is not independent proof of every collateral consequence. The present explanation retains the validated motion-denial and binding-merits distinctions and does not adopt a new categorical constitutional rule. Its reliance on a separately expanded and decided Maryland proceeding cannot be imported: no such expansion is established here, and Maryland's same-day result supplies no entering authority. These omissions do not defeat the independent unfavorable-vehicle basis for dismissal.
