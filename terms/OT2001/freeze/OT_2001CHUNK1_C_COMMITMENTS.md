## 1. United States Postal Service v. Gregory — November 13, 2001

### Preserved components and proposed judgment

All eight Associates would **vacate the Federal Circuit judgment and remand**. A contractual grievance's pendency alone does not categorically prohibit the employer and Board from considering the earlier disciplinary action. The return must preserve ordinary review of the actual Board decision and lawful treatment of the warning's arbitral reversal; it does not reinstate removal unconditionally or decide the adequacy of Bolling.

**Common sufficient rule.** The civil-service scheme permits disciplinary-history consideration subject to its agency justification, employee response, Board review and judicial-review requirements. It does not make final contractual grievance resolution a prerequisite to that consideration. Whether an earlier action remains pending, has been invalidated, or lacks adequate evidentiary support are separate inquiries. The present disposition rejects only the categorical pendency rule and leaves the actual legal consequences of the overturned warning to the properly available review process.

**Remedial limits.** The Court makes no final finding that the removal penalty survives without the warning, that the warning necessarily defeats it, or that Gregory's failure to notify the Board resolves every procedural consequence. No new grievance, mandatory evidentiary hearing, automatic reinstatement or backpay award is ordered. Bolling's collateral-review adequacy was not preserved and receives no statutory or constitutional approval by implication.

## 10. J. E. M. Ag Supply v. Pioneer Hi-Bred — December 10, 2001

### Preserved components and proposed judgment

O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer would **affirm** rejection of the categorical statutory-ineligibility defense. Stevens would **reverse and remand** on the narrower ground that the specific plant-protection scheme controls protection of the plant varieties it covers. The independent non-Stone disposition is therefore seven affirmance commitments and one reversal commitment; no Stone position is supplied.

**Affirmance rule.** A newly developed plant is not excluded from the general utility-patent statute solely because it is also within the subject matter of the Plant Patent Act or Plant Variety Protection Act. A utility patent still requires satisfaction of its own eligibility, novelty, nonobviousness, disclosure and other applicable conditions. The plant-specific statutes retain their independent requirements and exceptions for the rights they create. Neither a certificate nor a seed-sale restriction proves entitlement to a utility patent or establishes the validity and infringement of every asserted claim.

**Reversal rule supported by Stevens.** The plant-specific enactments' detailed allocation of protection and agricultural uses supplies the controlling statutory regime for the covered plant varieties. Applying section 101 to avoid that allocation would defeat Congress's particular solution. This position leaves Chakrabarty's microorganism holding intact because the organism there was outside the specifically protected plant categories; it does not assert a general ban on patents involving life or on separately qualifying processes.

**Shared limits.** Asgrow's former-law construction and the admitted April 4, 1995 transition remain untouched. No farmer exemption is imported automatically into utility-patent law; Stevens instead rejects that overlapping patent route for the statutory category. No particular certificate date, refiling, marking, farmer eligibility, exhaustion, contract or antitrust defense is determined. The judgment concerns the categorical question presented and produces no new infringement or damages finding.

## 12. Dusenbery v. United States — January 8, 2002

### Preserved components and proposed judgment

All eight reject an absolute actual-receipt requirement. O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would **affirm** on the adequacy of the demonstrated certified-mail and internal-distribution procedure. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would **vacate and remand** because the government-controlled terminal delivery procedure has not been shown sufficiently reliable under the circumstances. These are four affirmance and four vacatur commitments; Stone's addition is necessary before any Court judgment can be determined.

**Common governing rule.** Due process requires means reasonably calculated, prospectively and in the known circumstances, to convey the forfeiture notice and afford an opportunity to contest. A failure to prove actual receipt does not itself establish a violation. Government custody matters because it fixes both the known recipient's location and the delivery route available to him. Mailing, the mailroom signature, internal transmission and inmate delivery are distinct facts.

**Affirmance application.** Certified notice to the correct institution, the identified post-office acknowledgment, and testimony describing the contemporaneous collection, logging, unit-employee signature and inmate-distribution practice form a reasonably directed route. The Constitution does not require preserved proof of the last delivery in every case. No known failure or finding of institutional unreliability is supplied. Other attempted addresses do not independently cure the prison route, and actual receipt is not found.

**Vacatur application.** A system controlled by the forfeiting government must be assessed through its final custodial handoff, not just postal arrival at its own institution. The supported process documents transfers among employees, but supplies no adequate check on the last delivery to the confined recipient. Its lack of a comparable safeguard is legally significant where the government controls access and a forfeiture deadline ends the right to contest. The defect is not merely that no inmate signature survived discovery or that a prisoner denies receipt. The required inquiry concerns the reliability of the actual 1988 route and feasible safeguards under Mullane. No particular form of acknowledgment or guaranteed actual receipt is prescribed.

**Remedy and limits.** Affirmance rejects this notice challenge without adjudicating ownership anew. Vacatur would require reconsideration of the notice challenge under the custody-sensitive application; it does not order automatic return of the property, damages, a finding that the prisoner never received the notice, or an unrestricted new evidentiary hearing. No rule for known returned mail or proven delivery failure is decided on an invented factual premise.

## 16. Chao v. Mallard Bay Drilling — January 9, 2002

### Components and proposed judgment

All eight Associates would **reverse the Fifth Circuit judgment and remand**. They support both conclusions: the Louisiana barge is a workplace within the Act's relevant geographic coverage, and Coast Guard potential authority did not displace OSHA over the evacuation and emergency-response working conditions actually cited.

**Displacement rule.** Section 4(b)(1) excludes the working conditions with respect to which another federal agency actually exercises statutory authority to prescribe or enforce occupational-safety or health standards or regulations. Potential jurisdiction alone does not suffice. Actual comprehensive regulation of the pertinent working conditions may matter without a provision worded identically to an OSHA rule, but general vessel authority, regulation of other hazards or a post-casualty investigation does not establish such coverage here.

**Application.** This was an uninspected drilling barge. The supported record reports no Coast Guard regulations addressing the cited evacuation, emergency-response planning and training matters; the investigation and referral did not impose a comprehensive occupational-safety regime for them. The Fifth Circuit's industry-wide inference therefore cannot sustain displacement. This reasoning does not decide OSHA's authority over every inspected vessel, every uninspected vessel condition or all maritime work.

**Workplace rule.** Employment aboard this barge at the supported location within Louisiana falls within the Act's workplace-in-a-State coverage. Navigability alone does not remove that location from the State or create an unstated general maritime exclusion. The ruling is confined to the actual geographic and employment setting and leaves other territorial boundaries and offshore regimes undecided.

**Remedy.** The employer's coverage and displacement objections are rejected. The case returns for proceedings consistent with that resolution and the actual administrative posture. The Court does not independently find the violation elements or recalculate a penalty; neither a fresh citation trial nor a new factual hearing is required merely by this remand. The previously unchallenged citation merits are not newly reopened by recognizing OSHA authority.
