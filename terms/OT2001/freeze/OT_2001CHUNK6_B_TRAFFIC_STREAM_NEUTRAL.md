## Validation and lawful alternatives

The predecision petition and both merits briefs confirm the presented alienage question. The petitioner asks whether corporations organized in the British Virgin Islands and other United Kingdom overseas territories are citizens or subjects under section 1332(a)(2). The scoped question before this Court concerns this BVI corporation; a ruling need not determine every territory. Respondent argues that modern British nationality distinctions exclude colonial nationals, that federal alienage jurisdiction does not cover every foreign person, and that territorial status is unlike full citizenship. Petitioner answers that the relevant federal subject/allegiance category reaches a corporation created under British sovereign authority; diplomatic and commercial purposes confirm rather than independently confer jurisdiction. The corporate inquiry is distinct from the passport or immigration status of natural persons. A newly enacted nationality change cannot retroactively supply jurisdiction absent at commencement.

The BVI sovereign relationship is supported; no factual remand is needed merely because the parties dispute its federal legal significance. Reversal of the jurisdictional dismissal would restore appellate merits consideration without newly deciding the debt. Affirmance would require federal dismissal, not merits exoneration. Forum selection cannot create subject-matter jurisdiction.

## Current law and each Justice's own authority

Caterpillar (1996-12-10), O’Connor for all nine, requires actual jurisdiction notwithstanding litigation convenience. Rivet (1998-02-24), Ginsburg for all nine, prevents substituting an inapplicable federal basis. These are direct adopted positions of Stevens, O’Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. The exact reusable provision is **Diversity cured before trial after erroneous removal**, present operation effective Caterpillar, December 10, 1996. Neither prior holding decides the BVI category. Verizon Maryland (2002-05-20) separates actual jurisdiction from merits and remedies; its section 1331 rule does not replace the section 1332 text here. The legal forks are statutory subjecthood under sovereignty versus strict incorporation/nationality classification, not federal convenience versus an absence of any statutory category.

### Issue 1: Corporate foreign allegiance and federal jurisdiction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** The supplied jurisdictional holdings distinguish an actual statutory basis from party consent, federal defenses, and later merits considerations. Rivet v. Regions Bank of Louisiana refuses to manufacture federal jurisdiction from a federal defense; Caterpillar Inc. v. Lewis protects a completed judgment only where the necessary diversity actually existed before trial, rather than curing an enduring defect by consent or convenience.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the jurisdictional statute, the relevant parties and citizenship facts, and whether the actual sovereign relationship fits the statutory category. Review the legal classification independently, without allowing a contract clause to enlarge federal power.
* **Other Governing Law:** The alienage provision permits qualifying controversies between citizens of a State and citizens or subjects of a foreign state. Article III supplies the outer constitutional category. The United Kingdom’s sovereignty over the British Virgin Islands bears on whether a corporation formed there is its subject.
* **Material Other Authority:** Historical usage of citizens and subjects reflects both republican citizenship and allegiance within other sovereign systems. Executive recognition of the United Kingdom and the territory’s constitutional relationship assist the factual classification but do not replace interpretation of the jurisdictional statute.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** No supplied simulated holding resolves this territory’s corporate status. The established need for real jurisdiction leaves the precise statutory classification open.
* **Open Point:** Whether the corporation’s organization under a dependent territory’s law establishes the requisite relation to its recognized sovereign.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The court of appeals separated the territory from the United Kingdom and concluded that incorporation there did not satisfy the statutory category. It treated the jurisdictional defect as requiring dismissal despite the parties’ prior litigation of the debt.

**Bank.** The statutory reference to subjects reaches entities organized within territory under a recognized foreign sovereign’s authority. Denying jurisdiction would create an unintended gap for corporations owing allegiance within dependent territories. Personal nationality terminology should not displace the corporate sovereignty inquiry.

**Traffic Stream’s jurisdictional position.** Federal jurisdiction is limited, and the corporation is formed under British Virgin Islands law rather than the ordinary corporate law of the United Kingdom. The bank must establish a statutory category, not rely on convenience, forum selection, or similarity to foreign parties whose status is clearer.

**Limits.** The United Kingdom’s relationship with this territory is the decisive fact. Neither every possession nor every disputed political entity necessarily presents the same arrangement. The statute’s other diversity requirements and the merits of the financing dispute remain separate.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal would restore the alienage basis and return the litigation for further appellate treatment of any remaining issues. Affirmance would require dismissal for lack of federal jurisdiction, without adjudicating liability anew. A remand for jurisdictional facts would be appropriate only if the sovereign relationship were materially uncertain.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A corporation created under the law of a territory subject to United Kingdom sovereignty is a United Kingdom subject for this alienage category. That conclusion does not require recognition of the territory as a separate state or a finding about each individual inhabitant’s personal nationality. A contrary narrower construction would leave any jurisdictional gap for Congress to address.

**Uncertainty:** No separate finding about another corporation’s principal place of business or a wholly different territory should be inferred from this record. Forum selection supplies consent to a location, not the missing jurisdictional element.
