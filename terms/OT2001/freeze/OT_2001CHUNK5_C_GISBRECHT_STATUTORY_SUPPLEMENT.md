# OT2001 chunk 5 C: Gisbrecht statutory supplement

**Matter:** Gisbrecht v. Barnhart, No. 01-131; May 28, 2002.
**Purpose:** Complete the bounded statutory-source handoff for court-representation fees and the EAJA saving/refund qualification. No pending methodology, fee amount, vote or disposition is selected. Existing freezes and source files remain unchanged.

## Sources and verified version

The official [2000 United States Code section 406](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2000-title42/pdf/USCODE-2000-title42-chap7-subchapII-sec406.pdf) supplies the complete section, amendment notes and effective-date notes. All five saved pages were read as text; the operative section 406(b), incorporated subsection (d) and relevant amendment/transition notes were also inspected on printed pages 1192-1193. The official [Public Law 99-80](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-99/pdf/STATUTE-99-Pg183.pdf), approved August 5, 1985, supplies the complete section 3 amendment at 99 Stat. 186 and its section 7 transition at 99 Stat. 186-187. All five saved pages were read as text, and those two printed pages were inspected. The accompanying source receipt's PDF hashes and five-page counts match.

Section 406(b)(1)(A) authorizes the court, after the required favorable judgment and attorney representation, to determine and allow a reasonable fee for that court representation as part of its judgment. The ceiling is 25 percent of the total past-due benefits due by reason of that judgment. Certification is permitted out of those benefits, not in addition to them, subject to subsection (d). The paragraph restricts other payable or certified fees for the same court representation. An agreement is not itself a court award, and the maximum is not a guaranteed award.

Subparagraph (B) is part of the full text: the defined past-due-benefit base excludes the continued payments described in section 423(g) or (h), and amounts are determined before an applicable section 1320a-6(a) reduction. Section 406(b)(2) reaches an attorney's charging, demanding, receiving or collecting an amount above the court allowance for the covered court services; it specifies the misdemeanor and its fine/imprisonment limits. This source establishes no violation by either lawyer in this case.

Section 406(a) separately regulates representation before the Commissioner, including its own fee-approval, agreement, review and certification provisions. Those administrative procedures are not the court's subsection (b) method. The copied subsection (d), when its transition applies, assesses the attorney for determining and certifying fees, permits offset collection and prohibits direct or indirect claimant reimbursement; it preserves the appropriations limitation and other stated conditions. Its after-2000 percentage is set by the Commissioner for cost recovery subject to the 6.3 percent maximum, not automatically fixed at that maximum. This packet determines no applicable assessment rate or dollar deduction and decides no aggregate administrative-and-court fee question.

The Code notes identify the 1994 addition of subsection (b)(1)(B) and its application to favorable judgments made after 180 days after August 15, 1994; the Commissioner terminology separately took effect March 31, 1995. The 1999 amendment adds the subsection (d) qualification and assessment. Its express transition turns on when a fee is required to be certified for payment, after the later of December 31, 1999 and the last day of the first month after the enactment month. Because enactment was December 17, 1999, that latter date is January 31, 2000. Do not substitute the service date, the contract date or the eventual payment date for that enacted trigger.

The supplied 2000 edition is the authenticated provision and transition source for this handoff; it is not a representation that every possible enactment or administrative rate through May 2002 has been independently surveyed. No missing amendment affecting the disputed reasonable-fee methodology is identified in these sources. A calculation that depends on a particular favorable-judgment or certification date, benefit-base adjustment or assessment rate requires that claimant's actual dates and inputs. Those amount-specific matters do not prevent modeling the presented methodology question or an ordinary remand retaining statutory qualifications.

## Full EAJA saving/refund qualification

Public Law 99-80 section 3 allows an award of fees and other expenses under section 2412(d) despite the cited Social Security court-fee provision. Its second sentence removes section 206(b)(2)'s criminal restriction with respect to such an award only on the stated condition: when the attorney receives fees for the same work under both provisions, the attorney refunds the smaller fee to the claimant. The trigger is overlapping fees actually received for the same work; the direction is a refund to the claimant. The text does not authorize keeping both overlapping fees, eliminate subsection (b)'s independent reasonableness decision, merge the two statutory fee sources or make all expenses part of the smaller-fee comparison.

EAJA's government-paid award remains subject to its own eligibility, timing, substantial-justification and other requirements. The 1985 amending law is not a complete current codification of section 2412(d), and this packet supplies no current hourly cap or conclusion about every EAJA exception. The statutory saving/refund rule is sufficient for the requested overlap issue. Section 3 includes the amendment instruction to the earlier EAJA section 206(a); no unprovided remainder of that earlier subsection is reconstructed. The complete 1985 section 3 and complete section 7 are retained below.

### Complete section 406(b), printed page 1192

```text
(b) Fees for representation before court 
(1)(A) Whenever a court renders a judgment fa-
vorable to a claimant under this subchapter who 
was represented before the court by an attorney, 
the court may determine and allow as part of its 
judgment a reasonable fee for such representa-
tion, not in excess of 25 percent of the total of 
the past-due benefits to which the claimant is 
entitled by reason of such judgment, and the 
Commissioner of Social Security may, notwith-
standing the provisions of section 405(i) of this 
title, but subject to subsection (d) of this sec-
tion, certify the amount of such fee for payment 
to such attorney out of, and not in addition to, 
the amount of such past-due benefits. In case of 
any such judgment, no other fee may be payable 
or certified for payment for such representation 
except as provided in this paragraph. 
(B) For purposes of this paragraph— 
(i) the term ‘‘past-due benefits’’ excludes 
any benefits with respect to which payment 
has been continued pursuant to subsection (g) 
or (h) of section 423 of this title, and 
(ii) amounts of past-due benefits shall be de-
termined before any applicable reduction 
under section 1320a–6(a) of this title. 
(2) Any attorney who charges, demands, re-
ceives, or collects for services rendered in con-
nection with proceedings before a court to which 
paragraph (1) of this subsection is applicable any 
amount in excess of that allowed by the court 
thereunder shall be guilty of a misdemeanor and 
upon conviction thereof shall be subject to a 
fine of not more than $500, or imprisonment for 
not more than one year, or both. 
```

### Complete incorporated section 406(d), printed page 1192

```text
(d) Assessment on attorneys 
(1) In general 
Whenever a fee for services is required to be 
certified for payment to an attorney from a 
claimant’s past-due benefits pursuant to sub-
section (a)(4) or (b)(1) of this section, the Com-
missioner shall impose on the attorney an as-
sessment calculated in accordance with para-
graph (2). 
(2) Amount 
(A) The amount of an assessment under 
paragraph (1) shall be equal to the product ob-
tained by multiplying the amount of the rep-
resentative’s fee that would be required to be 
so certified by subsection (a)(4) or (b)(1) of this 
section before the application of this sub-
section, by the percentage specified in sub-
paragraph (B). 
(B) The percentage specified in this subpara-
graph is— 
(i) for calendar years before 2001, 6.3 per-
cent, and 
(ii) for calendar years after 2000, such per-
centage rate as the Commissioner deter-
mines is necessary in order to achieve full 
recovery of the costs of determining and cer-
tifying fees to attorneys from the past-due 
benefits of claimants, but not in excess of 6.3 
percent. 
(3) Collection 
The Commissioner may collect the assess-
ment imposed on an attorney under paragraph 
(1) by offset from the amount of the fee other-
wise required by subsection (a)(4) or (b)(1) of 
this section to be certified for payment to the 
attorney from a claimant’s past-due benefits. 
(4) Prohibition on claimant reimbursement 
An attorney subject to an assessment under 
paragraph (1) may not, directly or indirectly, 
request or otherwise obtain reimbursement for 
such assessment from the claimant whose 
claim gave rise to the assessment. 
(5) Disposition of assessments 
Assessments on attorneys collected under 
this subsection shall be credited to the Fed-
eral Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust 
Fund and the Federal Disability Insurance 
Trust Fund, as appropriate. 
(6) Authorization of appropriations 
The assessments authorized under this sec-
tion shall be collected and available for obli-
gation only to the extent and in the amount 
provided in advance in appropriations Acts. 
Amounts so appropriated are authorized to re-
main available until expended, for administra-
tive expenses in carrying out this subchapter 
and related laws. 
```
