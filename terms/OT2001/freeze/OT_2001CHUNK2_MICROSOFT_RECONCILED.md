## Final components, legal rules and remedies

**T1 — undifferentiated tying judgment:** All eight reject restoration of the District Court's undifferentiated per se judgment. Independent browser demand remains relevant to separate products under Jefferson Parish and actual Kodak; technological connection does not conclusively merge them. The platform-integration contention nevertheless requires an adequate inquiry into category fit and actual competitive mechanism. No efficiency finding follows from calling the software integrated.

**T2-R — rule of reason for this combination:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer affirm the rule-of-reason direction for the tying arrangement presented, including the contractual and removal restrictions. The holding is confined to this combination of a principal platform for third-party applications and complementary software functionality; it is neither software immunity nor a general rule that mentioning an efficiency defeats an established category. The actual explanation must be tested against each actual restriction. Distributing application interfaces may justify something different from forcing the full browser or prohibiting its subsequent removal. The rule permits a suitably abbreviated inquiry when actual experience, direct effects and the mechanism suffice; it does not invariably demand a comprehensive market-definition or industry study.

**T2-D — differentiated categorical route:** Stevens agrees that genuine functional integration requires fuller competitive inquiry but opposes a blanket direction covering every restriction. Where a separable contractual or removal restraint fits the ordinary forced-tie category and its remaining elements, that established category remains available. The lower court must distinguish that conduct from genuinely integrated functionality; technological accompaniment alone is no exception. He would modify the remand accordingly, without reinstating the whole trial judgment or deciding ultimate liability.

**T3 — preserved tying-remand limits:** Under T2-R, plaintiffs pursuing the claim must prove net anticompetitive effect in browser competition, considering the benefits actually supported for each restriction. Operating-system injury supporting section 2 does not itself establish the section 1 browser-market injury. The restrictions on OEM removal and the omitted removal utility remain possible section 1 theories; any additional remedy must be justified. The default-browser override's rejected section 2 ground does not foreclose rebuttal of its justification on the distinct browser-market question. Price bundling requires resolution of the positive browser-attributable price increment on this actual record, followed, if proved, by the effects/benefits inquiry. Conflicting evidence about competitors' discounts remains unresolved. Commingled code is not silently added as an independent trial tying basis.

The existing attempted-monopolization reversal is not reopened. Its case-specific remand preclusion remains: plaintiffs may not pursue tying injury dependent on a precise browser-market definition or independent entry barriers they failed to establish for the attempted-monopolization claim, apart from what is implicit in the tying arrangement. This is preservation of this lower mandate, not a universal antitrust rule. Stevens's differentiated route preserves the same unpresented-claim boundary and the ordinary requirements of the category actually used. No Justice grants final tying liability, a finding of reasonableness or automatic additional relief.

**D1 — violation and reassignment:** All eight affirm the established section 455(a) violation and assignment of further proceedings to a different judge. The cumulative undisclosed press relationship on pending merits and remedy questions creates a reasonable appearance of partiality. The violation does not depend on deciding actual bias. Even the former Liteky concurrence's extra judicial-source concern does not shield these outside dealings. The holding applies the governing objective standard; it does not adopt an independent constitutional structural-error rule or a ban on every judicial public statement.

**D2 — existing remedy:** All eight preserve vacatur of the entire June 7, 2000 remedial decree, both structural and conduct provisions, and require lawful remedial proceedings before another judge. The lower court's independent grounds—unresolved remedy-specific factual disputes without an appropriate hearing, inadequate remedial explanation and materially altered liability foundations—remain sufficient. Relief must be tied to the liability lawfully established and a proper remedial record. Neither mandatory divestiture nor categorical immunity from it is ordered; competing predictions about division remain proffers requiring adjudication at their proper stage.

**D3-F — further tailored liability inquiry:** Stevens, Kennedy, Souter and Ginsburg vacate the appellate judgment insofar as it conclusively rejects additional section 455 relief from retained liability findings and dependent conclusions. Remand through the appellate court for the new judge to apply Liljeberg's risks of injustice to the parties, injustice in other cases and damage to public confidence, and determine which material factual foundations require fresh determination. The inquiry must address the pre-findings timing and merits subjects of the concealed contacts. Ordinary clear-error review cannot itself replace neutral evaluation of contested credibility or permissible competing inferences. Undisputed matters and independently sustainable legal conclusions may remain at their established scope; prior legal rulings remain pertinent to facts lawfully determined. Reuse of an appropriate record is permitted. No all-issues retrial, automatic invalidation of every finding, or requirement to prove actual bias in a particular finding is imposed.

**D3-P — preserve the appellate allocation:** O'Connor, Scalia, Thomas and Breyer affirm retention of the liability findings and conclusions surviving ordinary merits review, while preserving reassignment and full decree vacatur. Their sufficient reason is the combined Liljeberg assessment: innocent plaintiffs' burden, the actual differentiation of sustained and rejected liability bases, limited additional deterrence and the public-confidence protection furnished by replacing the judge and entire remedy. They expressly account for contacts predating findings and do not say that all misconduct occurred after liability. They accept the lower allocation as sufficient in this case; they do not make actual bias necessary for broader relief or declare clear-error review an automatic cure. No new appellate finding or general harmlessness rule is adopted.

All eight accept the common Liljeberg and Rule 52 propositions. D3-F and D3-P disagree over the sufficiency of this particular allocation, not over whether actual bias is required or whether appellate review retries facts. Those shared rules cannot be used to erase the genuine remedial division.

## Selected separate-position substance for assembly

Stevens's tying position preserves the ordinary per se category for separable coercive restrictions that satisfy its elements. Functional integration and independent demand must both be considered; a possible benefit from common interfaces does not make compulsory retention of a complete browser an innovative design. His alternative is a differentiated legal classification and remand, not automatic liability. Jefferson Parish, actual Kodak and the concrete-restraint approach of his California Dental dissent join support the distinction. The seven T2-R Justices answer that the relationship between product definition, interface distribution and actual restriction should be resolved through the bounded effects inquiry here; that process can reject a justification that does not explain a given restriction.

The D3-F position requires more than remedy-stage replacement because the undisclosed conduct began before factfinding and concerned the findings' very subjects. Liljeberg's public-confidence and future-injustice concerns do not demand actual-bias proof; clear-error review does not independently choose among permissible credibility or expert assessments. A new judge should determine what may remain binding and decide anew the material foundations requiring reconsideration, while retaining independently supportable matters and reusing an appropriate record. This is tailored relief, not presumptive universal taint.

The D3-P position gives independent weight to innocent plaintiffs, the cost and limited additional deterrent value of repeating adjudication, the appellate court's differentiated merits corrections, and public confidence restored by reassignment and replacement of the entire remedy. The misconduct's most pronounced remedial expression helps justify the chosen line but does not erase its earlier beginning. These Justices find this particular allocation sufficient under all three Liljeberg interests. They do not adopt an actual-bias prerequisite, an automatic harmless-error rule or a claim that the appellate court conducted a new trial.
