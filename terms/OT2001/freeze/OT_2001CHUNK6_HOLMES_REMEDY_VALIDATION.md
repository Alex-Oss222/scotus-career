# Holmes Group — neutral transfer-predicate validation

**Scope and cutoff:** Matter 83, No. 01-408; June 3, 2002. This supplement addresses the predecision record and statutory predicates for a possible transfer. It supplies no vote, selected jurisdictional rule, or current disposition. Read it with [the validated neutral packet](OT_2001CHUNK6_HOLMES_VALIDATED_NEUTRAL.md).

## 1. Timeliness is affirmatively supported

Holmes, the appellee below and petitioner here, expressly states in its petition that Vornado **timely filed** its notice of appeal from the district judgment, but directed it to the Federal Circuit. Holmes repeats that representation in its merits brief, page i. The [control source](OT_2001CHUNK6_HOLMES_REMEDY_SOURCE.md) accurately copies the petition passage. These are affirmative representations by the party opposing Vornado's appeal, not an inference from a silent docket or from the Federal Circuit's decision to entertain the appeal.

The precise evidentiary status is a litigant's factual admission in its filed submissions, not a new finding by the Supreme Court or an independently inspected filing stamp. The exact calendar date of the notice has not been independently checked in this validation. No particular date should be invented. That verification limit does **not** justify labeling timely notice unsupported: the repeated positive admission supplies a supported procedural premise under the published-record approach. Party agreement cannot create subject-matter jurisdiction, but that principle does not require ignoring an affirmative representation about the filing's timeliness.

## 2. Availability of the Tenth Circuit is conditional on the allocation question

The actual district judgment, petition appendix 14a–15a and JA99–100, is dated March 27, 2000. It records the parties' joint Rule 54(b) motion, the court's express no-just-reason-for-delay finding, and entry of final judgment on Holmes's first cause of action. These are acts of the district court, not merely the parties' characterization of a tentative ruling. The adjudicated claim is the trade-dress declaratory claim; the later June 12 conditional order does not supply the original finality or notice date and must not be treated as having already dismissed the counterclaim with prejudice.

Then-operative §1291 gives the regional courts of appeals jurisdiction over final district-court decisions subject to its direct-review exception and the Federal Circuit's separate statutory grant. Section 1294(1), expressly subject to §§1292(c), 1292(d), and 1295, routes a reviewable District of Kansas decision to the circuit embracing that district, the Tenth Circuit. The district court itself identified that regional route if the case did not qualify for specialized patent jurisdiction, while declining to decide the allocation dispute. That conditional observation is not an adjudication rejecting §1295 jurisdiction.

Accordingly, **if the independent jurisdictional analysis concludes that §1295 did not assign this appeal to the Federal Circuit**, the actual Rule 54(b) final judgment, Kansas origin, and affirmatively represented timely notice support the conclusion that the appeal could have been brought in the Tenth Circuit when noticed. The destination is not made available for the first time by a later pleading change, the June 12 order, or a later change in substantive trade-dress law. Conversely, if the specialized grant applies, this supplement does not authorize disregarding its exclusivity. The unresolved allocation merits are a legal choice for the independent model, not a missing historical filing fact.

## 3. Section 1631 requires an interest-of-justice determination

The 2000 Code text, checked against the original 1982 enactment, covers an appeal noticed for or filed with a court defined in §610. When that court finds a want of jurisdiction, it **shall**, if transfer is in the interest of justice, transfer to another covered court where the appeal could have been brought at the original time. The appeal then proceeds as though noticed for the receiving court on the original date. Thus transfer is mandatory upon the statutory conditions, but the interest-of-justice condition cannot be omitted. A jurisdictional defect is not cured by deciding the merits in the wrong court for efficiency. A Supreme Court mandate can direct the lower appellate court's appropriate transfer action; this does not require treating the Supreme Court itself as a §610 transferor.

The following record and arguments bear on that judicial evaluation:

| Consideration | What the source establishes and what remains evaluative |
|---|---|
| A timely attempt to obtain appellate review | Holmes twice affirmatively represents timely notice. Preserving that attempt while correcting its destination is a concrete purpose served by §1631's original-date provision. No excuse for a late filing needs to be invented. |
| An identified available regional forum | The district judgment is a Kansas Rule 54(b) judgment; Holmes sought dismissal or Tenth Circuit transfer below and expressly asks for further proceedings in the Tenth Circuit in its Supreme Court merits conclusion. The requested forum and transfer theory are presented, not newly created by the operator. |
| A substantial existing basis for the chosen forum | Vornado relies on the Federal Circuit's en banc Aerojet decision and its established practice, with Xeta as another circuit's example. The district court described specialized appellate jurisdiction as possible although not entirely certain. These are concrete grounds to assess the reasonableness of filing there even if the controlling statutory analysis ultimately rejects that route. They do not establish jurisdiction by reliance or estoppel. |
| Claimed strategic manipulation | Holmes describes Vornado's destination choice as an effort to evade the regional judgment and criticizes the expense and instability it caused. Vornado attributes strategic manipulation to Holmes's selective declaratory pleading. Those competing accusations must be attributed to their proponents. The district court's observation about procedural maneuvering and conflicting circuit law does not itself establish that this notice was knowingly baseless or filed in bad faith. |
| Costs, continuity and the pending appeal | Holmes's merits brief invokes justice, judicial economy, efficiency and stability, then requests regional proceedings. Vornado describes an agreement staying district proceedings and withdrawing its ITC case while it pursued the appeal. Those procedural arrangements may inform preservation and delay concerns; their conditional triggers do not become satisfied merely because a transfer occurs. |
| The apparent strength of the trade-dress appeal | The parties contest collateral estoppel and TrafFix's effect. A neutral transfer assessment must not assume that either side necessarily wins those merits, use their historical descriptions to replace operative simulated TrafFix law, or treat a transfer as affirmance of the district judgment. |

Holmes's alternative request for dismissal below means it did not unconditionally stipulate to transfer as the only remedy. Its merits conclusion nevertheless expressly requests Tenth Circuit proceedings. Vornado's defense of Federal Circuit jurisdiction does not establish a separate adjudicated finding about the statutory interest of justice. The relevant choice remains whether the supported circumstances warrant preserving the timely appeal in the lawful forum; no interest-of-justice result is selected here.

## 4. Exact remaining limit and readiness

There is no unresolved source gap that requires treating the notice as untimely or the regional forum as unidentified. Timeliness rests on Holmes's repeated affirmative admission, finality and origin on the actual district judgment, and regional availability on the governing statutes **conditional on resolution of the specialized-jurisdiction question**. The remaining interest-of-justice determination is an application of law to these supported circumstances, not a missing litigant election or an undiscovered default.

If a proposed rationale depends on a particular notice date, a specific tolling motion, formal authentication of the notice, or a finding of intentional jurisdictional abuse, that added proposition requires the actual notice/docket or record support for the alleged conduct. None is necessary merely to recognize the admitted timely appeal. This validation makes no claim that every page of the lodged record was inspected or that no further argument exists anywhere in it.

Sources reviewed for this narrow question: the control extract; the petition's timeliness and proceedings passages and reproduced district orders; the petitioner merits brief's repeated admission, proceedings account, final argument and conclusion; the respondent merits brief's introduction, proceedings account, jurisdiction defense and conclusion; and the then-operative statutory text. The petitioner merits brief identifies its lower appellate submissions as lodged pages L203–245, with transfer discussion at L216 and L228–234. Those citations identify where additional original advocacy can be checked if a proposed rationale needs it; they are not falsely represented as separately inspected here.

```text
This is a case about an effort to skirt the unifying 
jurisdictional reach of the United States Court of Appeals for 
the Federal Circuit. Faced with respondent’s patent and 
trade dress complaint pending before the United States 
International Trade Commission, petitioner filed a 
declaratory judgment action in federal district court solely on

case. See Aerojet-General Corp. v. Machine Tool Works, 
Oerlikon-Buehrle Ltd, 895 F.2d 736 (Fed. Cir. 1990). In 
doing so, the court acted consistently with both the letter of 
the relevant jurisdictional statutes and with Congress’ 
salutary intent to bring about greater uniformity in the body 
of patent law and curtail the then-growing sport of patent 
law forum-shopping. 


Petitioner now wants to resurrect that long- 
discredited practice and, in the process, undo over a decade 
of settled procedure in the Federal Circuit. Nothing has 
changed since the en banc Federal Circuit, speaking 
unanimously through the voice of then-Chief Judge Howard 
Markey, thoughtfully examined this Court’s decision in 
Christianson v. Colt Indus. Operating Corp., 486 U.S. 800 
(1988). In his Aerojet opinion, Chief Judge Markey 
obediently examined this Court's opinion, delivered by 
Justice Brennan, and carefully distinguished from the 
Christianson setting the circumstance where, as here, the 
jurisdictional issue arises in connection with a compulsory 
counterclaim. That marked difference in contexts, 
particularly in the present setting of a declaratory judgment 
action, makes all the difference in the world. 

Not only is the Aerojet rule well-settled in Federal 
Circuit practice and administration, the en banc court 


l 


emphasized that its pre-Christianson analytic approach 
dovetailed with this Court’s eventual decision in Christianson 
itself. This consistent interpretation of the relevant
```

### Section 1631, 2000 Code

```text
§ 1631. Transfer to cure want of jurisdiction 
Whenever a civil action is filed in a court as 
defined in section 610 of this title or an appeal, 
including a petition for review of administrative 
action, is noticed for or filed with such a court 
and that court finds that there is a want of ju-
risdiction, the court shall, if it is in the interest 
of justice, transfer such action or appeal to any 
other such court in which the action or appeal 
could have been brought at the time it was filed 
or noticed, and the action or appeal shall pro-
ceed as if it had been filed in or noticed for the 
court to which it is transferred on the date upon 
which it was actually filed in or noticed for the 
court from which it is transferred.
```
