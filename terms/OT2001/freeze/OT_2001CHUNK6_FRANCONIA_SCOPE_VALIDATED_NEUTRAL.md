## Corrected affirmative framing

The case presents two questions about first accrual under the six-year period in 28 U.S.C. §2501, but those questions do **not** attach identically to both reviewed judgments. Contract accrual concerns the appealed pre-December 21, 1979 contract claims in both Franconia and Grass Valley. Takings accrual concerns the Franconia takings dismissals affirmed on February 15, 2001. Grass Valley's May 17, 2001 appellate judgment affirmed its April 12, 2000 dismissal of specified pre-1979 **contract** claims; it did not affirm a takings dismissal. Grass Valley's separate takings ruling was issued on January 2, 2002, after that appellate judgment and the September 10, 2001 petition.

This document corrects the scope and dates in [the earlier neutral preparation](OT_2001CHUNK6_FRANCONIA_VALIDATED_NEUTRAL.md), particularly §§1 and 6. Its contract, statutory and distinct-takings analyses remain available within these component boundaries. Addressing both accrual questions does not supply a second reviewed takings judgment in Grass Valley.

## Questions, assumptions and live disagreements

**Contract accrual, both groups.** The question is whether ELIHPA completed the alleged breach when enacted or repudiated a future duty to accept prepayment, leaving the distinction between repudiation, an effective election and actual nonperformance material to accrual. Petitioners invoke ordinary anticipatory-repudiation principles; the United States characterizes the option as continuously available and immediately impaired. The United States accepts the alleged unrestricted-prepayment promise for this accrual review while preserving ultimate contract defenses. That assumption establishes neither actual authorized formation, every contract's terms, ultimate breach nor damages. No common tender, refusal or election date is established for all borrowers.

**Takings accrual, Franconia.** The second question challenges the treatment of an alleged as-applied taking of real-property interests as an immediate appropriation of the contractual prepayment option. Petitioners rely on the statute's remaining routes to prepayment, an eventual agency decision or futility, and the difference between enactment and concrete application. The United States defends enactment accrual for the asserted contract interest and disputes a separate, sufficiently final regulatory claim. The property, governmental act and completed injury must be identified on their own terms. Contract accrual does not mechanically answer constitutional accrual. No taking, universal finality or compensation amount is established by the limitations posture.

**Statutory conditions.** The full original 1988 text and distinct qualifications are preserved in [the statutory source extract](OT_2001CHUNK6_FRANCONIA_SOURCE_EXTRACT.md). ELIHPA does not prohibit every prepayment. Its negotiation duty, conditional offer-for-sale process, discretion after 180 days without a qualified offer when funds are available, paragraph (5)(G)'s separate routes and paragraph (5)(F)'s distinct budget exception cannot be collapsed into categorical refusal. A lower opinion's statutory shorthand does not replace the text. No petitioner is assumed to satisfy an exception merely because it exists.

**Relief and reach.** Petitioners seek money under the Tucker Act, not invalidation of ELIHPA or automatic acceptance of every prepayment. A judicial resolution must identify the judgment component it addresses: contract in each reviewed judgment, and takings in Franconia. The possible effect of a legal ruling on the later Grass Valley proceedings differs from direct appellate disposition of the January 2 ruling. No outcome is selected here.

## Petition questions, in petitioners' advocacy wording

```text
QUESTIONS PRESENTED 


This case presents the question of when the six-year 
statute of limitations set forth in the Tucker Act, 28 U.S.C. 
§ 2501, begins to run where the federal government, by 
statute, attempts to modify its contracts as it did in the 
Winstar and Mobil cases. The specific issues presented are: 


1. Whether the government can eliminate contract 
rights ipse dixit through the enactment of legislation 
aimed at relieving the government of its contractual 
obligations, so that an aggrieved party’s contract claim 
accrues immediately when a statute is passed 
announcing that the government might not perform its 
future contract obligations, rather than at the later date 
when the government actually fails to perform? 


2. Whether the “as-applied” taking claims of 
property owners participating in a government housing 
program accrued immediately upon the enactment of 
legislation that expressly left open the possibility that the 
owners still would be permitted to leave the program 
and use their properties as they desired, rather than at 
the later date when the government applied the 
legislation to render a decision on the permitted use of 


the properties?
```

### Petitioners' merits brief p. 15 — different trial-court scope

```text
Following Franconia, the Court of Federal Claims issued 
a nearly identical decision on the contract claims of the 
petitioners in Grass Valley on April 12, 2000. The Grass Valley 
court agreed that the only performance due by the 
government under the prepayment clause was “to keep its 
promise to allow borrowers” to prepay at any time. App. 56; 
see also id. at 55 (rejecting argument that the contracts 
obligated the government “to accept prepayment”). 
However, unlike the Franconia court, the Grass Valley court 
dismissed only those claims on which the government had 
moved, i.e., petitioners’ contract claims.
```
