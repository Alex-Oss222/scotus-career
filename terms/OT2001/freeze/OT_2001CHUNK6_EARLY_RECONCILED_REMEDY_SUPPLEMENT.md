## Reconciled remedy and governing law

**All eight Associates support vacating the Federal Circuit's June 5, 2001 judgment and remanding to that court with instructions to transfer the appeal to the Tenth Circuit under 28 U.S.C. §1631.** The instruction is definite. It is not an open remand to investigate supported timeliness, search for a regional forum or reconsider transfer without a governing decision.

Section 1631 requires transfer when the transferor lacks jurisdiction, the receiving court could have heard the appeal when originally noticed, and transfer serves the interest of justice. The first predicate follows from the eight Associates' already reconciled conclusions, on their respective jurisdictional grounds. The second follows from the actual Rule 54(b) final judgment, regional statutes and positive admission of timely notice. Party agreement does not create subject-matter jurisdiction; the admission supplies a filing fact to which the statutory forum rule applies.

The interest of justice supports preserving this timely attempt at review in the proper court. The erroneous destination had substantial support in existing circuit precedent; that support explains the forum error without creating jurisdiction by reliance or estoppel. Transfer corrects the destination and avoids forfeiting the timely appeal. Holmes's request for Tenth Circuit proceedings confirms the remedy's presentation and practical availability. The strongest competing argument is that alleged manipulation, expense and disruption warrant dismissal or an initial transfer evaluation below. The record supplies competing accusations, not a supported finding that the notice was knowingly baseless or abusive. Given the established timely appeal, available destination and genuine dispute over the allocation rule, a further conditional return would postpone a legal application whose material premises are now supported. This is a case-specific interest-of-justice conclusion, not a universal right to transfer every misdirected or timely appeal.

Christianson supplies the prior procedural example of vacating an appellate merits judgment entered without jurisdiction and directing transfer to the lawful circuit. It does not authorize the wrong circuit to decide merits for efficiency. The mandate directs the Federal Circuit's transfer action; it does not treat the Supreme Court as a §610 transferor. Section 1631 then requires the appeal to proceed as though noticed in the Tenth Circuit on the original notice date.

The transferred appeal remains for lawful merits review under operative law and the ordinary preserved issues. Transfer is neither affirmance nor reversal of the district court's first-claim merits judgment for the June 12 order's conditions. The patent counterclaim remains stayed; the conditional permanent injunction, bond discharge, with-prejudice dismissals and reinstatement-on-reversal provision await their respective actual triggers. No automatic ITC reinstatement or resolution of collateral estoppel, TrafFix's effect or trade-dress rights follows.
