## Refined remedy conclusion

Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg would vacate the Federal Circuit's judgment and remand to that court **with instructions to transfer the appeal to the Tenth Circuit under §1631**. The instruction is definite, rather than an open return to investigate timeliness, identify a forum or make an unbounded transfer choice. Their different jurisdictional grounds remain exactly as frozen: six adopt complaint-based counterclaim exclusion; Stevens supports vacatur on the present nonpatent Rule 54(b) adjudication without adopting that categorical exclusion.

The statutory predicates are satisfied on those commitments and the supplemented record. Want of Federal Circuit jurisdiction follows from each supporter's frozen legal ground. The Tenth Circuit could have heard the timely appeal from this Kansas final judgment at the original notice time. Transfer is in the interest of justice because it preserves that supported timely attempt at review while correcting its destination, and the chosen forum had a substantial existing jurisdictional basis in the Federal Circuit's Aerojet practice and Xeta's treatment of a patent counterclaim. Those authorities do not create jurisdiction by reliance, but they make dismissal of the timely appeal an unwarranted consequence of the forum error on this record. Holmes's express request for regional proceedings further confirms that this is a presented and workable remedy; its earlier alternative request for dismissal is not falsely treated as an unconditional stipulation to transfer.

The opposing argument is that alleged forum manipulation, expense and disruption warrant dismissal or an initial discretionary evaluation below. The parties' competing strategic accusations establish their advocacy, not a finding that the notice was knowingly baseless or filed in bad faith. The documented disagreement over the allocation rule, timely notice and available forum are sufficient to decide the statutory interest of justice without adjudicating those accusations. A further conditional remand would postpone an application whose material predicates are now supported. Christianson's direct instruction to transfer an improperly allocated appeal supplies the procedural model, while §1631's own conditions supply the legal rule. No general rule makes every jurisdictional mistake excusable or every timely appeal transferable.

Section 1631 makes transfer mandatory when its jurisdictional, available-forum and interest-of-justice conditions are satisfied. The appeal will proceed in the Tenth Circuit as though noticed there on the original notice date. The Supreme Court's contemplated mandate directs the Federal Circuit's action; it does not treat the Supreme Court as a §610 transferor. The Tenth Circuit then considers the appeal under the governing law and ordinary preserved issues. Transfer does not itself decide collateral estoppel, the effect of the operative TrafFix rule or the trade-dress merits.

Breyer maintains his frozen opposition to vacatur and transfer. Under his action-inclusive reading, the viable patent counterclaim supplies partial §1338 jurisdiction and §1295 assigns this appeal exclusively to the Federal Circuit. The new proof of timely notice and Rule 54(b) finality does not remove that grant or supply the want of jurisdiction §1631 requires. He would reject the jurisdictional challenge and leave the Federal Circuit's existing remand effective within the reviewed questions. No conditional eighth vote for transfer is inferred from his acceptance of transfer as a lawful remedy in a different jurisdictional setting.
