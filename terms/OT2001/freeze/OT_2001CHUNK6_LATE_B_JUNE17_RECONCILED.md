## Barnes v. Gorman

### Reconciled outcome and component rules

**Eight Associates now support reversal of the Eighth Circuit's punitive component.** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter and Thomas retain the incorporated-remedy rule excluding punitive damages under Title VI and therefore section 504 and Title II. Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer now support reversal solely on the narrow municipal-defendant ground, reject joining the five's general statutory exclusion and do not adopt a general punitive-availability holding as necessary to this judgment. Compensation and the unreviewed liability determination survive under all eight positions. No reinstatement of the punitive award, punitive culpability trial or amount review remains required under either sufficient route.

**S — five-Associate statutory rule, unchanged:** The Title VI remedy incorporated by section 794a(a)(2) and section 12133 does not authorize punitive damages in these private actions. Appropriate compensatory and prospective remedies remain. Notice of the underlying nondiscrimination condition and responsibility to repair its intentional breach does not supply notice of exceptional punitive liability. Contractual remedial expectations inform that specific distinction without turning all statutory civil-rights actions into literal contract suits or importing a comprehensive contract-only remedy code. Title II's nonfunding coverage remains distinct; the remedial consequence follows from Congress's express incorporation, not an assertion that every Title II defendant accepted a federal grant. Section 794a(a)(1)'s different federal-employment route and the 1991 employment punitive provisions do not directly control these claims.

**M — three-Associate municipal rule, corrected:** The reviewed official-capacity defendants are not subject to punitive damages under the established municipal-immunity principle reflected in Newport absent clear congressional displacement of that governmental limitation. The incorporated disability remedies supply no such displacement here. This defendant-specific limit disposes of the award without deciding the punitive remedies of private recipients. It leaves the compensated plaintiff's actual loss distinct from punishment ultimately borne by the public entity. It is not sovereign immunity, a reversal of the arm-of-State ruling, or an immunity of every individual wrongdoer from punishment.

### Why the three initial affirmance commitments do not survive reconciliation

The neutral packet correctly establishes that Newport was neither raised nor decided below and that the previous arm-of-State contention was different. The independent handoff treated that nonpreservation as the reason for all eight to decide general statutory availability, and it consequently gave Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer affirmance votes. Historical Stevens, joined by Ginsburg and Breyer, expressly acknowledged exactly the same nonpreservation and nevertheless selected municipal immunity as the narrowest appropriate disposition. The historical Court's footnote 4 confirms the preservation objection; it does not contradict the three's knowledge of it. Thus there is no changed record or procedural premise supporting the provisional departure. Franklin, Kolstad, Davis and Great-West support the three's objection to a general contract-based restriction, but none abolishes Newport or turns those officials' award into a private-recipient award. The changed simulated attribution and remedy precedents therefore do not require affirmance of this award.

The strongest objection to correction is Stevens's own join in Brennan's Newport dissent, which insisted on the force of Rule 51 and rejected novelty as an excuse for failing to object. That source was read and is not misrepresented as his support for Newport's original merits ruling. The present use is of an established municipal-immunity precedent, on an existing preserved punitive-remedy question and official-capacity record, to avoid deciding the broader private-recipient remedial issue. No new evidentiary finding or retrial is needed to apply the narrower defendant limitation. The conclusion does not announce a general exception to preservation or treat municipal status alone as jurisdictional. The exact-case historical writing supplies strong evidence that Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer regard this confined choice as permissible notwithstanding the acknowledged default; the earlier procedural dissent supplies a serious caution, not a materially changed present premise.

Newport's majority itself reached a defaulted question only on its particular procedural circumstances; its lower court had addressed the merits, unlike Barnes's lower courts. This distinction is retained. Its 453 U.S. 255–257 procedural holding is not falsely cited as automatically authorizing the present reach. Its 258–271 municipal principle supplies the legal ground; the three's expressly confined reach is a distinct adjudicative choice under the current packet's already-listed municipal alternative. The ordinary discretion to decline a new ground does not make the opposite choice impossible. The independent handoff expressly permitted reconciliation to identify this Justice-specific choice. No additional neutral fact is adopted, and the original commitments are not overwritten.

## Watchtower Bible & Tract Society v. Village of Stratton

### Reconciled result and operative limits

All eight retain reversal and remand against the advance personal-registration/permit prerequisite for the reviewed ordinary noncommercial religious and political advocacy and handbill distribution. Seven retain the common sufficient explanation: even using the Village's less demanding content-neutral framework, the objective burdens on anonymous and spontaneous advocacy are insufficiently fitted to the asserted fraud, crime and privacy interests. Thomas retains direct anonymous-speech protection and strict scrutiny as his own sufficient framework, while agreeing the law also fails the lesser fit inquiry. His alternative agreement adds no full methodology join.

The common opinion must concern actual prior registration, public availability of identifying information and official permission. It must not invent a personal name printed on the permit, a fee, arbitrary denial, an actual revocation, the discarded 5 p.m. restriction, or absence of commercial-fraud evidence. Section 116.04's carry/display requirement is dependent enforcement of the invalid prerequisite within the reviewed class. The actual form lacks a separate personal-name field; historical footnote 9 did not resolve the permit-face dispute and did not make the constitutional judgment depend on it. The complete neutral and primary source already establish the narrower fact used here, so no historical fact extraction is needed.

Sections 116.01 and 116.03's broad cause language, the complete registration information, the actual section 116.06 grounds, routine free issuance and the lower court's narrowed administration remain as described in the original commitment. They reduce some burdens without eliminating prior identification and permission. The unchallenged resident-registration/posted-exclusion procedure in section 116.07 remains outside the invalidation. A willing-listener opportunity is not a right to enter against an owner's wishes. Fraud and trespass controls retain their own force. No ruling is made on every commercial, fundraising or financing-disclosure application, and no independent free-exercise, hybrid-rights or vagueness holding is added.

### Exact comparator test and Scalia's different join

Historically, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer joined the Court; Scalia and Thomas concurred only in the judgment. Scalia's writing identifies a concrete objection: subjective religious or patriotic refusal to comply cannot invalidate an otherwise lawful license. The historical Court's Part III used that refusal as a second independent example of the burden. The frozen common formulation does not contain that proposition. It instead identifies legal impediments objectively imposed before speech, public identifying disclosure, lost spontaneity and deficient fit even on the Village's asserted standard. These are independent burdens whether a particular speaker is willing to apply. Scalia's concurrence expressly accepted many of the historical reasons, and his prior full simulated Buckley joins, election-specific McIntyre dissent and Thomas opinion support precisely that objective distinction.

**Historical departure:** Scalia joins the confined common Watchtower explanation, although he historically joined only the judgment, because this formulation omits the historical proposition that a speaker's religious or patriotic refusal to obey an otherwise valid permit independently establishes a free-speech defect; his Buckley joins and Thomas opinion support the remaining objective burden-and-fit rule. His join must be withdrawn or reaudited if assembly restores the objectionable proposition or a general rule against all nondiscretionary permits.

This is a concrete changed rationale, not a claimed changed record or a response to private advocacy. No circulation history is invented: final compatibility with the independently frozen formulation is sufficient. It does not require a new substantive neutral input.

Thomas's separate framework follows his actual prior simulated McIntyre and Buckley writings. The historical Scalia concurrence, which Thomas joined, also rejected the subjective-objection ground and accepted much of the invalidity reasoning. Retaining Thomas's strict-scrutiny method and alternative failure-of-fit conclusion is compatible with that judgment and objection; it does not count him as joining the common lesser-scrutiny route. The source does not justify inventing a full Court join merely to match Scalia's reconciled choice.

Breyer's historical concurrence, joined by Souter and Ginsburg, separately criticized an unsupported burglary/violent-crime justification. That writing must not be expanded into a finding that the record contains no fraud or enforcement evidence: it distinguished ordinary crime from the fraud actually asserted below. The frozen common explanation acknowledges commercial scams, older residents, limited police resources and investigative benefits, then explains why those facts fail to justify licensing the whole noncommercial class. This preserves the material fit concern without requiring a broad absence-of-evidence finding. The three can join that common answer; historical separate authorship and a redundant separate writing are not imported.
