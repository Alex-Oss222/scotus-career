## Findings and limits

1. **Tennessee mitigation rebuttal:** Cozzolino supplies an actual relevance limitation; troubled-childhood explanation did not make present criminal proclivities relevant on its record. Later defense evidence could make premature evidence harmless only if it supplied real rebuttal relevance. Neither blanket admission nor blanket immunity follows. The 1987 state court's acceptance of counsel's strategy is separate from the legal scope of rebuttal. Respondent's assertion of legal error stays attributed.
2. **Invalid aggravator chronology:** Pritchett (1981) is date-eligible support for resentencing where one of two aggravators failed and reweighing could not be assumed. Cone (1984) later distinguished that situation when it applied harmless error. The review establishes those concrete rules and facts; it does not purport to exhaust every Tennessee case before April 1982 or adopt respondent's global historical assertion in footnote 7. Moore is used only as Pritchett recites it.
3. **District grounds:** Appendix page 80 contains the district court's own performance rationale; pages 80–81 add a distinct tactical-choice discussion of closing argument. Pages 78–80 also apply a prejudice analysis with Fretwell language. The mitigation record restriction rests on the court's separate default rulings. Reporting that the rationale exists does not adjudicate its correctness or resolve whether it survives the asserted errors.

The supplement's factual and procedural descriptions are attributed to their sources.
