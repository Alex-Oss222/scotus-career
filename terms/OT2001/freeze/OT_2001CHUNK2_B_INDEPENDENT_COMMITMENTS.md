## 23. National Cable & Telecommunications Assn. v. Gulf Power

### Components, alternative grounds and limits

**C1, commingled cable and Internet attachments:** Reverse the categorical absence-of-jurisdiction holding. Section 224(a)(4) identifies the attachment and provider; section 224(b) supplies regulation of covered attachments. A cable system does not cease to be a cable system merely because the attachment also carries Internet traffic. The sole-cable language in (d)(3) limits the specified formula, not the antecedent coverage definition. Coverage therefore does not require resolving Internet-service classification.

The competing construction treats (d) and (e) as an exhaustive description of covered services and regards the absence of a classification as an obstacle to regulation. Its strongest point is that the agency cannot invoke general authority to evade a mandatory rate formula. That point is accepted at its proper scope: each formula remains obligatory where its terms apply. It does not justify moving that limitation into (a)(4). The coverage construction's strongest objection is that it leaves a potentially important rate question unanswered; the neutral source establishes that the challenged questions concern jurisdiction and that the particular cable-formula rate ruling is not presented. Neither automatic approval of that rate nor a new rate adjudication is necessary to reverse the categorical exclusion.

**C2, wireless attachments:** Reverse the exclusion of qualifying wireless equipment. The telecommunications-service definition does not depend on the facilities used. The wire-use condition identifies the statutory utility and its facilities; it does not require every attached service to transmit by wire. The contrary construction's strongest argument is the surrounding wire-oriented statutory setting. It fails because it collapses distinct definitions and excludes a category that the amended provider definition includes. The holding reaches only an attachment and owner satisfying the statute; it does not cover every wireless tower.

**Common remedial boundary:** Reverse both jurisdictional rulings and return for proceedings consistent with coverage. Neither component determines a particular reasonable charge or commands a service classification. The utility exclusions, incumbent-carrier exclusion and qualifying state-regulation allocation remain intact. No new challenge to rate methodology or property coverage is invented.

## 24. Lee v. Kemna

### Competing lawful paths

**A, exceptional inadequacy:** Vacate the federal judgment insofar as it treats the continuance-rule omissions as an adequate bar to this due-process claim and remand. Rules 24.09 and 24.10 are legitimate and ordinarily enforceable. This application is inadequate because the sudden disappearance of subpoenaed witnesses who had actually attended, the substance already before the judge, the short requested delay and the judge's scheduling basis together make the additional formality an insufficient ground for withholding federal consideration. This is not an emergency exemption, a finding of compliance with state law, a rule requiring trial judges to announce every defect, or a waiver inferred from silence.

The strongest objection is that probable return, diligence, absence of a substitute and lack of procurement are not identical to materiality or earlier attendance. A sworn motion ordinarily could sharpen those matters. Path A answers at the level of this combined record and immediate request: the judge had the essential practical basis of the request and denied it for scheduling reasons; the later insistence on the omitted forms does not materially serve their purposes in those circumstances. No one disputed alibi facts into truth by giving an opening statement.

**B, adequate default:** Affirm the federal default ruling. Written and supported continuance requests serve ongoing purposes even in an urgent trial setting. Information about expected testimony and earlier attendance does not establish when witnesses will return or meet every substantive showing. A separate scheduling reason does not eliminate the appellate court's regular rule. The strongest objection is that this turns an unforeseen absence into a technical trap after the court understood the request. Path B answers that Osborne concerns a repeated presentation of an already rejected legal objection, whereas this request required practical information not established merely by counsel's account. Ford addresses a newly announced rule, not a known requirement applied to an unforeseen factual event.

**Remedy common to the issue's scope:** Path A removes this particular bar only. It decides no constitutional continuance violation, witness credibility, innocence, cause exception, new hearing entitlement or writ. The lower court's separate cause and innocence rulings are not independently reversed. Path B does not establish that every formal continuance omission is adequate in every setting. Neither path accepts the relatives' allegation that an officer directed their departure as an adjudicated fact.

## 25. Kansas v. Crane

### Entering-law status and lawful alternatives

Hendricks's controlling Ex Post Facto judgment concerned the cumulative original application. Part I was a non-dispositive discussion, not an adopted due-process holding, although Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer joined its bounded rejection of a diagnostic-vocabulary objection; Ginsburg reserved the subject. Seling preserves demanding proof of actual punitive operation and does not transfer Hendricks's findings. Foucha forbids the particular psychiatric restraint after its admitted illness predicate ended and reserves materially narrower preventive regimes. None already decides whether this grant requires an independent control determination.

**A, serious functional impairment:** Vacate and remand. Complete inability to control behavior is not constitutionally required. A qualifying present condition must cause serious difficulty controlling dangerous conduct, sufficiently limiting the class beyond ordinary dangerous recidivists. Nature and severity of the condition and the record of impaired control matter; diagnosis and predicted danger alone do not suffice. No exact numerical control measure, universal clinical label, involuntariness of every act or one prescribed jury instruction is required. Kansas's reasonable-doubt burden is preserved. The lower court must apply the proper standard; the trial commitment is not automatically reinstated.

The strongest objection is that this adds an imprecise constitutional element to a statute already requiring causal abnormality and dangerousness under a protective burden. The response is that proof intensity cannot supply a missing substantive limit: without substantial control impairment, a diagnosis and a risk prediction may cover ordinary repeat offending. The rule addresses the functional distinction, not a demand for a clinician to measure free will.

**B, no freestanding additional control element:** Reverse the complete-incapacity requirement and return for disposition under the statute's qualifying-condition and causally connected dangerousness requirements. The Constitution does not require a separate control finding beyond those substantive predicates. Hendricks Part I supports actual limitation by a serious recognized condition but does not itself require an added element. This path does not endorse a diagnosis-only rule or dispense with genuine continuing statutory predicates.

Its strongest objection is that a legislative diagnosis may simply redescribe dangerousness. Path B answers through the statute's qualifying abnormality/disorder and causal danger requirements, independently established under Kansas's burden, rather than a separate judicially created volitional element. It recognizes Foucha's different ended-predicate problem and obeys the Court's distinct punitive-operation holdings.

**C, complete incapacity:** The strongest basis is the need for a sharp distinction between ordinary criminal responsibility and civil restraint. Every Associate rejects that categorical measure: it is not demanded by any controlling prior holding, and it would exclude serious disorders merely because a person retains some choice. A clear constitutional limit need not mean total inability. No disposition rests on presumed later treatment, a new punitive-operation claim or a transfer of another detainee's facts.
