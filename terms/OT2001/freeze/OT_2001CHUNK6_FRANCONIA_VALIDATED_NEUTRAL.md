## 1. Both questions and the judgments reviewed

The petition and both merits briefs present **two** accrual questions: the contract claims and the Fifth Amendment takings claims. The runtime packet's contract-only framing is incomplete. The questions concern the six-year Court of Federal Claims filing period under 28 U.S.C. §2501, not an adjudicated entitlement to damages or invalidation of the housing law.

The Court reviews the Federal Circuit's February 15, 2001 Franconia judgment, 240 F.3d 1358, No. 99-5123, and its May 17, 2001 affirmance without opinion in Grass Valley. The Franconia Court of Federal Claims opinion is 43 Fed. Cl. 702, with reconsideration at 44 Fed. Cl. 315; Grass Valley is 46 Fed. Cl. 629. The Court of Federal Claims selected **May 23, 1988**, when implementing regulations became effective, as the latest accrual date. The Federal Circuit selected **February 5, 1988**, enactment of ELIHPA. The distinction matters even though both dates precede these suits by more than six years. Do not attribute one identical accrual rationale or date to every lower decision.

Franconia was filed May 30, 1997; Grass Valley was filed September 16, 1998. The petitioners now before the Court entered their loans before December 21, 1979. Some plaintiffs with later loans remained in the lower proceedings; their inclusion in a broader caption does not make them petitioners presenting those later contracts here. The grant of January 4, 2002 is invoked under §1254(1). No case-specific nonparticipation is established; the opening roster places all nine Justices in office at argument and decision.

## 2. Actual record and assumptions of the accrual posture

The Farmers Home Administration made subsidized rural-housing loans under §§515 and 521 of the Housing Act of 1949. The documents include loan agreements, promissory notes and mortgages, with restrictions on eligible occupants, rents, returns, maintenance and financial operation. Petitioners allege that their prepayment option allowed them to repay the balance and leave those program restrictions. The exact note provision is copied in the source extract; neither the option nor its asserted consequence establishes every borrower's ultimate contract claim.

The United States expressly accepts the alleged unrestricted-prepayment undertaking **as an assumption for the accrual question** in its merits brief, page 18. That is not a concession of actual formation, authority, breach, compensable loss or an unlimited promise in the ultimate merits litigation. Conversely, requiring final proof of every undertaking before addressing accrual would change the posture on which these dismissals are defended. Hercules and Winstar govern the ultimate actual-contract inquiry without erasing that assumption.

Some petitioners allege actual requests or refusals within six years of suit; others rely on the absence of a demand-triggered breach or on futility. No universal tender, refusal or election date is established. The Perri affidavit describes a November 1993 request, completeness in March 1994 and subsequent agency resistance. The United States objects that the affidavit first appeared on reconsideration and neither lower court relied on it. Its existence is supported; its admissibility, implications and application to other borrowers are not decided here. The Government also says petitioners did not complete the statutory offer-for-sale process. Do not turn that advocacy into a universal factual finding or a conclusive futility determination.

The 1979 restrictions were made nonretroactive to these pre-December 21, 1979 loans in 1980. Temporary moratoria followed in 1986 and 1987. ELIHPA imposed the rural preservation regime in 1988. The Act's rural subtitle was not the separate HUD subtitle's temporary regime. The 1992 legislation extended the rural restrictions to additional later loans; it did not newly impose them on these pre-1979 borrowers. The United States reports that petitioners no longer challenge that temporal distinction. No renewed 1992 accrual event is assumed merely because later codified text uses December 15, 1989.

## 3. Governing text and conditions that may affect the legal theories

The [source extract](OT_2001CHUNK6_FRANCONIA_SOURCE_EXTRACT.md) supplies §2501, the original 1988 §241 text from the official Statutes at Large, and relevant underlying note text by programmatic copy. Section 2501 runs from first accrual and contains its own separate rules for river-and-harbor claims, persons under legal disability or beyond the seas, and officer-fee accounts. None of those different provisions is an established exception for these borrowers. No general discovery extension or equitable tolling theory is substituted for the presented accrual dispute.

ELIHPA is **not an absolute prohibition on all prepayment**. Its operative structure must remain visible when considering present impairment, future performance and finality:

- Section 502(c)(4) requires reasonable efforts, before acceptance of prepayment, to negotiate a binding extension of low-income use for at least twenty years from the agreement. Incentives are subject to appropriations and a determination of necessity, fair return and the least costly consistent alternative. It does not compel a borrower to accept a negotiated incentive.
- If agreement will not be reached after a reasonable period, paragraph (5)(A) generally requires an offer to a qualified nonprofit organization or public agency at fair market value, with two appraisers and a jointly selected binding third appraisal if the first two disagree. Qualifications include management ability and a binding useful-life affordability agreement; a national or regional organization is considered if no local qualified purchaser exists. If no bona fide offer appears within 180 days, the Secretary **may** accept prepayment or request refinancing; that clause applies only when funds are available for the transfer. Permission is not an unconditional duty to accept.
- Paragraph (5)(G)(i) exempts a borrower who undertakes the designated continued use, for a period no shorter than the referenced twenty-year period measured from the loan, and the subsequent offer to a qualified nonprofit or public purchaser. The separate paragraph (5)(G)(ii) route requires a finding that minority housing opportunities will not be materially affected **and either** a binding obligation preventing displacement from a change of use or increased charges **or** an adequate supply of safe, decent, affordable rental housing in the market and sufficient action to make it available to each displaced tenant. Adequate supply alone is insufficient. Neither route is a finding that any petitioner qualifies.
- Paragraph (5)(F) restricts further acceptances in a fiscal year after the 5,000-unit or available-budget limit is reached, subject to (G). Its own exception applies after fifteen months with no available budget authority following the offer/request or enactment, whichever is later; funds are allocated in offer/request order. That exception is distinct from (G) and from paragraph (4)'s negotiation duty. Transfer financing, rent support and later-transfer conditions remain in the copied statute. An exception from paragraph (5) does not by its wording eliminate paragraph (4).

The original statute's actual text controls the 1988 enactment theory. Later codified text and regulations may govern a particular subsequent request, but no borrower-specific later act or amendment is presumed. The petition's OCR omits words in the original (5)(G)(i)(I); the official text supplies the complete period qualification. Public quotations should be checked against the original scan where OCR recognition remains doubtful.

## 4. Entering law and its boundaries

The operative baseline includes effective OT2001 public law before this event. Disregard the runtime statements that no current-term dispositions are supplied and that Young remains unauthenticated. Read the exact [B standards](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK6_B.md), [B holdings](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK6_B_HOLDINGS.md), [current public law](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK6_B_CURRENT_LAW.md), and [Franconia prior-public supplement](../entering-law/OT_2001CHUNK6_B_FRANCONIA_PRIOR_PUBLIC.md). These are reading copies, not new authorities.

**Actual federal contractual undertakings and allocated regulatory risk**, presently effective July 1, 1996, retains Hercules's actual-authorized-undertaking rule and Winstar's financial-risk holding. The Tucker Act provides jurisdiction rather than an implied-in-law promise. Winstar permits enforcement of an actual monetary risk allocation while Congress remains free to legislate; the applicable contract, its qualifications and ordinary loss requirements control. It supplies no majority comprehensive unmistakability framework, no categorical compensation for adverse regulation and no automatic takings entitlement.

**Mobil Oil Exploration / Marathon Oil, June 26, 2000,** is a particularly material current precedent omitted from the original neutral packet. Its unanimous Part I recognizes the actual continuing process promise and breach on four submitted-plan leases. Its eight-Justice Parts II–V also recognize material repudiation and an **anticipatory** repudiation of the same process on a fifth lease for which no plan was submitted; they permit restitution election, subject to actual restoration and monetary limits. Urging performance does not by itself surrender the election. Stevens's separate position accepts actual submitted-plan breach while disputing total repudiation, restitution and the fifth-lease anticipatory ground. This precedent must be faced on both sides. It does not directly decide §2501 accrual, make every regulatory impairment material or supply these borrowers with restitution.

Roehm v. Horst, 178 U.S. 1 (1900), supplies ordinary anticipatory-repudiation and election principles; their application depends on what performance was promised and when due. **Withdrawal-liability collection accrual and installments**, effective December 15, 1997 under Bay Area Laundry, distinguishes an underlying liability from the later complete collection claim, and treats effective acceleration separately. Its ERISA statutory periods and mandatory demand process cannot simply be transplanted into §2501.

**TRW, November 13, 2001,** rejects adding a general discovery rule to the particular FCRA structure beyond its express exception; it is not a universal holding that every asserted claim accrues upon notice of legislation. **Young, March 4, 2002,** suspends the particular bankruptcy tax lookback during a statutory stay that actually prevents collection; it does not toll every sovereign filing condition or decide when a housing contract is breached. Accrual and a later legal inability to enforce an accrued claim remain different inquiries.

For the distinct taking theory, **Suitum, May 27, 1997,** makes a definitive land-use decision final without selling transferable rights that cannot change the permitted use. **Palazzolo, June 28, 2001,** rejects needless materially equivalent applications after a sufficiently definitive restriction but preserves the significance of a genuinely different design or available exception; its holding does not establish a taking at enactment. A finality ruling differs from injury timing and claim ownership. **Tahoe-Sierra, April 23, 2002,** preserves the contextual Penn Central inquiry and rejects an automatic complete-taking rule from isolating a temporary interval; it does not decide whether ELIHPA takes any petitioner's property. **Lujan v. G & G, April 17, 2001,** recognizes an actually available contract action as adequate process for the disputed public payment there; its due-process holding is not a categorical bar to every taking claim involving a contract.

## 5. Contract hypotheses, objections and lawful remedies

**Future-performance and election hypothesis.** The alleged obligation is to accept a permitted prepayment when the borrower elects to tender it. ELIHPA announces a refusal to render that future performance. Ordinary repudiation permits an immediate suit upon a legally effective election but does not require it; absent election, accrual waits until the performance obligation is due and breached. Mobil's fifth lease and Roehm support treating future performance separately. The strongest objection is that the bargain allegedly guaranteed a continuously exercisable option: withdrawal of that option may itself be present injury, not merely a prediction about future conduct. The opinion would need to explain why the earliest ability to sue on repudiation does not necessarily mean first accrual under §2501.

**Present-option impairment hypothesis.** The alleged right is continuing freedom to escape the program at the borrower's choice. Binding legislation presently removes that freedom, making breach complete without a request officials lack power to honor on the old terms. Section 2501's first-accrual language, fiscal repose and the Government's consent to suit are invoked against unilateral indefinite delay. The strongest response is that valid legislation may leave the monetary contract obligation intact, and a conditional performance obligation differs from a continuing warranty against statutory change. Mobil expressly recognizes a legislative anticipatory breach on an unsubmitted plan; it cannot be dismissed as addressing only completed failures to perform. The existence of statutory exceptions also precludes describing every request as legally impossible without qualification.

**Narrower contract-specific hypothesis.** The documents may promise presently available freedom, performance upon exercise, or different obligations whose breach times differ. A confined remand can identify the actual assumed claim and any legally effective election without deciding every underlying promise or damages defense. Its counterargument is that these dismissals present a common legal characterization on accepted allegations; an unexplained demand for additional facts cannot avoid that legal question.

A disposition accepting the adopted early-accrual ground sustains the affected contract dismissals without deciding that performance occurred. Rejecting that ground permits reconsideration of actual claim timing and genuinely remaining defenses; it does not declare every borrower timely, award compensation, compel acceptance of prepayment or invalidate ELIHPA. The relation between a past election, actual refusal and each filing date must be preserved. No universal accrual event is supplied from missing borrower-specific evidence.

## 6. Taking hypotheses, objections and lawful remedies

**Distinct as-applied regulatory hypothesis.** Petitioners frame a taking of their real-property interests through restrictions that prevent exit from the low-income use regime. Statutory exceptions and the agency's application may leave the actual burden unsettled at enactment. They invoke final-decision principles and contend that a claim cannot expire before it is ripe, while arguing that some further efforts would be futile. The strongest response is that the properties were voluntarily subjected to contractual restrictions; the asserted lost interest may be the prepayment contract option itself, not an independently imposed real-property restriction. A claimant must identify the property, challenged act and actual injury rather than rely on a label.

**Contract-dependent taking hypothesis.** The United States argues that if a taking of these contractual interests occurred, it happened when ELIHPA allegedly destroyed the option, on the same early date as breach. It also contends that an ordinary enforceable contract remedy answers the alleged impairment and distinguishes HUD cases involving different government relationships. The strongest response is that an as-applied regulatory burden need not have a fixed constitutional character or magnitude at enactment when meaningful release conditions remain; contract and taking accrual cannot be merged without examining the pleaded interest and claim. Neither an assumed contract nor an available damages route automatically proves every constitutional proposition.

The Government alternatively invokes lack of finality if petitioners insist on a distinct regulatory claim without completing potentially effective statutory procedures. Petitioners answer with actual agency conduct and futility. Suitum and Palazzolo require testing whether another procedure could materially change the answer, not treating every statutory step as compulsory exhaustion or every denied request as sufficient. No futility finding, exact permitted use or completed process for every property is supplied.

The taking component requires its **own disposition and reason**: sustain a supported temporal bar; remove an unsupported enactment premise and remand for actual accrual/finality and remaining issues; or adopt a genuinely presented independent ground if the record and applicable law permit it. A contract limitations reversal alone is not a takings holding. A finality disposition is not rejection of compensation on the merits. No source reviewed establishes a present taking, a compensable amount or entitlement to leave the program.
