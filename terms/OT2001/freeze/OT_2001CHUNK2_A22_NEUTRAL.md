### Issue 1: Patent recording and state-law collateral perfection

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** No controlling holding establishes a federal patent-security perfection system. O’Melveny & Myers v. FDIC cautions against creating displacement of ordinary state law merely from a generalized federal interest where the governing federal scheme does not supply that rule.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the federal text’s covered transactions and protected persons before concluding that state commercial rules are displaced. Uniformity preferences do not themselves establish preemption.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Patent Act’s recording provision, applicable state commercial perfection law, and the Bankruptcy Code’s trustee strong-arm provision govern.
* **Material Other Authority:** The recording terms concerning assignments, grants, conveyances, purchasers, and mortgagees must be read in their legal setting. A trustee’s hypothetical lien-creditor status does not automatically make it every type of protected transferee.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The federal recording rule can govern ownership transfers without occupying all collateral-security questions. State-law perfection remains subject to its own filing and priority requirements.
* **Open Point:** Whether this nonownership security interest falls within the federal recording rule and whether the trustee has the kind of competing status that rule protects.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below:** The courts treated the security interest as outside the Patent Act’s ownership-recording requirement and applied ordinary state perfection law against the trustee. **Trustee:** A national patent right and the recording provision’s references to conveyances or mortgagees support a federal notice system. Allowing only state filing could leave interested parties unable to find encumbrances in the patent records. **Creditors:** Congress specified particular ownership transactions and protected transferees, not a comprehensive filing regime for every nonownership security interest. The trustee’s lien-creditor power does not create the status of a subsequent patent purchaser. State law supplies a workable commercial perfection system without changing patent validity or ownership. **Limits:** Administrative convenience cannot expand the federal statute, but neither can the state-law label control if the instrument actually conveys ownership. The decision must remain tied to the rights granted and the competing claimant’s actual statutory position.
