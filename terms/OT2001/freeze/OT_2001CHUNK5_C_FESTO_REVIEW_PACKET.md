## 2. Material facts and lower proceedings

The Stoll and Carroll patents concern magnetic rodless cylinder assemblies. A piston inside a cylinder moves under fluid pressure; magnetic coupling causes a sleeve outside the cylinder to follow it and convey articles. The asserted claims and SMC's designs differ at specific limitations.

| Subject | Supported prosecution or claim history | Accused variation and legal significance |
|---|---|---|
| Stoll Claim 1 | The original claim described sealing means at each end and cylindrical drive-magnet assemblies. The examiner rejected the claims under 35 U.S.C. §112 because operation was unclear. The amendment recited the sealing-ring structure and a sleeve of magnetizable material. The applicant's accompanying remarks also distinguished potentially pertinent prior art by the sealing-ring structure. | SMC uses a single two-way sealing ring with a lip on each side and an outer sleeve portion of nonmagnetizable aluminum alloy. The reason for adding each limitation and the relevance of that reason to each asserted equivalent require separate analysis. A §112 rejection does not establish that every added limitation had one identical purpose. |
| Carroll Claim 9 | Original Claim 1 did not recite sealing rings, although the written description disclosed them. During reexamination initiated in light of a German prior-art reference, the applicant canceled Claim 1 and added Claim 9, expressly reciting a sealing-ring structure said to distinguish the prior art. | The asserted equivalent is SMC's single two-way ring compared with the claim's pair of rings, each having a lip on one side. The existence of a change is distinct from whether the particular terms narrowed relevant subject matter and from what scope was relinquished. |

The United States reports that the district court granted summary judgment of equivalents infringement on the Carroll patent because SMC did not produce material evidence rebutting Festo's expert testimony concerning the rings. It reports that SMC did not argue prosecution-history estoppel against the Carroll patent at that stage. SMC did assert that defense against Stoll; the district court rejected it, and a jury returned a special verdict of Stoll infringement by equivalents.

The en banc court held that narrowing for any statutory patentability requirement can trigger estoppel, that voluntary amendments are treated like amendments responding to rejection, and that a triggering amendment completely bars equivalents for the amended limitation. It also applied a complete bar to unexplained amendments. The court treated the relevant amendments to both patents as narrowing and found no established nonpatentability explanation; it consequently reversed the infringement judgment. It did not decide its separate question whether infringement would violate the rule that each claim element remains material. Several judges disputed the complete bar; no unsupported reconstruction of the separate opinions' additional grounds is made.

The reported failure to assert Carroll estoppel below is a distinct preservation question. This packet does not find waiver, excuse it, or infer the contents of an unprovided filing. If a proposed disposition depends on that question, obtain the pertinent procedural record through clean preflight before resolving it. The appellate rule under review and the treatment of the two patents must not be merged merely because both appear in one judgment.

## 3. Questions and operative distinctions

1. Does a claim change actually narrow the pertinent claim, rather than merely alter its words, and is that narrowing related to patentability? Is narrowing to satisfy disclosure or another statutory requirement included? How should a voluntary amendment be treated?
2. Where the reason for required narrowing is unexplained, what explanatory burden and presumption already follow from controlling Warner-Jenkinson?
3. Once estoppel is triggered, what subject matter is surrendered: all equivalents at the affected limitation, the surrender established by the prosecution record, or a presumptive range subject to a defined showing?
4. What application and further proceedings follow for each patent and asserted limitation, including any properly presented preservation issue?

The presumption about an unexplained amendment's patentability-related reason is not the same as the United States' proposed presumption that a qualifying narrowing preserved no range of equivalents. A ruling on one does not silently adopt the other. Neither a patentability reason nor a presumption of surrender establishes that SMC's particular substitution was foreseeable, surrendered, technically equivalent or infringing.

## 4. Law entering the event

The authorized reading copy is [C entering law](OT_2001CHUNK5_C_ENTERING_LAW.md), particularly the register entry **Element-specific patent equivalents and prosecution-history surrender** and the Holdings entry **Warner-Jenkinson Co. v. Hilton Davis Chemical Co.** Those texts control over the government's account of the historical decision.

**Present operation effective:** Warner-Jenkinson, March 3, 1997. Its unanimous element-specific rule preserves equivalents under the 1952 Act, requires equivalence for each material claim element in its technical and patent context, and does not permit overall similarity to erase a limitation. Function, way and result may assist the substantial-difference inquiry without becoming an exclusive formula. Eight Associates also joined the rule that bad intent is not an element; no bad-intent requirement may be inferred here.

Warner-Jenkinson unanimously bars recapture of subject matter surrendered by narrowing to satisfy a patentability objection. When required narrowing lacks a disclosed legally sufficient reason, the patentee seeking an equivalent bears the explanatory burden. After fair consideration, inadequate explanation permits a rebuttable presumption of a patentability-related reason and estoppel at the affected limitation. A supported nonpatentability explanation must be considered. Older prosecution records must be assessed in context: nonexistent contemporaneous formulations cannot be demanded, and silence is not misconduct. This qualification supplies no automatic hearing, reopening or repeated evidentiary opportunity.

The judge determines claim meaning and legal surrender boundaries before unresolved infringement facts can establish liability. Genuine factual equivalence disputes that remain legally available retain their ordinary jury treatment. Warner-Jenkinson leaves the complete allocation of equivalents questions and appellate standards open. It does not conclusively extinguish all equivalents upon every amendment and creates no universal bar against foreseeable but unclaimed substitutes absent narrowing.

The statutory setting is the Patent Act's claim and patentability requirements, including the §112 rejection recited for Stoll. The clean source provides the rejection's asserted basis; it is not a verbatim statutory-text source for every requirement. Any proposed construction that depends on an unprovided statutory phrase requires an appropriate date-eligible legal excerpt before adoption.

## 5. Supported positions and strongest alternatives

**Festo:** A complete bar substitutes a categorical loss of all equivalents for examination of what the applicant surrendered. A change addressing a particular defect need not relinquish every nonliteral implementation of the added limitation. The controlling simulated decision preserves equivalents and contextual consideration of older prosecution records. This position still must give operative effect to actual surrender, the materiality of each limitation and the patentee's explanatory burden; it cannot rest on overall device similarity or an invented examiner purpose.

**SMC:** The applicant controls the proposed claims and receives the patent after making limiting changes. Competitors must be able to rely on those concessions without an indefinitely flexible reconstruction of purpose. Patentability includes statutory requirements other than novelty, so a disclosure-related narrowing is not necessarily outside estoppel. That position must separately justify why an amendment is genuinely narrowing and why a particular scope of surrender follows; public-notice concerns alone do not establish the facts concerning either amendment or either accused variation.

These party formulations rest on the authorized neutral section and are legal positions, not a certification that additional particular language appeared in either party's unprovided merits brief.

**United States:** The clean source supports a distinct proposal. First determine, in the context of the alleged infringement, whether the particular amendment actually narrows the relevant claim and surrenders relevant subject matter for patentability. If it does, presume that the narrowing preserved no range of equivalents. Permit the patentee to overcome that presumption through a concrete basis in the prosecution record, relevant to the specific infringement dispute, for extending the amended limitation beyond its literal terms. The government rejects both a complete bar and the former unrestricted flexible approach. It requests vacatur, then remand through the court of appeals to the district court to determine whether the claims were narrowed in the relevant way and, if so, whether Festo satisfies the proposed burden.

The supplied source verifies that general rebuttal proposal. It does not supply a closed list of exceptions. Unforeseeability of a substitute, the relationship between the reason for narrowing and the accused variation, or limitations of available claim language may be explored as analytical possibilities under the proposal. They are not facts found here, an adopted test, a verified exhaustive government list or a requirement to use a particular three-part formula. The source excerpt does not establish when either accused substitute became available.

## 6. Lawful decisional and remedial paths

- The Court may retain a surrender inquiry tied to the particular amendment and prosecution record, correcting a rule that automatically extinguishes all equivalents. The current burden concerning unexplained narrowing remains unless expressly changed.
- The Court may adopt an intermediate presumption about surrender after separately establishing a qualifying narrowing. It would need to state the burden, permissible rebuttal, scope and treatment of older prosecution clearly, and explain how this rule relates to Warner-Jenkinson's existing presumption about the reason for an amendment.
- The Court may consider the complete-bar position on its own grounds. That path requires express reconciliation with the controlling simulated limits on automatic estoppel and an explanation for any doctrinal change. It cannot be treated as already commanded by the historical account of Warner-Jenkinson in a litigant's brief.
- A preserved threshold or amendment-specific ground may warrant narrower treatment of one patent or limitation. No such ground is presumed established merely to avoid the central question. If additional record support is material, that path returns through clean preflight.

Rejecting a complete bar does not by itself restore an infringement judgment, establish technical equivalence or award damages. A remand must identify the legal inquiry left for each amendment and patent. Existing findings have their actual procedural status, subject to any valid preservation issue and the legal boundaries the Court adopts. No new evidence, hearing or repeated opportunity is automatic under Warner-Jenkinson. Conversely, retaining a complete bar would foreclose equivalents only for claims and limitations properly subject to it; the packet does not silently extinguish unrelated patent scope.
