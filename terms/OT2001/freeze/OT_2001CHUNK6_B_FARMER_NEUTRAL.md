## Validated record, concession and burdens below

The official Fourth Circuit opinion, 274 F.3d 800, has been read in full. It confirms civil seizures beginning July 16, 1998; a claim and $5,000 cost bond; no civil forfeiture action before the May 2, 2000 indictment; the July 21 hearing request and August 10, 2000 summary denial. The magistrate found effective indigency from Farmer’s affidavit and sworn answers. Counsel’s affidavit recounted agents’ identification of legitimate merchandise; it was a supported assertion, not a final ownership finding. The government disputed Farmer’s remaining resources and relied on the seizure warrants.

Material concession below: at appellate argument the government said it did not oppose a hearing if Farmer showed inability to hire an attorney; it contested the sufficiency of his initial need showing. Do not frame the government as categorically opposing all hearings. The Fourth Circuit placed on Farmer at the limited hearing the burden to establish by a preponderance that untainted assets had been seized without probable cause and that those same assets were needed for counsel; the government could present other-resource evidence and probable cause of taint. That allocation is the lower court's ruling, not yet a Supreme Court standard. Living expenses and interest were outside the basic trial-counsel issue below. The hearing would not finally adjudicate every asset or retry all charges.

## Entering law, lawful paths and Justice-specific sources

**Process before civil forfeiture seizure of real property**, present operation effective Good, 1993-12-13, supplies the independent process principle but reserves movable property and criminal forfeiture. Kennedy authored the process holding joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg (and the Chief and Blackmun); O’Connor’s separate Mathews application, Scalia’s historical-procedure ground and Thomas’s record-specific conviction/warrant/continued-possession ground rejected pre-seizure relief on Good’s materially different facts. Breyer’s own published Dusenbery dissent, joined with Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, stresses practical access to a meaningful response; its notice setting does not itself establish this hearing rule. Those public writings are copied in B_ASSET_SOURCES.md and B_CURRENT_LAW.md. O’Connor, Scalia and Kennedy joined Monsanto’s lawful probable-cause restraint of tainted property; Stevens joined the separate constitutional objection. Monsanto expressly reserved the adversary-hearing question. The remaining Justices’ Libretti statutory-boundary positions preserve actual authority and informed process without automatically deciding this question.

Mathews supplies private interest, risk of error/probable value of additional process and government burden. One lawful path affirms a focused hearing following this substantial threshold showing of need and plausible lack of taint; it leaves the actual funds and ultimate forfeiture for lawful adjudication. Another challenges whether the initial submissions satisfy a sufficient threshold or whether an available existing procedure adequately protects access; it must address the judicial indigency finding, detailed affidavit, two-year gap and government's concession. The mere warrant cannot be transformed into a final taint determination. No right to spend proved criminal proceeds or automatic release follows from either path. The Court may retain or expressly revise the lower court's hearing burdens, but must state the choice as a new holding rather than assume it is already controlling.

### Issue 1: A meaningful opportunity to contest the basis of restraint

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** James Daniel Good requires real justification for postponing appropriate property process; its real-property pre-seizure rule does not automatically decide this post-seizure personal-property hearing. Existing due-process law calibrates process to the protected interest and actual error risk.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the interest, deprivation and usable means of correction. A threshold need showing may screen hearing requests without requiring proof obtainable only at the withheld hearing.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fifth Amendment due process; Sixth Amendment access to chosen counsel using legitimate funds; the applicable forfeiture statutes.
* **Material Other Authority:** Mathews v. Eldridge; Caplin & Drysdale; Monsanto, including its reserved adversary-hearing question.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** No right exists to spend criminal proceeds on counsel. That proposition does not establish that every seized dollar is criminal proceeds.
* **Open Point:** The threshold, timing and burden allocation for testing claimed taint where defense access is at stake.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

Farmer points to sworn evidence of financial need and legitimate assets. The government disputes both and invokes the seizure warrants. A warrant can justify an initial seizure without conclusively deciding a later request to test which assets may lawfully remain unavailable for defense.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A hearing can resolve need and probable cause of taint without ordering automatic release or a full criminal preview. Denial requires treating the existing submissions as legally insufficient. Any hearing order should specify the trigger, the parties’ burdens and the consequence for funds unsupported by a lawful basis.
