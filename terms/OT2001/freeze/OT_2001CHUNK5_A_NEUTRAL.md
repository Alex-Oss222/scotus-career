## 65. Verizon Communications Inc. v. FCC

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Verizon Communications Inc. v. FCC
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 467; Nos. 00-511, 00-555, 00-587, 00-590, and 00-602.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-13. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Telecommunications Act permits the FCC to prescribe forward-looking network-element rates based on efficient current technology and configuration, and whether it permits rules requiring compensated combinations of network elements for entrants.
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** On remand in the consolidated regulatory challenge, the Eighth Circuit sustained forward-looking costing in principle but invalidated the FCC methodology insofar as it used an efficient hypothetical network rather than the incumbent's actual network. It also invalidated additional combination rules. Incumbents and federal and entrant parties seek review of different components.
* **Relief Requested:** Incumbents seek invalidation of the methodology and assembly duties; the FCC and entrants seek restoration of the challenged rules.
* **Material Record:** The Act requires incumbent local exchange carriers to provide network elements to entrants. The FCC methodology estimates total element long-run incremental cost using the most efficient currently available telecommunications technology and lowest-cost configuration while retaining the incumbent's existing wire-center locations. It adds a reasonable allocation of forward-looking common costs. Incumbents argue that actual sunk investment and the costs of an entire replacement network cannot be recovered under that benchmark. The challenged combination rules require compensated work to assemble elements under specified feasibility and nondiscrimination conditions. This is review of a methodology and regulatory duties, not adjudication of a particular incumbent's complete rate order or an established confiscatory loss.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The prior simulated decision in AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board establishes FCC implementation authority and preserves a separate inquiry into the legality of the specific rules. Do not reopen those resolved propositions. An as-applied takings challenge requires an actual rate and financial record; this facial methodology dispute supplies no finding that every lawful rate generated by the method is confiscatory.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument occurred October 10, 2001. The merits event is May 13, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** The five consolidated historical dockets are one listed matter. Verizon Maryland and Mathias concern separate later judicial-review disputes.
* **Participation Matter:** The historical record identifies O’Connor’s nonparticipation. No separate simulated recusal determination is supplied.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** National Cable & Telecommunications Assn. v. Gulf Power Co. and New York v. FERC precede this event but have no supplied effective outcomes. Recheck them only for an actually adopted relevant agency-authority proposition.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Pricing authority and methodology

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board recognizes the FCC’s general implementation authority, including pricing rules, while leaving this methodology unresolved. It does not authorize the agency to erase the statute’s cost and reasonable-profit requirements.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify delegated authority and statutory limits independently; then assess the reasoned exercise of authority within those limits. A national rule may coexist with state implementation. Economic disagreement does not itself prove absence of delegated power.
* **Other Governing Law:** The 2001 Telecommunications Act provisions governing interconnection, network-element access, cost-based rates, reasonable profit, and judicial review; the challenged FCC regulations reproduced in the judicial record.
* **Material Other Authority:** Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council supplies the received statutory-administration framework, subject to any authenticated intervening simulated change. FPC v. Hope Natural Gas Co. informs the distinction between methodology and unconstitutional rate results.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The FCC may prescribe a national pricing methodology. The statute excludes ordinary rate-of-return cost recovery as the required benchmark. Neither proposition settles whether every forward-looking network assumption is permissible.
* **Open Point:** Whether efficient technology with existing wire centers reasonably measures the cost of supplying network elements and whether the present facial record establishes constitutional confiscation.

### Issue 2: Additional combination duties

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board preserves the prohibition on breaking apart existing combinations and the independent necessity-and-impairment inquiry. It leaves the additional combination duties open.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Functional network elements are not limited to physical pieces. An implementation rule must remain connected to the statutory access duty and its limiting conditions; competitive access does not itself authorize every requested service.
* **Other Governing Law:** The network-element and interconnection provisions and the technically feasible, compensated combination rules.
* **Material Other Authority:** The prior simulated network decision is the principal authority; no later historical telecommunications decision supplies missing simulated law.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Incumbents cannot sabotage the lease of existing combinations. Whether they must perform new assembly work is distinct.
* **Open Point:** Whether the statute permits bounded, compensated combination work necessary to make authorized access usable.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Eighth Circuit distinguished prospective costing of the incumbent’s existing network from constructing a hypothetical efficient network. It treated the latter as insufficiently tied to the statutory cost of providing the leased element. It read the combination duty more narrowly than the FCC.

**Incumbents' position.** Congress required access to their facilities, not a price based on facilities no one has built. A benchmark that excludes actual investment can discourage replacement and require uncompensated access. They also argue that supplying unbundled elements does not require assembling a competitor’s network.

**FCC and entrants' position.** Historical monopoly costs would entrench incumbent inefficiency and defeat competition. Current technology, fixed existing wire centers, shared costs, and reasonable profit together create an economic estimate rather than a fictional free network. The Act can require work needed to make the access obligation effective, especially where entrants cannot perform that work themselves and incumbents receive compensation.

**Limits and unreached matters.** The federal position must preserve statutory prerequisites for which elements are obtainable. Upholding a price methodology cannot decide every actual rate or eliminate a supported confiscation claim. Upholding assembly rules cannot demand technically impossible work or discrimination against other entrants.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may sustain the methodology while leaving actual rates subject to statutory and constitutional review, or invalidate unsupported efficient-network assumptions and return the rule to the agency. A facial constitutional ruling for incumbents would require more than the possibility of an inadequate future rate. The combination rules can be considered separately: restoration would require compensated feasible work under their actual conditions; rejection would leave existing-combination protection intact.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A statutory holding may permit forward-looking efficient-cost models anchored to the existing wire-center layout without requiring that model in every regulatory scheme. A narrower invalidation could target assumptions that lack reasoned cost support rather than mandate historical-cost reimbursement. A combination holding may recognize agency authority to require work necessary for usable access while retaining compensation, feasibility, and equal-treatment limitations. None of these paths establishes a general right to an entrant’s preferred network design.

**Uncertainty:** No carrier-specific complete rate record establishes an actual taking. The source materials must be distinguished between the original order, the prior simulated decision, and the challenged remand judgment.

## 66. Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Ashcroft v. American Civil Liberties Union
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 564; No. 00-1293.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-13. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether the Child Online Protection Act is facially unconstitutional because its definition of material harmful to minors incorporates contemporary community standards for commercial material on the World Wide Web.
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Third Circuit affirmed a preliminary injunction against enforcement, treating the community-standards feature as independently fatal. The district court had relied on additional First Amendment defects.
* **Relief Requested:** The Government seeks reversal of the community-standards basis for the injunction; the challengers seek continued protection against enforcement.
* **Material Record:** COPA regulates commercial World Wide Web communication of material harmful to minors. Its definition combines a prurient-interest inquiry, patent offensiveness measured with reference to minors, and lack of serious value for minors. It includes age-verification defenses. Speakers challenged the law before enforcement. The Web allows geographically dispersed access, making a local-community benchmark potentially restrictive for a national publisher. The record also raises technological, financial, privacy, and adult-access burdens of verification. The appellate judgment did not finally resolve every definition, defense, technological claim, or less-restrictive alternative. The requested review therefore concerns a particular rationale for an interim injunction, not a final conviction or a complete trial record.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** Facial overbreadth and chilling injury permit review of a criminal speech restriction before prosecution where the plaintiffs show the necessary exposure. The preliminary-injunction posture matters: rejecting one legal reason does not establish the statute’s validity or require dissolution of an injunction supported by another adequate ground. The Government did not ask the Court to dissolve the injunction as an automatic consequence of rejecting the community-standards rationale.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument occurred November 28, 2001. The supplied decision event is May 13, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Free Speech Coalition concerns virtual and promoted-as-child depictions under separate criminal definitions. COPA concerns commercial web material harmful to minors and the challenged community-standards component, with different elements, defenses and adult-access burdens.
* **Participation Matter:** None independently established for the simulation.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition and Thompson v. Western States Medical Center precede this event. Any actual simulated protected-speech or tailoring holdings must be checked when supplied. The same-day Alameda Books matter has no supplied effective outcome.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Community standards and national Internet speech

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union rejects broad criminal suppression of protected adult Internet speech under a poorly bounded minors-protection rule. It does not hold that every use of community standards in an Internet statute is unconstitutional.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the regulated expression, the protected adult speech burden, the statutory limiting elements, and practicable alternatives. The medium’s actual characteristics matter, but neither a new medium nor protection of minors supplies an automatic constitutional exemption.
* **Other Governing Law:** The First Amendment; COPA’s then-enacted provisions and defenses reproduced in the judicial record; preliminary-injunction and facial-overbreadth requirements.
* **Material Other Authority:** Miller v. California provides the obscenity framework from which Congress drew portions of COPA. Ginsberg v. New York concerns material harmful to minors, without deciding nationwide Web geography. Their standards must not be detached from their actual subjects.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Protected adult speech cannot be broadly reduced to what is suitable for children. The presence of a community standard does not itself establish how broadly COPA operates when its other elements and defenses apply.
* **Open Point:** Whether this feature alone justifies facial invalidation and whether a permissible construction can avoid an uncontrolled national lowest-common-denominator restriction.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Third Circuit regarded the borderless Web as making a local-community definition intolerable, because speakers could not reliably confine access to permissive communities. It treated that defect as enough to affirm without disposing of all other grounds.

**Government’s position.** Community standards appear within a narrowed commercial-Web statute with multiple elements and age-verification defenses. Geographic variation alone does not prove substantial overbreadth. The Government seeks rejection of the categorical rationale and further review of the actual statute, not a universal rule that child protection outweighs adult expression.

**Challengers' position.** A national speaker cannot avoid exposure in restrictive jurisdictions, so local standards predictably suppress lawful adult material everywhere. Verification imposes costs and anonymity burdens. COPA’s contextual elements do not necessarily solve those burdens, and less restrictive user-side measures may protect children more effectively.

**Limits and alternative grounds.** Even if community standards are not independently fatal, the full definition, age range, defenses, and alternative protections remain material. A national standard is a possible construction only if the statutory text and ordinary interpretation support it; a court cannot simply rewrite the offense.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court may reject the categorical community-standards ground and remand while retaining the injunction for consideration of the unresolved grounds. It may affirm if this feature, properly construed with the whole statute, independently establishes substantial unconstitutional reach. A merits declaration that COPA is wholly valid would exceed the reason actually reviewed and the incomplete adjudication below.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A narrow holding may establish that geographic variation alone does not facially invalidate a carefully defined Internet offense. It leaves proof of adult burdens, verification feasibility, and less restrictive means open. An affirmance could establish that a national medium combined with unavoidable exposure to restrictive local standards creates substantial protected-speech suppression under this statute. Either rule must account for the statute’s commercial scope and additional elements; neither constitutionalizes every Internet business model or makes all nationwide regulation impossible.

**Uncertainty:** The record and lower judgment do not finally establish the efficacy of all verification technologies or alternatives. Historical same-term expression decisions are not available as simulated precedent merely because their dates precede this event.

## 67. Lapides v. Board of Regents of University System of Georgia

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Lapides v. Board of Regents of University System of Georgia
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 613; No. 01-298.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-13. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether a State that voluntarily removes an action to federal court may invoke sovereign immunity against state-law claims for which it has waived immunity in its own courts.
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The district court treated Georgia’s removal as a waiver of the asserted federal-forum immunity. The Eleventh Circuit reversed that determination. Lapides seeks reversal of the appellate immunity judgment.
* **Relief Requested:** Lapides seeks rejection of the State’s immunity defense after removal; Georgia seeks preservation of that defense.
* **Material Record:** A university professor alleged that state officials placed allegations of sexual harassment in his personnel files in violation of state law and his federal rights. He sued in Georgia court, asserting state tort claims and federal civil-rights claims against the Board and individual officials. The State joined in removing the action and then asserted immunity from federal adjudication. Georgia had waived immunity from the relevant tort claims in its own courts. The asserted federal damages claim against the State also confronts the separate rule that a State is not a person under the federal civil-rights statute. Allegations against individual officials cannot simply be merged with the Board’s immunity defense. No adjudicated tort liability or damages amount follows from the removal issue.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The viable state-law claims and the State’s own litigation act provide the concrete controversy. Waiver of immunity does not create a federal cause of action or establish that an invalid federal claim can sustain original jurisdiction. If federal claims are dismissed, the district court must separately consider the lawful treatment of supplemental claims. The authority of the State’s litigation representative to remove is not a license to invent an unauthorized private waiver.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument occurred February 25, 2002. The supplied event is May 13, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate listed brief.
* **Participation Matter:** None independently established for the simulation.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Raygor v. Regents of University of Minnesota precedes this event but has no supplied effective decision. Verizon Maryland and Federal Maritime Commission are later matters and cannot govern it.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Voluntary removal and immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board rejects consent manufactured from ordinary commercial activity. Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht separates an immunity defense to a claim from removal jurisdiction over the action and leaves the waiver question open. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank and Alden v. Maine do not recognize the historical categorical Article I immunity barrier.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Consent, statutory authorization, constitutional power, forum jurisdiction, and the merits of a cause of action remain separate. Actual litigation conduct differs from an implied waiver inferred merely from participation in regulated commerce.
* **Other Governing Law:** Removal and supplemental-jurisdiction statutes; the applicable Georgia tort waiver; the federal civil-rights statute; the constitutional limits on federal adjudication.
* **Material Other Authority:** Clark v. Barnard and Gardner v. New Jersey concern voluntary invocation of federal adjudication. Will v. Michigan Department of State Police addresses the separate absence of a statutory damages defendant in a suit against the State.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The registers reject a fictional commercial waiver and prevent one immune claim from destroying jurisdiction over an otherwise removable action. They do not determine the effect of the State’s deliberate removal here.
* **Open Point:** Whether voluntary removal relinquishes a federal-forum immunity defense to otherwise permitted state tort claims, and the scope of that relinquishment.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Eleventh Circuit distinguished the State’s choice of forum from an authorized waiver of sovereign immunity. The district court treated removal followed by an immunity objection as inconsistent positions concerning the same adjudication.

**Lapides’s position.** A State cannot choose federal adjudication of claims it permits in state court and then deny the chosen tribunal authority solely because it is federal. The requested rule attaches to an actual litigation choice, not presumed consent to regulation.

**Georgia’s position.** State waiver rules determine who may surrender immunity, and a litigation representative’s removal should not enlarge substantive liability or eliminate sovereign protections beyond authorized terms. The State also points to defects in the federal claim.

**Limits and alternatives.** Lapides need not establish a universal waiver of all state immunity. Georgia’s objection to an invalid federal damages cause remains available. The Court can resolve the forum inconsistency without deciding immunity from claims Georgia has not permitted in its own courts or the personal defenses of individual officials.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would preserve Georgia’s immunity defense despite its removal. Reversal would require adjudication of the state claims without that federal-forum objection, while preserving all substantive defenses and ordinary jurisdictional decisions. The Court may dispose of the invalid State-directed federal damages claim separately; that would not automatically answer the state-law removal-waiver issue.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A waiver rule can be confined to a State’s authorized voluntary removal of claims for which it already accepts adjudication in its own courts. A broader rule covering every removed claim would require attention to claims whose substantive immunity was never surrendered and is not necessary here. A no-waiver rule would permit the State to preserve an immunity defense while using removal, but would need to explain why its voluntary invocation differs from other litigation submissions to federal adjudication.

**Uncertainty:** The effect on supplemental jurisdiction depends on which federal claims remain. The packet does not resolve the professor’s allegations or imply that waiver alone establishes a remedy.

## 68. Voting Integrity Project, Inc. v. Keisling +

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Voting Integrity Project, Inc. v. Keisling +
* **Citation or Docket:** 259 F.3d 1169 (9th Cir. 2001). No simulated Supreme Court docket or grant date is supplied.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-13. Supplied simulation placement of the lower-court addition.
* **Question Presented:** Whether federal statutes establishing a uniform federal election day preempt Oregon’s vote-by-mail system because it permits voters to cast ballots before election day, while requiring that ballots be received by election day?
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The District of Oregon entered summary judgment for the state defendants. The Ninth Circuit affirmed, rejecting the federal election-day preemption claim. Voting Integrity Project seeks reversal.
* **Relief Requested:** Voting Integrity Project seeks preemption of advance voting under Oregon’s system; state officials seek affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Oregon distributes ballots before the federal election date and permits their return before that date. Ballots must be received by election day to count. The challenged system does not choose a federal winner on an earlier day through a completed preliminary election. The claimed defect is the advance casting of ballots itself. The record concerns the statewide mail system, not a particular disputed ballot, proof of fraud, or unequal treatment of similarly situated voters. It is therefore necessary to distinguish an individual voter’s completed act from the State’s legally completed selection of an officeholder.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The statutory issue can be resolved without adjudicating free-standing constitutional attacks on mail voting. Federal election-day provisions for congressional and presidential elections must be identified separately even though the challenged advance-ballot feature is common. The case does not supply a basis to replace receipt-by-election-day with a post-election receipt rule.
* **Relevant Dates:** The Ninth Circuit decision issued July 11, 2001. The supplied simulated date is May 13, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate listed brief.
* **Participation Matter:** None independently established for the simulation.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Schrader v. Blackwell precedes this event, but concerns party labels rather than the federal election date. No supplied decision in it changes the entering date rule.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Uniform election day and preliminary voting acts

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Foster v. Love holds that a State cannot consummate the final legal selection of federal officeholders before the federally prescribed election day. Its rule does not make every preliminary electoral act unlawful.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the legally decisive selection event rather than using the label attached to a state procedure. Distinguish a completed election from nomination, balloting, counting, and other steps that do not themselves finally choose the officeholder.
* **Other Governing Law:** The then-applicable federal congressional and presidential election-day enactments, including the provisions then codified at 2 U.S.C. §§ 1 and 7 and 3 U.S.C. § 1, and Oregon’s ballot-return rules as described in the judicial record.
* **Material Other Authority:** Foster v. Love performs the principal precedential work. Historical absentee-voting practice supports the distinction between preliminary votes and the final election, but cannot override an unambiguous statutory prohibition.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** A pre-election date final selection is impermissible. Oregon’s receipt deadline and continuing election-day voting distinguish its system from a preliminary contest that can eliminate any election-day choice.
* **Open Point:** Whether the uniform-date requirement also forbids advance casting even when the electoral result is not legally consummated until election day, including application to presidential elector selection.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Ninth Circuit read the federal date requirement as governing the completed election rather than every act of voting. It distinguished the early final selection condemned in Foster v. Love from ballots returned in advance for an election completed on the federal date.

**Project’s position.** An individual makes the decisive choice when casting a ballot, and a common federal day loses force if most votes are cast earlier. Congress could have expressly authorized Oregon’s universal mail system if it intended such a departure. The requested rule treats advance voting itself as the prohibited election.

**State’s position.** Elections are processes. Ballot preparation, absentee voting, and other advance steps have long preceded the chosen date without electing an officeholder early. The system leaves the legally operative choice open until the election-day deadline. Its rule preserves Congress’s common completion date while allowing States to administer how qualified voters participate.

**Necessary limits.** The State cannot use advance voting to make an earlier result legally final or cancel the federally required election. The Project’s concerns about late developments influencing early voters do not by themselves establish statutory preemption.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would leave Oregon’s system in force against this statutory claim. Reversal would require relief directed to advance voting in federal elections, with attention to lawful implementation rather than automatic invalidation of all state elections. A constitutional judgment on access, secrecy, fraud, or equality would exceed the issue and record.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court may hold that advance ballot casting is permissible when the State does not consummate the federal election before the uniform date and timely receipt is required by that date. This covers comparable early and absentee voting without deciding post-election receipt or early final certification. The competing interpretation would require all decisive voter acts on the federal date, a rule whose implications for absentee voting cannot be avoided by calling Oregon’s system unusual.

**Uncertainty:** The inquiry assumes the described receipt and final-selection rules. Proof that a particular procedure legally fixes the winner before election day would present a different application.
