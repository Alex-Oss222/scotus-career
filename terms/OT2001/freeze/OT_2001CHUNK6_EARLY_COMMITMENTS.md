## 80. Compaq Computer Corp. v. Commissioner — May 28, 2002

### Reach, judgment components and common sufficient ground

All eight Associates would affirm the Fifth Circuit's reversal of the reviewed substantive-sham disallowance and dependent negligence penalty. They support recognition of the actual 1992 ADR transaction under the governing income, capital-loss and foreign-credit provisions within the Commissioner's stipulation. The restored reporting treatment governs the reviewed deficiency; the alternative request to deduct expenses only if the transaction is disregarded has no operative work after recognition. The penalty falls because the reviewed substantive-sham adjustment cannot supply an underpayment on this transaction, not because limited investigation is invariably reasonable or tax planning immunizes negligence.

The supported common rule is that substantive-sham scrutiny survives the existence of a tax credit, but this actual purchase, dividend entitlement and resale cannot be disregarded on the Tax Court's hybrid computation or merely because obtaining favorable tax consequences was a dominant objective. In computing pretax return, the dividend includes the amount withheld to discharge Compaq's own Netherlands tax obligation. A worldwide after-tax computation must also give the corresponding United States credit its actual statutory effect; neither computation may count a foreign tax detriment while excluding the counterpart solely to manufacture a pretax loss. The purchase, resale, charges, real dividend entitlement and market exposure together establish genuine economic content here. The narrow legal conclusion does not rest on nominal ownership or reporting income alone.

Old Colony Trust, 279 U.S. 716, 729, and Diedrich, 457 U.S. 191, 199–200, establish that payment of a taxpayer's liability by another can be income to that taxpayer. Treasury Regulation §1.901-2(f)(1) and (f)(2)(i) identify the person legally liable notwithstanding remittance or assumption by another. The established record supplies that liability and entitlement here. Using the gross dividend and actual transaction costs yields approximately $1.894 million before tax; applying the credit within the stipulated reporting treatment yields approximately $1.25 million after worldwide tax. These legal computations do not themselves prescribe a general sufficiency ratio or prove every asserted business purpose.

The actual trading features provide the additional substance: independent existing market participants, NYSE transactions, a dividend right legally acquired on the record date, market prices that could and did move, and execution and dividend risks. The promoter's coordination, rapid resale, designated counterparty and the taxpayer's shallow investigation are significant contrary facts, but they do not turn this particular economic position into the self-contained, economically empty arrangement rejected in Knetsch or the accepted absence of both economic predicates in Winn-Dixie. Minimizing unnecessary risk is not an independent disqualification. No enhanced-risk requirement is imposed.

Historical subsidiary findings receive clear-error treatment; the legal characterization and correct treatment of withholding are reviewed independently under Frank Lyon, 435 U.S. 561, 581 n.16. The conclusion does not erase the findings about the one-hour meeting, spreadsheet, absence of separate cash-flow analysis or tax objective. It rejects the proposition that those facts, evaluated through the erroneous profit premise, legally compel disregard despite the actual economic interests acquired. No finding that management undertook extensive commercial diligence is supplied.

### Alternatives and operative limits

**Recognition path and its strongest objection.** The Commissioner can fairly contend that the expected favorable result was deliberately assembled around tax attributes and that a real exchange trade or formally gross dividend can accompany a tax shelter. That objection defeats any rule of automatic recognition based on legal title or a positive line on a tax return. It does not establish that these actual rights, expected pretax return and residual market risks are meaningless. Frank Lyon requires attention to the transaction's substantive allocation of rights and duties; Winn-Dixie expressly preserves meaningful economics, enacted benefits and mixed findings. The common conclusion rests on their combined application here, not on statutory formalism alone.

**Disregard or purpose-remand path and its strongest objection.** A purpose remand would be appropriate if a decisive factual dispute remained after correcting the legal measurement, or if a legally necessary nontax objective lacked supported findings. On this record the operative economic facts are established, and the accepted adverse process facts do not independently turn this actual profit-seeking market arrangement into a substantive sham. The Associates do not select a universal conjunctive or disjunctive economic-substance test. They reject a remand whose sole task would be to impose such a new, independent subjective-purpose prerequisite on this record. Conversely, no categorical immunity follows for every transaction with a small arithmetic profit.

The Court need not accept Compaq's distinct assertion that the credit regime wholly displaces substance scrutiny: Winn-Dixie's unanimously adopted distinction between avoiding a particular prohibition and satisfying the claimed benefit's substantive predicates answers that proposed immunity. The 1997 §901(k) holding-period change does not govern this 1992 transaction and is not made an affirmative command recognizing all earlier arrangements. Independent ownership, reporting or credit-limit objections outside the Commissioner's stipulation are outside the reviewed dispute. The disposition preserves their statutory force.

## 83. Holmes Group v. Vornado — June 3, 2002

### Reach and component commitments

The question is the Federal Circuit's appellate allocation under the then-operative §§1295(a)(1) and 1338(a). Ordinary district-court federal jurisdiction exists independently. The actual June 5, 2001 appellate order vacated a Rule 54(b) trade-dress judgment and directed reconsideration in light of TrafFix; it contains no independent jurisdictional analysis. No Justice attributes the Aerojet argument to reasoning the order did not state.

Six Associates—O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg—would vacate that appellate judgment and remand for lawful allocation because the patent counterclaim does not make the nonpatent complaint an action arising under patent law for this statutory purpose. Stevens supports the same vacatur and remand on the narrower judgment-stage ground below. Breyer would reject the jurisdictional challenge and affirm the Federal Circuit's exercise of jurisdiction, leaving its existing merits-reconsideration remand effective within the questions reviewed. His vote does not decide the trade-dress merits or independently adopt the historical description of TrafFix.

The seven vacatur supporters would return the matter to the Federal Circuit for disposition under §1631, including transfer to the Tenth Circuit if the statute's available-forum, filing-time and interest-of-justice conditions are satisfied. They do not direct a finding of an unverified filing fact. The appellate merits order cannot be sustained by convenience after lack of jurisdiction is established. The jurisdictional vacatur is neither affirmance nor reversal of the district court's first-claim judgment for purposes of its June 12 conditional order. The patent counterclaim remains stayed, not withdrawn, adjudicated or automatically dismissed; the conditional permanent injunction, bond release and with-prejudice dismissals await their actual stated trigger. Ordinary district jurisdiction and the underlying trade-dress judgment's status pending lawful appellate review remain distinct.

### Competing legal paths

**Complaint-based rule.** Section 1295(a)(1) incorporates the district court's §1338 arising-under basis; Christianson, 486 U.S. 800, 808–814, requires a well-pleaded claim created by patent law or necessarily dependent on a substantial patent question. Its uniformity discussion rejects substituting the case actually litigated for the statutory jurisdictional referent. Here every claim in the complaint is nonpatent, and the coercive action corresponding to the declaratory trade-dress claim is a trade-dress suit, not a separately available patent-infringement suit. A compulsory counterclaim remains a real claim, but its independent character does not change which pleading Congress's incorporated arising-under rule examines. Rule 13 supplies a pleading obligation, not an appellate-jurisdiction amendment.

The strongest contrary argument is that an affirmative patent counterclaim gives the district court a real §1338 claim, making its jurisdiction partly patent-based in the ordinary sense of the enacted words. Cardinal Chemical, 508 U.S. 83, confirms that an independent counterclaim is not a defense, and Xeta directly adopts the broader allocation rule. The six complaint-rule supporters answer that Cardinal Chemical preserves an already established patent controversy within existing appellate jurisdiction; it did not decide whether the counterclaim alone could supply §1295's incorporated original-jurisdiction predicate. Christianson's same-language analysis is controlling Supreme Court authority after Xeta. Its reasoning is extended to this counterclaim, rather than falsely described as a prior counterclaim holding. The text's 'in whole or in part' language allows mixed actions containing an appropriate original patent claim; it need not replace the source-of-claim rule with every later affirmative pleading. The policy goal of uniformity is real but cannot alone confer jurisdiction.

The operative exclusion resolves the compulsory patent counterclaim actually presented; it is not conditioned on a finding of bad-faith pleading. No present source establishes such bad faith. The six do not require a holding that an original complaint can never be actually amended to add a patent claim. That is a different source-of-claim event, and Christianson's express constructive-amendment reservation is retained.

**Stevens's narrower rule.** The Rule 54(b) judgment adjudicates only the nonpatent first claim, with no patent claim in Holmes's complaint and no actually tried, amended or adjudicated patent cause supporting that judgment. This appeal therefore falls outside the Federal Circuit's patent allocation. Stevens would not join a categorical declaration that the first complaint, immutably viewed at filing, always controls later appellate jurisdiction or that an appeal actually adjudicating an independent patent claim could never qualify. His Christianson concurrence, 486 U.S. at 819–823, distinguishes original and appellate timing and insists on claims rather than patent issues; the current patent counterclaim has not reached the adjudicated status central to that analysis.

The strongest objection is that §1295 refers to jurisdiction in the case rather than the legal theory of the appealed judgment, and a live stayed counterclaim is still a claim. Stevens's bounded answer is that the presence of an unadjudicated separate claim cannot redirect this separately final, exclusively nonpatent adjudication; a jurisdictional basis must connect to the claim being reviewed, not merely to a possible later patent adjudication. This is a narrower proposed interpretation supported by his earlier appellate-stage analysis, not an already settled Court rule or a finding of mootness. He accepts that Cardinal Chemical prevents declaring the patent controversy dead; he does not treat staying or conditional future dismissal as its present extinction. His support for vacatur is judgment agreement with the six, not agreement at their categorical counterclaim level of generality.

**Breyer's action-inclusive rule.** A viable, nonfrivolous patent-infringement counterclaim is an affirmative claim within §1338. The civil action therefore rests in part on that provision, and §1295 allocates its appeal to the Federal Circuit even when the particular appealed order concerns another claim. Breyer's Xeta panel join, 825 F.2d 604, 607–608, squarely accepted that rule for an antitrust preliminary-injunction appeal and rejected a requirement that the appealed order contain a patent issue. The present counterclaim is pleaded and stayed; a potential future dismissal if a nonpatent judgment is affirmed does not eliminate its current existence. The supplied neutral record establishes no frivolity or mere-joinder finding supporting exclusion.

The strongest objection is Christianson's later insistence on the well-pleaded complaint and on consistency between §1338 and ordinary arising-under jurisdiction. Breyer would distinguish Christianson's nonpatent claims and patent defenses from this actual affirmative infringement cause. His own Xeta opinion acknowledged the complaint rule while treating a viable counterclaim as independently jurisdictional, so generalized complaint language does not by itself answer his direct claim/defense distinction. Cardinal Chemical's later recognition of an independent counterclaim reinforces that distinction without being misrepresented as deciding today's allocation. He rejects uniformity as a free-standing jurisdictional grant; it supports his reading of 'civil action' and 'in whole or in part.' He also rejects judgment-by-judgment allocation because the statute and his Xeta join allocate the action as a whole. The narrower permissible limit is a requirement of an actual viable claim, rather than an immaterial, inferential or frivolous patent allegation. The handoff supplies no adjudication of patent validity or infringement.

## 81. SEC v. Zandford — June 3, 2002

### Judgment, statutory connection and remedy

All eight Associates would reverse the Fourth Circuit's categorical connection ruling and remand for further proceedings under the corrected §10(b)/Rule 10b-5 rule. They would not automatically reinstate the district court's final injunction or $343,000 disgorgement solely from the wire-fraud convictions. The lower court must apply ordinary civil proof and preclusion rules to establish what the record actually proves under the legal connection rule and determine the resulting authorized relief. The conceded ability to contest the legal securities nexus survives; factual preclusion requires its own necessary-issue analysis. This is affirmative remaining liability and remedial work, not an invented right to a new hearing or unlimited discovery.

The common rule is that a broker who, with the required scienter, deceives customers to whom he owes a duty by secretly selling their entrusted securities for the contemporaneous purpose of appropriating the proceeds commits deception in connection with those sales when the liquidation is an integral means of executing that concealed scheme. The alleged sales and diversion must actually have that relationship. A fair market price, ordinary execution mechanics or discretionary trading authority does not categorically remove it. Discretion to make investment decisions without advance approval does not authorize concealed liquidation for the broker's personal appropriation. The interstate instrumentality condition and the operative Rule 10b-5 clauses remain applicable; this legal ruling does not substitute wire fraud for the securities elements.

Bankers Life, 404 U.S. 6, supplies the directly relevant principle that fair value in the sale does not defeat a fraud claim where securities sales and diversion of proceeds are components of the scheme. Chiarella, 445 U.S. 222, requires the duty that makes silence deceptive; the customer relationship supplies the pleaded duty here. Santa Fe, 430 U.S. 462, excludes fiduciary unfairness without manipulation or deception; the concealed contemporaneous purpose and deceptive exercise of entrusted sale authority distinguish this alleged scheme. O'Hagan's controlling June 25, 1997 rule links deception to the securities use that consummates disloyalty; this case concerns liquidation of the victim's own entrusted securities rather than extending its information-source holding mechanically. Wharf's unanimous May 21, 2001 holding and Kravitz's unanimous rule distinguish a knowingly false transactional undertaking from later nonperformance. Wharf's actual purchased option is not attributed to these customers.

The controlling line must distinguish a subsequent independent conversion after an honestly completed sale from sales carried out as the means of the concealed conversion plan. The mere fact that a broker possesses money or securities is insufficient. Nor does an unfulfilled investment promise, inferior management, ordinary breach, broker status or temporal proximity alone establish transaction-connected deception. That limit gives the securities connection actual work without requiring a false statement about price or a fresh customer decision for every discretionary transaction.

Section 17(a) sufficiency is outside the requested review under the SEC's express merits-brief limitation. Naftalin's §17(a) reasoning is only a possible analogy. The excessive-trading theory is not revived after its stated disclaimer. Central Bank's existing private affirmative-assistance action remains intact in this timeline but supplies neither a necessary primary-enforcement theory nor an automatic set of private reliance and damages elements for this SEC claim.

### Two nexus paths and the Justice-specific comparison

**Integrated transaction path.** The alleged ongoing promise of loyal securities management, actual liquidation undertaken to obtain proceeds for personal use and concealment from the owners identify transactional deception, not only a later failure to account. Its strongest objection is that every broker theft could be relabeled as an investment scheme. The answer is the required contemporaneous concealed purpose and actual instrumental role of the sales, together with duty and scienter. A broker's custody before sale is not dispositive where sale is the allegedly deceptive exercise of entrusted authority.

**Separate conversion path.** Zandford contends that lawful discretionary market sales and later theft are distinct; money already has value without further trading, unlike confidential information monetized through securities use. That argument would justify rejecting this nexus if the civil facts established an honest authorized liquidation followed by an independently conceived conversion. It does not support dismissal of the materially different alleged scheme merely because the broker could control assets before sale or sold them at fair value. Bankers Life and the false-undertaking principles forbid converting that possible factual separation into a categorical legal barrier. The civil record must establish the integrated scheme; it is not established automatically by the conviction label.

### Separate agency alternatives and exact limits

Independent interpretation supplies the necessary decision ground. All eight may join a statutory opinion treating the SEC's actual longstanding interpretation as corroborative persuasive support without making controlling deference indispensable. They do not jointly commit to a new Chevron or own-rule holding. The alternatives remain distinct:

1. **Delegated statutory interpretation.** Section 10(b)'s express rulemaking grant and the broker-discipline adjudication provisions identified in the briefs are meaningful evidence of delegated authority. The SEC identifies Bauer, 26 S.E.C. 770, and Southeastern Securities, 29 S.E.C. 609, as formal decisions applying Rule 10b-5 to customer misappropriation and false pretenses. Mead requires both actual authority and its exercise in the interpretation asserted. Formal adjudication is relevant, not a conclusive label. The respondent's claim that no prior case requested Chevron treatment does not establish permanent surrender of delegated authority; the SEC's litigation request alone does not prove the content and force of each earlier decision.
2. **Persuasive reasoning.** The specific described grounds are substantial: Bauer treats the false promise to execute honestly or return customer property as deception; Southeastern rejects a power of attorney as authority for personal use without full informed disclosure. Their fit with the text and this alleged scheme is the reason they persuade. Longevity or the SEC's status alone does not decide the statute. The respondent's concerns about summary reasoning and a proposed universal broker-conversion rule require the Court to assess the actual narrower interpretation rather than adopt either side's characterization whole.
3. **Own-regulation interpretation.** The surviving Thomas Jefferson University and Auer rules give controlling weight to the responsible agency's fair, considered reading unless plainly erroneous or inconsistent with the regulation, subject to actual-text, authoritative-status, consistency and actual-ground limits. They remain separate from Mead. Rule 10b-5's securities-connection language cannot enlarge the statutory domain it implements. An own-rule label does not establish authority to make conversion alone sufficient or fill missing civil findings.

## 82. United States v. Mackby — June 3, 2002

### Judgment, coverage and governing remand

All eight Associates would affirm the Ninth Circuit's determination that this combined FCA recovery is at least partly punitive and subject to the Excessive Fines Clause, preserving its remand for a developed proportionality assessment. The affirmed false-claims liability remains intact. Coverage does not determine whether the $729,454.92 demand is excessive or select a replacement amount.

The fixed $5,000 sanctions on 111 claims total $555,000; trebling the $58,151.64 overpayment produces $174,454.92, for the combined $729,454.92. The sovereign seeks that recovery for culpable false claims. Per-claim penalties operating alongside multiplied recovery serve punishment and deterrence as well as compensation. Investigation costs, delayed recovery and unmeasured harm can give multiplied damages compensatory work; they do not establish that this entire combined exaction is solely remedial. Austin's partly punitive coverage rule therefore applies despite the civil label, and Bajakajian supplies the constitutional boundary of gross disproportionality to the gravity of the offense.

The lower court must evaluate the combined monetary demand in relation to the established offense, with appropriate attention to actual culpability, harm and the statutory sanctions. It may develop the facts required for that evaluation. The actual rendering of therapy is relevant to harm; it does not erase the false physician-involvement representation that established eligibility and avoided therapist payment caps. Supported lower-court findings remain facts, not a new statutory command. The two monetary parts need not be evaluated as if the other did not exist. Their compensatory functions remain relevant rather than being deleted by the coverage conclusion.

The gross-disproportionality standard gives substantial respect to legislative punishment judgments; neither Congress's authorization nor the minimum per-claim amount conclusively answers the constitutional question. Independent appellate review governs the constitutional evaluation, with clear-error review of supported underlying factual findings. No compulsory exhaustive checklist, fixed loss multiple, government-cost ceiling or maximum lawful dollar amount is adopted. In particular, the Ninth Circuit's example asking whether this much money is required for desired deterrence is not adopted as a least-punishment-necessary test or an independent requirement that the government prove this precise amount indispensable. The proper remand applies gross disproportionality, not strict equivalence or minimum effective deterrence.

### Alternative paths and aggregate-punishment boundary

**Coverage path.** Austin, June 28, 1993, treats payment to the sovereign that is at least partly punishment as subject to the Clause, notwithstanding remedial features. Bajakajian, June 22, 1998, requires an offense-related proportionality assessment separately from statutory connection. The fixed penalties and multiplied recovery's operation together meet that coverage threshold here. Bornstein recognizes both recovery and fraud-deterrence purposes; it is not authority for treating every punitive dollar as unconstitutional.

**Solely compensatory path.** The strongest counterargument is that direct overpayment understates the government's real injury, so multiplied recovery and liquidated sums may reasonably compensate investigation, delay and systemic fraud costs. Rex Trailer supplies genuine compensatory support, and Hudson preserves distinct civil classification for Double Jeopardy. The eight Associates accept those propositions but reject the conclusion drawn for this combination: neither unquantified compensatory purposes nor a civil designation eliminates the independent punishment function of sanctions linked to culpable false claims. Hudson's separate intent/clearest-proof and retained personal-penalty inquiries cannot replace Austin's Excessive Fines threshold.

**Narrower accounting path.** The Court could identify a genuinely separate purely compensatory recovery when the statute and record support it; it cannot mechanically remove all base-loss recovery or call every excess over documented costs punitive. For this presented combination, assessing the actual cumulative demand preserves the interaction between penalty and trebling while recognizing compensatory work. Whether an isolated treble-damages award in a materially different arrangement has the same character is outside the combined-recovery question. No aggregate assessment automatically dictates a reduction of either part.
