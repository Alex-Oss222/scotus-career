### Issue 1: Authority to bind nonparties

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Richards v. Jefferson County, decided June 10, 1996, requires a constitutionally sufficient relationship before a judgment binds absent persons' rights. Common interests, vigorous advocacy, public importance, notice, and a virtual-representation label do not alone establish that relationship.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Claim preclusion prevents relitigation of a claim by a person lawfully bound to a qualifying prior judgment. Privity means a legally sufficient relationship supporting binding effect, not merely political affinity. Consent, actual control, genuine substantive relationships, and authorized representative proceedings can supply a basis, subject to due process.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fifth Amendment's due-process constraint applies to federal adjudication. Applicable federal preclusion principles govern the effect of the federal judgment. No identified statutory amendment changes the governing nonparty rule for this matter.
* **Material Other Authority:** Hansberry v. Lee, 311 U.S. 32 (1940), for the due-process requirements of genuine representation. Richards supplies the controlling rejection of a general duty to intervene.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** A public dispute does not automatically become a representative action binding all interested voters.
* **Open Point:** No new legal rule of nonparty preclusion is necessary. Whether the record establishes an authorized binding relationship is an application of the settled rule; the alternative merits ground may independently resolve the judgment.

### Issue 2: Territorial design and alleged incumbent favoritism

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Shaw v. Reno, decided June 28, 1993; Johnson v. Miller, decided June 29, 1995; and Easley v. Cromartie, decided April 18, 2001, reject territorial racial predominance alone as a constitutional deprivation. Actually presented exclusion, intentional dilution, materially unequal participation, inferior civic membership, and other independent wrongs retain their own elements. Adarand's justification rule applies to an actual covered racial allocation; it does not supply a missing districting injury.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the governmental act and the protected interest it affects. Predominance describes the relative weight of a motive; it is evidence rather than a substitute for an actionable operation. A design-only theory challenges composition or motive without identifying the independently prohibited treatment. No universal electoral-defeat, financial-loss, second-act, or alternative-map requirement applies to every claim.
* **Other Governing Law:** Fourteenth Amendment equal protection; Fifteenth Amendment prohibition of racial denial or abridgment of voting. Voinovich v. Quilter, decided March 2, 1993, leaves Fifteenth Amendment coverage of dilution unresolved. The Voting Rights Act's statutory elements remain independent; Count II does not create a separate Section 2 claim merely by discussing minority representation. No later voting-law amendment governs this record.
* **Material Other Authority:** Anderson v. Martin, 375 U.S. 399 (1964), distinguishes official racial electoral treatment from geographical composition; Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S. 242 (1986), and Rule 56 preserve favorable inferences at summary judgment.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Protection extends equally to voters of every race. An electorate's composition alone does not impose racial eligibility, assign political beliefs, or make its representative legally responsible to only one race. Unequal eligibility or material interference with participation would require its own analysis even without a changed election winner.
* **Open Point:** No unresolved rule about design-only injury requires decision. Whether Count II supports an independently recognized deprivation requires applying its actual elements to the preserved claim. A categorical rule immunizing all incumbent protection is neither established nor required; the broader Fifteenth Amendment coverage question remains reserved.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

* **Controlling Reasoning Below:** The court treated the plaintiffs as virtually represented in Page and separately concluded that racial considerations did not predominate. It treated the incumbent claim as another territorial challenge. Those are independent grounds, and each requires examination under the applicable rule.
* **Appellants' Strongest Supported Position:** Political allies are not interchangeable parties. Count II alleges purposeful racial differentiation in governmental protection of incumbents, requiring analysis of the actual benefit rather than dismissal by relabeling it. Evidence of racial motivation must receive the favorable inferences Rule 56 requires.
* **Appellees' Strongest Supported Position:** The prior litigation concerned the same map and relief, and its relationships warrant examination. Independently, district composition and changed reelection prospects do not establish unequal civic rights under governing territorial doctrine, even if race influenced the plan.
* **Necessary Concessions or Limiting Principles:** Common electoral objectives alone cannot establish representation. Conversely, rejecting broad nonparty preclusion does not establish a merits violation. No right to unchanged reelection prospects excuses an independently unlawful racial allocation. These are legal limits, not attributed counsel concessions. No oral-argument concession is reported without a verified transcript.
* **Solicitor General or Amicus Position:** No decision-material separate rule or remand position has been verified. None is attributed.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance on an independently sufficient merits ground would leave the redistricting judgment intact without endorsing the virtual-representation rationale. Its holding could clarify that a favorable district electorate is not transformed into an ordinary racial allocation of candidacy rights merely by calling it an incumbent benefit. That rule would preserve review of an actually different operation.

If a preserved independent deprivation is supported by a genuine material dispute, summary judgment on that component could be vacated for its own claim-specific inquiry. Such remand would neither establish discrimination nor require a replacement map. A valid, actually established basis for nonparty preclusion could provide another lawful path, but similarity of interests cannot supply the missing authority.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The record supports distinguishing territorial composition from independent racial eligibility or participation restrictions, and lawful preclusion from unsupported representation by association. It does not support a comprehensive new partisan-districting test or an exemption for every racial decision benefiting an incumbent.
