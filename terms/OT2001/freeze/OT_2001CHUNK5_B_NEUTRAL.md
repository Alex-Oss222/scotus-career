## 69. United States v. Cotton

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** United States v. Cotton
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 625; No. 01-687.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-20. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether omission from a federal indictment of a fact increasing the statutory maximum requires automatic vacatur of an enhanced sentence despite no trial objection, overwhelming proof of that fact, and notice of the enhanced exposure. This paraphrases the petition’s question; jurisdiction and Rule 52(b) are its necessary antecedents.
* **Question Granted:** The petition’s automatic-vacatur question, including the jurisdictional premise and application of plain-error review; no separate challenge to the conspiracy convictions is added.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Fourth Circuit vacated enhanced drug-conspiracy sentences because the indictment omitted the quantity needed to authorize them, treating the omission as a jurisdictional defect. The United States seeks reversal.
* **Relief Requested:** The United States seeks restoration of the sentences; defendants seek sentencing relief for the missing quantity allegation and finding.
* **Material Record:** The defendants were prosecuted for a cocaine and cocaine-base conspiracy. The indictment did not allege the drug quantity that authorized the enhanced statutory range. The sentencing court made quantity findings and imposed sentences above the otherwise applicable maximum. The defendants did not preserve the indictment or jury-quantity objection in the trial court. The trial evidence included quantities far exceeding the enhanced threshold, and the reviewing dispute concerns whether that proof was overwhelming and uncontroverted. That description does not convert the sentencing finding into a jury verdict or an admission. It bears on the appellate remedy after an error is identified.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The federal court had statutory subject-matter jurisdiction over federal criminal offenses. The indictment’s substantive adequacy and the jury’s authorized punishment are separate questions. Forfeiture must not be mislabeled intentional waiver. Plain-error review must address its distinct requirements rather than use jurisdiction as a substitute for the rule. The case does not present a preserved error or a complete absence of a charging instrument.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument occurred April 15, 2002. The supplied merits event is May 20, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Harris and Ring concern which aggravating facts require element procedures; Angle concerns sentencing constraints and consecutive terms. Cotton presents an omitted indictment allegation with no timely objection, so jurisdiction and Rule 52(b) correction are separate from the underlying jury-allocation question.
* **Participation Matter:** None independently established for the simulation.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** United States v. Vonn precedes this event and could supply an effective plain-error rule, but no completed simulated disposition is provided. United States v. Angle, Harris v. United States, and Ring v. Arizona are later and cannot govern this decision.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Indictment omission and judicial power

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Apprendi v. New Jersey requires the jury to find beyond a reasonable doubt, or the defendant to admit, a fact necessary to increase the authorized statutory maximum. It does not declare every omission jurisdictional or settle state grand-jury incorporation.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Distinguish legal authority to hear the class of case from a defect in the accusation and from an unauthorized sentence. The label jurisdiction does not replace analysis of the governing jurisdictional enactment.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Fifth and Sixth Amendments; federal criminal jurisdiction; the charged drug statutes; Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 52(b).
* **Material Other Authority:** Ex parte Bain supplies the older jurisdictional language the parties contest. Johnson v. United States addresses forfeited failure to submit a legally necessary matter to the jury.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Quantity needed for a higher statutory maximum is a jury fact under the authenticated simulated rule. The sentencing judge’s finding cannot itself enlarge the jury-authorized ceiling.
* **Open Point:** Whether the charging omission removes subject-matter jurisdiction or instead remains a nonjurisdictional constitutional error.

### Issue 2: Forfeited error and correction

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** United States v. Olano requires error, plainness, an effect on substantial rights, and a separate discretionary decision concerning fairness, integrity, or public reputation. Johnson v. United States rejects automatic correction of the particular forfeited jury omission where the omitted matter was overwhelming and uncontroverted.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Preserve the distinction between forfeiture and waiver and between prejudice and the fourth-stage discretion. Neither an actual-innocence requirement nor an automatic-reversal rule follows from Johnson.
* **Other Governing Law:** Rule 52(b) governs the forfeited claim on direct review.
* **Material Other Authority:** Apprendi v. New Jersey establishes the substantive error; it does not itself supply the appellate remedy.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** Unpreserved constitutional jury errors are not categorically exempt from the ordinary correction inquiry.
* **Open Point:** How the strong quantity proof and the specific indictment omission bear on the fourth requirement in this record.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Fourth Circuit regarded the absent quantity allegation as depriving the court of authority to impose the greater punishment, and treated jurisdictional correction as obligatory.

**Government’s position.** Congress gave the district court power over federal criminal prosecutions. An indictment error does not withdraw that power. Under Olano and Johnson, the overwhelming uncontroverted proof prevents these forfeited errors from seriously compromising the fairness and integrity of the proceedings, even if substantial-rights prejudice is assumed.

**Defendants' position.** The grand jury never charged, and the petit jury never found, the fact required for the greater penalty. A lawful trial is not just a forecast that a properly instructed jury probably would have convicted of an aggravated offense. They request correction of sentences exceeding the authorized verdict, not acquittal of the conspiracy.

**Limiting principles.** Strong evidence does not make the trial error disappear. The Government cannot carry this argument into preserved claims or genuinely disputed quantities without the applicable separate analysis.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The Court can reject the jurisdictional characterization while remanding for proper plain-error review, or apply the fourth requirement to the established record itself. Correction would require lawful resentencing within the available authorization or other legally permissible proceedings, not automatic dismissal of the prosecution. Refusal to correct would leave the challenged sentences in place on this forfeited record.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** A jurisdictional holding can establish that omission of an offense element does not withdraw federal criminal subject-matter jurisdiction. A remedial holding can deny correction for this overwhelming and uncontroverted omitted quantity without adopting a universal harmlessness rule for uncharged elements. A rule requiring correction whenever the maximum is exceeded would need to confront Olano and Johnson directly rather than silently displace them.

**Uncertainty:** The characterization of the proof must rest on the actual trial record. A defendant who contested quantity or preserved the constitutional objection presents a materially different remedial case.

## 70. Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission of Maryland / United States v. Public Service Commission of Maryland

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Verizon Maryland Inc. v. Public Service Commission of Maryland / United States v. Public Service Commission of Maryland
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 635; Nos. 00-1531 and 00-1711.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-20. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether federal courts may review a state commission’s enforcement of an interconnection agreement for compliance with federal telecommunications law, and whether sovereign immunity bars prospective relief against commissioners enforcing the challenged order.
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The District of Maryland dismissed the challenge; the Fourth Circuit affirmed on jurisdictional and immunity grounds. Verizon and the United States seek reversal and an opportunity for adjudication of the federal claim.
* **Relief Requested:** Verizon and the United States seek restoration of federal review; the commission seeks to sustain dismissal.
* **Material Record:** The Maryland commission required Verizon to make reciprocal-compensation payments to competing carriers under interconnection agreements for calls delivered to Internet service providers. Verizon alleged that the commission’s interpretation and enforcement conflicted with federal telecommunications law. It sued the commission and commissioners, seeking declaratory and prospective injunctive relief against continued enforcement. The challenged orders affect payments between private carriers. The complaint is not simply a demand for retrospective compensation from the state treasury. Federal administrative treatment of Internet-bound traffic forms part of the dispute, but the present jurisdictional question does not establish which carrier is right about compensation.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** A substantial federal claim supplies an ordinary federal-question inquiry. A possibly unsuccessful claim is not necessarily a jurisdictionally insubstantial one. The existence of specific telecommunications review language does not by itself displace general federal jurisdiction. The State, the commission, and the individual officers require separate immunity analysis. A prospective-relief route can make adjudication of direct commission waiver unnecessary.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument occurred December 5, 2001. The supplied event is May 20, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** Both listed captions are the consolidated Maryland review matter. Mathias concerns a separate Illinois judgment and retains its own brief.
* **Participation Matter:** The historical record identifies O’Connor’s nonparticipation; no independent simulated participation order is supplied.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.

* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Jurisdiction over a federal-law enforcement challenge

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board recognizes a federal statutory implementation scheme with state commission functions. Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht requires a claim-specific immunity analysis rather than treating an immunity objection as destruction of all federal jurisdiction.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify a substantial federal controversy independently of its eventual merits. A specialized review provision displaces general jurisdiction only when the statutory structure supports that exclusion; forum allocation and correctness of an agency decision remain distinct.
* **Other Governing Law:** Federal-question jurisdiction; the Telecommunications Act’s interconnection and review provisions; the federal administrative rules actually effective for the challenged orders.
* **Material Other Authority:** Bell v. Hood supplies the distinction between a substantial federal claim and ultimate entitlement to relief. Ex parte Young concerns prospective restraint of state officers' ongoing federal violations.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** The federal character of the asserted statutory claim is not eliminated because the dispute also requires interpreting an interconnection agreement.
* **Open Point:** Whether the specific telecommunications review language excludes the ordinary federal-question route for enforcement decisions.

### Issue 2: Prospective relief and sovereign immunity

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Seminole Tribe of Florida v. Florida, Alden v. Maine, and Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank do not adopt a categorical Article I immunity bar. College Savings Bank rejects constructive consent merely from regulated activity. Those distinct propositions cannot be merged into a fictional universal waiver.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Determine the source of a cause, the defendant, and the relief before applying immunity. Prospective officer relief addresses ongoing unlawful enforcement; it does not authorize retrospective state-treasury damages under a different name.
* **Other Governing Law:** The constitutional supremacy of valid federal law, the applicable federal review scheme, and equitable relief against responsible officers.
* **Material Other Authority:** Ex parte Young supplies the officer-relief route. The particular remedial scheme must be examined for any displacement of that route.
* **Existing Rule’s Reach:** An allegation that commissioners will continue enforcing an order contrary to federal law is ordinarily prospective in character, notwithstanding private financial consequences.
* **Open Point:** Whether this scheme forecloses that route or instead permits federal adjudication without deciding direct commission immunity.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Fourth Circuit treated the specific review provision and immunity as obstacles to the federal action. Its reasoning risks turning the merits of the statutory allegation into a limit on the court’s power to hear it.

**Verizon and the United States.** General federal-question jurisdiction reaches the asserted conflict. The specialized review provision does not expressly withdraw it. Commissioners who continue to enforce an allegedly unlawful order are proper prospective defendants, and an injunction regulating that enforcement does not draw compensation from the State’s treasury.

**Commission and competing carriers.** State enforcement of an agreement should remain within the statutory review arrangement Congress actually enacted. A declaration affecting accrued payment obligations may function as retrospective relief, and participation in the federal scheme should not be treated as automatic consent to every suit.

**Necessary limits.** The petitioners must identify an ongoing federal violation and responsible enforcing officials. Obtaining jurisdiction does not establish the proper classification of Internet-bound calls or erase the contracts' valid terms. The Court need not decide waiver merely to make adjudication possible.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Reversal can restore the federal-question action against commissioners and leave direct commission immunity undecided. An affirmance based on statutory exclusivity would require a persuasive exclusion of ordinary federal jurisdiction. A remedy confined to retrospective payment from the State would present a different officer-immunity problem, but that is not the relief described here.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court can hold that general federal-question jurisdiction remains available for substantial federal challenges to a commission’s enforcement orders and that prospective officer relief is not displaced by this statute. The holding would permit adjudication, not decide telecommunications liability. A broader participation-as-waiver holding is avoidable and must confront the existing rejection of constructive consent.

**Uncertainty:** The scope of each requested declaration and injunction must be checked against the actual order. The merits of compensation and any separate contract-law issues remain unadjudicated by this jurisdictional ruling.

## 71. Alabama v. Shelton

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Alabama v. Shelton
* **Citation or Docket:** 535 U.S. 654; No. 00-1214.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-20. Historical opinion or disposition calendar date used as the simulated event date.
* **Question Presented:** Whether an uncounseled misdemeanor conviction, obtained without a valid waiver of counsel, may support a suspended jail sentence that can later be activated upon violation of probation.
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Alabama Supreme Court left Shelton’s assault conviction and monetary punishment intact but invalidated the suspended imprisonment. Alabama seeks restoration of that sentencing component.
* **Relief Requested:** Alabama seeks restoration of the suspended jail sentence; Shelton seeks affirmance of its invalidation.
* **Material Record:** Shelton represented himself in a misdemeanor assault prosecution and a trial de novo. The sentence included thirty days in jail, suspended, and two years of probation. The record does not establish a knowing and intelligent waiver of appointed counsel. No jail term had yet been activated. The constitutional claim concerns the prospect that the suspended term could be imposed for this conviction after a later revocation proceeding. The State emphasizes that the original proceeding caused no immediate incarceration; Shelton emphasizes that later incarceration would punish the uncounseled offense, not a newly tried criminal charge.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The absence of a valid counsel waiver is central. If a valid waiver existed, the constitutional question would be different. The Court should distinguish imprisonment for the original offense from punishment for a separate, counseled later crime. A later revocation hearing does not ordinarily retry the original assault charge with the full protections available at trial. The permissibility of a freestanding probation sentence without a suspended jail term is not necessarily resolved by this record.
* **Relevant Dates:** Argument occurred February 19, 2002. The supplied merits event is May 20, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate listed brief.
* **Participation Matter:** None independently established for the simulation.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** No supplied completed OT2001 decision resolves this precise counsel question. Earlier criminal-procedure briefs cannot supply an assumed expansion or contraction of Nichols.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Counsel and suspended imprisonment

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Nichols v. United States permits the particular later use of a valid uncounseled fine-only misdemeanor. The controlling coalition does not supply a broad rule allowing incarceration for an uncounseled conviction whenever imprisonment is postponed.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify the judgment that authorizes incarceration and the stage at which guilt is adjudicated. A lawful fine-only conviction and an uncounseled conviction used as the direct basis for imprisonment present different questions. Do not transform a fractured rationale into a broader controlling rule.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments; state appointment and waiver requirements; Alabama's suspended-sentence and revocation procedures.
* **Material Other Authority:** Argersinger v. Hamlin and Scott v. Illinois supply the received imprisonment boundary. Gagnon v. Scarpelli concerns the distinct, conditional counsel protection at revocation. They must be applied to the actual source of the jail term.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** The State may obtain a valid uncounseled fine-only conviction where appointment is not constitutionally required. It may not execute imprisonment for an offense tried without required counsel or a valid waiver.
* **Open Point:** Whether entering a suspended jail term crosses that boundary before activation and whether later revocation counsel could cure the original trial deficiency.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Alabama Supreme Court treated the suspended term as imprisonment imposed for the original offense. Because its execution could follow without a new constitutionally adequate trial of guilt, it invalidated that component.

**Alabama's position.** Scott uses actual imprisonment as the administrable boundary. Shelton was not incarcerated, and a suspended sentence can encourage compliance without expanding appointment costs to every misdemeanor. The State argues that later procedural protections can prevent unconstitutional incarceration if revocation becomes necessary.

**Shelton's position.** A suspended term is an adjudicated imprisonment sentence awaiting execution. Revocation adjudicates compliance with conditions, not guilt of the original offense. Counsel supplied only then cannot investigate, cross-examine, or contest the evidence at the original trial when it mattered.

**Necessary limits.** Shelton's rule does not require counsel for every petty charge or invalidate every uncounseled conviction. Alabama cannot rely on the possibility of later counsel without specifying a lawful proceeding that actually repairs the original defect. The Court need not decide independent probation or punishment for a separate later offense.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would preserve the conviction while preventing execution of its uncounseled jail component. Reversal would allow entry of the suspended term, with any required protection deferred to activation. A conditional rule allowing later incarceration only after a new counseled adjudication would require a valid state procedural route and cannot be assumed from an ordinary revocation hearing.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The Court may require counsel or valid waiver whenever a conviction directly supports a jail sentence, including a suspended term. That rule reaches the imprisonment authorization rather than every misdemeanor adjudication. The alternative actual-custody rule could defer appointment until liberty is immediately threatened, but must address why counsel at that later stage can protect the trial rights already lost.

**Uncertainty:** The record does not establish a valid waiver or a procedure that would retry the assault before incarceration. A stand-alone probation sentence is analytically distinct and is not resolved merely by striking the suspended term.

## 72. Brown v. Gilmore +

# SECTION I: NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

* **Case Name:** Brown v. Gilmore +
* **Citation or Docket:** 258 F.3d 265 (4th Cir. 2001). No simulated Supreme Court docket or grant date is supplied.
* **October Term:** OT2001.
* **Entry Stage and Event:** MERITS, decision-stage preparation under the closed supplied case list.
* **Simulated Event Date:** 2002-05-20. Supplied simulation placement of the lower-court addition.
* **Question Presented:** Whether Virginia’s requirement that public schools observe a daily minute of silence, during which students may meditate, pray, or engage in other silent activity, violates the Establishment Clause?
* **Question Granted:** The merits questions stated above are paraphrased from the reviewed judicial record; no exact grant wording is asserted.
* **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Eastern District of Virginia sustained the statute against the facial challenge. The Fourth Circuit affirmed. The student and parent challengers seek invalidation and injunctive relief.
* **Relief Requested:** The challengers seek an injunction against the mandatory silence statute; Virginia seeks affirmance.
* **Material Record:** Virginia had authorized a school minute of silence since 1976. The 2000 amendment made the observance mandatory. The law permits meditation, prayer, or other silent activity; it does not require a prayer or a profession of belief. Implementation instructions prohibit teachers from selecting or suggesting a religious activity and prohibit audible devotion. Legislative statements included religiously motivated support, while the Governor and other supporters also emphasized reflection, calm, and civility. The facial record therefore contains evidence relevant to purpose but no finding that every permitted observance is a devotional exercise. A child may remain silent without communicating belief. Allegations of future coercion or teacher direction must not be treated as established features of the enacted procedure.
* **Threshold, Preservation, and Vehicle Matters:** The challenge is to the statute and its required silent interval, not a proved classroom practice in which a teacher directs prayer. Compelled silence and compelled religious participation are distinct acts. The question requires attention to the actual institutional conduct and any substantial pressure, not merely whether some students choose to pray. The parents’ disagreement with religious supporters does not by itself resolve the constitutional classification.
* **Relevant Dates:** The mandatory amendment became effective July 1, 2000. The Fourth Circuit decided the appeal July 24, 2001. The supplied simulated event is May 20, 2002.
* **Companion or Related Matters:** None requiring a separate listed brief.
* **Participation Matter:** None independently established for the simulation.

## B. LAW ENTERING THE CASE

* **Current Through:** June 28, 2001, following the completed OT2000 decisions. No completed OT2001 disposition is supplied; later listed matters and proposed positions do not establish intervening law.
* **Earlier Same-Term Decisions:** None authenticated in the supplied materials. The dated case list establishes sequence, not adjudicative outcomes.
* **Unresolved Same-Term Dependencies:** Thomas v. Chicago Park District is earlier but concerns licensing, not official devotion. Zelman v. Simmons-Harris is later and cannot supply entering law for this event.
* **Institutional or Procedural Law:** Resolve a material jurisdictional or reviewability defect before substantive relief; distinguish the governing legal rule, the required findings, the standard of review, and the judgment actually under review.

### Issue 1: Neutral silence or official devotional exercise

* **Controlling Simulated Law:** Chaudhuri distinguishes a genuine silent opportunity for private thought from official worship. Agostini's modified Lemon rule examines governmental exercise of religious authority or function, with purpose, effects and entanglement informing that inquiry. Good News Club reaffirms the distinction between administering private access and adopting devotional activity. Lee and Santa Fe independently forbid official worship and substantial governmental religious pressure.
* **Current Reusable Doctrine:** Identify whether the institution itself conducts, directs, or substantially pressures participation in religious exercise. Private use of a neutral opportunity differs from official worship. Formal voluntariness does not excuse actual coercion, while universal silence does not become worship solely because prayer is one permitted use.
* **Other Governing Law:** The Establishment Clause and its application to public schools; the Virginia statute and implementation directions described in the judicial record.
* **Material Other Authority:** Lee v. Weisman supplies the school-coercion inquiry. Wallace v. Jaffree is the principal historical argument about prayer-oriented silence statutes; its purpose reasoning must be evaluated against the supplied simulated framework rather than substituted for it.
* **Existing Rule's Reach:** Genuine nondevotional silence is permissible, but official prayer and substantial religious pressure remain prohibited. The fact that silence replaces an earlier prayer does not automatically establish current worship.
* **Open Point:** Whether this mandatory school interval, statutory wording, legislative record, and implementation make it a religious exercise rather than the neutral opportunity already permitted.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Reasoning below.** The Fourth Circuit treated the statute as a permissible accommodation that leaves each student’s silent activity private. It relied on the absence of required prayer and on secular purposes supported by the record.

**Challengers' position.** The mandatory amendment and statements of its proponents reveal an effort to return prayer to public schools. In a compulsory school setting, the State's designation of prayer can communicate an expectation of religious participation even without spoken words. They ask the Court to treat the enacted interval as an official religious practice.

**Virginia's position.** The State requires only silence, which is useful for reflection and settling a classroom. Students may pray, think about unrelated subjects, or do nothing religious. Excluding prayer from a neutral silent interval would itself burden private choice. The implementation directions expressly disallow teacher-directed religious conduct.

**Limits.** Virginia cannot defend an actual prayer instruction by invoking neutral statutory text. Conversely, the challengers cannot equate every religiously motivated vote with an institutional devotional act when the resulting legal duty and implementation are nondevotional.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

Affirmance would sustain the facial statute while preserving challenges to coercive implementation. Reversal could require enjoining a state-directed devotional interval if the evidence supports that characterization. The Court should not declare all silence statutes valid or all mandatory silence religious.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** One path applies Chaudhuri to a school minute requiring no religious act, disclosure, or observance and supported by neutral implementation. It would cover comparable private-choice intervals but not directed prayer. Another path finds that the statute and operation communicate or impose an institutional religious exercise; such a ruling must identify that exercise or substantial pressure rather than rest only on the existence of religious supporters.

**Uncertainty:** This facial record does not determine every teacher's future conduct. Legislative purpose is evidence of institutional function but is not a substitute for identifying the required act under the current simulated rule.

## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

**Petitioners' position.** The commissioners seek removal of legal conclusions that could undermine state regulatory autonomy and immunity in later cases. They argue that federal courts lack or exceed the special review authority and that participation should not imply consent.

**Respondents' position.** The commissioners obtained the judgment they sought and cannot use appellate review to edit nonbinding reasoning. If the questions were reached, respondents defend federal review and a prospective officer route rather than an unlimited damages remedy.

**Reasoning below and its limit.** The underlying court addressed federal review and immunity, but the operative judgment was favorable to these petitioners. The distinction is not a technical concession that can be ignored once the substantive controversy seems important.

**Alternative route.** A different aggrieved litigant in an independently listed case can present related questions. That possibility is not a reason to invent an adverse judgment in this one.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

The lawful threshold path is to dismiss this proceeding for want of a reviewable adverse judgment, leaving the Seventh Circuit's favorable disposition intact without endorsing its unnecessary reasoning. If a genuine binding collateral consequence were shown, the Court could reassess reviewability and then address the merits; the present record does not show one.

**Plausible Holding Scope:** The available rule is procedural: a prevailing party may not obtain review merely to remove unnecessary, nonbinding findings. A general rule forbidding every appeal by a nominally prevailing party would be broader than this record, because an operative adverse component or binding collateral consequence can require separate analysis. No telecommunications holding can lawfully be generated simply to fill a merits template.

**Uncertainty:** The packet relies on the judicial record's express description of the findings as unnecessary and nonbinding. It does not assume that every unfavorable statement in every favorable judgment is harmless. The case remains a MERITS entry despite its threshold disposition path.
