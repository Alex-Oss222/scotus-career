## 83. Holmes Group v. Vornado — June 3, 2002

### Verified comparator and surviving premises

The historical judgment unanimously vacated the Federal Circuit's judgment and instructed transfer to the Tenth Circuit. Scalia's principal opinion had full joins from Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer among the present Associates. Stevens joined Parts I and II-A and wrote separately. Ginsburg, joined by O'Connor, concurred only in the judgment because no patent claim had actually been adjudicated. Those are comparator facts, not present authorship instructions.

Full reading materially refines the supplied synopsis. Stevens did not reserve all adjudicated patent counterclaims: he expressly accepted limiting the Federal Circuit's exclusive allocation to patent claims in an original complaint or an amended pleading filed by the plaintiff. His objection concerned the timing of appellate jurisdiction, genuine plaintiff amendments and unnecessarily dismissive reasoning about the competing interpretation. Ginsburg and O'Connor, in contrast, would recognize exclusive Federal Circuit review of an actually adjudicated compulsory patent counterclaim and other determinations in the case. Breyer's earlier Xeta panel participation is genuine direct contrary evidence, but he later joined the historical categorical rule in this very case.

The relevant simulated premises do not materially differ. Christianson survives. Cardinal Chemical preserves an independent live counterclaim in a case with established patent appellate jurisdiction; it does not hold that a counterclaim creates §1295 jurisdiction over a nonpatent complaint. Its in-world jurisdictional propositions do not displace the historical distinction. Rivet's defense rule and Caterpillar's actual-jurisdiction cure retain their bounded work and do not settle the counterclaim fork differently. Festo and TrafFix concern substantive law, not this allocation. The Rule 54(b) judgment remains exclusively nonpatent, the counterclaim remains stayed and unadjudicated, and the conditional order has not dismissed it. ### Reconciled propositions and disposition

All eight Associates support vacating the Federal Circuit's June 5, 2001 merits-reconsideration judgment for want of the required specialized appellate basis. Ordinary district-court jurisdiction is not eliminated. No trade-dress or patent merits ruling follows. The June 12 district order's conditions retain their actual triggers: jurisdictional vacatur of the appellate order is neither affirmance nor reversal of the district first-claim judgment, so it does not itself make the injunction permanent, release the bond, convert the conditional dismissals to dismissals with prejudice, dismiss the counterclaim, or authorize reinstatement based on a merits reversal.

**HG1 — shared result:** The present nonpatent Rule 54(b) adjudication, with no patent claim in the complaint and no patent counterclaim adjudicated, is outside the Federal Circuit's statutory patent allocation. All eight support this result; it is not a single shared general rationale for every future patent counterclaim.

**HG2 — plaintiff-pleading rule:** Sections 1295(a)(1) and 1338(a) do not permit a patent counterclaim alone to supply the incorporated arising-under basis when the plaintiff's well-pleaded claims are nonpatent. The compulsory nature and independent controversy of the counterclaim do not change which pleading supplies that statutory source. Six Associates support this proposition: Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer. It decides the counterclaim actually presented, while preserving the separate question of an actual or constructive amendment to the plaintiff's complaint. An exclusively nonpatent complaint does not acquire patent character merely because the declaratory defendant could bring a different patent coercive action.

**HG3 — principal statutory explanation:** Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Breyer join the incorporated same-language/source-of-claim explanation: §1295 refers to §1338 jurisdiction rather than making uniformity an independent jurisdictional grant, and an independent counterclaim cannot change that incorporated referent. They accept the genuine counterclaim/defense distinction but reject its asserted jurisdictional consequence. Rule 13 and Rule 54(b) do not amend the jurisdictional statutes. Christianson is extended to the counterclaim question, not falsely described as having already decided it. No showing of manipulative or frivolous pleading is needed for exclusion. Actual or constructive plaintiff amendment remains separate.

**Stevens's permissible joinder scope:** He joins HG2 and the present judgment. He can also join a lead formulation that preserves plaintiff amendments and appellate timing and explains the source-of-claim choice without asserting that appellate and original jurisdiction are immutably fixed at filing or that no reasoned contrary interpretation exists. He need not agree with every rhetorical or policy sentence. An opinion adopting those rejected assertions loses his joinder to that portion; his supporting explanation then remains separately identifiable. He favors the plaintiff's legitimate appellate-forum choice, respect for the statute's regional allocation of nonpatent intellectual-property claims, and administrable pleading rules that avoid merits and motive litigation over a counterclaim's jurisdictional adequacy. These support exclusion of counterclaims without denying the specialized circuit's uniformity purpose or treating it as conclusive. His precise final join depends on the actual formulation, not the historical part labels.

**Ginsburg/O'Connor's separate ground:** A compulsory counterclaim arising under patent law and actually adjudicated by the district court would support the Federal Circuit's exclusive review of that adjudication and other determinations in the same case. The present judgment adjudicates none, so the allocation fails here. Their view distinguishes the appellate allocation from a plaintiff's trial-forum choice, respects the independent character of a patent claim, and gives effect to uniform appellate patent adjudication. It does not sustain jurisdiction merely because a stayed claim remains on the docket. It is a separate noncontrolling rationale, not a logical subset to be aggregated with HG2 under Marks.

## 81. SEC v. Zandford — June 3, 2002

### Comparator, reconciled rule and disposition

The historical Court unanimously reversed and remanded. Its sole opinion, authored historically by Stevens, assumed the complaint's allegations and did not decide summary-judgment sufficiency or the denial of discovery. All eight current Associates joined. The full opinion and four notes establish the sale/conversion distinction, the absence of a requirement to misstate a security's value, and the limit against converting every fiduciary wrong into federal securities fraud. Historical authorship determines no present assignment.

Retain all eight independent commitments to reversal and remand on the integrated-sales statutory ground. A broker who, with the required scienter and duty, secretly sells customers' entrusted securities for the contemporaneous purpose of appropriating the proceeds uses deception in connection with those sales when liquidation executes the concealed scheme. Fair market execution and trading discretion do not authorize that personal appropriation. Actual sale, actual deception and their operative relationship matter; broker status, custody, inferior management, temporal proximity and later nonperformance alone do not suffice. All operative interstate-means and Rule 10b-5 conditions remain applicable.

The frozen rule requires a contemporaneous concealed plan, not successful later conversion as an additional statutory element. Once a sale itself executes the deceptive scheme, ultimate receipt of stolen proceeds is not indispensable to the legal nexus. Conversely, an honestly completed sale followed by independently conceived theft, theft of cash without an integral securities transaction, or disclosed disloyal conduct without deception does not satisfy this particular rule. These are the operative limits of the existing duty/deception/transaction standard, not new factual findings or an exhaustive list of all §10(b) applications.

Bankers Life directly answers the fair-price and diversion arguments; Chiarella supplies the duty making silence deceptive; Santa Fe excludes unfairness without deception. O'Hagan links use of entrusted information to consummated source deception but does not make every conversion a securities wrong. The present conduct uses the victims' own securities in the scheme. The in-world Kravitz rule and unanimous Wharf holding independently support the distinction between an initially deceptive securities undertaking and subsequent breach. Neither their private reliance elements nor Central Bank's separate affirmative-assistance action is mechanically imported into primary SEC enforcement.

The complaint alleges the required integrated scheme and therefore cannot be dismissed on the Fourth Circuit's categorical connection rule. On remand, ordinary civil proof, issue-preclusion and remedial rules govern the actual record and relief. The wire-fraud conviction label does not establish every securities element or automatically restore the injunction and $343,000 disgorgement. No new-hearing or unlimited-discovery entitlement follows. The unreviewed §17(a) claim and disclaimed excessive-trading theory do not become additional holdings.

## 82. United States v. Mackby — June 3, 2002

### Reconciled coverage, standard and remedy

Retain all eight independent commitments. Each would affirm coverage of the combined FCA recovery under the Excessive Fines Clause and preserve remand for a developed gross-disproportionality assessment. The affirmed liability finding is not reopened. The recovery combines $555,000 in fixed sanctions for 111 claims with $174,454.92 in trebled overpayment, totaling $729,454.92. Its culpability-linked penalties and multiplied recovery serve punishment and deterrence at least in part, notwithstanding compensation for investigation, delay and harms not captured by direct overpayment.

Austin supplies partly punitive coverage; Bajakajian supplies gross disproportionality to the gravity of the actual offense. Independent appellate constitutional review and clear-error review of supported facts retain their separate roles. Substantial respect for legislative punishment choices does not make statutory authorization or a minimum per-claim sum conclusive. The lower court must assess the actual combined demand with relevant attention to culpability, harm, the offense and authorized sanctions. Real therapy affects harm but does not erase the false physician-involvement representation that established payment eligibility and avoided therapist caps.

No mandatory exhaustive checklist, direct-loss ratio, government-cost ceiling, constitutional dollar cap or least-effective-deterrence test is adopted. The lower opinion's illustrative desired-deterrence question is not transformed into a government burden to prove that this precise amount was indispensable. Compensation and punishment may coexist; no separate accounting inference automatically invalidates every dollar above loss. An isolated treble award or materially different combination remains outside the presented question. The assessment may consider each component in light of the other; coverage alone does not compel a reduction or choose an amount.

The strongest no-coverage argument is that multiplied recovery compensates real unmeasured governmental harm. Bornstein and Rex Trailer support genuine compensatory functions, but they do not negate the independent punitive operation of this combination. Hudson's distinct Double Jeopardy rules, including the surviving in-world Halper inquiry, do not replace Austin's Excessive Fines threshold. Vermont Agency's actual in-world standing and sovereign-state-defendant holdings do not supply a categorical treble-damages punishment rule. Riley concerns private enforcement and does not dismiss this government-filed action.
