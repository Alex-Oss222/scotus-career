## Defined commitments and common disposition

All seven would reverse the judgment sustaining the categorical tenant-knowledge requirement, vacate the preliminary injunction insofar as it rests on that requirement or the additional categorical theories rejected below, and remand for consideration of properly presented individual grounds. None orders eviction, finds an alleged drug offense proved, validates every lease term, or finally rejects every individual constitutional application. Each of the five substantive components below has its own ground; the result on one does not dispose of the others by label.

### R1. Statutory cause, actual coverage and agency choice

Section 1437d(l)(6) makes qualifying activity by the specified tenant, household member, guest or other controlled person cause for termination without an additional requirement that the tenant knew or should have known. The clause requiring lease inclusion is mandatory; use of termination in an eligible case is permissive. The relationship and criminal-activity predicates must actually exist. The control language does not make constant supervision of every listed person's behavior a condition of statutory authority, and it does not cover a stranger through a merely remote association. The first branch covers criminal activity threatening other tenants' health, safety or right to peaceful enjoyment of the premises. The separate drug-related branch covers activity on or off the premises; the first branch's threat requirement is not silently added to it, and the later statutory geographical term does not rewrite an older actual lease. A general condition of personal tenant fault cannot be inserted into either by borrowing criminal liability rules.

Section 1437d(l)(2)'s prohibition of unreasonable terms, (l)(5)'s serious-or-repeated-violation or other-good-cause requirement, and the notice and document provisions remain operative. They do not erase the more specific listed-person ground. The strongest contrary path treats the general provisions, control language, housing purpose and constitutional avoidance together as a knowledge condition. That path fails because it rewrites the specific ground and treats mere ignorance as resolving every constitutional predicate. The alternative overbroad path, mandatory eviction whenever statutory cause exists, also fails: Congress required a lease ground, not its use in every case.

### R2. Due process, Bennis and individual defenses

Termination authority without personal tenant knowledge is not for that reason alone incompatible with due process. Government may impose lawful conditions concerning the conduct of a household occupying its housing and protect other residents without personally convicting the tenant. That proposition does not deny a protected tenancy interest or eliminate required process. Neither the civil designation, a signature on a lease nor discretionary mitigation defeats an independently established constitutional protection.

Bennis protects a separately owned interest in an ordinary lawful automobile when the claimant proves actual ownership, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all that could reasonably be expected to prevent misuse. Shared lawful use alone does not defeat the defense; constant monitoring, omniscience and prevention at any cost are not demanded. Culpable entrustment, known misuse and omitted reasonable precautions remain outside the protection. The ordinary claimant burden receives a meaningful opportunity for proof, without an invented heightened quantum. Once established, the protection requires preservation or restoration of the interest; equitable grace cannot replace it. A valid forfeiture otherwise does not automatically require compensation. These rules remain binding despite current nonparticipation by one earlier joiner.

The injunction's ignorance condition does not establish those predicates and Bennis did not adjudicate a continuing public tenancy. All seven distinguish that interest and the prospective household condition from the automobile forfeiture. All seven reject a universal innocence immunity, but do not aggregate their disagreement about possible future extension into a universal rule that Bennis can never matter to an individually demonstrated housing deprivation. The individual analogical question is reserved because the actual lease interest, prevention facts and challenged application have not been established at the necessary scope, not merely because a narrower opinion is preferable.

### R3. First Amendment association

The First Amendment supplies no categorical tenant-knowledge condition merely because the statute refers to household members or guests and may create economic pressure concerning them. A claimant must identify protected expressive association and a material burden attributable to the challenged governmental application; the supplied record establishes no particular expressive activity, message or expressive relationship impaired by the no-knowledge clause. This is an evidentiary limit, not a finding that these tenants engage in no protected expression. The rule addresses specified criminal conduct and occupancy relationships, not an identified viewpoint, compelled message or membership in a political organization.

Hurley protects an actual private expressive presentation from compelled alteration. Dale requires actual expressive impairment in its access-law setting and preserves direct speech, genuine message-bearing roles and intimate association; its organizational message-role formulation is not mechanically imposed as the exclusive test for all tenants. Lyng distinguishes an indirect economic effect of a benefit condition from a substantial constitutional burden and expressly recognizes that subtle interference can be actionable. No general benefits exception to the First Amendment follows. The strongest contrary path emphasizes pressure to expel a guest and the possibility of indirect retaliation; those matters could support an actual claim but do not establish the missing expressive predicate or turn every guest relationship into protected expression. Thus this categorical claim does not independently sustain the injunction. A properly presented expression-targeted application is not adjudicated away.

### R4. Fourteenth Amendment intimate association

Protected family and other genuinely intimate relationships require a distinct inquiry; their protection does not depend upon proof of an expressive message. The statute's authorization of termination for specified criminal activity, without tenant knowledge, does not by itself directly and substantially forbid a protected living arrangement or require relatives to separate. On the categorical theory supplied, protecting safe, drug-free housing and the other tenants' enjoyment is a legitimate objective, and the specified household, guest and control relationships rationally connect the authorized condition to it. Automatic strict scrutiny is not triggered by the personal-knowledge omission alone. The optional permission to continue occupancy without a responsible family member is not a statutory command to dissolve a family.

That conclusion does not treat displacement, homelessness or pressure on a family as nonexistent. The record does not establish the kind, strength or precise burden of each asserted intimate relationship, a compulsory severance, or a particular application whose direct and substantial effect requires further constitutional justification. Troxel protects actual parental direction against displacement merely by a judge's preferred arrangement; it supplies no majority-adopted universal strict-scrutiny or harm test for every governmental effect on a family. Shahar protects an actual enduring intimate relationship even outside civil marriage and rejects reliance on labels instead of the real governmental interest. Neither decision creates an unconditional entitlement to this dwelling irrespective of covered criminal activity. The strongest tenant path analogizes an actual expulsion-or-separation choice to a direct family restriction; that path is preserved for supported application, without assuming its predicates or adopting a universal no-fault defense now.

### R5. Excessive Fines

The statute's permission to terminate a conditional right to occupy government housing is not itself a punitive payment to the sovereign. The categorical attack supplies no sufficient basis to equate every such termination with confiscation of tenant-owned property as punishment. Government recovery of possession under a lawful occupancy condition and prospective cessation of assistance have a different operation from the statutory drug-property forfeitures in Austin or the criminal currency exaction in Bajakajian. The no-knowledge feature and the possibility of economic loss alone do not establish a fine. This ground rejects an alternative categorical basis for the injunction; it does not hold that every measure called an eviction necessarily falls outside the Clause.

Austin forecloses the argument that civil form, a remedial purpose or a noncash transfer alone defeats coverage. A leasehold can be a valuable legal interest; describing the building as government-owned does not establish that a particular punitive taking of a tenant's interest is impossible. A supported claim must identify the interest actually taken and how the challenged operation extracts it at least partly as punishment. A regulatory label or the mere fact that the loss is valuable does not answer that inquiry in either direction. No such particular punitive extraction is established by the categorical statutory theory and supplied application findings. That is not a final adverse factual finding about every tenant's interest or actual termination.

If an actual application establishes a covered punitive forfeiture, lawful statutory authority and a nexus to an offense do not end the separate constitutional excessiveness inquiry. Bajakajian's gross-disproportionality rule requires the actual offense, gravity and exaction to be identified; the Court adopts no new ratio, exclusive instrumentality rule, universal factor checklist, fixed leasehold value or remedy. The record supplies no adjudicated values or proportionality findings that could decide an actual punitive forfeiture. Ursery, Kurth Ranch and Hudson address distinct Double Jeopardy classifications and do not impose their clearest-proof or accounting tests as the fines threshold. The Court can reject the categorical injunction ground without resolving state incorporation; incorporation remains expressly undecided, neither assumed settled nor rejected. No alternative holding approves an otherwise established excessive fine.

### R6. Remedy and remaining proceedings

The lower courts must apply the statutory holding to the actual lease, alleged act, actor and relationship; assess any properly preserved individual constitutional claim under its own predicates; and apply the ordinary preliminary-relief requirements to any remaining request. The older lease and 1997 regulation's on-or-near-premises limitation is not silently replaced by the later statutory on-or-off-premises language. A visitor's status must satisfy the applicable definition, including the regulation's consent-of-a-household-member element for a guest. Facts supporting a legal control relationship are distinct from constant ability to prevent all conduct.

Section 966.4(l)(5) gives discretion to consider all circumstances, including seriousness of the offense, family members' extent of participation and eviction's effects on family members not involved. In appropriate cases it permits continued occupancy by remaining family members on a condition excluding family members who engaged in the proscribed activity. A family member who engaged in illegal drug use may be required to show successful completion of treatment as a condition of residing in the unit. Those options are not mandatory mitigation, automatic severance or a universal reasonable-prevention defense. Required notice must state the grounds and applicable response, document-access and hearing rights. When a grievance hearing must be afforded, tenancy cannot terminate before the request period expires or, if timely requested, before completion of that process. An agency relying on an authorized grievance exclusion must establish its application and decision to exclude the grievance; its notice must identify the judicial eviction procedure, HUD's determination that the procedure provides basic due process, the absence of a grievance entitlement and the applicable criminal-activity basis. No such exclusion is presumed from the allegation alone. Eviction through an administrative action without a court judgment requires authorization by local law, a due-process administrative hearing and the opportunity for the required pre-eviction grievance hearing. Under section 966.4(m), the tenant has a reasonable pre-hearing or pretrial opportunity, on request, to examine agency-held documents directly relevant to termination or eviction and copy them at the tenant's expense; failure to make those requested documents available bars proceeding with eviction. A preserved defense cannot be denied merely because mitigation is discretionary, and a claim's factual predicate cannot be inferred merely because eviction is severe.

The remand neither directs an additional evidentiary hearing in every case nor treats incomplete source retrieval as litigant default. It permits resolution of properly presented remaining issues on the appropriate actual record. The federal injunction cannot remain on the rejected categorical grounds; any renewed or retained protection needs its own supported legal and preliminary-relief basis. No tenant is adjudged removable and no state judgment is automatically entered or vacated by this handoff.

## Current-law refresh receipt

FERC distinguishes the enacted grant, a triggered duty and an actual discretionary choice; its divided treatment of agency explanation does not create a duty to evict or mitigate here. Edelman's verification cure remains statute-specific and supplies no tenant-knowledge condition. Ragsdale's entitlement/remedy distinction, including the four-Justice disagreement about a permissible designation condition, does not authorize a judicial rewrite of this explicit listed-person ground or establish a universal anti-regulation principle. These authorities confirm R1 and R6 without changing an Associate's commitment.

Delaney's individual awareness and response rules, Milstein's actual-function immunity and Zahrey's deliberate-deception and causal rules concern different constitutional claims. They do not establish a personal tenant-fault requirement, prove a housing-related constitutional injury or convert an individual claim into a categorical defense. Raygor's particular tolling exclusion supplies no rule for these housing components.

Ruiz, effective March 4 in the supplied 45-event copy, applies the PLRA to prospective prison relief, preserves the actual findings requirements and meaningful opportunity for proof, and retains Farmer's actual-awareness rule within that different claim. Its decree-remedy holding neither strips substantive leasehold protection nor supplies a housing defense. ## Complete R6 qualification

Under the 1997 section 966.51(a)(1), the grievance procedure ordinarily applies to an individual tenant's dispute with the public housing agency about action or inaction contrary to the tenant's lease or agency regulations that adversely affects the tenant's rights, duties, welfare or status.

The section 966.51(a)(2) exception requires a HUD determination that the jurisdiction's law requires an opportunity for a court hearing with the defined basic due-process safeguards before eviction. Once that determination exists, the agency may exclude an individual termination or eviction grievance involving either:

- Criminal activity threatening other residents' or agency employees' health, safety or right to peaceful enjoyment of the premises; or
- Drug-related criminal activity on or near the premises.

This is permission to exclude, not mandatory exclusion. The agency must actually elect the permissible exclusion. The first category includes agency employees, unlike the separately worded statutory termination ground; the second retains the 1997 on-or-near limitation. Neither is silently replaced by the later statute's drug-activity coverage on or off the premises. The judicial eviction procedure used must be one covered by HUD's determination. The exception dispenses with the agency grievance hearing through that qualifying court route; it does not dispense with the court opportunity itself.

Section 966.53(c)'s basic safeguards are adequate notice of the grounds for termination and eviction; the tenant's right to representation by counsel; an opportunity to refute the agency's evidence, including confrontation and cross-examination of witnesses and presentation of affirmative legal or equitable defenses; and a decision on the merits. Representation by counsel is not rewritten as a guarantee of appointed counsel. HUD need not use notice-and-comment rulemaking for its determination, but must publish the list of covered judicial procedures and make the supporting legal analysis available for public inspection and copying.

The separate notice and route requirements remain operative. Under the 1997 section 966.4(l)(3), notice is 14 days for nonpayment, a reasonable period considering seriousness not exceeding 30 days for threats to other residents' or agency employees' health or safety, and 30 days otherwise. Notice identifies specific grounds, reply rights, relevant-document access and any required grievance-request opportunity. A legally required notice to vacate may be combined with or run concurrently with the lease-termination notice. Where a grievance opportunity is required, termination must await expiration of the request period and, upon a timely request, completion of the grievance process, even if a state or local notice to vacate has expired. Where the agency permissibly elects exclusion, notice must state the absence of a grievance entitlement, identify the qualifying judicial procedure and HUD determination, and identify which of the two criminal-activity categories applies. These 1997 regulatory terms do not merge with the separately supplied statutory notice provisions; the actual legally applicable requirements must be satisfied.

Administrative eviction without a court action remains a different route: the jurisdiction must authorize it, a due-process administrative hearing is required, and the agency must afford the pre-eviction grievance opportunity. The judicial-procedure exclusion cannot remove those administrative-route safeguards. Section 966.4(m) still requires a reasonable opportunity, on request before the hearing or trial, to examine agency-held documents directly relevant to termination or eviction and copy them at the tenant's expense. If the agency fails to make those documents available as required, it may not proceed with eviction.

Section 966.51(b) separately excludes disputes between tenants that do not involve the agency and class grievances from the agency procedure. Its statement that the procedure is not a forum for initiating or negotiating group policy changes creates no jurisdictional bar to this federal suit and determines no individual defense.

No determination for the actual judicial procedure, agency election, qualifying activity, proper notice, document compliance, timely or untimely grievance request, or lawful choice of eviction route has been established here. Those remain actual application questions on remand. Missing source material proves no litigation default, waiver or failure of proof.
