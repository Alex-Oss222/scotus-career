**Participation:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer participate. The Chief Justice's position is outside this handoff.

## Components and competing paths

**A — replacement authority:** all eight accept that the absence of a timely lawful state replacement permitted federal remedial action. Growe's state-priority rule is satisfied by the described failed state proceedings; no Justice would revive the obsolete map or reach the separate legislative judgments. The congressional court remains bound by population equality and Section 2. This is an agreed premise, not a need to announce a new remedial doctrine.

**B — capacity rule:** the governing Court rule is Valdespino's alternative numerical-majority and rigorously proved functional-capacity routes for one protected group. Cohesion, relevant majority-bloc voting, geography, causation and independent totality remain. A proposal combining distinct protected groups is a different question; the actual lack-of-common-cohesion finding is not erased. The Justices' own continued disagreements with the governing rule are identified below and are not mislabeled as present law.

**C — appellate disposition:** a limited-vacatur path corrects the numerical premise and requires identification of any preserved single-group functional claim and independent lawful grounds. Its strongest objection is that a developed trial already produced compactness and totality findings, so remand may merely repeat a failed claim. An affirmance path treats those findings as independently sufficient, or adopts the Justice's retained statutory objection to the requested districting claim. Its strongest objection is that the compactness and totality language is itself keyed to additional majority-Latino citizenship districts and therefore does not show a functional-capacity inquiry under the governing rule. Neither path can infer that absent research exhibits were absent from the trial.

## Frozen scope and legal consequences

Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer support limited vacatur and the same stated remand rationale. O'Connor and Kennedy support affirmance while retaining their numerical-majority disagreement with Valdespino. Scalia and Thomas support affirmance on statutory noncoverage, supplying no join to either numerical or functional capacity analysis. Four votes for each judgment path among the eight non-Stone Justices do not determine the Court's action and are not a proposition-level majority.

A vacatur supported at assembly would concern the appealed congressional rejection of additional Latino districts. It would not revive the obsolete map, alter House or Senate judgments, decide the existence of a qualifying crossover district, automatically reopen evidence, or set an election date. The lower court would identify the claims and proof properly before it, apply both capacity routes where implicated, and determine whether an independent ground resolves the claim. Existing operative election arrangements remain unless lawfully modified. An affirmance supported at assembly would leave the congressional judgment in place; its public precedential scope must follow the actual rationale receiving the required joins and cannot import an unjoined numerical rule.
