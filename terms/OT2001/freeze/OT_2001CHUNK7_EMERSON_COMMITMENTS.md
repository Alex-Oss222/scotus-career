## Shared grounds and material distinctions

**Personal coverage.** Love's individual-holder holding and Hale's separation of coverage from regulation govern, and Napier applies them to an ordinary pistol. The right is not extinguished by describing the holder as subject to a domestic-relations order. The competing collective-only disposition is no longer lawful under entering doctrine. The individual-right question therefore requires application of settled law, not a new original-meaning decision or adoption of the Fifth Circuit's lengthy historical analysis.

**Statutory reading.** Subparagraphs (A) and (B) remain required; (C)(i) and (C)(ii) are alternatives. An express credible-threat finding is necessary for the first alternative, not a textual element of the second. An order satisfying those textual conditions can still impose an unconstitutional federal burden in a particular case. This distinction is already necessary to Napier's treatment of the alternatives, and no Justice makes the statute's disjunction disappear to avoid the constitutional inquiry.

**Civil process and prospective prevention.** Napier forecloses an indispensable-conviction rule and rejects the idea that government must wait for another assault. Its decided facts included completed violence and recurrence findings. It did not decide the precise threat-only boundary now presented. The present six-Justice commitment extends its principle to an adjudicated protective necessity established through the legally operative hearing-and-order process described below. The two contrary commitments do not demand a criminal conviction or require the statutory phrase; they demand further constitutional application to the actual full possession burden.

**Fair warning and statutory fault.** The public Staples holding requires knowledge of relevant weapon characteristics while rejecting a general requirement to know nonregistration or illegality. Bryan separately requires knowing unlawful dealing under a willfulness provision; its rule does not rewrite §924(a)(2)'s knowledge language. Lambert protects against the distinct combination of wholly passive conduct, no knowledge and no circumstances prompting inquiry. Emerson signed a form expressly identifying the restraining-order disability, later knew the order and its contents, and allegedly possessed the identified pistol more than sixty days afterward. The warning is not an irrebuttable finding that he remembered the law, and the present disposition does not find every offense element proved. It does establish that the blanket ignorance-of-law theory relied on below cannot support pretrial dismissal on this record. No additional warning from the divorce judge is constitutionally indispensable on these facts.

**Collateral review.** Ordinary relitigation of an operative state injunction and constitutional review of the additional federal disability are distinct. The six affirming Justices accept that the injunction retains its operative effect until lawfully altered and do not authorize routine federal retrial of the divorce evidence. They nevertheless evaluate whether the process and legal predicate can constitutionally support the federal restriction. Scalia and Thomas likewise reject ordinary collateral retrial but require a more complete restriction-specific constitutional determination. None joins an unlimited rule insulating every formally qualifying civil order from constitutional scrutiny.

## Affirming ground and its limits

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer commit to the following sufficient ground, at this level of generality:

A force-prohibiting domestic-relations injunction entered after actual notice and a meaningful opportunity to contest specific evidence of violent threats may supply Napier's constitutionally adequate civil danger predicate when the applicable law requires a real, nonspeculative protective necessity for the restraint, the order actually addresses that threatened injury, and the resulting disability remains dependent on that operative order. A completed assault and the precise (C)(i) words are not indispensable. On this record, the resulting possession restriction is a substantial regulation under Wright and satisfies intermediate scrutiny.

The application rests on the combined hearing, specific testimony, protective terms, substantive state-law requirement and continuing judicial control. The wife's testimony concerned a threat to kill her friend communicated to her; it was not testimony that Emerson threatened to kill her. But the state court could protect an intimate partner against violent intimidation communicated through a threat to another person, and its order restrained both that mode of threat and physical injury. A real threat may justify preventive protection before completed injury. The legally entered protective order after that hearing provides the adjudicative predicate; the Court does not manufacture an express finding or declare the underlying threat separately proved as a federal crime.

This is a judgment about the legal significance of an actual adjudication under the stated state-law standards. It is not a factual claim that the issuing judge uttered an unrecorded conclusion, and it is not a presumption that every civil-order form contains a danger finding. The best contrary argument is that the form could have issued routinely and that the judge never specifically evaluated a firearm disability. The answer of the six is that neither the federal Constitution nor Napier requires the predicate tribunal to anticipate every collateral federal consequence or use a particular formula; a real protective adjudication directed to threatened physical injury can justify the subsequently imposed order-dependent restraint. On this record the hearing and legal basis exist independently of the order's boilerplate wording. Requiring another adjudication solely because the order omitted the formula would collapse the statutory alternatives and mistake the record's missing words for missing process.

Preventing armed domestic injury is an important objective. Removing access to firearms while a qualifying protective order directed to violent intimidation remains effective substantially addresses the risk of armed escalation. A command not to threaten or injure does not itself remove access to the instrument of escalation; a restriction limited to carrying in public would not address access in private domestic encounters. Intermediate scrutiny does not demand proof that violence is inevitable or that a past completed assault involved a gun. The scope of the possession prohibition remains serious, but the demonstrated protective adjudication, actual opportunity to contest it and dependence on the continuing order supply the conduct-and-danger limits that distinguish it from withdrawal of the right by civil label alone.

The state proceedings permitted modification and mandamus review; direct interlocutory appeal was unavailable. Those are actual legal avenues, not a finding that Emerson obtained review or could secure immediate termination. The order ended at the divorce decree or further order, subject to the distinct treatment of any provision in a final decree. Neither a fixed short duration nor guaranteed restoration is used. Continued ordinary firearm access for other people supplies no answer to Emerson's individual burden. Wright's categorical prohibition on general abolition of ordinary defensive home possession remains intact; this holding addresses a defined individual protective disqualification and does not approve all temporary total prohibitions.

The six reject a finding that the later alleged pistol incident proves the September predicate. That conduct is not adjudicated in this posture and does no justificatory work. They likewise reject treatment of the wife's non-cross-examined account as a conclusive factual admission. An order issued without a meaningful opportunity to contest the protective basis, under rules requiring no protective justification, or directed to an unrelated interest is outside this adopted rule. Those are limits of this rule, not invented additional events or a catalogue of separate unresolved cases.

## Enhanced Justice-specific commitments

### Stevens

**Commitment and sources.** Affirm the arms application and Fifth Amendment reversal. His complete join in O'Connor's Napier opinion, June 1, 2001, accepts civil danger adjudication and intermediate scrutiny for a defined individual restraint; his join in Souter's Fraternal Order opinion accepts prevention of armed recurrence through conduct-based restrictions. His Bryan Part I join distinguishes statutory fault from an obligation to know every specific legal source.

**Alternative comparison.** A constitutional return could ensure that a standard divorce order was not mistaken for a danger determination. Its strongest support is Napier's insistence on adjudicated facts rather than allegations. Stevens nevertheless accepts the present legally entered force injunction after a hearing on specific violent intimidation, under a state-law protective-necessity requirement, as an adequate prospective predicate. The opposing categorical dismissal path fails because the absence of completed violence or express wording does not show absence of an adjudication.

**Joins and boundaries.** Joins the six-Justice rule and application above, statutory alternatives and confined fair-warning holding. Will not join collective-only coverage, strict scrutiny for every individual temporary disqualification, a generalized reasonable-regulation substitute for Wright, or a proposition that allegations alone suffice. A separate writing is unnecessary if those limits are honored.

### O'Connor

**Commitment and sources.** Affirm. Her authorship of Love's personal-holder and state-application holdings, Hale's distinction between coverage and permissible regulation, and Napier's civil-order holding requires both recognition of Emerson's personal right and an actual justification for the burden. She directly joined Wright's complete framework. Her independent Staples concurrence with Ginsburg protects factual knowledge, while her Bryan opinion preserves the distinct willfulness requirement where Congress imposed it.

**Alternative comparison.** Remand would be warranted if the court below had supplied only militia affiliation or a protective-order label. Its strongest argument here is that no express danger finding identifies what the issuing court credited. The existing hearing, threat subject matter, applicable necessity standard and force-prohibiting order permit a legal conclusion without treating omitted wording as dispositive. The requirement is adjudicative substance, not a universal form of written findings.

**Joins and boundaries.** Joins the six-Justice application as a threat-order extension of Napier, expressly retaining intermediate scrutiny's fit and anti-elimination requirements. Will not treat duration, safety purpose or the availability of guns to others as sufficient. Does not impose a criminal conviction, a second dangerousness hearing or a fixed restoration period. The present fair-warning disposition cannot dispense with proof of actual offense elements.

### Scalia

**Commitment and sources.** Affirm rejection of the statutory express-formula theory and the Fifth Amendment dismissal ground; vacate the arms application and remand. His Napier dissent, joined by Thomas, June 1, 2001, accepts personal coverage and governing Wright law but requires actual justification of the full restriction. His Staples majority join supports the distinction between factual knowledge and awareness of illegality. His prior Love join establishes personal invocation independent of affiliation.

**Alternative comparison.** Affirmance has a substantial argument: the operative injunction followed a hearing, addressed threatening conduct and was entered under a legal standard requiring protective necessity. Scalia does not reject that process as meaningless. But the step from a state injunction restricting violent conduct to federal prohibition of every firearm possession remains a constitutional question about the actual restriction. In Napier he required further application despite express violence-and-recurrence findings; this order's absence of an express danger determination and the threatened friend's distinct identity do not eliminate that concern. The stronger state-law inference cannot alone perform the restriction-specific assessment his dissent required.

**Joins and boundaries.** Does not require the (C)(i) phrase, completed violence, a criminal conviction or a new statutory element. Does not find the hearing defective or disbelieve the wife. Would remand for constitutional assessment under the governing framework using the existing record and ordinary further proceedings only as needed, including the actual protective basis, nature and operation of the full disability, and distinction between reviewing the federal burden and retrying the state order. No automatic evidentiary hearing, acquittal or firearm return follows. A purely verbal recitation of intermediate scrutiny would not answer this objection. Affirmance would require a legally supported demonstration of the full restriction's fit, not a new allegation or reinterpretation of the existing unrecorded findings.

### Kennedy

**Commitment and sources.** Affirm under the six-Justice ground. His joins in Love, Hale and Wright establish personal coverage and review by actual burden. His complete Napier join treats adequate civil process and danger adjudication as a lawful basis for substantial regulation without a conviction. His Staples and Bryan joins preserve the distinction between known facts and the particular legal knowledge required by the statute at issue.

**Alternative comparison.** The best remand argument is that a broad possession disability requires more than a generally valid state injunction. Kennedy accepts that premise but concludes the present record supplies the additional link: the proceeding heard specific communicated violent threats and entered targeted bodily-injury restraints under a substantive protection standard. A constitutional requirement of prior completed injury would defeat lawful prevention, while no such requirement follows from Napier. A narrower written-finding rule would make terminology control over actual process.

**Joins and boundaries.** Joins only an order-specific threat adjudication and intermediate-scrutiny application. Rejects a rule extending this result to every divorce injunction, a holding that civil status removes constitutional coverage, and any conclusive inference of guilt from the order. Judicial review's actual limits must be stated accurately; modification and mandamus are not described as direct interlocutory appeal.

### Souter

**Alternative comparison.** A return could prevent a generic injunction from silently acquiring the legal force of a danger adjudication. Souter accepts the threat-only extension here on the combined legal and procedural basis, not by importing the earlier facts: the September hearing considered actual violent intimidation, and the resulting operative order was constrained by a state-law protective requirement. **Joins and boundaries.** Joins the rule only with the distinction between a legal inference about an adjudication and a fabricated historical finding. No routine collateral retrial of the divorce case follows, but the new federal disability remains open to constitutional examination.
