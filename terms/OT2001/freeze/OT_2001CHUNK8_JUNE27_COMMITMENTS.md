## 117. Zelman v. Simmons-Harris; Hanna Perkins School v. Simmons-Harris; Taylor v. Simmons-Harris

## 119. Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb

**Reach, components and law.** Reach the party-to-candidate limits alone. Distinguish (A) admissibility and inferential force of comparable outside experience, (B) circumvention beyond formally earmarked gifts, (C) close fit of the actual Missouri limits and preservation of meaningful party advocacy, and (D) corrective relief. Independent expression is distinct from actual candidate resources. The grant excludes the separate earlier-individual-limit reliance contention; no new selective-enforcement claim is added.

Colorado II sustains its presented federal Senate ceiling on demonstrated risk and close fit, not every state amount. Russell and Shrink Missouri assign the justification burden to government and preserve meaningful association and actual campaign capacity; no literal-impossibility burden or dollar safe harbor is available. Colorado I protects genuine independent party expression, while actual resource transfers and genuine donor conduits retain their own analysis. A formal earmarking prohibition can miss tacit arrangements. Outside evidence need not have been in a legislative record, but must actually support the challenged operation and amount.

**Adversarial alternatives.** Affirm on comparable mechanism evidence and an adequately fitted Missouri ceiling; no completed local scandal or new Missouri study is indispensable. The strongest objection is that the lower opinion treats Colorado II as validating the statutes as a matter of law and asks whether association is effectively impossible. That substitutes neither actual comparability nor close fit. Corrected-review remand permits the federal evidence while requiring Missouri to justify the actual restrictions under the governing standard. Its strongest objection is unnecessary duplication where experience already shows the channel; the answer is that showing a channel and justifying a particular ceiling are distinct, and ordinary existing evidence may satisfy both without a mandated new trial. Immediate categorical invalidation also exceeds this record: lack of a local completed scandal is not a constitutional defect by itself.

## 120. Republican Party of Minnesota v. White

**Reach and law.** Reach the continuing announce-clause challenge and intended campaign speech. Credible enforcement supports prospective relief without completed discipline. The actual prohibition on announcing views on disputed legal or political questions is content based and requires a compelling interest and narrow tailoring. Suster, joined unanimously, rejects a judicial-office exemption from demanding First Amendment review, while preserving distinct ethics and recusal rules. Kruse rejects justification of a speech cutoff merely by saying enough speech remains. Liteky protects ordinary adjudicative views while requiring an actual contextual appearance inquiry; it is not a campaign-speech judgment. Thomas and Watchtower preserve actual-mechanism review without deciding this case.

**Components.** Separate (A) legal views, (B) litigant bias, (C) unwillingness to follow controlling law, and (D) a concrete commitment to a result. Their possible relationships do not make them identical. This case decides only the announce clause, not the distinct pledges/promises rule or any particular recusal application.

**Adversarial alternatives.** Invalidity rests on the mismatch between a broad ban on legal views and the interests in party impartiality, following law and open-minded adjudication. Judges can have legal views without prejudging a litigant or refusing argument. The strongest objection is that campaign announcements generate public commitments and electoral pressure qualitatively different from legal scholarship or prior judging. The sustaining path regards that pressure and its appearance as a compelling adjudicative interest and the actual announce prohibition as sufficiently fitted to it. Its strongest objection is that the rule suppresses nonpromissory views and leaves equally revealing statements outside the campaign setting untouched; separate promise and recusal tools are less restrictive alternatives. The sustaining Justices must defend the actual prohibition on campaign views, not pretend an authoritative construction limits it to promises. No such absent construction is invented. Judicial election itself is neither forbidden nor a waiver of judicial impartiality.

## 121. Board of Education of Independent School District No. 92 of Pottawatomie County v. Earls

**Reach and components.** Earls's standing supports review; no later mootness finding or need to resolve James's separate eligibility is supplied. Reach suspicionless urinalysis for the actual nonathletic competitive-activity population, distinct from athletics, reasonable-suspicion testing and all-student testing. Separate (A) protected bodily and informational privacy, (B) concrete school need and fit to the population, (C) the collection/use safeguards, and (D) supported implementation disputes at summary judgment. **Law.** Vernonia's search/no-waiver rule is unanimous; its athlete program is sustained by Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer on combined actual need, athletic privacy, collection and use safeguards. Invalidating the challenged extension rests on that insufficient need/intrusion fit, not a mandatory epidemic in each subgroup. Its strongest objection is that prevention and school protection need not wait for an epidemic or physical sport risk. ## 122. People Who Care v. Rockford Board of Education, School District No. 205

**Reach, components and law.** Review termination of the existing school decree. The unappealed 1994 intentional-discrimination liability is not reopened. Separate (A) full or partial compliance, (B) practical elimination of violation-linked vestiges and the district's causal burden for disparities it disclaims, (C) demonstrated whole-decree good faith, (D) retained-control necessity/practicability and remedial connections, and (E) the June 30 termination direction. There is no new right to equal test scores, university-admissions question or fresh liability claim.

Freeman permits but does not compel partial withdrawal after full and satisfactory compliance, practicable elimination of relevant vestiges, whole-decree good faith and attention to remaining remedial connections. The district seeking release bears the relevant causal showing; the students need not prove the adjudicated wrong anew. Jenkins reaffirms the complete showing and distinguishes spending/program installation from completion. National parity is not required; neither discrepancy alone nor integration in one domain settles every retained obligation. Existing findings receive clear-error review. Rufo modification and Ruiz's PLRA termination rule do not replace the school completion inquiry.

**Adversarial alternatives.** Termination can be lawful where the district proves the full showing; integration, expenditure and actual compliance are substantial supporting evidence. The strongest objection to the Seventh Circuit's chosen termination is its demand that plaintiffs partition residual disparities and its treatment of their failure to prove present causation as a reason for release, without the complete district showing. Continuing or partial supervision can be lawful for unfinished violation-linked work. Its strongest objection is indefinite control based on disparity, criticism of the decree or a waiting period. The corrective remand preserves neither automatic release nor indefinite supervision: it applies the proper burden, respects actual findings and determines which work remains. No fresh intentional-act showing is required from the students, and no presumption that every disparity is a vestige is created.
