# Hoffman: official statutory reading supplement

Source-only supplement for the March 27, 2002 inquiry. Each quoted span is mechanically copied from a saved official 2000 Code PDF text. All four complete PDF extractions, including neighboring provisions, source notes, amendment notes and pilot-program material, were read. No target-case outcome source was consulted.

## Temporal and legal limits

- Section 10(c) authorizes affirmative remedial action including backpay tied to NLRA policies, and separately bars reinstatement/backpay for an actual suspension or discharge for cause. Its material language predates the employment and discharge here.
- Section 1324a's knowing-hire/knowing-continuation prohibitions, verification system and employment-specific authorization definition originate in the 1986 enactment. The 2000 edition includes later amendments: 1996 changed acceptable-document lists, added technical/procedural good-faith compliance, specified multiemployer arrangements and express federal-entity coverage. Those changes do not establish the rules applicable to original hiring. Deletion of the birth-certificate category in 1996 does not make the earlier use unlawful for that later reason.
- Section 1324c was added November 29, 1990 and applies to violations on or after that date. It was not in force for the earlier hiring. This supplement finds no later job-specific violation. Its 1996 benefit/application additions are not retrospectively applied to the backpay period; the retained document-use prohibition matters only within its own temporal and substantive reach.
- Section 1546(b)'s prohibition of specified identification use or false attestation for section 1324a(b) verification was added in 1986; the 1988 technical amendment applies as if included then. The 2000 five-year maximum follows a 1994 penalty change and is not the original-hiring penalty. No criminal conviction, sentencing finding or new use event is established.
- These provisions do not generally criminalize an unauthorized worker's mere performance of work. The operative restrictions concern covered knowing employment, verification duties and specified fraudulent documentation. A remedial-conflict analysis must identify the prohibited act rather than rename every wage payment unlawful employment.
- The material original prohibitions predate the 1991 divergence. No later amendment relied on here abrogates a differently decided Supreme Court matter. Later pilot programs and unrelated neighboring provisions are not applied.

## 8 U.S.C. 1324a(h)

~~~text
(h) Miscellaneous provisions 
(1) Documentation 
In providing documentation or endorsement 
of authorization of aliens (other than aliens 
lawfully admitted for permanent residence) 
authorized to be employed in the United 
States, the Attorney General shall provide 
that any limitations with respect to the period 
or type of employment or employer shall be 
conspicuously stated on the documentation or 
endorsement. 
(2) Preemption 
The provisions of this section preempt any 
State or local law imposing civil or criminal 
sanctions (other than through licensing and 
similar laws) upon those who employ, or re-
cruit or refer for a fee for employment, unau-
thorized aliens. 
(3) Definition of unauthorized alien 
As used in this section, the term ‘‘unauthor-
ized alien’’ means, with respect to the employ-
ment of an alien at a particular time, that the 
alien is not at that time either (A) an alien 
lawfully admitted for permanent residence, or 
(B) authorized to be so employed by this chap-
ter or by the Attorney General.
~~~
