## 55 — Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition — April 16, 2002

### Judgment and supported rule

The eight Associates provisionally affirm the Ninth Circuit's invalidation of the challenged appearance and presentation definitions. Their common sufficient ground is substantial First Amendment overbreadth. They do not require a separate vagueness ruling or decide a freestanding constitutional challenge to the allocation of the affirmative-defense burden. Relief remains confined to the challenged definitions and their dependent enforcement; actual-child production, otherwise valid obscenity law and the unchallenged altered-identifiable-minor category retain their independent operation. This is a pre-enforcement facial judgment, not an acquittal or a finding about a particular seized work.

**Appearance category.** A criminal definition may not suppress substantial nonobscene depictions made without exploiting an actual minor merely because they appear to depict a minor in sexually explicit conduct. Ferber's production injury and continuing injury to the depicted victim do not exist simply because an invented image or an adult performance has that appearance. Miller's obscenity elements cannot be discarded on the premise that the depicted age supplies an alternative blanket exception. The challenged reach includes protected works regardless of their serious value; restricting the image to one sufficiently realistic to be mistaken for a photograph does not establish actual production abuse. Congress may punish actual exploitation and other independently unlawful conduct, but potential use of a work to stimulate or facilitate crime does not itself remove the work from protection.

The strongest contrary case is Congress's finding that realistic substitutes can be used in grooming, exchanged in a market containing actual abuse images, and used to cast doubt on whether an actual child was depicted. Those interests are serious. They do not establish why a criminal prohibition on the protected images themselves is necessary: actual abuse, unlawful solicitation and use directed at criminal conduct retain their own predicates, while evidentiary difficulty does not transfer the burden of the actual-child category into a general ban on protected substitutes. The judgment does not demand scientific certainty or disprove every possible effect. It rejects the sufficient constitutional connection asserted for this breadth of suppression.

**Presentation category.** Advertising, promoting, presenting, describing or distributing a depiction so as to convey that it depicts a minor is not, by itself, a constitutionally sufficient ground for treating the underlying work as child pornography in every connected handling or possession offense. Ginzburg permits examination of pandering in an obscenity inquiry; it does not dispense with that inquiry or make a nonobscene work unprotected in the hands of a possessor because another person characterized it. A genuine unlawful offer, solicitation or fraud may be punished on its own elements. Section 2256(8)(D) is a definition carried into the other offenses, not a distinct transaction offense limited to the person intentionally offering actual illegal images. Adding those limits would rewrite its operation.

**Statutory qualifications actually considered.** Minor means a person under eighteen. Sexually explicit conduct includes actual or simulated intercourse, bestiality, masturbation, sadistic or masochistic abuse, and lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area; mere nudity is not the last category. Knowledge of the image's characteristics does not cure the unconstitutional selection of those characteristics. Section 2252A(c) applies only to subsection (a)(1)–(4), requires actual adult performers at production and requires that the defendant did not present or distribute the work as depicting minors. It excludes wholly generated images and supplies no defense to ordinary possession under (a)(5). Section 2252A(d) separately protects possession of fewer than three images only when the possessor promptly and in good faith destroys them, or reports them and provides law-enforcement access, without retaining or allowing others access. That destruction/reporting defense does not preserve the right to retain protected expression. These distinct defenses cannot be combined into a general adult-expression exception. Section 2256(8)(C) requires an identifiable actual person whose image as a minor is used or who was a minor when the image was created or modified; its validity is outside this challenge.

## 56 — United States v. Craft — April 17, 2002

### Competing rules and remedies

Six Associates would reverse the Sixth Circuit's attachment ruling and remand. Scalia and Thomas would affirm that ruling. All eight preserve the distinction among attachment, valuation, priority, sale authority, nondebtor ownership and the excluded fraudulent-transfer theory.

**Attachment rule supported by six.** After demand and nonpayment, §6321 reaches a taxpayer's existing state-created rights that together amount to property or rights to property, even when the State protects the marital estate from ordinary separate creditors and requires the other spouse's consent to disposition. Michigan gives this taxpayer present use and enjoyment, exclusion of strangers, survivorship and a necessary role in a joint conveyance and its proceeds. Federal classification is not controlled by the state-law label that the marital estate is indivisible. No one of those incidents is made a universal sufficient test; their existing economic substance together supports attachment. The lien reaches the taxpayer's rights before the conveyance to his wife. The absence of unilateral alienation limits the interest and its possible value, but does not erase its present benefits.

Drye applies the state-definition/federal-classification division. National Bank of Commerce illustrates that local creditor restrictions do not settle the federal lien question; Bess separates the reachable economic right from other interests in the same arrangement. Drye's unilateral accept-or-redirect power is powerful evidence of property, not an exclusive requirement silently added to §6321. Rodgers concerns enforcement and the nondebtor's protections; it supplies neither a half-value rule nor permission to take the wife's interest. Romani preserves specific priority rules after attachment and is not an entireties exemption.

**No-attachment rule supported by two.** Section 6321 attaches property belonging to the delinquent person; it does not create an individual property estate from rights belonging to an inseparable marital ownership. On Michigan's terms the husband cannot unilaterally withdraw, sell, partition or redirect the estate. Drye and National Bank of Commerce concerned actual unilateral powers over value already held by the taxpayer. Joint enjoyment and a veto on conveyance do not establish such separate ownership here. Federal law may disregard a creditor exemption after identifying a taxpayer's property, but may not manufacture the antecedent individual asset. This ground would affirm the reviewed attachment judgment without deciding what another tenancy, a completed severance or a separately owned income right would entail.

**Alternative-path testing.** The attachment path's strongest difficulty is identifying a real individual interest without collecting what belongs to the wife. Its answer is a lien on the existing bundle only, with separate valuation and statutory enforcement safeguards; no automatic half-share is recognized. The no-attachment path's strongest difficulty is that the taxpayer enjoys substantial present economic benefits and can prevent their disposition, so treating the state unity label as dispositive risks letting state collection policy defeat federal classification. Its answer must rest on Michigan's actual inseparability and lack of unilateral extraction, not on the bare immunity of the property from ordinary creditors. The two dissenters retain that narrower account; they do not deny every restricted or nontransferable interest can be federal property.

## 57 — Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency — April 23, 2002

### Competing categorical rules

Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer would affirm rejection of the categorical claim. Scalia and Thomas would reverse that rejection and remand for the complete-deprivation inquiry, background-law defenses and the actual compensable period. Neither side reopens the abandoned Penn Central theory.

**Rule supported by six.** A temporary development restraint does not establish a categorical taking merely because it prevents all economically beneficial use during the interval selected by the claimant. For this regulatory moratorium claim the relevant continuing ownership may not be sliced into the precise period of the restriction to manufacture total deprivation. Lucas addresses complete deprivation of the relevant property; it did not decide every temporal denominator. Penn Central, Keystone and Andrus preserve attention to the whole interest rather than a selected burdened strand. First English requires compensation for the period of an already established taking; it does not establish that every temporary development prohibition is one. Physical occupation and appropriation of a leasehold are different governmental acts, not answers to the regulatory denominator question.

This rule creates no immunity for temporary restraints. Duration, impact, actual investment-backed expectations and the action's character can make a restriction compensable under the proper contextual inquiry; a nominal temporary label cannot convert indefinite destruction of the owner's interest into an ordinary planning delay. There is no fixed thirty-two-month safe harbor, automatic environmental exception or universal duration threshold. Here the owners lost their Penn Central theory below, did not pursue it and disavowed it. The Court therefore affirms the categorical judgment without remanding that abandoned claim.

**Rule supported by two.** A regulation that deprives an owner of every economically beneficial use for a defined, substantial period can take the use of property for that period even though ownership and possible future use remain afterward. The operative complete deprivation, not permanent extinction of title, supplies the Lucas trigger; subsequent restoration limits compensation rather than cancelling the loss. First English's compensation rule and Lucas's actual-taking-period remedy disfavor making planned expiration a categorical escape. This position does not impose compensation for every permit-processing interval: the challenged enacted prohibitions, the asserted complete loss and the thirty-two-month period distinguish routine administration. No invented minimum duration or broad per se delay rule is adopted.

**Two-path test.** The six-Justice path must answer the real economic value of present use and the risk of serial temporary labels. It does so by preserving contextual review and refusing to treat a label as conclusive, while distinguishing a taking's existence from its compensation period. The two-Justice path must answer why the claimant may define the relevant property by the restriction's duration when spatial subdivision cannot always produce a total loss. It treats the government's complete suspension of use as a taking of the distinct present-use period, not a selection of an otherwise useful physical fraction. Its remand still requires a sustained complete-deprivation finding and genuine antecedent property or nuisance limitations. Neither side treats beneficial environmental purpose as a substitute for the taking inquiry.

## 59 — Universal City Studios v. Corley — April 23, 2002

### Shared statutory and constitutional conclusions

All eight Associates provisionally affirm the Second Circuit's judgment sustaining the permanent injunction at its actual posting and knowing-purposeful-linking scope. The conclusions concern the functioning DeCSS tool and the proved dissemination, not every description of cryptography, scientific publication or hyperlink. The agreement below is direct at the stated propositions; it is not a general rejection of constitutional fair-use or public-domain claims.

**Statutory coverage.** Section 1201(a)(2) independently prohibits trafficking in technology primarily designed or produced to circumvent effective access control, technology with only limited commercially significant other purpose or use, or technology knowingly marketed by the person or a concerted actor for that circumvention. These are alternative predicates, not cumulative conditions. Effective control requires authorized information, process or treatment in the measure's ordinary operation; circumvention includes decryption or bypass without the copyright owner's authority. The demonstrated DeCSS posting supplies the designed-circumvention application. Section 1201(c)(1) preserves rights and defenses to infringement, including §107 fair use; it does not insert a general fair-use trafficking defense into (a)(2). Subsection (c)(2) likewise leaves contributory and vicarious infringement law separate. This judgment does not equate every circumvention with infringement.

**Exceptions and precise limits considered.** A class exemption under (a)(1)'s periodic rulemaking excuses the individual access prohibition at the specified scope; (a)(1)(E) prohibits using that exemption or rulemaking determination as a defense to other provisions, including trafficking. The nonprofit library/archive/educational acquisition exemption permits good-faith evaluation for acquisition only when an identical copy is not reasonably available in another form; retention must last no longer than necessary and the accessed copy cannot be used for another purpose. It expressly supplies no (a)(2) or (b) trafficking defense. Reverse engineering under (f) concerns a lawfully obtained right to use a program and previously unavailable elements needed for interoperability of an independently created program; identification and analysis must be for that sole purpose and noninfringing. Necessary tools may be developed and employed for that analysis or interoperability, and information or means may be shared solely to enable that interoperability, without infringement or other illegality. Good-faith encryption research under (g) requires lawful acquisition of a published work, necessity, a good-faith attempt to obtain authorization and no infringement or other illegality. Tool development is for the researcher's own qualifying research; provision is to a collaborator for qualifying research or verification. The direction of dissemination, relevant expertise and notice of findings to the owner inform qualification. Security testing under (j) requires the owner or operator's authorization and the sole purpose of good-faith identification or correction of a system flaw; no infringement or other illegality is permitted, the use or maintenance of information must not facilitate those wrongs, and tool permission is confined to performing such testing with the subsection's retained (a)(2) limitation. No supplied finding establishes that these defendants meet one of those distinct permissions. Their existence is not a factual finding that every legitimate scientific use is accommodated, nor a license to decide unpresented constitutional applications.

**Code and posting.** Executable code can convey ideas to human readers and perform an operation. Its expressive character brings the First Amendment into the inquiry; its functional operation is not erased by that character. The particular application targets unauthorized circumvention of access protection, without preferring an idea about encryption or a message in the films. Turner and O'Brien therefore supply intermediate review of the statutory burden: a substantial interest independent of suppressing expression and no substantially excessive speech burden. Protection of digital access controls against the distributed working decryption tool materially advances the statutory interest. A rule confined to completed infringement would not address the distinct unauthorized-access injury Congress regulated. This judgment does not adopt neutrality merely from a benign purpose or declare that software is always conduct.

**Injunctive fit and linking.** Madsen supplies the additional requirement that a neutral speech-burdening injunction burden no more speech than necessary to remedy the proved violation. The posting restraint concerns the working tool, not abstract discussion. Linking receives a separate analysis because a link can communicate a source or assist access. This restraint is sustained on clear and convincing findings that the responsible person knows the prohibited material is on the linked site, knows it is technology that may not lawfully be offered, and creates or maintains the link for the purpose of disseminating it. On those findings, limiting posting while allowing purposeful direct delivery through a link would leave the same demonstrated trafficking function available. A mere citation, reporting link or uninformed referral is outside these terms. The Court does not make the three requirements and this proof quantum universal constitutional minima or statutory elements for every linking case; it sustains this actual protected scope. The Government's reserved objection to the proof standard requires no enlargement of the decree.

**Fair-use and limited-times constitutional claims.** The defendants assert no identified personal fair use of a movie, and the record contains scant evidence of the effect on prospective fair users. The assertion that every lawful use entails a constitutional entitlement to a preferred digital-copying method does not defeat this injunction; neither does the judgment hold that technologically obstructed fair use can never present a constitutional problem. The limited-times claim was presented only in a footnote below and lacks an allegation or evidence of a plaintiff withholding a public-domain work or an injunction preventing copying of one. Both independent limits support declining that abstract challenge here. The disposition does not extend a copyright term, validate control of every public-domain work or resolve a later concrete application.

### Enhanced alternatives and individual commitments

The strongest alternative treats the code's informational content and links to mixed-content pages as a direct speech restraint, requiring narrower relief or invalidation because enforcement against infringement would burden less speech. Its force prevents categorical declarations that code is unprotected, that access controls answer every fair-use claim, or that all links can be banned. It does not overcome the operative decryption function, the distinct statutory access interest and the actual knowledge-and-purpose findings. The affirmance path must prove fit rather than cite the word technology; the supplied executable operation and purposeful dissemination furnish that fit at this scope. No unstated expert finding about all cryptographic publications or future interoperability is used.

## 60 — A&M Records v. Napster — April 23, 2002

### Shared preliminary judgment and component rules

All eight Associates provisionally affirm the Ninth Circuit's disposition sustaining the supported preliminary-liability predicates while requiring a fitted replacement for the overbroad original injunction. The original July 26, 2000 restraint remains stayed until modification conforming to that disposition and the statutory limits below. This is not affirmance or reinstatement of the original blanket order, an order to shut down the service, or a final judgment of liability or damages. The appropriate remand is to the lower court for a lawful preliminary decree and further proceedings, with genuine statutory safe-harbor questions left for development and application before relief exceeding an available statutory limit is imposed.

**Direct copying and fair use.** The prima facie direct-infringement showing was unchallenged on the reviewed appeal. The asserted sampling and space-shifting defenses fail on the supported preliminary record under §107's four factors together. Downloading complete creative recordings for ordinary listening is nontransformative copying of the whole; claimed promotion does not itself excuse taking the whole work. The record includes displacement of college sales and injury to the developing licensed-download market, rather than only the circular assertion that every unlicensed use deprives an owner of a fee. The contrary survey was considered and given little weight, not excluded. Complete downloads shared with strangers are not the private home time shifting assessed in Sony, and the asserted space shifting is not confined to moving one's lawfully owned copy between personal devices. The result holds even assuming the owners bear the preliminary burden of likely defeating fair use; it need not resolve that burden for all preliminary proceedings. Campbell bars a dispositive inference from commerciality alone. Artist-authorized files and other proved lawful uses remain lawful and material.

**Contributory liability.** Sony prevents imputing liability solely because a technology is capable of infringing use when it has substantial noninfringing capability. It therefore requires looking beyond current infringement percentages. That protection does not automatically immunize ongoing, materially assisting operation after knowledge of specific infringement. The notices identifying more than 12,000 infringing files, continued availability after asserted account terminations, and the search/index service support likely knowledge and material contribution at the confined scope. General awareness that copyrighted music is exchanged is not substituted for notice of unauthorized files. The ruling does not import any later inducement doctrine, condemn a device manufacturer solely for making a useful product, or require access to file contents the servers do not possess.

**Vicarious liability.** A direct financial benefit from infringement and the right and practical ability to supervise remain separate elements. The supported attraction of infringing recordings to the user base can supply financial benefit even before direct transaction fees, and Napster's account exclusion and index control can supply practical supervisory capacity over the assistance it provides. They do not give it access to the contents of users' MP3 files. This is a distinct sufficient preliminary theory at the proved scope, not a rule eliminating the knowledge requirement from contributory liability or imposing an unlimited duty to inspect every file. The architecture limits both the inference of control and permissible relief.

**Audio Home Recording Act.** Section 1008's protection is defined by covered devices, media and consumer recording acts. A digital musical recording under §1001(5) contains fixed sounds and material incidental to them; an object with computer programs is excluded except for instructions constituting the sounds or incidental material or instructions used to perceive, reproduce or communicate those sounds and incidental material. A digital audio recording device must have a digital recording function designed or marketed primarily to make digital audio copied recordings for private use, with professional equipment and primarily nonmusical dictation/answering equipment excluded. The protected consumer use of such a device does not create immunity for all noncommercial copying on general-purpose computers, nor for open redistribution through this service. This conclusion does not invalidate §1008 or classify every future dedicated player.

**Section 512.** Secondary-liability status alone does not defeat a safe harbor. The functions in (a)–(d) must be tested separately, and failure under one does not establish failure under another. Subsection (a) concerns automatic user-initiated transmitting, routing or connecting through the provider's system without provider selection of material or recipients except an automatic response, with no modification and no accessible copy kept beyond what transmission reasonably needs. Subsection (d)'s location-tool protection requires absence of actual knowledge or apparent-infringement awareness, or expeditious removal/disablement upon obtaining it; no directly attributable financial benefit where the provider has the right and ability to control; and expeditious response to a qualifying notice identifying the reference or link sufficiently to locate it. Subsection (i) additionally requires adoption, reasonable implementation and communication of a repeat-infringer termination policy and accommodation without interference of standard technical measures. Such measures must arise from broad open, fair, voluntary multi-industry consensus, be reasonably and nondiscriminatorily available, and impose no substantial provider cost or system burden.

A qualifying notice must substantially identify the copyrighted work or representative site list, the material or location reference and sufficient location information, provide contact information, bear an authorized signature, state a good-faith belief that the use lacks authorization from owner, agent or law, and state accuracy and authority under penalty of perjury. A materially defective notice is not automatically proof of statutory knowledge; when it substantially supplies work, material/location and contact information, the provider must attempt contact or reasonable assistance before claiming the defective-notice exclusion. The preliminary notices support the underlying knowledge issue as recited, but this freeze does not fabricate findings that each formal notice condition, service category or policy requirement has finally been established.

Section 512(m) does not permit conditioning safe harbors on affirmative monitoring or searching for facts of infringement, apart from qualifying standard technical measures, or on access/removal forbidden by law. Failure to qualify is not affirmative evidence against an independent noninfringement defense under (l). Under (j), a provider protected from monetary relief still may receive only the permitted forms of injunction: for non-(a) conduct, specified-site access restraint, termination of specified infringing accounts, or other necessary relief addressing specified copyrighted material at a particular online location that is least burdensome among comparably effective measures; for (a) conduct, only termination of specified infringing accounts and reasonable steps blocking a specific identified foreign online location. The court must consider cumulative system burden, likely owner harm, technical feasibility and effectiveness, effects on access to lawful material elsewhere, and comparably effective less burdensome means. Notice and an opportunity to appear are required except evidence-preservation or other orders with no material adverse network effect. These limitations must govern if their predicates are satisfied; an unresolved classification is not permission to evade them through a generalized policing command.

**Remaining defenses and preliminary objections.** Exploring commercial MP3 distribution does not show intentional abandonment of ownership, and the asserted implied license does not overcome the owners' express objection to this service. Misuse requires an improper extension of copyright; enforcing reproduction and distribution rights in a new medium is not itself that extension, and none is proved on this record. The requested additional discovery and hearing do not show an abuse of discretion in this preliminary proceeding; that is no rule dispensing with a hearing whenever copyright is asserted. The $5 million Rule 65(c) security is sustained against the competing valuation evidence, without requiring security equal to the service's claimed enterprise value. No statutory authority or special circumstance compels a royalty substitute; Tasini and Campbell do not create a compulsory license. The First Amendment requires lawful expression and authorized exchanges to remain outside the remedy, rather than supplying a categorical immunity for infringing copying.

**Fitted decree.** Owners must identify protected works and sufficiently locate the infringing files for feasible response; Napster must act within its practical index and account controls on adequately identified infringement. The order cannot make Napster guarantee prevention of all unknown infringement, assume server access to file contents, prohibit artist-authorized works or replace the distinct statutory notice rules. The district court must evaluate any applicable §512 protection and (j) limitation when framing the replacement. The original stayed order is not revived pending that work. The present likely-liability findings do not bind the final merits beyond the ordinary operation of preliminary findings.
