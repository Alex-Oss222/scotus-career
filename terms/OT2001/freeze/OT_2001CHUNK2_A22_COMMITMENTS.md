## Common component definitions and alternatives

C1: Section 261's assignment, grant and conveyance language concerns transfers of patent ownership, judged by the instrument's legal effect. A nonownership security interest like the stipulated one is outside that recording requirement. Waterman's title-transferring mortgage supports, rather than defeats, the distinction.

C2: Independently, section 261's subsequent mortgagee protection does not attach to the trustee's section 544(a)(1) hypothetical lien-creditor status. The preserved claim does not make the trustee a title-taking mortgagee. An alternative execution-creditor theory and purchaser theory were not preserved below and supply no enlarged status here.

C3: Former California sections 9104(a) and 9302(3)-(4) defer to federal law governing the relevant security transaction or providing a federal system for perfecting that interest. A national register of patent title does not by itself disable California perfection of nonownership security interests. The admitted California filing therefore establishes priority over this later trustee, and the challenged stay-relief ruling is affirmed.

The alternative national-filing path has a real notice/uniformity rationale and invokes the word mortgagee. Its strongest objection to C1 is that old title-mortgage terminology should adapt to modern security devices. But Congress specified ownership transfers and particular competing transferees; courts cannot treat all collateral as conveyances or all lien creditors as title-taking mortgagees. The strongest objection to C3 reads property subject to national registration literally, without asking what interest is registered. That would turn a title register with no mandatory perfection mechanism for the interest into a bar to any effective filing. The text and operation of the step-back provisions support a transaction-specific reading. The alternative fails on this stipulated instrument and preserved trustee status, without making every security instrument immune from federal recording.

## Remedy and complete resolution

All eight support affirmance of the Ninth Circuit's judgment sustaining California perfection, priority over the trustee's presented section 544(a)(1) status, and relief from the automatic stay. Ordinary foreclosure proceeds within the rights actually created and existing bankruptcy rules. C1 and C2 are independent federal reasons; C3 resolves the preserved state-law alternative necessary to the complete priority answer. No added factfinding is necessary on the stipulated nonownership transaction and filing compliance.
