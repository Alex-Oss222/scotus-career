provisions for alienage jurisdiction of federal courts in Article 
[Il of the Constitution and the original Judiciary Act of 1789

Virgin Islands International Business Companies Act § 5(1).

hundred years ago.” Koehler, 229 F.3d at 189 (Sotomayor, 
J., dissenting). The Matimak decision in its rush to render

compounded its error in Matimak by refusing to look at any 
of the indicia that clearly demonstrate that residents and 
corporations from the British Virgin Islands are under the 
sovereignty of the United Kingdom or to accord any weight 
to the position of the United States in this matter. A review 
of the Constitution of the British Virgin Islands shows that 
the United Kingdom retains full sovereignty over the islands, 
remains responsible for core government functions, and has 
the right to review all laws passed by a legislature that has 
sworn its allegiance to the United Kingdom. In light of the 
foregoing, as Judge Sotomayor has pointed out, “[t]he people 
of Bermuda [and the other United Kingdom Overseas 
Territories] would undoubtedly be surprised to learn that they 
are ‘stateless.”” Jd. at 188. This is especially true for the 
British Virgin Islands, whose own Attorney General has 
opined that the residents and corporations are subjects of the 
United Kingdom. (L 35-36). Defining millions of people and 
corporations from the United Kingdom’s (and other nation’s) 
territories as “stateless” contradicts basic notions of western 
jurisprudence, violates the intent of the Framers of the 
Constitution, and unsettles an area of law which has been 
applied without incident for two centuries.
