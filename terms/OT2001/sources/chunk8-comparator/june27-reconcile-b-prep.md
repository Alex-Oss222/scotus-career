## People Who Care: comparator and remedy

The historical comparator is a lower-court judgment, not a Supreme Court merits lineup. Posner wrote for the panel of Bauer, Posner, and Kanne. The judgment reversed and remanded with instructions to grant the school board's requested relief: dissolution effective June 30, 2002. The court expressly declined to provide earlier dissolution than the board requested. Do not restate this as an immediate termination on April 18, 2001 or as a historical Supreme Court merits ruling.

The opinion's central historical premises are its assessment that the schools had met the magistrate judge's racial-composition measure; the expenditures and improvements; the absence of plaintiffs' evidence separating educational disparities caused by the board's past discrimination from disparities attributable to other causes; open enrollment in advanced classes; and the distinction between compliance without exploiting loopholes and enthusiasm for the decree. It treated further discrimination after dissolution as a possible basis for new litigation, not as a reason to retain the old decree indefinitely.

Preserve these source distinctions:

- The 1994 intentional-discrimination finding was unappealed. The panel's later use of the phrase de facto segregation does not withdraw that finding or turn liability into an unresolved issue.
- The 15-percentage-point school-composition measure is the magistrate judge's reported measure. Satisfaction of it is not itself a finding resolving every possible remedial component under a different governing legal standard.
- The panel characterizes the plaintiffs' evidence and arguments. Its absence-of-evidence assessment does not independently establish that every continuing achievement disparity is unrelated to the board's violation.
- The panel reports that plaintiffs' lawyer could identify no decree violation when pressed at argument. This receipt has not separately inspected the argument transcript, all compliance evidence, or the underlying remedial orders. That reported exchange is not a newly certified finding of completion of every remedy.
- Litigation age and expenditure were part of the panel's reasoning alongside asserted achievement of remedial objectives. They are not an independent rule permitting termination solely because a decree is old or expensive.
- The circuit opinion cited historical Jenkins, Dowell, and Rufo. The supplied comparator expressly requires separate analysis under entering simulated Freeman and Jenkins. This receipt makes no determination about that analysis or any Justice's position.

## White: verified historical structure and retained distinctions

Historical disposition: reversed and remanded, 5-4; the grant of summary judgment to the defendant officials was reversed. Scalia's full Court opinion was joined by Rehnquist, O'Connor, Kennedy, and Thomas. O'Connor and Kennedy each concurred separately while joining in full. Stevens's dissent was joined by Souter, Ginsburg, and Breyer; Ginsburg's dissent was joined by Stevens, Souter, and Breyer. No partial joins or judgment-only votes.

No affirmative error was found in the scoped comparator or the existing verification memo. The following distinctions must survive later reconciliation:

- The majority and Ginsburg disagreed about the authoritative construction of the announce clause. The majority treated it as reaching nonpromissory views; Ginsburg understood it as targeting effective commitments and disputed modifying the state-adopted construction through counsel's oral-argument answers.
- The three meanings of impartiality have different treatments: insufficient tailoring to party impartiality; no compelling interest in judges lacking legal preconceptions; and no decision whether openmindedness is compelling because the restriction was underinclusive. The majority acknowledged that some prohibited speech could reveal bias against a class of litigants.
- The separate pledges-or-promises clause was unchallenged and undecided. Neither judicial elections nor all judicial ethics restrictions were categorically invalidated. Kennedy's broader candidate-speech position and reservation about general restrictions on sitting judges remain separate concurrence grounds.
- Stevens and Ginsburg supplied distinct, fully joined objections: institutional impartiality and campaign context; and the narrow construction, prevention of evasion of the pledges restriction, litigants' due process, and public confidence. A general label about judicial elections would omit material grounds.

## Zelman: verified historical structure and retained distinctions

Historical disposition: reversed in all three consolidated matters, 5-4. The opinion's operative direction is reversal; it does not separately command a remand or state a new injunction. Rehnquist's full Court opinion was joined by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, and Thomas. O'Connor and Thomas each concurred separately while joining in full. Stevens dissented alone. Souter's dissent was joined by Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer. Breyer's dissent was joined by Stevens and Souter. Ginsburg did not join Breyer's writing; Breyer's substantial agreement with Stevens is not a formal joinder in Stevens's separate dissent.

No affirmative error was found in the scoped comparator or the existing verification memo. Preserve these distinctions:

- The majority's private-choice analysis depended on religious neutrality, a broad beneficiary class, and genuine independent choice. Its treatment of religious-school percentages does not erase the genuine-choice requirement. Its favorable financial disincentives were expressly not necessary conditions.
- O'Connor treated neutral administration and genuine religious/nonreligious choice as separate requirements. Her absence-of-record-evidence observation about rejected applicants is not proof of unlimited secular-school capacity. Her comparisons with other government benefits supplied context, not an independent constitutional justification.
- Souter disputed meaningful secular choice and independently opposed substantial systematic financing of religious instruction even assuming the majority's choice conditions. Improving secular alternatives alone would not answer all grounds joined by Stevens, Ginsburg, and Breyer.
- Thomas's state/federal distinction and incorporation discussion were additional concurrence reasoning, not a holding abandoning incorporation. Breyer's religious-conflict account addressed formation of children's beliefs, taxpayers, religious minorities, institutional independence, and funding conditions; it was not merely dissatisfaction with parental choices.
- The statutory obligation to offer equal numbers of tutorial grants and tuition scholarships is distinct from equal actual uptake. Majority and concurrence financial figures concern different populations and measures. The competing capacity, tuition, subsidy, and parental-choice inferences remain disputed judicial assessments.
