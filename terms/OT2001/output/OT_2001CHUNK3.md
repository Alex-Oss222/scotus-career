## Decisions and Dispositions

### Porter v. Nussle, No. 00-853

Merits decision, February 26, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 14, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, which reversed dismissal of a prisoner's section 1983 excessive-force action because it excluded isolated assaults from prison-conditions exhaustion. The question is whether section 1997e(a) includes that category. The officers seek application of exhaustion; Nussle seeks a federal merits proceeding without prior exhaustion. His complaint alleges a beating and threatened reprisal; he filed without submitting a grievance. Those allegations have not been adjudicated.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

Apply section 1997e(a) to this prison-life action. On established failure to exhaust an actually available grievance process, dismiss without prejudice. An actually preserved and supported availability objection must be addressed on its facts; neither the alleged reprisal nor a description of a grievance system alone establishes availability or its absence.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Exhaustion covers individual incidents of prison life

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1997e(a) requires a confined prisoner to exhaust actually available administrative remedies before bringing a federal action about prison life, whether it concerns general circumstances or a particular episode and whether it alleges excessive force or another wrong. A responsive process must be exhausted even when it cannot award the damages sought; the statute does not deem an actually obstructed or nonresponsive process available.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices, supplies nine direct votes for the rule, its application and the stated remedial conditions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that prison conditions describes the subject of the action, not the number of prisoners harmed or the duration of the alleged wrong. McCarthy v. Bronson treated isolated misconduct as a matter of confinement under analogous magistrate-referral language. That context supports the enacted prison-life category here, although Bronson construed a different statute. Booth requires the responsive administrative opportunity Congress made mandatory, rather than an exception based on the particular judicial remedy requested.

Nussle's proposed distinction between enduring conditions and an officer's assault has no stable basis in this provision. The Eighth Amendment's different culpability rules for force and ordinary conditions answer a merits question; they do not define this exhaustion command. Administrative review can address individual misconduct, investigate and preserve evidence without immunizing violence or promising a monetary award. The allegation's seriousness does not itself supply a statutory exception.

Nussle's action concerns prison life and therefore falls within the provision. Actual availability remains a statutory predicate, and an allegation of reprisal is not a judicial finding of either obstruction or accessibility. The separate screening rule in section 1997e(c)(2) permits dismissal before exhaustion of a facially frivolous or malicious claim, a claim failing to state a claim, or one seeking money from an immune defendant. That permission neither compels such dismissal nor entitles a prisoner to bypass exhaustion for a viable claim. The prison-life category remains distinct from a challenge to the fact or duration of custody.

#### Precedent Treatment

McCarthy v. Bronson is applied by analogy for the inclusive prison-conditions category; its magistrate-referral holding remains confined to its own statute.

Booth v. Churner is applied to actually available responsive remedies; its reserved excessive-force coverage question is now answered.

McCarthy v. Madigan's earlier nonstatutory damages-only result supplies no exception to the later mandatory text; its prior judgment is unchanged.

Preiser v. Rodriguez supplies the distinction between prison-life litigation and challenges to the fact or duration of custody; that boundary survives.

Hudson v. McMillian and Farmer v. Brennan retain their distinct constitutional culpability functions; those functions do not narrow section 1997e(a).

#### Law After Decision

From February 26, 2002, individualized episodes of excessive force fall within section 1997e(a). The actual-availability and responsive-process conditions remain. The decision changes no grievance deadline or procedural-default rule and creates no free-standing futility exception; those matters are outside the categorical question reviewed.

#### Source Notes

534 U.S. 516; 42 U.S.C. sections 1983 and 1997e(a), (c), (h); McCarthy v. Bronson, 500 U.S. 136; Preiser v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 475; McCarthy v. Madigan, 503 U.S. 140; Booth v. Churner, 532 U.S. 731. The complaint's beating and reprisal accounts are allegations, not findings.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Second Circuit for application of the exhaustion condition and the stated dismissal rule. Any genuinely preserved availability objection is resolved on supported facts. The constitutional force claim proceeds only through the sequence permitted by the governing statute.

**End of entry: Porter v. Nussle, No. 00-853, merits decision, February 26, 2002.**

### Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A., No. 00-1853

Merits decision, February 26, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 15, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Second Circuit, which affirmed dismissal of Title VII and ADEA claims because the complaint failed to establish the McDonnell Douglas prima facie case. The question is whether that evidentiary framework supplies an additional pleading prerequisite. The complaint identifies demotion, discharge, national origin and age as the alleged prohibited grounds, relevant dates and circumstances involving a younger French employee. Swierkiewicz seeks reinstatement of the claims; Sorema seeks affirmance.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The complaint's dismissal is set aside and the Title VII and intentional-age-discrimination claims return for ordinary proceedings under Rule 8. Rule 11, lawful discovery management and Rule 56 remain available under their own requirements.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The prima facie proof framework is not a pleading checklist

**Holding and operative rule:** A Title VII or ADEA complaint need not allege facts establishing every element of the McDonnell Douglas prima facie evidentiary framework. A short and plain account identifying the challenged employment decisions, their alleged prohibited basis and the grounds of the claim satisfies Rule 8 when it gives fair notice; this complaint does so.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined in full by the other eight Justices, supplies nine direct votes for both the rejection of the added pleading requirement and this complaint's sufficiency.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a method of proving discrimination is not an additional requirement for commencing an action. McDonnell Douglas organizes circumstantial proof when direct evidence is unavailable. Thurston explains why direct evidence can make that inferential route unnecessary. Requiring its every element before discovery would therefore demand a particular route that the claimant may never need to use.

Rule 8 requires a short and plain statement giving fair notice. Rule 9 identifies heightened categories and supplies no employment-discrimination category. Leatherman rejects judicial creation of a heightened pleading code outside the Rules, and Crawford-El rejects a specially invented motive burden and a circular barrier to discovery. Reeves addresses the sufficiency of evidence, not the allegations required at filing.

The complaint describes the relevant employment acts, their timing and surrounding personnel circumstances, and the prohibited national-origin and age grounds asserted. That supplies fair notice. Sorema's concern about costly unsupported litigation does not authorize a new pleading amendment: Rule 11, the courts' lawful powers over discovery and Rule 56 address unsupported claims at their proper stages. The claimant retains the burden of proving unlawful discrimination. This holding applies the ordinary Rules to the actual complaint and does not make every conclusory accusation sufficient.

#### Precedent Treatment

McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green remains a conditional framework for circumstantial proof; its elements are not transformed into universal pleading requirements.

Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Thurston supplies the direct-evidence distinction that makes the proposed universal checklist unsound.

Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit is extended in its source-based procedural principle to this employment pleading requirement; its preserved immunity-pleading limits are unchanged.

Crawford-El v. Britton is applied for ordinary procedural control without an invented motive burden or circular discovery barrier.

Reeves v. Sanderson Plumbing Products, Inc. remains an evidentiary-sufficiency decision and does not supply an additional complaint requirement.

Conley v. Gibson supplies the governing notice-pleading background.

#### Law After Decision

From February 26, 2002, the ordinary Federal Rules govern these employment-discrimination complaints without a compulsory McDonnell Douglas checklist. Pleading sufficiency permits the case to proceed to the appropriate next stage; liability and evidentiary sufficiency remain governed by their distinct substantive and procedural rules.

#### Source Notes

534 U.S. 506; Title VII, 42 U.S.C. section 2000e-2(a); ADEA, 29 U.S.C. section 623(a); Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 8, 9, 11, 12 and 56; McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green, 411 U.S. 792; Trans World Airlines, Inc. v. Thurston, 469 U.S. 111; Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S. 41; Leatherman, 507 U.S. 163; Crawford-El, 523 U.S. 574; Reeves, 530 U.S. 133. The personnel events and discriminatory reasons are allegations.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Second Circuit with dismissal set aside for ordinary proceedings on the pleaded Title VII and ADEA claims. The lower courts apply the ordinary procedural rules as the case develops.

**End of entry: Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A., No. 00-1853, merits decision, February 26, 2002.**

### Guest v. Leis, together with the O'Brien consolidated subscriber claims

Merits decision, February 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted on the full merits record. On review of the Sixth Circuit's summary judgments following the 1995 searches of the Cincinnati Computer Connection and Spanish Inquisition bulletin-board systems. The questions concern retained privacy in subscriber information, the personal constitutional damages defense, and the appropriate effect on the preserved claims. The users seek vacatur of the categorical privacy disposition; the defendants seek affirmance. The warrants were offense-limited, equipment seizure and particularity were upheld, and the lower court did not resolve subscriber-specific probable cause because it denied the privacy interest. Each user's actual protected information and governmental acquisition remain subject to proof.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individual-capacity subscriber-information damages claims | Affirmed, 8–1 | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Stone-Zsela | Qualified immunity sustains the judgment on the reviewed privacy claim |
| Otherwise live institutional or official-capacity claims concerning confidential access credentials | Categorical privacy dismissal vacated, 6–3 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas | Determine the claimant's retained personal interest, actual intrusion, lawful warrant authority and independent institutional predicates |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Limited account entrustment does not categorically extinguish subscriber privacy

**Holding and operative rule:** A user's limited entrustment of nonpublic subscriber information to a bulletin-board operator for account administration does not by itself extinguish a reasonable expectation of privacy. Confidential credentials and identifying records of a restricted account remain protected when the user actually expects confidentiality and the account's terms, functions and practices make that expectation reasonable; each claimant must establish a personal protected interest and an actual governmental intrusion.

**Authority:** Part I of Breyer's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg, giving five direct votes for the complete rule. O'Connor also joins its application to confidential access credentials, giving that proposition six direct votes, but does not join its broader identifying-record scope. No aggregation under Marks is necessary.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that access for a limited operational purpose is not automatically surrender to unrestricted governmental examination. Katz protects a reasonable personal expectation of confidentiality. Carter's retained shared-privacy principle and Ferguson's distinction between a limited information relationship and a different governmental use support examining what the account actually protects. A password's function is to restrict access, not invite public inspection.

Miller and Smith remain binding for bank business records and dialed telephone numbers. Both involved information conveyed to another, and confidentiality alone therefore cannot distinguish them. Here the question concerns the user's own restricted account information, its exclusion and identification functions, the terms on which it is maintained, and the intrusion actually proved. The mere fact of an operator's possession cannot replace that inquiry. Nor does an operator's notice concerning access to messages conclusively establish that account credentials or identifying records were made public.

This rule does not immunize records from a lawful evidence search. Zurcher rejects a requirement that every person whose records are searched be a criminal suspect. The equipment could be seized for lawful off-site examination, but that authorization does not by itself resolve whether examination of particular subscriber information fell within the constitutionally sufficient, offense-limited warrant. The lower court used its categorical privacy conclusion to avoid that subscriber-specific inquiry. The affected institutional claims return for that application and their independent defendant, attribution and causation requirements. Publicly exposed information does not obtain protection through this holding.

##### II. The personal defendants retain conduct-date qualified immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** An individual official is protected from personal constitutional damages when the unlawfulness of the challenged conduct was not sufficiently clear under the law existing when the official acted, assessed on the supported facts material to that conduct. The 1995 subscriber-information searches at issue fall within that protection: the later account-privacy rule does not supply prior notice, and the officials need not prove actual reliance on authority expressly permitting each search.

**Authority:** Part II of Breyer's opinion is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Those eight direct joins sustain the personal judgment; Stone-Zsela dissents from this portion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the constitutional question and the personal defense have different temporal inquiries. Harlow and Anderson require an objective assessment of conduct-date legal clarity, with the right stated at the specificity the actual conduct requires. Wilson and Elder preserve consideration of all relevant law rather than an identical-case requirement. Saucier left that defense in place, and Denius distinguished later recognition of privacy from sufficient earlier notice.

The challenged searches occurred in June and August 1995. The later Carter, Ferguson, Kyllo and Denius decisions cannot establish notice at those earlier dates. Miller and Smith supplied substantial support for treating voluntarily conveyed operational information as outside the subscriber's protected sphere; no then-operative rule made the newly recognized account-specific distinction sufficiently clear to defeat the personal defense on the reviewed claim.

The proposed actual-reliance requirement would replace the settled defense, not apply it. Harlow protects objectively reasonable official action amid legal uncertainty without requiring an inquiry into the official's actual legal sources or subjective consultation. Section 1983's remedial purpose does not by itself remove that established protection. The ordinary merits rule's allowances for reasonable factual perceptions do not eliminate the distinct problem of uncertainty about legal obligations. The Court therefore retains the objective defense. This immunity protects the personal defendants, not a governmental entity, and does not decide whether the disputed searches satisfied the substantive constitutional rule.

#### Precedent Treatment

Katz v. United States supplies the personal reasonable-confidentiality inquiry, now applied to the specified restricted subscriber records.

United States v. Miller and Smith v. Maryland retain their holdings for conveyed bank business records and dialed numbers. Their third-party rationale is limited against an automatic extension to all confidential credentials and identifying records of a restricted bulletin-board account; confidentiality alone remains insufficient.

Minnesota v. Carter and Ferguson v. City of Charleston are applied at their retained-sharing and purpose-specific information boundaries, without creating a general rule for every third-party relationship.

Zurcher v. Stanford Daily is applied to preserve lawful evidence searches without requiring suspicion of wrongdoing by every affected record holder.

Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton retain their objective conduct-date personal defense; the proposed authoritative-reliance replacement is rejected.

Wilson v. Layne, Elder v. Holloway, Saucier v. Katz and Denius v. Dunlap are applied to separate violation, legal notice and personal relief. Later privacy recognition supplies no retrospective notice for these 1995 searches.

#### Law After Decision

From February 27, 2002, restricted bulletin-board subscriber information receives the stated personal, account-specific inquiry; entrustment alone is not a categorical forfeiture. Six Justices support that rule for confidential credentials and five support its stated broader identifying-record scope. The eight-Justice objective immunity holding preserves the existing personal defense and its conduct-date inquiry. A newly recognized constitutional application does not itself defeat that defense, and personal immunity supplies no institutional defense.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Confidential access credentials have an exclusion function materially different from ordinary provider records. She would vacate the categorical dismissal only at that scope and require a claimant-specific showing on remand. Names, addresses and other routine identifying information deliberately conveyed for administration ordinarily fall within Miller and Smith; expected confidentiality alone does not distinguish them. Ferguson and Denius require attention to what was entrusted and the actual use, but do not establish the broader account rule adopted by five Justices. She joins objective personal immunity and rejects a requirement of actual authoritative reliance.

**Scalia, joined by Kennedy and Thomas, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The three would affirm the subscriber-information judgment on the merits as well as on personal immunity. Miller and Smith do not depend on public dissemination: they address information voluntarily conveyed to a provider, often with an expectation of limited use. Calling the records digital or credential-related does not establish a constitutional distinction from that settled rule. Kyllo's protection of concealed home information does not control operational information held by a different person. Each claimant still must establish a personal interest and an intrusion; the existing equipment and private-message dispositions are distinct. The three join Part II's independent objective immunity ground.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The Chief Justice joins the full privacy rule and both institutional remands but would also vacate personal immunity. After an actionable violation is established under the complete substantive rule, including its reasonable-factual-mistake allowances, he would require the official to identify binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the challenged conduct, prove genuine reliance and show reasonable grounds for treating the permission as valid and applicable. An instruction accurately transmitting the authority may prove reliance; silence, unsettled law, departmental practice, general investigative authority or an ungrounded supervisor or prosecutor assurance would not. Knowing material falsification, concealed disqualifying facts or manufactured authority would defeat reasonable reliance. So would disregard of the authority's apparent limits, apparent unlawfulness under controlling constitutional principles, or a factual mistake already rejected under the substantive merits rule. The protection would include an unexpected change in controlling law that the official could not reasonably anticipate.

He would overrule Harlow and Anderson only insofar as insufficiently clear law independently excuses a proven violation, disapprove Wilson to that extent and retain Elder's consideration of relevant law for the new inquiry. Section 1983's remedial commitment, in his view, warrants that change while concrete reliance protects legitimate official decisiveness. He acknowledges an additional factual inquiry and litigation cost; ordinary pleading and summary-judgment procedures would remain. A valid warrant may defeat the constitutional claim, but its existence alone does not prove actual reliance, and later litigation citations cannot establish the official's 1995 knowledge. A prior judgment granting personal immunity would not itself constitute authority permitting the underlying conduct. He would allow the officials to make the newly required showing on remand. His proposal would not reopen final judgments, change the plaintiff's other elements, create an institutional remedy, displace an independent statutory defense or decide a separate absolute immunity.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Sixth Circuit judgment is affirmed on the reviewed individual-capacity subscriber claims. The affected institutional and official-capacity subscriber claims return for personal privacy, actual acquisition, subscriber-specific lawful warrant scope, defendant capacity, applicable source of liability, policy or authorized-decision attribution, causation and preserved defenses under the stated rules. The other adjudicated branches remain undisturbed. The remand does not itself require a trial where an independently sufficient legal or undisputed factual ground resolves a claim.

### Raygor v. Regents of University of Minnesota, No. 00-1514

Merits decision, February 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 26, 2001. On certiorari to the Minnesota Supreme Court under 28 U.S.C. section 1257. Former employees seek reversal of the state limitations judgment on the ground that section 1367(d) preserved their supplemental discrimination claims; the Regents seek affirmance. The federal action filed in 1996 combined ADEA and state claims. The Regents expressly asserted immunity, the federal dismissal became final after the appeal was discontinued, and the employees refiled in state court approximately three weeks after the July 11, 1997 dismissal. The question is the federal tolling provision's coverage of those state claims.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.** Minnesota's limitations judgment stands.

**Supporting:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Breyer, who would reverse the categorical exclusion and remand for application of the federal tolling provision.

The separate federal dismissal remains final. This judgment does not reopen that proceeding.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. STEVENS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and SOUTER and BREYER, JJ., joined. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a dissenting opinion.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Supplemental tolling does not cover this nonconsenting State's immune state-law claims

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1367(d) does not toll the limitations period for a state-law claim against a nonconsenting State that falls outside subsection (a)'s supplemental adjudicative grant because of sovereign immunity. A valid federal claim within original jurisdiction does not itself authorize the separate state-law claim or bring that immune claim within the linked tolling provision.

**Authority:** Thomas's opinion is joined throughout by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Ginsburg. Those five direct joins control the statutory rule and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory reference to subsection (a) limits which asserted claims receive subsection (d)'s protection. Pennhurst preserves a State's immunity from federal adjudication of its state-law duties without consent. Vermont Agency distinguishes constitutional power from affirmative statutory authorization to proceed against a sovereign. Subsection (a)'s general supplemental grant contains no unmistakable authorization for this nonconsenting State-defendant claim, and subsection (d) supplies no independent affirmative indication that Congress included it despite that exclusion.

The employees correctly distinguish tolling in a competent state forum from a federal merits judgment. But that difference does not eliminate the cross-reference Congress enacted. The word asserted does not make every related filing a claim selected by subsection (a). The ordinary protection lasts while a covered claim is pending and for thirty days after dismissal, unless state law provides longer tolling; those periods do not enlarge the class covered. The additional provision for other same-action claims voluntarily dismissed at or after dismissal of a covered claim does not govern these involuntary dismissals.

Kimel authorizes the federal ADEA action. Schacht accordingly prevents immunity on the supplemental claim from erasing the independent federal jurisdiction. Neither decision supplies consent to adjudicate the state claim. The Regents asserted immunity in their answers, and subsequent discovery, mediation and scheduling did not relinquish that express defense. College Savings Bank likewise rejects consent inferred merely from ordinary activity.

This statutory ground sustains the state limitations judgment without treating the federal ADEA claim as unauthorized or reopening its final dismissal. The Court need not decide Congress's constitutional power to enact a differently specified tolling rule: statutory coverage resolves the presented claim. The holding supplies no general rule excluding every dismissal outside subsection (c), and no general presumption against federal laws affecting States.

#### Precedent Treatment

Pennhurst State School & Hospital v. Halderman is applied to preserve the State's unwaived immunity from federal adjudication of its state-law duties.

Vermont Agency of Natural Resources v. United States ex rel. Stevens is applied for the independent requirement of statutory sovereign-defendant authorization; it supplies no general constitutional prohibition on procedural rules affecting States.

Kimel v. Florida Board of Regents is reaffirmed: the express federal ADEA action against state employers remains authorized. It does not authorize supplemental state-law adjudication.

Wisconsin Department of Corrections v. Schacht is applied to separate the immune claim from the action's valid federal jurisdiction.

College Savings Bank v. Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board is applied to reject inferred consent from participation consistent with an expressly retained immunity defense.

Florida Prepaid Postsecondary Education Expense Board v. College Savings Bank retains its distinction between constitutional power and unmistakable authorization. Today's statutory holding does not narrow its Article I rule.

Alden v. Maine retains its distinction between review of a new state judgment and reopening a terminated federal case. Only the former is before the Court.

Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs concerns equitable tolling in its own statutory setting; it does not independently place these immune supplemental claims within section 1367(d).

#### Law After Decision

Section 1367(d)'s linked tolling protection excludes the specified nonconsenting State's immune supplemental state-law claims. The independent federal ADEA channel remains valid, and immunity remains claim-specific. The decision rests on statutory coverage, leaving constitutional power to enact a differently specified tolling rule undecided because that issue is unnecessary to this judgment.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Stevens, joined by Stone-Zsela, Souter and Breyer, dissenting.** Section 1367(d) protects a related claim actually asserted alongside an authorized federal controversy even when immunity prevents a federal merits judgment on that claim. The filing-related word asserted does work distinct from maintainable. The pending-period and thirty-day protection, subject to longer state tolling, preserve an otherwise available state forum rather than create a new cause, impose liability or waive immunity. The subsection (a) reference supplies original-jurisdiction and relatedness predicates; it need not make eventual adjudicability a further condition absent from the dismissal protection. Pennhurst therefore survives this reading. Kimel supplies the valid federal ADEA anchor, while Schacht keeps the supplemental defense claim-specific. Vermont Agency's exclusion from a sovereign-defendant action does not answer this different procedural effect. The dissent would reverse the categorical exclusion, leaving actual filing arithmetic and remaining independent defenses for the state court and leaving the federal judgment closed.

**Stone-Zsela, separately dissenting.** Congress's authority to implement Article III, together with the Necessary and Proper Clause, permits this reasonable protection against forfeiture while a federal tribunal determines its authority over related claims. The clear statement needed to authorize a treasury judgment cannot be transferred without analysis to a rule preserving time in a state forum the State otherwise permits. Text and procedural structure support coverage: immunity dismissal does not erase the fact that the claim was asserted with a valid federal controversy. ### Delaney v. DeTella, reviewed Seventh Circuit No. 00-4145

Merits decision, March 4, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted on the full merits record. On review of the Seventh Circuit's July 9, 2001 judgment affirming denial of qualified immunity to the warden and six guards. The questions are whether a knowing, unjustified six-month near-total exercise deprivation satisfies the Eighth Amendment and whether the prohibition was sufficiently clear in 1996. The officials seek judgment ending the personal-capacity damages claims; Delaney seeks further proceedings. The legal review assumes the plaintiff-favorable account: prolonged confinement in a cell about 122 inches long and 43–56 inches wide, no meaningful exercise alternative, repeated complaints and substantial physical and psychological effects. Shackled showers, visits and medical trips were not meaningful exercise substitutes on that account. Knowledge, response, authority, causation and injury remain disputed.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed at the legal assumed-fact scope, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The case returns for ordinary proceedings under the stated constitutional and immunity rules. Each official retains individualized defenses concerning actual knowledge, authority, reasonable response, causation and the injury required for the relief sought. The assumed facts are not findings, and later summary judgment remains available on a proper record.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority:** Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. That direct unanimous coalition controls each proposition below. The constitutional section applies the binding, unchallenged Farmer framework; it does not independently enlarge what qualifies as punishment.

##### I. Legal immunity review uses assumed facts

**Holding and operative rule:** An immediate qualified-immunity appeal may decide whether the conduct on properly assumed plaintiff-favorable facts violated clearly established law. It may not resolve genuine disputes about injury, awareness, causation or an official's participation or authority; those factual objections do not eliminate a separable legal issue.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the officials' legal immunity claim is reviewable at this limited scope. Johnson v. Jones excludes appeals asking only whether evidence creates a genuine dispute. Behrens preserves legal review on proper assumptions without permitting fact selection. The officials may argue that the assumed conduct was lawful or insufficiently clear, but may not obtain immunity by substituting their account of Delaney's condition or their powers. The Court therefore decides the legal question while leaving the evidentiary disputes to the proper forum.

##### II. The assumed deprivation satisfies binding Farmer

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the binding and unchallenged Farmer framework, prolonged denial of meaningful exercise in confinement inadequate for it violates the Eighth Amendment when it creates a substantial risk of serious harm that the responsible official actually appreciates and unreasonably disregards. The assumed six-month deprivation, known serious effects and lack of a reasonable response satisfy that legal standard; negligence, collective knowledge and duration alone do not.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Farmer's separate risk, knowledge and response requirements. Wilson supplies the distinction between serious conditions and culpable administration; Farmer permits ordinary circumstantial proof, including obviousness and repeated complaints, without compelling an inference of knowledge. The cramped cell and shackled trips, on the assumed account, offered no meaningful exercise alternative. A known substantial health danger need not mature into grave injury before it can violate the governing rule.

Prison security remains material. Short restrictions, a genuine unavoidable security necessity, an acute risk posed by the particular prisoner, or reasonable alternative exercise or protective measures require their own facts. But a general lockdown label does not explain why all meaningful exercise had to be withheld for six months despite the known danger. A reasonable response defeats deliberate-indifference liability even if harm occurs. The prison's five-hour directive is not itself the constitutional measure, and the Court imposes neither a nationwide hour quota nor a requirement that officials always choose the least restrictive regimen. Each defendant's actual awareness and responsibility remain necessary.

##### III. The assumed violation was sufficiently clear in 1996

**Holding and operative rule:** On facts establishing actual awareness of a substantial health danger from six months without meaningful exercise and an unreasonable failure to respond, the law existing in 1996 gave sufficient notice to defeat qualified immunity. The conclusion depends on those concrete conditions and each official's knowledge and role; it does not remove protection for materially different conditions, reasonable security responses or absent personal responsibility.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the right was established with the particularity Anderson v. Creighton requires. Farmer supplied the actual-awareness and reasonable-response rule. Davenport addressed prolonged exercise deprivation among segregated prisoners at this institution; French and Jamison-Bey reinforced the serious danger. Anderson v. Romero preserved an acute-security-risk qualification, not a general authorization for the assumed six-month deprivation. Decisions involving shorter restrictions, meaningful alternatives or particular danger therefore do not justify this conduct on the assumed facts.

Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law. It does not require an earlier judgment with identical cell dimensions and duration, and circuit authority can establish notice without becoming a nationwide constitutional quota. Harlow's objective protection remains a distinct inquiry from constitutional sufficiency. The officials' asserted lack of power may establish a personal defense on the actual record; it cannot supply a favorable fact on this appeal. Later decisions furnish no retrospective notice. The affirmed legal denial thus preserves ordinary proceedings and the individualized factual and remedial inquiries rather than compelling liability or trial.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Farmer v. Brennan: applied as binding conditions law; actual awareness, permissible circumstantial proof and reasonable-response protection remain intact.
- Wilson v. Seiter: applied to keep seriousness and culpability distinct; harm alone does not establish a violation.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton: their objective, particularized personal defense is retained; no actual-authoritative-reliance substitute is adopted.
- Elder v. Holloway: applied to all relevant law existing when the officials acted; no identical-case or later-notice rule follows.
- Johnson v. Jones and Behrens v. Pelletier: applied to preserve assumed-fact legal review and exclude evidentiary-sufficiency review.
- Davenport v. DeRobertis, French v. Owens and Jamison-Bey v. Thieret: their preconduct exercise holdings inform particularized notice; Davenport's five-hour remedy becomes no national minimum.
- Anderson v. Romero: its acute-security qualification remains; materially different alternatives and genuine security circumstances require their own assessment.
- Saucier v. Katz and Guest v. Leis: the retained objective defense is applied without a mandatory merits-first sequence or a new reliance requirement; neither later decision supplies 1996 notice.
- Searles v. Van Bebber: its distinct injury and remedy conditions remain for any properly presented compensation issue; it does not defeat constitutional sufficiency merely because grave physical injury is unproved.

#### Law After Decision

The binding conditions standard reaches knowing, unreasonable maintenance of the described prolonged exercise deprivation. On those assumed facts the personal defense fails under the law existing in 1996. Individual knowledge, actual response, authority, causation and recoverable injury remain matters for ordinary adjudication; no universal exercise timetable or blanket lockdown exception is created.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Seventh Circuit's legal denial of qualified immunity is affirmed. The case returns for ordinary proceedings concerning each official's knowledge, response, authority and causal responsibility, and the injury and other predicates of the remedy sought. Proper later dispositive motions remain available. Factual disputes excluded from this interlocutory review receive no appellate resolution.

### New York v. FERC, No. 00-568, and Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC, No. 00-809

Merits decision, March 4, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued October 3, 2001. On certiorari to the D.C. Circuit's judgment sustaining Order 888. New York challenges FERC's authority over unbundled interstate transmission serving retail purchasers. Enron seeks compulsory extension of the open-access requirements to transmission in bundled retail service. Order 888 found discriminatory wholesale transmission access and required functional unbundling: separate generation, transmission and ancillary-service rates, equal tariff terms for a utility's own wholesale transactions and equal use of electronic transmission information. It also regulated existing independently supplied interstate transmission serving retail purchases. For bundled retail service, FERC both characterized the delivered transaction as a state-regulated retail sale and separately determined that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary to remedy the discrimination it found.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy or remand |

#### Opinion Topology

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The transmission grant includes unbundled interstate service for retail purchasers

**Holding and operative rule:** The Federal Power Act's separate grant over transmission of electric energy in interstate commerce includes independently supplied interstate transmission serving retail purchases. The wholesale limitation governs the separate sales grant; it does not narrow the transmission grant, and FERC may regulate the covered transmission's rates, terms and conditions without ordering a utility to begin retail wheeling.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court. That direct unanimous coalition controls the category and the following limits.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 201(b)(1) retains state retail-sale authority and, except as the Act specifically provides otherwise, excludes facilities used for generation, local distribution, intrastate-only transmission or transmission of energy consumed wholly by the transmitter. Its preservation of lawful state authority over hydroelectric exports remains. Section 201(b)(2) applies sections 210, 211 and 212 and their enforcement to the entities those provisions cover; compliance with a section 210 or 211 order does not otherwise subject an entity to general FPA jurisdiction. Section 201(f)'s exclusion of federal, state and local governments, their agencies, authorities and instrumentalities, corporations wholly owned directly or indirectly by one or more of them, and their officials acting in duty remains unless a provision specifically refers to them.

Section 212(g) prohibits orders inconsistent with state retail-marketing-area law. Section 212(h) prohibits an order conditioned on, or requiring, direct transmission to an ultimate consumer. Its separate rule for transmission to or for a resale entity permits the covered exception only when both conditions hold: the entity is a federal power marketing agency, TVA, a State or political subdivision or its agency, a corporation or association that has ever received a Rural Electrification Administration loan to provide electric service under the Rural Electrification Act of 1936, a person with a state- or local-law public electric-service obligation arising independently of contract alone, or a corporation or association wholly owned directly or indirectly by one or more of those listed entities; and it either served that consumer on October 24, 1992 or would deliver all the energy through transmission or distribution facilities it owns or controls. State and local authority over direct-to-consumer transmission remains preserved. Section 212(e) makes use of sections 210, 211, 213 or 212 unnecessary as a substitute for other lawful authority and, except as those sections provide, prevents them from limiting other Commission authority; it does not eliminate the retail-wheeling restrictions.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a service does not become a retail sale merely because it transmits energy that the customer purchases at retail. The statute grants authority over interstate transmission separately from wholesale interstate sales. Reading the wholesale limitation into both clauses would insert a restriction Congress did not enact. Section 201(a)'s general policy statement must be read with those operative grants and specific exclusions, not made a repeal of them.

New York's strongest objection is that federal regulation of retail-associated transmission shifts an activity traditionally administered by States. The answer lies in the actual service regulated. The order reaches independent interstate transmission already provided voluntarily or under state direction; it does not require new retail wheeling or transfer retail-rate and local-distribution authority wholesale. State siting, local reliability and retail-service functions remain, and no particular facility classification is decided. The connected grid identifies the interstate facts; it creates no power apart from the text.

Iowa Utilities Board supplies the distinction between an enacted federal assignment and independent limits governing its exercise. Gulf Power likewise separates coverage from a particular regulatory method. Mead requires a real delegation before agency interpretation acquires controlling force; the enacted categories resolve this question independently, without a new deference rule. The statutory boundaries thus answer the allocation objection while sustaining the order's limited transmission coverage.

##### II. This proceeding did not compel Enron's additional bundled-service remedy

**Holding and operative rule:** FERC could remedy the wholesale transmission-access discrimination established in this proceeding through the adopted open-access requirements while declining compulsory unbundling of bundled retail service on its independently sufficient determination that this additional remedy was unnecessary. Section 206 does require a lawful prospective determination and order after a hearing and finding that a jurisdictional rate, charge, classification or affecting rule, regulation, practice or contract is unjust, unreasonable, unduly discriminatory or preferential; the burden to prove that unlawfulness lies on the Commission or complainant, and the agency may not leave established discrimination uncorrected.

**Authority:** Part II of Stone-Zsela's opinion has the direct support of Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those six votes control. Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would remand this component for corrected agency analysis.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that authority, the trigger for a duty to act and the necessary extent of correction are different questions. Sections 205 and 206 preserve lawful rates, nondiscrimination, filed schedules and mandatory correction when the required finding is made. They do not make every proposed structural extension obligatory merely because the Commission may possess authority to consider it.

The agency did characterize bundled delivered energy as a state-regulated retail sale. That ground cannot be erased by calling the order purely discretionary. But the agency also separately determined that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary to eliminate the discrimination it found. The Court sustains that actual, record-linked remedial determination without adopting a categorical jurisdictional exclusion. The functional-unbundling requirements address the established wholesale-access discrimination; Enron's demand does not show why the findings require the additional restructuring in this proceeding. Counsel's claim of inadequate record support is not substituted for the agency's own explanation.

The opposing Justices contend that the jurisdictional characterization undermines the agency's understanding of the corrective duty. That concern would require remand if the refusal depended on legal powerlessness. Here the independent unnecessary-remedy determination adequately supports the refusal of the particular additional remedy sought. Good Samaritan permits considered administration within a mandatory statutory structure but rejects unsupported litigation rationalizations. MCI forbids dispensing with an enacted obligation, and Blumer distinguishes a mandatory trigger from a permissible means; none requires the Court to prescribe Enron's preferred remedy. Gulf Power's demand to preserve applicable classifications and formulas does not remove the actual independent ground present here.

The holding establishes no permanent bundled-service exemption. A universal allocation of bundled-service jurisdiction beyond the authority needed for this order falls outside the particular remedial demand and is not decided. The demand for compulsory additional unbundling in this proceeding is fully resolved.

#### Precedent Treatment

- AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board: applied to distinguish an affirmative federal statutory assignment from the independent constraints on its exercise and the functions Congress preserves for States.
- National Cable & Telecommunications Assn. v. Gulf Power Co.: applied to separate coverage from the validity of a particular regulatory method; its actual formula and classification limits remain.
- United States v. Mead Corp.: its actual-delegation requirement remains; independent statutory construction resolves the transmission category without a new general deference holding.
- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.: its prohibition on replacing an enacted mandatory obligation remains. This order's sufficient remedy does not dispense with section 206's triggered duty.

- Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer: applied to distinguish permission to use a method from the independent circumstances in which the statute commands action; no general exemption from a corrective duty follows.

Independent interstate transmission serving retail purchasers falls within the FPA transmission grant, subject to the retained facilities, entity, state-function and retail-wheeling boundaries. Section 206 remains mandatory upon its stated hearing and unlawfulness finding. This order's separate determination that compulsory retail unbundling was unnecessary sustains its limited remedy; neither the possession of regulatory power nor Enron's preference makes that additional remedy compulsory here. The Court establishes no permanent bundled-transmission exemption or general allocation of bundled-service jurisdiction.

**Thomas, joined by Scalia and Kennedy, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** The three join the unbundled-transmission holding and affirmance in No. 00-568. #### Chronology and Posture

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0, with the stated legal and mandate qualifications.**

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Congress may change the standards for future prison relief

**Holding and operative rule:** The PLRA may condition continued prospective prison relief on its termination and preservation criteria without reopening accrued damages or directing a finding that prison conditions are lawful. The State's consent to an earlier decree, and the prisoners' compromise in obtaining it, do not by themselves estop the State from invoking the later statute or create a vested right to unchanged future enforcement; courts must still afford a meaningful opportunity to present necessary proof and decide the statutory case.

**Authority:** Scalia's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, controls this holding and the two holdings below at their stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that an enforceable decree does not freeze the law governing its future operation. Plyler and Rouse distinguish prospective judicial duties from completed adjudication and accrued rights. Robertson permits Congress to change the governing legal criteria while leaving courts to apply them. Benjamin, Gavin and Hadix require that judicial application actually occur; they reject both perpetual enforcement of every negotiated term and termination without the required inquiry.

Texas's agreement to obey the decree does not establish a separate promise never to invoke a later statute. Consent decrees are among the instruments Congress subjected to termination. The negotiated exchange therefore supplies no exemption, and labeling future obligations contractual does not make them survive automatically. No distinct affirmative undertaking or independent accrued claim is established here.

The prisoners' general Article III and vested-right objections fail. That conclusion does not adopt the lower court's broader suggestion that prospective relief can never implicate due process. A concrete inability to offer necessary material proof would require review on its own facts. The reported fifty-hour-per-side hearing limit, without identification of a necessary excluded presentation and its consequence, establishes no such denial. Miller concerns an automatic stay under a different provision; it neither exempts this decree from termination law nor supplies a stay here.

##### Retention requires actual findings connecting relief to a qualifying violation

**Holding and operative rule:** A movant must establish the applicable termination ground: section 3626(b)(1)(A)(iii) independently reaches pre-enactment relief after the statutory two-year period, while section 3626(b)(2) turns on the absence of substantive original findings of need, narrowness and least intrusiveness, not missing ritual words. Under amended subsection (b)(3), each retained obligation, or coherently explained group, requires written, record-based findings of a current and ongoing federal-right violation, necessity, narrowness and the least intrusive necessary correction; generalized injury findings alone do not sustain every old term, and deficient remedial findings alone do not establish entitlement to automatic wholesale termination.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statutory conditions are mandatory and their application is a judicial task. Gavin and Hadix require a genuine connection between the violation and the relief retained. Rouse permits sufficient substantive findings without prescribed language. Adequate original findings can defeat subsection (b)(2)'s predicate without defeating an independently available elapsed-period motion under subsection (b)(1). Both routes remain subject to subsection (b)(3).

The amendment applicable to pending cases requires a violation that is both current and ongoing. Plyler's treatment of the original disjunctive text does not erase that change. This record establishes no violation excluded solely by the textual change, so the constitutional question about that particular operation is unnecessary to decision.

The 1999 findings identified constitutional injuries but did not adequately explain the fit of every retained decree duty. March's return for that explanation was proper. A court need not repeat identical reasoning under separate headings where one supported explanation covers a coherent group. It must nevertheless show why each obligation retained is necessary. A remedy may go beyond a minimum conduct rule, or operate throughout an institution, when the proved injury and practical remedial need support that scope; intrusion alone is no reason to leave a violation uncorrected when lesser measures cannot work.

Benjamin requires a genuine opportunity for necessary current proof when it has been refused. Rouse rejects an automatic demand for a new hearing following an actual assessment. Neither general injury findings nor the passage of time resolves that distinction. Retain or tailor duties meeting the test and terminate those that do not; the State's motion is not evidence of constitutional compliance.

##### The March judgment is reviewed in light of completed implementation

**Holding and operative rule:** The March appellate judgment is affirmed at the reviewed legal scope, with a return for further proceedings only insofar as they remain necessary and lawfully available after the June 18, 2001 implementation. No duplicate findings inquiry, renewed ninety-day deadline, automatic revival of terminated duties, independent adjudication of June's correctness, or new medical-liability finding follows; any preserved medical issue still requiring disposition must be resolved through its actual procedural route under the governing liability elements.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that both grounds maintaining the injunction were reviewable under section 1292(a)(1). The alternative findings were not an unreviewable contingency: they independently sustained continuing obligations. June's implementation then terminated most old duties, retained narrowed force enforcement and left the class action open for potential new relief. The separate August order concerned a prisoner's appeal from a pro se filing-management order; it did not close this class action. These events do not establish that review of March's governing legal judgment lacks any effect, but they prevent treating its ordered inquiry as wholly unperformed.

The reviewed legal standard is complete. Force, safety, segregation and medical claims retain their own elements. For medical and conditions claims, Estelle distinguishes negligence from constitutional mistreatment, and the existing Farmer framework requires actual awareness and unreasonable disregard while allowing circumstantial proof and recognizing reasonable responses. Outsourcing medical care supplies no categorical defense; serious inadequacy alone supplies no finding of the required culpability. Extreme isolation may establish grave harm on proper evidence, especially for vulnerable prisoners, but its label does not establish every liability or remedial element.

The March opinion supplies no separate reasoned resolution of the medical cross-appeal's evidentiary merits. This decision neither resolves those facts nor treats the absence of a systemwide finding as proof of adequate care everywhere. The return preserves any actual remaining adjudicative task without inventing a pending appeal. New relief under subsection (a) remains distinct from retention under subsection (b); an invitation for proposals is not an entered remedy. Any interim protection requires applicable statutory or equitable authority and supported current danger, not suspension of the PLRA by decree.

Inmates of Suffolk County Jail v. Rouse is applied to substantive original findings, bounded due process and the absence of an automatic hearing requirement; its factual and statutory predicates remain necessary.

##### I. Forfeited Rule 11 error is governed by Rule 52(b)

**Holding and operative rule:** A defendant who forfeits a Rule 11 objection must satisfy Rule 52(b): there must be error that is plain and affects substantial rights, ordinarily with the defendant bearing the effect burden, and correction remains discretionary only when the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Rule 11(h) does not exempt this plea-taking omission from ordinary forfeiture review; intentional waiver remains distinct.

**Authority:** Part I of O'Connor's opinion has the direct support of Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight votes control; Stevens dissents from this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the plea rule and the general review rule operate together. Rule 11(h) requires disregarding variances that do not affect substantial rights, but does not say that a failure to object has no consequence. Olano supplies the separate requirements and usual defendant burden. Johnson applies that framework to an important constitutional omission without making its importance an automatic exception.

The contrary argument correctly identifies the judge's affirmative personal duty and a defendant's possible inability to recognize an omitted warning. Those concerns establish why the colloquy is mandatory; they do not delete Rule 52(b) for the unpreserved claim. McCarthy enforced an earlier Rule 11 regime, and Rule 11(h) displaced automatic reversal without expressly abolishing plain-error review. McCarthy did not decide the present choice of review standards. The trial-counsel omission therefore remains error despite Vonn's representation and the prosecutor's intervention. His different withdrawal argument did not preserve it. The ordinary effect inquiry and independent discretion requirement apply without requiring actual innocence or treating substantial-rights impact as sufficient by itself.

##### II. Relevant portions of the existing record inform the error's effect

**Holding and operative rule:** In assessing the effect of the Rule 11 omission on substantial rights, a reviewing court may consider relevant information throughout the existing record, including earlier judicial rights advisements. Such information bears on actual understanding and effect; it neither cures the judge's omitted personal warning nor conclusively establishes understanding from representation or a signed form alone.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II of O'Connor's opinion, giving direct unanimous authority independent of the division over burdens.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that actual earlier court advisements cannot be excluded merely because they occurred outside the plea colloquy. United States v. Young permits review of the whole record in assessing error, and Olano separates a procedural breach from its effect. McCarthy's personal-colloquy requirement remains mandatory; admitting relevant evidence about consequences does not excuse noncompliance.

The magistrate twice advised Vonn of counsel rights and obtained acknowledgments of understanding. Those events may inform what he understood when pleading, but their timing, content and demonstrated comprehension must be evaluated. The Court supplies no off-record explanation and treats neither counsel's presence nor a form as an irrebuttable cure. A transcript boundary that excludes actual recorded advice would obstruct the inquiry into effect without improving enforcement of the duty itself.

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's use of the wrong burden and its exclusion of relevant existing-record material require vacatur and remand. **Authority:** Part III of O'Connor's opinion has the same eight-Justice coalition as Part I. Stevens agrees with vacatur and remand for whole-record consideration but would apply the Government's harmlessness burden.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court of Appeals did not assess this record under the governing framework. Olano requires the effect inquiry and the distinct judgment about correction to retain separate work. The case returns for that application and any other properly remaining appellate issues. The perjury-warning contention remains undecided because it was not resolved below and falls outside the review questions decided here. The case concerns direct review of this forfeited federal-rule omission, not collateral relief or the consequences of every constitutional plea defect.

### Young v. United States, No. 00-1567

Merits decision, March 4, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 9, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the First Circuit, which affirmed the determination that the relevant federal income-tax debt survived the Chapter 7 discharge. The question is whether the three-year priority lookback is suspended while a prior bankruptcy stay legally prevents collection of the same tax liability. The taxpayers seek a declaration of discharge in a reopened case; the United States seeks affirmance. Their 1992 return was due and filed October 15, 1993 without payment. They filed Chapter 13 on May 1, 1996, and Chapter 7 on March 12, 1997, one day before dismissal of the earlier proceeding. The prior stay prevented collection; its material duration and coverage are not disputed.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The prior legally disabling stay is excluded from the three-year calculation. The tax debt remains collectible through otherwise lawful procedures.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS and GINSBURG, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The three-year tax lookback excludes a prior legally disabling stay

**Holding and operative rule:** The three-year income-tax priority lookback in the applicable 11 U.S.C. section 507(a)(8)(A)(i), carried into dischargeability by section 523(a)(1)(A), is suspended for the interval during which a prior bankruptcy stay legally prevented collection of that same liability. This traditional suspension requires no bad-faith finding and does not extend the period for ordinary collection difficulty, every successive petition or every deadline in the Bankruptcy Code.

**Authority:** Breyer's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by all eight other Justices. Their direct unanimous agreement controls the rule, its statutory limits and the affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the Code's temporary prohibition cannot consume the collection interval that its priority and discharge rules protect. The lookback is backward-looking, but its finite protection performs a limitations function; other remedies surviving the interval do not eliminate that function. Irwin supplies a rebuttable traditional tolling background, not a power to disregard statutory terms. The present suspension removes only time during which the Code legally disabled collection.

The taxpayers emphasize the absence of an express tolling clause and Congress's extensions elsewhere. Those provisions do different work. Section 523(b) addresses debts actually excepted from an earlier discharge, not this dismissed Chapter 13 proceeding. Section 108(c) addresses covered proceedings outside bankruptcy; its distinct setting does not forbid this suspension. The different 240-day rule's express treatment of offers in compromise does not make a collection stay usable time under the three-year rule. A broader construction of section 108(c)(1)'s suspension language is unnecessary to resolve this interaction.

Brockamp's detailed tax-refund filing and amount structure rebutted the incapacity extension sought there; it does not make every tax-related period untollable. Noland forbids changing statutory priority categories by general fairness, but this rule preserves the legislated interval and category. Raleigh keeps the substantive tax burden distinct from federal dischargeability. TRW's refusal to add a general discovery extension to the FCRA does not govern a later statutory prohibition on collection. No fraud, concealment or abuse premise supplies the rule.

The return was due October 15, 1993. The May 1, 1996 Chapter 13 filing imposed the stay that continued when the Chapter 7 petition followed on March 12, 1997. Excluding that interval places this debt within the three-year lookback. Because the legal disability operated regardless of motive, affirmance requires no finding that the taxpayers abused bankruptcy.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Irwin v. Department of Veterans Affairs: its rebuttable traditional background is applied to the particular stay and lookback interaction; no universal deadline extension follows.
- United States v. Brockamp: distinguished by the integrated tax-refund filing, allowance and payment-window structure and the incapacity extension there; both of its statutory bars retain their force.
- United States v. Noland: applied to preserve Congress's priority categories while allowing lawful equity within the Code; no creditor class receives a new rank.
- Raleigh v. Illinois Department of Revenue: its preservation of the substantive tax burden remains distinct from this federal dischargeability calculation.
- TRW Inc. v. Andrews: its rejection of a general discovery extension under the governing FCRA remains; accrual or discovery and a later legal disability to collect are different questions.

#### Law After Decision

The stated three-year lookback excludes a prior bankruptcy stay that actually prohibited collection of the covered tax debt. Motive is immaterial to that legal disability. The tax's substantive burden and statutory priority categories remain unchanged, and other deadlines retain their own text and structure.

#### Source Notes

535 U.S. 43; 11 U.S.C. sections 362, 507(a)(8)(A)(i), 523(a)(1)(A), 523(b) and 108(c), in the versions applicable to the petitions; Young v. United States, No. 00-1567; In re Young, 233 F.3d 56 (1st Cir. 2000). The stipulated chronology establishes the collection stay; no finding of abusive or fraudulent filing is part of the judgment.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The First Circuit's nondischargeability judgment is affirmed. The prior stay is excluded from the lookback, and the surviving liability may be collected through otherwise lawful procedures. The reviewed dischargeability issue is concluded.

### Edelman v. Lynchburg College, No. 00-1072

Merits decision, March 19, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 8, 2002. On certiorari to the Fourth Circuit. After denial of tenure, Edelman submitted an unverified November 1997 letter within the applicable 300-day period and a verified form on day 313. The Fourth Circuit sustained dismissal because it considered the EEOC's relation-back regulation incompatible with Title VII. Edelman seeks reversal; the College seeks affirmance and preserves the distinct objection that the letter was not a charge. The reviewed question concerns verification timing, not the merits of the discrimination claim.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The Fourth Circuit's rule invalidating later verification is reversed. The original letter's status as a charge and the other preserved defenses remain for decision under the lawful relation-back rule.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a concurring opinion. O'CONNOR, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment, in which SCALIA, J., joined.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Concurring opinion | Thomas | No other Justice | Joins the Court and judgment | Express procedural delegation, reasonableness, consistency with Title VII and compliance with the Administrative Procedure Act |
| Opinion concurring in the judgment | O'Connor | Scalia | Reverses and remands | Reasonable delegated procedural choice within statutory ambiguity; does not join the Court's independent best-reading rationale |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### A timely charge may receive later verification

**Holding and operative rule:** An otherwise sufficient Title VII charge filed within the applicable filing period may receive its required verification afterward through 29 C.F.R. section 1601.12(b), with the verification relating back to the timely charge. Verification remains mandatory and must protect the employer before it is obliged to answer the substance; the cure does not extend the filing deadline or turn a document that otherwise is not a charge into one.

**Authority:** Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Breyer, giving seven direct votes for this independent statutory construction. O'Connor and Scalia support the same operative cure and judgment on their distinct delegated-choice ground; that rationale is not added to the Court's reasoning through vote aggregation.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that filing and verification are distinct requirements whose performance need not be simultaneous. Section 706(b) requires a written charge under oath or affirmation. Section 706(e)(1) supplies the filing period. Neither provision defines a charge as necessarily verified at the instant of its first timely submission. Prompt filing addresses staleness; verification assures that the complainant adopts the accusation under oath before the employer must respond on the merits.

The College would treat the verification requirement as part of the filing deadline merely by combining the two provisions. That reading adds a simultaneity command that Congress did not state. The regulation instead preserves both obligations: a timely charge initiates the process, and an actual later oath completes the formal requirement. The process is commonly initiated by laypersons, as Love and Commercial Office Products recognize, but ease of filing does not excuse either enacted obligation.

Section 713(a) expressly authorizes procedural rules. This relation-back rule is procedural, reasonable, consistent with the statute and promulgated in conformity with the Administrative Procedure Act. Becker provides a useful analogy for completing a timely filing through an authorized cure; Title VII and this procedural grant, rather than Becker itself, authorize this cure. The independent reading is sufficient without resolving the full degree of administrative deference.

The November letter preceded the applicable 300-day deadline; the verified form arrived on day 313. Those dates make the verification issue concrete but do not decide whether the letter otherwise constituted a charge. That independently preserved question, the subsequent correspondence and the effect of the agency's failure to give notice within ten days require the lower court's analysis on remand. Agency delay is not itself an adjudicated bar or proof that the initial document failed.

#### Precedent Treatment

Becker v. Montgomery is applied by analogy to distinguish completion of an existing timely filing from initiation of a late proceeding; its express cure rule is not transplanted into Title VII as independent authority.

Zipes v. Trans World Airlines, Inc. supplies the distinction between the timely-charge condition and subject-matter jurisdiction, without deciding every processing requirement's classification.

Love v. Pullman Co. and EEOC v. Commercial Office Products Co. inform the lay-initiated administrative process; they do not authorize disregard of the actual filing deadline or oath requirement.

United States v. Mead Corp. and Christensen v. Harris County retain their distinctions between delegated legal force and persuasive interpretation. The Court's independent construction makes a broader deference ruling unnecessary; O'Connor and Scalia rely on the actual procedural delegation for their separate ground.

Leatherman v. Tarrant County Narcotics Intelligence and Coordination Unit retains its source-based rejection of invented pleading requirements in its own field. Swierkiewicz v. Sorema N.A. governs the later federal complaint, not what constitutes a timely administrative charge.

EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. retains the distinction between statutory enforcement authority and the actual prerequisites for relief; it supplies no independent verification-timing rule.

#### Law After Decision

An otherwise sufficient timely Title VII charge may be verified after the filing period under the lawful relation-back regulation. Seven Justices adopt the independent statutory explanation; two concur in the judgment on a reasonable delegated-choice rationale. Actual verification, timely initiation and charge sufficiency remain separate requirements. The ruling governs verification cure, leaving the underlying employment-practice accrual question to its own statutory analysis.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**Thomas, concurring.** He joins the Court on the understanding that section 713(a) expressly delegates procedural rulemaking, this regulation is reasonable, Title VII does not prohibit it and its promulgation complies with the Administrative Procedure Act. All four conditions matter. The rule completes verification of an existing timely charge; it neither waives a statutory deadline nor recognizes an unrestricted equitable power to excuse procedural requirements. His qualification accompanies his full join in the Court's explanation, rather than substituting a judgment-only ground.

**O'Connor, joined by Scalia, concurring in the judgment.** Reading the provisions together most naturally calls for a verified charge within the filing period. But the statute does not expressly define charge in a way that unambiguously compels that reading. Section 713(a)'s procedural delegation therefore permits the EEOC's reasonable alternative, which preserves both timely initiation and later verified accountability. The rule's binding operation and notice-and-comment repromulgation in 1977 distinguish it from the revisable, nonbinding tariff letters addressed in Mead. Commercial Office Products supports deference on this technical agency procedure. The concurrence also questions how recognition of a possible future reasonable agency change can be reconciled with wholly reserving the deference issue. Neither Justice joins the assertion that the current rule is the best independent statutory reading. Both preserve actual verification, the employer's protection before a required merits response and the separate inquiry into whether the initial letter was a charge.

The original document's independent charge status and the preserved processing issues were not resolved by the verification ruling.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Fourth Circuit for analysis of whether the original letter was a charge, the preserved significance of later correspondence and the agency's ten-day notice failure, and any remaining defenses under the correct verification rule. Ordinary mandate procedure applies.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Investigation, personal accusation and public statements

**Holding and operative rule:** Absolute prosecutorial immunity does not protect ordinary investigative creation of evidence, a personal crime report made as the accusing witness or purported victim, detective work on the reported offense, or public press statements. The official's actual function controls; later advocacy does not transform these distinct acts into protected legal preparation, and a personal accusing-witness function does not invariably require an oath.

**Authority:** Part I of Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine adopt the rule and its application to the four functions described here.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that prosecutorial office does not confer one immunity on an entire prosecution. Buckley separates ordinary evidence gathering and press activity from genuine preparation and presentation of the State's case. Fletcher distinguishes supplying evidence personally from presenting it as counsel. These distinctions protect the advocate's role without absorbing every act that assists a prosecution.

Milstein alleges that the prosecutors recruited a witness to recant defense testimony and falsely accuse him. That alleged creation of evidence is investigation, rather than evaluation of existing evidence for advocacy. Their subsequent crime report allegedly made them the complaining witnesses or supposed victims. Its evidentiary function, rather than an invented oath, controls. This does not mean every unsworn charging paper is a witness statement. The further investigation between that report and the grand-jury proceeding likewise allegedly performed a detective's function. Its timing supports the account but supplies no universal boundary at indictment or probable cause. Finally, the challenged public statements perform the press function Buckley places outside absolute immunity.

The prosecutors cannot obtain absolute protection for these acts merely because the alleged evidence later supported a prosecution. Conversely, an allegation that protected advocacy was dishonest cannot alone turn it into investigation. The pleaded functions must remain distinct, and the allegations remain unproved. Lack of absolute immunity for a press statement does not make defamation alone a constitutional injury.

##### II. Legal advocacy retains absolute immunity

**Holding and operative rule:** A prosecutor retains absolute immunity for seeking an indictment as an advocate, preparing and filing legal charging and arrest-warrant submissions without a distinct personal evidentiary undertaking, and arguing a motion concerning counsel. Alleged malice, misleading advocacy or knowing use of false evidence does not withdraw that immunity; the Court retains the existing rule and rejects an additional exception for knowing material factual falsification within an otherwise protected advocacy function.

**Authority:** Part II of Stevens's opinion is joined in full by O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer, providing eight direct votes for the complete rule. Stone-Zsela joins Part II-C, protecting opposition to counsel's reappointment, and the protection of ordinary lawful advocacy, but not Parts II-A and II-B insofar as they retain absolute protection for knowing material false advocacy.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies Imbler's protection for initiating prosecution and presenting the State's case, Burns's protection for legal presentation in court, and Fletcher's express separation of advocacy from a personal factual certification. Obtaining the indictment is the advocacy function pleaded here, despite the allegation that the prosecutors misled the grand jury about their neutrality. The holding does not classify every grand-jury-related act as advocacy.

Directing a subordinate to sign a criminal complaint and pursuing an information and arrest warrant likewise concern the legal charging submission as pleaded. No personal oath by the prosecutors or the subordinate is alleged. A signature alone cannot be converted into Fletcher's sworn evidentiary certification; the initial investigative manufacture of evidence remains a distinct, unprotected act. Appearing to oppose reappointment of counsel is also advocacy, and alleged retaliation does not change that function.

The proposed knowing-material-falsity exception addresses serious injuries, but would change immunity from suit, not merely identify functions already excluded. Imbler deliberately accepts that some meritorious claims will lack damages redress to protect independent advocacy. An allegation of deliberate falsity can subject an honest prosecutor to costly inquiry into knowledge, proof and causation before its falsity is established. A narrow ultimate liability rule and ordinary discovery management do not preserve the same protection from that inquiry. Criminal sanctions and professional discipline remain meaningful safeguards. Fletcher retained this precise boundary, and the costs inherent in it do not justify withdrawing that settled protection here.

##### III. Further proceedings preserve the separate elements and defenses

**Holding and operative rule:** The claims based on investigative fabrication, the personal crime report, detective work and press statements may proceed under ordinary civil rules, while the dismissals resting on indictment advocacy, legal charging submissions and the counsel motion stand. A claimant must still establish the applicable constitutional injury, personal responsibility, culpability, materiality, causation and compensable injury; the expressly reserved qualified-immunity defense remains for adjudication under the law governing that issue.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part III's rules for the reinstated claims and its reservation of qualified immunity. Eight Associates join its preservation of the indictment and charging-submission dismissals; Stone-Zsela dissents from that remedial limit. All nine preserve the counsel-motion dismissal.

**Controlling explanation:** Fletcher separates an act's immunity classification from constitutional liability and personal defenses. Buckley likewise leaves those independent requirements for the proper proceedings after an absolute-immunity ruling. The Ninth Circuit expressly reserved qualified immunity, and this review of absolute immunity does not decide that separate legal defense. Its determination must use the law actually governing the issue when adjudicated, including any applicable intervening controlling decision, while keeping conduct-date notice distinct from later law.

Crawford-El preserves ordinary proof and lawful procedural screening; it authorizes neither a special heightened evidentiary burden nor a circular requirement to prove knowledge before obtaining discovery necessary to prove it. Those rules govern the surviving claims without substituting case management for immunity on the protected functions. The reversal of Milstein's conviction does not itself establish knowing falsity or an actionable deprivation. The lower courts must adjudicate the surviving claims and supported defenses act by act under these rules.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Buckley v. Fitzsimmons: applied to investigative evidence creation and press statements; genuine advocacy preparation remains protected, without a universal precharge or probable-cause boundary.
- Fletcher v. Kalina: applied to separate personal evidentiary accusation from legal preparation and presentation. Its functional reasoning reaches the pleaded personal crime report without an oath, but a signature on a legal charging submission does not establish its distinct personal sworn certification. Its refusal of an advocacy dishonesty exception remains controlling.
- Imbler v. Pachtman: retained for protected initiation and presentation, including allegations of knowing false advocacy; no partial overruling is adopted.
- Burns v. Reed: applied to legal presentation in court, including the counsel motion; its protected advocacy rule is retained.
- Crawford-El v. Britton: preserved for ordinary proof and lawful case management, without a new heightened quantum or circular discovery barrier; these safeguards do not replace absolute immunity for a protected function.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton: the separate personal qualified-immunity framework remains intact for proper adjudication of the reserved defense.

#### Law After Decision

Absolute prosecutorial immunity continues to turn on the act performed. The pleaded investigation, personal accusing-witness report and press functions remain outside it; the specified legal advocacy remains protected despite allegations of knowing material falsehood. No new dishonesty exception changes that advocacy rule. The surviving claims proceed under ordinary substantive and procedural requirements, with qualified immunity reserved for its proper adjudication.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela concurs in part and dissents in part; no other Justice joins. He agrees that investigative fabrication, personal accusation, detective work and press statements lack absolute immunity. He also preserves immunity for the counsel motion and ordinary advocacy outside his proposed exception. He would vacate the remaining dismissal insofar as it categorically protects knowing material factual fabrication or deliberate false presentation causing an actionable deprivation.

His exception requires knowing fabrication of a materially false fact, deliberate material falsification of an evidentiary record, or knowing presentation of such false facts; an independently actionable constitutional deprivation; the official's own responsibility; and the governing causal connection. Materiality concerns the fact's significance in authorizing or sustaining the deprivation. Error, disputed inference, adverse credibility, mistaken testimony, erroneous legal judgment, malicious motive, reversal of conviction and an unfavorable prosecution outcome do not suffice. The claimant retains ordinary proof burdens, and loss of immunity proves none of these elements.

He would partially overrule Imbler and Fletcher, and Burns to the same extent, because the categorical advocacy bar defeats redress for deliberate manufacture of the factual premise constitutional process must test. He acknowledges Imbler's common-law account and the especially strong continuity owed a statutory precedent. He does not claim that historical immunity already contained the exception or that congressional silence enacted it.

The strongest opposing interest belongs to honest prosecutors: easy accusations of deliberate falsity may be costly to disprove and chill independent advocacy. Criminal sanctions and discipline also supply real deterrence. Stone-Zsela accepts that his proposal increases discovery and litigation costs and weakens protection from suit; saying that no one may legitimately falsify evidence does not answer those costs. At this defined boundary, he gives greater weight to adjudicating a deliberately manufactured constitutional injury. Institutional planning around immunity is a real reliance interest, but no property title, completed transaction or final judgment must be undone. The change would govern this and future open cases without reopening final judgments.

Crawford-El permits clarification, dispositive legal rulings, sequenced relevant discovery and ordinary summary judgment. It does not permit a new heightened evidentiary quantum or a requirement to prove knowledge before obtaining discovery necessary to prove it. These safeguards reduce costs without eliminating them. The claim remains act-specific, not a general dishonesty tort or entitlement to sue for every malicious prosecution. The counsel motion does not become actionable merely because the prosecutors allegedly wanted a conviction.

He would remand for that bounded inquiry. Qualified immunity remains reserved and must be decided under the law actually governing it, including applicable intervening decisions; his proposed absolute-immunity change neither grants nor rejects that defense. Constitutional elements, personal participation, materiality, causation, damages and remaining defenses require independent adjudication. No separate witness immunity, independent constitutional privilege, municipal-liability rule, new interlocutory appeal or mandatory discovery or trial program is resolved.

### Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc., No. 00-6029

Merits decision, March 19, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 7, 2002. On certiorari to the Eighth Circuit's affirmance of summary judgment against Ragsdale's FMLA claim. She seeks an additional statutory allotment because Wolverine did not designate her company medical leave as FMLA leave; Wolverine seeks affirmance. She began employment March 17, 1995 and cancer leave February 21, 1996. The district court treated her as eligible at the March 18 extension. Wolverine provided approximately seven months of leave and discharged her September 20; her further leave or reduced-hours request followed September 26. Her physician released her in December. The reviewed question concerns the automatic consequence of absent designation, separately from the underlying notice duty.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.** The judgment rejecting Ragsdale's claimed automatic additional leave stands.

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, who would sustain the designation condition and remand for application of the rule and the separate statutory requirements for relief.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., joined. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Missing designation cannot automatically create a new twelve-week allotment without impairment of protected leave rights

**Holding and operative rule:** The Secretary's implementation authority does not permit former 29 C.F.R. section 825.700(a)'s categorical consequence that undesignated qualifying leave never counts against the FMLA entitlement, thereby creating an additional twelve-week allotment without demonstrated impairment of statutory rights. An employee may obtain relief for a notice-related violation when the applicable duty, record and statutory remedy conditions establish harmful interference; invalidating this automatic consequence does not invalidate every notice duty or make all company leave invariably concurrent with statutory leave.

**Authority:** Kennedy's opinion is joined throughout by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia and Thomas. Those five direct joins control the rule, its limits and affirmance.

**Operative entitlement and remedy limits:** The Act grants an eligible employee a total of twelve workweeks in a twelve-month period for qualifying family or medical leave. Greater contractual or company rights remain protected. Section 2617 requires a section 2615 violation affecting the employee. Recoverable lost compensation must result from that violation; absent compensation loss, actual monetary loss must directly result and is capped at twelve weeks' wages or salary. Interest is added to the compensation or monetary-loss amount, and liquidated damages equal that amount plus interest. The court may reduce liquidated liability when the employer proves both good faith and reasonable grounds. Appropriate equitable relief, including employment, reinstatement or promotion, is separately available; monetary loss is not an invariable prerequisite to every equitable remedy. These conditions cannot be replaced by the automatic additional-leave rule.

**Regulatory timing and exceptions:** Former section 825.208 ordinarily required designation within two business days after the employer obtained qualifying information, absent extenuating circumstances. The employee need not name the Act but must give a qualifying reason, and inadequate information can require inquiry. Oral notice requires written confirmation by the next payday, or the following payday if the first is less than a week away. An employer with qualifying information that misses timely designation generally may designate only prospectively. Later-acquired information can permit retrospective counting of the qualifying period. Designation after return is barred except for two circumstances. When the employer first learns the reason upon return, retrospective designation with appropriate notice is permitted within two business days of return. An employee seeking retrospective protection from an unaware employer must also give the reason within two business days of return; without timely notice, the employee may not later assert protection for that absence. When the reason is known but qualification or certification, including a second or third opinion, is pending, the employer should provisionally designate and notify at the start of leave or when the reason becomes known. Confirmation makes the designation final; medical certifications failing to confirm a qualifying reason require withdrawal with written notice. Intermittent or reduced-schedule leave ordinarily requires one notice unless circumstances change. No retrospective exception is established on this record.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that useful notice and a lawful sanction for its omission are different questions. Designation can enable an employee to make informed choices about protected leave. But section 2654's implementing grant does not authorize a consequence that treats qualifying leave already received as legally irrelevant regardless of whether the omission impaired those choices. The statutory total and section 2617's employee-specific conditions must still do their work.

The contrary view calls designation a condition on counting company leave rather than an extra entitlement. That characterization does not answer the rule's operation: after an unexcused omission, it confers a new full allotment even where correct notice could not have changed the employee's use of protected leave. Greater-benefit provisions preserve company promises; they do not compel sequential stacking of every company and statutory benefit. Employer generosity likewise does not excuse independently harmful interference.

The regulation's timing and information safeguards mean that not every first-day omission is incurable. They nevertheless do not cure the categorical consequence once it applies. The decision therefore addresses that consequence without treating its actual exceptions as unavailable in every case.

Mead recognizes genuine delegated rulemaking but preserves statutory limits. MCI likewise bars replacement of an enacted obligation. Mourning permits supported prophylactic implementation, not every sanction that might simplify administration. McKennon and Waffle House distinguish a violation from the remedy the particular record permits. These principles leave room for notice enforcement without this automatic additional entitlement.

Ragsdale received more than twelve weeks of leave after the district court's March 18 eligibility date. Her September 26 requests followed the September 20 discharge; her physician released her in December. The record supplies no demonstrated alternative use of protected leave lost because of absent designation. The Court therefore affirms the judgment on the categorical theory presented rather than creating an unsupported alternative claim. The general validity and precise content of other notice duties fall outside this reviewed consequence.

#### Precedent Treatment

MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co. is applied to require an implementing rule to preserve the enacted statutory scheme; no general hostility to prophylactic regulation is adopted.

United States v. Mead Corp. and Christensen v. Harris County retain the distinction between delegated binding rules, advisory interpretations and statutory validity. A formal rulemaking grant does not authorize contradiction of the statute.

Mourning v. Family Publications Service, Inc. is distinguished at the scope of its authorized prophylactic implementation; it supplies no unlimited power to alter the substantive entitlement and individual relief conditions.

McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co. and EEOC v. Waffle House, Inc. are applied at the distinction between a violation and lawful individual relief. Neither supplies a direct FMLA damages formula or makes monetary loss necessary for every equitable remedy.

New York v. FERC retains its separation of statutory authority, a mandatory corrective trigger and a sufficient actual agency remedy. Its FPA holding neither requires nor validates this different automatic counting consequence.

Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer and National Cable & Telecommunications Assn. v. Gulf Power Co. retain their requirements to respect actual statutory predicates and operative classifications; neither supplies a general regulatory exception to the FMLA's conditions.

Raygor v. Regents of University of Minnesota and Young v. United States retain their statute-specific timing holdings. Neither establishes a universal rule against procedural protection or authorizes enlargement of every statutory benefit.

#### Law After Decision

The challenged automatic no-count consequence cannot create an additional twelve-week FMLA entitlement without impairment of the employee's protected rights. Applicable notice obligations and claims for actual harmful interference retain their independent scope, as do greater company rights. The majority's rule invalidates this consequence rather than every designation rule, all prophylactic regulation or every equitable remedy without monetary loss.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, dissenting.** The FMLA does not give an employer an unconditional right to count all qualifying company leave retroactively against the protected allotment. Section 2654 permits the Secretary to require timely designation as a condition on that counting. The rule identifies which leave consumes the statutory right, rather than creating a monetary award for every omission. Greater-benefit provisions and the need for usable notice support keeping undesignated company leave distinct from the statutory allotment.

The rule's safeguards matter. Designation follows qualifying information; the ordinary two-business-day period permits extenuating circumstances. Later knowledge and provisional designation pending certification can permit the specified retrospective operation. The dissent does not treat every first-day failure as irreversible or require all company benefits to run sequentially. Nor does it assume that Ragsdale was eligible when she first stopped work in February; the supported eligibility date is the March extension.

The majority's lack-of-prejudice objection, in the dissent's view, confuses the entitlement's lawful definition with the requirements for an actual award. A predictable designation rule makes leave usable without forcing an employee to reconstruct every hypothetical choice. Mourning supports appropriate prophylactic implementation, and Christensen's distinction between an advisory position and a valid regulation leaves this delegated rule open for independent evaluation. MCI's explicit filing duty and Sigmon Coal's missing liable-person category differ from the absence of an unconditional concurrent-counting command here.

The four would reverse and remand to apply the undiminished entitlement and then determine the actual violation and appropriate relief under section 2617. They do not infer earlier work capacity, a medically available reduced schedule, actual monetary loss or automatic reinstatement from the omitted designation. Ragsdale's postdischarge request and December release retain their actual dates. Ordinary employer and employee notice obligations remain distinct, and no paid-leave finding is supplied.

29 U.S.C. sections 2612, 2615, 2617, 2619 and 2652–2654; former 29 C.F.R. sections 825.208 and 825.700(a); Eighth Circuit judgment, 218 F.3d 933. Posted-notice penalties under section 2619 are distinct from the individualized designation rule. The record does not establish paid leave, earlier work capacity, a qualifying reduced schedule or an alternative course lost through deficient notice.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The judgment against the asserted categorical additional-leave theory stands, and this merits review concludes. Ordinary mandate procedure applies. The decision establishes the stated limit on the regulation without reopening an unsupported alternative claim.

### Zahrey v. Coffey

Merits decision, March 19, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted on the full merits record. On review of the Second Circuit's July 20, 2000 judgment reversing the Rule 54(b) dismissal of the personal claim against Coffey on qualified-immunity grounds. The questions are whether intentional investigative fabrication causing pretrial detention violates due process and whether qualified immunity defeats that claim on the pleaded facts. Coffey seeks dismissal; Zahrey seeks to pursue personal damages for the confinement. The complaint alleges witness manipulation and fabricated accusations in 1996, followed by indictment, arrest, bail revocation and eight months of detention before acquittal. Coffey concedes investigative capacity only for this appeal and reserves the actual functional question.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed and remanded, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas.

The personal claim against Coffey returns for ordinary adjudication of actual function, deliberate falsification, materiality, causal confinement, injury and supported defenses. The appellate assumption of investigative capacity does not determine the actual functional-immunity question below. Grand-jury and bail advocacy retain their conceded protection.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. SCALIA, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

The seven-Justice opinion supplies direct controlling authority for each proposition. The dissenters' functional observations are stated in their separate opinion, not counted as joins to the Court's constitutional rationale.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority:** O'Connor's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer. These seven Justices directly adopt each rule and qualification below. Scalia and Thomas dissent on qualified immunity and do not join the constitutional holding. No narrowest-grounds aggregation is used.

##### I. Deliberate corruption of the process causing confinement violates due process

**Holding and operative rule:** An investigating official violates due process by knowingly creating materially false evidence for use in legal proceedings and thereby causing an unjustified deprivation of physical liberty. The rule requires the official's own deliberate falsification, materiality and actual causal deprivation; a false statement alone, mistake, disputed inference, innocent receipt of another's falsehood, acquittal or unused fabricated evidence does not suffice.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that government may not deliberately corrupt the evidentiary process it uses to imprison a person. Mooney, Pyle and Napue establish the protection against government procurement of a liberty deprivation through known falsehood. Their principle applies to the actual confinement allegedly procured here, even though Zahrey was eventually acquitted. The injury is the loss of liberty caused by the false process, not the existence of an inaccurate statement or an unsuccessful prosecution.

The complaint alleges Coffey's personal participation in manufacturing accusations intended for use against Zahrey, their use and eight months of confinement. That favorable account supplies the required link at the pleading stage. An alternative account that Coffey innocently received other officials' false information would not establish his deliberate wrongdoing; neither association nor pooled knowledge supplies the missing personal element.

Albright's fractured judgment on a claimed substantive right against prosecution without probable cause supplies no majority rule extinguishing this independent procedural injury. The Court does not recognize a general substantive right to be free of malicious prosecution or turn every inaccurate probable-cause assessment into a due-process claim. Nor does it decide an independently presented Fourth Amendment claim or when that Amendment's protection ends: those questions are outside the granted due-process issue. The narrow protection here concerns purposeful material corruption of the process that actually procures otherwise unjustified confinement. Buckley's denial of absolute immunity by itself would not establish this constitutional injury; the additional elements do that work.

##### II. The same official's later advocacy does not automatically erase the earlier causal act

**Holding and operative rule:** An official's later protected advocacy does not retrospectively confer absolute immunity on his own earlier investigative fabrication or, merely because he changes roles, necessarily sever that act's causal connection to the resulting confinement. The claimant must prove material causal responsibility for the actual deprivation; a truthful, independently sufficient basis for the same custody or a genuinely independent, untainted intervening decision may defeat causation.

**Controlling explanation:** Buckley classifies investigation and advocacy separately, and Fletcher preserves the same separation for personal evidence and legal presentation. An official cannot make his investigative manufacture immune simply by personally presenting its product. Nor is that change of function itself an independent judgment that necessarily breaks causation. On the pleaded account, Coffey created the material for the use that allegedly produced Zahrey's confinement; the Court does not infer the causal connection solely from foreseeability or a prosecutorial title.

Franks and Malley show why legal process does not automatically insulate the person who procured it through material deception. Fletcher and Clanton preserve the separate need for personal responsibility, materiality and injury: a truthful account may still justify the same custody, and the claimant must meet the causal requirements rather than rely on the mere presence of false evidence. The complaint's favorable allegations cannot be rejected by assuming such an independent truthful basis at dismissal.

Coffey conceded investigative capacity only for this appeal. The actual functions remain for adjudication on remand; he may seek absolute immunity on that record. Zahrey concedes protection for the grand-jury and bail advocacy, which remains protected. This holding withdraws no advocacy immunity and adopts no rule that every foreseeable third-party intervention preserves causation. The same-person chain alleged here is sufficient to reject the categorical interruption theory.

##### III. The alleged knowing procurement of detention was sufficiently clearly unlawful in 1996

**Holding and operative rule:** On allegations that an investigating official deliberately manufactured material evidence to procure the claimant's actual unjustified confinement and personally carried out that intended use, qualified immunity does not require dismissal under the law existing in 1996. This conclusion preserves the ordinary defense on materially different facts and requires proof of the official's own deliberate conduct, materiality, causal deprivation and injury under the applicable civil rules.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson require objective, particularized conduct-date notice, not a prior case with every feature repeated. By 1996, Mooney, Pyle and Napue established the deliberate process-deception principle; Franks and Malley made material official deception causing detention constitutionally significant and denied automatic insulation by judicial process. White v. Frank and Jones v. City of Chicago reinforced that an official's own corrupt procurement of confinement could not be detached from its intended consequence.

The contrary decisions in Buckley on remand, 20 F.3d 789, and Rhodes, 939 F.Supp. 1256, are material. Buckley's express merits reservation and Albright's divided constitutional-source accounts also caution against deriving a general fabrication tort from immunity law. But the asserted uncertainty does not encompass a lawful entitlement deliberately to manufacture the material premise of process used to imprison the target. The combination here is the official's own intentional creation, intended material use and actual unjustified liberty deprivation. Treating his advocacy step as automatically breaking that chain would let him escape responsibility for the investigative act precisely by completing its intended use. The controlling preconduct principles identify that wrong with sufficient specificity, without treating every lie as constitutional misconduct or circuit disagreement as irrelevant.

Elder requires consideration of all relevant preconduct law. Later Fletcher, Clanton, Lanier, Ricciuti and Scotto may explain present doctrine but supply no retrospective notice. Guest and Delaney likewise preserve, rather than replace, the objective defense. The reinstated personal claim therefore returns for ordinary adjudication of actual function, deliberate falsity, materiality, causation, injury and supported defenses. Compensation is limited to proved injury attributable to actionable conduct; acquittal alone supplies no damages.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Mooney v. Holohan, Pyle v. Kansas and Napue v. Illinois: their deliberate process-deception principle is applied to materially caused pretrial confinement; acquittal does not erase that completed liberty injury.
- Buckley v. Fitzsimmons: its investigative/advocacy separation remains controlling, while its reservation of constitutional injury is resolved only for the deliberate process-deception and actual confinement rule adopted here. It supplies no general fabrication tort.
- Albright v. Oliver: its fractured judgment remains without a controlling rationale; no general substantive malicious-prosecution right or rule displacing every independent procedural claim is derived from it.
- Franks v. Delaware and Malley v. Briggs: their preconduct material-deception rules inform the causal and notice analysis; legal process does not automatically insulate the person who corruptly procured it.
- Fletcher v. Kalina and Clanton v. Cooper: their functional, personal-causation and remaining-defense distinctions are preserved. Neither creates retrospective notice, an advocacy dishonesty exception or a general Fourth Amendment duration rule.
- Harlow v. Fitzgerald and Anderson v. Creighton: objective, particularized conduct-date immunity remains; no factual-twin requirement or actual-authoritative-reliance substitute is adopted.
- Elder v. Holloway: applied to consideration of all relevant preconduct authority. Lanier, Guest and Delaney remain consistent with that inquiry and supply no later-created 1996 notice.
- White v. Frank and Jones v. City of Chicago: their pre-1996 applications reinforce notice concerning personal corruption of process causing confinement. The adverse Buckley remand decision and Rhodes are considered but do not defeat clarity at the bounded scope held here.
- Crawford-El v. Britton: ordinary proof and lawful case management remain; deliberate wrongdoing must be established through the applicable civil procedures.

#### Law After Decision

Due process protects against an investigator's deliberate material corruption of the evidentiary process that actually causes unjustified confinement. The same official's subsequent protected use does not automatically erase his earlier actionable causal act. On the stated deliberate creation, intended use and actual deprivation allegations, the personal defense fails under 1996 law. Actual function and the ordinary elements, causation, injury and supported defenses remain for adjudication. The rule creates no liability for fabrication alone, no general malicious-prosecution entitlement and no exception to absolute immunity for advocacy. The litigated personal federal-officer action is preserved without adding an agency, corporate defendant or independent new damages category.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justice Thomas. They would reverse the reinstatement of the reviewed personal damages claim because the asserted due-process boundary was not sufficiently clear in 1996. They do not decide that deliberate fabrication can never violate the Constitution or that a properly presented Fourth Amendment claim necessarily fails.

Mooney, Pyle and Napue concern deliberate falsehood in the adjudication of guilt. Franks and Malley identify a specific seizure-based protection against material deception. Those authorities forcefully condemn the alleged conduct, but the present theory combines investigative fabrication, pretrial rather than postconviction custody, and the same official's later absolutely protected advocacy. Buckley separated immunity from an actionable constitutional injury and expressly reserved the latter. Albright produced distinct approaches to the constitutional source of a prosecution-related injury, not a settled general due-process rule.

The adverse decision on remand in Buckley, 20 F.3d 789, and Rhodes, 939 F.Supp. 1256, demonstrate substantive uncertainty about a closely related theory. The objection is therefore more than the absence of a factual twin. Anderson requires particularized legal clarity; neither the moral wrongfulness of lying nor a later explanation of why the claim should succeed supplies that clarity at the conduct date. The dissenters would not make every circuit disagreement sufficient for immunity, but find this combination of source, injury and capacity uncertainties material. Later Fletcher and Clanton cannot settle the question retrospectively.

Both Justices accept that title and subsequent advocacy do not transform a conceded investigative act into protected advocacy. They also preserve the distinction between lack of absolute immunity and constitutional or damages liability. Their proposed judgment rests on qualified immunity for this pleaded theory, leaves the ultimate scope of a properly presented constitutional claim open, and supplies neither approval of the alleged misconduct nor an extension of judgment to other defendants or excluded claims.

### Department of Housing and Urban Development v. Rucker / Oakland Housing Authority v. Rucker, Nos. 00-1770 and 00-1781

Merits decision, March 26, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 19, 2002. On writs of certiorari to the en banc United States Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit, 237 F.3d 1113, in consolidated review of a preliminary federal injunction.

Four Oakland public-housing tenants challenged threatened termination based on alleged drug activity by household members or guests. Their actual leases covered drug activity on or near the premises. The allegations concerned activity in a complex parking lot, a resident daughter's possession three blocks away, and a caregiver and others' possession in an apartment. The questions are whether 42 U.S.C. §1437d(l)(6) requires tenant knowledge and whether the contrary construction sustains the categorical due-process, First Amendment association, intimate-association or Excessive Fines objections. Actual lease coverage and individual eviction grounds remain for their proper proceedings.

Breyer took no part in the consideration or decision of these cases.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 8–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg.

**Not participating:** Breyer.

The preliminary injunction must be vacated insofar as it rests on the categorical tenant-knowledge requirement or the three additional categorical constitutional grounds rejected in Parts III–V. Any remaining or renewed preliminary protection requires its own supported legal ground and satisfaction of the ordinary preliminary-relief requirements. The named tenants' actual removability remains for the authorized proceedings; the mandate does not direct their eviction.

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER and GINSBURG, JJ., joined, and in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., joined as to Parts I, II and VI. #### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. Statutory authority and the actual lease

**Controlling proposition:** Section 1437d(l)(6) requires a lease ground permitting termination for qualifying activity by the tenant, a household member, a guest or another person under the tenant's control, without an additional actual-or-constructive-knowledge requirement. Inclusion of that ground is mandatory; termination in an eligible case remains discretionary and requires the actual activity, relationship and lease predicates.

The threatening-criminal-activity branch concerns other tenants' health, safety or peaceful enjoyment. The separate drug-related branch covers activity on or off the premises without importing the first branch's threat requirement. That later statutory wording does not replace an older lease's on-or-near limitation. Under the applicable 1997 guest definition, the person must be in the leased unit with a household member's consent. Control does not require constant ability to prevent conduct; a stranger's remote association does not establish a covered relationship. Drug-related activity means illegal manufacture, sale, distribution, use, or possession with intent to manufacture, sell, distribute or use a controlled substance.

**Authority:** Part I of Thomas's opinion, joined by all eight participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** Congress specified the actors, conduct and consequence without adding a tenant mental state. MCI and Christensen require respect for the enacted boundary rather than administrative or judicial amendment. Household membership and guest access supply a tenancy connection without imputing a personal crime to the tenant. The general prohibitions on unreasonable terms and termination without serious or repeated violation or other good cause remain operative, but cannot erase the specific ground. The tenants' strongest reading combines those provisions, control, housing purposes and constitutional avoidance into a knowledge condition. That reading rewrites the particular clause; ignorance also does not establish every constitutional predicate. Conversely, a mandatory lease clause does not make every permitted termination compulsory. The allegations here must still satisfy the actual lease, including its geographical terms, and the applicable definitions. This independent construction resolves the statutory question in both dockets without treating agency discretion as authority beyond the statute.

##### II. Due process and the defined Bennis protection

**Controlling proposition:** Permission to terminate a conditioned public tenancy for covered third-party conduct is not facially invalid merely because the tenant lacks personal knowledge or criminal fault. Protected tenancy interests, required process and independently established substantive defenses remain enforceable; civil form, lease assent and optional leniency do not defeat them.

**Authority:** Part II, joined by all eight participating Justices.

**Controlling explanation:** A prospective household condition protecting other residents differs from personally convicting the tenant. Bennis nevertheless prevents statutory permission or agency grace from answering every property claim. It protects a separately owned interest in an ordinarily lawful automobile when the claimant proves actual ownership, nonparticipation, lack of knowledge and all reasonably expected prevention. Shared lawful use does not defeat that defense; omniscience, constant monitoring and prevention at any cost are not required. Culpable entrustment, known misuse and omitted reasonable precautions remain outside it. The claimant receives a meaningful opportunity to prove the ordinary burden, without a heightened quantum; established protection requires preservation or restoration of the interest. Otherwise valid forfeiture does not automatically require compensation. The injunction's ignorance condition does not establish those predicates, and Bennis did not adjudicate a continuing public tenancy. That difference defeats a universal innocence immunity here. But a landlord label cannot foreclose a supported analogy before the actual lease interest, prevention facts and deprivation are established. The individual analogical question remains open because this preliminary record does not supply those necessary facts, not because optional mitigation substitutes for constitutional protection. Required notice and a meaningful opportunity to contest the agency's case remain distinct from the existence of a substantive defense.

##### III. First Amendment association

**Controlling proposition:** Household or guest status and resulting economic pressure alone do not establish a First Amendment tenant-knowledge condition. An association claim requires protected expressive activity or relationship and a material governmental burden upon it; no particular expressive predicate is established by this categorical challenge.

**Authority:** Part III, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment rejecting this independent injunction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The clause addresses specified criminal conduct and occupancy relationships, not an identified viewpoint, compelled message or political membership. Hurley protects an actual expressive presentation against compelled alteration. Dale requires actual expressive impairment in its access-law setting and preserves direct speech, genuine message-bearing roles and intimate association; its organizational formulation is not an exclusive test for every tenant. Nor must expression always take the form of spoken advocacy. Lyng distinguishes an indirect economic consequence of a household benefit condition from a substantial constitutional burden while recognizing that subtle interference can be actionable. The tenants' strongest argument is practical pressure to expel associates, potentially concealing retaliation. That can matter in an actual expression-targeted application, but does not supply the missing expressive connection here. The Court neither finds that these tenants have no expression nor creates a benefits exception to the First Amendment. A properly supported expressive application remains subject to its own constitutional inquiry.

##### IV. Intimate association

**Controlling proposition:** Family and genuinely intimate relationships receive protection independently of any expressive message. The statutory omission of tenant knowledge does not itself establish direct and substantial interference with a protected living arrangement or trigger strict scrutiny; the specified relationships rationally connect this categorical housing condition to the legitimate protection of safe, drug-free housing and other tenants' enjoyment.

**Authority:** Part IV, joined by the same seven Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment rejecting this independent injunction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The statute authorizes termination for specified conduct; it does not command relatives to separate. Optional continued occupancy excluding a responsible member is not compulsory family dissolution. The strongest contrary analogy concerns an actual choice between shelter and severance of a protected relationship. The record does not establish each relationship's kind and strength, compulsory severance or a particular direct burden requiring further justification. Displacement and family pressure are not therefore deemed nonexistent. Troxel protects actual parental direction from replacement by a judge's preferred arrangement, but supplies no majority rule demanding universal strict scrutiny or a harm showing for every governmental family effect. Shahar protects enduring intimacy beyond civil marriage and requires the actual governmental interest rather than a label. Neither establishes unconditional entitlement to this dwelling despite covered misconduct. This ruling does not dilute the standard applicable when infringement of an established fundamental parental right is actually shown; Thomas's agreement rests on the missing infringement predicate, not rejection of his scrutiny position. A properly demonstrated direct and substantial burden must receive its own constitutional justification.

##### V. Excessive Fines

**Controlling proposition:** Statutory permission to end a conditional public tenancy does not itself establish a punitive extraction, and tenant ignorance or economic loss alone does not make every termination a fine. A supported application must identify the legal interest actually taken and an operation extracting it at least partly as punishment; if that threshold is established, excessiveness requires a separate gross-disproportionality inquiry.

**Authority:** Part V, joined by the same seven Associates. Stone-Zsela agrees only in the judgment rejecting this independent injunction ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Recovery of possession under a lawful occupancy condition and prospective cessation of assistance differ from Austin's drug-property forfeitures and Bajakajian's criminal currency exaction. The strongest tenant argument nevertheless identifies a valuable leasehold deliberately forfeited to punish another's conduct. Austin makes civil form, remedial purpose and noncash form insufficient answers. Government ownership of the building does not prove that a punitive taking of a tenant's interest is impossible. The present categorical authorization and supplied application findings establish no particular punitive extraction; that is not an adverse factual judgment about every tenant's interest. If one is established, lawful authority and an offense nexus do not settle excessiveness. Bajakajian requires identification of the actual offense, gravity and exaction under its gross-disproportionality rule. No adjudicated value or proportionality findings support deciding an actual forfeiture here. Ursery, Kurth Ranch and Hudson concern distinct Double Jeopardy classifications; their clearest-proof or accounting approaches do not become the fines threshold. The Court adopts no new ratio, exclusive instrumentality test, universal factor list, fixed leasehold value or remedial formula. State incorporation remains expressly undecided because this categorical claim fails without resolving it. No alternative holding approves an otherwise established excessive fine.

##### VI. Remedy, discretion and operative process

**Controlling proposition:** Reversal requires removal of the preliminary injunction insofar as it rests on the rejected categorical grounds and remand for actual lease application and properly presented individual claims under their own predicates. Any retained or renewed preliminary relief must satisfy the ordinary requirements on a supported independent ground; actual eviction proceeds only through an authorized forum with applicable proof and process.

**Authority:** Part VI, joined by all eight participating Justices. The following procedural and discretionary terms qualify the remand; none is a finding that an individual predicate has been satisfied.

**Operative qualifications:** The 1997 regulations preserve these distinct requirements:

- **Discretion:** Section 966.4(l)(5) permits consideration of all circumstances, including seriousness, family participation and effects on uninvolved family members. In appropriate cases remaining members may continue occupancy subject to exclusion of members who engaged in the activity. A member who used illegal drugs may be required to demonstrate successful treatment as a residence condition. These are options, not compulsory mitigation, mandatory severance or a universal prevention defense.
- **Ordinary grievance coverage:** Sections 966.51(a)(1) and 966.53(a) cover individual tenant disputes about agency action or inaction contrary to the lease or agency regulations that adversely affects rights, duties, welfare or status. Section 966.51(b) separately excludes tenant disputes not involving the agency and class grievances; the procedure is not a forum for initiating or negotiating group policy changes. That agency-procedure limit does not bar this federal suit or adjudicate an individual defense.
- **Conditional judicial exception:** Section 966.51(a)(2) permits an agency actually to elect exclusion of an individual termination or eviction grievance only where HUD has determined that the jurisdiction requires a pre-eviction court opportunity with the defined safeguards. The grievance must involve criminal activity threatening other residents' or agency employees' health, safety or peaceful enjoyment, or drug-related criminal activity on or near the premises. Employees are included in this exception's first category; the second retains the older geographical limit. The actual court procedure must be covered by HUD's determination. The exception removes the agency grievance hearing, not the qualifying court opportunity. HUD need not use notice-and-comment procedures for its determination but must publish the list of covered procedures and make its supporting legal analysis available for public inspection and copying.
- **Court safeguards:** Section 966.53(c) requires adequate notice of termination and eviction grounds, the right to representation by counsel, opportunity to refute agency evidence through confrontation and cross-examination and to present affirmative legal or equitable defenses, and a merits decision. The counsel right is not a guarantee of appointed counsel.
- **1997 notice requirements:** Section 966.4(l)(3) requires written notice of fourteen days for nonpayment, a reasonable period considering seriousness not exceeding thirty days for threats to other residents' or agency employees' health or safety, and thirty days otherwise. It must state specific grounds, reply rights, relevant-document access and any required grievance-request right. A legally required notice to vacate may be combined with or run concurrently with that notice. Where grievance opportunity is required, termination awaits expiration of the request period and, on timely request, completion of the process even if the local notice to vacate has expired. An elected lawful exclusion requires notice of the absence of a grievance entitlement, the chosen qualifying court procedure and HUD determination, and which precise criminal-activity category applies.
- **Separate statutory notice:** Section 1437d(l)(4) requires adequate written notice no shorter than the applicable period: a reasonable period not exceeding thirty days when other tenants, agency employees or persons in the immediate vicinity face a health or safety threat, or for drug-related or violent criminal activity or a felony conviction; fourteen days for nonpayment; and thirty days otherwise, except a shorter state or local period where that last category permits it. These statutory terms do not silently replace or merge with the 1997 regulatory requirements. Section 1437d(l)(7)'s notice of pre-hearing or pretrial access to directly related documents, records and regulations also remains operative notwithstanding state law.
- **Administrative route and documents:** Eviction without court action under section 966.4(l)(4) requires local authorization, a due-process administrative hearing and the opportunity for a pre-eviction agency grievance hearing. The judicial exception cannot erase those safeguards. Section 966.4(m) requires a reasonable opportunity, upon the tenant's request before the hearing or trial, to inspect directly relevant documents held by the agency and copy them at the tenant's expense. Failure to provide that required access bars proceeding with eviction.

**Controlling explanation:** Statutory cause, discretionary choice and lawful implementation do different work. The contrary automatic-eviction position would erase the actual lease, the enumerated procedural conditions and the individual defenses preserved by Parts II–V. Conversely, optional mitigation cannot become a required hearing in every case or defeat an independently established constitutional protection. The actual actor, activity, relationship, older lease geography and procedural route must be established. No HUD determination, agency election, notice compliance or grievance-request timing is inferred from the drug allegation alone. Ordinary adjudication may resolve remaining issues on the appropriate actual record without a compulsory new evidentiary hearing in every case. The tenants' ADA and state-law claims are outside this review. The individual constitutional reservations rest on missing application predicates, while application of the settled statutory and procedural rules is ordinary remand work.

#### Precedent Treatment

- MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. AT&T Co. is applied to preserve the enacted boundary of agency authority; it authorizes neither an added knowledge requirement nor authority beyond the actual statute.
- Christensen v. Harris County is applied to distinguish statutory or regulatory permission from an extra compulsory condition.
- Bennis v. Michigan remains binding in its defined innocent-interest category, including reasonable-prevention proof and required protection. A conditioned public tenancy is distinguished; a supported individual analogy remains open on the missing lease-interest and prevention facts.
- Hurley v. Irish-American Gay, Lesbian and Bisexual Group of Boston is applied to actual expressive presentation and compelled alteration, not to guest status alone.
- Boy Scouts of America v. Dale is applied for actual expressive impairment and its preserved direct-speech, genuine message-role and intimate-association protections. Its organizational formulation and noncontrolling stronger advocacy requirement do not become universal tenant tests.
- Lyng v. International Union, UAW is applied to distinguish indirect household economic consequences from a substantial protected burden while preserving its recognition of actionable subtle interference.
- Troxel v. Granville preserves actual parental choice; its fractured grounds do not establish universal strict scrutiny or a harm test for every family effect.
- Shahar v. Bowers is applied to actual enduring intimate relationships beyond civil marriage and the real governmental interest; it supplies no unconditional right to this tenancy.
- Austin v. United States preserves coverage of at least partly punitive property extraction despite civil form or remedial purpose. It does not make every loss of occupancy a fine.
- United States v. Bajakajian preserves the distinct punitive-extraction threshold and gross-disproportionality inquiry; no exclusive instrumentality rule or new valuation formula is adopted.
- United States v. Ursery, Department of Revenue of Montana v. Kurth Ranch and Hudson v. United States concern different Double Jeopardy classifications. Their tests do not displace Austin's separate fines inquiry.

#### Law After Decision

Public-housing leases must permit termination for the statute's defined third-party conduct without a universal tenant-knowledge condition. Actual enforcement remains discretionary within the governing statute, actual lease and operative process. Ignorance alone supplies none of the four categorical constitutional objections rejected here. Due process, expressive and intimate association, and fines protections remain enforceable when their distinct application predicates are established. Bennis's defined property protection and Austin/Bajakajian's separate coverage and proportionality rules retain their force. The remedial ruling removes the invalid categorical injunction grounds while preserving supported individual proceedings; it creates no general innocence immunity or blanket constitutional approval of every eviction.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

### S.S. ex rel. Jervis v. McMullen

Merits decision, March 26, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Submitted on the full merits record. On review of the Eighth Circuit's September 13, 2000 en banc judgment affirming dismissal of the personal substantive-due-process action. The questions concern the actual custodial placement of a child into an allegedly appreciated serious danger and the culpability required after an opportunity to deliberate. The child seeks reinstatement; McMullen, Jacoby and Barnett defend the dismissal and retain their individual defenses. The lower court relied both on the child's exposure before state intervention and on insufficient constitutional culpability. The earlier panel judgment was vacated. March 8, 1996 began full-time residence with the father on an extended visit while the Division retained legal custody; the August 22 judicial order subsequently returned legal custody. The alleged assaults occurred November 15, 1996 and January 15, 1997, after that order.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**Vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The action returns for adjudication under the bounded actual-custody and culpability rules. Each official's personal conduct, contemporaneous knowledge, authority, response, causation, supported defenses and attributable injury require separate treatment. The state court's custody order retains its legal effect and must be considered at its actual scope. The excluded claim against Montgomery is not reinstated.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined.

All nine join the complete bounded opinion. It does not decide an independent noncustodial claim based on the August request alone.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**Common authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine adopt the actual-custody rule, its culpability requirements, individualized application and preserved defenses below. No broader noncustodial danger doctrine or narrowest-grounds aggregation supplies the decision.

##### I. Actual custodial placement is an affirmative act subject to review

**Holding and operative rule:** Officials exercising actual custody over a child may not knowingly use their placement authority to expose the child to a specifically appreciated serious danger through deliberate and unreasonable disregard when they have authority and a real opportunity to respond. Each defendant's knowing participation, authority, unreasonable choice and resulting attributable injury must be established; a prior encounter with the same danger does not categorically erase the official's later affirmative custodial act.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the relevant starting point is the protection and control the State actually exercises when it chooses the placement. City of Florence v. Chipman applies the protection against knowingly using actual restraint to deliver a person into an appreciated serious danger. Youngberg supplies the security principle arising from actual custody. That bounded principle extends to a personally directed protective-custody placement; it is not a general guarantee of competent social services.

DeShaney preserves the rule that officials ordinarily have no constitutional duty to protect a person already in private custody from private violence. Its discussion of returning a child to an earlier danger cannot categorically dispose of a separately culpable exercise of actual custody and placement power. Otherwise an official's knowing choice would disappear from constitutional analysis merely because the child had previously faced danger in that home. The State's actual intervention does not make it a permanent guarantor, but neither can it be treated as never having exercised control.

The complaint alleges state selection and continuation of S.S.'s full-time residence with her father while the Division retained custody. These alleged affirmative acts differ from a request that the State supply protection generally. A biological relationship and a legitimate reunification objective do not by themselves establish that every placement choice was reasonable or legally compelled. Actual control and placement authority are essential; a legal-custody label, nominal supervision or a voluntary private decision alone is insufficient. The rule requires neither removal of every child from danger, indefinite state custody, investigation of every warning nor a guarantee that a reasonable reunification succeeds.

##### II. The pleaded deliberate choice can satisfy constitutional culpability

**Holding and operative rule:** A considered custodial placement made with actual appreciation of a specific grave danger and deliberate, unreasonable disregard of a meaningful opportunity to avoid it can constitute the conscience-shocking abuse of custody that substantive due process forbids. On the detailed allegations here, intent to cause the eventual abuse is not indispensable, and the action cannot be dismissed by categorically treating the alleged official choice as negligence.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the demanding actual-custody rule rather than an unrestricted inquiry into whether an outcome is shocking. Daniels excludes negligence. Chipman requires personal awareness, actual authority, opportunity and unreasonable disregard in an affirmative custodial transfer. Lewis's purpose-to-harm rule concerns urgent pursuit decisions made amid instant competing obligations; it does not impose that same requirement on every considered custody decision. Farmer informs actual awareness, permissible inference and reasonable response without transferring prison law wholesale to child protection.

The allegations describe specific warnings of sexual-abuse danger and considered placement decisions, not only a poor prediction followed by harm. If proved, actual appreciation and deliberate unreasonable exposure within available authority can satisfy the required culpability. The assailant's conduct does not establish that the officials themselves acted unconstitutionally, and time to deliberate alone proves neither actual knowledge nor unreasonable choice.

Lawful family-restoration duties and the official's actual alternatives bear on reasonableness. A reasonable professional choice among lawful alternatives remains protected by this substantive rule even if it fails. But a general reunification label cannot settle the contested reasonableness of a particular allegedly knowing exposure at the pleading stage. McMullen's July investigation must be considered as part of her response; its occurrence proves neither that she acted reasonably nor that she appreciated and disregarded the danger. The complaint permits ordinary adjudication under these limits. The rule creates no fixed investigation protocol or constitutional right to the safest or preferred home.

##### III. Each placement act, information date and causal contribution must remain distinct

**Holding and operative rule:** Liability requires proof of each official's own knowing use of actual custodial placement authority and injury attributable to that act, assessed with the information and lawful options then available. Earlier physical placement, continued decisions during retained custody, and a later judicial transfer of legal custody cannot be treated as one undifferentiated act; an independent, untainted judicial decision may defeat attribution, while its existence does not establish that every earlier official choice was compelled.

**Controlling explanation:** March 8, 1996 began full-time residence on an extended visit while the Division retained legal custody. The August 22 court order later returned legal custody after McMullen's request. The alleged assaults occurred on November 15, 1996 and January 15, 1997. Because the injuries followed the judicial return, the claimant must establish the causal connection from an actionable earlier official act; the Court does not assume abuse during foster custody or disregard the intervening judicial decision.

The complaint attributes different information and acts to different defendants. McMullen allegedly received the September 1995 warning, continuing-contact reports, the May evaluation, July report and August 13 call. Jacoby allegedly documented warnings, symptoms and reported contact. Barnett allegedly received and approved reports and participated in placement decisions, including the August responsibility decision. These allegations permit individual inquiry, not collective knowledge or liability by supervisory title. Later information cannot establish what an official knew in March, although it may inform a later decision within retained authority.

The lower court must determine the actual order's constraints, each official's personal role and the causal significance of recommendations and implementation. A truthful recommendation, constrained execution, discretionary placement and deliberate deception are distinct functions; deception is not presumed here. Zahrey preserves personal causation and the possible significance of genuinely independent process, not automatic responsibility for every foreseeable consequence. Whether an August request detached from actual custody independently supports a noncustodial danger claim is left open because the order's full constraints and the request's actual relation to custodial implementation are not established. That reservation does not diminish the actual-custody rule governing the pleaded placement and its continuation.

##### IV. Ordinary proceedings and conduct-date personal defenses remain

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical merits dismissal is vacated so that the reviewed personal claim may proceed under the stated custody and culpability rules, with each official's function, knowledge, authority, causal responsibility, supported defenses and compensable injury determined through ordinary civil procedure. Qualified immunity remains a distinct, individualized inquiry into the law and information at the time of each challenged act; this decision makes no final immunity determination.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow and Anderson preserve objective, particularized notice, and Elder requires consideration of all relevant contemporaneous law without demanding a factual twin. Guest, Delaney and Zahrey retain that method. Their treatment of other injuries does not establish notice for this custody claim, and current recognition of the applicable constitutional rule cannot create earlier clarity.

Baby Richard was decided June 21, 1996, after the March placement. Its limited transition-interest holding may be relevant only to later acts within its actual scope; it neither supplies March notice nor automatically establishes this damages claim. Lewis and Chipman were decided in 1998 and likewise supply no retrospective notice. Pinder and Uhlrig retain their own conduct-date immunity judgments without resolving every later affirmative-custody claim.

Milstein requires examination of actual function; asking a court to change custody does not automatically make a social worker a prosecutor or eliminate a properly supported function-specific defense. The record therefore requires attention to each act and its legal setting rather than one immunity label for the whole relationship. Crawford-El preserves ordinary proof and lawful case management, and Swierkiewicz supplies no newly invented proof prerequisite for commencing litigation. Neither decision abolishes independent substantive elements or every individual-officer pleading requirement.

The reinstated action remains subject to properly supported dispositive defenses, including an immunity determination before further factual litigation when its lawful grounds are established. Recoverable relief depends on proof of attributable injury and the applicable remedial requirements. The excluded claim against Montgomery is not reinstated by this disposition.

#### Precedent Treatment

- City of Florence v. Chipman / Stemler v. City of Florence: its unanimous direct-restraint rule is extended to the alleged use of actual protective-custody placement authority, preserving personal awareness, authority, opportunity, unreasonable disregard and attributable injury. Its separate 1994 notice determination does not decide these defendants' immunity.
- DeShaney v. Winnebago County: retained for failures to protect people in private custody. Its return-to-prior-danger discussion does not categorically bar scrutiny of a separately culpable affirmative exercise of actual custody; a hypothetical absence of all state intervention does not erase that act.
- Youngberg v. Romeo: applied for security arising from actual custody, without mechanically adopting every institutional professional-judgment rule.

- Baby Richard v. Kirchner: its independent transition interest and fitted prospective procedure retain their bounded force. They do not establish this damages cause or supply notice before June 21, 1996.

#### Law After Decision

Officials who actually control a child's custodial placement are subject to the stated substantive-due-process rule when they knowingly and unreasonably use that control to expose the child to a specifically appreciated serious danger despite a real opportunity to respond. Earlier exposure to the same danger and a general reunification objective supply no categorical defense to that affirmative act. The rule requires actual personal knowledge, authority, unreasonable disregard and attributable injury, preserves reasonable professional choices and ordinary nonprotection law, and leaves conduct-date immunity distinct. The independent August-request theory detached from actual custody remains open on the stated record ground; application of the settled custody rule to each proved act is ordinary adjudication.

### Hoffman Plastic Compounds, Inc. v. NLRB, No. 00-1595

Merits decision, March 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 15, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the en banc United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit, which enforced the Board's limited backpay order.

Hoffman hired Jose Castro in 1988 after he presented another person's birth certificate and discharged him for union activity in January 1989. He lacked work authorization throughout the relevant period. The established labor violation is outside this review; the question is whether immigration law bars the limited award for unperformed work, as distinct from wages already earned. At the June 14, 1993 compliance hearing, Hoffman learned the disqualification. The Board denied reinstatement and ended the backpay period that day.

The Board and administrative law judge recount present-tense testimony supporting an inference that Castro continued using the borrowed certificate and false identification for work through the hearing. Their recitals identify no named interim employer or dated later tender. The judge's account that the driver's license and Social Security card apparently derived from the certificate is an inference, not a separately reproduced admission of that acquisition sequence. The Board found reasonable interim-work efforts; that finding does not establish how each job was procured. Counsel's proposed lawful-casual-work explanations and alleged Board inducement of fraud are not findings.

All nine Justices participated.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

Enforcement of the actual Board order remains in place. Its stated $66,951, plus the specified interest and less required federal and state withholding, covers backpay through the last quarter of 1992. January 1 through June 14, 1993 is the separately remaining period under that order; the stated sum is not a final total through discovery. The remaining amount is to be calculated within the Board's existing remedial framework. Reinstatement is denied and no backpay accrues after June 14, 1993 on the premise of continued unauthorized employment. The underlying unfair-labor-practice finding and other lawful relief remain undisturbed. Independent immigration enforcement and sanctions remain available.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and SOUTER, GINSBURG and BREYER, JJ., joined. O'CONNOR, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, KENNEDY and THOMAS, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### Limited monetary relief under the two statutes

**Controlling proposition:** IRCA does not categorically withdraw the Board's NLRA §10(c) authority to award limited pre-discovery backpay for an established antiunion discharge, even where the worker lacked employment authorization and obtained the job through false verification documents. The Board may remedy that independent labor violation through a retrospective monetary award that orders no unlawful employment, denies reinstatement and ends the ordinary lost-work remedy at actual discovery of the employment bar; the award supplies neither work authorization nor immunity from separate sanctions.

**Authority:** Stevens's opinion, joined in full by Stone-Zsela, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Their five votes establish this rule and enforcement of the actual limited order.

**Operative statutory limits:** Section 10(c)'s affirmative remedial authority remains bounded by the NLRA and other applicable enactments. Its separate bar for an actual discharge for cause is not established by a reason discovered only after an antiunion discharge. Section 1324a separately prohibits covered knowing hiring or fee-based recruitment/referral, requires employment verification, and prohibits knowing continued employment of an unauthorized worker. Authorization is employment- and time-specific, turning on permanent-resident status or statutory or Attorney General authorization. Good-faith verification can defend the knowing-hire charge; it does not permit knowing continuation after discovery. No earlier immigration-law violation by Hoffman is adjudicated here.

Section 1546(b) prohibits use, for employment verification, of identification known or reasonably understood not to have been lawfully issued for its possessor, false identification with that knowledge standard, or a false attestation. Its operative prohibition predates the 1988 hiring; the later five-year penalty does not govern the original act. Section 1324c applies only to violations on or after November 29, 1990, not the original tender; a later violation requires its own applicable act, knowledge, purpose and time predicates. Later benefit/application amendments and the 1996 deletion of the birth-certificate document category do not apply retroactively.

Both document statutes retain exceptions for lawfully authorized investigative, protective or intelligence activity of federal, state or state-subdivision law-enforcement agencies or federal intelligence agencies. Section 1324c(b) separately preserves activity authorized by 18 U.S.C. chapter 224; section 1546(c) separately preserves activity authorized by title V of the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970. The latter provision's State definition includes the District of Columbia, commonwealths, territories and possessions. No such activity is implicated here. Neither statute generally criminalizes the worker's mere performance of work.

**Controlling explanation:** Section 10(c) supplies the monetary authority, while IRCA supplies distinct employment, verification and fraud restrictions. Compensation for completed unlawful retaliation does not direct the forbidden future employment. Southern Steamship requires accommodation of another federal prohibition, not abandonment of every monetary labor remedy; this order commands no prohibited hiring or work. Sure-Tan's departed workers and unlawful-reentry problem do not establish a universal bar for the worker who remained here. ABF rejects automatic remedial forfeiture from serious misconduct while preserving actual statutory and remedial limits. McKennon likewise separates the unlawful decision from later-discovered disqualification. Its actual-would-discharge predicate and ordinary pre-discovery measure, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances, belong to its after-acquired-evidence setting; the analogy does not itself construe IRCA.

The dissent's strongest objection is that replacement wages value an employment opportunity federal law made unavailable and may encourage further fraud. The Court accepts the original fraud and the real continuing-use evidence. Neither mitigation duties nor an award authorize false documentation. But the identified prohibitions do not withdraw this bounded retrospective authority. Denying reinstatement and ending accrual at actual discovery give the employment restriction practical effect; independent sanctions retain their full force. Removing every backpay consequence would also reduce the cost of targeting this workforce for antiunion discharge. That consequence supports the statutory accommodation; deterrence alone supplies no remedial power.

The Board confronted the known disqualification and fraud, found reasonable interim efforts and adopted this limited accommodation. A particular preserved calculation, mitigation or explanation defect remains reviewable on its actual basis. None is demonstrated here merely by disputing the statutory choice or the reach of the continued-use inference. No additional formula, evidentiary hearing or explanation remand is required. The award remains restorative within §10(c), not a general punitive authority or automatic entitlement for every unauthorized worker.

**Application and remaining calculation:** Enforcement covers the order described in Judgment, including the remaining January 1–June 14, 1993 period; this decision supplies no new total. Ordinary calculation and mitigation requirements remain operative. The employer's specified initial answers did not challenge the method, but it contested calculations and periods at the March 4, 1993 hearing; the Board found its method valid and reasonable. Its no-exception observation concerned receipt of evidence and original fraudulent acquisition of the Hoffman job, not a newly established tender at a named interim employer. The continuing statutory objection, the administrative contest and the Board's effort finding remain distinct. The Court relies on no blanket waiver and finds neither every interim job lawful nor every job fraudulently obtained.

#### Precedent Treatment

- ABF Freight System, Inc. v. NLRB is applied to preserve reviewable remedial discretion and reject automatic forfeiture for misconduct. It does not protect relief contrary to another statute, misconduct defeating a remedial predicate, or arbitrary or inadequately explained relief.
- McKennon v. Nashville Banner Publishing Co. supplies an analogy separating an unlawful decision from later-known disqualification. Its actual-would-discharge requirement and ordinary pre-discovery backpay measure, subject to extraordinary equitable circumstances, remain intact; its ADEA rule does not decide the NLRA/IRCA interaction.
- Sure-Tan, Inc. v. NLRB retains labor coverage and the duty to accommodate immigration law. Its departure, reentry and legal-availability setting is distinguished from this limited monetary order for a worker who remained here.
- Southern Steamship Co. v. NLRB remains a limit on remedies conflicting with independent federal prohibitions. It does not bar this award, which directs no prohibited employment.
- New York v. FERC / Enron Power Marketing, Inc. v. FERC preserves review of an actual agency-authority or explanation defect. The Board's identified accommodation is the legal choice presented, not an unexplained alternative requiring an additional remand.

#### Law After Decision

An established antiunion discharge may carry the limited pre-discovery backpay consequence upheld here despite the employee's lack of authorization and original verification fraud. Labor coverage, the actual employer violation and the legality of a particular remedy remain separate questions. The award must stay within the governing monetary authority and ordinary remedial predicates; it neither guarantees all unauthorized workers backpay nor orders future employment. Immigration verification duties, knowing-employment prohibitions and independently applicable document sanctions remain enforceable. Actual calculation and mitigation objections require their own preserved factual and legal basis; neither blanket forfeiture nor an unsupported new administrative inquiry follows from the statutory dispute.

#### Separate Positions Relevant to the Decision

**O'Connor, dissenting, joined by Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.** The dissent would reverse enforcement of this backpay category because it replaces earnings from an employment opportunity federal law made unavailable and Castro obtained through fraudulent verification. Section 10(c)'s public remedial purpose does not permit the Board to restore that lost-work premise in conflict with the independent federal employment scheme. The payment need not itself constitute hiring, and IRCA need not expressly repeal backpay, for that remedial conflict to matter.

Southern Steamship requires the Board to respect the independent prohibition. Sure-Tan's legal-availability limit remains significant despite its different departure facts; the dissent's sufficient ground is the operative immigration scheme and the established original fraud and lack of authorization, not that precedent's sentence alone. ABF addressed misconduct that did not render the underlying employment federally unavailable and preserves only lawful remedial discretion. McKennon's later-discovered employer ground does not authorize compensation premised on employment forbidden by another enactment. Its actual-would-discharge requirement therefore does not resolve this different statutory bar.

The discovery cutoff and denial of reinstatement prevent future employment, but do not remove the defect in replacement wages for the earlier unauthorized position. The majority's concern that employers may exploit this workforce is substantial. The established labor violation, cease-and-desist and notice obligations, and other lawful remedies nevertheless cannot supply authority for this conflicting wage-replacement category. Nor can a public deterrence objective alone overcome the remedial limit.

The present-tense testimony supports continued-use concerns, but the dissent needs no finding that every interim job required new false documents, that the Board induced fraud, or that a particular post-1990 document violation occurred. It does not apply section 1324c to the original hiring. Its proposed reversal preserves NLRA coverage, the employer's violation, other lawful relief and independent immigration enforcement. It decides neither earned wages nor every remedy involving an unauthorized worker. It would return only for implementation of the backpay bar, not a new mitigation hearing or an additional punitive-remedy holding.
