### United States v. Angle, on review of Fourth Circuit Nos. 96-4662, 96-4672 and 99-4187

Merits decision, June 20, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

The consolidated appeals concern Corey Angle and James Edward Phifer. On review of the Fourth Circuit's en banc judgment, 254 F.3d 514, in Nos. 96-4662, 96-4672 and 99-4187. The questions concern judge-found present-offense facts raising binding Guidelines ranges within an outer statutory maximum and hypothetical consecutive terms as an answer to an excessive count sentence. The matter is decided on the merits submission.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Holdings

##### 1. Present-offense facts changing a binding punishment range receive element protections

**Holding and operative rule:** A fact concerning the commission or consequences of the present offense that is legally necessary to raise either the binding ceiling or the binding floor of punishment must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to a jury, unless a lawful jury waiver permits the judge to determine the element under that same proof standard. A larger outer statutory maximum does not make an enforceable inner Guidelines range discretionary; ordinary judicial assessment remains permissible within punishment independently authorized by the verdict or valid admissions.

**Authority:** Part I-A of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The law's actual constraint determines whether the additional fact changes punishment authority. Apprendi supplies the principle that a present-offense fact authorizing greater punishment cannot escape element protections through a sentencing label. Its express reservation of general Guidelines classifications and unchanged-maximum minima left this application for decision. The mandatory Guidelines withhold sentencing choices unless an additional quantity fact is established. A still higher outer ceiling does not erase that legal consequence.

The traditional judicial role continues inside the range already authorized. Witte and Watts preserve that within-authority role; they do not hold every binding constraint constitutionally immaterial. Mistretta establishes the Commission's institutional validity, a different question. Limited departures likewise do not make the ordinary mandatory range advisory.

A mandatory floor changes the legal obligation as a ceiling changes permission: a formerly permissible lesser sentence becomes unavailable. McMillan's contrary maximum/minimum distinction cannot sustain that allocation and is overruled to this extent. The floor matters here because quantity compelled the bottom of Angle's 210-to-262-month range even though his 210-month term remained below the 240-month statutory ceiling.

The Guidelines remain binding subject to these constitutional conditions. Prior-conviction questions, a general advisory-system remedy and consecutive sentencing independent of the disputed range premise lie outside the questions resolved. The existing resentencing must identify the lawful range; no unsupported numerical replacement is supplied.

##### 2. Federal charging protection accompanies the covered aggravated fact

**Holding and operative rule:** In a federal prosecution, a present-offense fact performing the aggravating legal work identified in Part I-A must receive the applicable grand-jury charging protection as an element. A quantity-free charge and verdict, ordinary sentencing findings, or notice of possible exposure do not themselves establish that element; a valid element admission or lawful waiver must satisfy its own governing requirements.

**Authority:** Part I-B of Souter's opinion, joined by Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct rationale votes. Stone-Zsela joins the disposition on this component without adopting the separate charging proposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Jones and Castillo supply the charging consequences of federal aggravated elements. Cotton confirms that omission of aggravating quantity is a genuine constitutional charging defect without withdrawing subject-matter jurisdiction. Part I-A identifies the additional fact by its binding legal consequence. Its placement in a Guideline does not eliminate the protection attached to that function. Here the indictment and verdict specified no quantity, and ordinary judicial calculations are neither grand-jury allegations nor valid admissions of an aggravated element. Proceedings on remand remain governed by existing charging, admission, waiver and sentencing law; this remand itself authorizes no new charging or jury procedure.

##### 3. Phifer's actual drug-count term is erroneous and plain

**Holding and operative rule:** Phifer's 292-month drug-count sentence exceeds the 240 months authorized by his quantity-free verdict and is constitutional error. Applying the established review-time rule in the settled-law-then-changed setting, the count excess is plain; substantial-rights effect and discretionary correction remain separate requirements.

**Authority:** Part II of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Thomas and Ginsburg; five direct rationale votes. Scalia agrees that the count is erroneous and assumes plainness without joining the affirmative timing application.

**Controlling explanation:** Apprendi supplies the verdict-authorized count limit. Available maxima on other counts do not enlarge it. Johnson supplies review-time plainness where settled trial law changes during direct review, and Cotton applies that framework to an aggravating-quantity omission. This decision applies those authorities to the actual count excess. It does not declare the newly resolved Guidelines question settled at original sentencing or announce a rule for every initially unsettled trial-law question. Olano continues to require substantial-rights effect and the distinct discretionary condition before correction follows.

##### 4. An unsupported mandatory aggregate cannot dispose of the remaining plain-error inquiry

**Holding and operative rule:** A demonstrated lawful and inevitable identical aggregate may bear on substantial-rights effect under Rule 52(b), but unused cumulative statutory exposure or a supposed mandatory total derived from the constitutionally disputed quantity findings does not itself establish that result. Because the categorical no-prejudice ruling depends on that defective range premise, the appellate affirmance of Phifer's sentence must be vacated for the remaining substantial-rights and discretionary-correction inquiry on the actual record.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Thomas and Ginsburg; six direct rationale votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The entered judgment imposes a 292-month drug term and concurrent 240-month money-laundering terms. A hypothetical judgment with different terms and consecutive service is not that judgment. The dissent's strongest point concerns mandatory inevitability: if a lawful total compelled identical imprisonment, correcting the count arrangement might cause no prejudice. But the total must first be valid under Part I. Section 5G1.2(d) does not independently establish the total whose legality is contested.

Olano keeps error, plainness, substantial rights and discretionary correction distinct. The defendant ordinarily bears the burden of showing that the error affected the outcome. Even when that showing is made, correction remains discretionary: a reviewing court may correct only an error that seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings. Vonn permits the relevant existing record to inform that inquiry. Cotton permits refusal of correction under the independent fourth condition on a properly supported record even while substantial-rights effect is assumed. Its particular overwhelming and essentially uncontested threshold evidence is not a finding about Phifer. A general assessment of sufficiency cannot replace that inquiry. Neder concerns its qualified preserved-error setting and does not transfer the burden to the Government here.

The court of appeals must consider the lawful range, actual evidence and any properly supported alternative. Vacating its insufficient rationale neither guarantees correction nor establishes harmlessness. Range calculation and the two remaining conditions are remand work under governing rules, not questions without governing law.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Apprendi v. New Jersey is extended to present-offense facts raising binding Guidelines ceilings or floors. Its earlier reservations are resolved to that extent; its within-authorized-range boundary remains.
- McMillan v. Pennsylvania is overruled insofar as ordinary judicial sentencing findings of present-offense facts may raise a mandatory floor merely because the maximum is unchanged.
- Jones v. United States supplies the federal aggravated-element charging consequences applied in I-B; its statutory holding retains its distinct scope.
- Castillo v. United States likewise supplies charging consequences for federal aggravated elements in I-B; its distinct statutory holding remains intact.
- Mistretta v. United States retains its institutional approval of the Commission; it does not settle allocation of aggravating factfinding.
- Witte v. United States retains its within-authority sentencing and later-prosecution holdings, without an exemption for facts first changing a binding legal limit.
- United States v. Watts / United States v. Putra retains ordinary consideration within constitutionally authorized punishment, subject to the new binding-range rule.
- United States v. Olano supplies the four distinct forfeiture conditions, ordinary defendant burden and discretionary correction. No condition is eliminated.
- Johnson v. United States supplies its settled-trial-law/intervening-law timing rule, applied to the count excess without a new general timing holding.
- United States v. Cotton preserves nonjurisdictional treatment of the charging omission and record-specific fourth-condition review. Its evidentiary result is not transposed to Phifer.
- United States v. Vonn permits relevant-existing-record review under Rule 52(b); its separate Rule 11 setting creates no burden shift here.
- Neder v. United States remains a qualified preserved materiality-error decision. It supplies neither a hypothetical quantity verdict nor the burden on this forfeited claim.

#### Law After Decision

Present-offense facts legally raising either end of a mandatory punishment range receive element protections, including binding Guidelines constraints below an outer statutory maximum. McMillan's contrary minimum rule is displaced. Applicable federal charging requirements attach under I-B's five-Justice holding. Judicial choice remains within the independently established range; the Guidelines are not made advisory.

Count error and appellate correction remain distinct. Phifer's count error and plainness are established while ordinary prejudice and independent discretionary correction remain. A lawful inevitable aggregate may matter to effect; theoretical exposure or the invalid range premise is insufficient. The mandate requires actual application and preserves the convictions.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Scalia joins the binding-range and federal charging holdings and the remaining Rule 52(b) remand. His published Jones range principle supports protection of both floor and ceiling. He agrees the count exceeds its verdict-authorized maximum but assumes plainness instead of joining the affirmative timing application, preserving his Johnson and Cotton limitation. That difference supplies no objection to the range rule or reconsideration of the defective prejudice rationale. He treats neither evidence as a verdict nor sentencing error as automatically correctable.

**O'Connor, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Kennedy and Breyer.** They preserve Angle's ordinary findings-based resentencing and would affirm Phifer. McMillan and their Apprendi position distinguish facts defining the statutory offense from rules regulating judicial choice within its authorized range. Making such rules binding does not, in their view, transform each aggravating direction into an element. Witte and Watts support the continuing judicial role. They acknowledge the Guidelines' legal force but reject the majority's classification and overruling of McMillan.

They accept that Phifer's actual drug-count term exceeds its limit but would deny relief at the substantial-rights condition. Under their constitutional premise, the judicially calculated total is lawful and section 5G1.2(d) requires enough consecutive service to reach it. That conclusion rests on an obligatory lawful total, not merely unused cumulative maxima. They reject automatic correction while retaining Olano's separate conditions.

United States v. Angle, 254 F.3d 514 (2001), Nos. 96-4662, 96-4672 and 99-4187. Governing provisions include the Fifth and Sixth Amendments, 21 U.S.C. §§841 and 846, then-mandatory 18 U.S.C. §3553, Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 52(b), and the applicable 1995 Guidelines, including §§2D1.1 and 5G1.2. The range quantities were judicial findings; the indictment and verdict specified no quantity. A lawful element admission or waiver must meet its own requirements.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Angle returns for the already required resentencing under a constitutionally established binding range and supported ordinary findings within it. The court of appeals reexamines Phifer's sentence under the remaining Rule 52(b) conditions, using the actual record and lawful range. Any resulting restructuring or further factfinding requires an independent basis in governing procedural and sentencing law. The convictions stand.

### BE&K Construction Co. v. NLRB, No. 01-518

Merits decision, June 24, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 16, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, 246 F.3d 619, which enforced an unfair-labor-practice order based on BE&K's unsuccessful litigation against unions. The question is whether the Board may impose liability for a losing retaliatory lawsuit even when it is reasonably based. BE&K seeks removal of that liability and its dependent remedies; the Board and unions seek enforcement. The underlying claims were lost or withdrawn. Their allegations of union misconduct are not findings.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**The Sixth Circuit's enforcement judgment is reversed and remanded; dependent litigation-based obligations are set aside to the extent based on the unsuccessful-reasonable-suit and broad retaliation standard, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

Further proceedings may address only a properly preserved, legally sufficient and supported alternative. The underlying lawsuit's adverse outcomes remain undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and SCALIA, KENNEDY, and THOMAS, JJ., joined, and in which STEVENS, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined as to Parts I and III. BREYER, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, in which STEVENS, SOUTER, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. SCALIA, J., filed a concurring opinion, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | O'Connor | Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas throughout; Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer in Parts I and III only | Supports reversal and remand | Parts I and III: narrow operative rule and remedy; Part II: five-Justice statutory-avoidance explanation |
| Concurrence in part and concurrence in the judgment | Breyer | Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg | Joins reversal and remand and Parts I and III | Preserves completed-litigation authority and the labor-law distinctions; declines Part II |
| Concurrence | Scalia | Thomas | Joins opinion and judgment throughout | Proposed objective-baselessness gate for an appropriate case; not an additional Court holding |

#### Holdings

##### I. The Board's unsuccessful-suit and broad retaliation standard is invalid

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 8(a)(1) of the National Labor Relations Act does not authorize liability for a reasonably based but unsuccessful employer lawsuit merely because it sought to stop protected union activity or was accompanied by hostility toward the union. An unsuccessful result and that broad conception of retaliatory motive do not distinguish genuine pursuit of judicial relief from abuse, so an order resting on that standard cannot stand.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this confined proposition. The four limited joiners do not join the broader explanation in Part II.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that losing a lawsuit is different from lacking a reasonable basis to bring it. An employer may genuinely seek a ruling that union conduct is unlawful even though the conduct ultimately proves protected. A desire to stop that conduct, or hostility toward the opponent, does not by itself show that the employer used litigation simply as an instrument of abuse. Bill Johnson's Restaurants protects reasonably based pending suits and permits consideration of a completed suit's result; it does not make defeat virtually sufficient proof of retaliation. Professional Real Estate Investors likewise distinguishes the objective basis for judicial resort from an unfavorable outcome, within its single-suit antitrust setting.

The Board's concern that litigation costs can suppress organizing is substantial. It does not answer the defect in a standard encompassing reasonable attempts to obtain judicial relief. The present standard and dependent enforcement therefore fail. This conclusion does not establish the reasonableness of every underlying claim or find a different abuse theory proved.

##### II. The statute receives a limiting construction to avoid serious petitioning concerns

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 8(a)(1)'s general prohibition on interference, restraint and coercion must be construed not to reach the entire class of reasonably based unsuccessful lawsuits brought with the broad retaliatory purpose used here, because that construction would burden substantial genuine petitioning and create serious First Amendment doubt. This statutory holding does not adopt the entire antitrust sham-litigation test as the NLRA's rule or invalidate section 8(a)(1).

**Authority:** Part II of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct votes for this explanation and construction. Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer withhold assent to this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that reasonable judicial resort retains petitioning value after a loss. It can air disputed facts, develop legal arguments and provide a peaceful means of resolving a genuine grievance. A declaration that the petition itself was unlawful burdens that activity through its legal and reputational consequences. The Board's screening of charges and remedial powers may make its burdens different from private antitrust litigation and treble damages; they do not eliminate the constitutional concern.

DeBartolo supplies the method: where the labor statute's general language admits a construction avoiding serious First Amendment difficulty, the Court adopts that construction. Nothing in section 8(a)(1)'s text requires the Board's whole class of liability. Bill Johnson's discussion of completed suits did not decide the precise reasonable-suit and retaliation question now presented; its protection of pending reasonable suits remains. Professional Real Estate Investors illustrates why objective and subjective genuineness matter, but its antitrust rule does not automatically govern every labor-law category.

The Board's actual standard is broader than a rule confined to an unsuccessful, reasonably based suit that would not have been brought but for a purpose to impose litigation costs regardless of outcome in retaliation for protected activity. Whether a properly confined rule may reach that different category remains open because this order was not based on it. Serial abuse, fraud and independently unlawful objectives likewise require their own rules and supported predicates; their precise standards are outside this decision. Court-imposed litigation sanctions and statutes merely authorizing fees against a losing litigant retain their force.

##### III. The enforcement judgment and dependent obligations fall on the rejected basis

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's enforcement judgment is reversed, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with Parts I and II; the litigation-based cease-and-desist, notice and reimbursement obligations must be set aside to the extent they depend on the invalid standard. Reconsideration may proceed only on a properly preserved, legally sufficient ground supported by the record, rather than merely relabeling defeat and union hostility as abuse.

**Authority:** Part III of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join this disposition and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that remedies dependent on an invalid liability rule cannot remain in force on that rule. The remand permits the proper court and Board to address a genuinely available alternative under its own legal and factual requirements. It supplies no finding that another violation occurred. The separate general question whether the Board possesses authority to award attorney's fees upon a valid unfair-labor-practice finding lies outside the question granted. The earlier lawsuit's adverse results remain undisturbed, and the decision does not displace independent valid sanctions imposed by courts.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Bill Johnson's Restaurants, Inc. v. NLRB, 461 U.S. 731: its protection of reasonably based pending litigation remains; its completed-suit discussion cannot sustain the Board's present standard. The Court's five-Justice explanation treats that discussion as insufficient to resolve the precise reasonable-suit question, while the four limited concurring Justices preserve its broader completed-litigation force.
- Professional Real Estate Investors, Inc. v. Columbia Pictures Industries, Inc., 508 U.S. 49: its distinction between objective baselessness and unsuccessful outcome informs this decision; its single-suit antitrust test is not automatically extended to the NLRA, fraud, serial proceedings or independently unlawful conduct.
- Edward J. DeBartolo Corp. v. Florida Gulf Coast Building & Construction Trades Council, 485 U.S. 568: the five-Justice statutory explanation applies its limiting-construction method to avoid serious First Amendment doubt.

#### Law After Decision

An unsuccessful reasonably based employer suit cannot be condemned under section 8(a)(1) merely because it seeks to stop protected union conduct or reflects hostility to a union. The five-Justice statutory construction prevents that whole class of liability without constitutional invalidation or wholesale adoption of the antitrust sham test. Genuinely different abuse theories require their own lawful rule and supported record. Existing court sanctions and fee-shifting statutes remain operative, and the Board's general fee-award authority is not resolved.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Breyer, concurring in part and concurring in the judgment, joined by Stevens, Souter and Ginsburg.** They join Parts I and III: the actual finding rests too heavily on the unsuccessful suit and antiunion hostility, which do not adequately establish abuse. They retain Bill Johnson's as meaningful authority permitting a properly supported inquiry into completed retaliatory litigation and reject the broader implications of Part II. A reasonable suit brought only to impose process costs regardless of outcome, or as part of a broader course of antiunion conduct, presents a different question; no such violation is found here. Labor law's central concern with employers' use of litigation against organizing, the Board's screening function and its remedial setting distinguish private antitrust actions and treble damages. Those differences preserve room for lawful Board action and prevent wholesale transfer of antitrust immunity. Their agreement does not depend on treating Bill Johnson's completed-suit discussion as mere dicta.

**Justice Scalia, concurring, joined by Thomas.** They join the entire Court opinion and favor applying the objective-baselessness-before-subjective-abuse requirement to an appropriate ordinary single-suit NLRA case, while preserving the existing category limits. Professional Real Estate Investors supplies the proposed gate: a suit must lack an objectively reasonable prospect of success before its subjective use of process as a weapon is examined. An executive agency's motive findings, reviewed for substantial evidence, can threaten access to an Article III court at least as seriously as a later antitrust suit adjudicated by a court. This institutional distinction answers the limited concurrence's reliance on differences between labor and antitrust enforcement. Their proposed extension is not the replacement rule adopted today. Sanctions imposed by courts under their own procedural rules remain distinct.

#### Source Notes

29 U.S.C. §§157, 158(a)(1), 160(c); BE&K Construction Co. v. NLRB, 246 F.3d 619; Bill Johnson's Restaurants, 461 U.S. 731; Professional Real Estate Investors, 508 U.S. 49; DeBartolo, 485 U.S. 568. The underlying litigation's defeat does not establish that every claim lacked an objective basis. The separate question of general Board authority to award attorney's fees is outside the granted issue.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Sixth Circuit for proceedings consistent with the decision. The litigation-based cease-and-desist, notice and reimbursement obligations must fall to the extent based on the rejected standard. Any reconsideration must rest on an available, preserved and legally sufficient alternative supported by the record.

**End of entry: BE&K Construction Co. v. NLRB, Merits decision, June 24, 2002.**

### Harris v. United States, No. 00-10666

Merits decision, June 24, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 25, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 243 F.3d 806, which sustained the seven-year firearm sentence. The granted question is whether brandishing that raises the mandatory minimum under 18 U.S.C. §924(c)(1)(A) must be charged and proved beyond a reasonable doubt; statutory construction is a necessary antecedent. Harris seeks sentence relief; the United States seeks affirmance.

The indictment charged carrying a firearm during and in relation to a drug transaction, without alleging brandishing. A lawful bench trial established the basic offense. Harris objected at sentencing that brandishing was an uncharged statutory element and added the constitutional argument on appeal. The sentencing judge treated brandishing as a close question, found it by a preponderance and imposed the seven-year minimum, which determined the guideline sentence under §2K2.4. Although the judge considered seven years appropriate, he declined to commit to that term if the enhancement were reversed. The basic conviction and separate evidentiary rulings are outside the granted challenge. Harris and Ring are separately decided matters; neither supplies a prior holding for the other. United States v. Angle was decided June 20, 2002.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**The judgment sustaining the seven-year firearm sentence is reversed, the sentence is vacated, and the case is remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg.

**Opposing:** O'Connor, Kennedy, Breyer.

Lawful resentencing from the five-year minimum and its applicable guideline consequence; another term requires an independently lawful basis and reasons.

The basic firearm conviction remains intact. The uncharged, preponderance-found brandishing condition cannot compel a seven-year floor. A possible future seven-year sentence is not an already imposed alternative sentence. Any further aggravated proceeding requires independent charging, waiver, procedural and double-jeopardy authority; the mandate supplies none automatically and fixes no replacement term.

#### Opinion Topology

J., and STEVENS, SOUTER, THOMAS, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined, and in which O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, and BREYER, JJ., joined as to Part I. O'CONNOR, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part, in which KENNEDY, J., joined. BREYER, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part.

II: constitutional floor, charge, waiver and proof, six. III: all four Rule 52(b) conditions and remedy, the same six. |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | O'Connor | Kennedy | Would affirm the firearm sentence | Joins Part I; rejects Parts II and III by seeking reconsideration of Angle's floor rule. |
| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Breyer |  | Would affirm the firearm sentence on a distinct constitutional ground | Joins Part I; continues opposition to Apprendi's sentencing allocation without adopting a logical distinction between minimum and maximum. |

#### Holdings

##### I. The amended statute uses a mandatory sentencing condition

**Holding and operative rule:** Amended §924(c)(1)(A) defines the basic firearm offense and assigns graduated mandatory minimums; brandishing is a sentencing condition under that structure, a classification that does not dispense with constitutional element protections. The opening exception applies to the extent a greater minimum is otherwise provided by §924(c) or another provision of law; brandishing requires displaying all or part of the firearm, or otherwise making its presence known to another person, for the purpose of intimidating that person, whether or not the firearm is directly visible.

**Authority:** Part I of Scalia's opinion, joined by all eight other Justices, supplies nine direct votes for this construction.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Congress's completed offense clause and ensuing directions for how the offender shall be sentenced identify a basic offense followed by penalty conditions. The five-, seven- and ten-year clauses establish the structure; the present claim concerns the seven-year brandishing condition. Jones requires attention to text, structure, tradition and consequence. Its statutory analysis does not make the placement of a provision conclusive. Castillo construed the materially different former firearm-type provision and therefore does not decide this amendment.

Defined intimidating conduct and two additional mandatory years make the aggravated-element reading plausible. The enacted arrangement nevertheless supplies the better statutory account. Avoidance does not license rewriting an ascertainable penalty provision merely to avoid deciding its constitutional operation. The statute itself does not prescribe preponderance factfinding or dispense with charging protection. Applying the independent constitutional requirements thus gives effect to its words without treating the sentencing label as dispositive. The greater-minimum exception remains intact; no dispute concerning its application is presented. The distinct discharge condition is not independently adjudicated on this record.

**Precedent treatment:** Jones supplies the interpretive method and retains its carjacking construction. Castillo is distinguished because its former firearm-type text differs materially; its statutory-element holding remains intact.

##### II. Compulsory additional punishment requires element protections

**Holding and operative rule:** A present-offense fact legally necessary to raise a mandatory minimum must receive the charging protections applicable to the prosecution and must be validly admitted as an element or proved beyond a reasonable doubt to the proper guilt factfinder. A lawful jury waiver permits the judge to determine the element under that same proof standard; it neither enlarges the indictment nor converts an ordinary sentencing finding into the required adjudication.

**Authority:** Part II of Scalia's opinion, joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplies six direct votes for the rule and its federal application.

**Controlling explanation:** Angle already holds that a present-offense fact changing either binding endpoint receives element protections and separately requires applicable federal grand-jury protection. The Court applies those holdings to brandishing. Before that additional finding, the law permits a sentence beginning at five years; afterward, it forbids a sentence below seven. The unchanged outer maximum does not erase that alteration of lawful punishment. Apprendi supplies the actual-authorization principle, and Gaudin and Winship supply the factfinder and proof protections once the aggravating fact is identified.

The Government and the dissent emphasize that seven years could have been selected within the outer range. That argument confuses discretionary selection with a new legal obligation. Judicial findings remain permissible for choosing punishment within a range already authorized; they cannot establish the fact that first changes the binding range. This is an allocation rule, not a policy judgment against mandatory sentences. Angle has already overruled McMillan's contrary unchanged-maximum distinction, and the Court adheres to that treatment. Legislative reliance does not sustain this pending sentence under a displaced rule; final judgments retain their own review doctrines.

Harris's indictment omitted brandishing, and his lawful jury waiver preserved the reasonable-doubt requirement. The contested preponderance finding supplies neither an element admission nor the missing aggravated adjudication. Prior-conviction facts, collateral retroactivity and a general redesign of sentencing are outside the present question; this decision creates no constitutional prior-conviction exception.

**Precedent treatment:** Angle is applied, including its prior overruling of McMillan to the stated extent. Apprendi supplies the punishment-authorization principle; Gaudin and Winship supply proper factfinding and reasonable doubt. Jones and Castillo retain their independent federal charging consequences.

##### III. This record satisfies all four plain-error requirements

**Holding and operative rule:** Assuming without deciding that Harris forfeited the constitutional theory, Rule 52(b) requires error, clarity at review in this settled-law-then-changed setting, the defendant's ordinary showing of outcome-affecting prejudice, and a separate determination that the error seriously affects the fairness, integrity or public reputation of judicial proceedings before discretionary correction is warranted. Harris satisfies each condition on this record; the Court reverses the sentence-sustaining judgment, vacates the firearm sentence and remands for lawful resentencing while preserving the conviction.

**Authority:** Part III of Scalia's opinion has the same six direct joins as Part II, including its affirmative plainness application, independent prejudice finding and discretionary correction.

**Controlling explanation:** Olano separates forfeiture from intentional waiver and preserves the defendant's ordinary prejudice burden. The statutory objection and later constitutional argument need not be treated as identical: even the stricter forfeiture standard yields relief. Applying Angle, compulsory use of the uncharged, lower-standard aggravator is error. Johnson makes it plain now because settled contrary trial law changed during direct review; the Court decides no timing rule for initially unsettled law.

Harris demonstrates outcome-affecting prejudice. He contested brandishing; the judge found it a close question under a preponderance standard; and the resulting minimum supplied the sentence through §2K2.4. The carrying verdict did not decide the distinct intimidation purpose beyond a reasonable doubt. The judge's view that seven years was appropriate is contrary evidence, but his express refusal to select that term independently leaves a reasonable probability of a different lawful sentencing outcome. This is an affirmative inference from the actual record, not presumed injury from lost discretion or a burden shifted to the Government. Possible future departure authority is not an entered alternative judgment.

Correction is independently warranted because genuinely contested conduct, never adjudicated with the required protections, compelled additional imprisonment. That circumstance seriously impairs sentencing fairness and integrity. Substantial-rights injury alone would not suffice, and actual innocence is unnecessary. Johnson's clear-materiality setting and Cotton's overwhelming, essentially uncontroverted threshold record are materially different. Neder's preserved-error inquiry supplies neither automatic reversal nor a hypothetical brandishing verdict; clear-error affirmance cannot supply the missing reasonable-doubt adjudication.

Resentencing begins from the five-year minimum and its applicable guideline consequence. Any different sentence requires an independently lawful basis and reasons. A further aggravated proceeding requires independent charging, waiver, procedural and double-jeopardy authority. Neither an automatic enhancement trial nor a particular replacement term follows.

**Precedent treatment:** Olano's four conditions and ordinary burden are applied; Johnson supplies limited review-time plainness but its refusal of correction is distinguished. Cotton preserves nonjurisdictional treatment and record-specific discretion. Neder retains its distinct preserved-error burden; Vonn permits relevant existing-record consideration without a burden shift.

### Kirk v. Louisiana, No. 01-8419

Summary merits decision, June 24, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Decided on the submitted record without oral argument. On review of the Louisiana Fourth Circuit Court of Appeal, 773 So. 2d 259, following denial of review by the Louisiana Supreme Court. The question is whether probable cause alone justifies a nonconsensual warrantless home entry to arrest and search when the appellate court has not decided exigency. Officers observed suspected drug transactions, stopped a departing buyer and entered Kirk's apartment before obtaining a warrant. They found drugs on Kirk and invoked concern about destruction. The appellate court upheld the suppression denial and conviction on probable cause without resolving that concern under the exception rule. Kirk seeks relief from that judgment; Louisiana relies on any properly supported exigency or independent-source ground.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**The state appellate judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains the warrantless home entry, arrest and search on probable cause alone, and the case is remanded, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, who would vacate and remand.

All nine agree that probable cause alone is insufficient and that the omitted inquiry must occur on remand. The formal reversal does not conclusively determine suppression or the ultimate conviction outcome.

#### Opinion Topology

Per curiam. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion concurring in part and dissenting in part.

| Concurrence in part and dissent in part | Stone-Zsela |  | Would vacate and remand rather than reverse | Joins Part I and the need for the omitted inquiries; preserves a different formal disposition |

#### Holdings

##### I. Probable cause alone does not authorize entry into a home

**Holding and operative rule:** Police may not make a nonconsensual entry into a home to arrest or search on probable cause alone: an appropriate warrant or a valid exception must independently authorize entry. When officers rely on exigency, both probable cause and circumstances sufficient to justify immediate entry without a warrant must exist; an arrest and a search incident to it cannot supply the missing authority to cross the threshold.

**Authority:** Part I of the per curiam Opinion of the Court, joined by all nine Justices. Stone-Zsela's disagreement about the formal disposition does not qualify his join in this rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a basis for believing a crime occurred is different from permission to enter a home. Payton protects that threshold even when probable cause would authorize an arrest in public. The Louisiana court treated the drugs' discovery on Kirk's person as sufficient because the officers had probable cause to arrest and could search incident to arrest. That reasoning omits the antecedent question whether they could lawfully enter to make the arrest.

The officers' concern that the departing buyer's arrest would lead to destruction of evidence is an asserted justification requiring assessment on the actual record. It neither establishes exigency by itself nor becomes irrelevant because probable cause existed. McArthur permitted a limited temporary restraint under a combined preservation justification and diligent warrant pursuit; it does not authorize this entry on probable cause alone. Kyllo likewise separates the need for authority at the time of an intrusion from a warrant obtained later. The later warrant cannot retroactively authorize the initial entry.

##### II. The omitted exigency and evidence inquiries require remand under the correct rule

**Holding and operative rule:** The Louisiana Court of Appeal's judgment is reversed insofar as it sustained the warrantless entry, arrest and search on probable cause alone, and the case is remanded for proceedings consistent with Payton. The receiving court must decide the actual exigency issue and address any properly remaining causal, independent-source and evidence consequences under their governing requirements; the conviction's ultimate disposition follows those determinations.

**Authority:** Part II of the per curiam Opinion of the Court, joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; eight votes for reversal and remand. Stone-Zsela agrees that the omitted inquiries require remand but would vacate the appellate judgment instead.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the appellate court's categorical legal error requires correction. Its omission of the exigency inquiry does not establish either that an emergency existed or that none existed. The competing record-based arguments must receive the adjudication the incorrect probable-cause rule displaced.

Entry authority and the admissibility of particular evidence are separate questions. Murray preserves use of a genuinely independent later source; a later warrant is not independent merely because it was issued after entry, and independence affecting admissibility does not validate the earlier intrusion. If that ground remains properly available, the court must determine whether the warrant and recovered evidence actually rested on a source independent of the unlawful entry, including whether the entry prompted the decision to seek the warrant and whether information from it affected issuance. Those are ordinary factual and legal applications on remand. The record supplies no final exigency or independence determination for this Court to substitute. The mandate therefore leaves the ultimate suppression and conviction consequences for the receiving court under the corrected rule.

#### Separate Writings

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** He joins Part I's home-entry rule and agrees that the appellate court must address exigency on the actual record and, if necessary, the causal and independent-source issues for the evidence. He would vacate the state appellate judgment and remand rather than reverse it. The omitted exception inquiry prevents this Court from replacing it with an invented factual determination. Probable cause answers whether a sufficient basis for arrest or search exists; Payton separately protects the home's threshold. ### Ring v. Arizona, No. 01-488

Merits decision, June 24, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 22, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Supreme Court of Arizona, 200 Ariz. 267, 25 P.3d 1139, which affirmed Ring's death sentence. The question is whether the Sixth and Fourteenth Amendments require the jury to find a present-offense aggravating fact necessary for death eligibility, notwithstanding Walton v. Arizona. Ring seeks relief from judicially authorized death; Arizona seeks affirmance and argues alternatively that the robbery verdict supplies harmlessness.

A jury convicted Ring of felony murder in an armored-car robbery but did not return a premeditated-murder verdict. The judge made the additional findings required for death eligibility and imposed death. The surviving aggravation included pecuniary gain; the appellate treatment of other aggravation supplied no jury finding. Arizona contends that pecuniary gain was implicit in the robbery verdict. The actual elements, instructions, required findings and relevant record control that claim. Harris and Ring are separate same-day decisions sharing the law entering June 24; neither supplies prior law for the other. Angle's June 20 decision did not itself overrule Walton.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**The Arizona Supreme Court's judgment sustaining the death sentence is reversed and remanded: seven votes to reverse, one to vacate, and one to affirm.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela would vacate the appellate affirmance and remand; O'Connor would affirm.

The State's actual harmlessness argument returns for proper consideration. Breyer supports reversal independently on an Eighth Amendment ground.

The felony-murder conviction remains intact. Stone-Zsela would reverse the ruling that judge-only death-eligibility factfinding is constitutional, vacate the appellate affirmance and remand for the same initial harmlessness inquiry. The Court's reversal and Stone-Zsela's proposed appellate vacatur each leave death-sentence relief conditional on that inquiry; neither itself unconditionally vacates the death sentence. Under the Court's five-Justice harmlessness rule, the State must establish noncontribution to the actual verdict beyond a reasonable doubt. If that showing fails, the death sentence must be vacated and any further sentencing must rest on independent state-law authority consistent with constitutional and double-jeopardy limits. If the State carries its burden, a consistent sentence judgment may be reinstated. The mandate creates no new Arizona procedure or automatic enhancement trial.

#### Holdings

##### I. The jury must determine the fact that first authorizes death

**Holding and operative rule:** A statutory present-offense aggravating fact necessary to make death legally available must be validly admitted as an element or proved to the jury beyond a reasonable doubt in a jury-tried prosecution. Walton is overruled insofar as it permits a sentencing judge, sitting without a jury, to supply that necessary aggravating fact; existing lawful jury-waiver requirements remain, and waiver does not lower the reasonable-doubt burden.

**Authority:** Part I of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg, supplying seven direct votes. Breyer's judgment vote rests on a different Eighth Amendment ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that capital eligibility turns on lawful punishment authority. Without an additional statutory aggravator, Arizona law does not permit death on Ring's felony-murder verdict. Finding that aggravator therefore enlarges the punishment the law allows. Apprendi supplies this actual-authorization principle; Angle reinforces the distinction between a binding legal condition and ordinary selection within existing authority. Neither earlier case decided Walton's reserved capital boundary. The Court decides it now.

The State's description of murder as generally punishable by death overlooks the additional statutory prerequisite. A sentencing label cannot do the constitutional work of an element. Winship supplies proof beyond a reasonable doubt, and Gaudin protects the proper factfinder's determination of the legally necessary fact. Judicial expertise or consistency cannot transfer that constitutional responsibility. Capital narrowing identifies the permissible class; it does not require that a judge find the fact establishing membership.

Walton's contrary allocation cannot coexist with this principle. Procedural reliance deserves respect in lawful implementation, but does not justify retaining the inconsistent rule for the fact that first permits death in a pending case. Judicial final selection and review are distinct from eligibility; Harris v. Alabama's adjudicated advisory-weight and override questions do not determine this allocation issue. Nor can appellate reweighing itself supply the missing eligibility verdict.

Ring's granted claim concerns the necessary aggravating fact. Mitigation, ultimate selection and complete weighing procedure are outside that question, as are state grand-jury incorporation, prior-conviction facts and collateral retroactivity. The holding supplies no constitutional prior-conviction exception or new general capital-trial procedure. Neither a valid admission nor a lawful jury waiver supplies the missing adjudication here.

**Precedent treatment:** Apprendi's rule is extended to the expressly reserved capital-eligibility boundary; Angle is applied as supporting range doctrine. Walton's contrary necessary-aggravator allocation is overruled. Winship and Gaudin supply proof and factfinder protections; Harris v. Alabama retains its distinct final-selection holding.

##### II. The omitted eligibility finding receives qualified actual-verdict review

**Holding and operative rule:** This omitted statutory eligibility finding is subject to the qualified Neder inquiry: the State must establish beyond a reasonable doubt that the omission did not contribute to the actual verdict, examining the actual instructions, the evidence they directed to the jury, required findings and material conflicts. Necessary equivalent jury findings suffice but are not exclusive; supported whole-record inferences may establish noncontribution only when reliably connected to the actual adjudication, without new credibility findings or substitution of sufficient evidence for a hypothetical verdict.

**Authority:** Part II of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Souter and Thomas, supplying five direct votes for the method and conditional remedy. Scalia and Ginsburg permit only genuine equivalent jury findings and do not join this broader method; neither Breyer nor O'Connor supplies an additional rationale vote.

**Controlling explanation:** Neder applies Chapman's burden to a preserved element omission through examination of the actual verdict. Its method is extended to this omitted statutory eligibility finding because the question remains whether the proper factfinder's actual adjudication makes the omission harmless, not whether an appellate court believes the aggravator probably true. Roy and Yates supply attention to the instructions, required findings and material evidentiary conflicts. Sullivan's invalid reasonable-doubt instruction remains distinct from omission of this particular aggravating element.

Arizona actually argues that the robbery verdict implicitly establishes pecuniary gain. The word robbery does not decide that contention. The reviewing court must compare the particular aggravator under Arizona law with the elements, actual charge and necessary jury findings, then evaluate any qualifying record inference. Strong or uncontested evidence alone is not an automatic cure; it must support noncontribution to the actual adjudication beyond a reasonable doubt. New credibility resolution is forbidden, and appellate reweighing of selection factors cannot substitute for the eligibility inquiry.

The narrower view would permit cure only where the jury necessarily made an equivalent finding. Such findings are sufficient, but Neder's qualified method also permits a reliable inference from the instructions and whole record without converting review into an initial factfinding trial. The burden remains the State's throughout. The argument's actual-record application was not decided below under the correct constitutional rule, so it returns for initial determination there. The Court determines neither harmlessness nor harmfulness here.

If the State fails its showing, the death sentence must be vacated; further sentencing requires independent state-law authority and compliance with constitutional and double-jeopardy limits. If the burden is met, a consistent sentence judgment may be reinstated. A remand for initial harmlessness review does not itself require unconditional sentence vacatur and creates no automatic enhancement trial.

**Precedent treatment:** Neder's qualified actual-verdict method is extended to the omitted eligibility finding; Chapman retains the State's burden. Roy and Yates supply the instruction-centered inquiry. Sullivan's distinct structural rule remains intact. Clemons retains lawful appellate reweighing but supplies no missing eligibility adjudication.

##### III. The appellate affirmance is reversed and the actual review contention returns

**Holding and operative rule:** The Arizona Supreme Court's judgment is reversed insofar as it sustains Ring's death sentence under judge-only eligibility factfinding, and the case is remanded for proper consideration of the State's actual harmlessness contention. The felony-murder conviction stands; the appellate reversal is distinct from unconditional vacatur of the death sentence.

**Authority:** Part III of Kennedy's opinion is joined by Stevens, Scalia, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. These six support the reversal rationale; Breyer adds a seventh reversal-and-remand vote on his independent selection ground. Stone-Zsela would reverse the judge-only constitutional ruling, vacate the appellate affirmance and remand, and therefore does not join Part III. His agreement with Parts I and II remains distinct from this formal disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** The Arizona judgment sustained the challenged allocation under Walton. The Court's eligibility holding removes that legal basis for affirmance. The State's alternative actual-verdict argument remains material and requires initial consideration below. A ruling about who must find the aggravator does not itself establish the outcome of that distinct review inquiry. Neder supports leaving initial harmlessness application to the lower court; agreement on this appellate remand supplies no additional join in Part II's broader method. The mandate preserves the existing guilt adjudication and leaves further sentence proceedings to the governing constitutional standard and independently available state law.

**Precedent treatment:** Walton no longer supports the challenged eligibility allocation. Neder supplies the distinction between reversing an erroneous legal premise and initially applying the proper harmlessness rule below.

The seven-Justice eligibility holding does not depend on Breyer's independent jury-selection theory or on combining inconsistent harmlessness methods. The State's actual-record application is remand work under the announced rule, not an unresolved legal standard. Preserved lawful state procedures and constitutional limits control any later sentencing.

#### Separate Writings

**Stone-Zsela, concurring in part and dissenting in part.** Stone-Zsela joins the eligibility rule and the qualified actual-verdict inquiry in Parts I and II. He would reverse the ruling that judge-only death-eligibility factfinding is constitutional, vacate the appellate affirmance of the death sentence, and remand for the State's actual harmlessness argument. The appellate affirmance should be set aside for that initial inquiry rather than reversed as in Part III. The State must establish harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt under the qualified Neder framework by examining the actual elements, instructions, findings and qualifying evidence, without resolving new credibility disputes or treating robbery as an automatic pecuniary-gain finding. If the State fails, the death sentence must be vacated; any later sentencing requires independent state-law authority and compliance with constitutional and double-jeopardy limits. If it succeeds, a consistent sentence judgment may be reinstated. He preserves the felony-murder conviction and supplies no universal structural-error rule or automatic new capital proceeding.

**Kennedy, concurring.** Kennedy joins the entire Court opinion while maintaining that Apprendi was wrongly decided. Its settled maximum rule nevertheless must be implemented in a principled way, and Walton cannot survive where an additional fact is indispensable to death eligibility. His disagreement with Angle's distinct floor extension does not justify withholding the jury protection required by the governing maximum rule here. Respect for state expectations counsels caution about other extensions and lawful implementation; it does not reconcile this judge-only eligibility allocation with Apprendi. He supports the qualified actual-record remand rather than a general jury-selection command.

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment; Ginsburg joins Part II.** In Part I, Scalia supports the eligibility holding despite his continued objection to the capital-narrowing requirements associated with Furman. Once the State makes an aggravating fact legally necessary, its origin does not remove the jury guarantee. Distinguishing factors adopted voluntarily from those responding to constitutional narrowing would not provide a reliable basis for dispensing with that protection. Gaudin and the punishment-authority principle underlying Apprendi and Angle require the fact to receive element safeguards. He preserves the distinction between that finding and the ultimate life-or-death selection, disagreeing with Breyer's independent Eighth Amendment approach. The concern that legislative sentencing labels could diminish the jury's constitutional role reinforces his allocation position; it is not a new factual finding about Ring.

In Part II, joined by Ginsburg, Scalia rejects the Court's broader whole-record inference method. Their Neder position permits harmlessness only through genuine equivalent findings the jury actually made. A court's assessment that the jury probably would have found pecuniary gain cannot supply an unasked element. They join Court Parts I and III and support reversal and remand, leaving the actual charge and findings to be examined, but withhold Court Part II. Ginsburg joins only this error-review limitation, not Scalia's separate account of capital-narrowing doctrine. Neither finds that robbery automatically establishes pecuniary gain or adopts a new appellate credibility determination.

**Breyer, concurring in the judgment.** Breyer continues to reject Apprendi's Sixth Amendment reclassification and joins neither the Court's eligibility rationale nor its broader harmlessness method. He nevertheless concludes that the Eighth Amendment requires a jury to take responsibility for the ultimate decision to impose death. He expressly reconsiders his own join in Harris v. Alabama and adopts the jury-responsibility view expressed by Stevens in that case and Spaziano. Gregg and Furman require special capital safeguards; Witherspoon identifies the jury's role in expressing community judgment, and proportionality requires attention to whether this defendant should suffer death. In Breyer's view, retribution is the principal justification for capital punishment, and a jury better expresses the community's moral judgment about its use in the particular case than a single official, even an elected judge. He treats death differently from ordinary guided sentencing. His ground reaches final selection, not simply an aggravator that makes death available: harmlessness of the omitted eligibility finding alone would not answer his objection to judge-only final selection. He concurs in reversal and remand, but his separate rule commands no Court majority.

**O'Connor, dissenting.** O'Connor would affirm against the allocation claim by retaining Walton and reconsidering Apprendi. She acknowledges that the two rules are incompatible; capital guidance does not harmonize them. Her Apprendi and Angle dissents defend legislative authority to distinguish offense elements from sentencing considerations and the historical judicial role in selecting punishment. She regards the Court's maximum-fact rule as constitutionally unwarranted and its extension as disruptive of settled state procedures. Those objections support her choice of which precedent to reconsider, not a finding that an aggravator is legally unnecessary. She does not join Breyer's Eighth Amendment rule, supply a vote for the Court's broader harmlessness directions, or sustain the sentence by making a substitute pecuniary-gain finding. Ordinary error review and collateral limits remain distinct from her preferred no-error ground.

The State's contention that robbery establishes pecuniary gain requires examination of the actual instructions, necessary findings and qualifying evidence; a general offense label does not resolve it.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Arizona Supreme Court's death-sentence affirmance is reversed, and the matter returns for initial application of the State's burden under the Court's qualified actual-verdict standard. The felony-murder conviction remains intact. If the State establishes harmlessness beyond a reasonable doubt, a consistent sentence judgment may be reinstated. If it fails, the death sentence must be vacated; any further sentencing proceeding requires an independent basis in state law and compliance with constitutional and double-jeopardy limits. The mandate neither creates a new Arizona procedure nor itself unconditionally vacates the death sentence.

### Trans Union Corp. v. Federal Trade Commission, on review of D.C. Circuit No. 00-1141

Merits decision, June 24, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

The judgment under review is the D.C. Circuit's decision in No. 00-1141, 245 F.3d 809 and 267 F.3d 1138. On review of the denial of relief from the FTC order, including the reported rehearing clarification, on the developed merits record. The question is whether restricting sale of targeted marketing lists compiled from consumer credit records violates the agency's First Amendment rights. Trans Union seeks relief from the restriction; the FTC seeks its enforcement within the FCRA's permissible-use scheme. The lists selected consumers through credit-file attributes and conveyed names and addresses; membership could communicate the selected financial trait. Statutory classification and administrative-law claims outside this First Amendment review are not reopened.

##### Statutory setting of the comparison

These are the statute's permissions and duties, not findings that any consumer, reporting agency, recipient or offer complied.

**Consumer direction and financial purposes.** Section 604(a)(2), 15 U.S.C. §1681b(a)(2), permits furnishing in accordance with the consumer's written instructions; silence or failure to opt out is not an instruction. Section 604(c)(1)(A) separately permits consumer authorization for the agency to provide that report to that recipient in a credit or insurance transaction not initiated by the consumer. That clause does not itself require a writing and does not authorize unrelated marketing generally. For the credit and insurance routes, the agency must have reason to believe the recipient intends use for credit extension, account review or collection involving the consumer, or insurance underwriting involving the consumer. Under section 604(a)(3)(F), the agency must have reason to believe that the recipient has a legitimate business need in connection with a consumer-initiated transaction or to review an account for continued compliance with its terms. Section 603(m) excludes a relationship holder's review of the consumer's existing account or insurance policy, or collection of that account, from the unsolicited-transaction definition; it does not create a general new-marketing permission.

**Offers without affirmative authorization.** Section 604(c)(1)(B) permits the unsolicited credit or insurance route only when the transaction consists of a firm offer, the agency has complied with subsection (e), and no effective subsection (e) election excludes the consumer's name and address. Under section 603(l), the offer must be honored if the consumer meets the specific consumer-report criteria used for selection. Further conditions may require application information establishing satisfaction of specific creditworthiness or insurability criteria fixed before selection for deciding whether to extend the offered credit or insurance; verification of continuing satisfaction of selection criteria through a report, application or other relevant creditworthiness or insurability information, or verification of application information to determine satisfaction of the applicable creditworthiness or insurability criteria; and collateral required before selection and disclosed in the offer. These are conditional offers, not guaranteed extensions, and the statute supplies no minimum economic value or favorable-rate requirement.

**Information and use.** Under section 604(c)(2), that route permits only names and addresses, a nonunique identifier used solely to verify identity, and other information that does not identify the consumer's relationship or experience with a particular creditor or other entity. The last category and list membership can disclose a financial trait; the permission is not confined to two address fields. Section 604(c)(3) separately bars furnishing unsolicited-transaction inquiry records except section 609(a)(5)'s disclosure to the consumer, on request with proper identification, of inquiries identifying that consumer during the preceding year. Section 604(f) separately requires an authorized furnishing purpose and a prospective user's general or specific purpose certification under section 607. A permitted transfer does not authorize unrestricted subsequent use.

**Consumer elections and agency duties.** Section 604(e) allows notice through the agency's notification system or its signed election form. Exclusion begins five business days after notice and applies to affiliates. System-only notice lasts two years beginning five business days after notice, unless cancelled sooner; a signed-form election continues until cancellation through the system. The agency must explain the two-year limit after system notice and provide the signed form within five business days if requested during that notice. Its system must include a toll-free telephone number and allow notice with appropriate identification. Publication at least annually in a general-circulation publication for the service area must describe possible file use and give the exclusion address and toll-free number; the original deadline was 365 days after September 30, 1996. A qualifying system and publication maintained for affiliates satisfies those obligations for them; nationwide reporting agencies must maintain the system jointly. These safeguards do not make an election immediately effective, a telephone election perpetual, or general publication proof of individual awareness.

**Separate duties of users.** Section 615(d), 15 U.S.C. §1681m(d), requires each written solicitation based on the firm-offer route to provide a clear and conspicuous statement disclosing use of report information, satisfaction of selection criteria, any applicable selection or further creditworthiness or insurability criteria or collateral conditions that may prevent extension, the right to prohibit unsolicited-transaction use, and how to exercise that right through the system, including its address and toll-free number. Selection, extension and collateral criteria must be retained for three years from the offer. These are separate user duties, not additional express elements of section 604(c)(1)(B); the written-solicitation trigger neither guarantees notice before first disclosure nor covers every oral communication. Federal and state unfair- or deceptive-practice authority remains. Section 615(c)'s distinct liability defense requires proof by a preponderance that reasonable compliance procedures were maintained at the time of the alleged violation; it does not erase those duties or authorize an otherwise impermissible section 604 purpose.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**The judgment denying relief on the First Amendment challenge to the restriction on the identified nonconsensual credit-derived targeted marketing lists is affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The order remains enforceable at that application. Its scope does not expand to independently collected public information, consented uses or statutory permissions, and claims outside review remain undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a concurring opinion.

#### Holdings

##### I. The identified marketing use receives independent speech scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** Restricting sale of these credit-derived lists for commercial targeting requires independent review under the governing commercial-speech standard, even though the agency lawfully possesses the data and can market through other channels. The government must establish a substantial interest, direct material advancement and a reasonable proportional fit; corporate status, payment and lawful possession do not themselves answer those questions or classify every publication as commercial speech.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court, joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the sufficient application in Parts I–III; Thomas retains his broader theoretical qualification without withholding an application join.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that names and addresses selected through a financial characteristic communicate that characteristic to a buyer. The product here supplies consumer attributes for commercial targeting. Its classification rests on its actual function, not merely the seller's incorporation or profit. U.S. West requires independent speech scrutiny of a marketing-use condition despite lawful custody and alternative channels. Central Hudson and Fox supply the substantial-interest, advancement and proportionality requirements. The Court applies those requirements to this external sale of credit-derived information. Statutory and administrative-law claims outside the granted review, public-concern publication and independently acquired information are not decided.

##### II. Preventing unrelated credit-file repurposing materially protects confidentiality

**Holding and operative rule:** Protecting confidential financial information against unrelated commercial repurposing is substantial, and prohibiting the identified nonconsensual sale of credit-derived marketing classifications directly and materially advances that interest. The authorization, financial-purpose and firm-offer permissions, with the actual conditions stated above, do not defeat that conclusion even though permitted prescreening can disclose the same financial trait without affirmative authorization.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies nine direct votes for this confined advancement determination.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that this restriction addresses the disclosure constituting the privacy harm itself. A buyer learns the selected characteristic even without receiving an entire credit report. Preventing the unrelated sale therefore protects the information against a further purpose and recipient; no prediction that consumers will refrain from buying products supplies the causal connection.

The firm-offer permission is a real qualification. It can transmit private selection information before individual notice, without affirmative authorization and without guaranteeing completed credit or insurance. The Court therefore does not describe the Act as preventing every unchosen disclosure. Its financial-purpose boundary, conditionally binding offer, information limits, consumer elections and restricted use instead distinguish a constrained financial transaction from unrestricted unrelated targeting. The residual information category is part of that assessment, not treated as harmless.

Discovery Network and Greater New Orleans require this comparison of actual permissions and prohibitions. Their materially inconsistent schemes do not make every partial confidentiality protection invalid. U.S. West recognizes protection against repurposing but does not transfer its different approval mechanism to this statute. The supported credit-source and external-sale setting supplies the direct relationship here; statutory commands supply no finding of actual compliance or universal consumer awareness.

##### III. The actual statutory arrangement reasonably fits this application

**Holding and operative rule:** The FCRA restriction on these unrelated credit-derived marketing lists reasonably fits its confidentiality interest when assessed with the separate consumer-direction routes and the defined financial-purpose, conditional-offer, information, election and user-duty limits; the D.C. Circuit's denial of relief on this First Amendment challenge is affirmed. Fox does not require extending a comparable opt-out regime to unrelated marketing merely because it would burden the seller less, and this holding neither declares opt-out generally inadequate nor makes written authorization constitutionally necessary for every information use.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous Opinion of the Court supplies nine direct votes for the fit application and confined affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the actual distinction is proportionate. Written consumer instructions authorize disclosure at their own scope; recipient-specific authorization separately operates within unsolicited credit or insurance. The alternative financial route combines an offer enforceable when its bounded criteria are met, restricted information and purpose, effective consumer elections, and separate written-solicitation and retained-criteria duties. Those duties have different actors and triggers and are not interchangeable consent exceptions.

Trans Union's proposed extension of opt-out is substantial: the statute itself shows that such a system can protect some privacy interests. Obtaining written instructions for unrelated use imposes a real burden, unlike U.S. West's flexible oral, written or electronic approval. But the proposed extension would expand the purposes and recipients allowed to obtain credit-derived classifications without consumer direction beyond the specifically constrained financial transaction. The credit-source relationship and the external repurposing at issue justify requiring instructions for that expansion. Fox requires reasonable proportionality, not the least burdensome imaginable arrangement.

The comparison includes the actual remaining privacy loss: the initial disclosure can precede individual solicitation notice, elections take five business days, system-only elections expire after two years, and residual information can reveal financial traits. No minimum offer value, actual awareness or universal compliance is assumed. These limits do not erase the financial transaction and information-use boundaries. Lorillard requires examination of the actual burden; Thompson's rejected product-promotion proxy differs from a restriction directly addressing the confidential disclosure itself. Alternative marketing channels mitigate the burden but do not independently validate it. The order remains confined to the challenged lists and does not reach consented uses, independently collected public information or other statutory permissions.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission, 447 U.S. 557: supplies ordinary commercial-speech scrutiny, including the government's substantial-interest and direct-material-advancement requirements.
- Board of Trustees v. Fox, 492 U.S. 469: supplies reasonable proportional fit without a least-restrictive-means command.
- U.S. West, Inc. v. Federal Communications Commission, February 7, 2000: independent review and the supported confidentiality principle extend to this external-sale setting; its flexible FCC approval mechanism is not imported into the FCRA's distinct clauses.
- Cincinnati v. Discovery Network, 507 U.S. 410: its requirement of a distinction responsive to actual harm is applied, with permitted prescreening's remaining financial disclosure expressly considered.
- Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association, Inc. v. United States, June 14, 1999: its examination of actual exceptions and separate advancement and fit inquiries is preserved; the constrained financial-use permission supplies the distinction here.
- Lorillard Tobacco Co. v. Reilly, June 28, 2001: separate advancement and fit remain necessary, and alternative channels do not erase the burden on protected expression.
- Thompson v. Western States Medical Center, April 29, 2002: its fit ruling against inadequately justified drug-promotion conditions is distinguished by the direct disclosure mechanism and actual safeguards here; its assumed advancement does not become a new finding.
- 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island, 517 U.S. 484: its independent scrutiny of truthful-information burdens remains; a broader separate objection to paternalistic suppression is not made the Court's rule.
- Reno v. Condon, 528 U.S. 141: its information-market authority holding supplies no independent answer to this speech question.

#### Law After Decision

The governing commercial-speech framework sustains the identified restriction because it directly protects against unrelated credit-file repurposing and reasonably fits that interest in light of the actual financial-use permissions. Permitted prescreening can cause a comparable disclosure, but its purpose, offer and information-use constraints distinguish it from unrestricted unrelated targeting. Neither all FCRA applications nor every possible data sale is adjudicated. Statutory compliance remains a separate matter, and opt-out is neither generally inadequate nor constitutionally compulsory here.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Thomas, concurring.** He joins the Court's sufficient confidentiality application and affirmance without treating ordinary commercial-speech scrutiny as the complete measure of First Amendment protection. His 44 Liquormart and Thompson positions reject suppressing accurate product information to prevent willing listeners from acting on it. The present restriction instead addresses repurposing another consumer's confidential financial information. He acknowledges that firm-offer prescreening can disclose a trait without affirmative authorization; the genuine conditional offer and information-use limits, not an absence of speech or inferior corporate rights, supply the relevant difference. The concurrence creates no universal data-ownership rule and does not withdraw any operative join.

Trans Union Corp. v. FTC, 245 F.3d 809 and 267 F.3d 1138; 15 U.S.C. §§1681a(d), (l), (m), 1681b(a), (c), (e), (f), 1681g(a)(5), 1681m(c)–(d). The statutory duties establish legal requirements, not actual consumer instructions, effective elections, individual notice, qualifying offers, user certification or compliance. The constitutional judgment concerns the identified lists and granted speech challenge.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The reviewed First Amendment denial of relief is affirmed, leaving the FTC order operative for the identified lists. Supreme Court review of that question is complete; ordinary mandate administration follows.

### United States v. Ruiz, No. 01-595

Merits decision, June 24, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 24, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 241 F.3d 1157, which vacated Ruiz's sentence and remanded for factual and remedial proceedings on the plea-disclosure condition. The questions are whether the Government may condition a fast-track sentencing recommendation on waiver of impeachment and affirmative-defense information, and whether the asserted constitutional injury permits sentence review under 18 U.S.C. §3742.

The proposed agreement retained a continuing promise to disclose information establishing factual innocence but required the identified disclosure waivers. Ruiz rejected the term, the offer was withdrawn and she later pleaded guilty without agreement. The judge understood departure discretion but denied the requested two-level reduction. The United States seeks reversal; Ruiz seeks a lawful sentencing remedy for the lost recommendation. This is a sentence appeal, not enforcement of an accepted bargain or a proved suppression claim seeking plea withdrawal.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**The Ninth Circuit's sentence vacatur and remand based on invalidity of the proposed disclosure condition are reversed, 8–1.**

**Supporting:** Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stone-Zsela, who would affirm vacatur and a limited sentencing remand.

The district sentence remains operative against this presented challenge. The conviction is undisturbed. The condition-based remedial proceedings directed by the Ninth Circuit cannot continue on the rejected theory.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined, and in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., joined as to Part I. THOMAS, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed an opinion dissenting in part.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Breyer | Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg throughout; Stone-Zsela in Part I only | Reverses sentence vacatur and condition-based remand | I: statutory inquiry; II: impeachment; III: affirmative-defense term; IV: sentence consequence |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Thomas |  | Supplies the eighth vote for reversal | Separate trial-function rationale; agrees with jurisdiction-determining inquiry and absence of accepted promise without joining the Court opinion |
| Dissent in part | Stone-Zsela |  | Would affirm vacatur and a limited sentencing remand | Joins Part I; qualified pre-plea disclosure duty and invalidity of the blanket condition to that extent |

#### Holdings

##### I. The asserted legal error must be examined to determine sentence-review authority

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 3742(a)(1) permits a court to determine whether an asserted constitutional condition connected to a sentence establishes that the sentence was imposed in violation of law; a merely discretionary refusal to depart, when the judge understood the discretion, supplies no such appeal. A mistaken belief that departure authority was absent could supply legal error, but that premise is not present here.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court has the direct support of Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer; Stone-Zsela also joins this statutory inquiry. Thomas agrees in his concurrence that the claimed statutory basis can be determined, without joining the Court opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that disagreement with the sentencing judge's choice differs from a claim that the choice followed an unconstitutional bargaining condition. The judge understood departure authority and declined to exercise it. That excludes the mistaken-authority route, but Ruiz's refusal of the waiver, withdrawal of the offer and request for the associated sentencing treatment connect her legal allegation to the sentence sufficiently to require its examination.

Mine Workers supplies the power to determine jurisdiction. Steel Co.'s insistence on an actual jurisdictional basis is respected: the constitutional question must be resolved to determine the asserted statutory basis, rather than being decided as unrelated merits advice on assumed jurisdiction. This inquiry creates neither general review of discretionary leniency nor a guaranteed bargain. The constitutional answer and its jurisdictional consequence follow in Parts II–IV.

##### II. The Constitution does not require advance impeachment disclosure for this plea agreement

**Holding and operative rule:** The Constitution does not require the Government to provide material impeachment information concerning informants or other witnesses before entering the plea agreement presented here. A competent defendant must actually understand the right relinquished, the charge and the constitutionally relevant consequences and act voluntarily; that requirement does not guarantee advance knowledge of every witness weakness or every fact useful to counsel's assessment.

**Authority:** Part II of the Opinion of the Court has seven direct joins: Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Thomas supports the result on his separate trial-function ground; Stone-Zsela dissents.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a constitutional trial-disclosure guarantee does not itself fix the timing of every item useful to bargaining. Brady, Bagley and Kyles protect fair adjudication through favorable evidence, including combined impeachment and preparation uses. Brady v. United States and the received plea cases nevertheless permit a knowing, voluntary decision made with uncertainty about the prosecution's proof. Knowledge of the charge and the constitutional choice differs from complete knowledge of evidentiary consequences.

The contrary position is substantial: known weakness in a decisive witness may materially affect investigation, competent advice and willingness to plead. A duty limited to useful known information would be narrower than open-file discovery. But practical importance does not establish the asserted constitutional timing duty. The usefulness of impeachment varies with the information and the defendant's existing knowledge; an actually informed choice can accept uncertainty about credibility without making that evidence irrelevant.

Legitimate early-resolution and witness-confidentiality interests also weigh against making trial-oriented preparation a constitutional prerequisite to this agreement. These are general considerations, not findings of danger to a witness here. The continuing factual-innocence disclosure promise and ordinary plea safeguards form part of this setting; neither is treated as proof of compliance or an automatic cure. Godinez and Bousley retain their requirements of capacity, actual choice and knowledge of the offense. Vonn preserves the judge's distinct duties and does not establish forfeiture here. The holding rejects this categorical timing claim, not a supported claim of actual deception, ineffective assistance or an uninformed plea.

##### III. The separate affirmative-defense term is not categorically invalid in this agreement

**Holding and operative rule:** In the context of the presented agreement, including its continuing promise to disclose information establishing factual innocence, the Constitution does not require advance provision of the identified affirmative-defense information as a prerequisite to the plea bargain. Affirmative-defense information is not equated with impeachment or factual innocence, and the holding does not decide a proved concealed dispositive defense, false representation, factual-innocence suppression, defective advice or an actual uninformed plea.

**Authority:** Part III has the same seven direct Court-opinion joins. Thomas reaches the result under his separate fair-trial account; Stone-Zsela dissents from the constitutional conclusion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the two disclosure categories require separate treatment even though much of the timing analysis is shared. A complete affirmative defense may matter despite admitted conduct and may fall outside the factual-innocence promise. That possibility makes the information important; it does not make a categorical advance-disclosure requirement a necessary condition of the offered bargain.

Informed choice can allocate uncertainty about proof while preserving the requirements of knowledge, voluntary decision and competent advice. The timing demand can also require substantial trial preparation before early resolution. The trial-fairness guarantee does not entail that demand in this agreement's setting. The continuing innocence promise remains a separate term, not a finding that all defense information was furnished or that all defenses establish innocence. The Court rejects the asserted categorical duty on its merits; absence of a particular withheld item is not an alternative rule that an unlawful bargaining condition never causes injury.

##### IV. The condition supplies no ground for vacating this sentence

**Holding and operative rule:** Because the identified disclosure condition is not unconstitutional at the scope decided, section 3742(a)(1) supplies no authority on this theory to review the understood discretionary refusal to depart; the Ninth Circuit's sentence vacatur and condition-based remand are reversed. Ruiz's unaccepted offer creates no enforceable recommendation, and her later guilty plea without agreement did not itself assent to the rejected waiver; her conviction remains undisturbed.

**Authority:** Part IV has the seven Court-opinion joins; Thomas supplies the eighth judgment vote on his separate ground. Stone-Zsela would affirm vacatur and a limited sentencing remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the asserted unlawful-condition basis fails because the condition is valid at the decided scope. It does not fail merely because Ruiz lacked an entitlement to the bargaining benefit or had not accepted the proposed contract. An unlawful condition can cause injury without such an entitlement, but that necessary predicate is absent here.

Mezzanatto enforces a knowing, voluntary and limited statement-use agreement; it neither creates a universal power to demand every waiver nor converts this rejected offer into an accepted bargain. The sentence therefore remains operative against this presented challenge, and the Ninth Circuit's remand to remedy the rejected constitutional theory cannot continue. The decision adds no innocence prerequisite or second harmlessness hurdle to a completed constitutional violation and changes no independent sentencing rule.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Mine Workers, 330 U.S. 258: applied for authority to determine the statutory jurisdictional basis, not unrestricted appellate review of a discretionary sentence.
- Steel Co. v. Citizens for a Better Environment, 523 U.S. 83: its requirement of an actual jurisdictional basis remains; the constitutional issue here determines that claimed basis rather than supplying advisory merits review.
- Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83: its favorable-evidence protection for fair trial adjudication remains; it does not itself establish the categorical preagreement timing duty asserted here.
- United States v. Bagley, 473 U.S. 667: its trial protection for impeachment information remains; practical usefulness to bargaining does not itself establish the asserted categorical advance-disclosure duty.
- Kyles v. Whitley, April 19, 1995: collective lawful uses and case-team responsibility retain their force in trial-disclosure review; they do not establish the categorical preagreement duty asserted here.
- Strickler v. Greene, June 17, 1999: its actual suppression, reliance and retrospective materiality inquiries remain distinct from this proposed timing requirement.
- Brady v. United States, 397 U.S. 742: permits a knowing, voluntary plea choice despite uncertainty about the prosecution's proof, without guaranteeing complete evidentiary knowledge.
- Boykin v. Alabama, 395 U.S. 238: preserves the requirement of an actually informed and voluntary constitutional choice.
- Godinez v. Moran, June 24, 1993: competence remains distinct from an actually informed and voluntary choice.
- Bousley v. United States, May 18, 1998: knowledge of the real offense remains necessary to the constitutional plea choice.
- United States v. Mezzanatto, January 18, 1995: its limited knowing and voluntary impeachment-use waiver remains operative; it establishes neither assent to Ruiz's rejected term nor a universal advance-waiver rule.
- United States v. Vonn, March 4, 2002: its mandatory plea-warning duty and relevant-whole-record review retain their scope; no Rule 11 forfeiture is inferred in this different constitutional-condition appeal.

#### Law After Decision

The Constitution does not impose the asserted advance impeachment-disclosure duty before this plea agreement, and the separate affirmative-defense condition is not categorically invalid in the agreement's context, including its continuing factual-innocence disclosure promise. Actual competence, knowledge of the offense, voluntary choice, counsel duties and distinct deception or suppression claims retain their own requirements. Section 3742 permits examination of the connected constitutional allegation to determine jurisdiction; its failure leaves no review of the understood discretionary refusal on this theory. No universal waiver rule or independent sentencing change results.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** He agrees that the asserted statutory basis may be determined and votes to reverse the sentence vacatur. Brady protects against an unfair trial; that guarantee does not itself create the claimed bargaining-stage disclosure duty, regardless of how useful the information might be to a defendant. He therefore reaches the result for both impeachment and affirmative-defense information without joining the Court's reliance on predicted degree of assistance and practical balancing. His objection is not that impeachment is unimportant, but that usefulness supplies neither the source nor the boundary of this fair-trial guarantee. He also rejects any enforceable promise from the unaccepted offer. His position does not count as a join in Parts II or III or an endorsement of every waiver.

**Chief Justice Stone-Zsela, dissenting except as to Part I's statutory inquiry.** He would affirm the sentence vacatur and limited remand because the blanket waiver is invalid to the extent it permits withholding known favorable information materially useful for investigation, counsel's advice or the plea decision. Before obtaining a binding plea, the prosecution must provide such information timely enough for meaningful use, including significant impeachment and affirmative-defense evidence. The obligation reaches case-team information already known after reasonable inquiry, not new evidence the Government must create or complete trial discovery. Genuine privilege and safety concerns call for protective arrangements, sequencing and judicial review compatible with an informed choice.

Credibility weaknesses in a central witness or a supported affirmative defense may change competent advice without conclusively proving factual innocence. The continuing innocence promise therefore does not answer the entire problem. Kyles's preparation and team-responsibility principles support meaningful use; the trial setting does not make the same information irrelevant when the defendant must decide whether to surrender trial. Mezzanatto remains intact because the defendant there knowingly accepted a defined use of his own inconsistent account. A blanket prospective surrender of unknown information needed to assess the very plea choice is different. He rejects neither all limited informed waivers nor bargaining pressure categorically.

The withdrawal of the recommendation after Ruiz refused the condition connects the asserted legal injury to her sentence under section 3742. He would require the sentencing court to address the lost fast-track recommendation on the corrected legal basis and consider lawful relief without treating refusal of the invalid waiver as a permissible reason to deny consideration. The prospective duty and relief from a completed plea or sentence require distinct inquiries; retrospective relief still requires its own causal and materiality showing. He finds no particular suppressed item or involuntary plea and would leave the conviction undisturbed. His remand would not guarantee a two-level reduction, compel a plea agreement, permit plea withdrawal or award damages.

The Ninth Circuit must implement the reversal of its sentence vacatur and condition-based remedial direction. The presented constitutional theory supplies no further sentencing proceedings. Ruiz's conviction and the district sentence remain undisturbed by this challenge.

### Board of Education of Independent School District No. 92 of Pottawatomie County v. Earls, No. 01-332

Merits decision, June 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 19, 2002. On certiorari to the Tenth Circuit, which reversed summary judgment for the school district and rejected suspicionless urinalysis of the challenged nonathletic competitive extracurricular participants. Earls has standing from her participation; James's separate academic-eligibility standing question need not be decided. The students seek declaratory and prospective relief, and the district seeks reinstatement of its judgment.

Tecumseh adopted the policy in fall 1998. Its implemented population consists of competitive activities sanctioned by the state activities association, including academic teams, band, choir, agricultural and homemaking organizations, and athletics. The granted challenge concerns the nonathletic expansion. Initial and random testing supplement a separate reasonable-suspicion provision. A monitor stands outside the closed stall and can hear production. Written restrictions confine access and use of results, prohibit police use and ordinary academic discipline, and impose counseling and participation consequences. Supported evidence disputes aspects of handling prescription information and results; no finding establishes that another student actually read the prescription information.

After a first positive, participation may continue with proof of counseling within five days of the parental meeting and another test two weeks later. A second positive produces fourteen days' exclusion, four hours of counseling and monthly testing. A third produces exclusion for the rest of the school year or eighty-eight school days, whichever is longer. Return for declaratory and appropriate prospective relief against the challenged expansion.

#### Holdings

##### I. School responsibility informs reasonableness without erasing the search

**Holding and operative rule:** Compelled urine collection and analysis are searches, and extracurricular participation does not waive Fourth Amendment protection. A claimed protective need beyond ordinary law enforcement must justify the actual program through a contextual assessment of the population, school responsibilities, concrete need and fit, bodily and informational intrusion, and operational safeguards; catastrophe, a numerical epidemic, safety-sensitive duties and exhaustion of alternatives are not universal prerequisites.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part I of Ginsburg's opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** Vernonia preserves students' protection while sustaining its particular athlete program on combined circumstances. T.L.O. recognizes school supervision without dispensing with reasonableness. Chandler requires actual need and operational fit, not a rigid checklist. The district's protective purpose and exclusion of police use distinguish the ordinary-crime program in Edmond and the coordinated prosecution operation in Ferguson, permitting the special-needs inquiry. They do not themselves answer it. A voluntary activity condition is part of the intrusion's setting, not consent to any search attached to that activity. The Court therefore declines both an automatic school-custody exception and a requirement that each club first develop a proven epidemic.

**Precedent treatment:** Vernonia's search/no-waiver rule is applied; its athlete holding remains contextual. Chandler's actual-need inquiry is applied. T.L.O., Edmond and Ferguson retain their distinct supervisory and enforcement boundaries.

##### II. This nonathletic expansion lacks sufficient contextual justification

**Holding and operative rule:** On this record, the district's general drug concern and the students' competitive academic or cultural participation do not sufficiently justify suspicionless bodily testing of the challenged nonathletic groups, even with the written collection and use protections. The judgment rejecting this expansion is affirmed; the rule does not bar a differently supported program or disturb the separately sustained athlete program.

**Authority:** Part II of Ginsburg's opinion is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor and Souter; five direct votes control.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the relation between the selected activities and the compelled specimen is inadequate. Vernonia's documented athlete-centered concern, athletic safety circumstances and communal privacy expectations were material to its balance. Band, choir and academic competition do not themselves reproduce them. The absence of those features is a contextual difference, not a mandatory athletic-risk checklist.

The district has a real responsibility to protect children and evidence of some drug use. Monitoring outside a closed stall, confined information access, nonpolice use and counseling-centered participation consequences reduce the burden. Those factors receive genuine weight. They do not explain why this nonathletic competitive population should be selected for suspicionless bodily searches merely because the same general drug concern affects all students. General prevention cannot make every educational activity a sufficient testing category.

The opposing position properly emphasizes prevention before a crisis and the limited consequences of refusal. Those principles reject a categorical epidemic requirement, but do not supply the missing relation in this record. The Court applies Vernonia's controlling balance, not a former dissent's alternative necessity rule. An affirmance on this independently sufficient substantive ground permits prospective relief without resolving every implementation allegation.

**Precedent treatment:** Vernonia's athlete application is distinguished while its balancing method is applied. Chandler reinforces the need for actual operational fit without prescribing a least-intrusive-alternative rule.

##### III. Written safeguards receive weight without becoming findings of compliance

**Holding and operative rule:** Written confidentiality and use restrictions reduce the asserted intrusion, but supported contrary implementation evidence cannot be resolved for the district merely by assuming perfect compliance at summary judgment. Here the program's insufficient justification independently supports declaratory and appropriate prospective relief even on the district's stronger account of its safeguards, so no independent disclosure claim or automatic trial is created.

**Authority:** Part III of Ginsburg's opinion has the same five direct joins as Part II. The opposing four agree that material implementation disputes require proper evidentiary treatment but seek a different disposition.

**Controlling explanation:** Urinalysis can reveal prescription and other medical information as well as illicit-drug results. Access and use therefore matter to the search burden. Vernonia's separate facial prescription ruling allowed consideration of a textually possible confidential method; it did not establish actual confidential practice or authorize intrusive implementation. Ordinary summary-judgment rules govern the evidentiary support and materiality of the present allegations. The Court finds no actual disclosure proved and does not turn an administrative lapse into a freestanding constitutional claim.

Because Part II's conclusion remains the same even crediting the written protection, the disputed operational account need not delay this judgment. Appropriate prospective relief addresses only the challenged nonathletic expansion. Any independently pleaded disclosure claim requires its own preserved source, elements and remedy. Neither that claim nor a replacement testing program is ordered here.

**Precedent treatment:** Vernonia's facial/actual distinction is applied without converting a possible confidential construction into a finding. Ordinary Rule 56 standards retain their separate factual function.

### Hope v. Pelzer, No. 01-309

Merits decision, June 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued April 17, 2002. On certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, which affirmed summary judgment for three prison officers on qualified-immunity grounds while accepting that the alleged punitive restraint violated the Eighth Amendment. The granted issue concerns its materially-similar-precedent requirement and fair warning; ultimate individual responsibility was not taken for review. Hope seeks reversal and continuation of his action under 42 U.S.C. §1983. The officers seek preservation of their personal-immunity judgment.

The favorable summary-judgment account concerns May 11 and June 7, 1995. In May, Hope was attached for two hours after an altercation; the raised position caused pain and wrist discomfort. Water and bathroom breaks were offered every fifteen minutes and recorded. McClaran allegedly ordered attachment, and Pelzer and Gates allegedly attached him. The June account describes approximately seven hours after Hope had been subdued, with scant water, no bathroom break and other aggravations. Gates is the only remaining respondent connected to June, and the alleged act attributed to him is attachment; the account does not itself allege his supervision of the seven hours, removal of clothing, denial of water or taunting. The attribution and even identity of the persons involved remain disputed. These are legal-review assumptions and alleged acts, not liability findings.

The governing assumption for each occasion is extended, painful attachment without continuing danger or work refusal. June's greater severity is distinct from that common premise and is not attributed to May-only officers. The constitutional characterization is accepted for this granted immunity inquiry.

All nine Justices participated at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**The similarity-based qualified-immunity judgment is reversed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia, Thomas.

Ordinary further proceedings concerning actual individual responsibility and independent defenses, under the resolved notice rule.

The six Associate Justices supporting reversal enter that decree with ordinary further proceedings preserved. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela also expressly directs remand for the remaining responsibility and factual issues. Scalia and Thomas would affirm personal immunity on the conduct attributed to these respondents. #### Holdings

##### I. The accepted constitutional premise remains individual and conduct-specific

**Holding and operative rule:** The granted immunity review takes the supported, plaintiff-favorable premise that each occasion involved knowingly imposed painful restraint continued after a security need and any work refusal ended, as gratuitous punishment. That conduct violates the existing Eighth Amendment rule, but personal liability requires each defendant's own supported participation, actual culpable awareness and causal contribution; a reasonable response to a known risk remains protected under Farmer.

**Authority:** Part I of Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Ginsburg and Breyer. The same seven directly join each following holding; neither dissenter joins the opinion.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court applies the accepted constitutional premise rather than deciding an ungranted challenge to it. Rhodes and Whitley distinguish justified control from unnecessary punitive suffering. Farmer requires actual appreciation of a substantial danger and unreasonable disregard; obviousness permits an inference of awareness but does not compel it or aggregate knowledge across guards. Hudson's established prohibition of nontrivial malicious beating does not become a universal rule for every restraint or eliminate the conditions inquiry.

May's regular offers of necessities mitigate the conditions but do not justify the assumed two hours of painful punishment after necessity ceased. June's aggravated conditions strengthen its account without supplying missing acts by McClaran, Pelzer or Gates. The dissent's personal-role objection matters to ultimate responsibility. It cannot change the granted legal assumption into mere initial attachment for a continuing lawful purpose. The Court leaves factual sufficiency and actual attribution to ordinary proceedings because they fall outside the accepted question.

**Precedent treatment:** Farmer's actual-knowledge and reasonable-response requirements are applied. Hudson retains its limited force holding. Rhodes and Whitley supply the distinction between necessary control and gratuitous punishment; Delaney's assumed-fact method supplies no missing allegations or 1995 notice.

##### II. Fair warning can be clear without a previously litigated replica

**Holding and operative rule:** An officer has qualified immunity unless the law when he acted made the unlawfulness of his actual conduct apparent to a reasonable official. An established rule can give that warning through an obvious application without a materially identical prior case; context, contrary authority and judicial hierarchy matter, and an earlier express reservation of the particular application may require greater factual specificity.

**Authority:** Part II has the same seven direct joins. Scalia and Thomas's agreement with the possible obvious-violation route does not join the Court's reading or application of the appellate judgment.

**Controlling explanation:** Harlow supplies an objective personal defense, and Anderson requires particularized clarity while rejecting immunity merely because the precise act has not been litigated. Lanier explains why civil clearly-established law and criminal fair warning share a notice inquiry. General moral disapproval alone is insufficient, but a settled prohibition can plainly cover new apparatus. A more particular precedent is especially important where earlier law expressly left this application open. Lanier's criminal willfulness element does not become a civil element.

The reviewed judgment rejected Gates and Ort as insufficiently similar despite the accepted after-necessity punishment. The dissent reads circuit law as already allowing obvious applications. That general allowance does not cure this judgment's failure to apply the governing warning to its accepted facts. The inquiry asks what preconduct law communicated, not whether an official actually consulted a favorable precedent. Wilson retains context-sensitive immunity; Saucier establishes no replacement defense or compulsory merits-first sequence. Lanier, Wilson and later decisions explain the method without creating notice retroactively. State policy and administrative warnings have contextual roles, not independent authority to create or erase the constitutional right.

**Precedent treatment:** Harlow and Anderson are retained; Lanier's warning method is applied without importing criminal willfulness. Wilson, Denius and Saucier preserve objective conduct-date inquiry. A categorical factual-twin prerequisite is rejected.

##### III. Pre-1995 law warned against the assumed gratuitous restraint on both occasions

**Holding and operative rule:** By May 11 and June 7, 1995, Gates v. Collier and Ort v. White gave fair warning that knowingly prolonging painful restraint as punishment after the prisoner was controlled and no work refusal continued violated the Eighth Amendment. The assumed conduct on each occasion therefore cannot receive qualified immunity merely because no earlier case involved this precise device, duration or combination of conditions.

**Authority:** Part III has the same seven direct joins, covering both May and June at the stated legal assumption. It establishes no collective or final personal liability.

**Controlling explanation:** Gates condemned prolonged handcuffing and awkward confinement as corporal punishment. Its place in the binding circuit line, through Bonner, matters more than the name of the apparatus. Ort upheld necessary coercion during work refusal while explaining the different constitutional position of later punishment and measures severe enough to endanger health. That distinction directly addresses the assumed end of necessity here.

Lane, Whitson and the other preconduct district decisions are the strongest contrary evidence. Their accounts involved continued refusal, opportunities to resume work, necessities or shorter duration. May shared some mitigating conditions but, on the accepted premise, lacked the continuing justification. Such lower-court decisions cannot override the superior circuit warning against this gratuitous punishment. The contrary interpretation does not establish immunity by treating the assumed prolonged punitive act as only lawful initial attachment.

The state regulation authorized restraints for refusal or disruption and prescribed return to work when an inmate was ready, quarter-hour necessities and a log. Those safeguards reinforce the coercion/punishment distinction; compliance with some safeguards is not a license for knowingly needless pain, and breach alone is not a federal violation. The preconduct federal report corroborates the warning without proving receipt by any respondent. Later district approval cannot retroactively erase the earlier rule. Each officer's actual knowledge and causal role remain necessary under Part IV.

**Precedent treatment:** Gates and Ort are applied at their specific punishment/coercion boundary; Bonner supplies circuit hierarchy. The district cases are distinguished, not treated as nonexistent. Later cases and the administrative report are not substitutes for preconduct constitutional authority.

##### IV. Reversal resolves notice while preserving the ungranted responsibility issues

**Holding and operative rule:** The reviewed similarity-based immunity judgment is reversed because the legal warning was sufficient on the accepted gratuitous-punishment account of both occasions. Ordinary further proceedings determine each respondent's supported acts, knowledge, causation and independent defenses; the June aggravations may not be attributed to May-only defendants or to Gates without a sufficient personal basis.

**Authority:** Part IV has the same seven direct joins in reversal and ordinary further proceedings. Stone-Zsela separately calls for an express remand; the six Associate reversal votes do not depend on that additional formal direction.

**Controlling explanation:** The grant reaches the legal notice issue, not final sufficiency of the pleadings and evidence concerning each named officer. Deciding notice on the court below's accepted premise therefore neither decides the factual dispute nor requires an open-ended repetition of the legal warning inquiry. The dissent would regard the limited attributed acts as enough to affirm immunity now. That inquiry exceeds the issue accepted for review; it remains available in the appropriate proceedings on an actual individual record.

The lower court must separate McClaran's alleged May order, Pelzer's alleged May attachment, and Gates's alleged attachments on the two occasions. It must apply Farmer's personal awareness and reasonable-response limits, ordinary causation requirements and lawful independent defenses. Later supported facts may materially differ from the assumed punitive conduct. This disposition preserves that work rather than ordering collective liability. The withdrawn claims against absent guards remain withdrawn.

**Precedent treatment:** Delaney confirms the distinction between a resolved legal issue and actual responsibility, without making missing allegations into a genuine factual dispute. Farmer and the ordinary summary-judgment rules govern the remaining individual inquiry.

### Missouri Republican Party v. Lamb, on review of Eighth Circuit Nos. 00-1773 and 00-2686

Merits decision, June 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of Eighth Circuit Nos. 00-1773 and 00-2686, 270 F.3d 567, on the full merits record. The Court reviews the November 2, 2001 judgment sustaining Missouri's challenged party-to-candidate contribution restrictions after reconsideration in light of FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee. The presented question concerns reliance on federal circumvention evidence despite the absence of comparable completed Missouri practices and the existence of earmarking controls, together with the justification for the actual party ceilings. The party and candidate plaintiffs seek relief from the restrictions; state officials seek to sustain them.

The restrictions concern actual money and in-kind candidate resources under Mo. Rev. Stat. §130.032.4 and .7, with contribution ceilings and enforcement consequences. Missouri also attributes earmarked contributions to their original source. The federal evidence describes tacit donor-party-candidate understandings, fundraising credits and allocation arrangements that can convey candidate benefit without an express earmark. The Eighth Circuit treated Colorado II as establishing validity as a matter of law and sustained the materially lower Missouri ceilings without a showing that association had become ineffective, also pointing to independent spending opportunities. Those are the challenged legal premises. The separate reliance contention and unpresented discrimination claim are outside this review.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**The judgment sustaining the challenged party-to-candidate contribution limits is vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

Determine the actual limits' close fit and corresponding relief through findings or record-based conclusions under the government's justification burden.

Relevant federal evidence already establishes a sufficient preventive interest concerning this intermediary mechanism. The remand concerns fit of the actual Missouri ceilings and resulting relief; it does not require a new local scandal, study or trial. Adequate existing evidence may support a properly explained decision. The judgment supplies no replacement dollar amount and does not invalidate every contribution restriction or decide retrospective liability.

#### Opinion Topology

STONE-ZSELA, C. J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. KENNEDY, J., filed a concurring opinion, in which SCALIA and THOMAS, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### I. Actual candidate resources remain distinct from independent and joint expression

**Holding and operative rule:** A State may regulate actual party-to-candidate resource transfers and genuine contribution conduits under the contribution standard: government must establish an objectively grounded, sufficiently important corruption or circumvention interest and a restriction closely drawn to it while preserving meaningful association and effective advocacy. Party identity, shared objectives, endorsement or ordinary access alone does not establish an actual candidate-resource connection or corruption; genuinely independent expression remains protected.

**Authority:** Stone-Zsela's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by all eight Associate Justices. All nine directly adopt each holding at its stated actual-resource scope. Kennedy, Scalia and Thomas separately preserve the distinct joint-expression question.

**Controlling explanation:** Buckley distinguishes financial support delivered to candidates from independent advocacy. Colorado I rejects presumed coordination based solely on party status and protects genuinely independent party expression. Colorado II permits justified regulation of an actually demonstrated circumvention channel, while leaving distinct expressive applications to their own characterization.

This grant concerns real money and in-kind resources conveyed to candidates. It does not require treating every jointly developed party message as candidate-controlled funds. The party has no categorical exemption for actual contributions merely because association with candidates is central to its purpose. Conversely, the State cannot relabel independent advocacy as a contribution from shared ideology alone. The common resource rule therefore resolves the presented category without erasing the expression distinction that the concurrence preserves.

**Precedent treatment:** Buckley's functional distinction and Colorado I's independent-expression rule are applied. Colorado II supplies no universal classification of every coordinated message or automatic validity of every party ceiling.

##### II. The federal record establishes a relevant preventive interest beyond formal earmarking

**Holding and operative rule:** Relevant experience outside the State may establish an objective preventive justification without a completed local scandal, mandatory new local study or proof that legislators subjectively relied on that evidence when acting. Here the federal record establishes a materially relevant risk that tacit donor-party-candidate understandings and fundraising credits can circumvent valid candidate limits despite formal earmarking attribution; that resolved interest does not by itself establish close fit of Missouri's actual ceilings.

**Authority:** Part II has all nine direct joins at the genuine-resource and conduit scope. It resolves the relevance and preventive-interest issue rather than postponing it.

**Controlling explanation:** Colorado II's record describes the intermediary mechanism Missouri regulates. Donors can support a party on an understanding that favored candidates will benefit, and candidate fundraising can earn later party support from a general treasury. An express source attribution rule need not detect those tacit arrangements. The State may prevent the demonstrated channel before a completed local evasion or bribe occurs.

The party's demand for exclusively Missouri experience would discard relevant evidence of the same mechanism. Objective justification does not turn on whether the legislature had that later-developed evidence in mind. The lower court's borrowed-evidence analogy is sound to that limited extent. It does not import a different First Amendment category's entire test or permit an unsupported corruption label.

Ordinary political agreement, access and a tally alone do not prove an unlawful transaction. Nor does this evidence show that every contribution is corrupt. It establishes the practical preventive interest; the chosen restriction must still satisfy the separate burden in Part III. Accordingly, the remand is not an invitation to require a new scandal or to repeat the already answered question whether federal experience can matter.

**Precedent treatment:** Colorado II's demonstrated informal-circumvention reasoning is applied. Renton's borrowed-evidence analogy does not displace the contribution-specific burden. Formal earmarking retains its own attribution function without becoming an exclusive remedy.

##### III. Missouri must justify its actual ceilings while preserving meaningful association

**Holding and operative rule:** Missouri bears the separate burden to connect the supported circumvention risk to the actual operation and amounts of its challenged party ceilings, showing close fit that preserves meaningful chosen-party association and effective candidate-connected support. A federal ceiling's validity, unlimited independent spending or challengers' failure to prove that expression becomes practically inaudible does not discharge that burden; no fixed dollar floor or universal numerical safe harbor governs.

**Authority:** Part III has all nine direct joins. The three concurring Justices accept this rule for actual transfers without accepting a lower-protection rule for every joint message.

**Controlling explanation:** Russell places the justification burden on government and evaluates actual recipient functions and campaign capacity. Shrink sustains its particular statewide individual ceiling on a supported record, not a presumption that every amount is valid until political speech becomes impossible. Day protects the chosen association and rejects reliance on other available speakers as a sufficient answer to its burden.

Colorado II similarly weighed association costs and the substantial candidate-connected activity still allowed by the federal Senate ceiling. Its federal facial disposition cannot establish the fit of materially lower state ceilings as a matter of law. The inquiry must relate Missouri's actual operation and scale to the mechanism, practical fundraising burdens and capacity for meaningful support.

Independent spending preserves an important freedom but may entail duplication, expense and lost communication; it does not replace the party's chosen candidate-connected channel. The strongest affirmance argument—the repeatability of circumvention—establishes a real interest, not the adequacy of every amount chosen to address it. Conversely, a lower number alone does not establish invalidity. Findings or record-based conclusions must perform the actual fit inquiry, using sufficient existing evidence where available.

**Precedent treatment:** Russell, Shrink and Day are applied to their government-burden and meaningful-association rules. Colorado II is applied at its limited federal record and amount; it supplies no statewide safe harbor or challenger-impossibility threshold.

##### IV. Vacatur requires a fit decision and corresponding relief within the presented scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The reviewed judgment cannot stand on automatic validation by Colorado II or a burden on challengers to prove practically ineffective expression. It is vacated and the case remanded for findings or record-based conclusions connecting the already supported mechanism to the actual Missouri ceilings, followed by appropriate relief for any limits the State cannot justify; adequate existing evidence may suffice without a new study or compulsory trial.

**Authority:** Part IV and the formal vacatur/remand have nine direct votes.

**Controlling explanation:** The lower court answered a genuine evidentiary objection but treated that answer as conclusive of constitutionality. Because interest and fit do separate work, the Court cannot preserve the judgment on that rationale or declare the ceilings invalid solely from their relative amounts. The proper proceedings apply Parts I–III to the actual limits and record, then determine the relief those conclusions support.

Dollar adequacy remains an application task under settled law. Any severability question requires its proper scope and record. The separate prior-restraint or reliance contention lies outside the grant, and the unpresented discrimination claim is not adjudicated. The Court therefore directs neither blanket invalidation, a replacement amount nor retrospective liability, and it does not require additional fact development where the existing record permits the necessary conclusions.

**Precedent treatment:** Colorado II's limited remedy is distinguished from automatic approval of a different State's ceilings. Russell and Shrink retain record-specific application and remedial boundaries.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Buckley v. Valeo: applies the functional distinction between contributions and independent expenditures; legitimate corruption prevention does not dispense with fit.
- Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee v. FEC, June 26, 1996: preserves genuinely independent party expression and the need for an actual candidate connection; party identity alone is insufficient.
- FEC v. Colorado Republican Federal Campaign Committee, June 25, 2001: applies demonstrated informal circumvention and close-fit review; its federal Senate facial judgment establishes neither validity of Missouri's actual ceilings nor a universal classification of joint expression.
- Russell v. Burris: applies government's objective justification burden, actual operation and meaningful-association standard, without converting its office-specific outcomes into a dollar floor.
- Nixon v. Shrink Missouri Government PAC: retains its record-specific statewide individual ceiling; it supplies no party-ceiling safe harbor or requirement that challengers prove political expression impossible.
- Day v. Holahan: protects meaningful chosen-intermediary association while preserving realistic conduit regulation; other channels alone do not cure the burden.
- Daggett v. Commission on Governmental Ethics & Election Practices: retains the affirmative contribution-justification burden; its other holdings do not decide this ceiling.
- Mariani v. United States: retains the distinction between direct candidate resources and materially different financing arrangements; its other holdings do not decide this ceiling.
- City of Renton v. Playtime Theatres: its borrowed-evidence analogy supports considering relevant outside experience, without replacing the contribution-specific constitutional inquiry.

#### Law After Decision

A State may rely on relevant federal experience to prevent demonstrated tacit circumvention of lawful candidate limits without awaiting a local scandal. For these actual party-resource transfers, that interest is established; Missouri must separately justify the operation and amounts of its ceilings under close-fit review preserving meaningful association. Existing evidence may suffice. The decision removes automatic validity and challenger-impossibility premises while preserving independent expression and the distinct joint-message question.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring, joined by Scalia and Thomas.** They join the complete opinion and vacatur/remand because the presented restrictions concern actual money, candidate-controlled financial substitutes and genuine conduits. Their Colorado II dissent accepted regulation of those transfers and the relevance of donor-understanding and fundraising-credit evidence. The absence of a completed bribe or express earmark is not conclusive against prevention.

They retain their separate protection for the party's own electoral message, including a jointly developed communication. Consultation can encompass expression beyond paying a candidate's bills or transferring financial control. Party-candidate collaboration serves the party's political purpose, and compelled separation can impose duplication, inefficiency and lost coherence. Unlimited independent speech therefore cannot itself justify a restriction on that chosen joint channel.

Allocation can also reflect electoral prospects and party priorities; a tally is not necessarily an illegal earmark. Source rules, disclosure and tailored conduit enforcement remain relevant alternatives when evaluating a burden on expression. Their prior objection concerned treating the entire coordination category as candidate-controlled support without an adequate operational answer. Today's bounded transfer rule makes no such classification and does not require them to abandon it. They neither demand strict scrutiny for every contribution nor immunize direct resources, undifferentiated transfers or sham conduits. Missouri must justify the actual ceilings under the Court's rule; the remand does not compel automatic invalidity or a new local study.

The case returns for a decision on the actual challenged ceilings’ close fit and the resulting appropriate relief under the government’s burden. The relevance of the federal mechanism and sufficient preventive interest are settled. The court may use an adequate existing record and must state findings or record-based conclusions linking risk, operation and amounts. ### People Who Care v. Rockford Board of Education, School District No. 205, on review of Seventh Circuit No. 00-3200

Merits decision, June 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of Seventh Circuit No. 00-3200, 246 F.3d 1073. The Seventh Circuit's April 18, 2001 judgment ordered dissolution of the desegregation decree effective June 30, 2002. The board seeks release; the student plaintiffs seek continued protection against consequences of the adjudicated discrimination. The question is whether the district established the complete release and causal showing and whether the appellate court properly reviewed the retained obligations and findings.

The unappealed 1994 intentional-discrimination determination led to the remedial decree. The district achieved substantial school-assignment integration and undertook extensive expenditures and programs; completion of the remaining educational work, good faith and the causes of achievement and advanced-course disparities remained contested. The appellate court treated assignment integration, expenditure and the plaintiffs' failure to partition residual causes as grounds for termination. Settled liability and previously adjudicated remedy provisions are outside this review. This case concerns release from the existing decree, not university admissions or a general school-finance adequacy right.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**The direction to dissolve the existing decree effective June 30, 2002, is vacated and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The displaced direction supplies no dissolution. The lower courts must apply the complete release rule, preserve settled findings absent clear error, and define any released duties and remaining obligations and supervision. They may order supported full or partial release; no automatic additional supervision period follows.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### I. The complete release showing governs every obligation proposed for termination

**Holding and operative rule:** A school district seeking release from an adjudicated desegregation obligation must establish full and satisfactory compliance, elimination of the relevant vestiges to the extent practicable, and demonstrated good faith toward the whole decree. A court may release a completed domain while retaining supervision elsewhere only after separately assessing whether continued control in that domain is necessary or practicable to achieve compliance elsewhere; release may not obstruct the remaining remedy, and satisfaction of the prerequisites permits, rather than compels, partial withdrawal.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court; all nine Justices directly join the rule, its qualifications and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that release turns on completion of the constitutional remedy. Freeman permits domain-specific withdrawal but requires the district's full showing, attention to whole-decree conduct and an examination of remedial connections. Jenkins applies that rule and distinguishes installing or funding programs from eliminating the injury those programs address. Dowell requires restoration of local control when the required work is complete; it does not substitute age or expense for completion.

Rockford's demonstrated progress in school assignments is substantial evidence for the domains it addresses. It does not alone determine whether retained educational measures still repair consequences of the adjudicated violation. Conversely, a disparity does not itself establish an unfinished constitutional duty. National achievement parity, maximum educational potential and every desirable program goal are not conditions of release. Courts must identify the remaining violation-linked work rather than supervise schools for general improvement.

Good faith is shown by conduct toward the whole decree, not compulsory enthusiasm. Elected officials may criticize the decree; criticism alone neither establishes noncompliance nor excuses it. Completed work and unfinished duties must be evaluated together where their operation connects. The court must decide whether control over a completed domain remains necessary or practicable for compliance elsewhere, assess nonobstruction independently, and define released duties and retained supervision. Rufo's modification rule does not replace this completion inquiry or add a changed-circumstances condition to release from fulfilled obligations.

##### II. The district bears the causal showing, with ordinary protection for settled findings

**Holding and operative rule:** The district must show that remaining imbalance or educational conditions for which it disclaims remedial responsibility are not traceable to its adjudicated violation; the students need not establish that violation anew or supply a particular comparative study to preserve the existing burden. Existing findings receive clear-error review, but findings about one domain do not decide unexamined causal and remedial connections in another.

**Authority:** Part II; all nine Justices directly join the causal allocation and review standard.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the request for release carries the causal burden established in Freeman and reaffirmed in Jenkins. Genuinely independent causes do not impose perpetual racial balancing or make the district responsible for every social inequality. But the appellate demand that the plaintiffs partition all remaining achievement differences cannot replace the district's showing concerning the consequences of its own adjudicated discrimination.

The lower court must weigh the actual record. Testimony, records, program operation and sound inferences may establish the relevant causes; a new statistical comparison is neither invariably necessary nor invariably sufficient. Substantial assignment integration and open advanced-course enrollment deserve their supported weight, without automatically resolving every retained educational obligation. Neither general social causes nor a reported absence of newly identified decree violations alone completes the showing.

Freeman protects properly settled findings against appellate replacement absent clear error. It also requires resolution of causal and remedial questions those findings did not answer. The remand applies both requirements. It is not a general retrial of the 1994 liability determination or an instruction to substitute new appellate factual conclusions for supported findings.

##### III. The June 30 termination direction must be vacated

**Holding and operative rule:** The categorical direction to terminate the decree effective June 30, 2002 is vacated because the appellate reasoning does not establish the complete release and causal showing. On remand the lower courts must apply Parts I and II, identify any supported full or partial release and define the obligations and supervision that remain justified.

**Authority:** Part III; all nine Justices join the disposition and remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the termination command rests on an incomplete legal inquiry. Neither duration and expense nor successful assignment integration permits the district's burden to be replaced by a demand for new proof from the students. The displaced June 30 direction supplies no dissolution of the existing decree. The lower courts must make a timely, supported determination of what constitutional work remains and end supervision where that work is complete. The remand imposes no automatic additional supervision period and presumes neither that every retained measure remains necessary nor that every obligation has been fulfilled.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Freeman v. Pitts, March 31, 1992: applied in full to permissive partial withdrawal, the district's release and causal burdens, whole-decree good faith, retained-control necessity or practicability, nonobstruction and clear-error review.
- Missouri v. Jenkins, June 12, 1995: applied to distinguish completed violation-linked repair from program installation and spending; its complete Freeman rule governs release.
- Green v. School Board of New Kent County, 391 U.S. 430: the affirmative duty to repair the adjudicated violation continues across unfinished obligations.
- Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg Board of Education, 402 U.S. 1: effective equitable means remain bounded by the violation and the work needed to repair it.
- Board of Education v. Dowell, 498 U.S. 237: temporary supervision ends when the requisite good faith and practicable elimination are established; age and expense do not replace that showing.
- Rufo v. Inmates of Suffolk County Jail, January 15, 1992: its distinct institutional-decree modification rule supplies no replacement for completion-based release or added changed-circumstances requirement.
- Ruiz v. United States (Ruiz v. Johnson), March 4, 2002: its PLRA statutory termination inquiry remains distinct from the Freeman/Jenkins school-decree standard.

#### Law After Decision

The district bears the complete showing for release, including the causal showing for conditions it disclaims. Completed assignment work is relevant without deciding every remaining educational obligation. Partial release remains discretionary, with a separate inquiry into retained-control necessity or practicability and nonobstruction. The Court changes no governing test; the lower courts must apply the existing rule to the actual retained duties and supported findings.

#### Source Notes

People Who Care v. Rockford Board of Education, 246 F.3d 1073 (7th Cir. 2001); [official court opinion](https://media.ca7.uscourts.gov/cgi-bin/OpinionsWeb/processWebInputExternal.pl?Submit=Display&Path=Y2001/D04-18/C:00-3200:J:_:aut:T:op:N:0:S:0). The appellate opinion reports counsel's inability at argument to identify a decree violation; that report does not itself establish the complete district release showing. Freeman v. Pitts, 503 U.S. 467; Missouri v. Jenkins, 515 U.S. 70; Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 60(b)(5).

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns through the Seventh Circuit for application of the complete release and causal standard. The existing decree is not dissolved by the vacated June 30 direction. The receiving courts must make a timely supported determination of completed and unfinished constitutional work, retaining only justified supervision and ending it where the required work is complete. Supreme Court review is concluded subject to ordinary mandate administration.

### Republican Party of Minnesota v. White, No. 01-521

Merits decision, June 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 26, 2002. On review of the Eighth Circuit's judgment, 247 F.3d 854, affirming summary judgment for the officials enforcing Minnesota's judicial announce clause. The candidate and party plaintiffs seek declaratory and injunctive relief against the continuing restriction; the officials defend it as necessary to impartial adjudication and its appearance. The question is whether that content-based restriction satisfies the First Amendment.

Canon 5A(3)(d)(i) prohibited a candidate for judicial office, including an incumbent, from announcing views on disputed legal or political issues. Adopted constructions permitted discussion of past decisions, general case law and judicial philosophy and confined the prohibition to issues likely to arise before the candidate's court. The Court reads the clause still to reach nonpromissory statements of current legal positions; the dissent disputes that reading and the weight given counsel's oral-argument answers. This is a disagreement about legal construction, not a shared stipulation that the clause reaches only literal promises.

Wersal sought to discuss such issues during a judicial campaign. His earlier disciplinary complaint was dismissed; no completed sanction under this clause is at issue. The continuing constraint and intended campaign speech present the prospective controversy. A distinct pledges-or-promises provision is outside this challenge.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**The judgment sustaining the judicial announce clause is reversed and remanded for declaratory and appropriate prospective relief, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer.

Relief must protect the plaintiffs' discussion of disputed legal and political issues against enforcement of this clause. The receiving court must confine the decree to that restriction; the separate pledges-or-promises provision and unreviewed ethics rules retain their distinct legal inquiries.

#### Opinion Topology

SCALIA, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, and THOMAS, JJ., joined. O'CONNOR and KENNEDY, JJ., filed concurring opinions. STEVENS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. GINSBURG, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which STEVENS, SOUTER, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Holdings

##### I. The actual announce clause fails strict scrutiny

**Holding and operative rule:** Minnesota's content-based prohibition of judicial candidates' views on disputed legal or political issues violates the First Amendment because the State has not shown that its actual restriction is narrowly tailored to a compelling interest in impartial adjudication or its appearance. A legal view does not, without more, establish litigant bias, a refusal to consider law and evidence or a promise of a particular result; the clause remains too broad in the speech it reaches and insufficiently fitted in the comparable speech it leaves unrestricted.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, joined by Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas; five direct joins in the complete sufficient rule.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that judicial office creates no exemption from demanding review of a direct content-based restriction on candidate expression. Suster rejected such an exemption for campaign expenditures while independently examining impartiality. Kruse rejected justifying a direct restraint by the speech left available. Eu supplies the demanding protection for political expression. This different restriction requires its own strict-scrutiny inquiry.

The State's three meanings of impartiality must remain distinct. Absence of bias for or against a litigant is a compelling concern, but the clause regulates broad legal views rather than targeting party bias. Some prohibited statements can disclose bias against a class of litigants; that possibility does not make the whole prohibition narrowly tailored. An absence of all legal preconceptions is not a compelling interest: knowledgeable judges necessarily have views, and those views do not invariably disable fair judgment. Laird preserves that distinction.

Even assuming willingness to reconsider prior views and its appearance are compelling interests, the clause fails fit. Its campaign-focused coverage leaves analogous pre-candidacy statements and permitted judicial teaching, writing and speaking outside the prohibition. Minnesota has not established that nonpromissory campaign views uniquely produce closed-minded adjudication. Seeking electoral support can create pressure, as the dissent argues, but that concern does not establish that every prohibited view functions as a binding commitment. Voters have a substantial interest in knowing candidates' views, while the judge's duty remains responsiveness to governing law and the particular case.

The adopted construction permits some general discussion yet still bars current positions without a promise. The Court does not resolve that breadth by inventing a narrower state canon. Liteky concerns actual federal recusal under its objective appearance standard, not a categorical ban on campaign views. Distinct disqualification and conduct rules retain their own inquiry. Judicial and legislative elections need not be regulated identically, and no candidate is required to announce a view.

##### II. Prospective relief addresses this clause alone

**Holding and operative rule:** The judgment sustaining the announce clause is reversed, and the case is remanded for declaratory and appropriate prospective injunctive relief protecting the plaintiffs' discussion of disputed legal and political issues against enforcement of that clause. The distinct pledges-or-promises provision and other unreviewed ethics restrictions are not adjudicated by this judgment.

**Authority:** Part II; the same five Justices join the disposition and the scope of prospective relief.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the continuing restraint on the plaintiffs' intended speech presents the prospective controversy; completed discipline is unnecessary. The constitutional defect defeats the enforcing officials' summary judgment. The lower court must provide relief against this clause consistent with Part I, without drafting a replacement canon. Separate promises, campaign-finance, partisan and recusal rules lie outside the question decided. Whether every formulation of open-mindedness is compelling also need not be decided, because the actual restriction fails tailoring even on that assumption.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Suster v. Marshall, January 28, 1999: its rejection of a general judicial-office exemption from demanding speech review extends to this direct content restriction; its own limited preliminary remedy is not enlarged.
- City of Cincinnati v. Kruse, December 16, 1998: its rejection of sufficient remaining expression as an independent justification for a direct restraint is applied, while materially different ethics restrictions retain separate review.
- Eu v. San Francisco County Democratic Central Committee, 489 U.S. 214: demanding review of direct interference with political expression supplies the strict-scrutiny framework.
- Laird v. Tatum, 409 U.S. 824: the distinction between prior legal views and disqualifying bias informs the rejection of an interest in judges with no legal preconceptions.
- Liteky v. United States, March 7, 1994: its informed-observer appearance standard under the federal recusal statute remains independent; it neither equates every legal view with bias nor establishes the validity of a campaign ban.
- Watchtower Bible & Tract Society of New York, Inc. v. Village of Stratton, June 17, 2002: its actual licensing holding remains at that scope and supplies no general judicial-ethics exemption.
- BE&K Construction Co. v. NLRB, June 24, 2002: its statutory petitioning protection remains distinct from this direct candidate-speech inquiry.
- Trans Union Corp. v. FTC, June 24, 2002: its commercial-data confidentiality holding does not lower review for candidate speech or establish a universal rule concerning statutory exceptions.

#### Law After Decision

The announce clause cannot stand under strict scrutiny. Judicial candidates' legal views are protected without equating those views with promises, party bias or refusal to follow governing law. Impartiality and its appearance remain substantial judicial concerns requiring an actual fit between the harm and the restriction. The separate promises, recusal, campaign-finance and partisan rules are not resolved, and the Court does not decide whether every formulation of open-mindedness is compelling because the challenged means fail tailoring even on that assumption.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice O'Connor, concurring.** She joins the entire strict-scrutiny opinion and remedy. Judicial elections and fundraising can create serious actual and apparent bias pressures. Those risks do not establish that suppressing candidate information through this broad clause is a properly fitted response. Minnesota's choice to retain elections does not make all legal views disqualifying, nor does invalidation of this means hold judicial elections unconstitutional. The concurrence preserves the force of impartiality concerns while explaining why they do not save this restriction.

**Justice Kennedy, concurring.** He joins the sufficient strict-scrutiny opinion and remedy while favoring broader protection against content-based candidate-speech restrictions outside recognized First Amendment exceptions. Judicial integrity remains an interest of the highest order; rigorous ethics and recusal standards, including standards more protective than the constitutional minimum, can address actual adjudicative harms. Voters and public discussion can assess unsuitable statements. His distinct theory is not attributed to the controlling five, does not condemn elected judges as a class and does not resolve a general restriction on sitting judges' speech under a public-employment theory.

**Justice Stevens, dissenting, joined by Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.** They would affirm under strict scrutiny, distinguishing an appeal for votes based on future resolution of legal issues from scholarship or pre-candidacy views. Candidates ask an electorate to approve an announced position and may face later pressure to honor that electoral expectation despite opposing argument. The judicial role requires impartial application rather than representation of a constituency's preferred outcomes. Actual and apparent open-mindedness, including protection against bias toward classes of litigants, therefore bear a special relationship to campaign announcements. Suster's expenditure decision preserves this distinct ethics inquiry. Mistretta supports concern for the judiciary's institutional character, while Liteky preserves an objective appearance concern rather than requiring proof of actual bias. Later individual recusal cannot always cure institutional harm produced by repeated campaign commitments. The dissent does not equate every prior legal view with bias or treat elections as surrender of First Amendment protection; it finds the actual campaign context sufficiently different to sustain the restriction.

**Justice Ginsburg, dissenting, joined by Stevens, Souter and Breyer.** They also defend the state-adopted construction as directed to effective commitments rather than general legal philosophy, and dispute enlargement of that construction through counsel's spontaneous oral-argument answers. Their independent ground is functional: a candidate can communicate a promised disposition without literal promissory language, and a disclaimer need not remove electoral and financial pressure to deliver it. A literal-promise rule alone therefore leaves evasion of the identified harm. Tumey, Ward, Aetna, Murchison and Withrow protect litigants against intolerable risks arising from adjudicative self-interest without demanding completed proof of actual bias. The dissent weighs those interests and public confidence against candidate expression within strict scrutiny; it does not invent a new authoritative construction, impose discipline or decide the independent validity of every promise or recusal provision. Both dissent grounds have all four sustaining Justices' support and neither is reduced to generalized balancing.

#### Source Notes

Minnesota Canon 5A(3)(d)(i); Republican Party of Minnesota v. Kelly, 247 F.3d 854; [White, 536 U.S. 765](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-536/pdf/USREPORTS-536-765.pdf). The clause's authoritative construction and the significance of counsel's oral-argument answers are disputed in the opinions. The challenge concerns prospective enforcement; the dismissed complaint does not establish completed discipline.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Eighth Circuit's affirmance of summary judgment for the enforcing officials is reversed. The case returns for declaratory and appropriate prospective injunctive relief against the announce clause under Parts I–II. The lower court must implement that protection without supplying a replacement judicial code or requiring any candidate to announce views. Supreme Court review is concluded subject to ordinary mandate administration.

**End of entry: Republican Party of Minnesota v. White, Merits decision, June 27, 2002.**

### Zelman v. Simmons-Harris, No. 00-1751; Hanna Perkins School v. Simmons-Harris, No. 00-1777; Taylor v. Simmons-Harris, No. 00-1779

Merits decision, June 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 20, 2002. The consolidated cases review the Sixth Circuit's December 11, 2000 judgment, 234 F.3d 945, affirming summary judgment against Ohio's Cleveland tuition-assistance program and associated federal injunctive relief. Ohio, the participating-school parties and parents seek reversal; the taxpayer plaintiffs seek effective protection against publicly financed religious instruction.

The question is whether the restricted tuition payments finance integrated devotional instruction despite parental selection, and what relief follows at the supported provider/payment scope. Parents endorse payments for redemption by participating providers. The program also includes tutorial assistance and potential participation by adjacent public districts; none of those districts participated in the cited school year. #### Holdings

##### I. Restricted tuition payments remain subject to the financed-function rule

**Holding and operative rule:** Ohio may not finance demonstrated integrated devotional instruction through a restricted tuition payment redeemed by a provider merely because eligibility is neutral and a parent chooses and endorses the payment. Where devotional instruction is integrated into the financed work without reliable separation, parental routing and other educational options do not cure that purchase; genuine private benefits and independently controlled, reliably separated secular services retain their own protection.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join this rule and its application.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the challenged act is the public purchase made by restricted provider-redeemed tuition. Agostini retains Lemon as a unified inquiry into governmental authority and function: a specific prohibition or established permissible relationship controls when applicable; otherwise ordinary proof resolves attribution and function. Jackson addresses financed integrated devotional instruction, and Strout applies that rule to ordinary capped tuition. Neither neutral criteria nor the form of parental endorsement separates the instructional function bought by the payment.

The State's educational purpose is legitimate, and parents remain free to choose religious education. Their range of options can bear on genuine independence and coercion. The strongest contrary argument is that independently selected schools deliver an individual benefit to the child rather than governmental instruction. But this restricted payment purchases the integrated service identified in the record. Parental direction alone does not answer that distinct financing prohibition. Strout already rejects treating the cap on ordinary tuition as sufficient separation. Sustaining this purchase on the asserted choice ground would require reconsidering those holdings; the Court applies them.

Mueller and Witters retain their actual tax and individual vocational-benefit relationships. Rosenberger preserves its independently edited publication and controlled printing benefit, whose combined safeguards do not equate every expenditure with a speech forum. Mitchell likewise preserves genuine individual benefits while requiring correction of actual financed religious use. The Court does not attribute all activity of a religious school to government or infer the purchased function from the school's identity. Reliably separated secular work remains subject to its own supported analysis. Zobrest supplied no Establishment Clause merits holding, and cannot provide the claimed continuous line authorizing this tuition purchase.

##### II. Actual funded function determines the supported scope

**Holding and operative rule:** The funded religious function must be established at the provider/payment scope necessary for relief; religious affiliation, participation percentages, possible diversion, fungibility or incidental institutional savings do not alone prove it. Existing record descriptions of integrated teaching must be used where they establish the purchased function, without demanding reproof of an established use or presuming that every participating provider operates identically.

**Authority:** Part II; the same six Justices directly join the complete proof rule and its record application. The three dissenters' agreement with the insufficiency of affiliation alone does not add them to this part.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that evidence of what tuition purchases does legal work that enrollment percentages cannot. Mitchell distinguishes demonstrated funded religious use from assumptions based on a recipient's character or the possibility of diversion. The present record includes descriptions of integrated instruction relevant to that inquiry. Those descriptions must receive their actual evidentiary force; they are not reduced to mere risk because the program includes other providers.

Conversely, high religious participation does not itself establish every provider's curriculum, the absence of reliable separation or the unlawfulness of every payment. The disagreement over available secular options need not be resolved through a new finding that all alternatives were inadequate or had unlimited capacity. Under Jackson and Strout, improving options alone would not cure a demonstrated integrated devotional purchase. Actual choice remains relevant to a genuinely different protected benefit, but cannot replace examination of the financed work here.

The Court requires neither separate corporate governance nor wholly distinct personnel as a categorical condition. Operational separation and reliable accountability must address the actual function. Whether a particular remaining provider or payment satisfies the governing rule is ordinary record application on remand, not a reservation of the governing constitutional standard.

##### III. Effective relief must preserve lawful severable applications

**Holding and operative rule:** Relief must promptly prevent the demonstrated public purchase of integrated devotional instruction at the supported provider/payment scope while preserving lawful severable secular, tutorial and public-school applications under Ohio severability law. The lower courts may use the existing record where sufficient and must identify any material additional issue; uncertainty about unexamined applications neither authorizes continuation of a proved violation nor justifies categorical exclusion of every religious provider.

**Authority:** Part III; the same six Justices join the complete corrective-remedy standard. Their formal treatment of the established-use portion of the lower judgment differs as stated in Part IV and the judgment table.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that correction must be both effective and confined to the established conflict. Jackson requires provider/payment relief and state-law severability, and Mitchell forbids reducing a proved religious use to a hypothetical risk or requiring the parties to prove it again. Those decisions also preserve independently lawful applications. Dalton and Leavitt preserve the distinction between the federal conflict and the state-law consequences of severing it.

The blanket program disposition does not perform that work. The lower courts must use sufficient existing evidence to prevent established unlawful purchases and determine the consequences for affected payments. They must preserve lawful applications where supported and severable rather than treat religious identity as an exclusion rule. An unexamined provider does not excuse continuation of a demonstrated violation elsewhere. Conversely, a demonstrated use does not itself establish programwide prevalence or inseverability.

The remand resolves those application and severability questions without an automatic new trial or an indefinite transition. It does not judicially redesign Ohio's program. Any broader consequence must follow from supported dependence and Ohio law, not administrative convenience or a newly assumed constitutional entitlement to funding.

##### IV. The supported invalidation is affirmed and its unsupported extension vacated

**Holding and operative rule:** In each consolidated docket, the judgment is affirmed insofar as it invalidates demonstrated integrated devotional purchases, vacated insofar as it categorically extends beyond that supported scope, and remanded for effective relief and Ohio severability under Parts I–III. The existing-record rule governs the receiving courts' work; neither reproof of established use nor blanket continuation while every application is examined follows.

**Authority:** Part IV-A's partial affirmance has five direct joins: Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer. Part IV-B's vacatur and remand has six: those five and Stone-Zsela. Stone would additionally vacate the part affirmed, with the same binding financing and corrective-remedy directions; Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas would reverse the federal invalidation on their separate attribution ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the lower judgment correctly prevents the demonstrated unconstitutional purchase but cannot retain an unsupported categorical extension. The majority therefore preserves the established invalidity and returns the remaining remedial scope for the precise inquiry required by the controlling expenditure precedents. The three consolidated dockets receive the same result because they review that common program disposition. The Chief Justice agrees that the categorical extension must be vacated and the financed-function rule must govern remand, but would vacate the blanket disposition rather than affirm its supported portion.

### Stewart v. Smith, No. 01-339

Merits decision, June 28, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Ninth Circuit's judgment, 241 F.3d 1191, reversing the federal District Court's procedural-default ruling. The state director seeks reversal; Smith seeks federal consideration of his sentencing ineffective-assistance claim. The question is whether Arizona's waiver ruling is independent of federal law when the necessary form of waiver depends on the kind of constitutional right asserted.

Smith was convicted in Arizona in 1982 of murder, kidnapping and sexual assault. He received death on the murder count and consecutive prison terms on the others. His 1995 third Rule 32 petition raised a sentencing ineffective-assistance claim omitted from two earlier petitions. The state court applied Rule 32.2(a)(3) and rejected the explanation that successive counsel's affiliation with the same public defender's office prevented presentation. The federal District Court treated the state ruling as a bar. The Ninth Circuit accepted adequacy but held the ruling nonindependent. The Arizona Supreme Court's subsequent authoritative clarification addresses what the rule required for the 1995 petition: the kind of right, not adjudication of its violation, determines whether personal, knowing, voluntary and intelligent waiver is required.

All nine Justices participated at submission and decision.

#### Judgment

**The Ninth Circuit ruling that the state waiver determination was nonindependent is reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer.

The state ground's independence is settled. The receiving court must preserve the adequacy determination accepted below and address only properly presented, legally available remaining requirements and excuses. The judgment neither resolves valid cause nor adjudicates the underlying sentencing ineffective-assistance claim.

#### Opinion Topology

Per curiam. All nine Justices joined the opinion and judgment in full.

#### Holdings

##### I. The clarified waiver rule classifies rights without deciding their violation

**Holding and operative rule:** Under Arizona's authoritative construction of Rule 32.2(a)(3) for the 1995 petition, whether a claim requires personal, knowing, voluntary and intelligent waiver depends on the kind of right implicated; rights within that heightened-waiver category are not forfeited by mere omission. Identifying the category does not itself adjudicate the alleged federal violation, although a state procedural determination that actually depends on a federal constitutional ruling is not independent.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's per curiam opinion; Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer directly join the complete rule and its stated limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that identifying a right differs from deciding whether it was violated. The Arizona Supreme Court's answer determines what the state rule required at the time of Smith's third petition. It makes the requisite form of waiver depend on the right at the claim's core, not on whether the particular ineffective-assistance allegation succeeds. The Court accepts that state-law construction without making Arizona's categories federal constitutional merits law or declaring every constitutional claim waivable by omission.

Ake supplies the rule that a state procedural question dependent on a federal constitutional ruling is not an independent ground for direct review. Even assuming the same independence standard governs this habeas question, the clarified classification satisfies it. Coleman preserves the distinct adequate-and-independent-ground requirements. No universal identity between direct-review jurisdiction and habeas default must be decided to resolve this case. Trest and Lambrix confirm that procedural default is nonjurisdictional.

The opposing argument correctly warns that a state label cannot insulate a judgment actually resting on federal merits. That warning requires examination of the particular order, not rejection of every rule that refers to a constitutional category. Delaware v. Prouse likewise distinguishes identification of a right from reliance on a federal ruling. The actual state judgment receives that separate examination in Part II.

##### II. The actual state order rests on omission and consideration of an excuse

**Holding and operative rule:** The state order applying Rule 32.2(a)(3) rests on Smith's omission of the sentencing ineffective-assistance claim from his earlier petitions and its rejection of the asserted office-affiliation excuse, rather than on adjudication of trial counsel's constitutional performance. Its colorability reference, read in that context, does not make this procedural ruling dependent on federal merits.

**Authority:** Part II of the unanimous per curiam opinion; all nine Justices directly join the actual-ground determination.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the order's function controls. The state court found the claim waived because Smith had not presented it in the earlier proceedings. It then considered his explanation that appellate and prior postconviction counsel were unwilling to challenge another lawyer from their public defender's office. Its reference to a colorable claim explained its rejection of that asserted institutional obstacle. The court did not first decide whether trial counsel had rendered ineffective assistance at sentencing and use that answer as the predicate of waiver.

Smith's concern about the particular order therefore fails on this record, even though it would be legally material in a genuinely merits-dependent judgment. Carey reinforces examination of an order's actual meaning rather than treating a merits reference as conclusive. This ruling neither finds loyal representation nor decides that every office-affiliation conflict is harmless. Mickens involved a distinct undisclosed victim-counsel appointment and does not make common office membership an automatic conflict or default excuse. Whether a legally available cause theory is otherwise established remains a separate question.

##### III. Independence is settled, while genuinely remaining requirements retain their own force

**Holding and operative rule:** The Ninth Circuit's nonindependence ruling is reversed, and the case is expressly remanded for proceedings consistent with the independent character of this state ground. Adequacy was accepted below and is not affirmatively reopened; any properly preserved, legally available cause-and-prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice question retains its own requirements, and a valid, applicable, preserved and unexcused procedural bar must be enforced.

**Authority:** Part III of the unanimous per curiam opinion; all nine Justices join reversal and the bounded express remand.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that correction of the independence error does not decide every condition for habeas relief. Coleman preserves the separate grounds and exceptions. Lee's exceptional adequacy decision concerns a different application of a state rule and supplies no new challenge to adequacy already accepted here. Trest and Lambrix preserve the actual predicates of default and reject its treatment as a subject-matter-jurisdiction defect.

The Court expresses no opinion whether Smith has established valid cause. Rejecting the asserted significance of office affiliation for independence does not decide every properly presented excuse. Bell preserves genuinely pending investigation and default questions, and Horn confirms that resolution of one habeas condition does not dispose of every other condition needed for relief. Further proceedings must address only matters legally remaining, without an automatic evidentiary hearing or reopening of settled issues. The underlying sentencing ineffective-assistance claim receives no merits determination from this judgment.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Stewart v. Smith, 202 Ariz. 446, 46 P.3d 1067 (2002): its authoritative state-law construction governs what Rule 32.2(a)(3) required for the 1995 petition, without deciding the federal violation or every application of waiver.
- Ake v. Oklahoma, 470 U.S. 68: its federal-merits-dependence standard is satisfied even assuming it also governs this habeas independence question; no universal direct-review/habeas identity is adopted.
- Coleman v. Thompson, 501 U.S. 722: its independent and adequate state-ground requirements and cause/prejudice or miscarriage-of-justice exceptions retain separate force.
- Delaware v. Prouse, 440 U.S. 648: its distinction between identifying a right and resting on a federal ruling informs the classification inquiry.
- Trest v. Cain, December 9, 1997: procedural default remains nonjurisdictional and depends on its actual predicates; the decision supplies no automatic hearing or cause finding.
- Lambrix v. Singletary, May 12, 1997: separate collateral-review gates and lawful sequencing are preserved; deciding independence establishes no underlying constitutional entitlement.
- Lee v. Kemna, January 22, 2002: its exceptional adequacy application is distinct and does not reopen an otherwise unavailable adequacy challenge here.
- Carey v. Saffold, June 17, 2002: examination of the actual state order is preserved; a merits reference is not conclusive of the order's procedural meaning.
- Mickens v. Taylor, March 27, 2002: its specific victim-counsel conflict setting supplies no automatic office-affiliation conflict or default excuse in this case.
- Bell v. Cone, May 28, 2002: genuinely pending investigation and default matters retain their distinct requirements; no automatic inquiry follows.
- Horn v. Banks, June 17, 2002: satisfaction of one habeas condition does not eliminate other preserved, applicable conditions for granting relief.

#### Law After Decision

Arizona's classification by the type of right does not itself make its waiver ruling dependent on federal merits. Federal courts must still examine the particular state order; this order rests on omission and consideration of an excuse. Independence is resolved without changing the heightened-waiver qualification, default's nonjurisdictional character, the accepted adequacy determination or the independent requirements of any legally remaining excuse and constitutional claim.

Stewart, 241 F.3d 1191; 28 U.S.C. §2254; [Stewart, 536 U.S. 856](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-536/pdf/USREPORTS-536-856.pdf). #### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The case returns to the Ninth Circuit for proceedings consistent with the independence holding. The convictions and sentences remain governed by existing judgments and lawful further proceedings. ### United States v. Bass, No. 01-1471

Merits decision, June 28, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On review of the Sixth Circuit's judgment, 266 F.3d 532, sustaining the District Court's discovery order. The United States seeks reversal and removal of the resulting death-notice restraint. Bass seeks dismissal of the notice or discovery concerning alleged racial selection in federal capital charging. The questions concern the source and preliminary evidentiary basis for that discovery and the consequence of refusing the current order.

A federal grand jury in the Eastern District of Michigan charged Bass, who is Black, with offenses including the intentional firearm killings of two people. The Government filed notice of intent to seek death. The District Court ordered selection discovery and excluded the death notice after the Government declined to comply; a divided appellate panel sustained the discovery order. Bass relied on the Justice Department's 2000 national survey of capital-eligible charges and plea outcomes. The aggregate figures did not identify the relevantly comparable eligible population or defendants passed over at the pertinent decision. Bass was offered a plea bargain and declined it. Under the protocol in place since 1995, local United States Attorneys decide capital-eligible charges and submit those charged cases for the Attorney General's separate death-authorization decision.

##### Rule 16 setting

Rule 16(a)(1)(C) concerns books, papers, documents, photographs, tangible objects, buildings or places within the Government's possession, custody or control. On a defendant's request, inspection and copying or photographing depend on one of three alternatives: materiality to preparation of the defense, intended use by the Government in its evidence in chief, or acquisition from or ownership by the defendant. Those triggers remain distinct.

Paragraph (a)(2) excludes internal attorney or agent reports, memoranda and other Government documents connected with investigation or prosecution, except the disclosures provided by (a)(1)(A), (B), (D) and (E) concerning defendant statements, prior criminal records, examinations and tests, and expert testimony. Government witness statements are separately governed by 18 U.S.C. §3500; no witness-statement production question is resolved here. Paragraph (a)(3) preserves the separate grand-jury disclosure provisions of Rules 6, 12(i), 26.2 and (a)(1)(A). No grand-jury exception is invoked as the source here, and none is decided. These provisions do not establish that every requested aggregate, criterion or deliberative document belongs to the same category.

Rule 16(d)(1) permits protective relief on sufficient showing, including an ex parte written showing whose full text must be sealed and preserved if relief is granted. Paragraph (d)(2) permits discovery, a continuance, exclusion and other just responses to noncompliance. These powers regulate an existing obligation; their existence is not a finding that a duty, exception, privilege or sanction predicate is established here.

#### Holdings

##### I. Armstrong's confined Rule 16 source holding governs this request

**Holding and operative rule:** Rule 16(a)(1)(C)'s defense-preparation materiality branch does not authorize discovery to prepare this selective-prosecution challenge to the decision to prosecute; Armstrong's holding remains confined to that claim rather than every affirmative defense unrelated to guilt. The distinct intended-use and defendant-origin branches are not established on this record, and protective or sanction powers cannot create an otherwise missing disclosure duty.

**Authority:** Part I of the Court's per curiam opinion has six direct joins: O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas and Ginsburg. Stone-Zsela and Breyer join only Parts II–III; Stevens concurs in the judgment on his separate ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the existing source rule applies to the requested inquiry. Armstrong distinguishes preparation of the defense against the prosecution's criminal case from an independent constitutional attack on the Government's selection decision. Its confined construction of the materiality branch controls here. It neither excludes every guilt-unrelated defense nor supplies immunity from constitutional review.

The three Rule 16 triggers remain disjunctive. The requested information is not shown to be material the Government intends to use in its evidence in chief or material obtained from or belonging to Bass. The distinct exclusions and protections stated in the statutory setting do not create a disclosure obligation and do not make aggregate information, selection criteria and internal deliberations interchangeable categories. A protective order may regulate an authorized inquiry; reducing the request or imposing a sanction cannot establish its original source.

Breyer reads the preparation branch to include material support for a genuine selective-prosecution defense. Armstrong resolved that construction at the confined scope stated here. In any event, Part II supplies a separate sufficient ground: this proffer lacks the comparative connection required for the requested inquiry. No alternative discovery source is established or rejected categorically by this decision.

##### II. The attainable preliminary standard is not met by this aggregate showing

**Holding and operative rule:** Before obtaining discovery on this indirect racial-selection theory under an available lawful source, a defendant must present some credible evidence tending to show discriminatory effect and purpose, including a reliable inference of different treatment of relevantly similarly situated persons of another race at the challenged federal selection stage; this is less than a prima facie merits case. Grounded statistics, circumstantial comparisons and a specifically justified alternative when comparator names cannot reasonably be obtained may suffice without perfect identity, universal naming or a fixed matching checklist, but Bass's aggregate charging and completed-plea figures lack the required effect comparison.

**Authority:** Part II has eight direct joins: Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Stevens agrees that the present order fails through his distinct supported-pattern and bounded-discretion analysis, not by joining this entire rationale.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Armstrong requires an attainable, grounded inference, not completed proof of discrimination. Its treatment of Wayte and Wade preserves constitutional review while requiring a meaningful preliminary connection. Its application of Kimberlin and Klinger permits statistical and circumstantial comparison tied to the challenged treatment; neither identity nor individual names are universal requirements.

The national charging totals describe selected defendants without connecting racial distribution to a relevantly comparable eligible population or persons passed over. An appropriate denominator or matched-person evidence could perform that work, but neither technique is mandatory. Official provenance establishes the source of the counts, not comparability of the unmeasured groups. McCleskey requires connection to the challenged decision without categorically excluding statistics; Yick Wo preserves inference from a sufficiently supported discriminatory pattern.

Local capital-eligible charging and the Attorney General's later death authorization are distinct decisions. The national authorizer makes nationwide evidence potentially relevant, but does not turn charging totals into a comparison of authorization decisions. The Court imposes no local-only rule. Likewise, completed bargains combine offers and acceptance. Bass was offered a bargain and declined it. The reported acceptance disparity therefore does not establish that he was denied an offer, without proving equal terms or equality throughout the system.

Information asymmetry is substantial. Armstrong answers it through grounded alternative proof, not a presumption that records are readily available. Discovery may develop a supported inference; the Government's possession of useful data, the official national scale, or a less intrusive request cannot alone create the missing comparative connection. The effect defect suffices. The Court makes no independent purpose-deficiency holding, no finding that comparators do not exist and no ultimate determination of discrimination or neutrality. Brown's rule governing deliberate racial classification in police investigation supplies neither an absent disclosure source nor that missing inference.

##### III. The unsupported order cannot sustain its dependent death-notice sanction

**Holding and operative rule:** The Sixth Circuit's judgment sustaining the discovery order is reversed, and the refusal-based exclusion of the death notice loses its unsupported predicate and must be set aside. Ordinary pretrial proceedings continue; a later request remains possible only under an available lawful source, a sufficiently grounded preliminary showing and warranted proportionate protective conditions, without any directed new inquiry.

**Authority:** Part III has the same eight direct joins as Part II. Stevens supplies the ninth reversal vote on his separate ground and agrees that the dependent sanction falls.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that a sanction enforces an existing obligation rather than creating one. Once the current discovery demand fails, the Government's refusal of that demand cannot support exclusion of its death notice. Armstrong supplies the distinction between removing an unsupported order and guaranteeing renewed discovery. Carlisle preserves supervision within governing legal limits.

Nothing necessary to decide this order requires a newly directed evidentiary step. Ordinary proceedings allow a genuinely different later request to be evaluated under its own source, evidentiary foundation and protective limits. That possibility is a limit on the decision's reach, not a command to collect evidence or hold a hearing. Failure of the present request does not establish neutral prosecution or foreclose all constitutional scrutiny. The proper sanction for refusing a valid discovery order and the remedy for proved discrimination are different questions outside this disposition.

#### Precedent Treatment

- United States v. Armstrong, May 13, 1996: its confined Rule 16 nonauthorization, attainable preliminary comparison and dependent-sanction principles are applied with their distinct coalitions; its ordinary-proceeding rule does not direct a renewed inquiry here.
- Wayte v. United States, 470 U.S. 598: constitutional effect-and-purpose limits remain, while its reservation of discovery is not treated as an independent source of this order.
- Wade v. United States, May 18, 1992: meaningful preliminary screening remains an analogy from sentencing, not a completed-merits burden for this selection discovery.
- Kimberlin v. Quinlan, June 12, 1995: grounded statistical and circumstantial proof remains available without a categorical direct-evidence demand, subject to the actual constitutional and disclosure predicates.
- Klinger v. Department of Corrections, December 4, 1995: relevant comparison follows the challenged treatment without requiring perfect identity.
- McCleskey v. Kemp, 481 U.S. 279: a statistical inference must connect to the challenged decision; the decision creates neither categorical exclusion of statistics nor a merits-level discovery burden.
- Yick Wo v. Hopkins, 118 U.S. 356: inference from a supported discriminatory-administration pattern remains available; the present aggregates lack the necessary comparison.
- Brown v. City of Oneonta, November 13, 2001: deliberate racial classification in police investigation is distinct from the unestablished selection inference and discovery source here.
- Carlisle v. United States, April 29, 1996: supervisory authority remains bounded by governing law and supplies no automatic disclosure duty or immunity from constitutional review.
- United States v. Williams, May 4, 1992: its bounded judicial-protection principle informs Stevens's separate discretionary ground; its different grand-jury duty and dismissal rules are not transferred to this sanction.

#### Law After Decision

Armstrong's source rule and attainable preliminary threshold retain their separate force. Official national statistics can be relevant, including to a national authorization decision, but this proffer does not compare relevantly similar eligible persons at the challenged stage. Offer and acceptance remain distinct. The present order and its refusal-based consequence fall without a ruling on ultimate discrimination or neutral administration. A later lawful and sufficiently grounded request remains possible; no source, new inquiry or universal named-comparator requirement is created. The rule concerns indirect proof and does not resolve whether direct discriminatory admissions dispense with a comparator showing.

#### Separate Writings

**Justice Breyer, concurring in part and in the judgment.** He joins Parts II–III and reversal but not Part I's Rule 16 construction. As in his Armstrong concurrence, the preparation branch performs work distinct from the intended-use branch and can include material support for a genuine selective-prosecution defense. That reading remains subject to the Rule's separate document, witness-statement and grand-jury restrictions and its protective provisions stated above; it does not establish an exception or entitlement to any protected category here.

The missing relevant comparison defeats present materiality on his own reading. National authorization gives a reason national evidence may matter, but the official totals do not connect materially similar eligible people to different treatment. Kimberlin and Klinger preserve grounded circumstantial comparisons; access disadvantage, reduced scope or a proposed initial stage cannot supply an absent predicate. He agrees that a later supported request remains possible under ordinary procedures, without a mandatory additional hearing or an independent finding that purpose evidence fails.

**Justice Stevens, concurring in the judgment.** He agrees that this order and its dependent sanction cannot stand, without joining the Court's complete source or general-threshold rationale. His Armstrong dissent distinguished a defendant's established discovery entitlement from a judge's bounded discretion to examine a supported disturbing pattern. There the defined local sample, firsthand state/federal professional comparison, substantial penalty differential and developed response together supported inquiry. That position did not require automatic investigation whenever a request lacked sufficient evidence.

Bass's official national figures have a reliable source and a potentially appropriate geographic scope. They nevertheless lack a grounded connection between relevantly comparable eligible persons and the challenged treatment. A national authorizer reduces a geographical objection; it does not create the missing comparison, and completed-bargain figures do not establish that Bass lacked an offer he actually received. Wayte's constitutional limits and the bounded judicial-protection principle he invoked in Williams do not eliminate the need for a supported basis. His discretionary theory therefore does not justify this order or require replacement discovery. He preserves lawful discretion on a genuinely supported later record without announcing a general entitlement, accepting government neutrality or deciding the remedy for proved discrimination.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Sixth Circuit judgment is reversed and the dependent death-notice exclusion is set aside. The prosecution continues through ordinary pretrial procedures, with any later discovery request subject to its own source, evidentiary and protective requirements. The Court directs no collection of evidence or new hearing and prescribes no further Court event. The proper sanction for refusing a valid order and the remedy for proved selective prosecution remain outside this judgment.
