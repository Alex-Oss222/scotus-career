### Barnhart v. Walton, No. 00-1937

Merits decision, March 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued January 16, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Fourth Circuit, 235 F.3d 184. The questions are whether the agency may require twelve months of disabling inability as well as lasting impairment, and whether actual substantial work within that period before a favorable disability determination defeats projected-duration and trial-work theories. The circuit affirmed the October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding but reversed the denial on both legal grounds.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The lower court must implement denial on the reviewed duration and trial-work eligibility grounds. Its affirmed October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding remains undisturbed.

#### Opinion Topology

SOUTER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined, and in which SCALIA, J., joined as to Parts I and III. SCALIA, J., filed an opinion concurring in part and in the judgment. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a concurring opinion.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I and III | Souter | All eight other Justices | Reverses and remands | Formal delegated-regulation ground; duration and timing rules, qualifications and disposition |
| Opinion of the Court, Part II | Souter | The seven Justices joining throughout, named above | Additional controlling ground | Sustained interpretation and administrative consideration; Scalia withholds this part |
| Concurrence in part and in the judgment | Scalia | — | Joins Parts I and III and the judgment | Withholds Part II |
| Concurrence | Stone-Zsela | — | Joins the Court's opinion and judgment | Adds independent statutory agreement |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The agency may require twelve months of disabling inability

**Holding and operative rule:** Under the twelve-month branch of the adult disability definitions in 42 U.S.C. §§423(d)(1)(A) and 1382c(a)(3)(A), the agency may require that the impairment cause inability to engage in substantial gainful activity lasting, or expected to last, continuously for at least twelve months. The expected-death branch remains distinct; the Court sustains a permissible delegated construction rather than declaring it the only statutory implementation Congress allows.

**Authority:** Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court, Part I, is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the formal delegated-regulation ground, the functional-duration rule and its temporal qualification. Part II's additional contextual support is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer; Scalia does not join that additional ground.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the agency's construction fits the integrated definition. The relative clause concerning duration follows impairment, but the defined disability is inability to work because of that impairment. The wording does not unambiguously require benefits for a lasting diagnosis after the disabling inability ends. The agency may read the lasting condition as the condition that produces the described inability. The expected-death alternative concerns the impairment; it does not mean that inability or work itself causes death. Title II's ordinary five-month waiting condition does not compel an impairment-only interpretation, and that waiting rule is not imported into SSI.

Section 405(a), incorporated into SSI by §1383(d)(1), supplies rulemaking authority consistent with the benefit statutes. The agency exercised it in binding implementing regulations and the final trial-work provision, after a proposal and notice and comment. Mead requires actual delegation and its exercise, not expertise or form alone; those predicates are established here. Chevron permits the resulting reasonable choice. The July 2000 final rule became effective August 10, 2000. Smiley permits use of its authoritative interpretation of the preexisting statute for antecedent events without backdating a new legislative condition, where clear contrary prior official guidance is absent. The longstanding duration approach and 1995 proposal supply continuity here; no contrary assurance to Walton is established. The lower court's no-retroactivity statement therefore does not exclude the interpretive use at issue.

**Additional controlling explanation in Part II:** The sustained administrative interpretation and the agency's detailed attention to the connected eligibility provisions further support the reasonableness of its choice. These considerations operate within the established delegation and formal exercise of authority; they create neither power from longevity alone nor a holding that every earlier letter, manual or Social Security Ruling has controlling force. Scalia accepts the sufficient formal-regulation ground while withholding this additional contextual ground.

##### II. Actual work before a favorable disability finding may defeat the initial duration claim

**Holding and operative rule:** When work demonstrating substantial gainful activity resumes within twelve months after onset of the disabling impairment and before notice of a determination or decision finding disability, the agency may use that known event to reject an initial claim based on an earlier expectation of twelve-month inability. Section 422(c)'s trial-work protection presupposes qualifying Title II entitlement and does not independently establish a missing initial duration condition.

**Authority:** Part III of Souter's opinion is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine directly adopt the permissible timing construction, the qualifications below, and the disposition of Walton's two legal theories.

**Operative qualifications:** A claimant returning to work more than twelve months after onset but before adjudication may qualify for trial-work protection if actual duration and the other entitlement conditions are satisfied; passage of twelve months alone does not cure disabling inability that ended earlier. A claimant returning within twelve months after onset, but after notice finding disability, may qualify even when that notice precedes the benefit-award notice. A disability finding, its notice, an award, payment and statutory entitlement are distinct; neither qualification requires prior payment. The separate exclusion for work during a required waiting period remains separate, and the pre-application exclusion concerns months before application, not permanent disqualification for every earlier work episode.

Trial-work services are distinct from substantial gainful activity. The operative nine service months occur within sixty consecutive months and need not themselves be consecutive. New medical or other evidence independent of protected trial work may establish cessation under the governing rules before nine months; a repeated trial-work period is not created within the same period of disability or entitlement. Ordinary Title II insured status, application, age and the five-month waiting condition, including the qualifying prior-entitlement or cessation route within the statutory sixty-month window, remain separate conditions. The Title II trial-work scheme is not transferred to SSI. Statutory blindness, the separate child definition, genuinely unsuccessful work attempts and distinct reopening or reliance questions concerning a claimant with an existing favorable determination are outside the presented adult eligibility dispute.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the statute does not fix an earlier moment at which an expectation becomes irrevocable before the initial favorable determination. Expected duration permits prompt awards before twelve months have elapsed; it does not require the agency to disregard actual work already known when initial eligibility is decided. The agency may distinguish establishing disability from deciding whether an entitled person's disability has ceased. A protection against using trial work to establish cessation cannot alone create the initial entitlement it presupposes.

Walton objects that entitlement need not await an award and that adjudication timing can produce unequal results. The first point is correct and is preserved by the two qualifications above. The agency acknowledged the second and reasonably accommodated two statutory purposes: prompt prospective awards and exclusion of demonstrated short-term disability. That explanation does not declare every administrative delay harmless; it answers the claimed requirement to treat an earlier expectation as conclusive here. Good Samaritan preserves reasonable administrative choice within the assigned program, while Smiley preserves qualified interpretive use for antecedent events. The actual statutory grant and rulemaking satisfy Mead; no new generalized deference test or resort to Auer is needed.

Walton's October 1995 substantial-gainful-activity finding stands. His later closed-period award was superseded on administrative review, and no favorable finding notice preceded his return. The circuit's impairment-only interpretation, accrued-expectation theory and independent unreasonableness conclusion therefore fail. Its challenged legal rulings are reversed, with remand to implement the denial on these eligibility grounds.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.:** Applied to a permissible choice within the agency's actual statutory assignment; the Court does not require its own sole preferred construction.
- **United States v. Mead Corp.:** Applied through identified statutory delegation and actual exercise in binding implementing regulations; complexity, expertise or notice and comment alone does not establish authority.
- **Smiley v. Citibank (South Dakota), N.A.:** Applied to qualified interpretive use for antecedent events without backdating a new legislative condition or disregarding clear contrary prior official guidance.
- **Good Samaritan Hospital v. Shalala:** Preserves reasoned administrative choice within the assigned statutory scheme.
- **Christensen v. Harris County:** Preserves the distinction between advice and delegated lawmaking; no blanket controlling status is conferred on every Social Security Ruling.
- **Auer v. Robbins:** No extension is required; statutory authority and the actual regulations supply the ground.
- **Wisconsin Department of Health and Family Services v. Blumer, Barnhart v. Sigmon Coal Co., Edelman v. Lynchburg College and Ragsdale v. Wolverine World Wide, Inc.:** Their distinct limits on agency authority and statutory consequences remain. Independent construction in another statute does not eliminate lawful delegated choice here.

#### Law After Decision

The agency may administer the adult duration definitions through a twelve-month requirement for disabling inability and use actual substantial work before the relevant favorable finding notice in deciding initial eligibility. The holding sustains that reasonable delegated choice; it does not compel every future lawful implementation. The stated pre-award and post-twelve-month qualifications prevent converting entitlement into prior payment or a universal award prerequisite. Title II trial work retains its own scope and conditions.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and in the judgment.** Scalia joins Parts I and III, including the formal delegated-regulation ground, permissible duration and timing constructions, qualifications and reversal. He does not join Part II's additional reliance on sustained administrative interpretation. The formal rule and genuine delegation suffice; duration of an agency practice alone should not confer special controlling weight, and the status of old informal documents cannot be assumed. His position does not demand the Court's independent preferred construction or a new multifactor exception to the governing delegation rules.

**Stone-Zsela, concurring.** The Chief joins the Court's permissible-construction holding and adds that the interpretation also survives independent reading. The lasting impairment must produce the inability that the definition describes; expected duration permits timely decisions, but does not require ignoring actual predecision substantial work. Trial-work protection does not itself create initial entitlement. The agency's longstanding, expert reasons deserve substantial persuasive weight because they fit those enacted distinctions, while its statutory rulemaking assignment and operative regulations separately establish actual delegated authority. This additional ground changes neither the Court's permission into a command nor the stated entitlement qualifications.

[65 Fed. Reg. 42772–42792](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2000-07-11/pdf/00-17138.pdf), effective August 10, 2000. #### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Further proceedings implement the denial on the reviewed eligibility grounds. The Court preserves the established substantial-gainful-activity finding and the distinct qualifications stated above.

**End of entry: Barnhart v. Walton, merits decision, March 27, 2002.**

### Mickens v. Taylor, No. 00-9285

Merits decision, March 27, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued November 5, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the en banc Fourth Circuit, reviewing denial of federal habeas relief. The appointed lawyer had recently represented the murder victim, and the appointing judge knew the relationship but did not inquire or disclose it. The question is whether that omitted inquiry dispenses with a showing that the conflict adversely affected representation. A federal evidentiary hearing resulted in a finding against adverse effect; the separate factual challenge to that finding is outside the granted question.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 5–4.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy and Thomas.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer, who would reverse and obtain a new trial on the separate grounds stated below.

The conviction and sentence remain undisturbed on this claim. The Court orders no new trial or additional conflict hearing solely because the judge failed to inquire.

#### Opinion Topology

KENNEDY, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, and THOMAS, JJ., joined. STEVENS, J., and SOUTER, J., filed dissenting opinions. BREYER, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which GINSBURG, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### An omitted inquiry does not itself establish the adverse effect necessary here

**Holding and operative rule:** Outside the established circumstances requiring automatic relief, a trial court's failure to inquire into a particular potential conflict it knew or reasonably should have known about does not itself establish a Sixth Amendment violation. A defendant invoking the conflict-based relaxation of ordinary outcome prejudice must at least show that competing obligations adversely affected counsel's performance; that showing is different from proving a reasonable probability of a different verdict under Strickland.

**Authority:** The five Justices joining Justice Kennedy's Opinion of the Court, named above, directly adopt the rule, the confined application and affirmance. The four dissenters support a new trial on distinct grounds and do not establish a common alternative holding.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the right protects loyal, effective representation rather than imposing automatic reversal as a sanction for a judge's omitted procedure. Holloway requires automatic relief when counsel makes an adequate timely conflict objection to joint representation and the court compels continuation without the required inquiry or protection. That distinct objection-and-compulsion predicate is absent here. Sullivan requires an actual conflict adversely affecting performance before ordinary outcome prejudice is dispensed with. Wood's actual-conflict inquiry does not create a separate rule awarding relief for divided obligations without an effect on representation in this setting. Cronic's exceptional denial-of-assistance principle does not convert this omitted inquiry into a complete loss of counsel.

The contrary positions emphasize that the State selected the murder victim's recent lawyer, the judge knew the particular risk and Mickens lacked the information needed to object. The Court accepts the seriousness of that combination and the duty to inquire. Swidler preserves confidentiality after death, so death does not eliminate the potential conflict. But neither continuing duties nor judicial silence establishes that counsel withheld a defense, altered a choice or otherwise performed differently. The appointment's gravity alone does not establish the structural denial or representation injury needed for the proposed categorical remedy.

The federal court conducted an evidentiary hearing and found no adverse effect. The granted question asks whether that requirement can be dispensed with; it does not reopen the separate factual challenge to the finding. Even assuming Sullivan's relaxed-prejudice route applies to this successive representation, the existing determination defeats this claim. The Court does not decide its application to every category of successive representation, which lies beyond the reviewed question. Holloway retains its established rule, and no ordinary outcome-prejudice burden is added after the predicates of an applicable conflict exception are satisfied.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Holloway v. Arkansas, 435 U.S. 475:** Preserved for compelled joint representation over an adequate timely objection; its automatic remedy is not extended solely because the judge knew a potential conflict.
- **Cuyler v. Sullivan, 446 U.S. 335:** Applied on the assumption favorable to Mickens that its relaxed-prejudice route is available; an actual conflict adversely affecting performance remains necessary here, without additional ordinary outcome prejudice.
- **Wood v. Georgia, 450 U.S. 261:** Its inquiry obligation and actual-conflict requirement do not establish the proposed separate exemption from adverse effect in this setting.
- **Strickland v. Washington, 466 U.S. 668:** Ordinary outcome prejudice remains distinct from the adverse-effect requirement under an applicable conflict exception.
- **United States v. Cronic, 466 U.S. 648:** Its exceptional presumed-prejudice circumstances are not established merely by this unperformed inquiry.
- **Swidler & Berlin v. United States:** Post-death confidentiality remains; its survival proves neither a particular confidence nor impairment of this defense.
- **Both Williams v. Taylor decisions:** Constitutional entitlement, concealed-fact cause, factual development and state-merits review remain distinct; the federal hearing does not supply a state merits adjudication.

#### Law After Decision

A known potential conflict and the judge's neglected inquiry do not themselves establish the adverse effect required for this conflict-based exception to ordinary prejudice. Holloway retains its distinct automatic-relief rule. The Court assumes Sullivan's route is available here without deciding its application to every successive representation. The existing no-adverse-effect determination defeats this claim within the granted question.

#### Separate Writings

**Stevens, dissenting.** Stevens would reverse the habeas denial and secure a new trial because the State appointed the murder victim's recent lawyer in a capital case without disclosure or the accused's informed acceptance, despite the judge's knowledge. The right to a meaningful lawyer-client relationship, counsel's disclosure obligation and the court's protective duty operate together. This exceptional State-created arrangement warrants presumed impairment under Cronic; Holloway, Sullivan and Wood supply the conflict safeguards, while Swidler preserves continuing confidentiality without proving any particular confidence. His rule concerns this undisclosed capital victim-counsel appointment, not every former-client relationship or ethical breach. He does not require another actual-conflict hearing before recognizing the constitutional entitlement.

**Souter, dissenting.** Souter would reverse and require a new trial under the court's prospective duty to address a sufficiently identified risk before representation unfolds. When the judge already has adequate notice, Holloway's preventive protection should not depend solely on an objection from a potentially conflicted lawyer. The judge must inquire, secure an intelligent waiver or arrange replacement when warranted. Souter distinguishes that prospective duty from later discovery: he reads Wood's retrospective inquiry as requiring actual conflict and adverse effect, rather than supporting an actual-conflict-only remand here. His discussion of possible adverse effects supplies no decision of the factual issue outside the grant and no direction for another hearing.

**Breyer, dissenting, joined by Ginsburg.** These Justices would reverse and secure a new trial on a narrower structural ground. The State supplied the recent criminal lawyer of the murder victim to defend the accused in a capital case; the immediate incompatibility, the capital proceeding's use of the victim's character and the State's creation of the arrangement produce a breakdown in the trial framework. Cronic and Fulminante support relief without reconstructing individual performance effects at that conjunction of facts. Young v. United States ex rel. Vuitton reinforces the concern for institutional fairness. They expressly leave the general relationship among Holloway, Sullivan and Wood unresolved and do not join Souter's general known-risk rule or an actual-conflict-only remand.

Each dissent seeks the new-trial remedy through ordinary conditional habeas relief, permitting the State to retry with constitutionally adequate representation. None orders immediate unconditional release, acquittal or a new sentence alone. Their constitutional positions do not adjudicate an independently presented collateral defense on predicates absent from the question decided. Counsel maintained the narrower actual-conflict theory; the dissenters give their own legal answers to the granted conflict question and the requested new-trial remedy.

#### Source Notes

[Official argument transcript, November 5, 2001](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/argument_transcripts/2001/00-9285.pdf), pp. 23–25 and 55–56, for counsel's actual-conflict limitation and refusal to concede adverse effect. Holloway, 435 U.S. 475; Sullivan, 446 U.S. 335; Wood, 450 U.S. 261; Strickland, 466 U.S. 668; Cronic, 466 U.S. 648. The conflict claim had no state merits adjudication; §2254(d) is not invoked merely from the federal filing date.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The habeas denial stands on the reviewed constitutional ground. The Court does not reopen the factual adverse-effect determination or direct another conflict inquiry. The broader successive-representation question remains outside the grant.

**End of entry: Mickens v. Taylor, merits decision, March 27, 2002.**

### Adams v. Florida Power Corp., No. 01-584

Dismissal as improvidently granted, April 1, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued March 20, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Eleventh Circuit, reviewing its answer to an interlocutory certified question whether disparate-impact liability is available under the Age Discrimination in Employment Act. The district court entered a categorical bar without deciding the factual sufficiency of the asserted impact theory; the separate class ruling is outside the certified appeal.

#### Public Action

The writ of certiorari is dismissed as improvidently granted. Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents. The Court announces no individual tally beyond that separate writing.

The Eleventh Circuit judgment remains undisturbed without approval of its statutory reasoning.

Per curiam. STONE-ZSELA, C. J., filed a dissenting opinion.

#### Basis and Current-Law Effect

The writ is dismissed as improvidently granted. No controlling proposition concerning ADEA disparate-impact liability is decided.

The Court alters no precedent. Hazen Paper's reservation of disparate impact remains; the dissent's proposed statutory rule is not adopted.

This dismissal creates no Supreme Court rule accepting or rejecting ADEA disparate-impact liability. The judgment below retains its ordinary force.

#### Separate Writings

Chief Justice Stone-Zsela dissents. He would decide the certified statutory question, reverse the categorical bar and remand. The district court actually entered and certified that legal ruling, and neither it nor the circuit majority adopted a finding that the impact allegations were insufficient. The unassessed factual merits therefore do not prevent correcting the legal bar. He reads section 623(a)(2)'s effects-oriented classification language together with section 623(f)(1)'s reasonable-factors-other-than-age provision to permit a claim directed to a specifically identified neutral practice that causes an adverse age effect. The 1991 Title VII amendments do not justify importing their entire business-necessity scheme into the ADEA. On remand, the lower courts would assess the actual practice, causation, reasonable-factor justification and procedural requirements; recognizing the theory would not itself restore a class or establish liability. This is his dissenting position, not the Court's statutory holding.

### Venetian Casino Resort, L.L.C. v. Local Joint Executive Board of Las Vegas

Merits decision, April 1, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Review of the Ninth Circuit judgment, 257 F.3d 937. The question is whether the replacement sidewalk along Las Vegas Boulevard remains a traditional public forum despite private fee ownership, given its continuing public-passage function and the government agreement replacing the former public sidewalk. The casino sought exclusion of union demonstrators from that route.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would reverse and remand.

The public-forum judgment for this replacement boulevard sidewalk stands. The casino's requested blanket exclusion based solely on private title is rejected; lawful neutral measures addressing actual obstruction, safety and access remain available.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. SCALIA, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The replacement public thoroughfare retains constitutional speech protection

**Holding and operative rule:** A sidewalk deliberately substituted for an existing public street route, maintained under a continuing, enforceable public-passage obligation and integrated into that route remains a traditional public forum for peaceful expression. Private fee ownership does not permit a general speech exclusion from that passage; the holding concerns the replacement route, not all privately owned walkways or customer-access areas.

**Authority:** The seven Justices joining Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, named above, directly adopt the route-specific governmental-attribution and traditional-public-forum rule, its application and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that constitutional responsibility follows the actual public-route arrangement. Government permitted removal of the former public sidewalk while securing a connected replacement, mandatory unobstructed passage, obligations binding successors, and retained protection against hazards. Marsh supplies the limited principle that private ownership cannot defeat constitutional protection of an established governmental public function. Brentwood and Lebron require actual governmental responsibility, rather than an inference from popularity or accessibility alone. Here the government's direct undertaking to preserve its street route supplies that responsibility before forum classification is considered. Grace then supplies the traditional expressive character of an ordinary street sidewalk.

The private-property reservation and future-dedication clause do not disappear. They preserve title, ordinary maintenance and lawful property interests; the Court does not construe the agreement as a present conveyance of title or decide a taking. The reservation itself is bounded by constitutional limits. The obligation to dedicate a further replacement if this walkway is removed, altered or abandoned protects continuity of passage; it does not transform the current mandatory through-route into an invitation the owner may freely withdraw. Lloyd and Hudgens preserve control of genuinely private commercial property, but this passage continues a displaced public street function. The dissent's proposed distinction between access and speech would let the same government-secured street remain compulsory public passage while losing its traditional expressive protection solely through relocated title. The First Amendment does not permit that result on this arrangement.

##### II. Forum protection permits lawful neutral protection of passage and safety

**Holding and operative rule:** The casino may not exclude the peaceful demonstration from this replacement sidewalk solely by invoking private title. Traditional-public-forum standards continue to govern: content-based restrictions require a compelling justification and narrow tailoring, while content-neutral time, place and manner restrictions must be narrowly tailored to a significant governmental interest and leave ample alternative channels; viewpoint discrimination is forbidden.

**Authority:** The same seven-Justice Court opinion adopts this consequence and preserves lawful neutral regulation of actual obstruction, safety and access.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that speech protection and continued pedestrian passage operate together. Perry supplies the governing forum standards; Ward permits genuinely neutral management of time, place and manner without requiring the least restrictive conceivable means, while forbidding burdens substantially broader than necessary. Thomas v. Chicago Park District preserves constrained neutral administration of shared expressive space. Those rules leave room to address actual obstruction and safety; public-forum status does not immunize such conduct. The casino's requested blanket exclusion rests on title and therefore fails at this location. No particular obstruction sanction or casino-interior restriction is presented, so the Court decides neither. The Ninth Circuit's judgment concerning this sidewalk is affirmed.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Marsh v. Alabama, 326 U.S. 501:** Applied to the established public-thoroughfare function; ordinary private commercial property does not thereby become governmental.
- **Brentwood Academy v. Tennessee Secondary School Athletic Association:** Its requirement of actual governmental responsibility is applied to the government's binding replacement-route arrangement; public benefit or association alone remains insufficient.
- **Lebron v. National Railroad Passenger Corp.:** Its rejection of dispositive private labels informs the attribution inquiry. Its special-creation and permanent-majority-appointment rule remains sufficient in its own setting, rather than a mandatory template for every public function.
- **United States v. Grace, 461 U.S. 171:** Supplies ordinary street-sidewalk forum protection after governmental responsibility has been established.
- **Lloyd Corp. v. Tanner, 407 U.S. 551, and Hudgens v. NLRB, 424 U.S. 507:** Distinguished because ordinary private shopping property does not continue a displaced government-secured public street route.
- **Perry Education Association v. Perry Local Educators' Association, 460 U.S. 37, and Ward v. Rock Against Racism, 491 U.S. 781:** Their traditional-forum and neutral time, place and manner standards remain governing.
- **Thomas v. Chicago Park District:** Preserves lawful, constrained neutral administration; no particular permit rule is adjudicated here.
- **The Lee companion decisions:** Supply no controlling airport classification or combined rationale; their fractured judgments are not converted into authority for this sidewalk.

#### Law After Decision

A government-secured sidewalk that substitutes for and continues an existing public street route under an enforceable passage obligation retains traditional-public-forum protection despite private title. This rule applies to that defined public route; it does not make commercial premises generally governmental. Existing content and neutral-management standards govern restrictions on the passage.

#### Separate Writings

Justice Scalia dissents, joined by Justice Thomas. They would reverse and remand because governmental responsibility for this speech exclusion has not been established. Their framework requires attribution before forum classification: Lebron's concrete structural predicate and the limits emphasized in Brentwood do not allow public benefit, traffic regulation or access covenants alone to substitute for governmental control. The agreement expressly preserves present private rights and requires dedication if the present walkway is later removed, altered or abandoned. In their view, that distinction shows that the State secured passage and safety while withholding a present public speech entitlement. The fact that the walkway replaces a former public route makes the contrary argument substantial, but does not supply control over the disputed exclusion. They do not independently classify the forum after rejecting attribution. Their proposed remand would permit ordinary disposition under that attribution rule, rather than direct arrests, award damages or resolve a taking.

[Venetian Casino Resort, L.L.C. v. #### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

The Ninth Circuit judgment remains in force. Lawful neutral regulation may address actual obstruction and safety under the governing public-forum standards. The decision concerns this replacement boulevard route and extends no access order to the casino interior or unrelated private property.

### Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition, No. 00-795

Merits decision, April 16, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

535 U.S. 234. Argued October 30, 2001. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 198 F.3d 1083, in a pre-enforcement facial challenge. The questions are whether §§2256(8)(B) and (D), covering apparent minority and presentation as depicting minors, substantially burden protected expression and whether faithful constructions or the enacted defenses save them. The Ninth Circuit reversed summary judgment for the Government, invalidated the two definitions, and treated the remaining categories as severable. No conviction involving an identified abused child is under review.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Opposing Justices | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section 2256(8)(B), youthful-looking actual adults | Affirm invalidation, 8–1 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia | Relief against enforcement of the challenged application stands. |
| Section 2256(8)(B), wholly virtual realistic images | Affirm invalidation, 7–2 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | O'Connor, Scalia | Relief against enforcement of the challenged application stands. |
| Section 2256(8)(D), presentation-based definition | Affirm invalidation, 8–1 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Scalia | Relief against enforcement of the challenged application stands. |

The Ninth Circuit judgment invalidating the challenged definitions is affirmed. Relief against enforcement through §§2256(8)(B) and (D) stands; the separate actual-child and obscenity prohibitions and the unchallenged identifiable-minor provision retain their independent operation. Substantial overbreadth supplies the ground without a separate Fifth Amendment vagueness judgment.

#### Opinion Topology

GINSBURG, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER, and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment. O'CONNOR, J., filed an opinion concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part, in Part II of which SCALIA, J., joined. SCALIA, J., filed a dissenting opinion.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court | Ginsburg | The five Justices joining the Court's opinion, named above | Affirms all three components | Parts I and II and all operative statutory qualifications |
| Concurrence in the judgment | Thomas | — | Affirms all three components | Present enforcement showing insufficient; preserves different narrowly drawn future question |
| Concurrence in the judgment in part and dissent in part | O'Connor | Scalia in Part II only | Affirms youthful-adult (B) and (D); would sustain realistic wholly virtual (B) | Part I: separate invalidity grounds; Part II: virtual-image sustaining construction |
| Dissent | Scalia | — | Would reverse all three invalidations | Narrow conduct, apparent-age and knowing-pandering constructions |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**I. Apparent minority does not replace exploitation or obscenity.** A criminal definition substantially reaching nonobscene depictions produced without exploiting an actual child cannot be sustained merely because the depicted person appears to be a minor. Section 2256(8)(B)'s restriction fails the narrow tailoring required for this content-based burden on protected expression, including its wholly virtual realistic and actual-adult applications.

**Authority:** The six Justices joining Parts I and II of Justice Ginsburg's Opinion of the Court, named above, directly support both rules and the statutory qualifications below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that Ferber's exclusion rests on injuries to actual children in production and circulation; visual resemblance creates neither injury by itself. Miller's independent obscenity elements remain necessary to that category. Reno and Playboy require the Government to justify this protected-speech burden, while Broadrick requires substantial protected reach relative to legitimate applications. The asserted grooming, demand and proof difficulties are serious interests, but they do not establish the fit required for this prohibition. A lawful image's possible misuse cannot itself supply production abuse, and difficulty proving an actual child does not permit suppression of substantial protected substitutes. A realistic-image construction does not cure that defect or supply the absent serious-value protection. Actual exploitation, obscenity and independently unlawful solicitation retain their own predicates.

**II. Presentation does not transform a protected work into contraband.** Section 2256(8)(D) is independently substantially overbroad because promotional presentation can make a nonobscene, nonexploitative work prohibited material in connected distribution and possession offenses, including for persons unconnected with the presentation. A court may not save this definition by rewriting it as a separately defined intentional unlawful-offer offense.

**Authority:** The same six directly join Part II and this independent invalidity ground.

**Controlling explanation:** Ginzburg makes pandering evidence relevant within an obscenity inquiry; it does not automatically remove protection from the underlying nonobscene work. The enacted definition is broader than the Government's proposed knowing-promoter construction, and ordinary scienter does not confine all connected liability to the original promoter. Reno permits genuine severance but not invention of absent offense limits. A differently enacted unlawful-offer offense lies outside the question, as does the validity of the identifiable-actual-minor definition in (C).

**Operative statutory qualifications.** A minor is under eighteen. Sexually explicit conduct covers actual or simulated genital-genital, oral-genital, anal-genital or oral-anal intercourse, whether between persons of the same or opposite sex, bestiality, masturbation, sadistic or masochistic abuse and lascivious exhibition of the genitals or pubic area; mere nudity does not itself establish the last category. Section 2252A(c) applies only to (a)(1)–(4), requires actual adult performers at production, and requires that the defendant did not advertise, promote, present, describe or distribute the work as depicting minors. It excludes wholly generated images and ordinary possession under (a)(5). Section 2252A(d), separately, concerns possession of fewer than three images: prompt, good-faith reasonable destruction steps or reporting the images to law enforcement and affording access to each image, without retaining any image or copy or permitting anyone other than law enforcement access to one. It requires reasonable destruction steps, not successful destruction, and provides no right to retain protected expression. The unchallenged (C) definition concerns a recognizable actual person who was a minor at the relevant creation, adaptation or modification or whose image as a minor was used; proof of the person's actual identity is unnecessary. These provisions do not cure the challenged protected reach.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **New York v. Ferber, 458 U.S. 747, and Osborne v. Ohio, 495 U.S. 103:** Their actual-child injury predicates are preserved; they are not extended to an invented image or an adult performance solely because it resembles a child.
- **Miller v. California, 413 U.S. 15:** Obscenity remains an independent category with its own elements, not a substitute apparent-age rule.
- **Reno v. American Civil Liberties Union:** Its protected-adult-expression and substantial-overbreadth reasoning applies; its distinction between genuine severance and judicial rewriting defeats the proposed new offense limits.
- **United States v. Playboy Entertainment Group, Inc.:** Its content-based scrutiny and governmental justification burden govern the fit of the challenged criminal restrictions.
- **United States v. X-Citement Video, Inc.:** Knowledge of depiction characteristics remains distinct from constitutional authority to suppress those characteristics; this case changes no surviving factual-knowledge requirement.
- **Broadrick v. Oklahoma, 413 U.S. 601:** Substantial protected reach, rather than a conceivable unusual application, supports facial relief here.
- **Ginzburg v. United States, 383 U.S. 463:** Pandering may inform an obscenity inquiry; it does not independently make a protected nonobscene work contraband for all possessors.

#### Law After Decision

The two challenged definitions cannot support enforcement against the substantial protected expression they cover. Apparent minority without exploitation or obscenity does not suffice, and promotional characterization does not automatically transform a protected work into prohibited material. Independent actual-child, obscenity and unlawful-conduct rules retain their predicates. The identifiable-actual-minor category and a differently enacted unlawful-offer offense are outside this decision.

#### Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring in the judgment.** Thomas agrees with both invalidations but does not join the Court's principal reasoning. Enforcement of actual-child prohibitions is the Government's strongest concern. The present predictive showing does not establish a technological proof obstacle sufficient for this breadth, but practical impossibility of distinguishing fabricated from actual-child images could present a different constitutional question. An appropriate affirmative defense or another narrowly drawn restriction might matter on that demonstrated premise. He neither requires a particular future defense nor approves shifting the actual-child element through a current presumption. His agreement in judgment does not endorse a categorical foreclosure of every enforcement-based restriction affecting protected expression.

**O'Connor, concurring in the judgment in part and dissenting in part; Scalia joins Part II of her writing only.** In Part I she agrees with invalidation of the youthful-adult application: adult performers' protected expression remains exposed because the limited adult defense does not reach ordinary possession. She separately agrees that (D)'s presentation category lacks an adequately justified compelling fit, particularly when describing adult performers as minors removes the defense. In Part II she would preserve (B) for wholly virtual images virtually indistinguishable from actual-child images. The narrow construction excludes cartoons, sculptures and plainly unreal depictions. Congress may respond to supported enforcement predictions before actual proof failure becomes universal; in her view the child-protection interest and limited application satisfy the constitutional inquiry. Broadrick and application-severability principles permit preserving that application without extending the adult defense to possession. Her vagueness conclusion is confined to the realistic-image construction. Scalia joins only this virtual-image sustaining ground, not her adult or presentation invalidations.

**Scalia, dissenting.** Scalia would sustain both definitions under narrowing constructions. The statutory conduct requirement reaches actual or realistically simulated hardcore sexual acts, not merely a film's subject matter, and apparent minority means realistic images virtually indistinguishable from depictions of actual children. He adopts O'Connor's narrow virtual-image enforcement rationale but does not accept her adult-application invalidation. For (D), he reads offense scienter together with the definition to confine liability to knowing pandering by the responsible actor, excluding innocent downstream possession. Ginzburg supports the relevance of deliberate presentation to this confined transaction in his view. The Court regards that reading as alteration of a definition used in connected offenses and finds the remaining protected reach substantial. Scalia does not treat all nonobscene adult expression as unprotected or enlarge §2252A(c)'s defense.

### United States v. Craft, No. 00-1831

Merits decision, April 17, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

535 U.S. 274. Argued January 14, 2002. On certiorari to the Sixth Circuit, 233 F.3d 358, in the second appeal of an escrow dispute. The question is whether the taxpayer husband's pretransfer Michigan entireties rights constitute property or rights to property under 26 U.S.C. §6321. After assessment and lien notice, the spouses conveyed the estate to the wife; a later sale placed half the net proceeds in escrow by agreement. The circuit adhered to its first appeal under circuit precedent and law-of-the-case constraints, while rejecting the wife's cross-appeal. Attachment, valuation, priority and enforcement remain distinct; the alternative fraudulent-transfer theory is outside the reviewed question.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Reversed and remanded, 6–3.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Stevens, Scalia and Thomas.

The federal lien reaches the husband's qualifying pretransfer rights. The lower court must determine their value and the lawful consequence for the escrow, preserving the wife's independent rights and applicable priority and enforcement protections. The agreed escrow percentage is not an adjudicated valuation; the Court prescribes no automatic half recovery.

#### Opinion Topology

BREYER, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, KENNEDY, SOUTER, and GINSBURG, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which STEVENS and SCALIA, JJ., joined. SCALIA, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which THOMAS, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**The actual entireties rights are federal rights to property.** After demand and nonpayment, §6321 reaches the taxpayer's existing Michigan entireties rights considered together—present use, exclusion, enjoyment of income, survivorship and participation in joint disposition—even though the whole estate cannot be conveyed or partitioned unilaterally and is shielded from ordinary separate creditors. The lien reaches those taxpayer rights and creates no ownership of the wife's independent rights; their value and lawful collection consequences require separate determination.

**Authority:** The six Justices joining Justice Breyer's Opinion of the Court, named above, directly adopt the attachment rule and all remedial limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Drye assigns definition of the rights to state law and their federal lien classification to federal law. Michigan's actual beneficial incidents remain valuable legal advantages despite consensual transfer restrictions. The contrary argument correctly begins with property belonging to the taxpayer, but incorrectly makes unilateral withdrawal or monetization indispensable without statutory text. National Bank of Commerce rejects the proposition that local creditor limitations alone settle federal reach. Bess separates the particular reachable economic right from other interests in the arrangement. No single incident, bare future expectancy or unified state label controls every case. Rodgers concerns enforcement and nondebtor protections, not automatic collection of a half share; Romani preserves specific priority rules. Attachment precedes the conveyance to the wife, while the escrow agreement's half share supplies no adjudicated valuation.

Remand must determine value and lawful treatment of the escrow while preserving the wife's independent interests, applicable priorities and enforcement protections. The excluded fraudulent-transfer theory remains outside this review; the Court does not reopen the unrelated cross-appeal merely by reversing the attachment premise. The former equal-share calculation and later $6,693 enhancement remedy are not adopted as the attachment value.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Drye v. United States:** Its state-definition/federal-classification method governs the actual present rights; its unilateral inheritance power is an application, not an exclusive condition silently added to §6321.
- **United States v. National Bank of Commerce, 472 U.S. 713:** Applied to distinguish state creditor limitations from federal lien classification.
- **United States v. Bess, 357 U.S. 51:** Applied to identify the particular reachable economic interest without transferring other interests in the arrangement.
- **United States v. Rodgers, 461 U.S. 677:** Enforcement and nondebtor protections retain their separate statutory requirements; attachment supplies neither an automatic sale nor a half-value allocation.
- **United States v. Estate of Romani:** Its specific priority protections remain applicable on their own predicates after attachment.

#### Law After Decision

Michigan entireties restrictions do not defeat federal attachment to the taxpayer's existing beneficial rights considered together. The lien follows those actual rights, while their valuation, competing priorities, enforcement authority and the nondebtor spouse's independent interests retain separate legal work. The rule does not turn a bare future expectancy into property or assume identical marital-property law in every State.

#### Separate Writings

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Stevens and Scalia.** Section 6321 reaches property belonging to the taxpayer; it cannot create a distinct individual estate from inseparable marital ownership. Michigan gives the husband neither unilateral withdrawal nor a power to sell, partition or redirect the estate. Drye and National Bank of Commerce involved those actual powers over value, and therefore do not settle this case. Joint enjoyment and a veto on conveyance are meaningful benefits, but remain incidents of the marital entity's ownership rather than separately owned assets. The longstanding collection treatment supports this statutory caution; it does not replace the inquiry. The dissent does not make a creditor-exemption label conclusive or declare all restricted, nontransferable interests unreachable. It would affirm the attachment ruling without deciding a different tenancy, completed severance or separate income right.

**Scalia, dissenting, joined by Thomas.** Entireties ownership also gives institutional security to the nondebtor spouse, including a spouse whose domestic contribution yields no separate income. Removing that protection through a new federal property classification counsels particular statutory caution. This additional ground neither finds that Mrs. Craft held that domestic role nor creates a general marital-assets exemption. Stevens joins the common statutory dissent, but not this additional writing.

[535 U.S. 274](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-535/pdf/USREPORTS-535-274.pdf), for the chronology and Michigan incidents. The sale escrow preserved the dispute; its half-share arrangement did not establish the value of the taxpayer's rights.

#### Mandate, Remedy, and Stage

Further proceedings determine valuation and lawful treatment of the escrow under the rights-specific attachment rule. The excluded transfer theory and the unrelated cross-appeal remain outside this review; the Court does not adopt either former lower-court remedial calculation as the attachment value.

### A&M Records, Inc. v. Napster, Inc.

Merits decision, April 23, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On full merits submission. Review of the Ninth Circuit judgment, 239 F.3d 1004, entered February 12, 2001, concerning the preliminary injunction entered July 26, 2000 and explained and slightly modified August 10, 2000. The Ninth Circuit affirmed in part, reversed in part and remanded. The questions concern the supported direct-infringement and secondary-liability predicates, sampling and space-shifting defenses, the Audio Home Recording Act and §512, the remaining owner-conduct and procedural objections, and the scope of preliminary relief. Napster's searchable directory facilitates music-file exchange; notices and account/index controls are established, but its servers do not possess users' MP3 contents. The review concerns the original restraint and its required correction, not a later replacement order.

#### Judgment

**The Ninth Circuit's mixed preliminary disposition is affirmed, and the case is remanded, 9–0.**

| Judgment component | Disposition and vote | Supporting Justices | Remedy or remand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supported preliminary infringement and secondary-liability predicates; asserted defenses and objections | Sustained, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | Findings remain preliminary; the $5 million security is sustained. |
| Scope of the original preliminary injunction | Requirement to replace the overbroad restraint affirmed; remanded, 9–0 | Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg, Breyer | The original July 26, 2000 restraint remains stayed until modification conforming to this judgment. |

The owners must supply usable identification of works and infringing files. The replacement order must fit the service's actual capabilities and applicable statutory limits. Further proceedings retain the distinction between preliminary findings and final liability.

#### Opinion Topology

STEVENS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, THOMAS, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The preliminary fair-use defenses fail under the integrated factors

**Holding and operative rule:** On this supported preliminary record, unrestricted exchange of complete protected creative recordings with unrelated users is unlikely to be fair use under §107. Nontransformative whole-work copying and supported substitution for existing and developing markets outweigh the claimed sampling or relocation purposes; artist-authorized and otherwise lawful files remain lawful.

**Authority:** Justice Stevens's Opinion of the Court is joined in full by all eight other Justices. All nine directly support each of Parts I–VII, including the independent contributory and vicarious grounds and the qualifications below.

**Controlling explanation:** Campbell requires purpose, nature, amount and market effect together, without a dispositive commerciality presumption. The findings include displaced college sales and injury to a developing licensed-download market; this is not merely loss of fees for every unlicensed use. The contrary survey was weighed, not excluded. A plan to buy music later does not erase the present redistribution. Sony's private home time shifting does not establish immunity for public exchanges, and the claimed relocation extends beyond one's private personal devices. The result holds even assuming the owners bear the preliminary burden of likely defeating fair use; the allocation of that burden across all preliminary cases is unnecessary to resolve. The prima facie direct-infringement showing was unchallenged on this appeal; findings remain preliminary.

##### II. Substantial lawful capability does not immunize specific ongoing assistance

**Holding and operative rule:** A service can incur contributory liability by knowingly providing material assistance to identified infringement, notwithstanding substantial noninfringing uses of its technology. Sony bars imputing the needed knowledge from capability alone; it does not immunize continuing materially assisting operation after specific knowledge.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The notices identified more than 12,000 infringing files, continued availability followed asserted account terminations, and the search and index functions materially assisted access. Those circumstances support likely knowledge and contribution. General knowledge that copyrighted music circulates does not establish unauthorized copying; lawful capability remains material and present infringement percentages are not an exclusive test. The rule requires no server access to MP3 contents the system does not possess.

##### III. Financial benefit and actual supervision independently support preliminary vicarious liability

**Holding and operative rule:** Vicarious liability separately requires a direct financial benefit from infringement and the right and practical ability to supervise it. Attraction of infringing recordings to the user base can satisfy the benefit element without direct transaction fees, while account exclusion and index control support supervision over the service's actual assistance; both elements are supported here at the preliminary stage.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** This is an independently sufficient preliminary ground, not a substitute for contributory knowledge. The architecture both supports limited control and restricts the remedy. A theoretical ability to close the entire service does not create a general duty to inspect inaccessible user files or guarantee prevention of unknown copying.

##### IV. The Audio Home Recording Act is confined to its defined coverage

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1008 does not immunize this general-purpose computer service's open redistribution merely because users characterize copying as noncommercial. Its consumer-recording protection depends on the defined devices, media and recording acts.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** Under §1001(5), a digital musical recording is a material object containing, in digital form, only fixed sounds and related, relatively minor material, statements or instructions, from which the sounds and material can be perceived, reproduced or communicated directly or with a device. Entirely spoken-word recordings, including speech accompanied by merely incidental sounds, and objects containing computer programs are excluded; instructions constituting the fixed sounds and incidental material or bringing about their perception, reproduction or communication remain permitted. A digital audio recording device must be of a type commonly distributed to individuals for their use, whether separate or part of another machine, have a digital recording function designed or marketed primarily to make private digital audio copied recordings, and be capable of doing so. Professional model products and dictation, answering or other audio equipment designed and marketed primarily to fix nonmusical sounds are excluded. The service cannot detach the exemption from those conditions. This disposes of the asserted exemption without classifying every future dedicated player.

##### V. Section 512 independently limits liability and relief

**Holding and operative rule:** Secondary-liability status does not itself defeat a §512 protection. The lower court must determine each invoked service category and condition independently and apply the corresponding injunction limits before imposing relief beyond them; failure under one category does not decide another.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts Part V and the complete operative conditions below.

**Controlling explanation:** The Court holds that the infringement theories and Congress's service protections perform separate work. Supported likely liability does not answer whether a particular transmission or location-tool function satisfies its statutory conditions. Nor does failure under one category establish another's failure. The contrary demand for immediate unrestricted policing would make the statute's separate notice, knowledge, control and injunction limits disappear before the necessary findings. The preliminary record permits continued consideration of fitted relief, while the court must establish the applicable classification and predicates before imposing relief exceeding a statutory protection. This is affirmative remand work under the governing rules, not a decision that every safe harbor is unavailable.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Secondary-liability status alone does not defeat a safe harbor. The functions in (a)–(d) must be tested separately, and failure under one does not establish failure under another. Subsection (a) concerns automatic user-initiated transmitting, routing or connecting through the provider's system without provider selection of material or recipients except an automatic response, with no content modification. Any intermediate copy must not be maintained in a manner ordinarily accessible to anyone other than anticipated recipients, and must not remain ordinarily accessible to those recipients longer than reasonably necessary for transmission, routing or connection. Subsection (d)'s location-tool protection requires absence of actual knowledge or apparent-infringement awareness, or expeditious removal/disablement upon obtaining it; no directly attributable financial benefit where the provider has the right and ability to control; and expeditious response to a qualifying notice identifying the reference or link sufficiently to locate it. Subsection (i) additionally requires adoption and reasonable implementation of a policy terminating repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances, with subscribers and account holders informed of that policy and accommodation without interference of standard technical measures. Such measures must arise from broad open, fair, voluntary multi-industry consensus, be reasonably and nondiscriminatorily available, and impose no substantial provider cost or system burden.

A qualifying notice must substantially identify the copyrighted work or representative site list, the material or location reference and sufficient location information, provide contact information, bear an authorized signature, state a good-faith belief that the use lacks authorization from owner, agent or law, and state that the information is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that the complainant is authorized to act for the owner of an allegedly infringed exclusive right. Under §512(c)(3)(B), incorporated by (d)(3), a notice that fails substantial compliance may not be considered in determining statutory actual knowledge or awareness of facts making infringement apparent. When a notice delivered to the designated agent substantially supplies the work, material or link location, and contact information but fails other elements, that exclusion applies only if the provider promptly attempts to contact the sender or takes other reasonable steps to assist receipt of a notice substantially satisfying all required elements. The preliminary notices support the underlying knowledge issue, but final satisfaction of each formal notice condition, service category and policy requirement remains for determination on remand.

Section 512(m) does not permit conditioning safe harbors on affirmative monitoring or searching for facts of infringement, apart from qualifying standard technical measures, or on access/removal forbidden by law. Failure to qualify is not affirmative evidence against an independent noninfringement defense under (l). Under (j), a provider protected from monetary relief still may receive only the permitted forms of injunction: for non-(a) conduct, specified-site access restraint, termination of specified infringing accounts, or other necessary relief addressing specified copyrighted material at a particular online location that is least burdensome among comparably effective measures; for (a) conduct, only termination of specified infringing accounts and reasonable steps blocking a specific identified foreign online location. The court must consider cumulative system burden, likely owner harm, technical feasibility and effectiveness, effects on access to lawful material elsewhere, and comparably effective less burdensome means. Notice and an opportunity to appear are required except evidence-preservation or other orders with no material adverse network effect. These limitations must govern if their predicates are satisfied; an unresolved classification is not permission to evade them through a generalized policing command.

##### VI. The remaining defenses and preliminary objections do not defeat fitted relief

**Holding and operative rule:** On this preliminary record, the owners' exploration of MP3 businesses does not establish intentional abandonment or permission for this service, and enforcing reproduction and distribution rights does not establish misuse extending copyright to unrelated property. No demonstrated abuse of discretion requires more discovery or an evidentiary hearing, the $5 million security is supported against the competing evidence, and no statutory basis or special circumstance requires a compulsory-royalty substitute.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The defense elements and record-specific review standard govern separately. Express objection to the service defeats the asserted inference of permission; toleration or business exploration alone is neither abandonment nor a license. Security need not equal the claimed enterprise value merely because that value is asserted. Copyright's remedies cannot be replaced with a new license solely for technological novelty. First Amendment interests protect lawful files and independent expression through the remedy's scope, without immunizing the identified infringement. Later materially supported defenses and security evidence remain matters for their own proceedings.

##### VII. The replacement injunction must connect usable notice to feasible service action

**Holding and operative rule:** Owners must identify protected works and sufficiently locate infringing files for practical response, and Napster must act within its actual index and account capabilities, including reasonable action against identified repeat infringers. The decree must preserve lawful files and authorized exchange, satisfy the remaining equitable predicates and any applicable §512 limits, and cannot make Napster guarantee prevention of every unidentified infringement.

**Authority:** The same unanimous opinion adopts this rule and its limits.

**Controlling explanation:** The original order shifted the full identification/prevention burden onto the service and exceeded the supported controls. Specific knowledge and practical contribution justify effective relief at their proved scope; they do not justify impossible filtering or a blanket technology prohibition. Remand is for a conforming preliminary decree under these rules. The original July 26, 2000 order remains stayed until conformity; neither a later replacement order nor final liability is assumed.

- **Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc.:** The integrated four-factor inquiry governs; commerciality alone creates no dispositive presumption, and concrete substitution is distinct from mere loss of a fee for unlicensed use. The preliminary outcome does not require resolving the general burden allocation.
- **Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, Inc., 464 U.S. 417:** Substantial noninfringing capability prevents imputed contributory liability from capability alone. - **New York Times Co. v. Tasini:** Its separation of actual statutory rights, use permissions and individualized remedy remains; it supplies neither a general digital-use exemption nor a compulsory license.
- **Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc.:** Its demand for a remedy fitted to the proved conduct informs preservation of independent lawful expression; infringement does not justify restraints on unrelated lawful files.

Specific knowing material assistance and separately established financial benefit with practical supervision can support preliminary secondary liability for an ongoing service despite substantial lawful capability. Fair use remains contextual; open whole-recording redistribution on this record receives no categorical sampling, relocation or device exemption. Congress's §512 categories and relief limits remain independently enforceable. ### Michigan v. Environmental Protection Agency

Merits decision, April 23, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On full merits submission. Review of the D.C. Circuit judgment, 213 F.3d 663, entered March 3, 2000, concerning EPA's October 27, 1998 NOx SIP Call. The three questions are whether highly cost-effective controls may inform required reductions after interstate contribution is established, whether statewide budgets unlawfully displace state means choices, and whether Congress sufficiently guides that authorized implementation. EPA first identified contributing States by the magnitude, frequency and relative modeled effects, then used controls estimated at no more than $2,000 per ton to derive reductions. This review concerns the one-hour ozone standard; the agency's eight-hour findings and the corresponding review were stayed. The circuit separately ordered geographic and notice corrections that remain outside these categorical questions.

#### Judgment

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. This implementation provision permits bounded consideration of cost

**Holding and operative rule:** After EPA establishes the relevant connection between upwind emissions and another State's nonattainment or interference with maintenance, §110(a)(2)(D)(i)(I) permits the availability of highly cost-effective controls to inform the emissions reduction required under that interstate implementation duty. Cost is permitted, not required; it cannot itself establish the interstate nexus, authorize unrelated reductions or replace the enacted air-quality objective.

**Authority:** The seven Justices joining Part I of the Chief Justice's Opinion of the Court, named above, directly adopt this statutory rule and its qualifications.

**Controlling explanation:** The evaluative term significant appears in an implementation assignment with no single enacted numerical cutoff. Congress assigned EPA binding plan-adequacy review, correction of interstate deficiencies and rulemaking under §301, while §110(a)(2)(A) recognizes diverse controls and economic techniques. Mead's actual-delegation threshold is therefore met, and Chevron permits a reasonable implementation choice within the remaining ambiguity; official endorsement or formal procedure alone would not suffice. EPA first identified interstate contribution through magnitude, frequency and relative modeled effects, then assessed highly cost-effective controls. The physical-contribution objection does not defeat that bounded implementation judgment because a demonstrated pollution nexus remains indispensable. Whitman excludes cost from §109 national-standard setting but preserves provision-specific implementation questions; it neither forbids all §110 cost considerations nor independently validates this benchmark. Union Electric's state-plan approval setting is distinct. MCI still forbids replacing a statutory command with administrative preference. The independent explanation of the exact $2,000 cutoff, particular modeling and surviving technical disputes lie outside these questions.

##### II. A lawful budget preserves state selection when real means remain

**Holding and operative rule:** A federally authorized statewide emissions budget does not unlawfully displace the States' control choices when actual options among sources and measures remain and the Act supplies lawful federal implementation instead of an unavoidable command to enact a prescribed state program. States need not adopt EPA's illustrative source mix or optional model trading program; the mandatory approval rule protects any submission meeting all applicable requirements.

**Authority:** All nine Justices join Part II of the Chief Justice's opinion at the lawful-budget level. Scalia and Thomas do not thereby approve cost-derived amounts they regard as unauthorized.

**Controlling explanation:** Train supplies initial state source-control choice, which can exceed the constitutional floor. The supported options include stationary or mobile measures, EPA's optional trading program, a state program or no trading. EPA and OTAG found VOC controls inadequate for this long-distance NOx transport problem; that is an attributed finding supporting this pollutant-specific result, not a statutory prohibition on all VOC measures. States can add them for associated local disbenefits. New York and Hodel distinguish federal regulation with a genuine alternative from compelled state lawmaking. Section 110(c)(1) requires a federal plan within two years of a missing/incomplete required submission finding or full/partial disapproval unless the State cures and obtains approval before promulgation. Those triggers are not newly found here. Under §110(k)(3), EPA must approve a compliant whole submission; it may approve a compliant portion and disapprove another, but the revision is not fully compliant until entirely approved. The constitutional alternative cannot erase the statute's greater means-choice protection.

##### III. Congress supplied the guidance; agency self-restraint supplies none

**Holding and operative rule:** The interstate provision constitutionally guides the authorized implementation judgment through identified pollutants, a demonstrated interstate relationship, downwind national-standard nonattainment or maintenance, significant-contribution limits and defined plan-adequacy/correction functions. EPA's present geographic restraint or chosen cost ceiling cannot cure a delegation lacking adequate congressional guidance.

**Authority:** The same seven Justices join Part III and directly support constitutional sufficiency and the no-agency-cure application. Scalia and Thomas do not reach this issue.

**Controlling explanation:** Whitman requires legislative criteria and independently rejects agency cure. The statute identifies the subject, purpose and limits; Congress need not select each numerical budget. Permitted cost analysis operates within that task after the nexus is shown, rather than allowing the agency to choose its own unrelated objective. Section 110(k)(5) requires the revision necessary to correct substantial inadequacy, public findings and state notice. EPA may establish a reasonable submission deadline no later than eighteen months after notice; appropriate original-plan requirements apply and dates may be adjusted, except an unelapsed Part D attainment date. Section 301 confers no authority to replace the statutory objective. An unexplained particular figure can fail ordinary review even though Congress's delegation is sufficient. The Court rejects the lower court's reliance on the confined exercise of power as an independent constitutional repair.

**Common statutory boundaries:** Section 110(a)(2)(D) requires adequate plan provisions consistent with Title I prohibiting emissions that significantly contribute to nonattainment in another State or interfere with maintenance by another State of the applicable national primary or secondary ambient air-quality standard. Significant contribution qualifies the nonattainment branch; the separate maintenance branch requires interference with maintenance. The adjacent significant-deterioration/visibility and §§115/126 compliance duties remain in their own settings and are not newly adjudicated here. Section 110(a)(2)(A) calls for enforceable limits and other means or techniques, including fees, marketable permits and emissions-right auctions, with necessary or appropriate schedules. Those methods do not independently authorize replacement of the statutory air-quality objective. Under §110(k)(5), correction follows substantial inadequacy to attain or maintain the relevant national standard, adequately mitigate the identified interstate transport or otherwise comply with the Act. To the extent EPA considers appropriate the finding subjects the State to requirements applicable when the deficient plan was developed and submitted; EPA may adjust applicable dates, but not an unelapsed Part D attainment date. The mandatory federal-plan, approval and notice rules stated above are not findings that any new trigger has occurred or new judicial deadlines.

**Review scope and remedy:** This judgment resolves the three categorical questions for the one-hour ozone standard. The eight-hour findings and their review remain stayed. The independent adequacy of the exact cost cutoff and particular unreviewed technical choices are outside the questions presented. The circuit's Wisconsin, Missouri and Georgia vacaturs/remands and its notice remands concerning the electric-generating-unit definition and the control level for large stationary internal-combustion engines remain intact. The sustained portions remain operative subject to those existing corrections; no additional agency remand is ordered on the three questions decided here.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Whitman v. American Trucking Associations, Inc.:** Section 109's exclusion of implementation cost remains; the distinct §110 implementation question is resolved on its own text. Congress's guidance requirement and the independent no-agency-cure rule govern the constitutional analysis.
- **United States v. Mead Corp. and Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc.:** Applied to actual delegated force and a reasonable resolution of genuine implementation ambiguity; formal procedure or institutional endorsement alone supplies neither authority nor validity.
- **Barnhart v. Walton:** Its formal delegated-choice ground confirms permission for reasonable implementation within an actual assignment; it creates no presumption authorizing every cost factor or power from longstanding practice.
- **Train v. Natural Resources Defense Council, Inc., 421 U.S. 60:** Its initial state source-control choice is applied; statutory choice remains independently protective even where the constitutional federal alternative exists.
- **Union Electric Co. v. EPA, 427 U.S. 246:** Its rejection of economic infeasibility as a basis for refusing a qualifying state plan does not decide this distinct federal interstate-adequacy question.
- **Arkansas v. Oklahoma:** Authorized federal interstate implementation and supported technical judgments retain their force; they do not supply authority apart from Congress's statutory assignment.
- **MCI Telecommunications Corp. v. American Telephone & Telegraph Co.:** Its prohibition on replacing a mandatory statutory task with agency policy remains binding.
- **AT&T Corp. v. Iowa Utilities Board and New York v. FERC:** Actual federal assignments, constraints on their exercise and preserved state functions remain distinct; neither decision supplies a general presumption of regulatory power.
- **New York v. United States; Hodel v. Virginia Surface Mining & Reclamation Assn., 452 U.S. 264; FERC v. Mississippi, 456 U.S. 742:** Their distinction between lawful federal regulation with genuine state alternatives and compulsory state administration applies. The constitutional floor does not erase greater statutory means-choice protections.

#### Law After Decision

Section 110 permits the bounded cost-informed implementation judgment after a demonstrated interstate pollution nexus. It neither mandates cost consideration nor generally imports cost into national-standard setting. States retain genuine selection among compliant source measures and programs, backed by the mandatory approval rule and a lawful federal-plan alternative. Congress supplied sufficient guidance for this assignment; administrative self-restraint cannot supply missing legislative limits. The one-hour review and the circuit's separate corrections define the judgment's reach.

#### Separate Writings

**Scalia, concurring in part and dissenting in part, joined by Thomas.** The enacted criterion concerns amounts of pollution contributing to another State's nonattainment or interfering with maintenance. Cheapness of removal measures expense, not that pollution relationship. Congress's express economic techniques permit ways of meeting an authorized result; they do not authorize EPA to redefine the result through cost. MCI and the limiting-criteria analysis in Iowa Utilities Board require those enacted words to constrain agency choice independently; Chevron supplies no power to replace them. Walton's acceptance of reasonable formal implementation does not help when the disputed choice falls outside the statutory criterion. The two would remand the affected cost determination for agency reconsideration under the pollution standard, without imposing a unique significance number or requiring elimination of every molecule of interstate emissions. They would not preserve the same amount merely by renaming its stage.

They join Part II because Train and the actual alternatives distinguish a lawful statewide result from compelled source selection. That join does not approve the challenged cost-derived amounts. Having resolved the rule on statutory grounds, they do not reach the constitutional sufficiency question. Their existing agreement that agency self-restraint cannot cure missing congressional guidance remains unaffected. The dissent preserves the separate geographic and notice corrections and chooses no judicial replacement budget.

### Tahoe-Sierra Preservation Council, Inc. v. Tahoe Regional Planning Agency, No. 00-1167

Merits decision, April 23, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

535 U.S. 302. Argued January 7, 2002. On certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, 216 F.3d 764, which rejected the categorical taking determination for two development moratoria. The question is whether the restrictions are compensable solely because they prevented all economic use during their thirty-two-month operation. The district court rejected the owners' Penn Central claim, and they did not appeal that ruling and expressly disavowed that theory. No physical occupation occurred. Later plans and judicial restraints have separate causal and procedural histories outside this question.

All nine Justices participate at argument and decision.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 7–2.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Kennedy, Souter, Ginsburg and Breyer.

**Opposing:** Scalia and Thomas, who would reverse and remand for the categorical inquiry and actual-period remedy.

Compensation is denied on the temporal-segmentation theory. The abandoned Penn Central ruling remains undisturbed; no remand revives it or determines compensation for separate later periods.

#### Opinion Topology

O'CONNOR, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., and STEVENS, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined. THOMAS, J., filed a dissenting opinion, in which SCALIA, J., joined.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

**A regulatory moratorium is not a categorical taking merely through temporal segmentation.** For a temporary development restriction without physical appropriation, an owner cannot establish Lucas's total-deprivation category merely by defining the relevant property as the exact time during which the restriction bars development. The continuing property interest must be assessed as such; economic impact, expectations, duration and governmental character remain subject to Penn Central when that claim is properly presented, and a temporary label supplies no immunity.

**Authority:** The seven Justices joining Justice O'Connor's Opinion of the Court, named above, directly support the rule, preservation limit and affirmance.

**Controlling explanation:** Lucas addresses complete deprivation of the legally relevant property without resolving every temporal denominator. Penn Central, Keystone and Andrus caution against manufacturing totality from a selected burdened strand. First English supplies compensation after a temporary taking is independently established, not the conclusion that every temporary prohibition is a taking. Physical occupation and appropriation of a leasehold are different governmental acts. The claimants' strongest objection—that present use has real value and serial temporary labels could evade compensation—is answered by preserving contextual review and refusing to make the label conclusive. No fixed thirty-two-month safe harbor, environmental exception or numerical duration limit follows. Palazzolo preserves the difference between meaningful remaining use and the distinct contextual inquiry. Here the owners abandoned the rejected Penn Central claim; categorical rejection therefore ends this reviewed claim without its revival.

The relevant restraints are Ordinance 81-5 beginning August 24, 1981, with case-by-case approval available for some single-family homes on Nevada-side Class 1–3 lots, but unavailable on stream-environment-zone lands or California-side parcels, and Resolution 83-21 from August 27, 1983 through April 25, 1984, initially ninety days and then extended. The categorical claim concerns their application to the relevant owners; the ordinance did not impose an identical prohibition on every parcel. Later plan and judicial-restraint periods have separate causal/procedural predicates outside review. No compensation follows on the preserved temporal-segmentation theory.

#### Precedent Treatment

- **Lucas v. South Carolina Coastal Council:** Its complete-deprivation category and genuine background-law exception remain. The Court rejects using the exact moratorium period alone as the relevant property for this regulatory claim; Lucas did not settle every temporal denominator.
- **Penn Central Transportation Co. v. New York City, 438 U.S. 104:** Its combined economic-impact, reasonable-expectations and governmental-character inquiry remains available for properly preserved claims, but this abandoned theory is not revived.
- **First English Evangelical Lutheran Church of Glendale v. County of Los Angeles, 482 U.S. 304:** Compensation is required for a temporary taking once independently established; it does not make every temporary development bar a taking.
- **Keystone Bituminous Coal Association v. DeBenedictis, 480 U.S. 470, and Andrus v. Allard, 444 U.S. 51:** Applied to the prohibition on manufacturing complete deprivation from a selected burdened strand of a continuing interest.
- **Palazzolo v. Rhode Island:** Its distinction between meaningful remaining ownership and the separate contextual inquiry remains. Neither regulatory notice nor a valid environmental purpose independently defeats compensation, and its unresolved general parcel question is not converted into a contrary temporal rule.

#### Law After Decision

A temporary regulatory development restraint does not become a Lucas taking solely by treating its precise duration as a separate property interest. Contextual review remains for properly presented claims, including examination of duration, actual impact, expectations and governmental character. Temporary status is no immunity, while compensation for an independently established temporary taking remains required. This preserved categorical claim is concluded.

#### Separate Writings

**Thomas, dissenting, joined by Scalia.** A complete suspension of economically beneficial use for a substantial defined period can take that present use even though ownership and future use remain afterward. Lucas protects the complete-deprivation category, and First English and Lucas's actual-period remedy distinguish whether a taking occurred from how long compensation runs. Later restoration limits compensation; it does not eliminate the earlier complete loss. The majority's whole-interest position recognizes future value, but in the dissent's view that value does not supply usable property during the defined prohibition.

The two would reverse and remand for a sustained complete-deprivation determination, genuine background property or nuisance limitations and compensation for an actual taking within the challenged thirty-two months. They impose neither a numerical minimum duration nor a rule covering every ordinary permit-processing interval. The case-by-case Nevada Class 1–3 single-family approval possibility, unavailable to stream-environment-zone lands and California parcels, requires attention to the owners actually covered. The State must substantiate an antecedent background rule independently prohibiting the use under ordinary proof requirements; a new legislative harm declaration is insufficient. The abandoned Penn Central theory is not restored, and later plans or judicial restraints cannot be attributed to the two enactments through an unproved six-year causal extension. Their remedy concerns the actual established taking period, not presumed permanent value.

### Universal City Studios, Inc. v. Corley

Merits decision, April 23, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

On full merits submission. Review of the Second Circuit judgment, 273 F.3d 429, entered November 28, 2001, affirming the August 23, 2000 permanent injunction after a nonjury trial. The questions are whether §1201(a)(2), applied to posting DeCSS and the proved purposeful linking, violates the First Amendment or Copyright Clause, and whether §1201(c)(1) supplies the asserted fair-use trafficking defense. DeCSS defeats effective DVD access protection and yields usable decrypted material; it is available as executable software and source expression. The actual linking restraint rests on separate knowledge, purpose and clear-and-convincing findings. No identified personal movie fair use or public-domain restraint is established.

#### Judgment

**Affirmed, 9–0.**

**Supporting:** Stone-Zsela, Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer.

The permanent restraint against posting the identified functioning tool and the specified knowing, purposeful linking remains enforceable within its terms. The linking restriction depends on clear and convincing findings of knowledge that the material is present, knowledge that it is unlawfully offered circumvention technology, and purpose to disseminate that technology.

#### Opinion Topology

THOMAS, J., delivered the opinion of the Court, in which STEVENS, O'CONNOR, SCALIA, KENNEDY, SOUTER, GINSBURG, and BREYER, JJ., joined, and in which STONE-ZSELA, C. J., joined as to Parts I–IV.

| Writing | Author | Joined by | Relationship to judgment | Scope joined |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion of the Court, Parts I–IV | Thomas | All eight other Justices | Affirms the posting and linking injunction | Trafficking and exceptions; code/function and posting; purposeful linking; concrete-record constitutional grounds |
| Opinion of the Court, Part V | Thomas | The seven Justices joining throughout, named above | Independent ground supporting the judgment | Limited-times preservation ground; Stone-Zsela withholds this part while joining Part IV's concrete-record ground |

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The separate trafficking prohibition has its own elements and exceptions

**Holding and operative rule:** Section 1201(a)(2) prohibits manufacture, importation, offering to the public, provision or other trafficking in a technology, product, service, device, component or part that is primarily designed or produced to circumvent effective access control over a work protected by the Copyright Act, has only limited commercially significant other purpose or use, or is marketed for that circumvention by the responsible person or a concerted actor with that person's knowledge; these are alternative predicates. Section 1201(c)(1) preserves infringement rights and defenses, including fair use, but does not supply a general defense to that independent trafficking prohibition.

**Authority:** Part I of Justice Thomas's Opinion of the Court is joined by all eight other Justices. All nine adopt the statutory rule, the application and the precise statutory limits below.

**Controlling explanation:** Effective control requires authorized information, process or treatment in ordinary operation; circumvention includes decryption or bypass without the owner's authority. The identified DeCSS posting meets the designed-circumvention ground, which suffices for this application. Separate infringement and trafficking provisions cannot be collapsed, and (c)(2) leaves contributory/vicarious infringement law separate. The class exemption under (a)(1) does not defend another provision under (a)(1)(E). The nonprofit acquisition permission likewise supplies no (a)(2) or (b) defense. The distinct exceptions retain their own conditions, stated below; this record establishes no entitlement to one.

**Operative statutory qualifications:** Under (a)(1), the Librarian's periodic rulemaking may identify a class whose users are or are likely during the succeeding three years to be adversely affected in their noninfringing uses by the individual access prohibition. The published class exemption protects those users as to that class for the ensuing three years; (a)(1)(E) prohibits using that exemption or rulemaking determination as a defense to other provisions, including trafficking. The nonprofit library, archives or educational-institution exemption permits access to a commercially exploited copyrighted work solely to determine in good faith whether to acquire a copy solely for conduct permitted by the Copyright Act, and only when an identical copy is not reasonably available in another form; retention must last no longer than necessary and the accessed copy cannot be used for another purpose. For a library or archives, its collections must be open to the public or available both to affiliated researchers and other persons researching a specialized field. It expressly supplies no (a)(2) or (b) trafficking defense. Reverse engineering under (f) concerns a lawfully obtained right to use a program and elements not previously readily available to the person and necessary for interoperability of an independently created program with other programs; identification and analysis must be for that sole purpose and noninfringing. Necessary tools may be developed and employed for that analysis or interoperability, and information or means may be shared solely to enable that interoperability, without infringement or other illegality. Encryption research under (g) identifies and analyzes flaws or vulnerabilities in encryption applied to copyrighted works to advance knowledge or assist development of encryption products. Qualifying good-faith circumvention requires lawful acquisition of the encrypted copy, phonorecord, performance or display of a published work, necessity for that research, a good-faith effort to obtain authorization before circumvention, and no infringement or other applicable-law violation. Tools may be developed and employed solely for the researcher's own qualifying research and provided to a collaborator for qualifying research or verification. Qualification factors include whether dissemination reasonably advances knowledge or technology development rather than facilitating infringement or other illegality; legitimate study, employment, training or experience in encryption; and whether, with what documentation, and when the researcher informs the copyright owner of the findings. Security testing under (j) requires the owner or operator's authorization and the sole purpose of good-faith testing, investigation or correction of a system flaw; the act must not infringe or violate other applicable law. Qualification factors include whether information is used solely to promote the owner's or operator's security or shared directly with the developer, and whether its use or maintenance avoids facilitating infringement or other illegality, including privacy or security violations. Those are statutory factors rather than categorical prerequisites. Developing, producing, distributing or employing tools is permitted solely for qualifying testing, provided the means do not otherwise violate (a)(2). The findings do not establish that these defendants meet one of those distinct permissions. Their existence is not a factual finding that every legitimate scientific use is accommodated, nor a license to decide unpresented constitutional applications.

##### II. The operative circumvention application is content neutral and adequately fitted

**Holding and operative rule:** Executable code can communicate ideas and perform an operation; its expressive character receives First Amendment protection without immunizing the demonstrated unauthorized access-circumvention function. Applied to the working DeCSS tool, §1201(a)(2) advances a substantial interest independent of suppressing expression without a substantially excessive speech burden, and the posting injunction burdens no more speech than necessary to remedy the demonstrated violation.

**Authority:** All nine join this part of Thomas's Opinion of the Court and its exact stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** Turner and O'Brien supply the statutory intermediate review; Madsen supplies the distinct injunction fit. The operation defeating effective access protection, rather than an encryption viewpoint or the films' messages, triggers this application. The tool's expressive value remains relevant. Restricting only completed infringement would not protect the distinct access interest Congress enacted. This reasoning does not depend on the proposition that all software is conduct, all scientific discussion is unprotected or a benign purpose conclusively establishes neutrality. The working-tool restraint, rather than abstract reporting, supplies the actual fit.

##### III. The proved knowing and purposeful linking satisfies this injunction's limits

**Holding and operative rule:** The linking restraint is sustained where clear and convincing findings establish that the responsible person knows the prohibited material is on the linked site, knows it is unlawfully offered circumvention technology, and creates or maintains the link for the purpose of disseminating it. Those findings connect this restraint to the demonstrated tool-delivery violation and exclude uninformed or merely referential links and mere reporting without the proved dissemination purpose; a reporting label does not exempt a link that satisfies all three findings.

**Authority:** All nine join this part of Thomas's Opinion of the Court and its exact stated scope.

**Controlling explanation:** A link communicates reference but can also deliver operational access. Barring posting alone while permitting knowing direct delivery for the same purpose would leave the established violation intact. Collateral protected material on a linked page is a real expressive cost, answered here by the actual purpose, knowledge and tailored scope rather than denied. The three safeguards and proof standard are sufficient for this decree; the Court does not prescribe universal constitutional minima or new statutory elements for all linking cases. The Government's reserved objection to the proof quantum requires no enlargement of this order.

##### IV. The presented broader fair-use and limited-times challenges do not defeat this application

**Holding and operative rule:** The defendants identify no personal movie fair use and provide scant evidence of interference with prospective fair users; the record likewise contains no allegation or evidence of a plaintiff withholding a public-domain work or the injunction blocking its copying. These record limits defeat the requested broader constitutional relief here without holding fair use constitutionally irrelevant or approving perpetual control of public-domain works.

**Authority:** All nine join Part IV and these concrete-record grounds.

**Controlling explanation:** A lawful use does not establish an entitlement to every preferred digital copying method on this undeveloped record. A concrete indispensable-lawful-use or public-domain application requires its own preserved facts. The actual injunction neither extends a copyright term nor resolves those distinct applications.

##### V. The limited-times contention independently fails preservation

**Holding and operative rule:** Presentation only in a footnote below did not adequately present the limited-times challenge for this merits review; this independent ground also supports declining the abstract claim.

**Authority:** Part V is joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Scalia, Kennedy, Souter, Thomas, Ginsburg and Breyer. Those eight adopt this independent preservation ground. Stone-Zsela joins the judgment and Part IV's concrete-record ground, but not Part V.

### Thompson v. Western States Medical Center, No. 01-344

Merits decision, April 29, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued February 26, 2002. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing its February 6, 2001 judgment, 238 F.3d 1090, holding the challenged compounding speech conditions unconstitutional and section 353a nonseverable. The question is whether conditioning the federal compounded-drug exemptions on the prescription-solicitation and particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion restrictions violates the First Amendment. The separate nonseverability ruling was not brought within review.

Licensed pharmacies prepared promotional materials for distribution by mail and at medical conferences. The challenge concerns truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding. Section 503A(a) permits the identified patient's prescription through unsolicited receipt or a prescribing practitioner's approved notation that a compounded product is necessary for that patient. The ordinary route requires compounding by a licensed pharmacist in a State-licensed pharmacy or Federal facility, or by a licensed physician, on the identified patient's prescription from a licensed physician or another practitioner authorized by State law to prescribe. The separate advance-compounding route permits a licensed pharmacist or physician to prepare limited quantities before receiving the patient's valid prescription, based on a history of receiving valid prescriptions for that drug generated solely within an established relationship between the compounder and either that identified patient or the physician or other practitioner who will write the prescription. Section 503A(c) prohibits particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion while permitting promotion of compounding services; the necessity notation does not exempt a message from subsection (c).

Section 503A supplies only exemptions from section 501(a)(2)(B)'s manufacturing-practice requirement, section 502(f)(1)'s adequate-directions requirement and section 505's new-drug-approval requirement. Bulk drug substances must comply with an applicable United States Pharmacopoeia or National Formulary monograph and the United States Pharmacopoeia compounding chapter; if no monograph exists, they must be components of approved drugs or, if neither condition is met, appear on the Secretary's regulatory list. They must be manufactured by a registered establishment, including a foreign establishment registered under section 510(i), and accompanied by valid certificates of analysis. Nonbulk ingredients must comply with an applicable monograph, if one exists, and the compounding chapter.

The compounder may not use a drug product on the Secretary's published list of products withdrawn or removed because the product or a component was found unsafe or ineffective. Regular compounding, or compounding in inordinate amounts as defined by the Secretary, of essentially commercially available copies is barred; a change for an identified patient producing a significant difference determined by the prescribing practitioner is excluded from that copy category. The product must not be one identified by regulation as presenting demonstrable compounding difficulties reasonably demonstrating an adverse effect on safety or effectiveness.

The interstate alternatives are compounding in a State that has entered a memorandum with the Secretary addressing inordinate interstate distribution and providing for appropriate State-agency investigation of complaints about compounded products distributed outside that State, or, absent that memorandum, out-of-State distribution no greater than five percent of the pharmacy's or physician's total prescription orders dispensed or distributed. The statute contains no enacted twenty-percent memorandum ceiling. PET compounded drugs and radiopharmaceuticals are excluded. Mixing, reconstitution and other acts performed according to the manufacturer's approved labeling and other directions consistent with that labeling lie outside the statutory compounding definition. These distinct provisions are not interchangeable exceptions to the challenged promotion rule.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### The challenged conditions burden truthful promotion more broadly than their justification supports

**Holding and operative rule.** The prescription-solicitation and particular-drug, drug-class and drug-type promotion conditions of section 503A violate the First Amendment as applied to truthful, nonmisleading promotion of otherwise lawful compounding because the Government has not established a reasonable fit between their informational burden and its asserted distinction between individualized compounding and manufacturing subject to approval. The holding applies the ordinary commercial-speech framework; it neither requires the least restrictive imaginable measure nor prevents restrictions on misleading claims, unlawful transactions, actual manufacturing conduct or narrower commercial-speech measures supported by an adequate justification.

**Authority.** The six Justices joining Justice Scalia's Opinion of the Court, named above, adopt the complete fit holding, statutory explanation and affirmance. Thomas accepts this sufficient application without endorsing the framework as the full measure of constitutional protection. This is a directly shared rationale, not an aggregation of distinct advancement findings.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that calling drug-specific silence a condition of an exemption does not answer the First Amendment question. Central Hudson and Fox require a proportionate relationship between a lawful commercial-speech burden and the substantial interest it serves. The Court assumes the Government's strongest combined account of drug safety, approval integrity, individualized access and material advancement. The defect is fit.

Product information can enable clinicians and patients to locate an individualized preparation without establishing manufacture for a broad market. Section 503A(a) allows the identified patient's prescription through unsolicited receipt or a prescribing practitioner's approved notation of necessity; its separate advance-compounding route permits a licensed pharmacist or physician to prepare limited quantities before receiving the patient's valid prescription, based on a history of receiving valid prescriptions for that drug generated solely within an established relationship with either that identified patient or the practitioner who will prescribe it. Section 503A(c), however, separately bars promotion of a particular drug, drug class or drug type while allowing service promotion. The necessity notation is not an exception to that separate speech prohibition.

Production, distribution, patient-prescription and commercial-copy controls bear directly on the conduct Congress seeks to distinguish. The Court recognizes the contrary concern: quantity limits can exclude medically needed preparations and may not address every demand-driven use of unapproved drugs. That does not explain why useful truthful product information must carry this broad burden. The Government has not adequately justified this proxy against the available conduct distinctions. The Court selects no production cap and finds no guarantee that every proposed alternative is equally effective. As 44 Liquormart and Lorillard require, the actual speech burden remains a distinct inquiry even when the regulatory objective and some preventive effect are accepted.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Central Hudson Gas & Electric Corp. v. Public Service Commission and Board of Trustees v. Fox: applied for ordinary commercial-speech scrutiny and proportional fit; no new tier or least-restrictive-means rule is adopted.
- 44 Liquormart, Inc. v. Rhode Island: applied for independent examination of the burden on truthful information; its broader separate theories are not converted into a controlling categorical ban.
- Lorillard Tobacco Co. v. Reilly: applied for separate advancement and fit inquiries and attention to the actual lawful-speech burden; this decision rests on fit and assumes the Government's advancement account.
- Edenfield v. Fane: applied for justification responsive to the actual professional setting; the physician audience is part of the covered speech burden.
- Greater New Orleans Broadcasting Association v. United States: applied for practical examination of the fit between a selective speech restriction and the regulatory objective; no conclusion that all compounded products are safe follows.
- Lanphere & Urbaniak v. Colorado: its benefit-condition principle remains applicable at its actual scope; selecting speech as the condition does not eliminate ordinary constitutional scrutiny.

#### Law After Decision

The reviewed compounding speech conditions fail the existing reasonable-fit requirement. The Court establishes no independent failure of material advancement, categorical commercial-speech immunity or replacement compounding program. The Ninth Circuit's unreviewed nonseverability determination remains part of the affirmed judgment. Substantive drug-safety and manufacturing authority retains its independent scope.

#### Separate Writings

**Thomas, concurring.** He joins the full fit holding because these restrictions fail even the lesser Central Hudson test. He separately regards suppression of accurate product information to prevent its effect on willing listeners as the mechanism his 44 Liquormart position rejects. Drug safety is legitimate, but that objective does not itself justify withholding accurate information; direct drug controls remain distinct. His concurrence is not judgment-only agreement and does not supply the Court with a broader categorical rule.

**Breyer, dissenting in part, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg.** The dissent would reverse the constitutional invalidation insofar as it rests on promotion to consumers. The statutory balance preserves medically needed preparations while preventing cumulative risks from a growing market of products that have not undergone approval. Product promotion can generate demand apart from a clinician's prior individualized assessment. Production caps, wholesale restrictions and controls on equipment or advance preparation may either exclude needed treatment or fail to address that demand. General service advertising and continuing physician involvement preserve meaningful channels. Under ordinary reasonable-fit review, the dissent therefore finds the consumer restriction proportionate despite its burden on truthful information. It reserves the distinct question of physician-directed promotion because that issue requires separate consideration; it neither sustains every communication restriction nor announces a benefit-condition exception to the First Amendment.

Shalala, 238 F.3d 1090 (9th Cir. 2001); [535 U.S. 357](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-535/pdf/USREPORTS-535-357.pdf). The Court's constitutional review does not extend to the circuit's separately unreviewed nonseverability determination. Neither the statutory exemption nor the judicial record establishes that compounded drugs are generally safe.

### City of Los Angeles v. Alameda Books, Inc., No. 00-799

Merits decision, May 13, 2002

#### Chronology and Posture

Argued December 4, 2001. On writ of certiorari to the Ninth Circuit, reviewing its judgment, 222 F.3d 719, filed July 27, 2000 and amended August 28, 2000, sustaining summary judgment, a declaration and an injunction for the businesses. The question is whether the City's concentration evidence can support the asserted noncommunicative harm underlying its combined-use prohibition. The district court's preserved expressive-targeting ground remains distinct; the court of appeals assumed neutrality and held the evidence insufficient.

Los Angeles's 1977 study linked concentrations of adult establishments to neighborhood crime but counted bookstore/arcade combinations as single establishments. Each plaintiff's retail and viewing operations occupies under 3,000 square feet, with one entrance and one supervising employee. The premises are physically undivided and the revenues are distinguished only internally. Neither plaintiff violates the separate one-thousand-foot adult-business or five-hundred-foot school, park or religious-institution distance rule. Those facts do not by themselves establish the actual effects of patron concentration.

Section 12.70(C) separately prohibits establishing or maintaining multiple adult businesses in the same building, structure or portion, and increasing an adult business's floor area in premises containing another adult business. Each defined adult-business category counts separately even when operated in one commercial enterprise. The combined-use amendment allowed combinations to continue until March 10, 1985, except a business already required to discontinue under the earlier transfer provision. That earlier provision required discontinuance within five years of a transfer allowed during the original ordinance's two-year transfer period if the business remained within the prohibited adult-business or school, park or religious-institution distance. After March 10, 1985, pre-September 1, 1978 uses could continue; if none qualified, one use could continue, with the earliest established taking priority in a dispute.

Continuation to March 10, 1987 could be granted for a written lease made before March 10, 1982 and extending beyond March 10, 1985, or undue financial hardship from pre-March 10, 1982 investment in real property, improvements or stock. An uncorrected violation of the governing zoning chapter after notice barred continuation. The verified application required prescribed supporting information, without a fee; a decision was due within seventy-five days unless the applicant and administrator agreed to an extension. A hearing could be set if necessary. The determination could not take effect until fifteen days after a copy of the written determination was mailed to the applicant; a timely appeal stayed proceedings on the challenged action pending determination. These terms establish no qualification of either plaintiff.

The separate exception for a site within five hundred feet of an A or R zone requires that no compliant site be reasonably available elsewhere. A reasonably available site must satisfy applicable zoning, be available for use, purchase or rent, have adequate street access, lighting and sidewalks, and be at least five hundred feet from uses that are or may become obnoxious or offensive through odor, dust, smoke, noise, gas, fumes, cinders, refuse or water-carried waste. Massage parlors and sexual-encounter establishments are excluded. The applicant must identify its current or proposed site and supply supporting data and the fee. A hearing and decision are required within sixty days; approval is required when the conditions are met, and denial requires findings identifying the deficiency. An appeal must be decided within thirty days after expiration of the appeal period. This is a residential-zone exception, not an exception to every combined-use prohibition.

#### Controlling Propositions and Authority

##### I. The concentration study supports the limited inference necessary to defeat this evidentiary summary-judgment ground

**Holding and operative rule.** The City's neighborhood-concentration study and reasonable inferences from it suffice to defeat the businesses' summary-judgment theory that the combined-use amendment necessarily lacks a relevant evidentiary basis because the study counted a bookstore and arcade combination as one establishment. A city may rely on reasonably relevant concentration evidence without separately studying every internal building configuration, but that initial sufficiency does not establish neutrality, final efficacy, adequate fit or constitutional validity.

**Authority.** The five Justices joining Part I of the Chief Justice's Opinion of the Court, named above, directly support this confined evidentiary proposition. Kennedy's additional speech-preservation ground is separately stated; it does not create agreement with every broader secondary-effects formulation.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the studied concentration of adult operations can support an inference about their concentration within one building. Renton permits reliance on evidence reasonably believed relevant to the noncommunicative harm addressed; it does not demand architectural replication. Turner preserves substantial respect for supported prediction while requiring independent judicial examination of the inference.

The study counted combined bookstore and arcade premises as one unit, and the plaintiffs occupy small, physically undivided premises. Those facts support a serious challenge to the City's theory, but they do not make the inference legally unavailable. Concentrating distinct operations can concentrate activity relevant to the identified neighborhood harm even without a separate exterior wall. The City need not prove that concentration is crime's exclusive cause or conclusively establish the ordinance's success at this initial stage.

The court of appeals did not announce an absolute new-study requirement; it rejected this inference. The Court disagrees with that conclusion on the asserted evidentiary ground. The businesses may still show that the evidence fails to support the City's actual rationale or supply contrary evidence and practical reasoning. A material challenge requires renewed support from the City, which retains the ultimate justification burden. No presumption that expression causes harm follows. The separate question whether compelled division alleviates the demonstrated harm without impermissibly burdening protected expression remains governed by the scrutiny applicable to the ordinance's actual justification and operation.

##### II. The preserved targeting and fit grounds require adjudication before the ordinance can be upheld

**Holding and operative rule.** On remand, the courts must examine the preserved expressive-targeting ground and the ordinance's actual advancement, fit and access to protected expression under the scrutiny its operation and justification warrant. Under City of Erie, a regulation whose actual justification and operation principally target communicative adult entertainment cannot receive incidental-burden treatment merely because the City describes its concern as secondary effects; the City's initial evidentiary showing does not discharge its ultimate constitutional burden.

**Authority.** The same five Justices join Part II of the Court's opinion, adopting this remand rule and its nonvalidating consequence. It applies the directly controlling Erie targeting boundary without adopting Erie's separate nonmajority strict-scrutiny account or its independent evidence analysis as a whole.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that evidentiary relevance and constitutional classification do different work. The court of appeals assumed neutrality and therefore did not resolve the district court's preserved targeting ground. Erie requires attention to actual operation and justification. O'Brien supplies the requirement of a governmental interest unrelated to suppressing expression for incidental-burden treatment; Renton preserves genuine land-use regulation of noncommunicative harms, subject to adequate alternative avenues. If the actual classification requires stricter review, the Government must satisfy that review rather than borrow a more permissive label.

The businesses may defend the existing decree on the preserved ground without seeking an enlarged decree, as El Paso Natural Gas permits. Recognizing an initial concentration inference therefore cannot itself remove that ground or determine final validity. The causal support, the burden of separating these operations and alternative expressive opportunities require adjudication on the actual record. Ordinary evidence and reasoning can test the City's predictive account; no mandatory new local study or new hearing is created. The City must justify its response under the applicable standard, and the courts must promptly reconsider the existing injunction consistently with that inquiry.

The dissent would preserve the businesses' declaration and injunction on this sufficient evidentiary ground.

**Souter, dissenting, Part I, joined by Stevens and Ginsburg only.** Content-correlated regulation requires independent attention to actual effects and evidentiary safeguards against disguised suppression. Lindh excludes the ordinary chapter 153 amendments at issue from this pre-enactment noncapital application. The later standard for unreasonable state adjudications therefore cannot decide this claim. Miller v. Fenton supplies the distinction between respect for supported facts and independent evaluation of the constitutional legal question.

The State invokes Teague to demand acceptance of reasonable state interpretations even in this earlier proceeding. Nonretroactivity performs different work. It bars a newly announced constitutional rule from supplying collateral relief unless an applicable exception permits it; it is not a general replacement standard for legal review. The published record does not establish a separately developed defense identifying some other specific new rule or an independent waiver of such a defense. The Court must nevertheless determine whether the relief it grants follows from finality-era law. It makes that determination below rather than treating rejection of the State's proposed deference rule as abolition of Teague.

##### II. Existing law dictates the sentence-specific result

**Holding and operative rule.** The finding of gross disproportionality for this first conviction, this exceptionally small completed sale and life imprisonment without ordinary parole applies the law already established by Rummel, Solem and Harmelin before Henderson's conviction became final. Relief therefore depends on no new categorical first-offender exemption, drug-weight ceiling or retroactivity exception.

**Authority.** Part II of Justice Souter's Opinion of the Court is joined by Stone-Zsela, Stevens, Ginsburg and Breyer. All five support the complete old-rule conclusion and its stated boundaries. The four Justices who reject relief do not join it merely because they accept independent pre-AEDPA review.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that identifying an old general principle is necessary but not sufficient: the result must follow from the earlier law at the relevant level of specificity. Henderson's direct-review affirmance occurred in 1995, after Rummel, Solem and Harmelin. No disputed exact subsequent finality date affects the availability of those precedents.

Solem already distinguished discretionary executive commutation from ordinary parole and required assessment of the actual offense and culpability. Rummel's repeated convictions and meaningful parole eligibility are absent. Harmelin preserves a narrow proportionality inquiry and sustained punishment for a quantity capable of extensive distribution; it does not make every retail sale equivalent to that large-scale offense. Together those settled distinctions require attention to the extraordinary combination present here. The comparisons corroborate the exceptional mismatch; they create no new sentencing schedule.

The contrary position would give legislative latitude conclusive force despite the surviving threshold. The Court gives that latitude substantial weight, but it cannot erase the actual offense or convert discretionary grace into parole. Neither disagreement about the application nor the absence of a prior case involving precisely 0.238 grams makes the result new. Graham's distinction between applying a dictated protection and extending it supports the inquiry; its capital-mitigation holding supplies no imprisonment rule. The Court changes no threshold and grants relief only for the existing constitutional violation.

##### III. Permanent imprisonment is grossly disproportionate on this offense and offender record

**Holding and operative rule.** A life sentence with no ordinary parole eligibility is grossly disproportionate for Henderson's first conviction of delivering 0.238 grams of cocaine base on the established record. The rule reaches materially equivalent first-offender conduct and punishment; it neither equates sale with possession nor invalidates every life sentence for a drug offense or fixes a maximum lawful replacement term.

**Authority.** The same five Justices join Part III of the Court's opinion, directly supporting this sentence-specific application and the conditional sentence-only remedy. The Court does not derive a new imprisonment formula from the distinct law of monetary forfeitures.

**Controlling explanation.** The Court holds that the severity of the punishment must be compared with this offense and offender. Solem supplies that individualized comparison and the distinction between parole and executive grace. Harmelin's narrow proportionality principle requires substantial legislative latitude and comparative inquiry only after the exceptional initial inference arises. Permanent imprisonment for this first conviction and tiny completed sale crosses that threshold.

The record concerns a completed twenty-dollar delivery, not innocent possession. It includes evidence of two additional rocks and interference with a witness. Even considering the additional rocks, it supplies no Harmelin-scale distribution predicate. The Eighth Circuit reports that the sentencing jury was not instructed to use the additional acts in selecting punishment. That is the appellate court's account of the charge; it establishes neither that the conduct did not occur nor that witness interference supplied the sentence's basis.

The comparisons confirm the mismatch at their supported scope. Arkansas authorizes life as a maximum for first-degree murder, rape and kidnapping. Later advisory guidelines do not govern Henderson; their three-and-one-half-year suggestion remains subject to a ten-year statutory minimum and permits aggravating departures, including for witness interference. Other-State evidence distinguishes authorizing life from actually imposing it and retains the uncertainty about Idaho parole. The reported federal ranges—ten to sixteen months, or fifteen to twenty-one with an obstruction enhancement—corroborate disparity without dictating a constitutional term. No complete nationwide survey or numerical ratio decides the case.

The State rightly emphasizes distribution's grave social harms. Those harms justify severe punishment, but they cannot substitute a hypothetical violent enterprise, recidivism or the entire drug trade for this individual's proved conduct. Arkansas retains the task of selecting a lawful replacement sentence in a lawful sentencing proceeding.

#### Precedent Treatment

- Lindh v. Murphy: applied to exclude amended section 2254(d) from this pre-April 24, 1996 noncapital petition; no exemption from every AEDPA provision is announced.
- Miller v. Fenton: applied to separate independent constitutional legal review from the former treatment of supported facts.
- Teague v. Lane: applied as a distinct nonretroactivity limit, not a general deference standard; relief rests on an old-rule application, without reliance on an exception.
- Rummel v. Estelle: distinguished because its recidivism and meaningful parole predicates are absent.
- Solem v. Helm: applied for actual-offense proportionality, corroborative comparison and the difference between ordinary parole and discretionary grace.
- Harmelin v. Michigan: its surviving narrow gross-disproportionality principle governs; its wholesale-quantity application does not decide every completed drug sale.
- Graham v. Collins: applied only for the distinction between dictated application and extension of old law; its capital-mitigation result does not govern imprisonment duration.
- United States v. Bajakajian: remains confined to its fines framework and actual-offense application; its monetary proportionality rule is not converted into a prison-term formula.

#### Law After Decision

Under the existing narrow proportionality rule, permanent imprisonment for this first conviction and materially equivalent tiny-sale conduct is unconstitutional. The conviction remains intact. The law establishes neither a general drug-weight schedule nor the replacement term. Pre-AEDPA independent legal review and Teague's separate finality requirement retain their distinct operation.

#### Separate Writings

**Kennedy, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Parts II–III, joined by O'Connor throughout the separate writing and by Thomas in the assumed-framework application only.** The dissent would restore denial of the sentencing claim. The tiny quantity, first conviction and lack of ordinary parole make the sentence severe, but the completed distribution offense and supported record do not meet the extraordinarily demanding threshold under Harmelin. Legislative judgments can account for distribution's preventive and social harms without measuring sentence length arithmetically by weight. The comparison evidence does not convert the exceptional severity into a constitutional prohibition. The dissent acknowledges the real difference from Harmelin's quantity and treats clemency as discretionary grace; it assumes neither a large enterprise nor prior convictions. Thomas's agreement assumes narrow proportionality for this disposition and does not adopt its constitutional foundation. Merits denial makes a separately unestablished new-rule bar unnecessary to these positions.

**Scalia, concurring in Part I and dissenting from Parts II–III.** Harmelin's distinct textual and historical account rejects prison-duration proportionality as an independent requirement of the Cruel and Unusual Punishments Clause. The actual sentence and comparisons may present a sentencing-policy objection, but they do not establish the asserted constitutional right on his ground. His acceptance of the Excessive Fines inquiry in Bajakajian supplies no prison-duration rule. He accepts the controlling temporal law and does not rely on amended section 2254(d) or a fictitious criminal history. His rationale is distinct from the other dissenters' conclusion that this sentence passes a narrow proportionality test.

The Eighth Circuit judgment is affirmed.
