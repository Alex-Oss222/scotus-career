## B. LAW, APPLICATION, LAWFUL PATHS, AND UNCERTAINTY

### Completed application to all presented questions

**Record and posture correction.** The taxpayers are petitioners from a Tenth Circuit judgment holding the punitive damages taxable. Kelly O'Gilvie's refund action and the Government's erroneous-refund actions against the children were consolidated; Nos. 95-966 and 95-977 retain separate procedural questions. The children received their refund checks July 9, 1990, although the Government wrote them July 6; the Government sued July 9, 1992. In the husband's case the August 26, 1992 judgment was withdrawn, November 30, 1993 resolved the separate interest issue, and December 7, 1993 entered the operative judgment resolving the punitive-damages claim. The Government appealed February 1, 1994.

**Question 1—taxability.** Simulated *Commissioner v. Schleier* (June 14, 1995) supplies a five-Justice receipt-specific causation holding. Tort-type rights and receipt on account of personal injury are independent requirements. It assumed the first for the ADEA payments and held willfulness-based liquidated damages outside the second; it did not establish a general punitive-damages rule. The Kansas tort plainly supplies a different remedial setting, but its punitive award's solely punitive and deterrent character makes the causal inquiry material. The neutral merits choice is whether injury's but-for role suffices or the particular payment must compensate injury. Simulated *United States v. Burke* has no controlling statutory rationale under *Marks*: its fractured reversal of the identified old Title VII exclusion cannot be turned into a general remedial-range or physical-injury test. The applicable pre-amendment § 104(a)(2), § 61 baseline, and Kansas award character govern; later amendments are not applied retroactively.

**Question 2—erroneous-refund suit.** Sections 7405(b) and 6532(b) authorize recovery and impose two years after making the refund. The issue is whether mailing/writing or actual receipt fixes the start. Receipt places the July 9, 1992 action within two years; mailing would make it late on the children's account. No supplied simulated holding resolves that exact phrase. *United States v. Wurts* and ordinary mistaken-payment accrual provide relevant pre-divergence construction; convenience of a postmark is a counterargument, not a textual override. This issue is independent of taxability and must be answered rather than generically reserved as computational work.

**Question 3—Government appeal.** The then-applicable Rule 4(a)(1) supplies sixty days when the United States is a party. The controlling question is which order finally resolved the claim, not whether a later clerical amendment can automatically restart time. The withdrawn August judgment and incomplete November judgment support treating December 7 as operative, making February 1 timely. Simulated *Ibarra* concerns timely substantive reconsideration in a Government criminal appeal and does not itself create a civil tolling rule here. Simulated *Smith v. Barry* permits timely functional notice but does not excuse untimeliness; no substitute-notice issue is established. Apply actual civil finality to the documented orders before the merits.


## C. DECISION-MATERIAL LITIGATION POSITIONS

The taxpayers argue that the wrongful-death injury is the indispensable cause of the punitive payment and that the tort action meets the regulatory requirement. The Government answers that Schleier requires the particular award to compensate injury and that Kansas uses this component solely to punish and deter. Taxability and statutory causation must be decided without treating the later amendment as governing these tax years.

The children contend that the Government made the refund when it wrote or mailed the checks, placing the recovery suits outside two years. The Government treats receipt as the act that delivers the mistaken payment and starts the recovery period. In the father's appeal, the taxpayer invokes earlier judgment dates; the Government relies on withdrawal of the August judgment, the limited subject of the November ruling, and final resolution on December 7. No party's characterization replaces examination of the actual orders.

## D. LAWFUL PATHS AND UNCERTAINTY

A timely Government appeal permits adjudication of taxability; an untimely one requires the appropriate jurisdictional disposition regardless of the preferred tax interpretation. The children's recovery actions independently depend on the statutory start date. Rejecting both procedural challenges and treating the punitive awards as taxable supports affirmance; accepting a procedural challenge changes the relevant branch's judgment without necessarily deciding exclusion. A contrary reading of the exclusion would require the corresponding merits disposition for the correctly identified parties. No new tax amount, generic tolling rule or compulsory computational remand is established.
