## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Case Name:** Citizens for a Better Environment v. Steel Co.
- **Citation or Docket:** 90 F.3d 1237 (7th Cir. 1996); court-of-appeals No. 96-1136
- **October Term:** 1996
- **Procedural Category:** Simulation-assigned appellate merits
- **Lower-Court Event Date:** 1996-07-23; this is a source date, not an assigned simulation event.

- **Court and Judgment Under Review:** The Seventh Circuit reversed dismissal, construing EPCRA to allow citizen enforcement of missed reporting deadlines although forms were submitted before suit. Standing and jurisdictional sequencing in the current assignment require independent examination; the appellate statutory answer does not establish Article III.
- **Relief Requested:** A declaration of past violation; periodic access to the facility and records at the Company's expense; copies of future EPA compliance reports; $25,000 per day per reporting violation, payable to the Treasury; and investigation/prosecution expenses claimed under EPCRA's litigation-cost provision. No compensatory payment for lost timely information is authorized by the invoked penalty remedy.
- **Material Record:** CBE identified unfiled chemical-inventory and toxic-release reports for the Company's Chicago steel facility. It sent statutory notice to the Company, EPA and Illinois authorities on March 16, 1995. The Company then filed the overdue forms; EPA did not commence enforcement within the notice period. CBE sued August 7, 1995, with filings current. The District Court treated wholly past violations as outside the citizen-suit provision. The complaint identifies members living near or visiting the facility who use the information for chemical-risk assessment and emergency preparation. It alleges late reporting and resulting effects, but no continuing violation or imminent future violation. These existing complaint facts are confirmed by the later primary opinion's account, used here for record verification only; its holding is isolated in Section III.
- **Vehicle Limits:** The notice interval, filing-date standing, statutory cause, and each requested remedy remain separate. Later filing cannot be treated as cessation after an originally live suit: the forms were complete before this complaint. No recurrence allegation may be invented.

### Questions Presented

1. Whether the citizen-suit provision of the Emergency Planning and Community Right-to-Know Act authorizes private plaintiffs to sue for violations of statutory reporting deadlines when the defendant has submitted the required information before the action is commenced.
2. Whether an environmental organization and its members satisfy Article III's injury-in-fact requirement by alleging the deprivation of information that federal law requires regulated facilities to disclose.
3. Whether a plaintiff satisfies Article III's redressability requirement when the defendant has already corrected the reporting violation and the requested civil penalties would be paid to the United States Treasury rather than the plaintiff.
4. Whether a federal court may assume the existence of Article III jurisdiction to decide a disputed statutory cause-of-action question on the merits, or must establish jurisdiction before reaching that question.

## B. ANSWERS AND REASONING

**Question 4 — jurisdiction first.** Stone would require Article III before deciding the disputed statutory merits. His jurisdictional commitment concerns the Court's actual authority, not an intuition that the statutory question is easier. A substantial cause-of-action dispute is not automatically jurisdictional merely because the enforcing subsection also mentions courts' jurisdiction. He would distinguish a permissible threshold jurisdiction ruling from issuing a binding merits answer while assuming constitutional power.

**Question 2 — informational injury.** Stone would recognize deprivation of an individually held statutory informational right as the holder's injury where the law supplies a remedy; he would not demand an additional separate loss merely to relabel that invasion as concrete. Impaired actual use in protecting health, planning activity, or participating in a legally relevant process independently demonstrates the practical interest here. An interest in seeing regulations obeyed is insufficient. He would require CBE to identify the affected interests through litigation-stage-appropriate allegations or evidence, distinguishing a personal informational entitlement from abstract compliance. Lujan's actual majority limits govern the Court; any interpretation requiring extra injury beyond invasion of such an individually held right would conflict with Stone's expressly stated jurisdiction position, which he would identify as a proposed departure. This injury position does not supply redressability for a completed violation.

**Question 3 — redressability.** Treasury penalties do not redress a completed personal information loss merely by punishing a violator. Deterrence can matter to a shown continuing or imminent injury, but the all-reports-filed record supplies no automatic recurrence finding. Investigation and litigation costs incurred to establish a violation do not themselves manufacture jurisdiction over penalties. The verified complaint contains no continuing-violation or imminent-recurrence allegation. Future inspection and report-copy orders therefore do not repair the lost timeliness; a declaration does not resolve a live dispute over the acknowledged past default; the Treasury penalties do not compensate CBE; and the invoked cost-shifting provision does not create a separate compensatory claim for information-gathering losses. Stone would dismiss this complaint for lack of redressability, not remand to search for an injury that was not alleged.

**Question 1 — complete proposed statutory answer, conditional on jurisdiction.** If a live controversy is established, Stone would read EPCRA's reference to statutory reporting requirements to include timely submission, not submission at any convenient date. Its own wording differs from Gwaltney, and the 60-day notice provision supplies a chance to cure and for agency enforcement without expressly extinguishing every accrued violation. He would therefore reject a categorical statutory bar to past reporting defaults while enforcing notice, governmental-enforcement bars and legally authorized remedies. This is the user's approved conditional statutory position, not a merits holding Stone would issue in a jurisdictionally deficient case.

## A. CASE, RECORD, AND POSTURE

- **Citation or Docket:** 93 F.3d 861 (D.C. Cir. 1996); court-of-appeals No. 95-7077

Jane Doe, then a young child living with her mother's friend, allegedly remained unprotected after relatives reported neglect to assigned social workers. 2. Whether mandatory obligations imposed upon recipients of federal funding create enforceable rights for intended beneficiaries, or instead constitute conditions enforceable only through the federal government's supervision of the funding program.

**Provision-specific statutory enforcement.** Simulated *Suter v. Artist M.* rejects the particular §671(a)(15) reasonable-efforts and §671(a)(9) reporting rights asserted there, and separately rejects the implied causes of action. It does not make all funding or state-plan conditions unenforceable, does not treat administrative funding withdrawal as automatic foreclosure, and leaves other provisions for individual examination. Its remand of §671(a)(16) is not a holding that the right exists. Here CAPTA's then-applicable §5106a(b)(2) directs prompt initiation of an investigation after an abuse report and immediate protection following a finding. The question is whether that language creates an individual entitlement or requires a funded jurisdiction to establish a system.

Pre-divergence *Wright*, *Wilder*, and *Thiboutot* remain operative: identify the beneficiary, a binding sufficiently determinate obligation, and a cognizable right, then ask whether Congress foreclosed §1983. Simulated *Livadas v. Bradshaw* applies this separation to a definite federal labor right and refuses to turn Suter into a universal enforcement bar. **Procedural liberty is separate from statutory enforceability.** Simulated *Sandin v. Conner* preserves genuine substantive constraints and meaningful protected interests while rejecting mandatory procedural wording alone. Its sufficient prison-liberty rule is not automatically a universal child-protection entitlement, and historical Sandin's replacement hardship test does not govern. *DeShaney* rejects a general affirmative constitutional duty to prevent private violence outside custody but does not erase every independently created statutory entitlement. Simulated *Pinder v. Johnson* preserves the distinction between mere omission and an official's own affirmative deprivation without declaring every increased-risk theory established. The pleaded failure to investigate cannot be relabeled custodial abuse based on custody acquired only after the injury.

**Remedy and defendant.** Even if a federal right exists, §1983 requires personal or lawful institutional attribution, causation, and appropriate damages; municipal employer status alone is insufficient. The local notice statute and the certified question have their own sources and cannot be decided through an assumed federal merits conclusion.

The Court may affirm the federal dismissals, distinguish CAPTA from Suter and remand the statutory claim, or require a more precise right/foreclosure analysis. It may separately reject a due-process claim founded only on investigative procedures without declaring all noncustodial entitlements impossible. Local certification should remain intact; the Supreme Court simulation does not answer unresolved D.C. statutory notice law or invent a state-court response. Recognizing enforceability would not establish that prompt investigation would have prevented these injuries or automatically support damages against every defendant.

### Questions Presented

1. Whether a plaintiff alleging that a public official acted with an unconstitutional retaliatory motive must establish that motive by clear and convincing evidence, rather than the ordinarily applicable preponderance standard.
2. Whether qualified immunity authorizes courts to impose special evidentiary and procedural requirements in constitutional tort actions where an official's subjective motivation is an element of the underlying violation.
3. Whether a district court may require a plaintiff to establish unconstitutional motive before permitting discovery necessary to obtain evidence of that motive.

## B. ANSWERS AND APPLICATION

1. **Use the ordinary preponderance standard.** Stone would reject a clear-and-convincing motive burden created solely to reduce civil-rights litigation. The statutory remedy and ordinary civil rules provide no such category. Kimberlin has already rejected direct-only proof; replacing that barrier with an unsupported elevated burden recreates much of its exclusionary effect without a source. The protection of officials from unfounded cases warrants use of existing screening tools, not alteration of the underlying plaintiff's burden.

2. **Qualified immunity does not authorize an independent judicial evidentiary code.** Leatherman's reserved individual-officer question must be answered rather than falsely called settled. Stone would extend its source-based reasoning here: the Rules control pleading and proof, subject to their authorized particularity, reply, and case-management mechanisms. Motive remains an element where the constitutional claim makes it one. Officials may contest protected activity, personal involvement, causation, and the Mt. Healthy same-decision issue; none requires pretending motive is legally irrelevant.

3. **No categorical requirement to prove motive before obtaining the discovery needed to prove it.** Stone would permit sequencing of a genuinely dispositive legal issue and restrictions against irrelevant or disproportionate requests. The judge should identify what factual issue requires discovery and why the proposed request bears on it. The prisoner must supply a cognizable, nonconclusory claim, but circumstantial proof, comparator handling, and evidence held by Britton cannot be excluded merely because they are not yet in his possession.

**Express personal-immunity position.** Governing Harlow/Anderson and Kimberlin preserve the broader clearly-established-law defense. Stone's current Remedies commitment proposes replacing that defense with a merits-first inquiry and a narrow defense, borne by the official, for genuine reasonable reliance on identified binding precedent or enacted authority directly authorizing the conduct. Legal silence, ordinary departmental practice, or the absence of identical facts would not suffice. This is a proposed departure, not Kimberlin's holding. It preserves reasonable factual mistakes and lawful discretion within the underlying constitutional rule and protects genuine reliance on authoritative legal permission, while rejecting a second immunity barrier detached from such reliance. The present result also follows under the existing defense: its objective character does not justify the new proof burden.
