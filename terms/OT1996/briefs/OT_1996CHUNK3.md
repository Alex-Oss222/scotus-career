# 25. Chamber of Commerce v. Reich

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether federal courts may entertain an equitable action against subordinate executive officials to challenge the legality of a presidential executive order independently of an APA action against the President, whose APA status remains reserved in the simulation.

2. Whether the absence of an APA cause of action precludes judicial review of executive action alleged to exceed statutory authority or conflict with another federal statute.

3. Whether the Federal Property and Administrative Services Act authorizes the President to exclude federal contractors that hire permanent replacements for economic strikers, notwithstanding rights and limitations established by the National Labor Relations Act.

## B. RECORD, POSTURE AND RELIEF

Executive Order12954 made federal contractors' use of permanent replacements for economic strikers a ground for debarment. Employer organizations challenged implementation by the Secretary of Labor. The district court dismissed; the D.C. Circuit reversed on a nonstatutory review theory and found the policy inconsistent with the NLRA. The pleading had not been amended to challenge the later final regulations through an APA cause. The selected vehicle therefore requires attention to officer-directed equitable review, not an invented APA action against the President. The relief sought concerns the procurement restriction, not reinstatement of particular strikers.

## C. ENTERING LAW APPLIED TO THE RECORD

Simulated Franklin v. Massachusetts actually holds the Secretary's completed census determination final and reviewable despite later presidential transmission. Its five-Justice APA holding, authored by Stone, reserves presidential APA status and coercive relief against the President. The supplied question's historical assumption that the President is definitively outside the APA must therefore be corrected. Simulated Dalton v. Specter unanimously rests on the Base Closure Act's particular preclusion structure; its separate recommendation-finality theory is not controlling. Neither case creates categorical immunity for every subordinate act traceable to a presidential order.

The available route separates 28 U.S.C. §1331 federal-question jurisdiction, traditional equity to restrain an officer acting beyond statutory authority, and sovereign immunity. Section 702's nonmonetary-relief waiver can cover an official-capacity claim against the implementing Secretary even when the plaintiff does not plead an APA cause of action. The pre-divergence Larson–Dugan officer doctrine supplies an additional confined immunity analysis for action outside delegated authority. Neither §1331 nor the waiver itself creates a cause of action, and a statutory preclusion scheme could displace the equitable route. These sources require examination of the Secretary's challenged implementation rather than an assumption about the President's APA status. The Procurement Act's economy-and-efficiency authority must be read with the NLRA's treatment of economic strikes and permanent replacements. Simulated Boston Harbor recognizes genuinely proprietary project conditions analogous to private contracting where Congress has not displaced them. Its narrow construction-sector application does not authorize all policies labeled procurement. A government-wide employment rule unrelated to a particular project's bargain requires its own statutory support. The federal-law conflict here is statutory construction, not simply application of state-law preemption labels.

## D. PARTY POSITIONS, AVAILABLE DISPOSITIONS AND LIMITS

The Secretary invokes procurement economy, executive discretion and restrictions on reviewing presidential choices. The challengers identify a presently operative disqualification implemented by an officer and argue that a general procurement delegation cannot erase the NLRA's balance. The Court could sustain the program on a supported harmonious statutory reading, or reject it through confined prospective officer relief. It must first identify a review route and any genuine preclusion. A ruling on the President's own APA status or an injunction against him is unnecessary.

## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether a nationwide class of individuals alleging nicotine addiction and related misconduct by tobacco manufacturers satisfies the predominance and superiority requirements of Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(b)(3) when the claims involve differing state laws and individualized questions of injury, reliance, and causation.

2. Whether a district court may certify a nationwide mass-tort class based on a substantially novel and untested theory of liability without first conducting a rigorous analysis of the applicable substantive law and the anticipated difficulties of trying the claims on a classwide basis.

## B. RECORD, POSTURE AND RELIEF

The certification order sought common treatment of core conduct and punitive questions while leaving substantial individual issues outside the class phase. The Fifth Circuit accepted a §1292(b) appeal and reversed certification. This is not a mandamus petition. The manufacturers seek decertification; the class seeks reinstatement. ## C. ENTERING LAW APPLIED TO THE RECORD

Shutts limits use of a single State's law for claims lacking adequate connection.

Simulated Matsushita requires constitutionally adequate representation before absent members can be bound, with notice and opt-out opportunities insufficient to cure inadequate representation. Its judgment-recognition setting does not itself decide prospective Rule23 certification here. It nevertheless forecloses treating administrative convenience or an opt-out form as a complete answer to materially conflicting member interests. The reserved scope of collateral adequacy review and Stone's separate structural-failure proposal must not become new Rule23 holdings. The present record requires a concrete law-and-proof map for fraud, reliance, causation and remedies, including any proposed punitive phase. Novelty creates work for that map but is not a statutory categorical exclusion. Prepared Amchem and Adams positions remain undecided inputs.

## D. PARTY POSITIONS, AVAILABLE DISPOSITIONS AND LIMITS

The class emphasizes common manufacturer conduct and proposes later individual proceedings. The defendants identify state-law differences, individual reliance and causation, and a punitive structure that might disconnect punishment from legally established liability. A manageable grouping or issue class requires a demonstrated trial plan and cannot arise merely from relabeling remaining work. Possible coercive settlement pressure should be connected to actual procedural defects rather than assumed from claim size. Affirmance of decertification need not bar every narrower future proposal; reinstatement requires satisfaction of the actual rule.

# 27. In re Deuel

# SECTION I — NEUTRAL MODELING PACKET

**Merits docket status: SIM COURT GRANTED — TO BE HEARD ON THE MERITS.** Review is granted on the questions stated in this packet. This case is on the OT1996 merits docket under the established simulation calendar.

## A. QUESTIONS PRESENTED

1. Whether a claimed DNA molecule is obvious under 35 U.S.C. §103 when the prior art discloses the corresponding protein's amino acid sequence and provides known methods for identifying the DNA encoding it, but does not disclose the claimed nucleotide sequence.

2. Whether the predictability of a method for obtaining a chemical or biological compound is sufficient to establish the obviousness of the resulting claimed compound, notwithstanding uncertainty about its precise molecular structure.

## B. ACTUAL APPLICATION, RECORD AND POSTURE

Thomas F. Deuel, Yue-Sheng Li, Ned R. Siegel and Peter G. Milner appealed the Board of Patent Appeals and Interferences’ November 30, 1993 affirmance of the examiner’s final §103 rejection of claims 4–7 of application 07/542,232, Heparin-Binding Growth Factor. The Federal Circuit reversed on March 28, 1995. The restored Supreme Court proceeding reviews that appellate judgment at the government’s request. It is not an infringement suit, an issued-patent invalidity action or an application to the Supreme Court for a patent.

The application concerns isolated and purified DNA and cDNA encoding human and bovine heparin-binding growth factors, proteins that stimulate cell division and tissue repair. Claims 5 and 7 recite the particular human placental and bovine uterine cDNA sequences obtained. Claims 4 and 6 broadly cover DNA sequences encoding the stated 168-amino-acid proteins. Different nucleotide triplets can encode the same amino acid, so protein identity does not by itself identify a particular DNA sequence. The specific compounds and broad genera require distinct analysis.

The examiner combined Bohlen’s protein disclosure with Maniatis’s general gene-cloning manual. Bohlen disclosed brain mitogens, including their first nineteen amino acids, described them as brain-specific and suggested homology between species. It supplied neither complete protein sequences nor corresponding DNA/cDNA. Maniatis described library screening with probes, without target-specific library selection or probe design. The examiner inferred a reason to clone a useful protein’s gene and asserted, without supporting prior art, that tissue and species differences were immaterial. The Board accepted that motivation and characterized cloning as routine. Neither separately justified rejecting the genus claims.

Deuel challenged the unsupported equation of Bohlen’s proteins with the application’s growth factors, the inference across tissue types and the substitution of a research program for evidence about the claimed compounds. The appellate court held the references insufficient for claims 5 and 7 and found no independent basis supporting rejection of claims 4 and 6. It expressly reserved the latter claims’ possible disclosure and enablement defects under §112. The granted issues concern §103, not a new §101 eligibility question, priority contest, infringement finding or §112 rejection.

## C. ENTERING LAW APPLIED TO THE CLAIMS

The applicable pre-AIA §103 inquiry concerns the claimed subject matter as a whole at the time the invention was made, viewed through ordinary skill in the art. Pre-divergence Graham v. John Deere requires identification of prior art, differences from the claimed subject matter, ordinary skill and relevant objective evidence. Novelty and utility do not establish nonobviousness. Conversely, the mere availability of familiar laboratory techniques does not establish obviousness of everything a researcher might obtain with them.

For claims 5 and 7, the required connection is between the disclosed fragment and methods and the particular cDNA products. The inquiry includes whether an ordinary worker would predictably obtain those products despite incomplete protein information, nucleotide redundancy and uncertain tissue selection. A requirement that prior art print the exact sequence would improperly collapse §103 into anticipation. But a general incentive to find a useful gene does not establish a supported expectation of reaching the claimed product. No objective-indicia findings independently decide this appeal; commercial success, others’ failure and unexpected results must not be invented.

Claims 4 and 6 cannot automatically receive the entire defense of the narrower claims. An obvious route to a species within a genus can matter without prior identification of every genus member. The Board nevertheless gave no separate genus analysis and did not support its assumptions about protein identity, appropriate libraries or recovery. A reviewing court may not replace that missing explanation with a new factual ground supplied by litigation counsel. Nor may it use a possible §112 deficiency as though it were the rejection actually reviewed. Further lawful examination remains distinct from affirming this unsupported rejection.

Simulated Markman v. Westview requires identification of the public claim’s operative meaning while preserving the distinction between claim construction and factual work. Here that means distinguishing precisely recited cDNA from broader encoding genera before comparing either with prior art. The Court may not convert product claims into method claims or reduce a genus to the disclosed examples. Markman’s eight-Justice allocation of embedded technical construction disputes governs despite Stone’s nonjoin, but establishes neither an obviousness test nor appellate review of every PTO finding. This administrative appeal presents no jury-allocation question and calls for no invented construction hearing.

The authoritative Holdings and Standards establish no completed simulation holding resolving the DNA-obviousness issue. The Federal Circuit’s reasoning under review remains subject to §103 and Graham, not itself Supreme Court law. Later historical obviousness and genetic-patent decisions cannot supply the standard; other prepared OT1996 patent packets are also not precedent.

## D. COMPETING POSITIONS, REMEDIES AND LIMITS

The PTO maintains that incentive to obtain a useful gene and a routine method offering a reasonable expectation of success can render the resulting product obvious even before its sequence is known. Deuel responds that this art identifies only an incomplete target and a general technique, leaving unsupported the connection to both the specific compounds and claimed genera. The dispute requires the ordinary worker’s demonstrated capabilities at the invention date, not hindsight from a successful application.

The Court may affirm reversal of this rejection on the existing record while limiting categorical reasoning about methods; vacate for claim-specific reconsideration if an erroneous categorical rule prevented necessary assessment; or restore the rejection only if the proper test and supported agency findings establish its grounds. None automatically issues a patent or resolves all examination requirements. A remand cannot simply authorize a new rejection without proper notice and procedure; an affirmance does not immunize the application from other properly supported grounds.

## B. COMPLETE ANSWERS

**Question 1 — not on this showing, but absence of a printed nucleotide sequence is not a universal safe harbor.** Stone would affirm because the actual combination establishes a reason to investigate, not a supported conclusion that this claimed subject matter was within ordinary skill. This qualifies the appellate opinion’s broad language rather than finding that the Office supplied the missing evidence. A target-specific route with predictably bounded results differs from general library screening plus an incomplete fragment and unresolved tissue assumptions. Stone rejects the Office’s use of the applicants’ failure to disprove those assumptions as a substitute for its initial burden. Ordinary skill includes what the evidence establishes a capable worker knew; it cannot be filled with the invention itself.

He would not decide §101 or the reserved §112 issue. Disclosure of only two cDNAs despite broad encoding claims may warrant examination, but a different statutory defect needs its own ground, notice and record. The proposed result protects the statutory examination process and does not award exclusivity merely for research effort.
